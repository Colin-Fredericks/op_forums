    is this rote rule official?

    11-17-2017, 09:23 AM
    supposedly malcolm shepard wrote it but i can't find any evidence. anyway it goes like this:
    when you make a rote you can make an intelligence+ occult/science roll at difficulty 8. every 5 successes allow you to remove one dot from a normally necessary sphere and increase another one(or even add a new one) but you can't lower any sphere to less than 2 or more than 2 ranks and for each rank you change you also have to add one more sphere dot to show the relative inefficiency of doing it this way. all needs to make sense paradigmatically

    for instance teleporting a massive amount of people at once is correspondence 4 life 3,but if you get 5 successes you could make it correspondence 3 life 3 prime 2(or entropy 2 or mind 2 etc)
    TBH i like this rule and i'll use it anyway but if it's word of god ill have an easier time getting my grumpy storyteller to accept it

                          I can see an in-game justification for this. It's just figuring out different ways to accomplish the same effect. As a simple example, lighting a candle usually uses something like Forces 2, presumably to heat up the wick enough to cause it to ignite. However, couldn't someone try using Matter to say, break apart the molecules in the paraffin, thereby causing an exothermic reaction? One might say that then yeah, they can just go ahead and do that, but that was the player doing the Science roll there instead of the character. And since usually characters are better at Science (or Occult) than players, that's what the roll is for, so the player doesn't have to come up with an in-game explanation that probably requires knowledge they don't have, just like any ability roll.

                          As for play balance, I don't see it as being able to 'get around the rules for free', instead it is rewarding those who are intelligent and knowledgeable, as well as those willing to put in the time and effort to figure out an alternative method. Think of it like MacGyver; if he doesn't have some piece of equipment he needs, he can usually build it out of what's around him (if there's enough other things of course). Not many people can do that. So instead of looking at it like powergaming, its more like simply being smart and hardworking. Even the inefficiency rule is true to life (often MacGyver's inventions don't work as well as the real thing would), and is another "cost" needing to be paid for this approach. If you had the right spheres to begin with, it would both work better, and you wouldn't need to put in all that time and effort (as well as risk since you may not find another way) into researching an alternative approach.

                          And while it may not be in the spirit of "play balance", magic is a largely mental activity, so smarter people are going to do better at it in general. If they've figured out a way to affect that with magic, then yes, that's a big deal, but entirely realistic. The only way around that is to not allow that kind of magic to begin with.

                          As for the needing of lots of successes, often Science (and other research/development type) rolls can be cooperative, and also are often extended. Groups of scientists/occultists/programmers/etc. working together for a long time to solve very difficult problems. This could be just like that.

                                  1) My name has two Ls and two Ps.

                                  2) That was a prototype rule I meant to further tweak and find a place for in order to give rotes some heft. Certainly you'd always need ST permission and in-paradigm justification.

                                  3) The idea that the Spheres are absolute, eternal governors of how willworking functions is something *characters* may believe, but *players* should not. The nature of Awakened magic/k has changed at least once in its history, and probably changed once before. Phil may now work with a line that the Spheres are eternal (and that's his right, absolutely), but this wasn't the case in the early 2000s.

                                  4) In game terms, we're talking about a maximum 2 point difficulty break and the ability to learn a single constricted Effect more easily. Where it can get serious is where the 2 point break stacks with other modifiers, but in MRev you could knock almost anything down to difficulty 3 quickly enough. Where it also gets interesting is in its teachability, which means you can make your apprentices very good at a few tricks. Teachability and the use of grimoires would have been part of any full treatment.

                                  5) In execution it would accompany a discussion of how, while this is the current state of the art for modifying the Effect/Sphere relationship, like the Spheres itself, this is probably not the *only* way, and various oddities in how many rotes are constructed may come from other forgotten or never-duplicated methods.

As a mechanic, it's nonsense to say that Spheres amount to *any* kind of *truth*. A mechanic hasn't truth value, it has a strictly *utilitarian* value

                                      Now, I don't like mages fiddling with Sphere levels to "make this rote easier/harder to learn", like they know about dots and XP costs. I want my mages as far from the System as I can get them. I can see Paradigms coupled with long research discovering easier ways to do stuff (which mechanically can be represented many ways) but I don't see mages purposely gaming the "enlightenment" needed to cast something to provide apprentices with an advantage.

                                      If I wanted mages to be able to learn constrained Rotes outside the normal system (to represent the advantages of having a Mentor, Grimoire or a Tradition that strongly focuses on some spells that are normaly very hard - like, say, shapeshifter shamans or exorcists), I would invent a system so they can do just that - learn constrained Rotes at lower Arete/Sphere levels - instead of changing the way Spheres relate to feats. It's a Rote feature, not a Sphere feature, there's no need to alter the whole Sphere System. IOC we justify it saying that it's easier to learn a static, formulaic, trick than the whole of truth (or something like that). That way we don't have mages thinking "What Spheres can I add to this Adept level Rote to make it Apprentice level?" but rather "What Rotes can I invest into my Grimoire, knowing that perhaps an Apprentice can learn them?" - the difference between these two being that the first one actually aknowledges the Spheres as an absolute governor (that can be cheated, and thus exist) while the other merely aknowledges the fact that Apprentices can learn potent rotes under special conditions.
