                  The freebie points to XP math is already horrifically imbalanced. Letting people get rid of Flaws isn't going to make it worse.

                  The books have consistent stated that it can be done, but very inconsistently express how to do so mechanically.

                  Most groups I've been in that have dealt with this have used one of two options, because esp. with Mages most Flaws can be removed with enough magic if no other option is available:

                  Flat Flaw value x 3 (so a 3 point flaw costs 9 XP to remove).

                  Current Flaw value x 3 (even if it isn't a scaled Flaw). So a 3 point Flaw would cost (9 + 6 + 3) 18 XP to fully remove.

                  Both of these are inspired by the XP costs for Backgrounds, and the Revised Adversarial Backgrounds (which were just Flaws with a different name anyway).

                  That said, I would seriously consider reducing a Flaws value if it is easily corrected, or has become more easily corrected since the Flaw was printed.

                          There are official suggestions in various books, but there's no RAW because buying off a Flaw with XP is essentially an optional rule to an optional rule and there was no simple RAW method printed.

                                  In the house rules I suggested? Yes.

                                  Some of the books would disagree, as some lean more towards treating Merits and Flaws like Backgrounds before XP for Backgrounds was introduced (that is: they are part of points for char-gen, but go up and down at ST discretion in play).

                                  For Mage specifically though, this is already kinda how things work. If you increase your Strength with Life, it's a short duration temporary effect so XP doesn't get involved, or a long term effect where you face issues like Permanent Paradox or Pattern Bleed... until you end the effect or buy Strength with XP.

                                  It's important to remember that XP is a metacurrency, not something that actually exists in the game-setting. The point of XP is to pace character advancement and some mixture of keeping characters roughly even in abilities while also rewarding certain actions over others (which means certain player can get ahead of others). From this perspective the in-game justification for why you're removing a Flaw isn't really what matters to how much XP it costs. After all, it doesn't matter to how much a physical Flaw gives you in freebies if it came from a birth defect, a severe injury, or a magical curse with a permanent effect.
