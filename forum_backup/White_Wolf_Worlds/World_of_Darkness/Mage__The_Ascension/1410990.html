    How do you all handle experience?

    08-29-2020, 09:36 AM
    Little background: My high school friends and I have reconnected during the pandemic are playing mage again for the first time in fifteen years (obviously very awesome on a lot of levels). Needless to say, we're all rusty as hell and this is my first time GMing white wolf. This story is largely social interaction and exploration, and I'm planning for very little combat. We've also agreed that epiphanies and sphere gains will happen at narratively significant moments.

    One thing I remember disliking about white wolf is the XP and character advancement system. It feels so nebulous to me as far as how to dole out experience in a way that doesn't allow characters to power level in what is in-game a very short period of time, yet still allowing that satisfactory feeling of growth. Generally in RPG's its easy to structure XP gain around combat, but because that's not the kind of game I'm running this format simply will not work. Furthermore, because players will not need to devote experience points towards advancing arete and spheres the math behind meaningful rewards changes dramatically. As it stands the only thing for players to spend XP on is attributes and abilities.

    Any suggestions as to how to go about doing this? Should I give a flat reward per session, of say, 2 XP? Or do any of you have alternative formats of character advancement that you use?

    Thanks in advance!

      Well, for any RPG with experience, the first question when giving it out is, 'How quickly do I want the characters to improve?' The second question should be, 'What will the characters be improving with their XP?'

      Mage gives you 1 automatic XP per chapter, which is basically a session. Stories take multiple sessions--or might be a long session.

      Since you're working epiphanies and sphere gains per a narrative model, then you probably don't need to have players spending experience on some of the most expensive things in Mage, Arete (Enlightenment for Technocracy characters) and Spheres. Thus, you can drop the XP per session. Some versions of the game allow players to buy backgrounds. If you're giving them through the narrative, then you don't need to reward XP to use in buying backgrounds.

      That means, you're looking at attributes and abilities and Willpower as purchases. Attributes are 4x the current rating, abilities are 2x the current rating (though there are some iterations of the game that made Knowledges cheaper) and Willpower costs the current rating to raise. Since there are 9 attributes and Willpower, your main question here is how you want to handle abilities. Are you using broad abilities, are you using the secondary abilities found in the Book of Shadows and similar books which expand the abilities with more and more niche abilities? Are you using required specializations and some of the rules about well-rounded craftsmen in Mage 20? How important are those other abilities relative to the standard abilities? How quickly will you let the PCs get good at abilities?

      Then you're into the metagame of incentivizing good play. If everyone gets 1 XP per session and those who play their character well get a little more, what's playing the character well? This benefits the players who can roleplay their concept well over the other players. Success in a session is also difficult to define, so how often you award success for great success in a story matters.

      Here's how I've done XP since I ran Mage back when 1e came out. I haven't run Mage regularly, but I have run all editions plus a GURPS Mage: the Ascension game. Currently I'm running M20 with a flavor closer to 2e than any other edition:

      A. Backgrounds are awarded in play for work in play. XP cannot be spent on backgrounds. Mostly characters are going to pick up backgrounds that are mundane, like Allies, Contacts and Resources or represent stuff, like getting a magic item.
      B. I award study points for the use of a Library using the Mage 2e rules and I use the library's reduction of study time for Spheres using The Bitter Road book from Mage: Revised. Though characters are going to need to put in a lot of time even with the 5-point shared chantry library to get above rank 2 in a sphere.
      C. I award study points for subjects characters are significantly studying with a library or a teacher. It's not a huge amount, but this can raise abilities or attributes. If a PC is working out regularly for months and has low Physical stats, they'll probably be getting a little better.
      D. There's no rule on languages in M20 since they got made into Merits, so I'm pretty much stealing from GURPS and how it does languages and making them free. It's about 1200 hours of good study with an instructor or quality instructional materials to become fluent in speaking and writing a language. If a character already had some time in it from another knowledge, like say a lawyer already knowing a few Latin words, that shaves off some time.
      E. I make getting past rank 3 in any Ability difficult--you don't suddenly become an expert in something even if you are a mage. If your character was at rank 3, they still need to show effort to get rank 4. The player who is playing an Akashic Brother has 6 months or more of in-game time to get rank 4 in Do, and that's going to be tough as there aren't a lot of trainers and this is one of those Akashic Brotherhood characters played by a player who skipped taking any language besides English and hasn't gotten into the Tradition's philosophy and history much. No one else is really pushing for rank 4, but it would still be difficult.

      Then we get into XP awards. I do the automatic award and then I tend to split the remaining totals between study points for character traits substantially used in the session or which are new revelations. Other XP awards come from good roleplaying per the guidelines. For a 6-hour block of gaming, which might be one session or two sessions, the characters usually pick up 2 or 3 XP and a couple of study points.

      However, they do have to buy Arete and Spheres, so even starting them at Arete 3 and with a few extra freebies, no character has gotten to Arete 4 since we started in January (maybe February) 2020. The closest character was buying a few things here and there is played by my best player. And that character would be further away from Arete 4 if all of their rolls for Library and Mentor to learn Forces 2 hadn't been terrible.

      I recommend talking with the players about what growth rate they want and how important things are. A flat reward is simple, but doesn't incentivize good play. And since Mage isn't just about combat unless you make it just about combat, the rewards of character growth should reward good character play.

          Mage is perhaps my favorite game to run, with the current chronicle in its 7th year, I think. baakyocalder poses two good questions for your to ask, to which I'd add a third: How much direction do I (as Storyteller) want to provide the PC's in their development? I typically give 3xp per session, sometimes 4 if the PC's experienced something profound, learned valuable information, or if the session included a time slip. Sometimes I give extra xp that is directed in some way, such as to an Ability a given PC has been using but hasn't prioritized adding a dot to. If the PC's had an epiphany of some kind, I might direct it to raising Arete. So the end of a session might look like: "Everyone gets 3 xp plus 1xp that can be spent on an Attribute."

          At the end of a story I give a bonus of 1/2 x (# of sessions in the story)xp. Some of that xp will be directed. I also have players roll their Library and Mentor ratings. 1+ sux = 1xp (the Mentor usually decides where it can be spent). On a fail, they just get a luck point (reroll) on a magic roll. On a botch they get nothing.

          My stories run 6 sessions on average, but it all depends on the unfolding of events.

          So depending on what degree of progression you want, you could designate some xp given as general and 1 or 2 points as designated toward Attributes, Abilities, what have you. You could even do this with Spheres and Arete, for a more staged progression. "Tom earns 8xp toward increasing Time," for example. Enjoy your game~

              You might consider swiping some ideas from Chronicles of Darkness. In particular, I'm thinking of its Aspirations system, where each character specifies things he or she wants to achieve; and experience gets awarded based on progress toward those goals. Just be sure to allow aspirations to change at dramatically appropriate points in the game. This can provide an alternative way to structure character growth that doesn't depend on combat.

              As well, you might consider downplaying the importance of character growth. In narrative-driven games, you often have characters who start out quite competent, and never really improve much: they can already do enough of what the player wants that adding dots to character traits such as attributes and abilities ceases to have as much of a sense of accomplishment. As well, even characters who start out as novices eventually get to that point after a while. I've been working on ideas to change the paradigm of spending XPs for such characters by adding other things that experience can be spent on other than self-improvement.

              Mage is especially good for this sort of thing, because the premise of the game is “you have the power to change the world”. As such, I've been experimenting with a system where experience points can be used as a fuel for Magick, as a way to make magical Effects more impressive and/or lasting. The idea is that the character types who have plateaued in terms of personal capability continue to earn experience; but now they mostly spend it on changing the world instead of improving themselves. And with experience spent on Coincidental Effects, especially using a variant of Magick Enhancing Abilities, everything the mage does has the potential to be more impactful.

              I've considered the possibility of restricting this ability to spend experience on things other than self-improvement by means of the aforementioned Aspirations; but ultimately, I don't think such a requirement is necessary: experience points are so rare and precious (or at least should be) that I don't see players spending them frivolously: any time a player spends an experience point, that is a statement that “this matters to me!”

              My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                  I generally prefer high-powered games to more prosaic games when doing Mage--rank 3 and 4 Sphere effects are what I like handling at baseline. That being said, even at that level and with substantial XP awards, there is so much to buy in Mage if you allow secondary Abilities that I don't find players wasting XP. If they buy something, there's usually a specific use for it; I also consider secondary Abilities or the flavors of Esoterica to be part of magical focuses so they get their due.

                  For example, the PC with a 2-dot Blessing for informational visions rolls a lot on Lucid Dreaming. They have it at 2 dots, which could be higher, but they also get to use it regularly to get some idea of what's going on in those cryptic visions and dreams.Do is powerful as an ability, but if it comes up, the Akashic Brotherhood character gets to use it. If there is a secondary Ability that is more in line with information on something than a main Ability, such as Lore versus Occult, I make the difficulty lower for the secondary Ability.

                  For example, someone who has Order of Hermes Lore is going to know an obscure fact about Hermetic magic of sacred circles that has entered the general Occult writings better than someone who just has Occult.

                  I don't like dump skills in any game--and see that if a player takes something with XP or character currency during character generation they should get a chance to use it.
