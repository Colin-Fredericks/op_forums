    System for Determining In-Game Time Interval for Arete Rolls When Extended Casting

    11-09-2019, 03:17 AM
    I’m curious as to how storytellers handle the determination of how many times a mage can roll to produce a magical effect from moment to moment, as opposed to when they have to go to rolls happening at larger time intervals (half-hours, hours, multiple hours, whatever). The books have always said this was definitely a thing (extended rolling in the middle of a scene to produce a larger effect than you could achieve by only rolling once), but there was only an official system made for this in M20, and even then it’s optional (and, I think, a bit ill conceived—but that’s not the point of this post).

    I made my own version of this system (explained below) when I started a Mage game in ’08-’09. All we had at that time was Revised, and it was no help—it just says that the ST will mediate when or what kinds of effects would require greater time intervals between rolls (turn-by-turn versus hours). I thought it was unfair, and probably unworkable, for everyone to just rely on my split-second pronouncements. Even as the final arbiter of what happens, it’s still important to be at least a little consistent and even-handed. I felt players (and characters) needed at least basic idea of what they could reliably pull off under normal conditions without having to rely entirely on my caprice from one moment to the next. I’m a firm believer that the system is as much there to alleviate the burden or the ST/GM as it is to give players buttons to push. So this is the system I came up with:

    There are basic three intervals of time that a mage can cast in: turn-by-turn, 15 minute-by-15 minute, and hour-by-hour.

    Turn-by-turn (“T-b-T”): a mage can roll T-b-T a number of times equal to their Arete, or can achieve a maximum number of successes equal to their Arete+rating of the highest sphere used. (example, if you Juan has Arete 4 Forces 4/Prime 2, they can achieve a maximum of 8 successes on a For3/Prime2 blast rolling turn-by-turn). Turn-by-turn rolling stops as soon as either limit is reached.

    15m-by-15m (15m): a mage can roll Arete x 2 times, and may garner [(Arete+Highest Sphere)x2] successes. If the mage decides to roll something at the 15m interval when they could otherwise have done T-b-T, they may roll Arete x 3 times, and receive a -1 Diff.

    Hour-by-hour (“Hourly”): Unlimited number of rolls (subject to Stamina, fatigue, etc., as usual); usual cap of Arete x Willpower. If the mage decides to roll something at the Hourly interval when they could otherwise have done T-b-T, they receive a -2 Diff.; if decide to roll something at the Hourly interval when they could otherwise have done 15m, they receive a -1 Diff.

    I got charts! (though the math is pretty simple if you really get down to it)

    All other rules regarding the accumulation of a backlash, failing and continuing at higher diffs, etc. All that stuff from the book still applies—my rule only reaches putting a certain interval on rolling and accumulating power/successes.

    Common Questions:

    Q. How does this compare to M20's system?
    A. M20 uses more or less the same system (or, at least, we use the same categories of control, albeit with different values). You're capped at a maximum, global number of rolls for any single effect equal to your Arete+WP; and a maximum, global number of successes equal to your Arete x WP. Interval-wise, your Arete doesn't even matter for the first interval--per M20 absolutely everyone is capped at 2 rolls and 5 successes in turn-by-turn rolling (aka "brief rites", which they construe as taking less than 5 minutes altogether); up to 10 successes for Hour-by-Hour (aka "Ceremony"); and 5-hour-by-5-hour for what they call "Grand Work" for more than 10. Notably, there is no 15 minute interval here, and apparently there's nothing that takes between 6 and 59 minutes to cast.) (So if you're Master--i.e., Arete 5--and your dice are really hot, so you're getting nothing but successes (which isn't that out there if you consider that you could be rolling at Diff 3 the whole time), casting a magical effect is either going to take you 5 minutes, or 2 hours, or 15 hours. That strikes me as bizarre.)

    Q. Are there things that MUST be rolled at 15m or higher, or can only be rolled at Hourly?
    A. Yes, but there’s not set system for that. Mostly it’s the sort of stuff you would imagine (major weather work or summonings; creating wonders; scrying across dozens or hundreds of square miles; dividing by zero; etc.)

    Q. Can I start off rolling T-b-T, and then move into 15m, and then Hourly?
    A. Yes, but having played with this system for about ten years now, I would not do so again. As it stands, even if my players know they’re going to need 18 successes or something because they want to make a particularly powerful version of something otherwise humdrum, they rarely start off at 15m or Hourly—they’ll get their quick four T-b-T rolls in (they’re all Arete 4 at this point), then move on to their eight 15m rolls. It’s not as detrimental or unbalancing to the game as you might think since obviously all rival mages work on these same principles, so really all I did was amp up the amount of magic in the game, which is fine with me for the most part. If I’m irked at all it’s not because I’m noti getting the game output that I want, but rather because I find this part of my system inelegant. If I could do it again, I would add a rule that if the amount of successes a character needs is such that we already know they’re going to have to move into another interval of rolling beyond T-b-T no matter what, they MUST start the rolling at that next higher interval (i.e., character has Arete 4 Life 3, but wants a 10-successes Better Body effect—the max successes they can get T-b-T is 7, so they would be obligated to start at the 15m interval). Something else I would differently if I did this today: add a fourth time interval for 5-hour-by-5-hours. It's the only part of the optional system M20 for extending casting that I like.

    Q. How does spending Willpower factor in?
    A. If a Willpower is spent on a successful roll the successes from which ultimately be capped based on the limit of successes that could be achieved at that level, the Willpower point can be counted as exceeding the regular success cap (Example: you have Arete 3 and Life 3 and are casting Better Body T-b-T—so a max of 6 successes is permissible; your first two rolls net 3 and 2 successes, respectively. On your third roll you spend a WP and roll three natural successes. Typically, you would be capped at one successes because of the T-b-T limit of 6 successes—however, in this case, because you spent a WP on the roll, you would in fact get a total of 7 successes. In this way, spending WP is always good.)

    Q. Isn’t this a little brutal for low-Arete Mages?
    A. Yes, but, in my conceptualization, that brutality and limitation is not (strictly speaking) thematically inconsistent with the rest of the game. It sucks to suck—Arete 1 mages are basically Human+, and only get limited insights, powers, glimpses, and enhancements on the fly. If they learn to harness their power into rituals and the like, then they can be slightly less hampered.


    Thank you if you got this far! I always welcome discussion and notes, and I’m reallyinterested to know what everyone uses at their own tables for this sorts of matter (if anything)!!!


    ***NOTE: this post has been edited to reflect that M20 caps maximum rolls for any single given effect/ritual/etc. at Arete + WP rolls; and this cap is separate and apart from the optional Rite/Ceremony/Grand Work system. Thank you to Tytalus for the correction!

          Originally posted by Tytalus View Post
          The base rules on page 541 also say 1 hour per roll.
          I don't think they do. If you do a word-search for the word "hour" on page 541, the word is only even mentioned with reference to "Rituals and Stamina". There it states as follows:

          Extended rites are exhausting. As a general rule, assume that a character may work for one hour without penalty for each dot in his Stamina Trait. After that, you’ll need to make a Stamina roll for each subsequent hour… and the difficulty for that roll is the base difficulty of the ritual itself. A successful roll allows the mage to go on for another hour; the second roll suffers a +1 penalty to its difficulty; the third suffers a +2 penalty, and so on.
          This paragraph doesn't speak to how often a Mage may cast magic to produce a given magical effect, or the time interval of those rolls--all it does set forth rules for how long the mage can physically be at the task of casting magic. For the purposes of determining whether a mage is physically capable of sustaining his effort (which is separate from metaphysical limitations on how much magical power may be garnered in any given span of time), the basic unit is one hour. In essence this has the side-effect of making it so that if anyone wants to cast a "Grand Work" (i.e., 5 in-game hours between every Arete roll), the mage needs a minimum of Stamina 5 to be able to make even a single roll without having to start overexerting themselves.

          Additionally, on page 529 it says that some rituals (referring to the practice of rolling more than once to achieve something greater than you would otherwise have been able to achieve in a single turn) involve "brief activity, and other can take hours or even days[,]" which basically implies that there is some amount of time that a ritual (i.e., extended casting) can take that is less an hour.

          Originally posted by Tytalus View Post
          What I am not clear on is if you don't use the optional rite rules... I believe you only get one roll.
          The language I reference above from page 539 says "[s]ome rituals involve brief activity, and others can take hours or even days." Following that they refer to the optional system, seemingly as a means to systematize the underlying implication. Reading that entire section together, it's clear the game intends for there to be "rituals" that take less than a hour, regardless of whether their optional system is used to draw where the bright lines go.
