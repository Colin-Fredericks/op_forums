                    Books about the Technocracy have a tendency to portray it all through the lens of the New World Order. For example, the talk in Technocracy Reloaded about what field agents in the Technocracy look and act like sounds suspiciously like what Black Hats would look and act like. The thing is, I have a hard time believing that any of the other Conventions would fall in line so readily. Well, maybe Iteration X. Maybe.

                    But in many ways, The Syndicate is the polar opposite of the New World Order. There's a reason why the revised Convention Books talk about an upcoming Technocratic Civil War painted the New World Order and The Syndicate as the rival poles in that conflict.

                    So yeah, I tend to agree that Syndicate power dynamics and NWO hierarchy don't mesh very well. That's not just a matter of sex; that's the whole ball of wax.

                    While the worst of the Syndicate shows a callousness about the well-being of others, and shows just how damaging rampant selfishness can be, the more moderate Syndic is a potentially noble figure; a champion of individual rights. The New World Order, meanwhile, it's pretty much as far opposite in terms of individual rights and you can get. It would not surprise me in the least, for example, if the Syndicate had a secret deprogramming center, to keep their personnel free of NWO brainwashing. For an entirely different reason from why the Void Engineers do it, mind you; but the result would be about the same.

                                    Originally posted by Asmodai View Post
                                    I have never picked up the new convention books post ItX. Are they worth it? Does it cover this tension between the conventions well? Does it give something new and special to the factions that's worth a look? Also anything on Syndicate sex stuff?
                                    Oh, they had a lot of stuff that was new and special — which, for the most part, promptly got incorporated into M20. Remember: with the exception of the Iteration X book, the Revised Convention Books were actually produced a year before M20 was announced.

                                    NWO introduced the Data Sphere, and updated their gear to something more appropriate to the 21st century. It also introduced the ATLAS program, where the NWO took over Iteration X's now-defunct cyborg program (Iteration X had shifted its attention to Hardsuits) and was putting the brains of captured Awakened personnel into cyborg bodies.

                                    I don't recall much of what the Progenitors book did, other than to set them up as the Convention that was trying to heal the divide between three NWO and The Syndicate. That, and there was less emphasis on them as a source of body horror and more emphasis on them as doctors.

                                    The Syndicate book is where the concept of Primal Utility was introduced; before that, the most common perception of the Syndicate was that money magic centers around the probabilistic elements of Entropy. So that was a big new development — dare I say, a game changer. And for the first time that I'm aware of, the Syndicate paradigm was presented in a way that made sense, and wasn't a balancing act between “are they even Awakened?” and “Are they even Technocrats?” I still go to that book for advice on how the Syndicate's Practice and Instruments work.

                                    The Void Engineers were completely reinvented: no longer peaceful explorers of the Umbra who were on the verge of abandoning the Technocracy, they were now space marines fighting a desperate war against a danger that their fellow Earthside Conventions were uniquely vulnerable to: Threat Null. Any notion of abandoning the Union was gone, because you don't abandon someone who is in so much danger. It also introduced Tychoidean Cosmology, a subject touched on in M20 but much more thoroughly explained in the Convention Book, as well as revising Dimensional Science from “just like Spirit, except that you can create portals to the Umbra inside Technocratic strongholds” to a full-on variant Sphere that places more emphasis on Gauntlet Manipulation, less on Extra-Dimensional Entities, and what EDE focus remained shifted from binding and controlling them to fighting them: more Ghostbusters. There was also a short section on “Void Correspondence”, which took the Correspondence Sphere in the opposite direction from what Data did to it, making it more about Space and less about mystical connections. Oh, and the Voidships. The VE Convention Book gives design rules for their spacecraft.

                                    Frankly, I'm glad I got all of them when they came out. In today's environment, I'd be less inclined to get them. But I'd prioritize the Void Engineers, with the Syndicate coming in a close second, followed by the NWO. The Progenitors would be a distant fourth, and Iteration X (which came out a decade earlier, just as the original World of Darkness was closing up shop) dead last — although its Hardsuits and the general shift away from them being cyborg warriors to being mechanical geniuses set the course for the revisions that the rest of the books underwent.

                                    But no, nothing about sex.
