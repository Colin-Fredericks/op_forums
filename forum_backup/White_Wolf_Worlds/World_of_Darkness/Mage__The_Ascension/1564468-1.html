    Originally posted by ~Ozz~ View Post
    Okay maybe I am wrong but I was looking at spirit like a toolbox, more tools equals more power but if they can reach any spirit they want it doesnt really matter how much I populate the penumbra. I am getting that with a little more effort a PC can negotiate/force any kind of spirit
    That's the thing: they're not.

    Players who talk about how strong Spirit is as a means to do anything are not talking the whole story because they're already used to it.

    Spirits are individual creatures with their own ecology and societies, their own interests, and the Sphere of Spirit does not force them into doing much of anything. Period.

    Also they don't literally do whatever. You don't wake the spirit of a bonfire and suddenly have a portable fire spell. The process is more:

    1. You rouse the spirit of the bonfire.

    2. The spirit doesn't care about you and will not cooperate just because, you have to negotiate, be nice for a long time, or good old fashioned violence will do the talking.

    3. Now you have a measure of control over that bonfire. And not enough to stop iqt from going out when fuel runs out.

    4. A roused spirit has little power, just a modicum of influence over its direct cause. It doesn't even have stats because it's little more than an object with opinions.

    5. Other spirits simply have their stats. They do what they can do, and since they're not moored to a specific object anymore, their stats are also the end of it.

    Spirit let you interact with spirits. Not even binding (level 4) can force their compliance. A weak Gaffling isn't smart or independent, but it is not dependent on anyone, they're shadows of the consciousness of a greater spirit. This is a description of intelligence and personality, not of ease to command.

    Individually you still have to communicate and negotiate. Or use other Magic in conjunction with Spirit. And if you Bind a spirit into a Fetish against its will, this Fetish will be both hard to use and dangerous to have around.

        That

        In a sense, it's a very "social" power. It's not all that unlike how you don't need Forces to lit your ciggarrete if you have a friend with a match. It's a Sphere that opens a lot of posibilities, but also requires a lot of interpretation and can get you into no end of trouble.

        Forcing a spirit to comply with magick it's possible (I don't remember the exact Spheres), but it's very much a can of worms of it's own. To put it simply, unless the spirit you're forcing it's somehow ok with what you're trying to do, you've gained an eternal enemy. Gain a lot of these and powerful spirits of the kind nobody can easily deal with may start taking notice.

        Can a Mind Mage control the President of a Superpower, and try to start wars with countries? - RAW, yes. Should a mage do this? - hell no. Not unless you want to see what the Technocracy's migth can do, and that's if they get to you first.

        Note: Ofc, spirits are much more "esoterical" in the way they work than people (because they're a fictional element). We don't know exactly how all of the Umbra works, and neither do Mages. From Wraith you can learn how could Ghosts would respond to mages. But all spirits have their own hierarchies, responsabilities, etc...Have fun thinking how that interacts with the mage's wishes on cases where you think they're using a little too much spiritual help.

        Like: If a Mage it's creating too many fires with the help of spirits that left alone wouldn't do that, a higher envoy of the Elemental Court of Fire could want to have a talk. If the mage wasn't using force, and just persuaded the spirits, the minister doesn't need to be hostile. Could be curious, could want *in* on whatever deal the spirits have, could tax the mage for repeated use of resources that belong to the Court...it depends on how you think the Fire Elemental "gig" works.

            How does the spirit world look to the Mage? However their Focus says it should. A Mage's Paradigm is the filter through which they see everything, whether just looking in from the outside or stepping sideways.

            Even different Mage groups who gravitate towards the same Penumbra type (Astral, Wild, Dark) differ in the specifics.

            A Celestial Chorus miracle worker might see the Astral Penumbra as a place of pure white sunrays and lots of lesser angels flying around. Whereas a Virtual Adept who looks at the Astral Penumbra might see flows of digital information and free-floating programs.

            A Verbana witch's vision of the Spirit Wilds might be covered in roots and vines and sylphs. While an Ecstasy Cultist's vision of the Spirit Wilds is a psychedelic 60s drug trip (even when they're not high).

            A Euthanatos necromancer looks at the Dark Penumbra as a place of fog and shadows and flitting phantoms, maybe with a healthy dose of Hindu stylings (or whatever particular brand of death/fate magic they happen to rep, because the Chakravanti is a big tent). Whereas a Hollow One looks at the Dark Penumbra as something out of a Tim Burton film or gothic horror story. And an Etherite scientist looks through their SpectroScope^TM (patent pending) and sees a lot of grainy images, glitches, and suggestive smears or shapes (basically, look at any "ghost hunter" shows).

            If only one PC is doing the bulk of the Spirit work initially, you can fairly easily tailor the aesthetics of the Umbra/Penumbra to suit their Paradigm.

                A very simplified break down of the Spirit Sphere in clear language would be as follow:

                1. You can see invisible ghosts and spirits.

                2. You interact with invisible ghosts and spirits.
                - Also minor Gauntlet tricks.

                3. You can travel to other worlds.
                - Also make things somewhat aware.

                4. You can open inter dimensional portals.
                - Also make Fetishes.

                5. You can empower spirits and make souls.
                - Also your own "pocket" dimensions.

                There is more? Yes. But those will cover 98% of any practical uses.

                Levels 1 and 2 only matter to deal with the Penumbra most of the time, so they deal with spirits close to Earth, but not materialized.

                Levels 3 and 4 mostly deal with travel or making magic items, but their magic items give the Mage less control. You can make an item with powers you don't have, but they're full of their own rules and costs to use. So the bigger impact is often the travel.

                Level 5 can make a lot of things, but they're among the hardest feats of Magic in the game and still require other Spheres for details. The main use ends up being give or drain Essence, which is pretty useful, or create weird matter in other realities, kind like Matter 5 but don't work on Earth. The next simplest feat is to create a human soul and it already requires IIRC 15-20 successes and 5-10 Quintessence.

                And do other Magic work in other worlds without Spirit? Yes, pretty much. They can have stranger rules and effects on Magic. And they can be normal. If you are already in the same dimension as a spirit, most of your Magic will work normally most of the time. Spirit gives an edge, but most other Magic should work just fine.

                                        Cosmology in Mage has three big problems.

                                        1. It uses overly complicated language,

                                        2. It is inconsistent,

                                        3. It is referential to other WoD games.

                                        Both are partially deliberate, but end up being mostly confusing. In the end it's more useful to start from a baseline just as complete, but with simpler explanations and simplified systems you can complicate later, as you learn more.

                                        So the layers are more or less like this, with marks where you have to decide things. Don't worry too much about the decisions:

                                        1. The Gauntlet. Just a barrier. It's the same for everything and has a rating that varies by local.

                                        2. Quasi-Earths. When you cross the Gauntlet without Correspondence you usually have to go to one of those, defined by the Magic. Realms marked as Penumbrae may be the same, your choice:

                                        2a. Astral Penumbra, maps perfectly to Earth and is its idealized version, shaped by emotion, beliefs and symbols. Has natural connections to the Astral Reaches (Level 3). Probably it's one of the Penumbrae.

                                        2b. Spirit Penumbra, maps perfectly to Earth and is where its vitality sits, shaped by things and landscape on Earth, but empowered by "importance" (probably emotions, beliefs and symbols, but embodied in the thing they're linked to). Has natural connections to the Spirit Wilds (Level 3). Probably it's one of the Penumbrae.

                                        2c. Low Penumbra, maps perfectly to Earth and is a decayed reflection, whatever you decide it means, empowered by whatever, maybe by memory of things gone. May have natural connections to the Shadowlands (Level 2), the Underworld (Level 3) or nothing, choose. Probably it's one of the Penumbrae.

                                        2d. The Shadowlands, maps perfectly to Earth and is a decayed reflection, whatever you decide it means, empowered by memory of things gone. Has natural connections to the Underworld (Level 3). May be the same thing as the Low Penumbra. Probably it's one of the Penumbrae. COMES FROM WRAITH AND IS OPTIONAL.

                                        2e. Chimeric Reality, maps perfectly to Earth and is a psychedelic trip made real, empowered by wonder and creativity. Has natural connections to the Near Dreaming (Level 3). May be one of the Penumbrae, or may be on Earth directly, or may be on the Dream Zone (Level 3/4).COMES FROM CHANGELING AND IS OPTIONAL.

                                        2f. Everett Volumes. Those are NOT Penumbrae. They're alternative Earths. Usually they are other timelines, but may be stranger and have different rules of reality. They may be "Level 0" like Earth or stay Level 2, your choice. It's unclear if Material worlds too extremely different, like fantasy settings, exist here, your choice.

                                        3. Otherworldly Expanses. Those places are unmoored from Earth completely and work like infinite hubs: their only commonality is that you can reach them from a Level 2 place:

                                        3a. Astral Reaches, the Realms of Ideas. Has its own mind-based rules for some things and the place you arrive is an infinite extension of incongruous environments called the Vulgate. Think of places here like The Idea of a City, or the Toon Frontier: they're symbolic, but not terribly abstract, not specific and not important.

                                        3b. Spirit Wilds, the Realm of Spirits. Yes, the game will make you use the word "spirit" to 3 or 4 different things. It's an infinite sequence of normal environments, but more grandiose. It has no other relevant name.

                                        3c. Underworld, the Realm of Memory. Yes, of Memory. All destroyed things that are still remembered come here. They're not Ghosts, and they may not remain intact for long. If you are lucky or smart you'll arrive at a place called a Kingdom where the dead of a given area or something managed to establish protection against The Tempest. If not, you arrive at the Tempest and will start to get badly hurt, maybe die, become a Ghost, and be shredded again. COMES FROM WRAITH AND IS OPTIONAL, KINDA

                                        3d. The Dreaming, Realm of Glamour. This is an infinite expanse of stories. Most of the place is wilderness, but may house anything. Here lives fairies, and they're weird. It has Paths where travel is safe-ish, and you risk not remembering shit if your trip when leaving it if you don't protect your mind enough.

                                        4. Realms and Zones, the "final destinations". Those are other realities in any sense, usually limited in size, but can be gigantic, even bigger than Earth.
                                        - Realms: you arrive at them from a Level 3 expanse. The Spires of the Astral Reaches have passages to Realms, the Spirit Wilds have paths to Realms. In the Underworld and the Dreaming this is usually deeper parts of the place, like the Labyrinth, but you can put Realms there, too.
                                        - Zones: just like Realms, but they extend their entrances to anywhere, including sometimes Earth. You're on your merry way and suddenly you see a strange door, big hollow tree, or any other thing that isn't very normal where you are, and if you enter you're in a Zone. The good side is that if you understand the rules of the Zone it isn't usually hard to leave. The Dream Zone has all dreams of everything that dreams, the Web is a reflection of the current Internet, both are reasonably easy to reach directly and leave safely.

                                        5. The Near Horizon. It's like a tougher Gauntlet that separates all those things from Near Space. It is actually VISIBLE for everyone, but invisible just in the sense that it is colorless and transparent. Stars and planets actually shine through it, and Horizon Realms can be visible here too sometimes. IIRC it currently sits a little beyond the Moon.

                                        6. Near Space. For Mages this is the best definition of "where space begins". Few but significant Realms exist here, like the Shard Realms that may or may not be the source of Magic. This is prime Real Estate for artificial Realms, so you'll find most of them here, the Horizon Realms.

                                        7. Far Horizon. This is a Gauntlet so tough it requires Spirit 5 to even try to cross. There Lies Monsters. Reality as we know it has no meaning​ beyond this threshold, yet all other stars lie there. If anything isn't accessible from the other Umbrae, it is from here, but it's incredibly dangerous. Aliens do come from there too, they're an actual, real threat. Do what you want from here on, only one Realm was ever built and/or visited this far, and it is now lost.

                                                    It's simpler than it seems, just follow the numbers and study only one thing at a time. Anything you don't need at the moment just ignore, w

                                                    Heaven and Hell are Realms. Anything you think off as another reality in the usual sense is a Realm. Sometimes multiple Realms that have the same name because they're different versions of the same thing.

                                                    So you find them in the Astral Reaches, in the Spires.

                                                    You can simplify a lot, but I'd recommend just ignore bits until they matter.

                                                    Let's try the Minimum Viable Umbra, just in case your players want to go there:

                                                    1. Gauntlet

                                                    2. Penumbrae. Keep 3 and keep them separate, that increases size but simplify use:

                                                    2a. Astral Penumbra
                                                    2b. Spirit Penumbra
                                                    2c. Shadowlands

                                                    3. Expanses. They have some places to wander and also contain Realms:

                                                    3a. Astral Reaches
                                                    3b. Spirit Wilds
                                                    3c. Underworld

                                                    4. Realms and Zones. Everything, basically.

                                                    Hell, Heaven, Hades, Arcadia, Pangaea, Abyss, anything at all is a Realm. Choose if it is in the Astral Reaches, the Spirit Wilds, or it is a Zone.

                                                    Zones are just Realms that have connections everywhere.
                                                    - Dream Zone (Reaches anything that dreams),
                                                    - Digital Web (Touches every computer with a connection),
                                                    - Aelder Bole (The giant tree, has passages everywhere),
                                                    - Mirror Zone (Connects all mirrors).

                                                    So the map is:

                                                    Gauntlet
                                                    |
                                                    |> Astral Penumbra > Astral Reaches > Realms
                                                    |> Spirit Penumbra > Spirit Wilds > Realms
                                                    |> Shadowlands > Underworld > The Labyrinth
                                                    |> Everett Volumes (any alternate Earth)

                                                    Zones are outside the map.

                                                    And that's it. Everything else is optional complications you can explore later. And every world you can conceive is just a Realm.

                                                    What a Realm/Zone is? Whatever you want, if you want to define at all. The map isn't about what anywhere means, just how to get there.

                                                        The way I interpret the Shadowlands is that there's the Dark Penumbra, and then there's the "Shadowlands proper".

                                                        The former is where the Tempest and Wraith Society is. The "deeper" parts of the underworld, basically, that you "descend into". Wraiths go there to have adventures and get involved in Ghost Politics.

                                                        The latter is the parts nearest the Skinlands, and thus most resembles it. From what I understand as someone with very little WtO knowledge, Wraiths can only hang out here nearby to objects or locations important to them in life (their Fetters). They typically "go topside" for a variety of reasons. To watch over their loved ones/descendants. To contact spirit mediums. To guard their Fetters. To gather Pathos. To torment the new owners into leaving their house. To investigate their death. To avoid Ghost Politics. To avoid the Tempest. To avoid getting turned into ashtrays. Etc, etc, etc.

                                                        Under this model, a Mage game involving the Spirit sphere will include ghosts only in those places where it makes narrative sense, and then not all the time. A school might have a kid bullied to a permanent end hanging around. Hospitals might have an elderly person who spent years there, either continuously or on and off. A house might be haunted by its former owner(s), obvs. The home of a spirit medium might have the ghosts of people whose Fetters the medium holds for safekeeping. A street corner might have a ghost of one who got run over or mugged to a permanent end. Etc, etc.

                                                        And, of course, a murder scene MIGHT have the ghost of the victim (or another ghost who saw what happened). The victim's ghost might still be in their new-ghost "cocoon", and thus unable to talk. Though this might, itself, be a quest; figuring out how to either free them, or get another Wraith there to wake them in their place.
