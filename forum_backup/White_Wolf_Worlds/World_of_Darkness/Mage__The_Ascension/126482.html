    Sorcerer: A better class of caster

    05-06-2014, 02:21 AM
    So I'm playing a Sorcerer using Sorcerer Revised and I need some help.

    For you that don't know, let me state some things. Sorcerers are weak and need a lot of experience to be effective. They're super squishy, and are only able to cast good spells as long as they have temporary willpower.

    Firstly: This book is terribly written. I mean half the needed stuff for a Sorcerer isn't defined any where in the book and they have multiple words for a single concept, like number of successes. It took me multiple read throughs of the casting mechanics to understand them because they are so horribly written. Like holy crap, I'm still not sure if I'm throwing fireballs correctly.
    Secondly: This book is terribly written. I mean this as in, they literally took the Sorcerer's hand book, copy pasted it, spent a Saturday to update some stuff, and published it. There are things in this that don't even exist in the rest of the book. Like the Path of Cursing or Ephemera. Also you can do aggravated damage, but it doesn't say whether Path of Healing actually helps since it's for healing mortals, and mortals don't care about aggravated. Also some of the rolls don't make a lick of darn sense. Alchemy is Intelligence + Alchemy, but Advanced Chemistry is Intelligence + Science. It says you are normally using Attribute + Ability for a roll, yet I can't find an ability named Alchemy, but there is one for science, so do I use the Alchemy Path rating? Then there's Weathercontrol which uses Manipulation + Willpower for it, so you're getting a huge number of successes. So there's a bunch of Paths that ignore the go to rule already. But then there's the Path of Healing which uses Manipulation + Intuition. Intuition was removed in Revised IIRC, and is not even a choice on the sheet anymore. Ignoring why would you use Intuition over Medicine FOR A HEALING BASED POWER, why is this in here?
    Thirdly: Useless Paths are all over the place. Shadowcasting, Alchemy, and Enchanting are all terribly useless. Alchemy and Enchanting are ritual only paths, meaning you can't do them in combat really, which makes sense. However, they require a 3 experience point investment to learn a ritual, what this means is that you can have a single point spent into Enchanting, and can only make a single magical item that has super weak effects. Enchanting and Alchemy are suppose to be the Bread and Butter of a Sorcerer, yet he has to blow 30 experience to even bling himself out with basic stuff. Why would anyone ever touch this? Removing the ritual only part of this would make it so much better, yet it's effectively pointless to take at all since it's so costly.
    Finally: Spells are in general hard to cast. Want to throw a fireball? Enjoy your minimum 3 turns of casting time for that Aspect 3. If you don't get the 5 successes required to do your 1 lethal damage fireball, you need to spend another 3 turns casting. You could hang the spell and use it later but now you're doing 2 willpower per spell, so you can fire 5 spells that do super low damage. Hanging spells also makes any rolls involving social way harder because you're focusing, so you can be a prepared Sorcerer, or a social one. Well you could just spend mana to lower the difficulty, but that also makes the spell require something and you can only hold 5 mana at a time. Assuming you survive the 3 turns, you will actually be able to kill something by blowing a lot of your mana. You could avoid willpower by casting the spell at a lower level, but then you're not doing much of anything at all. Sure it's easier, but your spells aren't as good really compared to a shotgun and cast way slower still. You can also use willpower to cast faster but that raises the difficulty so you need more successes since you need 1 per aspect you want in your spell effectively doing nothing.

    So what's the point of being a Sorcerer if they're literally a 10 shot shotgun (assuming they maxed willpower). My question is how do you make a viable Sorcerer that can actually be useful, since a pistol seems better in all sense, and what houserules are basically required to make them actually some what good, because gods know they need it. I hope Sorcerer does get a new book after M20 because it's a lot of neat stuff like for psychics which are top tier amazing, but Sorcerers are way worse do to their super long cast times, and willpower limits. I'm playing a Sorcerer in an upcoming game so if anyone has any advice, please feel free to speak up.

      For houserules, I remove the '1 turn per dot' rule, and make it 1 turn as a base for related paths. Extended casting remains the same; 1 turn for 1 roll, 2 turns for 2, 4 for 3, etc etc. I then make it so the rule to remove the WP cost for +2 difficulty works even if you don't have +2 dots in the Path. Also, remember that rituals for Enchantment/Alchemy are the same as anything else; 1 * rating. 3 is the freebie cost at creation for extra ones, and you always start out with one for free per path dot.

          There’s little chance of an M20 Sorcerer any time soon, unfortunately; but once M20 comes out, I intend to tackle this issue. A preliminary thought I’ve had is that M20’s new Focus rules assign common Abilities to the Style as well as common tools: for M20 Sorcerer, the Attribute + Ability pool for a given Path should be dependent on the sorcerer’s Style. As an extreme example, a sorcerer who’s Style involves bargaining with supernatural forces might use a social attribute such as Charisma rather than Intelligence when forming his dice pool, and/or an Ability such as Etiquette or Performance instead of Occult.

          In terms of house rules: consider dropping most of the Willpower costs; the casting times are more than adequate to prevent the sorcerer from spamming their rituals; and consider allowing the use of mana in place of Willpower in those cases that still have a cost. Consider removing the effects of Aspects from the Difficulty and Time determination; the fact that you must allocate successes already implies increased chance of failure and a longer casting time due to the need to accumulate more successes.

          My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                      I actually rather liked Sorcerer. Sure, it had some editing/concept issues, but nothing that couldn't be easily tweaked on the fly, as needed (expand on Mana, using it in tandem and/or instead of Willpower, etc.). Also, the game itself suggests adjusting the rules to fit your game/setting, as appropriate (having spells be cast in a turn, rather than a turn-per-level; having Sorcerers start with a comparable point spread to other splats; use certain merits to give them perks that compensate for their less potent powersets; etc...). Sorcerers, as they exist in the WoD, aren't *meant* to be as powerful as Mages or other "hard-cover" splats, but it literally only takes the slightest adjustment to make them on par by just stepping up the potency of the listed powers by one step, as it is a general rule-of-thumb that their path/hedge magic/powers cusp approximately a dot behind the major splats' powers, more or less. Easy peasy.

                      Now, if you want an edited-for-shit WoD book, talk to me a decade ago when Demon first game out, and I was one of the poor souls who got the first print of *that* utter garbage (even when I got my exchanged, "better edited" copy, I was still like "Dude -- this is *still* giving me headaches..."). *That* was a book to be bitched about! Sorcerer was a positive gem by comparison...

                                                      White Wolf never failed, either. The merger with CCP was done by mutual consent, not as a takeover (hostile or otherwise); and it wasn’t instigated by any sort of fear that White Wolf as a business was in any sort of trouble. Rather, it was done as part of a move to help White Wolf get off of the “supplement treadmill”. The closest thing there’s been to a failure since then is that CCP has a less-than-stellar record managing the White Wolf IP. Which has nothing to do with the quality of an editing job that took place years before CCP ever entered the picture.

                                                      OPP is White Wolf, in all but name. The company didn’t go bankrupt, and it didn’t fail; it transformed itself.
