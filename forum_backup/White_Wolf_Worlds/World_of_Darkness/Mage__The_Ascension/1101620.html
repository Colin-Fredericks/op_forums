    M20: Worth it for my Game?

    06-08-2017, 09:51 AM
    It looks like my group's current game is ending, and I find myself with an opportunity to maybe, possibly, finally introduce them to Mage. Currently I have a few copies of Mage Revised, and I'm not sure if I should invest in M20, and ask my players to do the same.

    A few things about the game I'm planning to run:
    • I'm dumping about 90% of the fluff around the Traditions, Crafts, and the Technocracy. I'm replacing it with my own Mage groups, some of which draw vague inspiration from canon.
    • I'm drastically changing the overall metaplot, for example the Ascension War simply never happened. The Council exists to exercise power over the supernatural world as a whole.
    • I'm 100% sold on doing the above.
    With that in mind, does M20 have anything to offer me that Mage Revised does not? I would be most interested in cleaner or clearer explanations for magick and useful NPCs, but general opinions are welcome.

    And If M20 happens to be controversial on the whole on this particular forum, I humbly ask that this thread not become a venue for that controversy. This is my first time engaging with the online Mage community in about a decade; please be gentle. Thanks!

      M20 has a lot of useful stuff about Paradigm, and many side-rules / alternate rules that are very interesting. It gives a lot of posibilities.
      For instance, the rules of Reality Zones (areas where what's Coincident and what's Vulgar shift according to a local Paradigm), the rules for breaking stuff. It also gives expanded rules for Rituals and new rules for Paradigm that are both very good and clear.
      M20 rules are much more benevolent than Revised in regards at what a mage can accomplish on a single (or a few) turns, but less benevolent with Rituals. Which are two gains as far as I'm concerned, but that's a personal preference.

      Revised, however, explains MUCH better how to work magick with the Spheres, and what the limmits should be.
      If you're interested in "cleaner or clearer explanations for magick" you won't find them in M20.

          One of the strengths of Mage 20 is that you get a pretty nice comparison of the way Revised rules work against previous editions, and ways to incorporate/discard metaplot as suits your purposes. The latter may be less useful to you since you're revamping your setting. On the other hand, it helps break up the metaplot elements into individual components, which makes it easier to determine which parts, if any, you might want to keep. There isn't anything by way of NPCs though, sorry.

          I would recommend it for new Focus rules alone. I found it very helpful in terms of helping me and my players around the conceptual use of magic, beyond just "I'm using Forces 3, Matter 2". Paradigms and practices are much more clearly defined, and I really like the changes in the instrument rules (basically having a number of general or personal instruments rather than one specific tool/focus for one specific sphere.)

          There are also a lot of gameplay charts: magic, combat, et cetera and so forth, which can be really helpful for quick reference. (Especially if you're using the pdf, since there is a very thorough table of contents and internal hyperlinks.)

          Aleph's right about the sphere write ups though. They're very short in M20. (Probably because of the How Do You Do That? supplement.) If you're specifically looking for comprehensive sphere write-ups, I'd suggest looking at the Sorcerer's Crusade core rule book. I've always thought it had the clearest and most comprehensive write ups. (And some very nice charts to boot. I'm a big fan of charts. :-))

              Oddly, I actually like the M20 approach to explaining the Spheres more. Both M20 and the previous editions do the same basic thing: a paragraph that explains the essential idea of what each dot of each Sphere does. In previous editions this was followed by a bunch of sample rotes. The sample rotes can be illustrative, but use up a lot of space without a lot of explanation of how things work. M20 shoved all those sample effects into a simple to reference set of charts to cover common effects that works just as well if not better than the sample rotes do. How Do You Do That? is a bit of a contentious subject around here, but a lot of that is disagreeing with the answers being good ones more than if the book does the job of really digging into the idea of how to work the Spheres.

              I think the biggest issue against M20 for the intended game is that M20 is huge and the vast majority of the book is going to be tossed out. As with the rest of the X20 core books, M20 has a large and comprehensive discussion of factions both big and small within the official groups, lots of discussions of the setting and metaplot, and so on. It's a big book, and an expensive book, that you're probably going to end up ignoring around half of the content. But it's hard to say if really nice things M20 does (something that wasn't brought up is that M20 actually acknowledges and takes a position on the HAP vs HOP/RBD vs. PBD debates), are worth it anyway. There is a M20 Quickstart that might be worth looking at first as a much smaller investment into seeing what M20 is like before grabbing the tome of smiting.

                              I think you could make one copy of M20 + a bunch of copies of Revised could work if include a pages of charts from M20 for the players.

                              The amount of moving parts didn't change significantly. Multiple actions are like the rest of the main X20 games. The Ability list change slightly but people would have their character sheets for that. They added Vidare, which isn't really a moving part, it just adds a bit to paradigm as it relates to the Umbra. There's a few changes to some of the Backgrounds, but you're probably not going to feel that from just Revised Core during play (some of the bigger changes are going to be during char-gen and XP spending). There's a few small differences in specific effects and what dot rating they require in Spheres (but that's all in the summary chart). Paradox is probably the biggest change that would mess up players a bit if they're looking at Revised's core, since the default M20 version is a mix of 2e and Rev. on that (of course you should be using Malcolm Shepard's clarification/errata for that anyway which never got printed). There's a lot of combat maneuvers, and equipment, that aren't in Rev. core, but again printing out a bunch of charts. Combat also has a major change in it, though it's actually a reduction of moving parts: M20 dropped the declaration phase in combat.

                                      I would personally recommend M20 for the Focus section alone. Unlike previous editions, it actually explains how mages can see the world, what their general methods are for tinkering with it, and what tools they use to do that (Paradigm, Practice, and Instruments, respectively). It's all modular and thematic, and quite extensive. It even goes into how the same Paradigms and Practices can differ wildly, depending on which cultural and esoteric tradition one subscribes to.

                                      If you're copying sections of the text and printing them out, considering making this part available to everyone. It'll make explaining what magick is about more streamlined, while letting your group pick and choose elements that appeal to them.
