    46) A certain chantry - a large manor house in the deep south - is run by a kindly older woman (though she genuinely looks good for her age). A mother of four, she opened up her home to her fellow Tradition mages when the last of them moved out. The mothering instinct seems not to have left, however, as she cares for those who frequent the chantry like her own. And she's such a sweet lady, wanting nothing but to make sure everyone - especially the young and inexperienced - is happy, healthy, and prepared for the future. She bakes them cookies. She mends their clothes. She heals their wounds. She teaches them how to fire a gun. She shows them where to strike a man such that it will bring him down in one blow. If their stores of ammunition are low, she'll offer a clip or two from her own stash.

    This woman has a name, but many of the chantry's residents affectionately call her "Mother Death". A Euthanatoi Master, Mother Death was once a feared individual in the criminal underworld - an assassin spoken of in whispers, for those who spoke of her too loudly had a habit of biting the dust. They fear that figure - the golden-haired woman with glasses that masked her eyes with glare, holding a gun in one hand and a blade in the other - even now. She was said to be a cackling maniac, or else a silent, professional knife in the back. In truth, the rumors and conflicting stories were part of her strategy. The only consistent fact is that she was implacable against a foe deserving of the Good Death; a killer whose blade thirsted for blood.

    Mother Death has, obviously, mellowed out since her retirement. Having a kid - and then having three more - woke up compassion she thought stillborn in her young heart. She is amused by her nickname, and is willing to tell tales of her exploits...if the other person is old enough to hear it. She really does care about her growing family of misfits and weirdos, and wants what's best for them. Sometimes that means not telling a story of her infamous past, if she thinks her audience is too young to be hearing of such things. Sometimes it means teaching them how to fight and kill, for the Ascension War is long, and the Traditions' enemies legion. Mother Death is also unbending and orderly - if she sets a rule for the house, it is written in stone. Woe be unto a mouthy Hollow One or Virtual Adept who gains her ire. Usually, they appear the next day, suddenly VERY polite to Mother Death. They are too frightened or traumatized to do otherwise. It's for their own good, really.

    She may be getting on in years, but she must not be underestimated. Give Mother Death cause to oppose you, and she'll leverage her significant magical and mundane power in kind. She despises child abuse, rape, and human trafficking more than anything; she often sends chantry residents on missions against such criminals, if she doesn't start dropping curses herself. The Children's Crusade is working to get the old girl into their fold, and Mother Death is warm to the idea.

    And if someone threatens a member of her "family", well...Mother Death will have to march out to fight, herself. Naughty children need to be punished. Mother knows best.

        47) Once upon a time, in an age where monsters still stalked the land and the Scourge was but a figure in the distance, an accomplished hunter grew melancholy. He'd stalked every prey worthy of hunting, and brought them down. It came to be that the hunter gained no pleasure from the sport of a mundane hunt. He needed a greater challenge. And so, against the advice of his peers, he set out to hunt the most elusive of quarry: the Unicorn.

        It took years of searching, but he picked up the trail of one of these noble, savage beasts. It took years more in hunting it, for it was swift of sprint, cunning as any man, and deadly in a fight. The hunter acquired more than a few scars in his attempts, and lost all friends, a result of his monomania. But eventually, near the twilight of his years, he bested the beast, driving it over a high cliff and following it down. Driving a spear through its side, the hunter slew the unicorn. Parched and giddy from his hard-won victory, he removed the unicorn's horn as a trophy, then drank its blood.

        The lifeblood of a unicorn, however, is a strong tonic. Its holy properties restored the hunter's youth, but also bleached his curly, graying hair stark white. Moreover, however, it rose to his brain and struck his mind with a horrific clarity. In that moment, he Awakened, realizing how his obsession had not only squandered his life, but deprived the Earth of one of God's pure, majestic living wonders. There was great wailing and gnashing of teeth, as guilt and sorrow filled the hunter's heart. He had killed that which ought to be immortal. Weeping, the hunter buried the unicorn's blood-splattered body deep in the ground, then took his ill-gotten trophy into self-imposed exile. He retired to hermitage far from civilization, taking after the holy mystics of his time. Seeking redemption through contemplation, to quiet his tumultuous heart and make peace with his expanded consciousness.

        Because of the unicorn's blood, the hunter became ageless. He remained in retreat for centuries, dimly aware of the advance of technology, and ambivalent towards the hardening of reality.

        That was, until the cave system that served as his latest retreat became host to criminal enterprise. Their trade: human chattel, specifically children. Fury filled him, at the sight of casual traffic of young lives, and he put his mystic power and his skills as a hunter to good use. He led the poor children from the caves, his cotton robes stained scarlet. But while he was no longer at peace, he felt a refreshment he had not in centuries. For indeed, there was a satisfaction in saving the lives of pure creatures - blessed in the eyes of God - rather than taking them. Shortly after leading the little ones from bondage, he came into contact with Tradition mages, who labored in the name of the Children's Crusade.

        The unaging hunter has adapted a bit to modern day. He wears a leather jacket, and carries a prepaid phone, when his allies need to communicate with him. Horses stand out gravely in this age, so he's taken up a new steed that needs no food, water, or sleep: a motorcycle. But his old skills still serve him well. Skill with spear and bow. Knowledge of tracking and the wilderness. The holy magic honed from years of quiet prayer and contemplation, that allows him to reject earthly forces through concentrated austerities. The unicorn's blood that flows through his veins, and a wand crafted from the unicorn's horn.

        His prey may have changed, but his identity has not. He is a hunter, this time of those who prey upon the weak and innocent.

                        53) The product of a long-term Technocracy project, to train a generation of soldiers using virtual reality. Almost her entire life was spent in simulated reality, until she came of age, topping the rankings in virtual missions. One day, however, she found glitches in the system, and managed to hack into the mainframe controlling it. She discovered that her whole life, and that of her peers, was a fabrication. She Awakened that day from the shock, and spent weeks learning the code that governed, not just her prison digital prison, but the Construct it was built in. Finally, she staged a breakout, shunting her fellow VR babies back into reality. She convinced her brothers and sisters that they'd been abducted, and that they needed to fight their way out. And so they did, the Technocrats put in disarray by Construct-wide system failures, and automated defenses that seemed to turn on their masters. It would only be later, as they combed through bullet-blasted mainframe computers, that they realized what happened. By then, she had led her people out, into the deserts of the middle east (where their Construct had been built, far from prying eyes).

                        But she didn't stay with her brothers and sisters long. She was wounded in battle; the physical damage was minimal, but it rocked her to her core. Up until then, she'd never really feared death. Being gunned down in the simulation merely meant failure. Now, death was permanent. For the first time, she felt truly, viscerally afraid. She had to get away. Disappear. Escape the conflicts that threatened to kill her. In her weakness and terror, she abandoned her brothers and sisters, leaving an army behind without commanders, or a war of their own.

                        She made her way to Europe, her explorations of the Internet bringing her into contact with those who shared some of her skill set: the Virtual Adepts. Their outlook on Reality also matched her own. Ever since she escaped the simulation, she could never be sure she wasn't in another, larger one. Hell, she couldn't be sure she wasn't in an elaborate version of the same simulation, though this fear at least she tamps down, for her own sanity. She's armed herself with trinary computers and hypertech equipment, along with as many guns and other weapons as she can. Old habits die hard, and she's been forced to learn how to stockpile munitions, rather than taking what the simulation gave her.

                        The last thing she wants is to fight, though. She's terrified of her own mortality. However, she's also wracked with guilt. In her adjustment period, she's built safe spaces for herself - places where she can hold out if the Technocracy ever comes. But she also kept track of her fellow VR babies, as best she could. They've become a company of mercenaries and bandits, infamous in the third world countries, not just in the Middle East, but around the globe. A small army of demented former child soldiers, who never learned right from wrong, and who think violence is a game. They've stolen military hardware and cargo planes, to better keep their operation running and moving around.

                        She caused this. She unleashed a hoard of killers with skewed priorities on the world. Sooner or later, she's going to have to swallow her fear, and make it right. Before they commit inhuman atrocities. Before they attract the Technocracy's attention, and either get wiped out, or re-absorbed into the Union.

                            54) Magic is not normal. Not natural. It defies sane, rational modernity, and puts the common person in danger. For this reason, he became a witch-hunter. Their magic was anathema, so he learned the old ways to counter them; his pockets were always filled with iron nails and protective amulets, and he learned dozens of warding signs. All to stymie their occult powers, and put an end to them. For the longest time, he thought that Witches, Wizards, Hoodoo Men, and other assorted magical humans were members of some bizarre, empowered bloodline. He'd hunted more than a few Verbana especially, for whom passing down magical knowledge (and therefore power) through subsequent generations was as natural as breathing. Ergo, it wasn't surprising he would think that.

                            And he did, up until he Awakened himself during a pivotal moment during a chase. His quarry, sensing the hunter's sudden inability to handle their knew senses, informed them of the truth. That True Magick had nothing to do with heredity, and everything to do with knowledge and perception. Now the gift was passed on to him, and there was no going back.

                            At first the witch-hunter retreated, sitting in denial of his new state. As the reality of the situation sank in - that he could see what man was not meant to see, and reshape it to his whims - he was horrified. He'd become everything he hated. Turned into the very thing he'd hunted, fought, and killed. He couldn't stop fighting the abuses of mage-kind, but it rocked him to his very core. His once iron resolution - his assurance of the morality of his actions - now stands as a poor, unstable firmament.

                            He's at a transitional phase of his life. Where once, the witch-hunter studied the craft of his enemies in order to find defenses against them, to look upon the old witchcrafts is to be shown weapons he could employ in full. This disturbs him - how to examination of witch-tools and the learning of witch-words gives him...ideas. At the moment, he employs his magick only to sense the presence of the supernatural, or to guard his mind against their manipulation, or as the basis for Countermagick. Still...the Old Ways tempt him. His crusade is hard, and his life so vulnerable. It will soon come to pass that the witch-hunter will need to call upon greater, less elementary spells. Effects to push away Forces, give him a bit of good luck, read surface thoughts, strike back against the ephemeral, or make his possessions deal and soak aggravated damage. With each reasonable compromise - with each adoption of the abhorrent Art - he becomes inured to the prospect of wielding it. It won't be long, now, before he becomes just another witch.

                            So, too, does he find himself compromising with his old enemies. He can no longer dismiss the Mage as something inhuman - he IS one. That means coming to understand them more and more. That means beginning to ask which witch is worthy of death, and which witch is best left alone. He knows, though does not wish to think it, that he will need to interact with his new peers in a fashion that is not antagonism. Will have to talk to them - even make allies. They would know, more than anyone, which of their number is most dangerous. They would also be the ones to go to, to sate his growing curiosity. He possesses books and items plundered from past targets - past victims - that he cannot entirely understand. They would act as invaluable learning aides and bargaining chips, so long as he could consult with experts in the field.

                            Of course this presents obvious danger. He came into these items by murder of their kin. He must be cautious, lest his identity as a witch-hunter be revealed. Not all of his materials, either, were intended as tools. His most grisly Instrument is the remains of a witch. He'd started collecting them before his Awakening - less a trophy for his own gratification, and more out of half-baked theory that a witch's remains could hold some of their blood-born power. That they could be incorporated into countermagic somehow. He knows now that magick is a matter of knowledge and perspective, not an inborn trait, but it's a habit he never outgrew. With his burgeoning Paradigm, he's still inclined to assign weight to those remains - that the bones, hair, blood, or eyes of a witch could still, after a fashion, carry a hint of their power. It is, nonetheless, a huge risk, to continue using them. If other witches ever found out about him...

                            Lastly, of course, there's the matter of his fellow witch-hunters. He'd never been too religious, so his ties to groups like the Society of Leopold are tenuous. But he's made connections throughout the country, with other hunters. Partnered up with them time and again. They, too, would look at him with horror and disgust, this time for having become everything they fought against. They'd see him as a traitor, or as someone tainted by evil power. How long will it be, before they start to come for him?

                                    58) Born with mental disability, the boy was perceptive enough to realize that his lack of intelligence was burdensome on those around him. His parents suffered long because of him, no matter how much they tried to hide it or tell him he was their "special boy". From the taunts of his classmates, he came to understand, vaguely, what "special" meant. It frustrated him, to grasp in way he couldn't put into words just how much less he was. Wanting to be less of a burden on his family and special education aids - and to get the damn bullies off his back - he tried to learn. To make himself smarter, as best he could. He would even pray to heaven, asking if he could be blessed to "not be so dumb". His attempts availed to little, though, and in time he began to question if God was really listening.

                                    Perhaps He had been, for one day, as the boy sat in his secret place in the woods, staring in anger at his inability to understand the library books he'd obtained, a mysterious figure came upon him. The figure was ensconced in a thick overcoat and hat, despite the summer heat, and had a strange voice that the boy could never describe. They asked the boy what tormented him so, and the boy cried, "No use! No use! I'm a stupid, stupid dummy! It's all I'll ever be!". Seeing the cause of the boy's distress, the figure removed a strange fruit from the folds of their coat, and handed it to him.

                                    It was like no fruit the boy had ever seen, and would later learn did not exist in known record. When the boy asked what the fruit was, the figure replied. "Eat it, and your wish will be granted. Eat it, and your brain will shine like a star. You will be...smart. You will know." Hesitant, the boy's desire won out over his fear, and he bite in. As he greedily consumed the bitter-sweet flesh, his mind caught fire, and he sank to the ground in pain, clutching his head. By the time he rose to his feet, the figure was gone, leaving only the sticky core of the fruit behind. In that moment, the boy's mind raced, filled to bursting with fuller cognitive prowess. Like a ton of bricks, however, he also came to a conclusion about what he'd done. It filled him with guilt.

                                    From then on, he was a different, opposite child to the one he'd been. Where once he was the imbecile, now he was a boy genius. He devoured books by the stack, focusing on chemistry, biology...and, oddly, theology. For at the moment of Awakening, he came to his own conclusion about what had happened: he'd repeated the error of Adam and Eve. The fruit had been, obviously, from the Tree of Knowledge of Good and Evil; from which the first man and woman were forbid taking and eating. As they had been simple and innocent, until they ate the fruit and gained knowledge of their wrongdoing, so had the boy consumed the fruit and ceased to be simple. For all the good it did - he was brilliant now, ahead of his class soon by multiple grade levels - it denied him the innocence of youth. He, who would have retained that innocence all his life by virtue of disability, now saw the world - and himself - as the truly were. He had sinned, and now had to live in a sinful world. Perhaps the Lord had blessed him, in fact, by having him born simple.

                                    While the boy genius - now thirteen, and working his way up to college level work - is privately disturbed by the emotional and spiritual implications of his actions, he soldiers on anyway. There is no un-eating the forbidden fruit, no putting the ills of the world back into Pandora's Box. He could only move forward, and make best use of his intelligence. He is rapidly developing his specialty: Biblical Biology. Studying botany, he seeks to cultivate and study the Forbidden Fruit, using the seeds found in the core of the one he was given. He hopes that with study of plants and animals from a theological perspective, he can grasp the intricacies of God's creation.

                                    However, he's been backed into a bit of a corner. His stunning shift from dullard to prodigy has not gone unnoticed. Two organizations will shortly attempt to recruit him: the Progenitors, and the Celestial Chorus, with its Alexandrian Society. The latter would be the most excellent fit, marrying science and faith in a way most gratifying to him. But the former would stop at nothing to wipe any thoughts of Eden from the boy's mind, while also plundering the seeds of the Forbidden Fruit for their own research. If the boy becomes aware of the Progenitors, he will realize this part of their plan in short order. Such a blasphemous plot, of course, must not be allowed to continue.

                                        A few months have passed and the city's power structure - both Nightfolk and mundane, including corrupt politicians, cops and gangs - is in shambles as the Widderslainte of the Destruction Essence rose to Arete 3 rapidly and started - with Vulgar explosions, Coincidental accidents involving electricity, and spectacularly guided and arranged collapses of sections of buildings - threatening, disabling, ruining and ultimately killing everyone the Etherites could identify and confirm as part of the plot and attacks on them; or anyone that the three Etherites thought should be neutralized or removed - like those running or trying to run rackets. Some of former wrongdoers have been forced by the Cabal, or skillfully manipulated, into paying them a tribute of fairly earned money.
                                        The Widderslainte's family lives far away from the city, and she never had a connection to anyone else in her life - until she met the Etherites. So far there have been only a few tenuous leads as to the possible fate of the lost Acolytes - some point that they are held hostage and forced to design technology and make money for those threatening them..yet at the same time it could be that they have fled to relative, tenuous safety and are holed up somewhere.
                                        So far there has been no Tradition or Technocracy involvement in the situation, since last eight months - at least not in any way the Cabal can discern. The Etherites never in their lives had any contact or dealings with the Technocracy or the Nephandi.

The Cabal is looking to confirm this; at the same time they are continuing their campaign of punishment against any who threaten them, or hurt others.

                                            60) Sometime in the waning years of the 20th century, a fifteen year old boy living in suburbia was dealing with bullies...or something. The exact circumstances are hazy to him in retrospect, given the far more unusual event that happened. While hiding in an abandoned forest house, he slipped between the cracks of time and wound up in the Dark Ages for a while. He met a princess, got help from a wizard, braved a dangerous swamp, retrieved a missing item of power, learned to wield a sword, used it to slay a dragon, and saved a kingdom.

                                            It was a whole thing.

                                            Once his coming of age story was concluded, the wizard was kind enough to use his magic to send the boy back to his own time. He barely noticed how the wizard seemed gravely disturbed by the boy's tales of the future - a bleak, technological urban paradise for artifice and economics - and returned to his world. He would never forget the adventure, and used his newfound confidence to settle whatever trifling problem he'd left hanging.

                                            He then proceeded to spend the next fifteen years pretending none of it ever happened.

                                            What was he supposed to do, really? Magical adventures are great and all, and he was thankful it happened. But obviously, he could never speak of it to anyone, or betray his experiences. They would all have thought he was crazy. Instead, he settled into the mundane life of a modern person living in the developed world. He went to college, dated a few girls his own age, and got a job in the business world. His one concession to that past was to privately maintain his skill with a blade - he always feared he might be snatched back in time, and so kept prepared. Further adventures in time never happened, though, and so turned to the business of business. He would almost have thought the insane hero's journey a figment of his boyhood imagination, had he not retained very tangible souvenirs from it. A couple distinctive scars, a tiny hat snatched from the head of a squat goblinoid creature (with a foul mouth and even fouler breath), and the ring given to him from the young princess. The ring, especially, was important; a royal family heirloom, enchanted to allow the wearer to speak and understand all languages. Indeed, it was precisely for that magic that he was given the ring, as he couldn't understand or be understood by the natives of the time. (In retrospect, he figured out he'd found himself somewhere in medieval France, and took the time to learn French in the intervening years). The ring rarely left his hand, and proved a valuable asset in the world of business.

                                            As a matter of fact, he was among a number of individuals - strong-willed, smooth, unflappable, and professional - who were being scouted for recruitment by the Syndicate. That is, until his magical past came back to haunt him. When the man visited a prominent New York hotel, he was surprised to receive a package being held by the hotel staff. They'd been holding the large box for over a decade, but the box itself was obviously of a centuries-old style. Someone had sent it in, and asked that it be held until the man got there to pick it up. In the back of his mind, the man vaguely recalled mentioning New York to the wizard, all those years ago, and name-dropping the hotel, as he'd stayed there as a child. Up in his hotel room, he opened the large chest, finding items inside that were magically proofed against the ravages of time. One was a letter from the wizard, apologizing for not being able to get the package to him in a direct manner, and that he hoped his apprentice's apprentice managed to make the delivery correctly. Another was a tunic the boy had worn in his stay, tatters and tears mended as best could be mustered, and with ruddy stains still visible. Still others were a bound leather tome - a spell book - and a carved wooden wand. Last, but not least, was the boy's sword, wrapped in silk. The sword taken from a fallen knight, and that had been bathed in dragon's blood. The man's eyes filled with tears, as the old memories flooded back in a torrent.

                                            Then the man actually picked up his sword. Holding it aloft, he was transported mentally back to his youth, feeling all those feelings again. Quite unexpectedly - though perhaps not to that old wizard - the man Awakened on the spot.

                                            Days later, his bank account was empty, and notice of his immediate quitting was delivered to his boss's office. He dropped off the map, on a quest to find the old wizard's kin: the Order of Hermes.

                                                61) He had been just a normal Virtual Adept - brilliant, cocky, and largely sedentary. That is, until he fell into a dangerous situation, and his computer equipment was rendered inoperable. He realized that day that a strong mind and powerful tools could only be leaned on so far. Until such time as Reality 2.0 truly came to be, he couldn't escape meatspace forever, and meatspace was a harsh place. But it was, nonetheless, a system, with its own rules. And systems can be hacked. They can be improved. And the rules that govern meat reward effort.

                                                Taking to the gym, he pushed his soft body to the limit. Gritting his teeth through the pain, he worked to overcome that weakness that nearly got him killed. At his computer, he learned to type one-handed - with either hand - in order to pump iron with the other. And on that computer, he created models for his physical body, and ran simulations. Scanned his fleshy data, in order to create targeted methods of self-improvement. He studied martial artists, capturing their moves on video and analyzing them, in order to understand the mechanics behind physical action...so that he might maximize its effects. He even goes without calculators wherever he can - his mind, too, must be strengthened - so he crunches numbers in his head while he does actual crunches.

                                                The power of technology is great, but he won't use it as a crutch. Since Reality 2.0 seems little closer now than it was twenty years ago - a true New Reality, unmoored from the physical - he will have to make his body as perfect a vessel for his mind. His efforts are paying off - he is quite a physical specimen, and not just by VA standards - but he will not be satisfied yet. To stop improving is to stagnate, like a computer system that receives no upgrades. And if he can drag other members of his Tradition out of their dark dens and get them moving, all the better. Even if they moan and complain now, the pain they experience now will make them stronger. In the end, they'll thank him.

                                                    62) To his eldest son, the merchant used what wealth he could to send him to university, that he may triumph over apocalypse with reason and natural philosophy.

                                                    63) To his middle son, whom the merchant treasured, the merchant sent to join the priesthood. If God brought down Judgement Day, it was only sensible to curry favor in the Kingdom of Heaven by being one of the Lord's faithful.

That the witch the boy was apprenticed to was not an Infernalist, but rather a mere Verbana practicing the Old Ways, did not occur to the man. Not that he cared overmuch either way.
