    Sphere Oddities in M20

    05-12-2023, 09:14 AM
    I'm going through the M20 example charts and Sphere writeups to see where they call for Conjunctional Effects and I keep finding oddities that sound good but don't actually make sense when you dig into them. A lot of them actually. I give HDYDT a lot of crap for being all over the place rules-wise, but M20 itself is a mess of contradictions, arbitrary extra spheres, rules that were assumed but never actually stated, and examples that make less than no sense.

    Here's some of what I've found:

    Correspondence

    There's no real examples of Conjunctional Effects for Correspondence 1, but for Rank 2 we start off with Clairvoyance. This is listed in the Common Magical Effects chart. It's a simple Correspondence 2 spell... except for Psychics for whom it is Corr 2/Mind 3. Mind is not doing anything in this spell except raising difficulty (because the highest sphere is now rank 3 instead of 2) and providing an extra method of countermagic. Not only that, but it means psychics have to have Correspondence 3 to cast the spell because a Mage's rank in Correspondence caps the max level of another sphere that he can bring to bear in a Correspondence spell. The spell remains Correspondence 2, but he can't cast it unless he has Correspondence 3.

    Next up, the Common Magical Effects Chart lists Corr 2/Prime 2/Other Sphere 2+ for Warding or Banning. Meanwhile the Correspondence writeup calls for Corr 4/Other Sphere 2+. Correspondence 4 is clearly the level for Wards and Bans, so I think that's the correct one. I have no idea what Prime would even do in this context.

    Then there's the Summon Small Objects or Creatures at Corr 2/Matter 2 and Corr 2/Life 2. This appears to be an example of Spheres being wrongly added for targeting purposes when that's not a rule. Correspondence alone is explicitly enough to teleport small objects.

    Next up we have levitation/flight. Despite the sheer nonsense that is HDYDT's levitation section the Common Effects Chart is quite simple. Correspondence 3 (or Forces 2) to levitate things, Life or Matter as locking spheres. The only real problem here is that nowhere in the Correspondence writeup does it say that the Sphere is capable of this. The idea of Correspondence based flight has been in Mage for a decades, though, so I'm guessing this was an oversight.

    However, then the Sphere writeup says: "Also, by combining Correspondence 3 with Forces, Life, or Matter, the mage can move things around from a distance, levitating, manipulating or teleporting them without physical contact." That's cool, but levitation at a distance, manipulation at a distance, and teleportation at a distance are several very different effects. The recommendation works for remote levitation and remote manipulation since the first is the same spell as above (with a range augment that's already covered) and the second is a Forces, Life, and/or Matter spell with a range augment. On the other hand remote teleportation and just physically picking up and moving objects from a distance are merely Correspondence 2 on its own, no other spheres required.

    Create Multiple Images is listed as a mere Correspondence 3 effect, which makes sense, but Create Multiple Objects is listed as Corr 5/Matter 3/Prime 2. I can only think this was supposed to be Correspondence 5 OR Matter 3/Prime 2.

    Conjure Earthly Being is stated to be Corr 4/Life 2. The only possible reason for Life 2 is for targeting, which is unnecessary.

    Colocation is an interesting one. Correspondence 4 is enough to accomplish it, but you need Mind 1 to function effectively and Life 2 to be able to act independently at each location. This is an odd one because Life is either completely unnecessary or it is too low. Either we automatically treat every body like it is different and Mind is enough, or we need some special augmentation to make every body functionally different and Life 2 is too low for that.

    Just to muddy the waters a little more, Colocate Senses is described as Correspondence 5 / Life 3. But Colocate senses is already available at Correspondence 3 and doesn't need Life 3.

    I'll end on a piece of sanity. The Correspondence 5 writeup says that Corr 5 can be combined with the appropriate sphere at 2+ to stretch or compress objects, bodies, or forces. If we are charitable and assume it is a spacial distortion locked to a pattern, that would mostly track.


    That's 3 examples of arbitrary sphere additions (one practice based), 2-3 examples of unnecessary targeting spheres, and 3 spheres included at the wrong rank. Only 6 of the 15 examples I looked at made sense completely.

                  This is more direct and goes beyond Correspondence. In quite a few places spells require Life when cast on living creatures, I've found a few examples in M20 so far. The Awakening Substances sidebar, for instance, shows that awakening a drug is just Spirit, but awakening a disease inside someone is Spirit and Life. Prime implies that consecrating inanimate things is just Prime while consecrating living things is Prime and Life.

                  Here's the next round of issues I found:


                  Entropy

                  Entropy, oddly enough, is fairly light. All three examples are in the Common Effects Chart.

                  Bless/Curse can be done with Entropy 3/Life 3 (among other things). If this is a locked effect it should be Life 2, but its not even clear this needs a locking sphere.

                  The Entropic version of Harm Ghost is an odd duck at Entropy 3/Prime 2. While Entropy is the sphere for tapping into the low umbra no rank is ever given for when it can do this. Normally, though, Entropy can't be used to attack until Rank 4. Meanwhile, Prime 2 can use Quintessence to hurt Spirits already and it doesn't need Spirit to do so, so there's no particular reason it would need Entropy to harm ghosts. Especially as there's a Spirit 3 version of Harm Ghost.

                  Finally, I initially thought Disintigrate Object should be either Entropy 3 or Time 3, but this is a case where the combo makes sense. Entropy 3 only speeds the decay, Time 3 only ages the target. The two together should allow disintigration with fewer successes than Time 3 alone.

                  Final tally: 3 effects, 1 arbitrary extra sphere, 1 unneeded targeting sphere.



                  Forces

                  There aren't many I find objectionable here. Invisibility and Levitation are simple Forces Manipulation Effects locked to a target (Forces 2/Locking Sphere 2) and making things attract or repel can be accomplished the same way (though if you actually wanted to graft the forces to a target that would be Forces 3/Life 3).

                  The classic Transforming another pattern into Forces is here, as is using Forces 3+/Prime 2 to manifest energy out of the ether. Transforming Thoughts or Spirit to Energy is are here as Forces 3 and either Mind or Spirit 3. This too works well as Mind and Spirit are actually doing something.

                  It can't last forever, though.

                  Conjure Physical Illusion suggests Forces 2+/Prime 2 and its not really clear what is happening here. Is this a Body of Light backed up by Forces? There's not a lot of information on Body of Light, so maybe? On the other hand Forces 2 is enough to manipulate Light, Sound, and Momentum on its own. It's not that this is wrong, its just that it isn't helpful to understanding spheres.

                  Then there is Erode or Enhance Energies at Forces 3 and some level of Entropy. Forces 2 would probably be enough to reduce or enhance energies on their own. The Forces 3 in a Forces 3/Prime 2 fireball spell weaves the raw quintessence into a Fire pattern, so Forces 3 alone would be enough to convert a Forces pattern back into raw quintessence. You wouldn't need another sphere to just let it disperse though its probably really vulgar if you do. Perhaps Entropy is helping dissipate those raw forces seamlessly, but if so it is only needed for erosion.

                  Finally there's the very strange Set Triggers for Energy Fluctuations. The problem here is that it is not at all clear what Energy Fluctuations are in this context. We can guess, but as an example it is not good at showing us what Forces 3 can do. Triggers at Time 4, at least, makes sense.

                  Final Tally: 12 effects. 0-1 arbitrary sphere additions. 4 confusing effects.

                      This time up, here's what I see in Life and Matter

                      Life... uh... finds a way... to be completely baffling

                      First there's the near nonsensical Sense combinations. Sense Distant Organisms (Life 1/Corr 2) is the only one that makes sense as it is a sense spell at augmented range. Perceive a Creature Forward or Backwards in time is listed as Life 1/Time 2, but that's supposed to be a locked effect (the terminology in Time 2 is very similar to the terminology in the Locking section) so it should be Time 2/Life 2 (I reversed the order because it is easier to put locking spheres at the end). The suggestions of Life 1/Entropy 1 to see potential sickness/misfortune or Life 1/Prime 1 to see binding quintessence streams is completely off. Entropy and Prime can accomplish those tasks on their own.

                      Create Simple Body is listed as Life 2/Prime 2 in the chart while the Life writeup makes it clear that should be Life 3/Prime 2.

                      The Chart also gives a trifecta of spells to harm supernatural creatures. Heal/Harm Fae at Life 3/Mind 3. Heal/Harm Vampires at Life 3/Matter 2. Heal/Harm Werecreature at Life 3/Spirit 2. M20 makes it clear it takes more than Life to harm the major splats so the extra spheres are fine, but I don't understand the logic of the Ranks required. Either Mind is too high or Matter and Spirit are too low.

                      At the very least the spells to create bodies listed in the Life writeup are consistent with the rules. Life 3 or 5/Prime 2 and transform bodies Life 3 or 5/Appropriate Sphere.

                      De-ageing, though, is another case of surplus requirements. The two options are Life 5/Entropy 4 and Life 5/Time 3. Life 5 should be more than enough to de-age anything living. Entropy 4 should be enough as well. Time 3 could, though the target just be rewound so I could see using Life 3 for memory retention.

                      Finally we come to the quagmire that is Reviving the Dead (Life 4/Spirit 4/Prime 3), Giving a Body Consciousness (Life 5/Mind 5), and Binding a Spirit into a body (Life 5/Spirit 5). It's not really clear what each sphere is doing here. Unlike many other cases where I say that, it is clear they are doing [/i]something[/i] it's just not clear what.

                      Suppose we say that this is grafting effect. That involves transmuting two pattern types, so grafting a mind to a body would be Life 5/Mind 5. Except moving a Mind to another body (from the Mind writeup) is Mind 5/Life 4. That's either wrong or this isn't a graft. Meanwhile, Spirit 5 is explicitly enough to instill a soul within an empty shell, no Life needed at all. And don't even get me started on the Prime requirement for Reviving the Dead. Some of these are wrong, but its difficult to figure out which ones.

                      Final Tally: 17 effects examined. 2 arbitrary extra spheres, 3-4 spheres at the wrong level. 2 accidentally merged spells. Three... somethings.


                      After the mess that is Life, Matter has much better structure.

                      Of the 13 effects I looked at, 8 were transforming matter into something or something into matter, and only 4 of those have any issues at all:

                      Converting quintessence to matter at Matter 2/Prime 2 works, but the reverse should either be Matter 2 if you don’t care where the released quintessence goes, or Matter 2/Prime 3 if the Mage is pulling it into his own pool. Matter 2/Prime 2 could effectively channel the released quintessence back into the ether, so this one isn't wrong, it just isn't explaining things well.

                      Converting Matter to Living Tissue is stated as Matter 2+/Life 4+. That should be Matter 2+/Life 3+.

                      Converting a car to a robot is listed as a Matter 4/Forces ?/Prime ?. Forces is a judgement call and depends a great deal on what’s going on inside the car and the robot. Prime is completely unnecessary.

                      If I’m being pedantic, Transforming Matter is listed as Matter 3/Other Sphere 3+ but should actually start at Matter 2 for simple matter. Matter 3+/Other Sphere 3+ is the right spell for anything more complex than a brick.

                      As for the other effects, Matter 2 says you can “move an object through space” with Matter 2/Corr 2+, and “move an object through time” with Matter. We already know from the Correspondence writeup that Matter is completely unnecessary to teleporting or moving an object around in space, there’s no indication it is any more necessary for doing the same in Time. These are purely Correspondence and Time effects.

                      Awakening the Spirit in an Object is listed in the Matter writeup as Matter 2/Spirit 3. Spirit alone should be enough to target a spirit so Matter is unnecessary even as a locking sphere. Most likely this is another example of unnecessary targeting. Indeed we can see in other locations in M20 that awakening the spirit in an object is just Spirit 3.

                      Finally there is the delightful ability to melt steel with a thought as a Matter 5/Mind 3 spell. Matter 3 is enough to alter states of matter and Mind is doing nothing at all.

                      Final tally: 13 effects examined. 1-2 arbitrary Sphere addition. 3 unnecessary targeting spheres. 3 effects with the wrong sphere rank listed. 5 of the 13 effects have no problems

                              Mind the Gap

                              There’s not actually a problem I can find until we get to Mind 3, but then they start coming fast and furiously. The Mind 3 writeup says: “combined with Correspondence, Forces, Matter, or Life, he is able to employ telekinesis, pyrokinetics, and psychophysical assault, influencing objects, elements, or people with the power of his mind. That’s a staggering array of effects and a few might meaningfully use Mind, but most of them can be accomplished with just Correspondence, Forces, Matter, and/or Life:

                              Moving objects around is Correspondence or Forces alone.
                              Controlling fire is Forces alone.
                              If by Psychophysical assault we mean biokinetically attacking someone’s body, that’s Life alone (more on this later).
                              Influencing objects or elements is vague but outside telekinesis most effects would be Matter or Forces alone.
                              Influencing people by psychically controlling their limbs (as opposed to their mind) would be Life alone,
                              Influencing people by psychically controlling their minds would be Mind alone.

                              None of the above (except the last one) actually use Mind as a Sphere. Of all of the suggested effects, only two reasonably use a combo with Mind:

                              A Psychophysical Assault where the mage is specifically going for a psychic attack or illusion that has real world effects (Matrix style “the mind makes it real” type effects or similar) would be a combined Mind/Life effect.

                              Pyrokinetic effects where you create fire could use Forces/Mind to generate the fire out of thoughts rather than Forces/Prime to create the fire out of quintessence.


                              Deep breath…

                              See Avatar is listed as Mind 3/Prime 2/Spirit 1. I don’t know how to complain about this one because Avatars are somewhat vaguely defined anyways. That said, while I can accept that Spirit, Prime, or Mind might have a role in seeing one, there’s no particular reason you’d need all three. My best guess is that this was supposed to be Mind or Spirit, but not both. It’s hard to say because this one comes from the Common Magical Effects chart and has no explanation.

                              Compel Sleep is listed as Mind 4/Life 3. That should really be Mind 4 OR Life 3. There’s nothing wrong with using both spheres, but either alone can do the job.

                              Astral body of light is not well defined and whether the sphere combo of Mind 4+/Spirit 3/Prime 2 works depends on what it is. If it means creating a spiritual body from the ether and then projecting your mind into it, that would be the right sphere combo. That’s not specified to be something projection can do, but it’s also not specified as something projection can’t do. The problem here is vagueness.

                              Finally Move Mind to Another Body continues the confusion about how Minds are linked to bodies, though I already covered it in the Life section.

                              Final tally: 15 effects examined. 7 arbitrary Sphere additions. No extra targeting spheres. 1-2 overengineered spell. 1 inconsistent unstated rule. 6 of the 15 have no problems.





                              Well that’s just Prime…

                              Starting off with the animation rules. Animating a corpse is listed as Life 2/Prime 2. Animating a skeleton is listed as Matter 2/Prime 2. We could interpret that as Prime 2 providing some kind of animating force, while Matter and Life are locking spheres. The only problem is that Prime 2 doesn't list any such ability, none of the ranks of Prime do. Also Life could repair and animate flesh on its own without anything else, though I would not say the same for Matter.

                              Physical illusions are listed as a combination of Prime and Forces. Manipulating light, sound, and momentum are all within the realm of Forces 2 alone, with no need for Prime. Prime could be generating a Body of Light and Forces giving it the feel of substance, I suppose, so its not that the combo of Prime 2+/Forces 2+ is wrong, just overly specific.

                              Consecration of an object is listed as Prime 2, while consecration of a living being is Prime 2/Life 2+. I’m surprised it took this long to get to an example, but Mage sometimes assumes that casting on living things is more difficult than casting on inanimate ones and so throws in extraneous Life requirements. If its ever spelled out as a rule I haven’t found it, and it is applied with reckless disregard for consistency and common sense.

                              A similar thing happens in Prime 3 with Enchant Lifeform requiring Life 3, when no such addition is required for enchanting an object. Notably the Common Magical Effects Chart does not require Life for enchanting lifeforms.

                              Creating Soulgems and Creating Soulflowers requires Matter 4 and Life 5 respectively and there’s not a good reason why or why not. We could argue that making this change involves a change to the actual matter or life pattern. We could also argue the other way. This effect implies that there’s deeper elements to the metaphysics of Mage but never actually explains what those are.

                              10 total effects examined. 4 arbitrary sphere additions and 2 more that may or may not be arbitrary depending on metaphysics. Overall Prime is plagued by vagueness

                                  Spirit

                                  Spirit is perhaps the lightest section with only three significant problems:

                                  Spirit 1 says that combined with other spheres a mage may spot forces, places, or items with unusual ties to the spirit world, such as Awakened objects, elemental spirits, possessed organisms, shallowings, Nodes, etc. Spirit can accomplish most of that without help. There’s nothing wrong with using more spheres and there are benefits, but the book makes it seem mandatory and it isn’t.

                                  Spirit 3/Mind 2 is suggested to read the resonance, synergy, and hierarchy of spirits and I don’t see what Mind is bringing to the table in this arrangement. It would certainly be Mind 2 to determine a person's likes/dislikes, what they do, and who they work for, but for spirits that kind of thing is metaphysically deeper and speaks to their very nature as spirits. When you pull this information from a spirit, you aren't reading their mind you are reading them. That said, you could read their mind for the information as well, but in that case I don't know why you would need Spirit.

                                  Then commanding spirits is stated in the chart to require Spirit 4/Mind 4. This should probably be Spirit 4 or Mind 4, as Spirit 4 is explicitly enough to bind and command spirits.

                                  Final results: 8 effects examined. 1-2 arbitrary additions. 1-2 merged spell.


                                  Time Enough at Last

                                  Like Spirit, Time attempts to expand the utility of Time 1 by tacking on other spheres to allow a mage to “detect the influence of the Time Sphere on other spells or Patterns. It should be able to accomplish that on its own.

                                  Then we have Prophecy, which is purely a Time 2 spell unless the mage is psychic, if he is he must add Mind 2 because the universe hates psychics. I wouldn't complain if Mind was an optional sphere that allowed better understanding of the Prophecy in some way but the Common Effects Chart lists these as the same spell.

                                  Reverse ageing. We covered this from the point of view of Life, but there are two Time based versions Time 3+/Life 4 and Time 3+/Matter 2. In either case Time is rewinding the clock on them on its own. The best I can come up with for Life and Matter is that the writeup is assuming that the spells are locked and that the spell is keeping memories intact. If so, the default spell is Time 3, it needs Matter 2 or Life 2 if the target is moving, and it needs higher levels of Matter or Life if you want to rewind some elements of the target while keeping others (memory, current levels of radioactivity, etc) intact. That's reasonable but not clear.

                                  Then there’s the extremely vague “Combining this Rank with other Spheres, the mage can affect other Patterns (Forces, Life, or Matter 2),” which is completely unhelpful. I think perhaps this was meant to be part of the previous paragraph (in the book) on rewinding time and was incorporating a locking effect (technically still optional, but without it the rewind only takes things back to the point they entered the target area). In its current location it means nothing.

                                  Speaking of meaning nothing, with Time 3/Entropy 3 the mage can invoke multiple probabilities. What the mage can invoke multiple probabilities to do is left to the imagination of the reader.

                                  Apparently it takes Time 3/Life 3/Mind 1 to move back in time while remembering events from the future. Firstly, I think Life or Mind alone could accomplish that. Secondly, its completely unnecessary as the Mage doesn’t have to rewind time for the target while moving it about. I suppose he could, but that’s not what’s being described.

                                  Time 4 says that by adding in Correspondence, Forces, Life, Matter, and/or Spirit a mage can trap a phenomenon in a time bubble. Charitably interpreted (and ignoring the suggested Correspondence) that’s the addition of locking spheres. You could probably get away without them, though if so you'd be trapping an entire area in timeless space and that would be difficult to describe. More problematically, the example is a Time/Forces/Entropy effect to trap a tornado. While I do think there’s an association between Entropy and weather, that has no bearing in this case and this should just be a Time/Forces effect.

                                  Final Tally: 12 effects examined. 3 arbitrary sphere additions (I'm only counting the extra sensory spheres as one, else I'd have to say 11 arbitrary sphere additions).

                                          I took a look at the rotes. I wrote down analyses of the first few then just jotted down thoughts on the rest.

                                          Black Card/Black Box – Hack a system even from unconnected ports. Corr 2/Forces 2/Mind 2.
                                          I’d say this should be Correspondence 2 alone. That said, if we are charitable to the book and uncharitable to the players we could say that the overall data stream is the pattern and we are teleporting it piecemeal. In that case we would need Forces for the connection. I don't see why Mind is here. Were this effect replacing the actual hacking I might see the need for Mind, but this effect specifically says the mage needs to do the hacking himself. Unnecessary sphere or spheres added.

                                          Hot Shotting – Amplify the sound of a gun for increased damage and intimidation factor. Forces 2. Optional add of Mind 2
                                          The Forces side of this is odd because it gives reduced difficulty and an extra success that can either be used for accuracy or intimidation. Adding the extra Forces success to intimidation is sketchy but cinematic. There’s nothing wrong with using Forces for either effect alone so they can certainly be used together, its just a unique way of approaching the system. I like the addition of the Mind sphere variant because it adds a unique extra intimidation effect against Willpower and there’s a good reason to cast what would otherwise be two separate effects together.

                                          Hands of Death – Enhance hand to hand damage. Life 3 or Forces 2 or both. Optional adds of Entropy 2 or Prime 2.
                                          A basic Life attack on an unarmed strike or a Forces augmentation to that unarmed strike. The Life attack is not aggravated damage unless the strike is truly theatrical, so in most cases the Forces version is undeniably superior (lower difficulty and more damage). Entropy addition reduces armor, durability, or soak. Prime 2 allows the Forces version to get aggravated damage (still vulgar so Life 3 doesn’t need the help), though the Common Effects chart indicates that cost a point of quintessence and the spell doesn’t call for that. That quintessence cost is the only issue I see.

                                          Right Click – Instant Data with a Mind Computer Interface. Mind 3/Data 3, with Forces, Life, Matter, Prime, DimSci options.
                                          The description is a little vague but the spell allows the mage to pull up a lot of information about what he sees through a combination of mental scans and internet access. Mind is specifically used to read surface thoughts while Data grants remote access. The problem here is that surface thoughts only require Mind 2 and a worldwide connection only requires Data 2. The optional spheres add other senses to the data as appropriate and DimSci 2 allows the rote to work from the umbra. The Sphere requirements are too high.

                                          Hypernarrative Influence – Mental Influence via Tropes. Mind 4 with an Entropy 2 option.
                                          Influences people to behave in accordance with common tropes and, with the Entropy addon, influences reality to do the same. The basic rote lets the mage trigger the desired behavior with a successful Manipulation + Appropriate Skill roll. There's no real system for the Entropy effect, that's in the hands of the ST. I see no issues here.

                                                  I started digging more into the Vampire Lawn Chair rote, but my breakdown was becoming more of a rant so I'll shelve that for now and just give my final thoughts:

                                                  I suspect that the rules changed a few times during production and not every instance was caught in editing. Sometimes reading M20 feels like an archaeological dig where you can see the newer layers of rules built on the ruins of the old ones. There are a lot of accidental additions of spheres, ‘ands’ instead of ‘ors’, statements that are contradicted a paragraph later, and descriptions that don’t actually describe anything.

                                                  Beyond that there is also missing information in the rules that really needs to be present. The Locking Spheres section, for instance, does not say what rank you need to use to lock. Certain spheres seem to omit capabilities that other parts of M20 assume they have. Others seem to be hurting for not enough to do. So when there's a discrepancy it is often hard to tell if the book is appealing to rules it never stated or ignoring the rules.

                                                  Four spheres in particular really suffer:

                                                  Correspondence and Time are a mess of contradictory statements and vague descriptions that don’t give enough information. There are clauses and sentences that are either out of place or need to be cut entirely, and descriptions of spells that indicate one thing is happening paired with sphere requirements that indicate another thing.

                                                  Forces suffers from not having enough meat, and I think it always has (I need to check my library). The other pattern spheres can divide up into simple and complex versions, but all the forces a mage typically wants to play with (motion, light, sound, heat, electricity, etc.) would be simple forces. Things like gravity and storms are interesting but situational. So Forces takes things like magnitude, which would ordinarily be a function of successes, and pushes them to higher ranks. It’s not terribly elegant, and it makes the higher levels of Forces seem like Inspector Leopard of the Yard: “The same, only more violent!”

                                                  Prime is small, vague, and uncertain. It is a short section with only a few concrete abilities per rank, and half of them claim to need other spheres to even make work (even when that isn’t clear) On the other hand there are not one but two abilities attributed to it that are never stated anywhere in its writeup (animating things and illusion). Additionally, it seems to be the catchall requirement: any time it seems like something isn’t difficult enough there’s a good chance Prime will be tacked on as a requirement.
