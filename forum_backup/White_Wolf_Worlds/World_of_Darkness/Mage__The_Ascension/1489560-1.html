                            08-11-2022, 09:11 PM
                            Some of those Crafts were there, they faced all the same problems that led the Traditions to put aside their differences or more, and decided to be independant. Of course, people change and some of them may be partial to make "their own" (with blackjack and hookers)...but...

                            I fell it's not so much that it's unthinkable, as it's a thing with the execution. Perhaps it was too sudden, and not too well explored yet.

                            My understanding it's that M20 tried to explain it by recycling the Revised ploy that was used to "fold" them into the Traditions: That they were too weakened by the Technocracy. Aint that it?- I don't like that plot point. Precisely in Revised, when the Technocracy and the Traditions are at their weakest, and declared a cease of the Pogrom/War, times are "too dangerous" for the Crafts to keep resisting like they did during the whole of the Ascencion War?.

                            One can't really say "it's the same as when the Traditions formed", because the Traditions did form, and that changes the history. One has to take into consideration that the Craft ate the whole of the Ascencion War alone, and proud of it, but precisely now times are changing for them more than they ever did before.

                            It's not enough to say "these things happen", sell/show us that it IS happening. Make it every bit as momentous as the Tradition's forming.

                            Like, what did the Nephandi do - this time - that was so monumental it caused these factions to change?. The Technocracy blew up a chantry, and scared the fuck up of the greatest Seer ever, and then attacked the Council itself while fostering a brutal Inquisition, all of that helped quite a lot to join enemies together: That being so, what did it for the Disparate?. It HAS to be ... cool. I don't like if you can just gloss over it

                            Originally posted by Kakost
                            And what's worse, the Traditions were almost an Hermetic imposition upon the others, but yes, without the Hermetics it would never be, however their diplomatic skills werent exactly the best for the job; on the other hand, the Disparate Alliance have the guidance of the Ahl-I-Batin
                            Interestingly, the Order of Hermes originally didn't wanted to join. Hermetic do like to impose stuff, but this one was an imposition uppon *them*. Master La'Salle had to Duel so many Masters in non-lethal Certamen to build the oaths to *force* the Hermetics to join with the other sects, that he died ... Afterwards, of course they were dicks with everyone and bossed everyone arround. Which of course alienated many would-be members.

                            The main diplomats of the old Council were the Ahl-I-Batin (well, perhaps below the Verbena) , which it's also one of the main, if not the main, faction that founded the principle of Ascencion followed by the Council. They grew disinchanted with that vision of Unity, but they were among it's authors

                            The Ahl-I-Batin are the thing that, in my opinion, gives the most credence to any unity between these disparate factions on the Alliance, because they're experts at this and have done it before. But, as said, they also failed to recruit the current Disparate back then, when they had luminarias like Sh'zar the Seer giving credence to their plot. It would be nice to see how it's that they succeeded now.

                            *Let's not make a Traditions 2.0

                            I don't think we need Traditions 2.0. And I don't think the Alliance needs to be it in order to exist.

                            This has been brought in these forums a lot, but it bears repeating: The Traditions have a very particular idea about "unity", that of leaving aside your Focus, accepting a common magical lingo (the 9 Spheres), working in concert. Not everyone (perhaps not even a majority) really do all of this, but it's at least paid lip service. And at least the Systers of Hippolita explicitly refused to join because they rejected part of this meta-paradigm.

                            This is not the only way to forge a unified faction. Even more, it's not the only idea about Unity, the Technocracy also has one of it's own

                            So let's make a new faction with a different idea about Unity: Unity in diversity, explicitly eschewing a central power instead of trying to "make" one, allowing the members to rule themselves

                            One thing that I really disliked about the Disparate was their legal system. Because their legal system forces members to be judged under the authority of at least two "wise man" of other Crafts ... and I don't buy it ... I can't see the fiercely independant Crafts just "accepting" a member it's going to be executed under the premises of a Paradigm not their own. I can't see them *not* closing files if they think the "criminal" doesn't deserve such punishment under their own law
