    Sosolviya — social division of Russia’s awakened

    05-29-2018, 06:58 AM
    This is a writeup for our MtA games set in the modern Russia (and wider post-Warsaw pact area) for local players. They are aiming for both keeping over-the-edge mood of World of Darkness while making setting little more detailed and nuanced.

    A little historical background: sosloviya, in real life, is a Russian term rougly analogous to “estates of realm” — broad class categories of medieval and early modern society. There were around ten sosloviya in Russian Empire (the most notable difference from estates of realm system being a separate class of merchants). Russian Revolution intended to establish classless society, but privileged nomenclatura class appeared soon, along with other social divisions.

    In our view of World of Darkness, magickal traditions in pre-revolution Russia were tied to soslovia. The three primary groups were orthodox Christian theurgy, learned occultism (primarily hermetic) among the nobles, and folk magic (primarily syncretic) among peasants, soldiers, merchants and craftsmen. Among Awakened, this division corresponded roughly to Celestial Chorus, Order of Hermes and Verbena. After the revolution, the situation changed greatly. Most hermetics were forced to exile by the new regime or purged; religion and folk superstitions were equally persecuted. But the three groups remained, albeit in the very changed form.


    Starovery (The Old Faithful)
    Nicknames: Veruny (“Faithers”, gerogatory), Bogatyri (after epic folk heroes, by Western traditionalists)

    Those religious mages who were not killed and did not escape Soviet Union went deep underground, either joining already existing underground religious movements (including orthodox christian Old Rite schismatics and secret pagan cults), forming their own tight-knit cabals, or in solitary hermitage. They live either hiding among small, most often rural communities (which was difficult during Soviet collectivization, but somewhat easier now), or in their own monasteries and villages in remote places, mostly in far corners of Siberia or atop Kavkaz and Altai mountains. Some risk their lives as itinerant preachers. This is most fervently anti-Technocratic faction, often rejecting modern civilization entirely. They differ on how to deal with it, some secluding themselves from the world, some trying to change it, often violently.

    Despite of centuries of persecution (or perhaps because being hardened by them), Starovery are faring quite well now. They have largest number of Masters (some of whom are centuries-old and very powerful), access to the most powerful Nodes, good libraries of Grimoires and tabernacles of Talismans and Relics. Among sosloviya, Starovery have strongest ties with the Western traditionalists, who often view them as heroes and as the Russian branch of the Council; among these allies is Master Maga Ananiya Divraniya, Prima of Hermetic House Flambeau. Another powerful allied force is tribes of shapechangers, most often Garou, Gurahl and Corax. They are, however, rarely have chance to put all these resources to good use because of widespread acedia, sectarian conflicts, and fear and certain naïvete in dealing with worldly matters.

    Goals and priorities: spiritual improvement toward the Ascension, protection of the land and the people, destruction of the Technocracy and all demonic forces.
    Represented splats: Most often Celestial Chorus (primarily Christian and Muslim Anchorites), Verbena (most often Guardians of the Tree); also Cult of Ecstasy (khlysty and other folk Protestants, some indigenous shamans), Tâftani, Sons of Tengri, Dreamspeakers, Akashic Brotherhood Jnani, possibly Ahl-i-Batin.
    Concepts: travelling medicant monk, mad visionary, child of a secluded witch family on a quest out in a wider world.


    Chud (from Baltic Finns tribal name)
    Nicknames: the Underground, Scrapfolk, Those Who Hide, Chudaki (“Eccentrics”), Cravens (derogatory)

    While conservative learned occultism was hit hardest during the revolution of 1917 and the following civil war in Russia, many among awakened welcomed the revolution as a possibility to create from scratch a new society and even a new world order. They were a driving force among the Futurist and Constructivist movements and enjoyed a decade of florescence, until the opposition from the establishment forced them to either cooperate, be destroyed, of hide. From those who chose to do the latter a new soslovie was forged. Unlike Starovery, most Chudes choose to hide “in the plain sight”, leading outwardly ordinary lives and practicing very low key coincidental magick. Still, the movement was dwindling during the first half of the 20th century. Often, newly awakened mages lacked a proper mentor and learned from scarce grimoires and rumors. The situation improved a bit in the second half of 1950s, but by then, Chudes were only a loose network of cabals, hermits and societies. From that period, the soslovie grew slowly, but unpredictably, re-establishing contact with awakened around the world and experimenting with new forms of magick.

    Still, even now the Eccentrics are weakest among the three sosloviya. They lack both mystical resources of Starovery and the mundane ones of Systema; their only asset is secrecy. Some hide among the mundanes; other travel strange spirit-worlds or create citadels in dreams. Western Traditionalists usually view them as Orphans, or, worse, Dissidents, who pervert traditional teachings without a proper guidance; but the Hollow Ones see kindred souls in them, and two groups overlap somewhat. Chudes also have allies among changelings and wraiths.

    There is a tendency among more recently awakened mages to reject Chud secrecy; some of them want to create new modern form of magick, emulating futurist of old not by rote, but in spirit. The most prominent cabal of this movement calls themselves Proteists, and this label is sometimes applied to all neofuturist mages.

    Goals and priorities: survival, exploration, experimentation, creation of the new form of magick.
    Represented splats: Hollow Ones, Mirainohmen, Cypherpunks, Children of Knowledge, hermetic House Merinita, dissident factions among Traditions (Euthanatos Order of the Black Willow, Acharné ecstatics, Mad etherites), Independent Dreamspeakers, rogue Void Engineers.
    Concepts: avant-garde artist, collector of curiousities, archivist researcher, .


    Systema (“The System”)
    Nicknames: The Establishment, The Regime, Blat (The Old Boy Network), Chaldeans (derogatory)

    The Russian Revolution was product of many forces, but from supernaturals’ point of view, it was primarily a product of great social experiments by two forces: technocractic convention of New World Order and vampiric clan Brujah. The experiments, for the most part, were not joint, but in efforts to control their course, necessary agreements were made. Later, technocrats and mystics united against various hardships which young Soviet Union was facing, culminating with World War II; and this truce weren’t dissolved as swiftly when war was over as it was in the West. And even though the grand experiment is ultimately failed, there is still hope to build better society together.

    This is approximation of the history of Systema as told by its members; inasmuch as it is true, the Russian Technocracy was surprisingly tolerant toward those mystics and supernaturals who agreed to cooperate. Western Traditionalists see Blatny mages as sellouts or brainwashed pawns, and Western Technocratic Union is seeing this cooperation as a corruption of Precepts of Damian. The truth is more complex: The Union branch is not fully in control in Russia and would not survive without these allies, and they would not survive without the Union.

    The Systema has the most of mundane resources, including vast wealth in money and stocks, influence in governments and corporations, mass media support, and human resources in form of cults, spy networks, and private armies. Various members of Systema have allies among foreign branches of Technocracy, Anarchs and Camarilla, and some powerful individuals among Crafts and Traditions. It has troubles to consolidate enough mystical power, though: the mistrust between its members complicates arcane study, and furious opposition and sabotage from shifters and Starovery undermines Quintessence supply.

    Goals and priorities: protection of status quo; for more utopian members, prevention of large-scale conflicts and gradual imporvement of the society.
    Represented splats: New World Order, Celestial Chorus, Euthanatos Golden Chalice, Sons of Ether Utopists.
    Concepts: private military company commander with an interest in occult, church hierarch with an government connections, field anthropologist with a spy mission.
