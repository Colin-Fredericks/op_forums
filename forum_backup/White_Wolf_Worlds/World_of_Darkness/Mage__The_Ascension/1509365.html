    Hekau for Sorcerers

    04-23-2023, 08:36 AM
    This is my attempt to convert Mummy Hekau into sorcerous paths compatible with S20. The goal here is to provide some crunch to mortal members of the Cult of Isis, Children of Osiris, and on the other side the various cults of Bane Mummies.

    Alchemy and Amulets are already covered by their respective Paths (with Enchantment for the latter), so I didn’t see a reason to create new ones. The rituals given in the Mummy rules make some good example spells, though.

    For Celestial, I tried a few different things. Invoking celestial powers, a general buff based on astrology, and so forth. The results were either underwhelming, boring, insanely complex, or some variation where Weather Control, Divination, and Fortune ended up as Aspects of the Path. I don’t like this for linear magic. It should do one thing and increase in power with proficiency.
    So rather than re-invent the wheel it makes more sense to just use those Paths as-is, with Astrology as Casting Ability.

    Necromancy is in a somewhat similar spot, as it has aspects of Ephemera, Necromancy (duh), and Starlight. The remaining powers are very specific to Mummies in that they deal with dying and reviving, something that obviously doesn’t apply for mortal Sorcerers. I flirted a bit with a version that grants command over the different parts of the soul as per the ancient Egyptian understanding of it, but again ended up with mostly emulating powers of the above Paths. A specific Casting Ability (for example, Egyptian Thantology) makes more sense.


    For Ushabti, I plundered the Akhu version of the same name in Blood Sacrifice: the Thaumaturgy Companion p.27, 28
    The conversion was ridiculously easy, and the lack of Vitae in the procedure is more than compensated by the increased difficulty from introducing additional Aspects.

    Aspects: Agency, Realism, Time Period (from Divination) as Duration

    Agency (Attributes of created Ushabti as described in BS:tTC)

    • Laborer
    •• Servitor
    ••• Guard
    •••• Overseer
    ••••• Gift of Khnum

    Realism (As per extra success table on p.28 in BS:tTC)

    • Crude
    •• Fairly realistic
    ••• Moderately lifelike (difficulty 6 to detect)
    •••• Incredibly lifelike (difficulty 8 to detect)
    ••••• Indistinguishable from life

    Finally, Ren-Hekau is perhaps the most interesting Path to convert. It cheats as linear magic a bit in that its effects can be very versatile if used creatively, something which I like. At the same time, its scope is limited by the knowledge of True Names the Sorcerer has. Discovering a True Name requires intensive study of a subject, research, and practice.
    In game terms, a Sorcerer has to have a minimum level in a corresponding Ability to learn a new Name (eg. Animal Ken for “Dog”). The XP invested in the Ability reflect the time invested in study of the subject, the minimum threshold is the level of the Names Aspect.
    Additionally, command of True Names gets more difficult the more complex the target of the spell is. Names for simple things are equally simple, but the True Name of a person can be much more difficult to command.

    To use the Path, a sorcerer forms a simple command using words of power and the True Name of the target together with a Command (“Earl Grey, become Hot!”).

    Aspects: Names, Command, Duration

    Names

    • Simple things – Stone, metal, liquids, a book etc.
    •• Plants and fungi
    ••• Animals and machines
    •••• Humans – every individual has their own True Name, but parts of their anatomy (“Heart”) may share a common one
    ••••• Ephemeral and Supernatural beings – as above

    Command

    • Change a simple attribute – Temperature, size, mood, color etc.
    •• Learning the secrets – Discover hidden characteristics, flaws, or motivations of the target
    ••• Borrow an attribute – Gain a characteristic from the commanded thing. “Stone” skin, “Dog” hearing, “J.R. Dobbs” face etc
    •••• Compel into action – Force a target into an action (if able), similar to Dominate 2. Also works on non-living things to an extent (“Car, drive forward!”).
    ••••• Create something into or erase something from existence.


    Ideas and criticisms are welcome. This isn’t set out as strictly for NPCs, but also something for my PCs to discover and learn if they are interested.

    I would especially welcome ideas for more interesting Celestial and Necromancy Paths, because just splitting them into their components still feels a bit like a wasted opportunity to me.

          Hekau is simply the old Egyptian word for magic and is used as an umbrella term for spells of that tradition. Awakened Magick is referred to as Ra-Hekau in the books. Mummy Hekau are neither Sorcery nor True Magick as described in Mage, but more akin to linear magic. They have access to their own Quintessence flavor called Sekhem, but can't learn True Magick.

          That said, Hekau as a mortal Sorcery does not differ from any other tradition in that a Sorcerer with access to grimoires or teachers can learn it. In fact, one of the core tasks of the Cult of Isis is to teach it to new Mummies, which wouldn't be possible if there was no mortal equivalent. The books never spelled them out, but Sorcerer Revised had a short paragraph about it.

          Seeing how ancient Egyptian grimoires are readily available for anyone interested in real life, I don't think direct involvement with those groups would be strictly necessary for a WoD denizen to learn them. But like with most specialized groups, they would probably have access to better learning resources. Other local factions that could have deeper insight would be the Followers of Set and Bubasti.

          I should also mention that despite the names of the Paths, none of them are exclusive to the Egyptian magic tradition. Ushabti exist in other forms all over the world, and Naming magic is one of the oldest forms of Sorcery that probably started with the development of language.

              There's a book in Storyteller's Vault called Pillars of Ma'at which is designed as an update guide for Mummy: the Resurrection, bringing it up to date with the 20th Anniversary gamelines (which it refers to as “fourth edition games”). Chapter One addresses the Hekau, including a comparison between them and mortal Paths, as well as between them and vampiric Thaumaturgy. Chapter Two addresses the Children of Osiris, including character creation rules for them. They're mostly mortal, with a handful of immunities (to the Embrace, The Veil, Delirium, etc., as well as to non-supernatural diseases), and a perhaps heightened ability to develop psychic numina or to learn hedge magic.

              Chapter Three covers the Cult of Isis, who are described as using an ancient Egyptian flavor of True Faith – namely, Ma'at. Having Ma'at allows them access to Miracles the same way that True Faith does in Hunters Hunted 2; and the Spell of Life is described in this supplement as a Miracle that's available at Ma'at level 3 or 4, depending on who you're resurrecting. But it also lets them learn and use Hekau (spending Willpower instead of Sekhem), which conceptually separates the Hekau from Hedge Magic: it's more of an elaboration of (one particular flavor of) True Faith rather than being a kind of Sorcery.

              On the other hand, the Storyteller's Vault supplement Sorcerer: Paths of Power (the direct inspiration for M20 Sorcerer) describes both the Children of Osiris and the Cult of Isis as sorcerous orders, with the former specializing in Alchemy and Summoning, Binding, and Warding (Ghosts) while the latter get Alchemy, Divination (Astrology), Enchantment (primarily amulets and statues), and sometimes Healing. In contrast to Pillars of Ma'at, this book suggests that it's probably the Children of Osiris who can learn Hekau.

              Of the two books, I prefer ​​​​Pillars of Ma'at's take on mortals learning Hekau; though I might supplement that with allowing the Cult of Isis to take what Sorcerer: Paths of Power calls the Technê Background, and which Sorcerer Revised called the Mana Background, and to use that to fuel their Hekau instead of Willpower.
