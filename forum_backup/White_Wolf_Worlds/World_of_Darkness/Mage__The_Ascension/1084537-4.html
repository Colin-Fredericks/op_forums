            Personally, I wouldn't mind a reimagining of the Foundation-and-Pillars system that builds off of M20's magic system: have the mechanics for working magic be the same as in M20 (to the extent that M20 has a single set of mechanics for working magic) with the same sort of “optional rules” boxes that M20 used to incorporate MRev mechanics. For instance, the default assumption would be that you do your Foundation to work magic, and that you can't raise our use a Pillar higher than your Foundation; but there would be boxes suggesting the ability to use Foundation+Pillar dice pools or the freedom to raise a Pillar above your Foundation for a price.

            I'd keep the Foundation and Pillars; but I'd tie each Foundation to an M20 Practice, and I'd list a few M20 Paradigms for each Fellowship. Pillar write-ups wouldn't differ much from how they're handle now: a brief one-to-five-dot progression with about as much detail as we got for a Sphere in M20, coupled to a set of M20 Instruments and Abilities (a subset of the ones from the Foundation's associated Practice) that are particularly suitable for that Pillar. Where appropriate, point out those Pillars that went on to develop into one of the Sorcerer's Crusade Spheres (mostly the Forma; but IIRC al-Hajj bears an uncanny resemblance to Correspondence, and I'm sure there are a few others). For other Pillars, gives brief summary of how to compose them from Sphere specialties (e.g. “you could get something similar to the Summer Season by taking Forces limited to heat and combining it with Life limited to transformations” — this is off the top of my head; don't expect it to hear any resemblance to DAMage's Summer Season).

            Scrap the Old Faith and the Spirit-Talkers: replace each with one specific forerunner to the Verbenae and Dreamspeakers, respectively, and name-drop a few similarly-themed Fellowships that may or may not use the same or similar Foundation/Pillars. In fact, every Fellowship write-up should name-drop a few similar Fellowships that don't get write-ups, with the expectation that interested Storytellers will flesh them out. Kill the notion that there are only six Fellowships running around during the Dark Ages.

            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                            I've had a chance to read through V20 Black Hand, and had a look at that section about the True Black Hand's mages. They are specifically the Idran - the Itarajana Craft - that were a specific group of Chakravanti during their conflict with the Akashayana. Until obviously they tried to achieve enlightenment through blurring the line between Life and Death. This is an established bit of lore from Euthanatos Revised, and V20 Tal'Mahe'Ra just expands on it, and makes it more relevant to vampires (instead of the Nagaraja being created from pure Awakened magick, it used a bit of purified Setite blood in the process, like the Tremere's origin). The True Black Hand has both Nagaraja, and these mortal practitioners.

                            Itarajana magick has Dharma as the Foundation, and the Pillars are the Loka, representing various worlds of Buddhist and Hindu thought (read: Umbral Zones). Each focuses on a different one of these areas of the Umbra, and the inhabitants therein. The Pillars are as follows:

                            -Naraka, The Torment World: Basically hell. Combines elements of Forces, Entropy, and Spirit (Infernal).
                            Keywords: Destruction, Curses, Heat, Cold

                            -Preta, The Underworld: The Shadowlands. Combines elements of Entropy and Spirit (Ghost).
                            Keywords: Chaos, Death, Ghosts, The Shadowlands

                            -Triyagyoni, The Animalistic World: The Spirit Wilds. Combines elements of Life, Mind, and Spirit (Naturae).
                            Keywords: Nature, The Middle Umbra, Passions, Spirits

                            -Deva, The Divine World: Astral Umbra. Combines elements of Prime, Correspondence, and Mind.
                            Keywords: Creation, Correspondences, Fate, Thought

                                    To me, it was worth the investment, but YMMV. Based on your love of in depth exploration of the mechanics of Mage magic, it might be very well worth it.

                                    As a , it is explicitly DA Foundation/Pillar based in the way in which the magic a mage possesses is measured, but the implementation of effects borrows HEAVILY from standard non-DA Mage, though some things (like Paradox) are trimmed back for functionality. (TBH, it would have taken a huge chunk of the entire book to deal with even the basics of Mage Paradox. and it's ostensibly about vampires, though it's got a huge Wraith, significant Mage and minor Werewolf with a touch of Changeling components.)

                                    They really don't come across as Sphere/Pillar hybrids. They're Pillars per DA in a game sense. The language used for what each Pillar encompasses is very much drawn more from Mage than the more Focus/ideology driven per level descriptions in DA Mage. Meaning, when Correspondence effects are built into a Pillar, they use the language pretty much right out of a bullet description of the particular signature Correspondence effects. Which is a weakness and a strength both. It's not exactly like reading a DA Pillar and having to tease out, "Oh, that's pretty much a Matter 2 effect as a part of Pillar X."

                                    So, even though Itaranga/Chakravanti magic seems like it's Spirit oriented, it includes huge chunks of Correspondence and Entropy, etc. I'd wager that the "different Spirit worlds" was an attempt to aspect them to maximum utility for the environment, e.g. living in a city in the Tempest, making the gates everyone uses back and forth to the Skinlands, having Abominations as buddies, etc.

                                                I like that the Idran Pillar write-ups make it easy to map their capabilities onto the Spheres — though I get Ajax's point about losing some of the “built-in Focus” flavor. Still, the most important part is still there: what you can do with the Pillars is tailored according to the principles of magic that the Foundation represents. I think they could do better than the near-direct cut-and-paste used there; in the hypothetical DA:M20, I'd want them to try for a balance between “Oh; this part of this Pillar works just like this part of that Sphere” and strictly Practice-centric lingo.

                                                I am reminded that a fundamental difference between Arete and Foundation is that the former is a ten-dot trait while the latter is a five-dot trait. This means two things: F/P needs the two-trait pool in order to remain competitive with Arete; and while F/P generally has larger dice pools than Arete, they're not that much bigger. I'm thinking that DA:M20 should retain the five-dot Foundations and the F+P casting pool by default, with a “Future Fates” bit of text explaining the eventual rise of Arete as a replacement for the Foundations. And instead of providing a list of foci, DA:M20's Foundations should explicitly reference an M20 Practice — indeed, I think it would be fair to describe a Foundation as “having dots in a Practice”. Also, DA:M20 should adopt M20's “Focus: Paradigm, Practice, and Instruments” terminology in place of pre-M20's “foci”.

                                                Finally, DA:Mage is definitely a Revised-era product. Look at how it handles Scourge: it almost exactly mirrors Revised-era Paradox. For a DA:M20 product, I'd be inclined to make Scourge work like M20 Paradox, up to and including being customizable in the same ways. Though I'd definitely include the Black Hand book's streamlined Backlash mechanics: roll the Scourge pool as if it were a casting pool using the Pillars that triggered the Backlash, and have the Storyteller work up a malicious Effect that achieves that many successes. Simple, straightforward, and generally able to replicate the entire Paradox Backlash system. It's a bit Newtonian (for every action there's an equal and opposite reaction) in an era that predates Newton; but it seems appropriate to me.

                                                    I think there's a fundamental flaw in this argument. Mage really falls apart when Arete 6+ comes into play. The power levels are significantly higher than what happens when a vampire diablerizes their way to a low enough generation to get 6 dot Disciplines or a Werewolf gets enough Renown for Rank 6/Level 6 Gifts. Spheres at 6 are earthshaking/ST fiat level stuff. They have more in common with the stuff Incarnae can do and Vampires down in the low low ends of the generation pool. And the Arete system unlocks that incredible power, that, being bound only by ST fiat, is de facto, power that PCs aren't normally able to access (vide "ST fiat"). That's definitely been stated outright in the newthink (post Masters of the Art, Brucato-style Spheres).

                                                    Which means that Arete isn't a 10 dot scale power stat. It's effectively and practically a 5 dot scale, no matter what it says on the chart. Willpower is a 10 dot scale stat. "Juice" stats are 10+ dot scale stats. 10+ dot scaling stats are for expendable assets (Quint, Pathos, Glamour, Blood). Arete is a functional mess because it offers 10 dots and acts as if that's a practical measure for what characters can do with it,when that's not the way the game is built. It could work, if there was something in play more like Awakening. But, from a game design standpoint, it's effective crap. It makes almost all effects subject to a ST handwave to determine outcome, because the manifesting an effect boils down down to accomplishments based on very, very, very few successes.

                                                    That's why Foundation+Pillar (or Awakening magic) is structurally more coherent in a White Wolf style system where accomplishment is measured in numbers of successes based on a 0-10 dice system, not a 1-5. The dice pools end up being MUCH bigger. Foundation 2 + Pillar 2 = 4 dice. That's twice as many as Arete 2 + Sphere 2. And, once the "coupling limitation" is done away with, the dice pools become significantly different. Foundation 2 + Pillar 5 = 7 dice (Or, Foundation 5 + Pillar 2). Arete 2 means ONLY Sphere 2. So it's 2 dice. If it's Arete 5 and Sphere 2 it's 5 dice, but that's significantly harder to raise Arete vs. Spheres in play. Then, take into account that it's easier to figure out what those successes mean when they can be allocated as opposed to "3 successes accomplishes whatever you can convince the ST would be 'reasonable' for a complete success". (It also solves some of those messy, Mages vs. other supernaturals comparisons too).
