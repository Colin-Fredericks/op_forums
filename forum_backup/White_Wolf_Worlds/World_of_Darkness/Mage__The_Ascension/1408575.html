    Questions about something like "Money-Shaman paradigm"

    08-16-2020, 04:59 PM
    Hey guys! First of all, I am not a native english speaker, so sorry any mistakes in my post.

    So, i'm in a chronicle of MTA with others players, the game takes place New York city and all of us have characters with some urban integration and urban-kind paradigm. But, a players has this strange idea: He wants to create a money-based mage, probably a House Fortune one, but with a diferent kind of paradigm - the guy wants to be a "money-shaman".

    Probably the dreamspeakers or euthanatos paradigms won't allow this kind of thing, this is the reason that he chooses the House Fortune.

    This is his idea: He want to be a mage who works with spirits (or elementals) of money/fortune/capitalism (the "prime sphere" will be capitalism [?]) and he wants to choose the own capitalism as a Totem. We advise he to take some God, like Hermes to be a totem, but he willing to take the own capitalism god-form as totem. He plans, in the future, to use both entropy and mind to have a "good luck" touch and manipulate everyone to provide resources to him. Yet, he too wants to have a "money-spirit fetish" in a coin that give a boost the fortune thing, making opportunities to earn more money by manipulating the surround area. And, of course, his have a idea for a future "pot of greed" talisman. His character wuold constantly seeks influence, power and control over city areas and influent people (like politicians, businessmen and bankers).

    I, particulary, have a lot of questions on it, but here have some of them:

    1 - Is this possible?
    2 - Won't the "men in black" track him fast and "make everything" disappear?
    3 - I was thinking, the rotes in his paradigm may seens "coincidental", but making money with the aid of magick and messing with the capitalist system won't be a higher chance to "attract" more paradox attention?
    4 - Won't the Urban Spirit of the city be mad with someone messing up the natural flow of wealth of the city? I mean, this kind of spirit as an enemy can destroy his pretensions really fast...
    5 - Let's assume that "money elementals" or "spirit of capitalism" does exist: what are the conditions for dealing with them? Them powers, charms, attributes, shape and limits? Will them have something like "entropy 2" thing? The more powerful ones can do the "meme thing" with "entropy 5"? Seems very hard to me.
    6 - Is it even possible to have the Capitalism as a Totem???
    7 - And a completely random question: if money elementals or fortune spirits does exist, is possible to cause financial loss to someone in a shaman way - causing harm or death of some sort of money spirit accompanying wealth of this guy?

    Any tips, answers and ideias about this would be great!

    Thank you all!!!

                                                      I tried writing a response to this earlier on my phone, but kept getting pull away by RL and then it got lost when my phone logged me out >_<

                                                      I think how well the idea can work depends a fair bit on how one chooses to interpret the word "shaman," but I think there are at least two ways to handle this character concept:

                                                      When saying they want to be a "shaman," your player might simply mean "a Mage whose magic is centred around summoning, interacting with, or binding spirits." If that is all, then a member of the Hermetic House Fortunae might actually work really well. As posts above have mentioned, the concept of "capitalism" is a bit abstract for the Middle Umbra. However, it seems rather appropriate for an entity of the High Umbra, and summoning Angels from the High Umbra has been a component of the Hermetic tradition for a long time now.

                                                      The more specific connotation of the word "shaman" goes beyond just summoning and interacting with spirits, however. It also tends to include reverence for the larger system, not simply a desire to exploit it for your advantage, along with a role as a mediator between the community and the spirit-world. If that is the sort of thing you're looking for, an "Urban Shaman" Dreamspeaker inspired by the Glass Walker material cited above would likely be more appropriate. "Pure" capitalism is a bit at odds with the idea of a shamanistic character in this sense, but someone who played the role of "mediator" between people and "the market" might try to achieve "balance" through things like regulation, non-profit work, etc.
