                                    The NWO Convention Book started out with the following (p.12):
                                    In another reality, the Time of Judgment came, a Red Star its herald. The world ended, either through mass Ascension, war, or catastrophe. Yet, here we are in 2012, our world for the moment still spinning. For us, the Red Star was not a harbinger of doom, but a passing moment of curiosity, is selling dread in those most paranoid. Our destinies certainly changed, and we can only hope for the better.

                                    The world didn't get off scot-free, though. The Avatar Storm continues to savage the Horizon Realms, cutting the Traditions and Technocrats alike off from their leadership. Some of these possible fates from that other reality hover over ours like the Sword of Damocles, particularly a Technocratic Civil War. as for the rest of the details in the Time of Judgment, such as the Rogue Council and the Panopticon, maybe they're in your chronicle's future. According to one school of thought, “Judgment Day is inevitable”. But another says there is no fate but what we make.

                                    In effect, the (last four) Revised Convention Books rolled back the end-of-the-world scenario to shortly before the rise of the Rogue Council and the Panopticon, and charts a different course from there which at least postpones the end of the world and possibly bypasses it.

                                    And that was before M20 rolled out and established its own “this is your game; do what you will” mantra, providing tools to use M20 as a continuation from the Revised Convention Books, as a continuation of the 2e material, or something else entirely. The closest thing it has to a “metaplot development” is to plant suggestions of what Brucato intended to do if not for the aim of the book to primarily be a celebration of the previous iterations of the game. Things like the notion that the Avatar Storm happened, but mostly in the past tense, the establishment of the New Horizon Council in New Zealand with renamed Traditions, the impending restructuring of the Union and a purging of reform elements in its ranks, and the secret development of a Disparate Alliance.

                                    When I talk about “what has happened”, this is more or less what I go with; but again, M20 doesn't lock you into this the way MRev attempted to lock you into its changes.
