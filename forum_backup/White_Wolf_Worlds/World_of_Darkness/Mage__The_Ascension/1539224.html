    Rethinking the Ascension War

    07-25-2024, 01:22 PM
    Okay, its time to reimagine Mage's history again. We played with this once before in Alternative Traditions Origins. This time the idea is to throw out the current mix of Traditions and Conventions entirely. We are going to go back to the 1400's and figure out what the mystic world was like at the time, what kind of mages/mage equivalents existed at the time, the stakes and ideologies of the brewing Ascension War, etc.

    Unlike last time, I do not have a specific idea of who the major players are or which side (or sides) of the conflict they will fall on. Nor is there any need to make sure this ends up in the modern MtA we are familiar with. I'm more interested in who could be there and what their motivations are.

    Because this is such an open ended idea, though, I will bring in two ideas from the previous thread.

    1) The Hermetic Schools - An loose association of scholars and professors piecing together mystical and non mystical insights from the vast and often contradictory libraries left behind by the Roman Empire. These guys look a lot like the typical academicians of the time because they are, there's just a much wider range of study than in our world.

    2) The Wildlands - Also a product of the fall of Rome and the repeated invasions. Europe is filled with a mix of imported and often conflicting mythologies, monsters, and folkloric traditions and creatures. The Cities are largely untouched but the countryside has never quite stabilized.

    So, who and what would you expect to see?

              It seems to me that if we’re looking at the 1400s, at least in Europe, a significant player has to be whatever Awakened factions align themselves with the hegemonic world religions (particularly Christianity & Islam in the western context but also Buddhism as you start heading east)

              You might take various organizations that are posited by the canon WoD as precursors to the Celestial Chorus & Batini and the like. Something I like to do with my WoD is take inspiration from irl esoteric and occult beliefs for use in my WoD setting, regardless of their real-world veracity (it’s kinda like having sewer gators in NYC, it’s unrealistic but too juicy to ignore). In that vein one might play around with the myriad hypotheses that have been posited for antique occult practices and their influence on the hegemonic churches of the Middle Ages (particularly with the real world occult revival that happened alongside the renaissance).

              Scattershot of possible ideas: the influence of Mediterranean mystery cults (filtered via Plato, Pythagoras, and the like) into the theology of the Church. The idea of sacred monotheists, perhaps tracing themselves back to the Egyptian cult of Aten, and their influence on the rise of the world religions. The concept of quasi-shamanic ecstatic religious groups present in Mediterranean antiquity whose ecstatic sacraments would become the partial basis for elements of baptism, Eucharist, etc. in later theology. The concept of mystery priesthoods dedicated to Mithras, Sol Invictus, etc. cross-pollinating with early Christians in Rome. The hypothesis that the black stone venerated by the Emperor Elegabalus may have had some connection with other stones used in the veneration of Canaanite gods, possibly making Elegabalus the first Roman Emperor to venerate ‘the god of Abraham’. Anything from the gnostic movements surrounding the development of the early Church tbh.

              Regardless of if one wants to make use of any of that, going later into history, one still finds plenty of juicy tidbits that one could mine for a hidden history of European Christianity. One could do something with the conceit of the crypto-Gnostic Cathars of southern France (beloved by occultists to this day) and the hypothesis they may have been influenced by the Bogomils of Eastern Europe and/or perhaps even a crypto-Islamic troubadour tradition which arose in southern France.

              Or one could do something more with the Knights Templar (who don’t get nearly enough juice in canon imo) in all sorts of ways: maybe in the WoD the Knights Templar really did adopt some sort of crypto-Islamic and/or crypto-Gnostic beliefs after arriving in the Levant during the Crusade period; perhaps developed because of their contact with the legendary Assassins of Alamut, which did happen to some extent even irl. Maybe they did discover some great secrets under the Temple Mount (which one could relate to ideas given above or Dan Brown stuff of what have you). Either way one can also use their (irl) highly developed financial systems (for Europe at the time) and posit their Awakened counterparts as an important historically progressive group helping to usher in the modern age. In that vein, perhaps one adds a bent of Awakened politics to their suppression by Louis the Fair.

              Speaking of their suppression: perhaps, as various occultists have suggested, in the WoD the suppression of the Knights Templar was not so successful as it was in our world. Perhaps remnants of the Knights Templar really did escape their suppression on the continent and go underground in the Scottish Highlands, where their secrets would later re-emerge as (Scottish Rite) Freemasonry. Perhaps, as some have suggested, some Templar really did reach the New World pre-Columbus, and used their greater knowledge to further their plans for a new world (and maybe left some buried treasure at Oak Island, have some have suggested). Perhaps in the WoD, this Awakened Knights Templar, or at least an enlightened core cadre, plays a crucial role in the development of what we would come to know as the Order of Reason.

              If none of this stuff interests you, there’s still plenty of irl cool stuff going on in pre-modern Christianity that one could mine for Awakened interest. Google the: ‘Clerical Necromantic Underground’ or watch the Dr. Justin Sledge/Esoterica YouTube video about it, it’s pretty fascinating imo.

                                                          This explains the other great "holy Graal" of Alchemy, besides the Transmutation of lead into gold. And that is the idea of the "Philosopher Stone".

                                                          Many have heard of that, but very few have any idea what it means.

                                                          The Philosopher Stone is, in fact, the very first idea about ARTIFICIAL LIFE. Ever heard about "Synths"? Androids, Biorroids (biological androids) and such Sci Fi tropes?

                                                          Well, the Philosopher Stone was... Something like that.

                                                          It was the idea of creating a "Stone" - therefore, an inert element - that had "blood", that had a "vital pulse".

                                                          A.
                                                          Stone.
                                                          That.
                                                          Was.
                                                          Alive.

                                                          That's right. That's the idea of a Philosopher Stone: to create a living rock. Something that blurs the lines between crude matter and life.

                                                          Hell, but that's a freaking robot! Hehe.

                                                          I once saw a brilliant video saying that our Smartphones are the culmination of the dreams of the Alchemists of old... We are, each one of us, carrying a "Philosopher Stone" constantly in our pockets; a piece of "rock" (made of several metals and silicates and plastics) that is nevertheless "alive" (it has vitality, pulse).

                                                          Again, the idea was the same, to leave the world of platonic "shadows" and reach the "real" world of forms.
