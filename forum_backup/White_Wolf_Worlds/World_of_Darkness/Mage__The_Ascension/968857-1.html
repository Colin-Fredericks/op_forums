                    Originally posted by Aya Tari View Post
                    How? It completely contradicts the cosmological of Changelings, Fera, and Kuei-jin.
                    And in turnabout, those contradict each other, as well as them contradicting Mage. That's the point. That's the *problem* when it comes to tieing everything together. It doesn't work.

                    In Mage, humans are the center of the Universe. In every other gameline, humans are not the center of the Universe, they are annoyances or prey.
                    And it also makes up the core mood, philosophy and style of the game. Crossover is not the default WoD Modus Operandi.
                    So Brother's statement stands: No matter which gameline you pick as the dominant one when doing crossover, you are shafting another's fluff and mood, at least in parts. And that is fine.

                    What isn't fine is suddenly demanding that one of the gamelines is 'right and true' for everybody, no matter what they preferred before.
                    Which is also why I highly doubt this:

                    Originally posted by Undead rabbit View Post
                    Besides is the inevitable destiny of the setting, with the 5th edition there will be only one world of darkness (as it is now by the way,as it is officially since the week of nightmares, but with the 5th edition this whole charade of "it's not the same setting!" will be thrown into oblivion forever).
                    It's *possible*, but I highly doubt it. it will not suddenly unify all the contradicting origins and themes.
                    One World of Darkness seems to be more about having the same specific WoD lores in all media (And the new WW seems to be all about spreading WoD in as many media as possible). So in a Vampire television series, you will get the WoD Vampire lore. No deviations, no 'inspired by' or 'based on'. In a Mage Novel, you will get the whole Mage mood shebang. in a Werewolf Movie, the writers will stick to the WtA lore instead of simply taking leeway and liberties. And the stories in those media flow right back into the core WoD.
                    That's what One World Of Darkness seems to be about.

                    But you cannot unify all the lore of all settings and not shaft one of the gamelines as written, hard.

                    Case in point, even Chronicles Of Darkness, which is touted as being much more coherent and unified, basically keeps that unification to having a central rulebook with all the mechanics that are common between the splats and basic mood set for the World Of Darkness in general. And, well, more fleshed out mechanics.

                    Beyond that you get the same old contradictions again, with things like the God-Machine (Oh how I love the God-machine!) not meshing with the Exarchs, Abyss and Supernal World of Awakening, etc.

                    I doubt One World of Darkness is striving to do better. It's, as I said, more about having coherence between different media. What they *are* planning on doing is making the big metaplot more player-centric, which kind of also lessens some of the problems, as huge, global things that contradict between gamelines might pop up less.

                    EDIT:
                    P.s. that said, I love CofD's Lovecraftian feel and horror. CWoD has become such a bland, non-horror Superheroesque Splat Brawl in parts. See: Threads like these.
                    Or heck, try to have mortal or mortal-esque characters meet a weird mystery in CWoD without the players going 'oh, it's a X'.
                    CWoD needs a big dash of 'There is more than the games presented' primers and suggestions. Another reason not to unify and melt down stuff to a common denominator.
                    If there ever is a unification of the WoD origins, none of the previous cosmologies and origin stories should be correct. None. It's what characters might believe, but all of them, all of it should actually be wrong, and the truth should be a mystery. That's the only way to handle it fairly between all splats and settings. And it might make things suspenseful and strange again.

                                        Christ - how many Mage vs. Vampire threads do we need?

                                        The primary goal of Mage, Vampire, Werewolf, and every other WoD/CoD game is to be fun to play. They all use some version of our real world, so it makes those games more fun if they assume actual real-world events are caused by major players in their game. So it's totally fine for a Vampire game to decide that the Giovanni were behind the Greek financial collapse, and for a Mage game to decide it was an enterprising Syndicate VPO who ate too much Paradox with his hypereconomic experiment. Those choices make those games more interesting.

                                        It doesn't really pay to try to answer all these questions with an optimal answer for all the splats. You end up with Week of Nightmares stuff, where everyone starts playing together with Technocratic space mirrors and Antediluvians and bodhisattvas and everything. That combo cosmology stuff walks a tightrope - at best it is kind of okay, and at worst we end up with 5000 forum threads about who can kill an Antediluvian.

                                        If you want to play a crossover game, it is up to you to decide what parts of Mage cosmology to use and what parts of Vampire cosmology to use. The games are similar enough to make it somewhat easy, and you can use the good combo bits to help, but they are local choices for your local game.

                                        To answer the initial question - the Syndicate doesn't care who has the money, they care that they care about having money - that it's perceived as having value. The Syndicate wants profitable trade; it doesn't need to own every dollar in the world. The Syndicate is much more worried about hippies who share their worldly possessions freely and say that "you can't own property, man." That attacks their worldview much more than any Giovanni.

                                                            I don't think that the Syndicate could solo the Giovanni so easily. Trying to ruin them financially would likely cause as much problems for the Syndicate as for the Giovanni and the Giovanni would likely be able to reestablish their wealth relatively quickly. The Giovanni have access to ghosts which would be incredibly difficult for the Syndicate to deal with and would allow the Giovanni to gain information on the Syndicate and attack them in a variety of ways. The Syndicate could assassinate some Giovanni vampires but I assume by "the Giovanni himself" you meant Augustus - I don't know if the Syndicate could kill him. Assassinations would likely cause reprisals and it only takes one Syndicate Mage to be captured by the Giovanni and Dominated/Blood Bond to open up all of the Syndicate secrets to the Giovanni and give them access to a servant with true magic. I don't even think that the rest of the Technocracy would let the Syndicate take on a major foe like the Giovanni alone, it would be like a single US state declaring war on a major foreign nation.

                                                            Now, if they are sure that the Giovanni and going to destroy the shroud between life and death, then yes it may be worth the risk and would certainly justify getting the rest of the Technocracy (and maybe even the Camarilla) on board with taking down the Giovanni. But unless the Syndicate see that as an immediate possibility, they are unlikely to go to war alone against such a powerful foe. It just wouldn't be profitable.

                                                            Of course, as haldir says, the game is about having fun and if you want to have the Syndicate take down the Giovanni in your game, have at it. Though I think that it would make more sense given the respective nature of each group and would likely be more fun as well if (given the Syndicate and Giovanni fight) that it was a more even and drawn out battle.
