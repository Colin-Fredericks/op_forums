    [Union games] How dark to make the Technocracy around you?

    10-24-2021, 01:29 PM
    I should note I'm primarily a Technocrat player. When I share this online, I tend to get a bit of disbeief because I often am the first in line to talk about Union abuses and making them a dangerous Orwellian organization. A lot of gamers assume that I want to make the Union one-dimensional or that I am a fan of 1st Edition Technocrats. Quite the contrary. The Guide to the Technocracy is my all time favorite supplement.

    However, it comes with the caveat that my games would be RUINED if the Technocracy's leadership wasn't evil. I am a huge fan of La Femme Nikita, Nowhere Man (The Bruce Greenwood series), The X-Files, and Day of the Condor. Which is to say that I am very fond of the idea the player characters are good guys trapped in a situation where they are working for the bad guys but there's no good alternative.

    Usually, when introducing the concept, I explain like such:

    1. You can be effective.
    2. You can be moral.
    3. You can be rich.

    Pick two.

    CT Phipps' Technocracy is the single most effective organization in the World of Darkness for holding back the night. Even more than werewolves, it is there to deal with incursions of ghosts, zombies, demons, Nephandi, Maruaders, and baby-snatching Lost Ones that don't care the Middle Ages are over. It is also guilty of state-sponsored terrorism, murder, brainwashing, torture, kidnapping, and genocide. The latter including plans to exterminate all supernaturals, their Kinfolk, Kinian, and followers. If you read the Technocracy's history, they're always one step behind virtually every Western colonialist power and believing it's all justified in enlightening the world. They're not like that anymore (albeit some still hold those views because your boss' boss is a former Dutch East India company executive) but that's because people PUSHED BACK.

    Can it be saved?

    Is it better than the alternative?

    Is it the only game in town?

    Part of what I enjoy about Technocrat games is that they're very useful for a lot of moral questions. One adventure may be to get take down a pedophile murderer Ronin Garou (The Nappy Napper from Project: Twilight) and the next to be to forcibly recruit a 12 year old Dreamspeaker that has been kidnapped from his family. The morality of the latter one is the point and seeing how players react to being told he's going through Room 101. Do they let him go? Do they erase his memory of his past lives? Do they willingly betray their fellow agent who wants to kill the "thing" in a body of a child?

    Like La Femme Nikita the series, the player characters are as much working against their superiors as they are the Traditions. You need to play the game, assassinate, gather blackmail information, accumulate power, and increase your magical power to eventually become head of the Union yourself because that's the only way anything is going to change. In WW2, the Union was supporting fascism until half of them revolted and destroyed the other half.

    Surely it's not going to take THAT to save it again, right?

    ----

    So how dark should the Union be if you're a Technocrat? We've got a lot of different versions of it with the horrible Null-B to the Guide to the Technocracy to Iteration X's apologia for the Technocracy.

              My personal problem is with the idea of a monolithic Technocracy. Simply put, I can't buy it. I know how big organizations work. I've studied them for decades and I've been working at one for 6 years while observing its inner workings.

              So, for me, beneath the surface few things actually apply to the whole Union. There are amalgams empowered to employ a small army of state-of-the-art HIT Marks at any problem and receiving frequent, detailed missions. And there are amalgams with an old carcass of a Mark V and a can of spray paint that they can make work sometimes if they try hard, and the only communication they get from higher ups is their salaries and semestral reviews limited to checking if any problem went to the local newspaper. If not, every one gets a free pen.

              Two key concepts to understand large organizations are Middle Management and Resource Allocation. Middle Management means that between the highest higher ups and the factory floor there are many cogs, and those cogs aren't just for show. Upper Management can't actually manage most aspects of the organization, otherwise this micromanaging overwhelms them easily. So most decisions that affect your life are actually taken by Middle Management and there is where the organization "happens". The most well-meaning Upper Management is frequently powerless in face of the very structure that allows bad Middle Management to happen.

              Resource Allocation means that enough resources isn't a thing for an organization, ever. It doesn't matter how much you have, your global work by definition uses up everything and some. You have to start new projects, you have to deal with logistics, you have to pay for your entire existence. And you have to send this money and other resources to every cranny and nook. All the while accounting for losses of all kinds, and counting, again, on a lot of Middle Management.

              Large organizations are fragmented and self diverse. Both their best and their worse are depending on where you look. They are also conflicted, but in a very complex way, not simply as "your boss is evil".

                          My Technocracy is still recovering from the disaster of the avatar storm. The Union Directorate (situated on earth, they are the "temporal" leaders of the union, an emergency committee formed by representatives of the most influential symposia, at least till contact with the Masters is reestablished) has its hands full with the recovery efforts, which leaves them with little time and resources to spare, something that they try desperately to hide. The illusion of Control must be maintained, or the whole Union falls into chaos.

                          The darkness comes from the fact that many amalgams are not sure whose timetable they're following. The chaos of the avatar storm has been used by some technocrats (nephandi, greedy assholes, and other malcontents) to turn their symposiums into their personal kingdoms. The Directorate can do little about it since purging a symposium would lower moral, expose a weakness to their enemies and leave the Union without much-needed resources. This is something that the rogue technocrats know and exploit, leaving the Directorate no choice but to reach a usually unfavorable agreement with them.

                          Constant paranoia is a must in a technocray game, but here is ramped up by the fact that, more than ever before, you receive contradictory orders from many sources and all claim to be the ultimate authority of the Union. It's hard enough to do your job without having to second guess every order that you receive because you could be harming the Union without knowing it. But even if you find out your boss is a traitor, what can you do? Hell, should you do something? It's your ass on the line... Then again, nobody said being an agent of a global conspiracy would be easy.

There's a group of them who want to totally extract the Appalachia Mountain Range and build huge Arcologies with the resources taken. In advance of gentrifying a city, they targeted youth likely to be affected - and thus in the way - and got them all hooked on steroids and amphetamines. They are attempting to involve a Tradition Mage in building a total-surveillance drug rehab facility so they can move the unwanted population out of the way of affluent tech workers (effectively human trafficking). A Progenitor Mage tricked a kid into drowning so she could resuscitate him by injecting a foreign organism into his lungs. She later apologized to one of the PCs, saying she was only following orders.

                                  I make antagonists rationally self-interested, afflicted by Quite (usually Clarity), ideological in the mundane sense and, as a last resort, evil (nature: sadist). On the other hand, many Union NPCs are pursuing genuinely positive goals and some are actually powerful. In a Technocracy Chronicle, I would prefer the players looking up at the top of the hierarch and wondering which bosses are evil rather than being able to simply conclude that the whole leadership is evil. The solution to pure corruption at the top is Revolution.

                                  It might be interesting to put the worst Technocrats all in one Amalgam in a city close the Construct from which the players are operating. They know the whole group is bad, but they bring in revenues, have a high standing in the Union and firmly control their territory.

                                      I don't need the leadership of the Technocracy to be evil, per se, I need them to be detached. I don't need the Technocracy to be evil, per se, I need them to be corrupt in specific ways.

                                      Plenty of forms of corruption plague the Technocracy, but I think there are three specific ones that define its dark side for me: Noble cause corruption (the pursuit of ideals to such a degree that you sacrifice ideals), institutional corruption (the temptation for the organization to protect itself at the expense of its purpose), and systemic corruption (flaws in the structure that encourage personal corruption).

                                      Between those three you have the push for individual amalgams to do monstrous things in the pursuit of technocracy goals, the inescapable reality of favor trading and graft, a leadership willing to ignore or even endorse both, and a resulting culture of silence. That's everything I need to explain most of the states the Technocracy can be in.

                                                          Point of clarification: only in Mage Revised is Jhor a form of Quiet. Before and after, Jhor is a kind of Resonance. This was, in fact, one of the motivations behind renaming the three quiets in M20, where Clarity became Denial and Jhor became Morbidity. Jhor could cause a Morbid Quiet, or not; the coupling between Resonance and Quiet is fairly lax. So even if Morbidity causes the mage to want to tear down the Shroud, that still doesn't mean that using Magick to kill means that you'll go Morbid.

                                                          Edit: I would also suggest that the Technocracy's tendency to rely on Devices means that agents are less likely to develop Resonance of any sort: an agent shooting you with a BFG-2000 isn't performing a Procedure or an Adjustment, and so technically isn't “using MagickScience to kill you”. The BFG-2000 itself, OTOH, is a different story.
