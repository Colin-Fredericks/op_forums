        No spell you are casting has quintessence as a mandatory requirement. What prime is used is if you seek to create something out of nothing (i.e. giant honking ball of explosive flame). You can use forces 3, but you need a source first - i.e. you could use Forces 3 to use a house on fire to create a vortex of flame that will murder everything in the street. Or you could make gas pipes rapture and ignite with Forces 3.

        Prime 2 is a "easier" source of Aggravated damage, but...
        a) you don't really always have to use aggravated damage
        b) Forces is more efficient, remember that it gives you extra damage for all effects based of Forces

        What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.

                                            01-22-2015, 12:01 PM
                                            Baseball bats don't do aggravated damage without Prime. Neither do bullets.

                                            Yes, Entropy 4 and Life 3 and all of that can be aggravated (usually vulgar). But for the low, low cost of Prime 2 (3 if you want to make them charms!) you can use a sniper rifle to deliver coincidental aggravated damage at 1000 yards.

                                            So while a mage who can rock your world with some direct magick is dangerous, a mage who can provide a team of sleepers with aggravated damage weapons and consumables is also very dangerous. Life 3 doesn't allow your acolytes to wield a night-stick that can hurt Vampires. Prime 3 does, if only temporarily. Prime 4 does, permanently. Prime 5 could let them lose the night-stick and deliver aggravated damage with their fists.

                                            Prime is still useful, even though it isn't the only useful Sphere.


                                            EDIT: Prime being used to make things aggravated isn't really aimed at magick, but at the mundane. By infusing something mundane with excess quintessence, it's kind of like driving nails into the end of a club... you are turning normal damage into magick aggravated damage. This really is a huge deal if you do a lot of crossover or "mage on the street" stuff, but less so if you are mostly focused on "mage vs mage" stuff.
