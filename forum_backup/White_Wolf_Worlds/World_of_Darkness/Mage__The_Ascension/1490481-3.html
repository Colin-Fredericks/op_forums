                While it's true that modern technology would have been developed anyway (and in the real world, it was), you do have to apply a little bit of suspension of disbelief. In this case, the idea is that in the world of darkness, modern technology would otherwise have taken longer to develop; and the technocracy is largely responsible for it having gotten developed as quickly as it did. Mage, or at least mages, tend to assume that Sleepers aren't able to do much on their own — that, as I mentioned in my last post, they're somewhat infantile, since they're not Awakened. In a particularly dark take on the World of Darkness, that may even be true to an extent. Certainly, Mage has a tendency to attribute a lot of real world developments to mages: the theory of relativity was the doing of the void engineers; quantum mechanics was invented by the Society of ether; LSD came from the children of knowledge (or perhaps the cult of ecstasy; that one's a little bit fuzzy). Heck, the entire imperialist March of the 19th century British Empire is laid at the feet of the late order of reason, early technocracy. Again, none of these are true in the real world. But Mage isn't the real world, however much it may look like it superficially. So allowances have to be made.

                In particular, it's bad form to choose to attribute all of the bad things that happened to a Mage faction without a tributing any of the good things that happened to them.

                                                            Kakost You are correct that any Mage can be a benevolent or not-so-benevolent tyrant and that many of them do. You are overstating it, though. There's nothing in the Mage condition that pushes them to be tyrants and it is not the only way that they can abuse their powers.

                                                            What they do have, and what we as humans have, is the strong tendency to assume their opinions are correct. Those opinions do not always lend themselves to tyranny. Sometimes they lead to the opposite problem, that of callously ignoring the world. Indeed, one of the common complaints about the pre-Traditions was that they didn't concern themselves with the world or the people therein.

                                                            Nor can we just say that changing the world (or for that matter retreating from it) is inherently tyrannical. The method also matters. Some mages try to convince the people to embrace the idea of a better world. Some try to coerce the people into accepting one they wouldn't otherwise accept. In typical Mage fashion, the Technocracy and Traditions complicate matters by doing both.
