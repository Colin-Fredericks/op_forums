    Pawn Shops to get around the Syndicate?

    06-02-2024, 08:24 PM
    So the Syndicate has its eyes everywhere (supposedly) and that is why trying to make gold or diamonds or emeralds and selling them to a jeweler or online is a big nono. However, I read a book awhile ago where the main protagonist had the ability to create gold and silver and could fix items to mint condition magically. To stay under everyone's radar, and hide the fact that he was a super, he bought a pawn shop which he then used to launder gold and to return antiques to mint condition to sell for a large profit. Later, he opened up pawn shops in multiple cities, as well as car lots that were filled with junkers he magically restored to mint condition and then upgraded, and made a fortune off of them.

    How plausible is it that a mage could get away with something like this? Is this the sort of thing that will set off alarms for the Syndicate?

      Having “eyes everywhere” is an NWO thing, not a Syndicate thing. The Syndicate operates on a more decentralized basis, with individual power players doing their own thing and upper management largely giving them free rein so long as they get their cut.

      And chances are pretty good that at least one of those individual power players will notice your “pawn shop” strategy. But don't worry about them coming to shut you down; that's not how the Syndicate operates. No; the thing you need to worry about with the Syndicate is much more insidious: they'll invest in your pawn shops, and attempt to finance them. Help you grow and to make more money for yourself. You're setting up a really nice Venture, after all; and the bigger it gets, the more Primal Utility they can extract from it. It becomes a win-win situation: you get rich, and they get powerful.

      And if you get on their shit list for any reason, they'll yank their funding, extracting as much Primal Energy from your Venture as possible as they exit, and watch the resulting Market forces collapse your business. (Read about what happens when someone extracts too much Primal Energy from a Venture; it's not pretty.)

      Now, one way to get on their shit list is to use Magick to literally make money, as with the alchemist who creates precious metals or jewels ex nihilo and sells them for a profit. But even there, the Syndicate is less concerned with how you made your money than with the likelihood of your stunt destabilizing the Market — or, more pragmatically, disrupting their own money-making schemes. That's when the Enforcers show up. Your pawn shop, or frankly any other money-making scheme that's subtle enough to integrate into the Market instead of disrupting it, won't attract that kind of attention.

              With all due considerations to fun in a game over strict realism:

              The second-hand market has pretty close eyes on it precisely because people think it's a good way to launder things (like selling stolen goods in pawn shops/etc.). Primary retailers (who have much stronger political pull since they have much bigger bank accounts) also push for greater regulations on the second hand market.

              So if you're operating a pawn shop, when you do your taxes for your business, you have to include what you paid for everything you then sold. If you're selling a lot of gold without buying any gold, or buying gold on the ridiculously cheap.. welcome to audit town, because you're going to look like a fence or a front for something illegal. And as a small business owner without an army of lawyers, you're an easy target compared to when big companies do shady things. There's ways around this, esp. for a mage,but they add time, energy, and complexity to the operation. You can go to things like storage auctions and "find" a giant stash of gold/etc. in it, but that only works if you're bidding on units regularly and you accept plenty of bust or low profit ones too. If you always find the ones full of high value items, people are going to accuse you of colluding with the storage unit owners or some other legal issues.

              As well, the more high value items you want to sell... the more of an online presence you need to have to turn a profit. People got to the second hand market for value, not rare/expensive items. If you want to move high value items, you need buyers to know you have them, and that frequently requires a significant reputation, and these days means putting up stuff like that online.

                              Overall not trying to control the market through magic. You can make good money with judicious use of Magick and a lot of old fashioned work. That's likely to not attract their attention, at least not in a bad way.

                              But dealing in money is by definition stepping in their turf. It doesn't mean they instantly know you're there, but it does mean you can't shine without them noticing.

                              Your best option, if you really want to be rich, is for your business to have as little as possible to do with your magic making. And definitely not to try to become rich through magical shenanigans.

                                  Or just be normally greedy. The Technocracy doesn't care if you get rich playing their games by their rules. They care if you start messing up their stuff.

                                  More broadly, if a mage wants money, you start with why. Mages are extremely capable of living off the grid if they want. They don't need money to get the things normal humans do. If mages don't need money, then they want money for something. And that goal/desire is going to inform a lot about how to get money without getting the Technocracy's attention. The more mundane the ends, the more mundane the means can be.

                                                  The strategy with pawn shops and the like is to know a few specialty markets and play those.

                                                  Precious metals are going to be thought of by all sorts of people, but did you know certain kinds of vintage costume jewelry are in demand? Certain styles of costume jewelry are in demand for the craftsmanship and the look. Recreating that look can be profitable for the Matter mage and unlikely to get much notice. Sure, if I waltz in with a mint condition copy of Action #1 eyebrows will raise. But there are all sorts of other comic books worth a few hundred bucks that will pass with little notice. Know the niches and avoid going for outrageous scores.

                                                  "Finding" (Entropy) a lovely set of matched carnival glass plates and drinking glasses could net nearly as much as a copy of Fantastic Four #1 with far less attention. Less attention means less need to hide in the first place.
