    04-23-2021, 01:04 PM

As I said elsewhere, this is something that is a non-issue after it's destruction or loss at the start of the 20th century, and possibly wasn't a big deal at some point early on in Batini history.               (With the caveat that I've no idea exactly what Victorian Mage specifically covers and in what detail.)

              Two things:

              Hawaii (and possibly the rest of Polynesia). I mentioned this one in my Ages of Darkness thread, as it is the transition period when Hawaii was an independent kingdom (and something of a client state of the British) before eventually being overthrown by American business interests. Besides the fact that its a place where a number of Traditions (East, West, and Native) can exist together, and a potentially interesting place for the Technocracy, it's also a great reason to discuss the Kopa Loei craft and what they are doing during the time period.

              First Wave Feminism. Growing out of the 18th century works of people like Mary Wollstonecraft (the mother of the author of Frankenstein), Judith Sargent Murray, and others, and from the organization of the abolitionist movement, this is the era of people like Elizabeth Cady Stanton, Susan B. Anthony, Victoria Woodhull, Matilda Gage, Sojourner Truth, and many others advocating for women's suffrage, changes to divorce, property and inheritance laws, and the ability to attend certain types of higher education. This ties in with various changes to the medical establishment, with Florence Nightingale'a efforts to overhaul the practice of nursing and other aspects of hospital management, and the first recognized women medical doctors. Also the Temperance Movement, female missionaries, and other religious issues, and the continuing tradition of women scientists from the Enlightenment Era. This sort of thing ties in with almost all of the female centric/exclusive sub-factions within the Traditions (such as the Sisterhood of Gabrielle and the Kiss of Astarte), especially the Verbena. Also the Sisters of Hippolyta.


              (Honestly, it'd be kind of amusing the have a Victorian Mage sourcebook entirely based around the original Book of Crafts, looking at things like Egypt and the post-Napoleonic Egyptology fad, Christianity in the 19th century, Victorian era pharmaceuticals and apothecaries, the First Wave Feminist movement, the Chinese Diaspora and European imperialism in China, the Kingdom of Hawaii and other Polynesian regions/cultures, and the Afro-Caribbean/Afro-Brazilian cultures of places like Haiti and the Empire of Brazil. Maybe also some of the original WoD: Sorcerer stuff with West Africa, the Mormons, what's going on in the Yucatan and Central America, the Order of the Golden Dawn, and possibly Theosophy as a forerunner of the Thal'hun.)

                          I don't remember the Lions of Zion being mentioned. There's been a few issues with the Craft since they were introduced. There's been a whole lot of talk over social media to get an Umbra treatment for VA Mage, as well. One thing I'm trying to get my head around is how the Shard Realms were treated before their physical planet counterparts were "discovered" by mundane science. Was there a Pluto before the 1930s? And think about this, Uranus can technically seen by the naked eye or with basic optics, but ancient observers never even considered the possibility of another planet. I think that someone could have. Even if not, the seeds of a story are planted. I would also like to see how much the Umbra has changed/is different from the modern era. The Middle Umbra Near Realms would be much the same, as would the Underworld. The High Umbra might have undergone a previous change though. My own personal theories on the Umbra have been developing along such wide tangents as well.

                          There should also be a deeper dive into Hedge magicians. With the high degree of occult societies around, I would think there would be a greater number of sorcerers and the like. Many paths and practices would be slightly different than today's. More of these smaller groups could also be a focus for Crafts, especially the budding Hollow Ones. This would also be one of the last great ages for Bygones. With such strong themes of exploration and colonialism, the need for a last great exodus wold be a great theme. One last look at these being before leaving earth as it were.

                              • Eighth Xhosa War

                              • Crimean War

                              • Seminole Wars
                              • Anglo-Persian War
                              • American Civil War
                              • January Uprising
                              • Franco-Prussian War

                              The era was rife with wars, many of them with long lasting consequences and many of them seeing a clash between what might broadly be called a technocractic warfair style and other forms of conflict. Any such war might suck in the PCs.

                                      The Victorians tried to build multiple Utopian comunities. This wasn't only a US thing. A small supplement on utopian projects, what kind of people built them and where would have serious gaming possibilities.

                                      Since areas of lower population densities with less Technocratic influence would have weaker or no paradox, maps of which areas of the USA had what level of paradox when would be very gamable. As an example, New Orleans would have had lower paradox than New York City or Philadelphia, with some of the nearby bayous being paradox free up until after the civil war. Large areas of the Apilachians and Ozarks would have been paradox free into the early 20th century.
