    [M20] What happens when someone dreams?

    09-02-2024, 05:38 PM
    I'm not exactly well read on Changling: the Dreaming lore, but what happens when someone falls asleep and dreams? According to MtA 20th rulebook, they enter Maya, the Dream Zone. Then someone sleeps, their dreams take form, and goes away when they awaken. So is this Dream Zone the Dreaming? And how are individual dreams separated?

      It may or may not be, there's actually no official answer on how to reconcile the descriptions found in each splat.

      But the Dreaming has a very distinct description, and overall exists independently from humans. Most of it isn't a dreamscape, but a reality of its own, and Changelings need specific powers and proximity to a sleeping person to enter a dream.

      It is possible, though, if hard, to find and enter a dream through the Dreaming, at least in theory, and/or exit a dream to the Dreaming.

      So, if the Dreaming is Maya, then it is bigger than Mages know it. If it isn't, then some connection is still possible, but not a default state.

          The below is based on my understanding of how things were originally presented, but as the different games evolved, it is possible things changed and current rules and setting say something different.

          In World of Darkness terminology, a Zone is an inherent part of the structure of the Umbra, but they are hard to define or measure. Zones have their own laws. They bleed into many different aspects or layers of the Umbra. So while they permeate the Umbra, they are also separate and distinct from them. So by definition the Dream Zone is a fundamental part of the Umbra, but very hard to define. It is simply a mystery. (There are other Zones that just the Dream Zone, but there is no need to get into them here.) Mages refer to the great totality of everything in the Dream Zone as Maya or the Dreaming.

          Individual dreams are a product of a chimare. When people fall asleep and dream, they create chimares which are small umbral domains in the Penumbra. These individual chimares link to and are part of the Dream Zone. Someone who enters a chimare, or even the dreamer herself, can theoretically enter the greater Dream Zone from that dream. So individual dreams are separate to their individual dreamers, but also connected through the Dream Zone. But trying to find a specific dream in the Dream Zone is like trying to find a specific air bubble in the ocean.

          The Dream Zone seems to be a place of imagination and creativity. Besides the dreams of humans, the Dream Zone also includes the dreams of other beings, probably also including the Celestines and other umbral denizens. They definitely include the mysterious Oneira, the dream lords about whom we know very little. The Incarna of the Stargazer tribe of Garou, Chimera, is one of them. We're not given much more information about them, but they probably act much like Morpheus in Neil Gaiman's Sandman comics.

          Within the Dream Zone are the Dream Realms, shards of imagination. Each Dream Realm is a long term, perhaps sometimes permanent, realm that exists in the Dream Zone. As more and more people believe in the ideas or thoughts behind it, that Dream Realm becomes more stable. So an individual person's dream disappears forever after they wake up. More potent dreamers who continually create the same kind of dream might give birth to a small realm that persists even after that person wakes up, and when they dream of it again, they revisit that realm. Such realms may last the lifetime of such dreamers, but likely disappear some point after they die. When a person shares their imagination and creativity with others and thus have more people thinking of it, those realms can attain an existence of their own, being constantly fueled by the imagination and dreams of other people. So long existing folklore, popular culture, legends, stories, and so on may exist in some format as a dream realm as well. If aspects of those beliefs are forgotten or changed, those dream realms likely begin to change or degrade over time.

          It is believed that once individual dream realms become so mythic, so fundamental, so omnipresent, that they become lodged into the Near Umbra. That's where we get the Near Realms of the Garou (which the Mages call Mythic Realms). There are likely equivalent realms that lodge elsewhere, but we don't have any data for them.

          Only if you keep travelling further and deeper into the Dream Zone, do you get into realms of the Far Dreaming which don't seem to be built by human imagination. Instead, they either represent something metaphysical or are creations of the Oneira. This is where Arcadia would be.

          So the Changeling Dreaming is not the Dream Zone, but it is somehow connected to it. The term, "the Dreaming", is used by multiple groups, but don't always mean the same things. When spoken by Mages, it tends to refer to the entirety of the Dream Zone. When spoken by fae, just their corner of it which is the fairy otherworld which could be one of the stronger and long lasting dream realms, or perhaps something else that is strongly connected to it. So it is part of the Dream Zone, but would represent only a small part of it (simply because of how vast and powerful the Dream Zone is).

          Personally I would say that the fairyland we call the Dreaming is a creation of one of the unnamed Oneira, perhaps in collaboration with Gaia. I don't think that has ever been stated though.

              It is highly possible that "Maya" is like a "Penumbra" of sorts to the Dreaming. Mages can enter Maya, Mages can enter the dreams of sleeping people, but they are still subjected to the laws of Glamour - meaning, Mages will still forget interactions with Changelings, Chimeras and the Dreaming in general, as if they were mere sleepers.

              Sure, Mages CAN use effects (mostly Mind) to shield themselves and keep their memories, but as soon as these effects are over, so are their memories.

              That seems to simply that, although connected to Maya in some way, the Dreaming is a distinct thing, or at least a distinct part of the whole. Perhaps Maya is the surface, but the Dreaming is the core of it.

              The way I like to envision it is that Maya is a reflection of the Dreaming that serves as a superficial envelopment of the Dreaming, while the Dreaming proper is the "essence of Dream-stuff", the source of it.

              It is theoretically possible for Mages therefore to travel from Maya to the Dreaming, but first, it is hard, this is a VERY weird zone, and second, they keep forgetting everything (or a good chunk of the travel) once they return.

              I'm also of the theory that both Mages and Changelings have a barrier of semantics in the way. While I do believe that "Maya" is the "surface" while "The Dream" is the "core", I think that Mages call ALL of it ("Maya" + "The Dreaming") as simply "Maya", while the Changelings call it all (again Maya + Dreaming) as "the Dreaming", just to add to the confusion, born out of ignorance and necessary understanding to be able to make that subtle differentiation.

                  Originally posted by Magus Maximus View Post
                  It is highly possible that "Maya" is like a "Penumbra" of sorts to the Dreaming.
                  There's one thing I think needs to be said here.

                  Regardless of how Mages deal with things, from the Changeling's perspective there's no "Penumbra". That's not how the Dreaming works.

                  While the contact between the Material and other Realms is usually through a Penumbra, a mirror reality, the contact between the Dreaming and the Material is the Material itself​​​​​.

                  Chimeras, the manifestations of imagination made of solid Glamour, do not form in an adjacent realm, they inhabit the same plane as everyone else. It's just that they can't easily interact with matter while people are looking.

                  So, Changeling's cosmology works like that:
                  1. Chimerical Reality exists as a superposition to everyday reality. It's in the same place, and interact as normal. By the way, Chimeras are perfectly solid even if invisible to normal eyes;
                  2. The Dreaming itself is another realm. The Near Dreaming reflects some aspects of the Material world, but still diverges far more than a Penumbra would. It does have contact points, though;
                  3. Beyond that is the Far Dreaming, and beyond even that's the Deep Dreaming.
                  Maya could work as a Penumbra filled with Dream Bubbles, but in that case the Changelings and Chimeras wouldn't usually interact with it. Which may very well be how it works.
