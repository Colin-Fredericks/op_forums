    What would you like implemented into M5?

    04-27-2023, 01:14 PM
    Me personally, I would like it if they brought back the Foundation and Pillar system from DA:M, of course updated to fit the modern age. I would also like it if they could simplify the Umbra a bit to make it a little easier to understand, for example, combining the Underworlds and Astral Reaches into one and give an explanation of how there is no difference betweem Mind and Soul, or something like that, maybe move aspects of the underworld such as Grandmother to the Middle Umbra. I think it would be cool if they made reality less defined by consensus and encouraged the players to uncover the truth of reality by solving occult mysteries.

    This post actually explained how the Pillar system could be implemented into MTA: https://forum.rpg.net/index.php?thre...pheres.889868/

    What about you?

          Trying my best to put my WoD5e pessimism aside:

          1) Foundations, not Pillars, are the things to take from DAM. Foundations give Arete more flavor by Tradition/etc. helping new players to more quickly understand what the rather broad groups of Mage are about. Foundations can be a much more refined implementation of the Predator Archetypes from V5: a carrot to play your character in a specific fashion by getting some bonuses while doing things their preferred way.

          2) Not mess up stealing Mage: the Awakening 2e's much cleaner implementation of the casting rules. The 13 Practices that are universal so each Sphere has the same broad progression makes it much easier to do free-form magic on the fly, Attainments to better handle some meta-magic mechanics,and making rotes actually matter, are all big things to help make Mage more accessible and more updated to modern game play. One roll casting is also a big improvement, as is putting way more of the Paradox risk on player choices to casting riskily rather than arguments over witnesses.

          3) Realize that there is no point to Ascension without real world cultures, and figure out a way to handle that. Trying to do what they're doing with W5 where the Garou are global pan-cultural phenomena doesn't work in Ascension. I cannot see M5 succeeding if they try to make the Traditions more like Awakening's Orders, and thus ruin the conflicts that make Ascension matter as a game.

              I am quite afraid they will indeed remove the consensus reality element of Mage for M5 and that would be a tragedy as it is one of my favorite elements of the game. The whole struggle between the Technocracy and the Traditions really only make sense in the context of a physical reality that is subject to (or even completely determined by) the consensus.

              Instead I would highlight that while Mages know reality has a consensus component, they don't really know how or why it works, they don't know why some things are more resistant to it than others, and they don't know what it means. That leaves plenty of room for occult mysteries and meaning while still letting both the Technocracy and the Traditions be a thing.

                          First and foremost: a major goal that I would set for M5 is a drastically reduced page count compared to M20. If you can get it down below 2e, even better.

                          That means that you need to be extremely choosey about what goes into it. That alone is reason not to include anything about Foundations or Pillars in the core book. Ditto with things like the Technocratic Sphere variants and Minor Spheres: if anything like that gets included at all, it should come in supplements.

                          The tight page count also means that two-page spreads for every Tradition, Convention, and Craft is a non-starter. Instead, I would reserve the two-page spreads for Arcane Approaches, as shown in Mage Made Easy: they're kind of like Awakening's Paths, but as something that the mage chooses rather than being something pushed on him but a mystical Watchtower or Avatar or whatever. The Societies, meanwhile, get reduced (in the core book) to one-paragraph summaries that specify which Approaches are common in that Society and what makes that Society unique. Basically, complete what M20 started and shift the focus away from the Societies and onto the individual mages: rather than the choice of Society being the thing which defines what your Magick is like, the choice of Society becomes almost an afterthought — something that you can potentially skip over without hurting the character.

                          For the Magick system, borrow heavily from Awakening 1e (which was written essentially as “what we would have liked to write for Ascension of we could have started from scratch”), with the main changes being:

                          1. Practices already has a meaning in Ascension, due to M20; so find another name for what Awakening calls Practices. Likewise, other terms use their Ascension counterpart names (e.g., Arete instead of Gnosis; Spheres instead of Arcana; Effects instead of Spells).

                          2. Entropy instead of Fate and Death. For better or worse, it's deeply rooted in Ascension. That said, I have no problem shifting things around a bit as to what each Sphere deals with.

                          3. Quiescence (the tendency of Sleepers to forget any Magick that they see) isn't a thing. As tempting as it is to keep it, that's always been one of the things that differentiated Mage: the Ascension from Changeling: the Dreaming.

                          4. Borrow the Yantra mechanics from Awakening 2e as a game system for a mage's Instruments, with the tweak that

                          5. The notion of the Supernal and the Abyss gets stripped out. Not that you can't do it that way if you want to; but if you do, it's because that's your Focus; not because it's the fundamental truth about how reality works.

                          ​​​​​To the extent that the subjective nature of reality gets downplayed, it's only in the sense that that's also not guaranteed to be the absolute truth of the setting. I would introduce out in the write-up for the Traditions, as a description of the mechanism that they use to forge bonds among the individual Traditions.

                          That is, it's no longer guaranteed to be true; but it still might be true. Which, ironically, makes it more true to the concept than actually making it an absolute truth.

                          Rework Paradox around Subtle/Uncanny/Extreme instead of Coincidental/Vulgar/Vulgar with Witnesses. Possibly remove the concept of Witnesses entirely; if not, downplay it to an optional rule. Or ditch Paradox entirely, and replace it with Resonance: Paradox is what some mages call Resonance that bites you. If you go with this approach, there should be four types of Quiet corresponding to the four kinds of Resonance, rather than three that correspond to the Metaphysical Trinity.

                          I'd also downplay the Metaphysical Trinity as a holdover from Mage's earliest days as a sequel to Werewolf, and instead play up the Avatar Essences, kinds of Resonance, and types of Quiet in their place.

                          Finally, shift the presentation of the Magick system away from the Spheres and onto the Focus: for instance, put the Focus into Step One of the character creation process; and reference it whenever any aspect of the mage's Magick comes up. You choose your Avatar Essence with your Focus in mind; you choose your Spheres with your Focus in mind; whatever Rotes you know are chosen with your Focus in mind; and so on.

                          The first supplements should be sourcebooks for the Traditions, the Technocracy, and the Disparates. In addition to going into much more detail about the individual Societies in those books, this is where you introduce mechanics that are “Society Secrets”, like something akin to the Technocratic Sphere variants — although personally, I'd handle those as Merits that take the existing Spheres and tweak what the character can do with them.

                                      Looking at the structure of V5 and thinking of it in terms of how it would change if it was written for mages rather than vampires:

                                      Beliefs definitely incorporate what M20 calls Focus, seeing as how Focus is all about what the mage believes about Magick. Predator Types would be replaced by something like M20's Practices: rather than how you hunt prey, they're about how you work magick.
