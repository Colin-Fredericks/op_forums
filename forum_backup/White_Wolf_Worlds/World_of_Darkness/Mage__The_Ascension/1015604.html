    Teleportation and Coincidence

    12-11-2016, 04:54 AM
    I tend to assume that teleportation of yourself or another person can't be done Coincidentally outside the Umbra and Sanctums and the like. Moving an object from one concealed location to another would be Coincidental under HAP, but the idea that someone could step into a walk-in closet in Toronto and Coincidentally come out of a walk-in closet in Rio de Jinero never seemed right to me. I think I've figured out why. When I think of a Hypothetical Average Observer observing a teleporting mage, I don't think of them just being in the departure point and arrival point--I think of the Observer as going along for the ride. And as soon as they step out of the closet and into a setting where the buildings are different, it's going to be obvious that something unnatural happened. Even if you say the HAP is only in one location, an ACTUAL observer observed themself teleporting.

    This leaves the possibility of Coincidental teleportation to areas built to look identical, as long as the traveler stays confined to these areas until they go back to their original location. This could facilitate in-person interaction of mages from around the world.

          The way How Do You Do That? handles the matter of teleportation is that when it says "Hypothetical Average Observer", it means a hypothetical average human being who is in the room. For this reason, it states that so long as the HAO can't see the Mage wink out of existence, and there's a reasonable chance they could have left the area through mundane means, it's coincidental. Darkness and thick smoke or fog are very useful in the former regard, and a room simply having a window or other door (or corners one could duck around in the case of more open areas) can apply to the latter.

          For instance, I'm trying to escape some punks, and I duck into a nightclub bathroom. There's a window in the back large enough for a human to exit through, so I flip the lights off and do my thing. The punks storm in, someone turns the lights back on, and then they spend a few futile seconds scouring the area for my missing ass. No one saw me disappear, and it's entirely possible I effected my escape through the window. Coincidental. It would be even easier if it were in the dead of night, outside, where I could run off into the darkness and, for all intents and purposes, give any pursuers the slip.

          This is also why Mages would benefit from smoke grenades. They aren't nearly as loud as frag grenades, and they provide plenty of cover for a magickal getaway. Flashbangs too, given the bright flash and deafening sound. By the time any Hypothetical Average Observer rubbed sight back into their eyes, the Mage would be long gone, even if they "walked right past them".

                          The problem is the very concept of HAO. It is extremely vague, and worse, it doesn't make a lot of sense. It does make sense that an actual observer causes Paradox, meaning that Paradox comes from defying Consensus. It does make sense that a certain degree of "Reality Bending" causes Paradox without an observer, meaning that Paradox comes from defying local Pattern. It does make sense that pulling a trick the Mage proper fear being observed causes Paradox, meaning that Paradox comes from the very anxiety of the Mage.

                          But HAO doesn't make a lot of sense. It isn't dependent on any, absolutely any IC quality, but on the very fact that the game is a game. The only measure of a HAO is the OoC observation of the scene. How does it fits with Mage? It would be interesting to see a game that works on such terms, or a given Mage's Paradigm working on such terms. But Mage doesn't seem to me a game that calls for that kind of meta-narrative by default, except on the rules for Paradox alone.

                                  Two examples as food for thought.

                                  Think of the Professor's "Transported Man" trick from The Prestige. Two doors in their frames, facing the audience, maybe 10 feet apart. He bounces a ball and steps back through one door, and emerges from the other door in time to catch the ball. Let's set aside how it's accomplished in the movie (as it is half of the major reveal at the end) and assume he's some sort of mage and is using Correspondence 3. Is it coincidental? The audience is expecting to see unbelievable things. It's possible he's doing some mundane stagecraft. I'd argue it should be coincidental, but that says more about how much I prioritize setting and expectations of the witnesses when making that decision.

                                  From Dark Ages: Mage, the Ahl-I-Batin have set up many-pillared rooms in several major centers of Islamic power through the world (I always imagine Dwarrowdelf from LOTR). If you walk a very careful path through the pillars, you will emerge from the counterpart in another city - for example, from Baghdad to Cordoba). In game effects this is a permanent Correspondence - no wait, Al-Hajj 4 effect. Is this effective multiportal coincidental? I'd say yes, because the room is complicated enough that no HAP could be sure the mage wasn't already there when in fact he was just in another city. But I'm guessing most people will disagree with me here.

                                                      It's so subjective it's hard to make those calls though.

                                                      To step outside of Teleportation, since that seems to be the usual kludge for coincidental vs. vulgar, here's a situation from our game last night.

                                                      Our Hermetic was walking willingly into a Syndicate stronghold (because, you see, it was run by his long lost relatives and they asked nicely....) The group had the whatever-he-is-now technomancer (started off as a VA but a Technocratic Union VA from the 40s.... he's either becoming a Marauder or a Son of Ether at this point) give the Hermetic a souped-up ear bud. When he crossed the threshold, it went dead (of course), so the VA/SoE immediately started using his technomancy to reconfigure the ear bud at a distance (so that it used ultra-spacial gravo-metric gradients to carry the signal... or some such nonsense).

                                                      So.... I had to make a call... coincidental or vulgar? The VA/SoE stated that he was physically changing the ear-bud from a machine that worked one way to one that worked another. I ended up deciding it was vulgar with no Sleeper Witnesses because it was in the Hermetic's ear. The Hermetic felt the ear bud get warm, buzz and wriggle around a little.

                                                      But what if the Hermetic hadn't been wearing it? He took it out a few minutes later at the polite request of the Syndicate goon... I mean security guard (since they had detected the VA/SoE's manipulations... they didn't know what had happened, just that something had). What if it had changed when no one was touching it and no one had any reason to assume it wasn't a normal ear bud? Since there's no retroactive Paradox, if it looked like a normal earbud how would they ever know it was different if they didn't use it or take a look to see if it was made differently? (And what if they didn't have the Technology to know what ear bud innards are supposed to look like?) It allowed for two-way communication using bone conduction as well,,, but you can't SEE that it can do that)

                                                      Later on he reconfigured it again, so he could project his voice through it at normal volume. (Sorta a tragic thing where he wanted to talk to his long lost mother, with whom the Hermetic was conversing.... since she was also the Hermetic's long lost daughter.... it's a chronicle fraught with a lot of complicated family relationships... and time travel.) Was it the sound projection from an "ear bud" that was vulgar? Or was it having a machine reconfigure itself at a distance so that it could do that?

                                                          HDYDT so commonly gives bad advice. I'm not saying the Batman wouldn't work, but you really have to take everything that book says with a grain of salt. The whole thing about not being seen is questionable.

                                                          The thing is, even in HAP you usually can't hide from the hypothetical observer. There are ways but they usually feel like they are pushing the edges of the allowable. If there is a vantage point one can reasonably sit and say WTF from, it is Vulgar W/O Witnesses. So he question isn't how do you hide yourself from the Hypothetical Observer, it is how do you make every potential Hypothetical Observer think something normal happened?

                                                          People get disoriented with sudden light changes, so you change the lights. People lose track of each other in a crowd, so you run into a crowd. If an observer could reasonably believe that he just lost sight of you in that instant, congrats, you've reached coincidental for the departure. If an observer could reasonably believe he just hadn't noticed you before, congrats, you've reached coincidental for the arrival.
