    Psychics vs Sorcerer

    10-17-2023, 12:42 PM
    Hi.

    Mages are too powerful for my purposes. I prefer working with the Sorcerers and Psychics in the WoD, but I'm not sure what the difference is between the two regarding feats. What are the pros and cons of playing one or the other? Is it that Psychic feats are more immediate, but Sorcerer feats are more long-range? Help me out here!

      Psychic powers tend to be specific and binary (either they go off with one role, or they don't). This makes them better for on the spot powers, but lacking in versatility or ways to build up effects if they need to push past their current limits.

      Sorcery powers tend to be broader, and with more gradation in terms of what they can do and how well you do. This makes them better for ritual powers that can cover a lot of bases, and get boosted with more resources, but less capable of using anything that's not prepped ahead of time on short notice.

      There's also a setting conceit where psychic powers are frequently more "wild" and finding teachers or groups that understand and can train psychics is harder, while sorcerers tend to have much longer standard organizations to join and gain access to training and support. This is not inherently necessary to how their rules function, but is a consideration (and it does stem from how they tend to operate).

          Another difference is one of "Feel".

          Psychics tend to just require the power of their minds to cast Effects. For this reason, the power itself is far more centered in the character, and what they use it for. So you're allowed to run a "generic Psychic", in the sense it could be anyone and have most any power.

          Sorcerers, much like Mages, are deeply reliant on a pre-defined Focus (Paradigm, Practice, and Instruments). The act of doing Sorcery requires the props and trappings relevant to the Sorcerer's magical style. This might be Witchcraft, Hermetic High Ritual, Weird Science, Martial Arts, etc. HOW the Sorcerer performs the Effects is as much, if not more important, than what Effects they do. There is no "generic Sorcerer". You have to define what kind of Sorcerer they are. This provides more opportunities for creating memorable NPCs, but also demands another layer of work to flesh them out. Figuring out how not only the Paths they know and their backgrounds, but how those backgrounds intersect with and inform/are informed by their magical style.

          Being a Psychic or Sorcerer implies different things about how the character acquired their powers. Psychics get away more readily with just having been a natural talent. They might have their abilities unlocked somehow, but it's very easily to say they were just born with it. A Sorcerer, meanwhile, must almost always learn their magic externally. Whether this is through personal research, reading old books, having a Mentor, or being initiated in a Sorcerer organization, group, or society. Either mode implies a great deal about the character and what their deal is, which must be considered when designing them.

                  Originally posted by Squiggles View Post

                  Well, Heavy Arms already mentioned it, but sorcery is more versatile. Aspects allow you to tweak a Path effect to your needs. You can use teamwork to cast stronger spells. You can use Rituals (which is probably the most significant difference between the two imo). The downside is, it's complicated and increasingly difficult to cast. Focus might get in the way of teamwork. If you play by default rules, spells may take several rounds to cast.

                  Psychic Phenomena are less complex, but also less versatile. On the upside, they don't care about Witnesses and scale gradually in both power and difficulty. Focus isn't a concern, what counts is just Willpower. Effects are almost always instant.

                  It didn't come up for me yet, but if I had a psychic player in my group I would probably have a good look at importing psionic rules from Trinity.

                      It matters a great deal what tone and power level you're going for.

                                              “Eruption” is never used for psions; it's specific to Novas. Even if you insist on psychics Awakening as something other than mages, I'd still use the term “Awakening” for it.

                                              I'd also aim less for psions than for psiads (those being the ones who Awaken without Prometheus Chambers, but who also don't have the “single Aptitude” restriction that psions have); and depending on which edition you use, I'd either use the Freeform Techniques from the first edition's Trinity Player's Guide (starting on page 161) or the Aptitude Shaping from the more recent edition's Prometheus Unbound (starting on page 162).

                                              The advantage of going with the first edition is that the underlying game engine is more similar to Mage, making the work needed to make the two compatible a bit less.

                                              Finally, you could make psions (as opposed to psiads) a Technocratic or Etherite thing: there's a Tasty Bit for Trinity Continuum: Aeon called The Phoebe Trust which details a 20th and 21st century organization that has developed some imperfect ways to unlock a psion's potential and Awaken him or her, at a cost.

                                              My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
