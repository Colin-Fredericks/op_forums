    How Broken would a Phenomenologist style character be?

    01-15-2017, 10:22 PM
    So, for those who don't know, Phenomenologists were one of my favorite Baramins from the fan-game Genius the Transgression. Part of the Lemurians, they believed the idea of logic itself was simply incorrect. That objective truth didn't exist. And so they could switch out their entire belief system as was convenient for them.

    Combining the merits Poker Face, Perfect Liar, and Hollow Soul. Alongside a focus on the mind (And to a certain degree time and Entropy) sphere seems like it would do the most justice to them... but would this concept be... for lack of a better word broken? Given their paradigm is more or less that "There is no such thing as objective truth, and so why not let the subjective truth be whatever is most convenient for me at this moment."

      I'll be honest, it's a pretty flimsy paradigm. If your beliefs are so malleable that they can change to suit any given scenario, are they really beliefs at all?

      In a way, it's sort of like applied nihilism. However, to say "I don't believe in the objective, everyone and everything therefore is right" doesn't translate to "I can use Science! Channel the energy of God, Allow the forces of nature to speak through me, and tap into the secret geometry of the universe".

      I'd say such a Mage would be more of a "Jack of all trades" at best. They'd think anything which did magic is as good as anything else, but they'ed be pretty terrible at doing any of it due to a lack of focus and coherent internal logic.
      After all, why would they practice any one thing when it's all the same?

          I agree with Saikou. Even Chaos Magick, a Practice known for its cosmopolitan methods, requires the practitioner to decide on something. They may believe that any tool is theoretically valid for doing magic, but they still need a reason for why that is so. They might believe that symbols, when charged by mental attention, gain leverage to change the world. They might believe themselves psychic, in which case Instruments are merely tools to focus that psionic might. They could believe in any number of reasons why their eclectic style works, so long as there is an actual reason. If anything, their Paradigm is all the more important.

          One of the parts of M20 that I adore is its Focus section. And when it talked about Paradigm, it had a list of bullet points showing that, while humans can believe in anything, not all beliefs are equally useful as a Mage Paradigm. This passage is particularly relevant:

          Originally posted by M20 p. 571
          Magickal Paradigms Demand Strength: Mages use their paradigms as levers that move the world. A weak lever – say, belief that Mickey Mouse is God – will break under that kind of stress. As a player, then, avoid facile or ridiculous paradigms. Your mage lives and dies by his beliefs, so those beliefs – and the people who accept them – must be strong enough to hold up under pressure. Sure, you can argue the fine points of Pastafarianism and the exact commandments of the Flying Spaghetti Monster, but will they help you survive a civil war? Probably not.

                  The problem I have with that is conceptual. Belief simply does not work that way. If you can choose what you believe as easily as flicking a switch, what worth is there in your beliefs? If your beliefs have no staying power, then they can't possibly be strong.

                  Belief by its very nature takes time. I had a friend who was agnostic and had a very christian family to whom they felt somewhat alienated from due to their difference in belief. One day they decided to stop resisting and start accepting christian doctrine in their life. They really wanted to be a part of their family again, get away from this divide, but wanting it, not matter how strongly they wanted it, wasn't enough to believe the messages of the bible. It took time. They needed to figure out the scripture for themselves, recontextualise it in a way that made sense to them, and get used to the amount of acceptance that comes with such an endeavour. Years later and they are a regular churchgoing christian without need for agnosticism to this day.

                  In a way, they shifted from a uncertain sense of disbelief, or unbelief, into a strong sense of belief, and it took a great deal of work to do so. They often describe it like this. Their faith was sort of like a boulder, they were unable to push it or move it directly. But by shaping the land around it, they could manipulate it into rolling down to a different place.

                  If a belief can change so easily, then it can't be strong. If it's a strong belief, it can't be changed easily.

                  The only way a character with a paradigm such as that stated would work is a marauder, to whom the normal rules do not apply.

                      Even if we assume that a person's beliefs allow them to bend into any Paradigm (a huge assumption), there's the matter of whether they CAN employ any Practice or Instrument. M20 had Minimum Abilities as an optional rule, but I personally think it's only logical for a Mage's ability to act upon their Beliefs be gated by their practical knowledge and skill. You can't hack reality with a computer if you barely understand how computers work (having no dots in Computer). Nor will belief in the efficacy of mystical kung fu do much for a person who can't throw a punch to save their life (no dots in Brawl, Martial Arts, Melee, or Do). Forget trying to whip up a concoction with a set of lab equipment if you have no dots in Science (Chemistry) or Esoterica (Alchemy).

                      The Phenomenologist might be able to get away with having such a schizophrenic Paradigm, but they wouldn't be able to get very far with it. Even if he could claim any Instrument as one he can use, does he have any idea how to use them? A character like that would need to spread all his Ability dots thinly, to cover the widest selection of Talents, Skills, and Knowledges. A situation that, with Minimum Abilities in play, would mean he'd at best be able to cast Perception magick, and not much else. Like Saikou said, a jack of all trades, master of none. The only way for their Focus to be useful is if they either took a phenomenally long time gradually raising their Abilities one at a time - all of them - or have them concentrate on one area. And at that point, they're already moving away from a universalist Paradigm to a focused one.

                      Like I said earlier, even Chaos Magicians have to have areas of expertise. While the section in M20 detailing the Chaos Magick Practice says they could use any Instrument, it's more to indicate they could choose any given set of Instruments as their tools. They still have to choose one set of tools over another.

                                      Originally posted by Genius the Transgression, pg 341
                                      Humanity has toyed with non-rational philosophy for centuries, from Chinese or Sufi mysticism to European Romanticism to American Transcendentalism, and geniuses have followed along, delighting in mundane philosophies that reflected the Inspired experience. However, during the late 19thth and early 20thth centuries, these kinds of philosophies reached their peak in Europe. The results in the mundane world were devastating, but to many geniuses, it was a siren-call: no longer would mundane humanity strangle Inspiration; instead, a combined World Spirit of Inspiration was being born; soon it would grow and transform the world utterly, wiping away contradiction and consistency, replacing it with a new concept of truth.

                                      Phenomenologists are drawn from a philosophical tradition, though unlike the Scholastics, their philosophy is linked completely with political and social agendas that change constantly. They are fundamentally irrational, not like madmen or idiots are irrational, but in the way that only very smart people can not make any sense. Phenomenologists treat logic and evidence with contempt or disinterest, instead building makeshift worldviews from bits of rhetoric, sustaining those ideas for as long as they are convenient, then discarding them as effortlessly as they believed them. Always one step ahead of their own contradictions, Phenomenologists can be found in any field where connections, rhetoric, and cults of personality are strong enough to replace traditional philosophical or scientific inquiry. They find their way into social sciences, philosophical groups, and the humanities; recently, some have nestled into mathematics, where they work to stay one step ahead of someone standing up and saying, "Wait a minute, those equations don't make any sense." Some have even migrated into theoretical physics.
                                      It is worth pointing out that Genius is a loving send up of Mage, and the factions in it are satires of some of the mental gyrations necessary to make Mage work. It also spoofs the trends in scientific thought over the years.

                                      That said, if you want to bring Phenomenologists into Mage there is a way to do it. Instead of using their belief in subjectivity to switch paradigms on a dime, they can use their belief in subjectivity to ignore major logical inconsistencies in their own approaches. They do still form theories on the fly, but those theories are based on myriad psuedoscientific principles that they've studied extensively. If those quick and dirty theories don't hold up to scrutiny the next day then "oh well, you can't expect consistency when you are working at theoretical levels like these."

                                      And you can just about imagine members of several traditions saying something like that, can't you?
