    Collaborative Casting

    11-09-2016, 06:25 PM
    So I'm going to outright say that this is a house rule thread up front. One thing I don't like about the collaborative casting rules as is in the system is that at least one mage needs to have all the spheres necessary to do the effect themselves in order for mages to work together to do effects. I much prefer the idea that mages should be able to work together to use their strengths to compensate for an allies weaknesses. This especially becomes a problem for me when it comes to big things that might require mastery in multiple spheres in order to do. I'm running a game and I have told them that I will allow this. They will still require knowing at least one dot in each of the spheres used but it's possible for example to have a player with Mind 5 and Life 1 one to work with another that has Mind 1 and Life 5 to create some creature. I'm just curious about any ideas any of you have to keep a balance with that. Like how successes should be counted and so on.

      Some points:

      1- There is already a Merit that does something like this, called Twin Soul, or something. You should consider it, or at least consider how this rule can affect this Merit.

      2- Your system will raise humongously the options available to a cabal, and make it for optimization both by your PCs as by the world at large. If it works that way, so there is no sense in not encouraging overspecialization and the individual broad knowledge that is cornerstone for Mage becomes a weakness. A chapel with 9 Mages, each a master of one Sphere, becomes able to do absolutely anything with ease.

      3- Before you institute this rule, think about breaking processes and projects. Some things can be accomplished by letting each Mage be responsible for one fragment. Lets say you want to build a sentient super robot. At first, you need Matter (for the body), Forces (to give it animation) and Mind (to make it able to think). But if a mage create the body, another makes a electrical system able to feed this body with motion, and a third makes for an AI in a drive that can be installed at the body, you effectively created the robot with three Mages, without any being able to do it individually. This process is usually, for me at least, more rewarding and as efficient as using the collaborative casting.

          I would allow this as an ST with the caveat that the each of the mages know one of the spheres at the level needed and the other spheres involved at minimum of two lower than required (lowest required being 1).

          So if three mages wants to do a spell that requires Mind 5, Life 5 and Matter 4 one mage needs to know Mind 5, Life 3 and Matter 2, One mage needs to know Life 5, Mind 3 and Matter 2 and the last mage needs to know Matter 4, Life 3 and Mind 3. If another Sphere was added at 2, one of the Mages need to know it at 2 and the others at 1.

          This allows the cabal to cover each other's weaknesses but still need to be fairly good at all the Spheres involved in the spell.

                                  I agree that there are some things that could be changed about collaborative casting - but I would not go as dramatic as to say 'Each Mage only needs to know one sphere of the whole effect'.

                                  That, in my opinion, is way too simplyfying it.

                                  I think each Mage participating in the collaboration would need the spheres to achieve *their part* of the effect.

                                  Example: Two mages want to create a whirlwind and lock it onto a moving target in a single round.

                                  Mage 1: Uses Prime+Forces to create a whirlwhind out of nothing.
                                  Mage 2: Uses Forces+Matter to lock the whirlwind onto the pattern of a moving car to follow it and make for one hell of a bad driving day.

                                  Mage 1 needs both Prime and Forces to create the tornado, but lacks matter. Mage 2 lacks Prime to create forces out of nothing, but they have Forces and Matter to lock the thing Mage 1 created onto the pattern.


                                  So in a way that's a hybrid approach, that makes things more flexible if the Magi in question have practice with casting effects collaboratively.
