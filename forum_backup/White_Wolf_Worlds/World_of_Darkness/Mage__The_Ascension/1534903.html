              I can give you my approach. Fair warning, this is what I came up with as I was beating my head against the wall over the terrible ward writeups. It hasn't come up in my games and so I haven't playtested it.

              Trigger Conditions - Wards and Trigger spells are confusing because the depend on something that is never given rules: sometimes spells need to be able to sense or determine or in some other way restrict themselves to their intended targets. Trigger spells need to be able to determine when the trigger conditions happen. Your ward needs to be set for Vampires.

              My method is this always takes the relevant spheres at rank 2. Setting triggers does not involve transmuting, altering, or in any way affecting a pattern so the rank doesn't vary with the target. Instead it functions like sensory magic. Rank 1 is sufficient to detect anything appropriate to the sphere. Rank 2 is sufficient to add that to a spell. Setting triggers against iron is Matter 2, against cats is Life 2, and against Vampires is Life 2/Matter 2.

              Wards - By default Correspondence 2 can set wards that inhibit or enhance the actions of targets within the ward (difficulty modifiers) and Ward 4 can actually bar entry or exit. Anything else requires adding that capacity. Because this does affect the relevant patterns it requires the Spheres at the appropriate level to cast the effect being added to the ward. SEP fields (Somebody Else's Problem) require Mind 2, for instance, and fire attacks require Forces 2 and an appropriate source of fire.

              Unlike teleports this does not require higher levels of Correspondence for higher associated spheres.

              So your vampire ward is Corr 2 (for the ward), Life 2/Matter 2 (to set it for Vampires), and Forces 2 or Forces 3/Prime 2.

                      Originally posted by quelsec View Post
                      It could be future scrying. So you transport the spell in time till something happens.
                      Indeed, in which case the trigger needs to be set for Vampires, and I use the same Rank 2 guideline for that. Indeed I use that guideline for situations where a spell has a special interaction with the domain of a sphere but doesn't alter the domain or use any of the powers of that sphere. Locking is another case of this, and M20 is pretty consistent on using (Pattern Sphere) 2 for locking.

                      Which reminds me. Since wards need to last for a while it is a good idea to lock them to avoid concentration issues. Time trigger spells already bypass concentration issues.

                      When do you roll for arete? when you create the trigger or when it triggers?
                      You roll arete when the spell is cast. So you roll at the creation of the ward or the time trigger, not when they activate. The idea with both of those spells is that they exist whether they are actively doing something or not. The mage doesn't need to carry around the components for them use his casting action for the turn to fire them off.


                      Are wards vulgar?
                      Vulgarity, as it pertains to casting difficulty and paradox accrual, is determined by what the consensus thinks of the spell when it is cast. Because Wards don't tend to have visible effects at casting, they are usually coincidental. Reality might object when they start getting active, but that objection will manifest as unbelief rather than paradox points or backlashes.

                                      Originally posted by quelsec View Post

                                      Can you share?
                                      Of course. I didn't expand because I thought you were interested in the rules as written, not in house rules. But in the case of wards, they are so vague and poorly written that I do recommend people to hunt for some good house rules instead.

                                      One thing to note is that my own tables play with heavily modified rules, I have basically re-wrote all of the Spheres because I utterly dislike the utter mess rules they have, but I'll try to stick as close as possible to raw.

                                      What Ramnesis wrote above is actually pretty close to what I use, which just some minor modifications; for instance, I consider Vulgar Wards (those that "unleash" Vulgar effects when triggered) to be Vulgar, even if nobody is watching (it's the difference between "Vulgar with no witnesses" and "with witnesses").

                                      Second, under my house rules, all Triggers require Entropy 2. It actually requires a combination of Entropy 1 + Entropy 2, but because Entropy 2 already obviously contains Entropy 1 already, there's no need to state Entropy 1 also.

                                      I'll explain: Entropy 1 is needed to "identify the circumstances", meaning that it is what the Ward uses to "identify" the targets. So, if your ward is supposed to activate exclusively on targets that are angry and wearing a red cloth made of cotton, then it is Entropy 1 that will make that selection and exclude targets that don't fit the parameters.

                                      Then Entropy 2 is the actual "Trigger", basically causing the spell to "fail" when the designed circumstances are not the ones specified. Entropy 2 therefore works as a sort of"switch on/off" for your ward.

                                      According to RAW (rules as written), Triggers require Time 4 and Entropy 2, but I have always disliked that, first for the massive Spheres requirements - you'd need to have Time 4 just for that, which isn't cheap, and while Entropy 2 is a bit easier, that's just the Trigger, you'll still need whatever other Spheres to actually create the effect.

                                      Also, that never made much sense for me. One of the problems that I have always thought about in this game is that the writers almost never explain WHY or WHAT each Sphere level in a single effect actually does. Their explanation on what Time 4 is actually doing there is pretty vague at best and nonsensical at worst. "Holding the spell in time" doesn't make much sense if you can just simply spend some successes in extending the duration of it.

                                      In the case of Wards, it is mandatory to have SOME time extended, since I doubt that a single turn ward would serve for anything. Since the main use of them is to protect a Mage's sanctum, most of the time they would probably be made permanent, and since most of the time a Mage's sanctuary will be fine tuned to his own Paradigm, this means that those will be Coincidental anyway.

                                      Time 4 would only make sense for Triggers if it were combined with Time 2, as in "hold the spell in suspension until X time", but that's never hinted or explained in the text of the rules.

                                      Besides, I don't really see this as a spell being "held on hold", particularly because you are already using Entropy 2 to cause it to "fail" under pre set conditions. So, imo that's just an unnecessary addition. The spell is not "on hold", but rather is selectively affecting different targets while being "always on". It's no different from creating an instantaneous fireball that only incinerates pregnant women (wow my own personal mind, what kind of example was that?)

                                      So, I always like to explain the reasoning behind every choice of Spheres for an effect, forgive me if that sounds a bit prolix. But in my opinion, that's what truly helps others to use the Sphere system, by comprehending how to build up their own effects based on what each level does in detail. I prefer that way rather than just saying "yeah, just use Entropy 2", because then you wouldn't understand the reason as to why that is.

                                      Anyway, Entropy 2 for Triggers, and I hope my explanation has made a good case as to why.

                                      Then you just simply add the desired effects. Like Ramnesis said, a "simple" "You Shall Not Pass!" is Correspondence 4. But you could also build an "Invisible Wall" using Forces 3, but in this case, an intruder could actually force his way in using his muscular strength in an opposed roll, because with Forces 3 you would be creating a sort of "Invisible Hand" that pushes intruders out. Correspondence 4 however creates an area of "Infinite Space", therefore it just can't be forced in.

                                      Other effects, just add the Spheres as if you were throwing the effect without being a Ward, then add Entropy 2 and the successes required for the amount of time that you wish it to last.

                                      For example, a Ward against "Bad Thoughts" that causes anyone that enters the area to enter a state of peace of mind, would require Mind 2 (Mind 2 is basically "Empathetic Magic", or the manipulation of emotions).

                                      A Ward that burns intruders would require Forces 3, Prime 2 and 1 point of quint; alternatively, you could use Prime 4 so that you don't need to constantly feed it a quint point for every single cat intruder that it burns.

                                      Ramnesis claimed for example, that to target a Vampire you'd need Life2/Matter2. I disagree in most cases.

                                      I also don't think you need anything other than Entropy 1 in order to "discriminate targets". Sure, Entropy 1 can NOT tell you if a coin is made of gold or lead; however, it doesn't need to. You don't need to identify metals for Entropy. Entropy detects "Destiny", therefore it is able to discriminate basically anything, without telling you what it is.

                                      Let me try to explain: you'd need Life 1 to determine if an individual is a male or female. Because Life 1 actually allows you to "see" the individuals' morphology, genetics and so on.

                                      Entropy however, will just state "0% or 100% chance of being female".

                                      You can't detect emotions with Entropy 1, for that you need Mind 1, but you CAN use Entropy 1 to determine, in a room full of people, which one is the angriest of the mob.

                                      So, you don't need anything other than Entropy 1 for the, let's call it, "Lock On Target" effect required for the Ward.

                                      Thus, you need Entropy 1 for the "Lock on Target" part of the effect;
                                      Entropy 2 for the "Conditional Trigger" part of the effect
                                      And then whatever other Spheres for whatever other effects you wish to integrate.

                                      But what about targeting Vampires? Sure, you need Life 2 + Matter 2 to affect Vampires... But in my opinion, that is EXCLUSIVELY for effects that specifically target their Pattern. What I mean by this is, if you want to launch a "vampiric flu disease" at them, or if you want to shapeshift them, then you'd need Life2/Matter2 in addition to whatever other effects; but NOT if you want to read their minds or rip off their souls (with Spirit 5).

                                      What's more, if you're just throwing a fireball, or in the case of a ward, incinerate vampiric intruders, then no, you don't need Life2 Matter2 combo, because the "target" is actually a "fire in the hole" being shot at where they are at. So, a simple Ward of Entropy 2, Forces 3, Prime 2 + 1 quint (or Prime 4 and no quint) would suffice.

                                      Finally, the Arete roll, number of successes, amount of damage, duration, reach and any other considerations about the magnitude of the Ward, those are all done when the Ward is created. You create the Ward in a ritual and stipulate how many successes you'll need and how many successes you want to expend on duration, damage and so on.
