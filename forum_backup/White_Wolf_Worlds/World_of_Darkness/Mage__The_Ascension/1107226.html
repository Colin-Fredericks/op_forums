    Space! (tellurian vs technocratic paradigm)

    06-24-2017, 02:11 AM
    So hey folks,
    Here's something that's been bugging me, I hope you can help.
    I've not got very far into the book of worlds yet, but from everything I've read so far, there's no such thing as space, only the umbra, and the technocratic paradigm defines the umbra as space.
    Now given the fact that the penumbra is meant to be the first realm one reaches after stepping sideways, how come nasa goes straight from earth to the horizon?
    Actually that's just one issue I have, while overall I just have real cognitive dissonance. As a "sleeper physics graduate" in our world, I can think of tons of issues with the idea of space just being the umbra. I'm looking to try and get a grip on how it works in WoD please!

    Is everything space agencies like nasa do fakery to convince the masses and therefore create space?
    Does the technocracy police every child with a telescope?
    How does Kepler spot exoplanets and look at different stars if everything outside our solar system is the far umbra?
    How come sleepers can do so much in the way of astrophysics if it's all meant to be the umbra?

    Waaaaa! Etc.

    Thanks in advance.

          Ah, the umbra. The single most confounding aspect of the World of Darkness

          The thing to keep in mind is that, in Mage, the Umbra shifts and changes constantly depending on the assumptions of the viewer. You've probably already come across the videre of the penumbra, that's one example. Mages such as Hermetics, Virtual Adepts, and the Akashics will see the Astral Videre, and even then their individual perceptions will be slightly varied, The Akashic might see the spirits of their ancestors, and perhaps a couple of dragons floating about, the Hermetic will see a number of angels, and the Adept may see the numbers that code up the Matrix (it's all paradigm specific).

          Dreamspeakers and some Verbena will have a more consistent experience, seeing the Videre Spiritum and having a similar experience to the Garou when they look through the penumbra. while Euthanatos and certain Hollow Ones will instead see the very surface of the Shadowlands of Wraith.

          This same principle of paradigm based access holds true for the rest of the umbra as well. While the penumbra videre are all in the same location, the rest of the umbra is a bit more dramatically detached.

          If you manage to make it to the middle umbra, also known as the Spirit Wilds, the more technologically minded of us may have a hard time accessing all of it. The Astral Heights, for example, are completely cut off. Any Adept that tries to fly above the clouds of the middle umbra, instead of seeing the great pantheon of spiritual incarna, they will go directly into the cold and dark stratosphere. Space as defined by the Technocrats.

          This is explained a bit more in both the Infinite Tapestry and the revised Void Engineer's book. Suffice to say that the Technocracy never really experience the exact same kind of space as other mages, and the more technomancer type mages are likely to experience a different space themselves.

          Space works exactly how you believe it to work. If the mage thinks it'll be a vacuum up there, then it will be. If they think you can breathe the ether, then they can, if they think they can literally walk through the stars or along the pathways provided by the gods, then they can.

          Humanity sees what they expect to see. If they've lived all their life thinking that space is what the Technocrats think it is, then that's what they see. Children might see things a bit different, but then they've always had rather over-active imaginations

          Things get a bit more complicated the deeper you go, but the long and short of it is that there are many different layers to space, and like the videre, they are all in the same place, yet not quite.

              There's more on space in the Void Engineers Convention book. In essence space as we modern folk know it is called Conventional Space in Mage. Conventional space is... essentially areas of the Umbra the Technocracy has conquered and forced to follow the laws of physics. Most of what space agencies like NASA do is provide human eyes on the subject, which extends the Anthropic Effect out into the Umbra, which forces the Umbra to follow the rules, which creates Conventional Space which is then Seperate from Umbral 'subspace'

              In previous editions, Conventional Space was HUGE, extending over a large chunk of the galaxy. But as of revised and the Dimensional Anomaly/Avatar Storm most of that space was just... lost. Chewed back up by the Umbra.f

                      Originally posted by Eyrelliah View Post
                      I've not got very far into the book of worlds yet, but from everything I've read so far, there's no such thing as space, only the umbra, and the technocratic paradigm defines the umbra as space.
                      The Book of Worlds discusses "consensual space" - the Earth-realm parallel to Umbral space, on our side of the Gauntlet - on page 138. As we are on the Earthrealm side of the Gauntlet, our telescopes show Consensual space, in the same way that we cannot look through a microscope and see spirits, and non-Awakened vessels stay within Consensual Space (and, as Enginseer noted, actually help to expand it by thickening the Gauntlet) for the same reason a plane leaving Earthly Des Moines lands in Earthly Tulsa.

                      (You may be getting tripped up by the metaphysical position of the Book of Worlds, which treats the Earth Realm as just one of many options, rather than drawing a stark "Real vs. Umbral" divide.)

                      Space has a very thin Gauntlet, to the point of near non-existence in places, and at the periphery of Conventional Space (the Far Horizon), it basically fails altogether. This doesn't mean that Space ceases to exist beyond that point, merely that the barrier that separates that Realm from others becomes fallible. CB:Void Engineers notes that (as per the telescope example above) we just can't perceive all the nonsense happening beyond that point (most of the time.)

                      Now, in addition, Technocratic experience of umbral Realms ("subspace") has generally hewed closer to their expectations of Earthrealm physics. This is why they use rocketships to go to the Far Horizon instead of psychedelic mushrooms, and why in the Near Horizon they have historically required protective equipment - their experience of that Realm of subspace is that its physical conditions were akin, but not identical, to the physical conditions of the parallel Earthrealm space.

                                          I've evolved my own interpretation to replace the one in the books.

                                          Back when the Order of Reason first got going, the proto-VE was divided into two groups - the Celestial Masters and the Void Seekers. The Void Seekers traveled the mundane world, discovering new lands and peoples, and claiming them in the name of wherever they came from. The Celestial Masters explored the Umbra instead - first by sailing ships "straight" rather than following the curve of the Earth, and therefore up into the sky, and later by flying airships directly upwards. So - their method for Umbral travel was simply "go up high enough".

                                          Originally, the Celestial Masters thought they'd reach some lovely Heavens or whatnot. Of course, they didn't - they found all sorts of Umbral nastiness, like creatures alien to their experience, and started to doubt their mission. But eventually they keep going, now with lots more weapons on their skyships. So - you don't just find the Umbra you seek, you find the Umbra that is there; otherwise they would have actually reached the Heavens they sought. But you can travel to the Umbra by whatever method works for you.

                                          Reformed later as the Void Engineers, they discarded the ether/phlogiston theories of the Sons of Ether and instead decided that beyond the Horizon (that is, beyond the atmosphere) was a dead vacuum of space. So they set out to make it so. They went on expeditions in their voidships and formatted whatever was in that Umbral realm before into dead, empty Conventional Space. Then conventional human space exploration went into those formatted zones to reinforce the Consensus belief in that - and they use the Consensual method of getting there (going up high enough). So the VEs go out further all the time, formatting Umbra into Conventional Space like you would an old hard drive. As they do so, they encounter Tradition mages whose realms are built to accommodate them without all the space suits and nonsense that the VEs come dressed up with - a Dreamspeaker around a fire, for example - and they fix that right up so that being without a spacesuit there is deadly. They also encounter loads of Umbrood that they dispatch with plasma rifles and such. It's sort of like Making Space Safe for No One, a scorched-earth approach. They see the farther parts of the Umbra only as sources of danger. As they go, they set up Constructs as military outposts or things to trade to other Conventions to get more plasma rifles.

                                          There are of course other areas of the Umbra. The Near Umbra, not beyond the Horizon, is more contested and the VEs don't currently plan to make it quite so homogenous as Conventional Space; completely deadening it tends to have deleterious effects on the actual world of which it is a reflection. The Low Umbra creeps most VEs out and they typically avoid it (especially if you include the Avatar Storm and its sparking events). And naturally they aren't done with the Deep Umbra yet, and they lose ground to Nephandi monstrosities, Marauders, or Ethership armadas just as often as they gain it.
