                My ideal list:

                1. Twenty years after the fact, the Avatar Storm is finally subsiding. Breaching the Gauntlet is still dangerous if one attempts to cross unprepared or in an RZ unsuited to one's paradigm, but it's no longer nearly fatal to all but the most powerful and accomplished mages.

                2. Threat Null proves itself highly dangerous in the Umbra, but they stick to their own devices and aren't the existential threat the VE's originally thought they were. Later observation and research confirms they're essentially Stasis' answer to Dynamism's Marauders and Entropy's Nephandi, and at least for the time being they're perfectly happy out in the Deep Umbra codifying and solidifying reality beyond the Far Horizon.

                3. The Technocracy doesn't self-immolate after all. Cooler heads prevail, and yet another Housecleaning and Reorganization occurs which includes a pretty thorough inquisition and Nephandic purge of the ranks. The powers-that-be don't talk about it (mainly to avoid humiliation), but what's clear to anyone with a brain is Control had been infiltrated which was the source of heightened tensions potentially leading to civil war.

                4. Instead, the Technocracy changes its core mission and splits into two divisions along the Loyalist/Utopian divide. The core mission, as always, is to create and enforce a global Consensus that trends towards scientific and technological advancement to benefit everyone, but with allowances for cultural and regional reality zones when those values aren't in direct conflict with the Technocratic consensus, and pose no substantial threat to Sleepers. This calls for a shift and lessening of the Pogrom, which becomes a mission to investigate reality deviance on a case-by-case basis, identify whether said deviance represents a threat, and then negate threats when discovered. Utopians' mission is outreach, especially to non-Technocratic mages to establish a state of detente, and to spread the Technocratic baseline; Loyalists' mission is to enforce the Technocratic baseline in reality zones in which it already exists.

                5. Outwardly, this appears as if the Technocracy really has split into two, with the "good" Technocrats parting ways amicably (for now) and a whole new phase of the Ascension War on the horizon, since the peace will not hold. Really, it's a divide-and-conquer strategy aimed specifically at destroying the Traditions by appealing to its newer and younger mages, who trend towards paradigms friendlier to the Technocracy but also lack the centuries' worth of distrust and hatred. Most shockingly, the ploy works, and younger Tradition mages welcome the opportunity to work with "rogue" Technocrats in an environment where all are free to share knowledge and technique, and benefit humanity in their own ways...

                6. ...which causes the Traditions to detonate. For older and more conservative Tradition mages, this is unspeakable treason, evidence of Nephandic influence, a sign too much has been conceded to the Technocracy already, and everything else wrong and bad with the world. They're absolutely correct in their summation of Technocratic intent, but the younger, more naive and idealistic, mages can't see it and refuse to listen. Lines must be drawn in the sand, and younger mages have to be saved from themselves lest a new, even more oppressive and dangerous, Technocracy comes to be. Older and conservative Tradition mages congregate, and collectively offer an ultimatum: you're a mystic or a technocrat, no more fence-sitting. And just like that, the Traditions cease to be, because the younger mages and technomagi tell the old farts, whose leadership led them down a path of destruction, stagnation, and oppression for centuries, where to stuff it.

                7. Meanwhile, the Loyalist/Utopian "split" proved itself simultaneously the Technocracy's biggest success waging the Ascension War, and its biggest fuck-up. A lessened Pogrom means less Conditioning and control over Utopians, and the influx of now-ex Tradition mages means a two-way exchange of information, values, and methods. Utopian Technocrats realize they were pawns in a long-con to destroy the Traditions gone horribly right, and they're already so far off the reservation that were their realizations to be discovered the Loyalists would go after them. Idealistic and naive Tradition mages who jumped ship realize they're now considered enemies of the mystics, and there's no going home again.

                The only way forward is together, especially since these mages realize they were the moderating influence on the excesses of Technocracy and Traditions, and Loyalist and Conservative alike are now free to reinvigorate the Ascension War with a heretofore unexplored ferocity. Thus, the Order of Reason is (informally) reborn, and they find themselves fighting a clandestine two-front war.

                8. On the mystic side of things, a complete shitshow ensues. The Order of Hermes tears itself apart and dissolves into its constituent Houses, many of which join the rebranded Order of Reason, both of which calling themselves the true Order of Hermes and declaring Wizards' Marches on each other. The Dreamspeakers, who were never very cohesive to begin with, vanish overnight when they disband into regional, cultural, and religious crafts. Verbena and Choristers fare little better, as the groups on the fringes simply walk away. The Akashics, Euthanatoi, and Ecstatics are cohesive enough to remain intact, and the Mercurial Elite manage to pretend they're mystic enough (nobody understands their work anyways) to stick around. The Society of Ether simply says fuck this, and jump ship for the new Order of Reason.

                In the end, the remnants of the Traditions decentralize, eat their big slice of humble pie, and gravitate towards the Disparate Alliance. The DA's don't exactly welcome them, but need prevails and they're willing to cooperate so long as the ex-Tradition mages don't act the damn fool by trying to take over and reform the Tradition Council. Obviously that's going to happen, but for now the Traditionalists are behaving and trying to make allies of old (and new) Crafts as best suits their regional, cultural, and strategic need.

                So, final analysis...

                First, you have the Technocratic Loyalists, who are the Technocracy as always published. Five Conventions, Methodologies within those Conventions, yadda yadda. For now they think the new Order of Reason is on their side, but it's only a matter of time until they discover how far off the reservation they've gone. To them, now that the Traditions have been divided and conquered once and for all, and the RD's capable of reform have been separated from the RT's, the Disparate Alliance is the bigger threat. They're still the biggest, meanest kid in the playground, all the stronger for having purged Nephandic influence from the ranks, but that just means if the balance of power shifts they may cross the Godzilla threshold and look to Threat Null for answers.

                Then, you have the new Order of Reason. They reorganize to accommodate defected Traditionalists, not by paradigm but instead by operational focus and strategic role. Ex-NWO work alongside Quaesitori to ensure operational and informational security, Syndicate enforcers work with Euthanatoi to investigate and bring fraudsters and other criminals to justice, Iterator fire teams accommodate Verbena medics and special forces, and the like. It's uncomfortable, it leads to fuck-ups and friendly fire from time to time and it barely works, but it works, and neo-Order mages realize more and more even if they have different methods and hate each other for those methods, their common ground is wanting to do right by humanity which makes them stronger together than their constituent parts. They just have to survive internal strife and a clandestine two-front war long enough to build a solid foundation for a cohesive whole based on that common ground.

                Last, is the Disparate Alliance. The lessening of the Pogrom and shift of priority to establishing a global Technocratic baseline Consensus, with allowances for regional/cultural variation in reality zones, has given them a lot of freedom to rebuild and grow their paradigms and practices. Together they're smart and experienced enough to know trying to flip reality zones is a bad idea, but on the other hand they no longer have that need. The problem is, the Traditionalists are reeling from younger mages' mass defection to the Order, but fail to see it's led to something truly new.

                At the same time, many resent having to work with the Crafts as equals (perish the thought!), or as part of a decentralized global network of Crafts absent a vertical power structure, and want to reform the Tradition Council. More want payback for centuries of oppression, and want to turn that inch of concession from the Technocracy into a mile. The big question for the Disparates is, now that they have the freedom to practice and seek personal Ascension which is what they said they want the whole time, can they reign in their baser impulses as a sect to follow through or will they kick start a whole new chapter in the Ascension War?

                Mechanically, the one thing I'd love to see is a reformulation of Foundations/Pillars as opposed to Arete/Sphere. I love how freeform the latter system is, but on the other hand it can get really goofy at times, like for example how Akashics need Prime to do anything with chi, despite their being a Mind/Life Tradition and how few if any chi-based Effects actually need Prime mechanically-speaking, or the same phenomenon with Wu Lung and Spirit. And, how easy it is to get lost in the weeds figuring out what sphere level a given Effect may be, and what interplay conjunctional Spheres have with determining Effect level -- like for instance how one can make an area-effect fireball with either Forces 3 or Forces 2/Correspondence 5, or how doing "direct" damage with Entropy is Entropy 4 despite the fact every level before it can be used as part of a damage-dealing attack to some degree. I perhaps wonder if moving to a Foundation/Pillar system, based on minor spheres or somesuch, might be a preferable option for preserving player freedom while mechanically reinforcing the importance of paradigm.

                            While MtAs is still a WoD game with all the "crapsack world" status that implies, it's still fundamentally different than its sibling games in that it carries what the designers have always deemed an "uplifting" message, which just translates to however shitty WoD is, there's still hope for a better world. That's nothing new to anyone who frequents the forum, but it's important to keep in mind as a framing device for exactly who and what the Nephandi are, and what their narrative role is. That being, the Nephandi cannot and will never win, so to speak; their narrative and thematic role is to keep the sects of MtAs teetering on the knife edge between damnation and salvation, preserve the balance of power by keeping the sects from ever realizing their true, best selves, and thereby indefinitely suspending any forthcoming answer to the key dramatic questions of Mage: is Ascension truly possible, who can achieve it, and what does it mean to Ascend?

                            The problem, and the underlying brilliance, to the Nephandi in this is they're a narrative redundancy. The sects are their own worst enemies; the Technocracy wants a world of collective Ascension at the cost of personal freedom, the Traditions want a world of personal Ascension at the cost of collective safety, and all sects are too entrenched in their own belief systems and values to free themselves from their own baggage. The rise and fall of sects is cyclical, each new hegemon falls victim to the same sins of pride and sloth as the one they supplanted, and eventually decays and collapses to the next.

                            It's just as easy to forget the transition from Order to Technocracy was a bloodless coup which exploited the Order's failings, that it is to forget the Order itself was born of hegemonic power wielded by the Hermetics and Messianic Voices during the Dark Ages. Meanwhile, the Tradition Council's own inception was one of hegemonic influence and oppression that saw shamanic, pagan, and non-European arts forced to second-class status within the nascent sect, with only the explicit threat of a common foe to maintain the status quo -- and the birth of the Disparate Alliance centuries later being its inevitable conclusion.

                            Sure, Nephandi love to infiltrate and subvert other sects to their own ends. To great success, now and then. But, on the other hand is "Nephandic infiltration" the cause of all the sects' woes, or is it one of many convenient excuses for each and every sect to spout in the face of failure to avoid genuine introspection? The greatest comfort for a mage, and their biggest hindrance to Ascension, is their ceaseless capacity for to deluding themselves into believing their own failures were someone else's fault.

                            The funny thing about Nephandi, is they only really seem to show up precisely when and where it seems the sects might be on the verge of asking themselves the hard questions about who they are and what they really want, thereby confronting uncomfortable truths about themselves, and actually getting somewhere with this whole "Ascension" thing. Then, in the face of a common enemy, mages are free to set the really hard questions aside until the crisis is over. Of course, none of that matters when individual mages, groups of them, even entire sects, have rivals to demonize, war against, and indefinitely kick that "Ascension" can down the road just a little more.

                                        When the V20 supplement about the Black Hand was written, the mages in the Black Hand's employ were given a Foundation and Pillars because the whole thing could be described much more succinctly than attempting to include either Arete and Spheres or Paths of sorcery; I strongly recommend looking at that supplement for an example of how to incorporate Foundation and Pillars into a modern chronicle. That said, it works because a single Foundation and its Pillars is tailored to what amounts to a single practice of magick: when you start expanding to multiple practices, the multiple Foundations and Pillars quickly become the more cumbersome approach. And, as Aleph said, much of the simplicity that a single Foundation has comes from a certain ambiguity in what its Pillars can do.

                                        For M5, I do not want Pillars or Minor Spheres. Heck, I don't even want the Technocratic Sphere variants that the Convention Books gave us and M20 incorporated, nor the Qlippothic Spheres from the various editions of Book of Madness. I do think that makes should be able to tweak what they can do with their Spheres to better reflect their paradigms; but as I said earlier in this thread, I think the best way to do that would be through a series of supernatural Merits that each explicitly spell out one change to how you interact with the Spheres.

                                        For instance, a Dimensional Science Merit that alters the Gauntlet chart for Spirit to better reflect the notion of other dimensions, and the Correspondence chart to downplay the connections aspect of Correspondence in favor of its “manipulating space” aspects. An Information Science Merit could replace the Correspondence chart with something more along the lines of the Data chart that was introduced in the Revised NWO Convention Book; and a Hypereconomics Merit could let you use Prime to tap into Primal Ventures and more easily craft Wonders at the cost of not being as good at dealing with Quintessence as a mystical energy source. Likewise with various benefits that individual Traditions were given in earlier editions, like the Order of Hermes' True Names.

                                        This lets you be more detailed in what the Spheres can and can't do, because you're only one Merit away from making an exception to those rules; and it lets you codify those exceptions about as succinctly as possible.

The entire thing is somewhat ridiculous anyways, because if a mage is going to have those sorts of paradigmatic limitations, there's a 99.999...% chance they have those spheres anyways which makes it no real restriction at all, and simply confounds fluff with mechanics for the sake of doing it.

                                            I mean, not long ago I played an Etherite whose paradigm was based entirely around Hugh Everett's many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics, from which I took inspiration from the M20 sidebars about alternate realities in how to properly build the character. You can be goddamn sure he had Sphere Natural (Entropy), but just because that was his paradigm and because he had Entropy, doesn't mean I needed to add Entropy to a basic-ass DimSci or Correspondence (his other two starting spheres) rote. [Fuck, I loved that character.]

                                            Anyhow, moving on. My ideal for an M5 reformulation for how mages learn and use magic mechanically. Make players name the "Arete" rating in accordance with their paradigm (Arete, Avatar, Dharma, Genius, Enlightenment, Daemon, Divinity, whatever) , ditch the Avatar merit and peg Quintessence caps to this rating, and balance that by pegging Resonance to it. Justify it by saying the mage's own enlightenment doesn't grow, but also their connection to their Avatar and capability to commune with it and channel Quintessence, but that comes at the cost of stronger Resonance (which it does anyways as a degree-of-separation "derived stat" thing). This rating reflects paradigm in a mechanical way by acting as a limitation on what powers they can have...

                                            ...because we're getting rid of Spheres. Entirely. Put all the powers relegated to the individual spheres in a giant list, as dictated by levels 1-5. Level 1 powers are "Detect Forces", "Sense Thoughts and Emotions", "Immediate Spatial Perception", etc.; level 2 powers are "Time Sight", "Heal Self", "Body of Light", etc.; level 3 powers are "Affect Predictable Patterns", "Firewalling", "Pierce Gauntlet", etc. Players buy the powers a la carte as fits their paradigm (in other words if a character doesn't have a paradigm that justifies "Step Sideways", for example, they can't buy it). The only exception to all this, is we're getting rid of "Effuse Personal Quintessence", and making the ability to draw ambient Quintessence and hold more than their Avatar rating, innate to being an Awakened mage.

                                            Give starting mages six levels' worth of powers at character generation, and make each individual power cost something like 4x level in experience (4, 8, 12, 16, 20). Cheaper than spheres, more variation, and players are in complete control over what their characters can do as best-fits their paradigm. No more players trying desperately to mental gymnastics their way into being able to use discrete Sphere powers they already have that conflict with their paradigm, feeling screwed for having to buy requisite sphere levels just to do one thing conjunctionally (lookin' at you, hanging Effects), or looking cross-eyed at powers within a given Sphere level with little if no utility for that individual character and wondering why that crap cost them 8xCR in experience.

                                            And, we're killing the Arete cap on power levels. "Forge Psyche" is still going to be a level 5 power, but if a starting mage wants to make a sentient whatever at the cost of being able to do only that, fuck it. Go for it, have fun rolling those 1-3 dice at difficulty 8 until you get your 20 successes or botch out (the more likely outcome). "One of the things druids are most famous for is their mythical ability to control weather, so why is it going to take me half the chronicle to get Arete 4, Forces 4, and Entropy 3, and their specialty sphere's Life so I don't even get a cost reduction!" not any more, motherfucker, tornado away!

                                            That's just my take on it, anyway.

                                                    Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post

                                                    ...The variant spheres have always been a bit of a tripping hazard to that...
                                                    Frankly, in my opinion it's not merely the variant spheres in later editions that are the root cause of the problem, it's merely an obvious symptom. The problem is the use of spheres to abstract mechanical growth and granularize powers, and how that conflicts with paradigm and factional fluff. Variant spheres aren't the only tripping hazards, after all -- look, for example, at ongoing "why does Entropy also handle necromancy stuff, but why does necromancy stuff also require Life and Matter?" discussions that were a part of splitting Entropy into Death and Fate in MtAw, and a source of confusion from that action. Or, how Mind is the purview of astral realm and dream-related stuff, which spurs confusion as to what Spirit actually does and what are the real constraints of the sphere.

                                                    If Mind is what you use to go "up" from the Penumbra and Entropy is what you use to go "down", then Spirit is what you use to go "sideways", right? Well, how does that compare and contrast to established metaphysics which pegs Horizons to physical spaces, the First Horizon being the edge of the atmosphere and the Far Horizon being the asteroid belt, which implies one can cross the Gauntlet with mere Correspondence? Don't forget DimSci isn't just a paradigmatic variant of Spirit, but rather more a hybridization of Spirit and (conventional) Correspondence to accommodate for those metaphysics.

                                                    Don't even get me started with the Digital Web and how it can be accessed with basically any means. Whether you use Entropy to go down and sneak in via the Haunted Web, Mind to go up and breach via Cyber Realm portals, or Spirit to step sideways and climb the Pattern Web, you can do it. And vice versa, if the mage is so-equipped, but at what point in that process does one cross the Avatar Storm? Hell, the Digital Web is a virtual space comprised of data, which is itself under the Technocratic Consensus expressions of (electrical) Forces -- a mage should be able to achieve immersion absent penalties (or even the need for VR gear) with a Forces/Mind Effect, forget Correspondence.

                                                    Obviously, this entire post is focused around a single aspect of all possible magic, and how just that one aspect gets quite confusing and frustrating at times. But, frankly, it's the one that always comes to my mind first and foremost, specifically because I had a Dreamspeaker player in my old game who would get really grouchy about what he could and couldn't do with Spirit, the need for conjunctional Spheres to do even simple tasks when it came to the Umbra but the simultaneous lack of Spheres' full power in the Umbra, and the level of other Spheres' overlap with Spirit which he felt diluted the overall power of the Sphere. Especially when, as he rightly pointed out, once he hit mastery, all the experience he spent to buy spheres for select conjunctional use became redundant. As he put it, he felt more like a Pattern mage who could do stuff in the Umbra, than he did a Spirit mage.

                                                    Don't forget the weirdness that sees Correspondence cover physical space and the navigation thereof, and sympathies, of which a base-level reading implies some connection between the two concepts. What if I, as a player, want to use sympathetic magic but not necessarily have purview over physical space, or vice versa? Or, if my character's paradigm extends to one concept or the other, and the other is harder if not impossible to justify? I'm buying an entire sphere, up to five potentially due to the requirement of Correspondence level being equal to the highest-level of the Effect, just for conjunctional use. Yes, I know sympathetic magic is powerful and there must be some limiting factor on it, but on the other hand the constraints placed upon it by the system are quite excessive, especially considering the baseline requirements in terms of success thresholds, difficulty modifiers, and background work for rituals to employ it also being limiting factors.

                                                    In any case, it's exemplary of why I feel a more paradigmatic, or a la carte, take on powers would be more appropriate to the game system than standardization. No matter how one approaches it, the system's going to be complex and confusing with a pretty significant learning curve for a tabletop RPG, that's just Mage. Allowing players to approach the system in a way they can at least easily contextualize which also maximizes player freedom, I feel, would be preferable to one that's necessarily easier to adjudicate.
