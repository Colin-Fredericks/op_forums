    Reconciling the classical/technology-as-magic ideas (an outsiders question)?

    10-18-2017, 08:48 AM
    Discalimer: I have never played a Mage game in my life.

    With that out of the way, how does the setting, and more importantly, the players, reconcile the occultist/esotericist/spiritual ideas of the game (concepts which are, in our own reality, generally hostile to modernity and sometimes even hostile to the material world as such, late 1800's ideas about magnetism and mesmeism and such nothwithstanding) with the high-tech, Asimov-quote-inducing concepts like the Void Engineers.

    I can get behind the "classical" esotericist ideas, and I can get behind the "Nazi UFOs powered by magic" flair of the Technocracy, but not both. I get that both being equally true at the same time is a central conflict of the setting, but it just never really clicked for me.

    How do yyou do it, forum?

      The thing in Mage is... everything can be true, you're the one who forms your own reality. Exept there are billions of people on the planet and everyone has some kind of idea how reality should be like. Now you got a really big mess and that's where the consensus comes in. It takes all the "agreed on" aspects of peoples imaginations and puts them together in a more or less singular reality (not considering regional reality zones and stuff). So here you have quite the stable, fleshed out, capital-R Reality. This Reality is true to everyone.

      Exept there are mages, who basically say: "Peoples reality is not my reality." And they influence Reality in a way they themselfes belief it should be instead. They can believe in everything they want, but it has to be a very fleshed out, powerfull belief. They have to reassure themselfes constantly that THIS is how it really should be. So your occultist summoning demons and the Void Engineer busting "Extradimansional Entities" are actually dealing with the same creatures, who use the same rules, but ones alien from another dimension is the other ones demon.

      There is one very major point about the Technocracy though. They don't believe in Magic (capital M). They believe in Science! There's no thing such as a demon summoning or homeopathic medicine or whatever. That's bullshit from a technocrats point of view. They work actively to make people believe science was real and magic was not, flipping the consensus in their favour.
      At the same time, mages have, by now, mostly adapted to the technologic world of the 21st century. Hermetics use smartphones and even most Verbena will probably have a Whatsapp group for their witches circle.

                      Well, part of the idea of the Technocracy is that they are a foil for the Traditions. Technocrats are pursuing science that is so advanced and outside the norm that it is to normal people, the Sleepers, essentially magic. The Technocracy doesn't want to admit the supernatural exists, but their inspired inventions that exceed the bounds of current belief take time for people to accept. Think of people who are laggards on cell phones or won't use a computer and you see some of the trouble the Technocracy has in pushing science. For the Technocracy, properly controlled science leads to a better world for people. The Technocracy wants people to believe in the scientific method and refuses to acknowledge that each scientist makes their own reality with their hypothesis, or tries to underplay this.

                      The Traditions, representing those who are supernaturally-oriented or use weird science (Sons of Ether and Virtual Adepts), know reality is shaped by will and as such, oppose the Technocracy trying to say 'this is reality.' Remember that Technocratic reality is very materialistic and excludes spiritual ideas and motivations--which is bad for most of the Traditions.

                      If we go with a very simplified version of the Triad of Wyrm, Weaver and Wyld from the Werewolf game, I put the major mage factions as this:

                      Nephandi--purely destructive and dangerous Wyrm. They seek to bring more death and disorder and destroy the world. Their foil in the Traditions is the Euthanatos, who clear the cancerous growths out of society by providing the Good Death.

                      Traditions--A balance between the Triad, as they develop on individual terms, build coalitions and destroy ossified and problematic ideas. Each Tradition has its own focus, but combined they are working to create a better new reality on the ashes of old realities. The High Mythic Ages, when dragons lived and you had a medieval focus in Europe (basically the Ars Magica RPG) failed, but the golden age will return. The Sons of Ether and Virtual Adepts are the outliers, given they use technology as magick, but these two groups see individualism in their technology and reject the consensual, controlled reality of the Technocracy. For the Wyrm part, they are the good Wyrm for breaking down the Technocratic reality.

                      Technocracy--The agents of the Weaver gone mad, the Technocracy wants control. If they could build a closed system where science explained everything, they would. So, the Technocracy hunts those who disagree and works to rob Sleepers of their initiative so there is no dissent. There are many shades of 1984, Brave New World and cyberpunk here, but over the years the Technocrats have gone from the powerful overarching boogeyman with Men in Black and other things out of conspiracy theory to a more nuanced faction. Still, they are scientists with a timetable and no brook no dissent, so like science in Soviet Russia, those who disagree with the science will be eliminated or reprogrammed to think as the Technocracy wants them to.

                      Marauders--These mages are pure Wyld, with their own reality become everyone's reality around them when they can. Essentially Marauders represent mages withdrawing into schizoid mental landscapes and projecting those onto others. Since each Marauder is different and they are all chaotic, they represent a danger to any kind of consensual reality that other factions are building.

                      So, you need the Technocracy to have a plausible foe that is constant if you play the Traditions mages. If you decide on a Technocracy game where the Union operates, you are playing a game where brave scientists keep the supernaturals down. Mage the Ascension first edition made the conflict more of a Cold War, which given the time made a lot of sense.

                      Both the Technocracy and Traditions give a damn about Sleepers. Each group wants to protect the Sleepers and make them better, while well aware that strange experiments or happenings like reanimator or rains of frogs will disturb Sleepers and cause a backlash. So, with the Technocracy you get a foe fighting the war for minds with the Traditions, who is organized and who isn't necessarily evil or difficult to find a goal for.

                      As for paradoxes and ideological issues, Mage is a game about exploring them. Different characters have different views and reality is what you make of it.

                                  You really have to fully deconstruct your understanding of IRL reality, throw it all away, and start fresh when it comes to the metaphysics of Mage. Nothing works like you think it works. As immutable, reliable, and constant as you may believe things like our basic physical laws in our world, they simply are not in Mage. The Laws upon which the world of Mage are build are literally up for interpretation and severe editing.

                                  First and foremost: Reality is ruled by the Consensus of humanity. What the great majority of people believe, is. Flying surely isn't possible, right? Well, convince enough people about this weird idea called "aerodynamics" and how it works over curved surfaces, and eventually your "flying machines" stop failing all the time (Disbelief/Paradox) as more and more people believe. The Consensus slowly begins to accept that flying is possible, so long as it uses these "basic principles of aerodynamics." "Physics" didn't make airplanes work; belief, and therefore the Consensus did.

                                  See, this is what Mage boils down to when you start looking at the Ascension War and the difference between the factions. It's all about messaging and leading the Masses around to get behind your ideas. This is how the Technocracy "won," and this is where modern "technology" comes from. They got wise to the power of the Masses and started disseminating Technocratic "methods/tools/science" to everyone, got them believing, and so it spread. Previously, magic wasn't really shared outside of the people who could use it themselves, so it never became widespread. However, had the Order of Hermes mass produced magic electric wands and flying broomsticks, that would be what everyone believed in, but they didn't, so this is where we are now.

                                  Technology isn't "powered by magic." In Mage, technology is literally magic. Look at it this way: very few people understand the principles upon which our modern-day smart phones operate. Electrical theory, electronic design, semiconductor properties, etc. That shit is straight magic to most of us. In the world of Mage, because we've all been led to believe that they work, they do.

                                              The key to understanding the bit about the Technocracy not believing in magic despite all the "evidence" to contrary is to remember Clarke's Law; any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic.

                                              There is no magic, say the Technocrats. There's just scientific principles we don't fully understand yet. The Traditions are dangerous and need to be shut down because they're playing with forces that are not well understood and have a tendency to run out of control (i.e. paradox backlashes). They also oppose giving matches and gasoline to five year olds for essentially the same reason.

                                              So while it may look like that Euthanatos is spirit bonding with an aspect of Shiva through some repeated mantras... what's actually happening (according to the Technocrat) is that this lunatic is creating some sort of extra-dimensional resonance that is drawing a dangerous alien entity from another dimension into this one.

                                              The idea that they should NOT be mages though and that magick must be some force opposed to modernity though completely destroys the core fundamental truth of the setting... That Magic(k) is about CHANGING THE WORLD.

                                              True Magic is not ancient spells or lost secrets (though for some it can be). Its changing reality through the force of your Will and making it work the way you think it should. What you believe becomes real. So if belief in God or Spirits or True Names or the Power of You can change reality then belief in science has to work as well or the underlying metaphysics collapse.
