    And this is where I bring up Earthly Foundations, where “if enough people believe it” isn't necessarily the be-all and end-all of what works and what doesn't. That it is a factor is what distinguishes Mage from the real world; but it's only a factor. If there wasn't some sort of clash between “how people believe the world works” and “how the world works”, science wouldn't work. And while we might well still have much of the convenience of modern life, it would more likely take the form of herbal poultices rather than vaccines; flying carpets rather than cars.

    The Earthly Foundations make science and technology possible; without them, progress would be “more of the same, only better”.

                            One of the things is that in Mage, holistic medicine and such actually work, or at least they can work if the right individual is administering the medicine, or if it's being used in the right place. Anti-vaxx can also work. In the real world if a child has cancer and his parents call in a faith healer to pray for him instead of taking him to chemotherapy, those parents can (and should) be arrested. In the World of Darkness though, faith healers can cure blindness and cancer through prayer, so it could be viewed as entirely reasonable if the family brings in a member of the Celestial Chorus or Dreamspeakers who can do something like that. And, of course, the Technocracy would respond by trying to hunt down and imprison or kill the "reality deviant" who gives families an option of how to treat their child's cancer.

                            MtA isn't necessarily "anti-science" but to a degree, modern science, medicine, etc are largely the tools of the Technocracy, a vast conspiracy puppetteering the world from the shadows. Many of the Traditions are pushing for a world in which the sorts of things that don't work in our, real life world, will be able to work on a large scale in the World of Darkness.

                                        I think it's also important to consider the notion of unintended consequences: you get two very different impressions of the Union depending on whether every aspect of their technology was deliberately chosen, or not. If everything is deliberate, then the Technocrats are a bunch of sadists who deliberately choose to promote technologies that cause cancer, lung disease, environmental destruction, and all other manners of pain and devastation; of not, then they're arrogant idealists who are too full of themselves to properly consider the consequences of their actions. Former strikes me more as Nephandi territory; and in fact Book of the Fallen has a number of types of Nephandi which could loosely be described as “[insert Convention here], but sadistic”. As such, I tend to prefer the well intentioned extremist take on the Technocracy.

                                        Some of this can be chalked up to the Paradox Effect, or some variant thereof; but some of it can't. There are some choices that the Technocracy has made in developing their technology that simply don't make sense if everything was driven by belief or willworking. Which is where my citation of Earthly Foundations comes in: these ground rules, which exist even when a given Reality Zone has been completely depopulated (see the Kickstarter manuscript for Victorian Mage) and include such things as “water flowing uphill is always Vulgar”, provide an objective element to the setting that Traditionists tend to ignore, but (in my view of them) the Technocracy embraces. At the very least, a Technocrat can justify the Union's design choices as “that's what's most compatible with the Earthly Foundations”.

                                        It also gives Enlightened Scientists something to apply the scientific method to: they're students of the Earthly Foundations. And that's where Technocracy Reloaded's take on Article 4 comes in: rather than portray Technocrats as willworkers who are trying to force an arbitrary standard on everyone, it portrays them as Scientists who are trying to force conformity to the Earthly Foundations on everyone, while expressing the fascination that they have with learning everything they can about the Earthly Foundations. That is, it removes the arbitrary nature of their technology. The Technocrats are still control freaks (it is, after all, in their name); but they're also scientists, and not just pretenders. (I have always hated how earlier editions of the game portrayed Technocrats as either liars or dupes: either they know that reality is as the Traditions say it is and are deceiving everyone about it, or they don't know, and are acting out of stupid ignorance. This more nuanced take removes that aspect of things, recasting Enlightened Scientists as people who are focusing on a truth that the Traditions have largely rejected, removing the clear-cut “Traditions are metaphysically right, and Technocrats are metaphysically wrong” distinction. Now, they're both Blind Men groping an Elephant, each focusing on an aspect of the truth but missing something else. You can still critique the Union for trying to force conformity on everyone; but that's distinct from whether or not Enlightened Science has to be a lie. And that, in turn, opens up the possibility of playing an Enlightened Scientist who isn't a Technocrat, and who isn't an “anything goes if you just believe” Etherite, either.)

                                            Well I think that a lot of the "either idiots or liars" dilemma goes away when we treat consensual reality as something that's easy to say and hard to understand. From our privileged position we can (if we want to) take a very simple approach, but that is not what it looks like in universe. It took thousands of years for anyone at all to figure out that reality was mutable, and in all that time plenty of people were finding truths, constants, and other important and revelatory things. That didn't stop once the Consensus was "discovered," nor did everyone give up on the idea of actual truth instantly, or at all.

                                            There are still truths (seeming truths or otherwise) for the Technocrats to apply their methods to. They aren't liars if those truths aren't the Earthly Foundations, unless you take the approach that everything is a lie.

                                            Instead, what I think is going on with the Technocracy is that they have to balance three equally important factors:
                                            What will bring about the best results for the world and society?
                                            What can they actually get the sleepers to believe and embrace?
                                            What fits with their own personal and institutional understanding and paradigm?

                                            This is the element that makes them look more hypocritical and confused than other factions. It's not that they have their head in the sand where others don't, it's that these three factors work against each other and a certain amount of compromise of all three is required. What should be sometimes clashes with what can be and what must be. They are both scientists and aware that they can, with much effort, force the facts to fit the data in a very literal way.
