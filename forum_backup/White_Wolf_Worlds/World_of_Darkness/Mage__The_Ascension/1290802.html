    Mage Bloodbowl; "OP" starting characters

    02-22-2019, 12:35 PM
    This is just for fun. If you like crunch and exploring mechanics, then enjoy the thread. If you don't like this sort of mechanical tweaking/min-maxing then that's fine. Just submit a dumb/broken/OP concept and come up with ideas to make the other concepts even more broken.

    RULES:

    - Standard M20 character creation. However we're going to assume that all "optional" mechanics and setups are turned on. All merits can be purchased, that sort of thing. Let's just assume you took 13 points of flaws you damned munchkin, for 28 freebie points. If a flaw is important to the character's OP powers then mention it in the writeup.

    - No Marauders, since there are mechanical upsides and they come for """free""". Also worth noting that M20 doesn't give us creation rules for Marauder PCs.

    - Remember that mind control isn't going to be over in a single roll unless your target is some sort of spineless thrall or sub-standard mook. Mind can be incredibly potent, but when it comes to ending an encounter without throwing a single blow... Dominate it ain't. Then again I might be wrong, and it might be possible to consistently take another mage out in the first round or two with Mind 3. It would be interesting to see.

    - This isn't going to be a total white room. If a concept demands approaching stealthily, observing the target and striking when they least expect it, then cool. If your concept demands the use of spirits then you won't be somewhere with Gauntlet 10 (although the fact that you're weaker with a higher Gauntlet is something to keep in mind of course).

    - Unless you have some cool way of overcoming space/weight limitations (like using Correspondence or a Wonder to pull whatever gear you need out of your base), then it's worth remembering that there are only so many Prime 2 Forces 3 Charms you can carry and deploy... for example. If all else is equal, innate trumps dependent.

    - Allies, Retainers, Backup etc. are off-limits simply because "I have my five bodyguards dogpile my opponents" is kinda cheesy. However "with my Mind and Charisma and cop badge it's very easy to get a crowd to attack people" is fair.

    - You're allowed to argue that something is powerful because it can grow in power very efficiently. "And with 8 EXP they'll be able to-" is something valid to consider.

    - It has to fit within some form of Paradigm, naturally.

    Ambrosia thanks for tips on designing rules

          Tiger's Fist

          tl;dr son of Tiger has Aggravated claws, is spec'd into clawing things.

          Attributes: Physical Primary. Even spread for best results, or min-max to minimize EXP cost.

          Abilities:

          - Martial Arts (a specialized form based upon the use of the Bagh Nakh, a "claw" weapon that rests in your palm).

          - Do 1 or 2, to get the Well Trained Advantage.

          - Athletics.

          - Stealth.

          Merits: Nephilim, representing being the son of Tiger, or some tiger deity. There are a lot of benefits to this, but one is that for a Derangement he gets 7 points in Advantages. For 6 points he gets Claws that do Strength + 2 Aggravated. 1 point of rapid healing. Beyond this, 7 points of Quintessence a day is very good. The extra Res/Syn means that once per story you can throw in an extra 4 dice to an Arete or ability roll.

          Now before we factor in magick he's rolling Dex + Martial Arts difficulty 5 to do Strength + 2 Aggravated damage. This is already pretty neat.

          Backgrounds: By taking Tiger as a Totem with our 7 Background points and 1 Freebie we get Totem 4 which gives us +3 to Martial Arts. 3 in Avatar, leaving us with 11 freebies.

          Spheres: Entropy 1 for reducing the difficulty of the Damage roll, Correspondence 2 for clawing from afar, otherwise as preferred. Mind 1 is always a good investment and fits a martial arts paradigm. Same goes for Life 2.

          Arete: 8 freebies give Arete 3, since the dice pool is just very useful. 3 freebies left.

          Finishing touches: 2 freebies pushes us to Martial Arts 4, which means we get a spec, which means more damage with claws. 1 freebie point into Willpower.

          EDIT so with an extra 13 freebies, we're gonna invest in Arcane 5 (because of course we are) leaving 8, and put dots into Melee and Stealth to put them both up to 5.

          So for example, with Dex 4 and Martial Arts 5, with the +3 from Tiger, means 12 dice in an attack roll. Rolling at Difficulty 5 (no magick) or difficulty 2 (magick with quintessence spent and maybe Willpower) we get 7.3 or 9.6 successes... well something above 9.6 anyways, the chart I'm using is great but has 3 as the minimum difficulty.

          So if we go with roughly 7 successes on average (what we'd get without magick), and assume our target will get 3 successes on dodge, that gives 4 successes, with +3 dice to damage.

          Strength of 4, damage bonus of 3, claws give +2. 9 dice of damage against difficulty 6 (pretty sure well trained advantage doesn't apply since the damage step isn't the maneuver). That's 4 aggravated, which isn't bad, but we can do a lot better. With a bit of preparation we can use Entropy 1 to reduce the difficulty of this step. At difficulties 3, 4 and 5 the damage becomes 6.3, 5.5 and 4.7 respectively. This means that with one point of Willpower, some Quintessence and a good roll we can one-shot someone with 7 health points, which covers almost all mages.

          If we're able to ambush, then we roll Dexterity + Stealth + Arcane (14 dice) against difficulty 6, opposed by the target's Perception + Alertness. If we get more than 1 success then we get extra dice to the original attack. Assuming we get 5 successes (which is below average) and our target gets 2, then we get +2 dice to the attack roll. So that's 8.4 successes on attack, leaving 5 successes with 3 dodge, giving +4 dice to damage. 10 against difficulty 3 is 7 damage, so it will give an insta-kill 50% of the time assuming an even distribution.

          Conclusion: sneak attack with this build for a 50% chance of an insta-kill, with one magick effect preparing the turn before. Spend Willpower on the damage roll to all but guarantee an instant death.

                          Taser

                          tl;dr coincidental methods like tasers and guns for Aggravated assaults.

                          Attributes: Physical Primary. Even spread for best results, or min-max to minimize EXP cost. Strength can be ignored.

                          Abilities:

                          - Firearms

                          - Alertness

                          - Athletics

                          - Science/Crafts for explosives like grenades

                          - Gunsmithing for when Prime 4 eventually lets you make gun wonders... plus making your own guns or tweaking them means -1 difficulty to all those effects

                          - Technology, ditto but with tasers

                          Merits: Hands of Daedalus for tinkering with and reworking instruments. Essentially -1 difficulty to all effects, as long as the equipment is stuff you've used before.

                          Backgrounds: Avatar 5, Resources 2. Invest Resources 1 in two different level 2 "Primal Ventures", and now you're getting around 20 quintessence a week from your monetary investments.

                          Spheres: Forces 2 for amplifying the damage from an attack with a taser, stun gun, firearm... Primal Utility 2 to inflict Aggravated damage and make Charms like Grenades. Primal Utility is trumping Prime for access to nearly infinite Nodes. Mind 1 is good for buffing perception for Aiming, which is a great way to increase damage and pull off impossible shots.

                          Arete: 8 freebies give Arete 3, since the dice pool is just very useful.

                          Finishing touches: Remaining freebies into Firearms, and backgrounds to represent access to all the relevant kit.

                          Now with Dexterity 5 and Firearms 5, we do come to a big question; when we use Forces 2 to amplify the velocity of a bullet, and Prime 2 to charge all the bullets with Quintessence (1 per shot, hence investing so much into Primal Utility for rapid refills with Currency/Quintessence), do we add the extra damage on top of the bullet's ordinary damage? I can't get a 100% clear answer from M20 currently.

                          So with Prime 2 alone we're rolling our 10 dice at difficulty 6 (although it could be as low as 3 and as high as 10 depending on the shot), with a spec, for 5.2 successes. We can always boost our successes by having a relevant Technocratic Totem (with Spirit 1 to justify it), since we have many freebies left over. That could give up to 3 bonus dice to Firearms... not that I'd personally agree to a player making up a new Totem that happens to boost Firearms for them. Assuming 13 dice, that's 6.6 successes. With dodge we might end up with 4 successes, so standard damage + 3. Standard revolver is 4 dice of damage, so that's 7 dice of damage 3 times in one turn thanks to the rate of 3.

                          3.2 Aggravated damage, 3 times. So on average dealing 9.6 Aggravated damage. However I may have misunderstood how firearms damage works in M20, feel free to correct me.

                          If we go further we can apply a Forces 2 effect after energizing to increase velocity. Then we're looking at 3 dice against difficulty 5 (coincidental), for a 2 success average. That's 6 points of damage per shot that hits... so whether or not we actually apply the damage of the normal Firearms result seems moot. At least M20 tells me in an example that each shot gets the extra damage. And it's not like anyone is soaking 6 Aggravated three times in a row.

                          Conclusion: Dealing magickal damage with a firearm might actually be completely broken, in a way that concerns me since pulling it off even accidentally might be easy? "I fight with forces 2, prime 2 and a gun" is a very reasonable concept after all.
