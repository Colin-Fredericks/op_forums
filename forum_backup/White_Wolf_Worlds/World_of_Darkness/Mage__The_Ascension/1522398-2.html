                        …which is why the original post posited at least some degree of reform in the Union. Obviously, if the most dystopian version of the Technocracy wins, it would be a disaster.
                        ​​​​​
                        Note also that I gave reasons for the reform: Mage was first published in the early 90s, at a time when the general zeitgeist featured a thoroughly corrupt and irredeemable Man oppressing everyone. We're now 30 years later, and the zeitgeist has changed: now, it's even more apparent than ever that the system is broken; but there's also a growing attitude among the younger generation that it can't and should be fixed. And there's reason to think that one way or the other, the current crisis will be resolved, and sober rather than later. The world will not be as it is in ten years, any more than it is now what it was ten years ago.

                        So going on the premise that the system will undergo some sort of reform in the near future that will lead to a world where trust in the system gets restored, and noting that the Technocracy in many ways serves as the in-game stand-in for “the system”, that implies some sort of reform to the Technocracy that fixes its most broken aspects. With that happening, should the Technocracy win? Not “the Technocracy at its worst” or even “the Technocracy as it is now”, but rather the Technocracy that I'm anticipating.
