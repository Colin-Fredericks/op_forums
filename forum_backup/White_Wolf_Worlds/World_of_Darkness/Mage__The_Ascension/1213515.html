    Transmuting an attack into Quintessence

    04-30-2018, 04:12 PM
    So with Forces 2 you can try and dampen an incoming attack. Change the path of a bullet, disperse a fiery strike, redirect a lightning bolt. It has to go somewhere, so the bullet ricochets or hits something else, the fire's embers lash out into the surroundings, and the lightning strikes something metallic around you.

    With Forces 3 you can transmute the incoming force. The bullet's velocity becomes heat, the fire becomes wind, the lightning becomes radiowaves, you get the picture.

    Now here's the bit that I find really surprising.

    Add Prime 4 to that transmutation effect, and you can transform the fire or lightning into pure Quintessence and absorb it. 1 point for every point of damage that you are converting.

    Say our attacking Mage is Arete 3, attacks with Forces 2, redirecting electricity from the mains, using their body as a conduit. Vulgar, but no witnesses, so it's difficulty 6. 2 successes, so that's 3 points of damage.

    The defending Mage has Arete 4, and is performing a vulgar Prime 4 Forces 3 feat, so that's difficulty 8. Their magical staff negates the raised difficulty from Rapid Casting. They spend a point of Willpower, since that's very sensible, and they also spend a point of Quintessence to push down the difficulty. 3 successes (2 of their 4 dice work out, plus the Willpower). They now absorb 3 points of Quintessence for a 2 Quintessence profit, and completely negate their foe's attack.

    That example isn't that crazy, since the defending Mage is more powerful. Here's my problem.

    A Mage could simply absorb Quintessence from a bonfire, using Forces 3 and Prime 4. Take half of the damage it's producing each turn as Quintessence and you'll be loaded up to your limit in no time.

    With that quirk of the rules, doesn't that make Nodes much less valuable for many Mages? Obviously not every Mage will accept gathering Quintessence from a bonfire as part of their paradigm, but many will. Whether the Dreamspeaker performs a ritual sacrifice to spiritual entities and dances around, absorbing the essence of fire spirits, or a Verbena leaps through the flames, using their sacred connection to the earth to emerge unburnt, or a Hermetic draws runes around a bonfire to invoke essential energies and draw out some primal force... you get the picture.

    Let's be conservative, very conservative, and say that a large bonfire could get you 5 Quintessence. We know a bonfire will have the potential to do more damage than that, but 5 will do. Throw a deer (large animal) on there to make it 15. Now you're a hippy burning a deer in the countryside, claiming to anyone who asks that it "rejuvenates you". Heck, that might even be Coincidental; when I see hippies dancing around Stonehenge and coming out buzzing I assume they're high on drugs, not the primal essence of creation.

    With decent Stamina, the ritual will easily capture all of the Quintessence. And if it doesn't... well that's fine? Even if you only pick up 7 points, you've burnt a deer (easily caught with Spheres reducing difficulties for traps and hunting) and some wood (there's plenty of it).

    Why are nodes a big deal if this is possible? A level 5 node is a precious thing, but it's giving 10 Quintessence a week, -1 difficulty to magick, and a weaker Gauntlet. This is great, sure, but it doesn't seem that special when placed in perspective. And a weak Gauntlet and a Level 3 Cairn come with their own problems, of course, since Spirits aren't always friendly and Garou sure as hell aren't.

              Because it eats up room for other spheres. Prime has its advantages sure, but not everyone is interested in becoming a living battery. Some people prefer to invest in Time for the promise the high level side of it can bring. Some people think matter and Entropy offer too many solutions. Or when another player found themselves in a 1920’s New England bar, and couldn’t tell what the heck was going on. Often times, the one sphere you absolutely need for a situation will always be the one you don’t have.

                      This isn't cannon, it's just how I generally approached it. Nodes have resonance themselves, and the quintessence they create is coloured accordingly, but their resonance is a broad concept such as creative urge or enlightenment. You can frame a lot of effects through that. You can rule that drawing quintessence from objects has the quintessence become very heavily coloured by the resonance of the item. So the juice gained from a bonfire can create, well, fire. That's it. Keeping it in your pattern for long amounts of time might colour it with your own soul's resonance, in which case you're golden, but you can contain only so much, and you need to wait for the process to happen. Drawing quintessence from living things is better, since the living have more expansive concepts to their resonance, but you need to get through the antithetical resonance issue - ie, get a willing sacrifice.

                      Exceptions include treasured keepsakes, which bear the resonance of the owner, and unique items that have symbolic value to a lot of people, which bear the appropriate resonance (a pebble from the Parthenon).

                      In the case of a Scientific Paradigm, ignore the above mumbo jumbo. It's just a matter of energy conversion. If you want to make a bonfire heat something, it's energy transference, but if you want to make a bonfire into a bowl of petunias, then you need to make so many energy conversions that the productivity of your effect is crap.

                      Taking all this into consideration, quintessence is a limited resource in the short term. Prime 4 mages still have a serious benefit - they can probably start a story with 20 points, as they can reasonably claim to have absorbed enough object quintessence for long enough for it to become keyed to them. And they can still use quintessence from objects, just in a far more limited fashion.

                      Note: If you're playing with the 10 quintessence per dot per week, ignore this. You effectively are giving infinite quintessence, unless you make nodes insanely rare.

                      TL;DR: You can rule that quintessence is heavily afflicted with resonance from its (object) source, to the point that it can only effectively replicate its source until it is cleaned through time spent in the mage's pattern - thus making untainted quintessence fitting for any effect rare.
