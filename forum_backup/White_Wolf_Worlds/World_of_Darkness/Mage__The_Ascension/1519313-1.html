    Originally posted by Lord Revan View Post
    Third, players could abuse that to get -1 Arete difficulty by sneaking in their affinity sphere.
    Okay, so, on abuse of instruments... The rules say that mages start with at least 7 instruments, one of which is personalized, and which can be set aside, one by one, from Arete 3 upwards. However, there's nothing to say how many of these instruments are required to produce any effect, nor how the use of those seven instruments is distributed among the effects that the mage uses.

    So there's nothing in the rules to stop a player saying, "My character's Arete is 3, and his instruments are a personalized wand, and six backup wands. Every effect is produced by pointing a wand, and what I want to happen, happens. And I've set aside my personalized wand, so I can get all of my effects to happen without pointing it, and if I do point my personalized wand at something I get -1 difficulty to the roll."

    But if I player says that to me, I know he's taking the mickey.

    Now, there's really good reasons why these rules are as loose as they are. They need to be able to handle an extraordinarily broad range of approaches to magic. Some mages should have an instrument that is locked in a wall safe and gets brought out once in the campaign for some special event. Some should have a go-to instrument that provides most of their day-to-day effects. That's fine, but if I'm running the game, it's up to me to negotiate the use of these instruments with the players, to maintain balance between characters, and balance against the rest of the world, and provide for good stories.

    So if I have players I can't negotiate this with, and who are looking for ways to trick me into stuff that is problematic, I'm already in trouble. And frankly, if I have a player like that, I'm more inclined to suggest that they are not compatible with the game I intend to run.

    (And I have successfully run systems that are far more structured than this part of MtA, and others far less structured than this part of MtA.)

    Now, I get that some of you want to play MtA in a style that expects players to be hunting for an angle, and you want rules that provide less opportunity for players to pull off a trick that you don't see coming. And that's fine. I'm not suggesting that every game should adopt something like I proposed at the start of this thread. And I've had some contact with the Camarilla/WW Fan Club/Beyond the Sunset, so yeah, I'm aware that not every group is going to play nice.

    But maybe not everything that is discussed and considered has to come from that very defensive position. It's not all groups that are like that.

    I'm not going to let a player link every effect to a sphere, call it an instrument, claim that instrument has then been set aside, and get -1 difficulty on everything. It's really easy to just point out that this approach doesn't really make sense, and isn't really good for the story. I'd be willing to listen to a player who thinks otherwise, but I find it hard to see this tool being set aside until most or all other tools have been set aside.

                        And now, for the sake of comparison, I’m going to take a brief look at the rules that HDYDT gives for the sorts of characters in question. From page 35 onwards…

                        For a psychic elementalist (telekineticist and similar, presumably including characters like Eleven from Stranger Things and Carrie from Carrie and similar examples):
                        • Add Mind 3 to the pattern for relevant effects; no problem for the character who already has Mind 3, but may in some cases increase (never decrease) the difficulty of the associated Arete roll (if 3 is higher than any other sphere in the pattern).
                        • The option to spend Willpower for an automatic success on the Arete roll… which was already an option, as far as I can tell. This is identical to the standard use of Willpower.
                        • Make an additional Willpower roll in order to move objects, control fire, or create objects.
                        • Optionally (on the part of the ST) spend a temporary Willpower point in order to move large objects (telekinesis).
                        • Spend a temporary Willpower point in order to create solid objects.
                        These are all downsides for the psychic elementalist. There is no mechanical advantage at all to playing this type of character, as far as I can see.

                        For the spiritual elementalist, I think you can make an argument that the rules under Telekinesis, Pyrokinesis, and Psychomanifestation are not intended to apply. That would leave these changes:
                        • Add Spirit 2 to the pattern for relevant effects; no problem for the character who already has Spirit 2, but may in some (rare) cases increase (never decrease) the difficulty of the associated Arete roll (if all other spheres in the pattern are at 1).
                        • The option to spend Willpower for an automatic success on the Arete roll… which was already an option, as far as I can tell. This is identical to the standard use of Willpower.
                        The downside here is much smaller, but it’s still all downside. If you do think the rules under the subheadings apply, the downsides get more serious.

                        In no case does a character of these types get an advantage.

                        Under this approach, playing a telekineticist would just suck. I would hate to have a player say they have a cool idea for a telekineticist, and have to tell them that they get extra chances to fail, but still have to make mystic gestures or use a physical tool (just like the regular spellcasters - and something that doesn’t fit a telekineticist’s concept well), and get no compensatory advantages at all, however minor. (If I had decided to play a telekineticist myself, and an ST told me that, I might not be worried about the mechanical disadvantages. After all, I once played a mute character in a system with no compensation for disability just to see how well I could roleplay using my body alone. I’m not often worried about having a disadvantaged character. But I may be worried about the poor modelling of telekinesis.)

                        So the published method of dealing with these characters is not great, and the prevailing opinion in this forum seems to be that the published method sucks.

                        Which leads me to wonder, how would others handle a request to play a telekineticist? What instruments do you allow for them, and how do you balance that against the character who needs his hands free to gesticulate, and the other character who needs to manipulate an object? Or do you just allow some forms of magick to be easier to access than others?

                                                By adding the “bit of Spirit” as per the Sidebar in How Do You DO That? p. 16, a Dreamspeaker changes the difficulty of their effects to be based on the Gauntlet rather than the usual rules based on Highest Sphere and Vulgarity.

                                                            Like I said, the idea is to give some sort of benefit to adding Spirit •• to the Effect, so that it's not just flavor.

                                                            The existing rules already allow Spirit Magick to replace the standard difficulty calculations with something else entirely; see M20 p.544, Spirit Magick and the Gauntlet:
                                                            • Spirit Effects cast against someone on the same side of the Gauntlet have a difficulty based on the highest Sphere + the usual coincidental, vulgar, or vulgar with witnesses modifiers.
                                                            • To cast Spirit Effects across the Gauntlet, check the Gauntlet Ratings chart to find the difficulty of your spell.
                                                            This just leverages that rule by saying that the mage who uses A Bit of Spirit is working the Effect with the cooperation of spirits on the other side of the Gauntlet, and that they're doing much of the “heavy lifting” for working the Effect; so the Effect counts as “crossing the Gauntlet” even if it normally wouldn't.
