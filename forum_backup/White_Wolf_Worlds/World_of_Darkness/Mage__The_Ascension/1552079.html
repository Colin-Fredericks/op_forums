    Result based determinism (RBD)

    07-05-2025, 05:54 PM
    In M20, we are presented two options to determine how an effect takes place and which spheres are needed.
    Process based determinism (PDB), which is the default one (I guess) and RBD.

    The first asks you *how* you want to change reality, while the last seems to ask "what" you want to do.

    There is the famous example of "I want to go from point A to point B". While there are several ways of accomplishing that, in RBD the magr could "summon" a taxi and get all green signs and no traffic and simply go to point B. While in PBD this couldn't be done (you would need Matter, Life, Forces, etc etc etc to spawn a taxi and a driver).

    This only example does not explain much to me, so I present some questions:

    1) Is RBD determined by the ST or the player has some say about it? I mean, the player just says "I want to go from A to B" and the ST chooses what happens or the player can choose how?

    2) If the player chooses in the above question does this means that RBD is a PBD with more options?

    3) Is RBD always coincident?

    4) If the the player says "I want to electrocute this guy" what would be the differences in the processes of magick rules under PBD and RBD? Would the required spheres change?

    Feel free to shed some light on how RBD works!

      Part of the problem with addressing this, is that pairing with HAP/HOO matters a lot to how either RBD and PBD feel in play. Generally speaking, I've seen most people feel that PBD + HOO far too restrictive to actually get anything done, and RBD + HAP far too easy to avoid all the intended pitfalls of magic. So PBD tends to be paired with HAP (which is the default M20 sets out, and is fairly direct about, no need to guess), and RBD tends to be paired with HOO. Both have different feels to them, but by pairing the more restrictive of one category with the less restrict of the other category tends to mean you get something of a happy middle ground where magic can do things, but has some meaningful limits on it.

      But without that pairing made, it's hard to say things like how easy it is to make things coincidental with RBD.

              So, omniscient is something I always felt was a bad word for how the earlier discussions on this intended. The HOO isn't a deity. They're still a human. The classic example is that the HOO can see inside your pocket that nothing was in there, and then suddenly something was in there, while the HAP cannot. The HOO can't perceive quantum mechanics in action, or see X-rays, or anything else beyond normal human sensory ranges (at least in its early incarnations, Internet discussions have dragged these terms around a lot as defenders and detractors have both equivocated in attempts to bolster their positions). The way it was intended originally was more a long the lines of: when magic is being cast, time pauses and a human with 5 in every Attribute and Ability is allow to fully search the entirety of everything being manipulated by the magic. So the HOO has limits, even if very weak ones.

              So, if you use magic to summon a business card into your pocket, the HOO would know and it would be vulgar (probably w/o witnesses, which really isn't as unplayable as people think unless you use a lot of on-the-fly magic in your games), because in that moment your pocket and the state of the business card is something they can observe. However, if you use magic so that reality warps such that the card was in your pocket since you added it in on your way out the house, the HOO would see that you put a business card in your pocket and carried it around until you had a reason to pull it out of your pocket.

              If you're running PBD, that's a ridiculously difficult feat of magic. But RBD generally makes it a far more tenable option, because the result of "I have a card in my pocket" is all you need to account for mechanically. This is, in effect, both why some people love RBD + HOO, and some people hate it. For some the fun of the game is coming up with ways to trick the HOO despite the "omniscience" in how you describe the process since the mechanics only care about the results. For others, this is entirely unfun because it becomes all about the player's skills and knowledge rather than their character's abilities.

                      So, presuming my take on HOO:

                      Originally posted by Lord Revan View Post
                      1) Is RBD determined by the ST or the player has some say about it? I mean, the player just says "I want to go from A to B" and the ST chooses what happens or the player can choose how?
                      There's two different parts here.

                      What Spheres are needed is ultimately up to the ST. "I want to go from A to B" can be accomplished by a few Spheres (esp. with RBD), but Forces 1 isn't enough to fly you there if you want to use Forces to do it.

                      The description of what happens in RBD is generally handled by the player with ST oversight; usually based on the Spheres used.

                      That isn't some sort of sneaky PBD stuff, but more like "you can't add in doing damage by having your driver run someone over if you're just using Correspondence to travel quickly and you're describing it as a taxi just happening to be there and the lights being in your favor." The results still have to be factored in, and doing damage is adding results to the goal.

                      2) If the player chooses in the above question does this means that RBD is a PBD with more options?
                      Not... really? RBD vs. PBD is which Spheres are necessary for an effect. The player agency is largely placed in how the effect is described and thus how things like Coincidence vs. Vulgar is assigned.

                      3) Is RBD always coincident?
                      No, but it can feel that way depending on the ST and players. Since the players are granted wide leniency to describe how the effect plays out, it's much easier to keep effects coincidental, but is ultimately a player skill mediated interaction. Even then, some results can't simply get explained away. If I have my mage turn themselves into a kaiju, it's going to be vulgar because kaiju don't exist and there's no coincidental way to explain away how a human can turn into one.

                      As well, Reality Zones are also going to impact things in this arena. What might pass in the general Consensus ruled normal reality isn't necessarily going to work in a Technocratic lab. VAs and SoEs might have enough training as former Technocratic groups to know how to pass off magic in a Technocratic Reality Zone, but most Tradition mages don't know the inner workings of Technocracy paradigms well enough to know how to explain lots of things as coincidental that would fly in other places.

                      4) If the the player says "I want to electrocute this guy" what would be the differences in the processes of magick rules under PBD and RBD? Would the required spheres change?
                      The biggest difference here would be if there's a nearby source of sufficient electricity to take advantage of. If you're out in the middle of nowhere on a clear day, PBD and RBD both agree that zapping someone with lightning conjured from nothingness is a Forces 3/Prime 2 effect. RBD gets the edge once there's high levels of electricity around to take advantage of. If there's a nearby power line, in RBD it would be relatively easy (Forces 2, maybe a few other options) to zap someone by bringing the wire down on them (Forces 2 allows you to influence existing Forces as attacks without the Forces 3/Prime 2 making a fireball from nothing approach). PBD could still potentially skip Prime, but would increase the number of successes needed (and possibly need to add Matter ) because they need to account for actually ripping the wire down and aiming it at the target.

                                  I've considered the possibility of mixing PBD and RBD in the same game, using the former to represent mages who view Magick as an impersonal force that the mage harnesses, while the latter is used to represent mages who view Magick as something willful that the mage interacts with. Examples of the latter might be an animist, a priest, or the magician in The Last Unicorn ("Magick, so as you will") — or, more generally, a Mage counterpart to Changeling Unleashings.

                                  In setting, this would be represented by a different relationship between the Mage and the Avatar: with PBD, the Avatar restricts itself solely to the role of advisor or guide, and doesn't participate in the actual working of Magick beyond enabling the Mage to work it. With RBD, the Avatar serves as an assistant when working Magick, determining the specifics of how the Magick works after the mage sets out the general guidelines. OOC, the player can still suggest the details of how the Magick works, but the final call gets left to the Storyteller.

                                  But that raises the question of whether it should always be HAB, or if the RBD mages should also operate under HOO.

                                  My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                          It's always the player's choice on what the effect does. You can get a taxi and manipulate traffic to get you from A to B with PBD too; it just takes more Spheres to account for actually doing all of the steps (aka the process in PBD) instead of just having to determine the result in Sphere selection. The player always determines the intent of the effect; including what it's ideal results look like (qualified because of things like opting for a partial success).

                                          If you have a Mage with every Sphere at 5, and Arete 10, the difference between PBD and RBD is nearly meaningless (maybe a slight difference in base difficulty here or there, or the number of successes needed). At the maximum bounds, RBD cannot do more than PBD. RBD is however less restrictive for the average PC mage. In a sense, at average PC levels RBD can "do more" but only because it reduces the Sphere requirements on many effects significantly, and Spheres are expensive to raise so it takes a very long time (or starting with a lot of extra dots) to start to feel the gap close.

                                          RBD allows for more explanations without increasing the mechanical burden on the effect. The explanations are theoretically available to PBD as well, but the PBD approach means many explanations are effectively cut off because the mechanical burden gets too high for the PC in question.

                                          The mage wants to use Life to make someone sleepy: That is probably coincident in both PBD and RBD. Also would only require Life, but under RBD the target could "just happen to be sleepy", or "took too much chamomile tea earlier", while under PBD you would have to actively do something to the target system. Is that correct?
                                          No. They're equal in this case. And it's worth remembering that the rest of the game still exists beyond this axis of game-play modes. Rules like, "your effect has to match your Focus," still matter. In either case you need to explain how the effect operates within your PC's Focus as well as what it looks like. Either mode is still "I make them sleepy, and here's how," even if they might put more stress on some aspects of the explanations than others.

                                          This also raises another question, wouldn't RBD be like a constant war between player and ST.
                                          It can be. But it depends on the group. Even hardcore PBD + HOO proponents can run into this because Mage is a game with a high degree of player agency and powers that are designed to encourage creative application. Whichever interpretative approach a table takes, the dynamics of ST and player interactions is very quickly going to push towards an antagonistic or a cooperative approach because of the inherent basis of how the game works.

                                          The mage wants to get invisible and walk past the guard: Under PBD you can get literally invisible with vulgar magick and just walk past, or try to use shadows or other distractions to avoid detection, but this would only mean that you get some stealth bonus (or the target gets perception penalties). While under RBD you could just say the shadows and distractions happened and the mage walked past the guard, no additional roll or penalties needed. Both would require Forces 2. Is everything here correct?
                                          RBD doesn't change the mechanics. If you're running RBD + HOO, in this example the RBD would still result in a difficulty modifier because that's how the base mechanics model making yourself more stealthy by magically taking advantage of shadows and timing to move past the guard. Though I would argue either PBD or RBD could go the difficulty reduction route with a Rank 1 effect, and not just with Forces (Entropy - sense the weakness in the guards' patrol pattern - and Time - knowing the perfect timing to move before a guard or camera can turn around - are both plausible options).

                                          If my analysis is correct, notice that RBD can do anything PBD can and more...
                                          One on hand, yes, RBD is the more permissive of the two options.

                                          That said, again I'll stress, there is the rest of the game to consider as a mitigating factor here. Running the game with RBD is still bound by what the Sphere levels say they can do, how the game describes what the outcome of magical effects are mechanically (dealing X damage, modifying difficulties, etc.), what the Feats of Magic success table says, what the duration table says, the Correspondence "range" table, etc., etc., etc.

                                          An Arete 1 mage under RBD might be able to attempt a bunch of effects that they might not even be allowed to roll for in PBD, but that doesn't mean they insta-succeed as if they rolled a bucket of successes. They're still limited by what the effects can do within the rest of the system, and they're still stuck at 1 success (or 2 with WP) for a single roll as the most they're going to get out of it.

                                                      So, I think the important thing is to stress that RBD vs. PBD is what Spheres you need to use more than anything else. Take everything else out. Ignore HAP vs. HOO because you're in a Sanctum. Assume you've got some decent stats compared to a starting PC but not the maxed out level of stuff.

                                                      The most illustrative examples are when the player wants to do something that has a simple intention, but would be a lot of steps without magic.

                                                      Basically, you're not going to figure this out with examples like "trying to sneak past guards," because even a mortal in the WoD can just try to roll Stealth to get past guards. It's not complicated. Mages can just do it better.

                                                      That's why the "taxi" example is kinda standard. Though part of the context of it that I feel got lost is the Seven Leagues Stride effect that started it. Seven League Stride is an older example Corr effect that was dropped pretty fast because of all of this causing debates. The basic point is that instead of teleporting (obviously vulgar) you could travel faster. This goes back to old Abrahamic faith traditions holding that prophets/saints could simply walk to where they were needed to be and arrive exactly when intended, neither late nor early (if you're getting LotR vibes... that's not accidental on Tolkien's part). There are apocryphal stories of such holy folk being seen just walking normally by people walking in the opposite direction, but they'd be spotted hours or even days worth of walking be travelers that couldn't have possibly seen the same person.

                                                      The "make this work for modern mages, and especially not religious mages," is what led to the "taxi cab plus lots of luck with traffic" effect. The debates started because on one hand "just travel faster" shouldn't require different Spheres for different paradigms/Focuses. If pulling a Seven League Stride is just Correspondence for mages pulling from those faith traditions, then it should also just be Correspondence for a modern gutter punk mage using a taxi cab. What we now condense into the PBD camp was usually arguing that this aren't actually the same effect. The saintly Chorister is creating and following a magical path that doesn't obey the laws of physics to get somewhere faster, so it's just Correspondence, while the taxi cab is moving like a normal taxi cab and the effects use different spheres because the actual effect is the manipulation of the city traffic systems to make the trip as short as possible. What we condense into the RBD camp took the opposite approach that there were the same effect just with different "set dressings" for different styles of magic.

I'm doing my best to be even handed on why people do because I don't think they're wrong to play the game in a fashion they find fun; and you deserve a chance to understand why they do.

                                                          The point to understand is that RBD isn't "being incredibly lucky". It may be appearing to be incredibly lucky.

                                                          In the taxi example, the taxi and the traffic still behave as they do because the Mage did Magic.

                                                          You create the Magic exactly the same way you would do in PBD. Same factors, same requisites, same rolls. You still apply Instruments the same way, and you still must follow your Paradigm in the resulting effect.

                                                          What changes is that instead of accounting for every aspect of the resulting spell, you only accounts for the result. In the taxi example, the result of the spell is to travel fast. However this travel happens, as long as it doesn't give other benefits, isn't important to determine Spheres, Successes, or the roll. It is important for Paradigm, Instruments and Coincidence, though.

                                                          So, what happened? The taxi may be there by coincidence, or even be an entirely magical construct. The traffic may be really easy, or some shortcuts may actually defy the laws of physics, just not in an obvious way. The taxi isn't just a coincidence, because that would be an Entropy effect. The whole thing is, still, Magic. It's just that the Mage doesn't have to account for every detail when defining the spell's parameters.

                                                          Focus on Spheres and number of successes. They're the main difference between RBD and PBD. It's just that PBD accounts for every detail in the process to define them, while RBD will only account for the result.
