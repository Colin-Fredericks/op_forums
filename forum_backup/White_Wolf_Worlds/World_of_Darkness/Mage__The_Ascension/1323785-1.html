            Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
            I've found that M20 summed up something about Focus/Paradigm that I've been doing for awhile and it's worked well: More useful paradigms out compete less useful paradigms even in the Traditions.

            Humans have always been rather flexible in their beliefs and been willing to adjust them (sometimes slowly) when it becomes untenable not to. We put so many loopholes in our societal belief structures that there's very little you can't justify doing if you twist the logic just right.

            The Order of Reason rose to power by disposing of the idea that magic is somehow for the chosen elite to lord over normal folks, and splintered from the Order of Hermes to bring magical power to the mundane and give it to the masses. This was useful because it gave them a resource their enemies didn't have and allowed them to survive and then overcome. The Traditions chose to abandon being strict traditionalists and adopt belief systems that viewed paradigms as individual journeys to the same end state. This created a group narrative that all magical practices come from a neutral equality and only the morality of use and wisdom of enlightenment tempers, and those the Traditions can function as a single entity against the Technocracy despite their wildly disparate ideals and practices.

            Being M20 made this case, I already stopped worrying about "the purple paradigm" (a concept I think is dumb anyway) or using paradigms as sticks. Debates and arguments about whether a mage can't do this, or can't do that, or at least needs to do X first, but no Y first, or either... it's not fun and it's not the actual point of the game.

            Magical style should be a carrot. It is a carrot in the system. Highlight this, make it clear that the more the players take their characters' magical styles into account when describing what and how they're doing it, the more bonuses they get thrown their way, rather than focusing on using paradigm as a stick to deny people things. Use it as a spectrum and let players have some fun taking risks with penalties for stretching beyond their character's norms rather than coming down with the Paradigm Hammer
            This. In my campaigns, the “purple paradigm” is merely the Traditions' unifying narrative (also known as “We Are Gods In Disguise” in the Book of Secrets), and is not a fundamental truth of the setting; the Technocracy has its own competing unifying narrative (“Tech Holds All Answers”) which is at odds with the Traditions' shared worldview but is no less valid. The actual truth of the setting is… up to the Storyteller. In one campaign, I may decide that the Traditions have it right and that “we are gods in disguise”; in another, I may decide that tech does indeed hold all answers. In yet another, they may both be wrong — or both right, in a “blind men and the elephant” sort of way, with the actual truth being largely irrelevant. (This last one is the one I use by default.)

            And yes; paradigm should be a carrot. It tends to be thought of as a stick because the earlier editions described the increase in Arete as a gradual abandonment of paradigm, thus implicitly framing it as training wheels at best and a hindrance to Ascension at worst. I, too, prefer M20's approach where increased Arete doesn't liberate you from your Paradigm; it gives you a deeper understanding of it, and through its subtleties the ability to do things with it that your earlier, more naive self would have thought impossible.

            Though really, none of this has much to do with the Sphere System itself, other than the fact that the Spheres should always be handled through Focus, never just as Spheres. The Spheres are what you can do; your Focus is how and why you do it (Practice is the how, Paradigm is the why).

                                I am not a fan of Dark Ages magic. Ars Magica is a far superior rules system for that specific era of history. It is both more well defined, and the definitions make it far easier if you want to "free-form" it. But that system doesn't work well in a modern time frame, and everything is strictly defined by an Aristotelian/Hermetic Paradigm (one that is far different from Mage, and serves as the default physical laws of the mundane world). And it is a very complex game. The advantage of Mage and Spheres is simplicity. WoD games are much easier to start up and create characters. The Sphere magic system, which does require some self discipline on the player's part, it is easy to bend it to make it emulate whatever you are imagining.

                                I do not want to revert to anything as rigid as old AD&D or tap cards for mana. I would prefer a tad bit more structure, but M20 is structured well enough that I can work with it. I am looking into some of the ideas presented in Awakening. I am not into the setting so much, but I admit that is because it is unfamiliar (and I am HermesFlambeau fanatic). But I am into some of the structured elements. Not all of them. I prefer the Spheres. I am just keen on the idea of having an advantage in leaning and using specific Rotes.

                                                            I actually really do like the Sphere system, but as a roleplayer first and foremost I hate the purple paradigm and the troubles it brings when playing with someone who is trying to powergame. It /is/ much simpler to run than the Foundations and Pillars.
                                                            That said I love the Foundations and Pillars of DA:M the most. I love the thematic-ness of it, and the creative challenge it creates. It really eliminates the presence of the purple paradigm powergame by baking a countermeasure into its mechanics. Lorewise the Foundations and Pillars make much more sense to me than the Sphere System.
                                                            In the end use which one you like, but my advice would be, really only play Mage with people you know, like, and know how they play. Otherwise Mage will just make everyone crazy and/or angry. Its definitely a game where everyone at your table really needs to be on the same page otherwise its not going to be fun for anyone.
