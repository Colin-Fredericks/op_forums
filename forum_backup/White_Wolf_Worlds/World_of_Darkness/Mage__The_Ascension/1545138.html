    A Sci Fi Scenario Book, which Scenarios?

    11-01-2024, 12:01 PM
    I know we aren't scheduled for anymore books period, but this is a fantasy. Picture we get a book with five Sci Fi settings for M:tA. It's a thick book, 280 pages. Each scenario is to get 50 pages and the frame and supporting tools get the other 30.

    The question is "Which Scenarios?"

    Myself, I see the basic scenarios for a Sci Fi rpg setting as, A) Cyberpunk, B) Post-Apocalyptic, C) Exploratory Space Opera a couple of centuries from the present (think Star Trek, but also Alien ), D) Space Opera a millennium or more onward (think Dune, Traveller RPG), and E) Time Travel/Time Wars.

    But how do you see the possibilities? Would you work with my categories or swap them out for other ideas? Some of these settings are traditionally gritty, others tilt glam. Would you lean into that of flip the script?

    Please give your visions and fancies.

                                  I think an important element in any book like this is stepping out of the current Ascension war. The current balance of the Ascension War (Trad vs Tech, the current traditions and conventions, freedom vs safety, culture vs progress, etc.) is not baked into the setting. Each scenario can and should choose an Ascension Conflict that relates to the themes of that setting. If we do keep the Traditions and Convensions and goals that we currently have it should be because the scenario offers a fresh and interesting take on what we thought we knew.

                                  The only two that seem capable of that, to me, are the Time Wars and (strangely) the Cyberpunk settings. The Time Travel game has the most potential to dig into the meat of what each group believes, what they've done, and what shaped them. Cyberpunk, meanwhile, gives the opportunity to see them in action on a dramatically different world and how they would function with different worldly rules.

                                  Now, in one sense, all of the scenarios allow that, but we really only need to hit that idea once. After that it becomes stale. So I'd recommend picking one to carry that water. Of all of them I think Cyberpunk and Near Future Space Opera do it best. NFSO has a lot more potential to spread its wings, though, so I'd stick with Cyberpunk.

                                  For the remaining three, I'd recommend coming up with completely different struggles. Indeed, I'd recommend one that doesn't have an Ascension War per se (post apocalyptic would be a good choice) and one where the nature of the Ascension War is public knowledge (FFSO would work well).

                                  Mage: The Ice-ension: An Epic Game of Reality on the Rink

                                      Digging into cyberpunk a little.

                                      Cyberpunk on the surface seems very Technocratic, but often in a pyrrhic sense. Technology and business are in ascendency, but usually beneath the surface things are in decay. Unintended consequences abound, especially when it comes to the new technology, and things are often put to uses they were never intended to.

                                      So what I see here is a world where the Technocracy was winning, and then they brought fully sapient AIs and androids (complete with Avatars) into the Consensus. This revolutionized the world, but the Technocracy never accounted for Citizen 2.0 being just as opinionated and uncontrollable as Citizen 1.0. They fell into disarray as their timetable was rapidly made obsolete.
                                      It's been a generation since then. On the Technocracy side, they appear to have regained control, but much of that is in appearance. In reality they are fighting an uphill battle to reassert their plans. It's a struggle, but one they intend to win.

                                      On the Traditions side, things look very different. There's a generational struggle between the old guard who are trying to preserve their practices from the world and the new guard who are adapting to it. Overall, though, they are going strong, and while some members are uncomfortable with it, almost every Tradition now has AI members.

                                      This isn't a setting of VAs vs Technocracy. Instead everybody is using AI and Hacking the same way everyone in the standard setting uses cell phones and lightbulbs.

                                      That's my rough crack at the idea, anyways.

                                                  Alien, strangely enough, is closer to Cyberpunk than to Space Opera. What you describe is almost like a war between a society out of a Cyberpunk setting and a society out of a NFSO setting. That's not a criticism in the slightest, its a good setting idea. I just don't know what I would call it or what themes I would play up.

                                                  It reminds me though of an observation about the eras of Star Trek and how they map to the level of communication we had in the real world when the shows/movies came out. That connection to the real world isn't really important here but the distinctions between the eras is.

                                                  Star Trek TOS is a setting where travel is difficult, settlements are small and isolated, and backup is a long way away. The Enterprise is one part an exploratory craft, one part military vessel pulling into a remote port for shore leave, and one part US Marshal riding through town. Events tend to be contained to the local area and communities are small. Socially, Kirk and crew sit at the tension point between the ideals of the Federation and the practical reality of wherever they happen to be.

                                                  Though it isn't as overt as Firefly, TOS is a space western.

                                                  In TNG, on the other hand, travel is much easier and the galaxy more developed and interconnected. The Enterprise D is usually capable of calling up HQ on short notice and frequently visits established planets and civilizations on diplomatic missions. And Picard and crew aren't just representatives of Federation ideals, the ship is big enough that it is an actual example of Federation society. The show is much more about living in this new society and exploring what that means and where it is difficult.

                                                  TNG is closer to what we think of as Space Opera.

                                                  If I had to choose, I'd say Space Western is the better setting for a struggle between idealism and cynicism. The more isolated setting gives room for the cynical elements to run amok and also leverage to strongarm planets and stations. It also puts the players in a situation where they are the ones who have to deal with the situation because the elders are too far away to reasonably help.

                                                          Well the way I would develop it as a Mage scenario is to break out of the current Traditions/Technocracy divide into a conflict of more general ideals.

                                                          What I think would happen is that mages would initially divide themselves up by approaches as they spread out. The Traditions and Technocracy as organizations would dissolve as space kept them out of contact with each other, especially the Traditions. The Trads, Conventions, and Crafts would start down the following approaches:

                                                          There would be the Old Guard that would set up colonies that more or less represented their beliefs. A few of these grow into distant core worlds for the setting. There would also be a handful of smaller but well established worlds.

                                                          There would be the Utopians who tried to institute their experimental societies. Some would succeed as thriving worlds, others would fall into dystopian nightmares.

                                                          Then there would be the ones who weren't interested in spreading their current society, which would include the Adapters who sought to integrate into the worlds they found (e.g. a group of shamans more interested in learning the spiritual ecology of a new planet than holding on to their traditions), the Isolators who retreated from society to focus on their specific goals, and the Explorers who stayed on the move.

                                                          None of these are meant to be factions, by the way, just movements to determine what is out there and how it is set up. I don't see a clean division of each Tradition and Convention into one of these categories, though the majority may favor a certain way.


                                                          Fast forward to several generations later. The colonies are growing big enough to expand out again and travel is getting just a bit easier. Just enough that interstellar organizations can form, but difficult enough that it takes a lot of investment.

                                                          That's the point that this scenario can get started, because the struggle is between the various organizations that start growing out or connecting all these disparate worlds. Who they are, why they want to do this, and what they hope to accomplish are the core questions. That should give good grounds for both bright and noble factions and dark, rapacious ones.

                                                          My first thoughts for themes is Ideals vs Idealism or perhaps Ideals vs Ideology. The clash between what is right and the system that needs to be fed.

                                                          The details of what ideals are out there and what ideologies are spreading would evolve from which mages joined each of the above movements and which colonies/outposts/endeavors were successful.
