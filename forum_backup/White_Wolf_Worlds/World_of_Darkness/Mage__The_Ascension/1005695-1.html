    So to summarize what has mentioned so far, I see four distinct categories for Time 1 (and again only Time 1)

    1. Detect / Analyze Temporal Distortion (covering time travel, time scrying, time loops, etc)
    2. Perfect Chronometer (covering accurate passage and measurement of time, which, personally, I can see helping with Ability checks when coordination or timing with something already perceived is important, but not with unknown schedules, plans, intentions or other occurrences not yet observed... That's where Time 2, Entropy or Mind start get involved)
    3. Detect / Analyze Age (knowing exactly how old something is by seeing how much of the time stream that pattern has been exposed to)
    4. Adjust Natural Time Sense (mess with another's ability to gauge time. Very limited as it would have to be willing and via touch, but if we can get an answer about if sharing senses with objects, it could still be useful to mess with clocks)

    So, are there any other _categories_, not specific examples that anyone else can think of.

    (I.e. I am not wanting an exchanges on if specific effect X could/couldn't work with just Time 1. That will always be up for ST approval and house rules. I am looking for whole new categories)

                                Stop on a dime bit:
                                Ok, fair point. Perhaps you'd need a bit of Mind 1 to sync up your reactions with your time sense.

                                I'd argue, however, that Perfect Time is more than just "knowing the amount of time it takes" it's about feeling the flow of time, being able to incorporate rhythm into your senses. You can how long someone takes to run away and roughly where they'll be if they keep running at that speed, so you can adjust your aim to compensate for that and thus it's easier to shoot them as they run.

                                Similarly in a fistfight, if you've been fighting for a while you should be able to tell the rhythm of their moves and then be able to parry and doge much more effectively. Sort of like how Studied Combat is described for the Investigator Class in Pathfinder.


                                Paradigm and Sphere bit:
                                Is it really any different than saying your Biologist could change hair colour with Matter 2/Life 2 instead of just Life 3?

                                Anyway, the idea of this came from several sources.

                                Time 3 rote Time Warp:-
                                "Naturally, this spell is so difficult and specific that very few mages use it at all. Some paradigms just don't accommodate the idea of "rewinding time" while others facilitate it."
                                This is what first fuelled the idea for me that there spheres are not the same for everyone. It states that my Time 3 is not the same as another's Time 3, and that this difference in how the spheres function between mages stems from paradigm, the basic tenements in belief of what these elements of reality include and how they operate, and thus the logic should be that Paradigm and Belief should dictate how certain patterns are affects by certain spheres.

                                Forces:-
                                "Every magical style has a different means of addressing these forces. Science considers certain forces to be the absence of others, while mystical Traditions usually consider negative and positive forces - heat and cold, light and dark, motion and stillness - to be opposite but independent entities."
                                So if Light and Dark are independent entities, it stands to reason that you'd need Forces 3 to transform light into dark, but Forces 2 to move dark around, or move light around. Otherwise you're ignoring the mystic paradigm.

                                Entropy:-
                                "Mages who study Entropy often choose the path of either order or chaos. Students of order look into patterns, into the ways that events flow from one to the next and into the means by which new things build on the old. Students of chaos study the dissolution of patterns, the destruction of matter and the random elements that infect happenstance and probability."
                                So, it stands to reason that mages who are more confident with "order" and believe in the concept of fate will have a much easier time looking into their fate and intuiting their outcome. While mages who study "chaos" and think all forms of order are nothing more than an illusion would find it much more difficult to "feel the fate" that simply isn't there, and instead would have to force reality to give them a path.

                                Then there's that bit in HDYDT called "A bit of spirit" which addresses the issue that paradigms based on "all universal forces are actually spirits" means that all magic a mage does is essentially Spirit magic, and so would at least need Spirit 1 in everything that they do. This too fuels the idea that paradigm shapes how the spheres are required rather than the spheres themselves defining the effects.

                                Essentially what I'm saying is that the logic that goes behind the effect from the point of view of the mage should dictate the sphere level required, rather than just matching like-with-like rotes to sphere levels. This is also a good way to combat the ever present Purple Paradigm. If my spheres are different from your spheres, then there simply can't be a purple paradigm as our magic is inherently different by proxy of our understanding of the arts.

                                Remember, it's not reducing or increasing sphere use, it's using the logic of the paradigm to dictate what the definitions of the sphere levels are referring to.


                                Purple Jedi bit:
                                Really easy way to combat this. Just like the Dream Speaker needs a bit of spirit in all their effects, your Jedi needs a bit of Mind in all of theirs. Raising Physical Attributes would therefor be a Mind1/Life1 rote for them. So yes, everything is just "mind" to them, but the way their mind influences "Life" is how they study the Life sphere and how their mind influences movement is how they study the Forces sphere. Going further, you could say that no sphere can be higher than their rank in Mind. The trade off could be that everything they are doing is still treated as a Mind effect, meaning that most of the effects are harder to detect if you're not looking for Mind patterns, or that dispelling their effects is impossible without similar ranks in Mind.


                                Dot on the face bit:
                                Problem is that the number of successes doesn't reflect the relative complexity of these tasks either.

                                Going RAW, healing yourself for 4 points of Lethal should only require 2 Successes.
                                Putting a dot on your face, however, would need 1 success for being a simple feat, and at least 1 other success so it lasts longer than an instant. so at least 2 successes (3 if you want it to last a day).
                                So with healing 4 points Lethal requiring an equal amount if not fewer successes than putting a dot on your face, to say the former requires Life 2 while the latter Life 3 is to ignore biological complexity for the sake of making everything fit into these neat little sphere boxes by sheer category. And I for one think it's a shame to stifil the system in such a way.
