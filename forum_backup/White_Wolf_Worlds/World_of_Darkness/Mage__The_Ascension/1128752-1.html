                                Nephandi apologists are somewhat amazing.

                                There are things that, currently, to us, our time and our culture are not okay. Some of them are weird. Killing lots of people in an action-adventure sense is much more okay than, say, showing people having sex. But there are MANY things that are deep, serious taboos: cannibalism, incest, slavery are a few outstanding examples, but there are reasons that those and other topics that are repeatedly called out with trigger warnings in the source books. They are things that, on a gut level, our society and culture think are just plain BAD. Sure, they may not have always been. And they may not be now to particular groups, but, in general, people who are okay with them are effectively othered with extreme prejudice. For example, it's just not okay to eat other people (even though cannibalism has ranged from being common practice to a religious sacrament depending on time and place).

                                That seems to be the intent behind the Nephandi as a group. They the things that exist and the things that people do that aren't just not okay, they're fundamentally abhorrent to us (here and now). They are that personified, like the Marauders, Technocracy, Traditions and Disparates (the last two being close to the same thing with different emphasis) are personifications of their own things.

                                It's easy to try to make backflips to justify Nephandi as something basic or fundamental or the "guys who really know the TRUTH", but it's largely sophistry. It's trying to redefine the inherently immoral as moral. It's trying to prove that negative is positive. I mean, look how the arguments usually go... Starts with: "There is no objective truth/right or wrong....". Ends with "The Nephandi are the ones who know the TRUTH and are the ones who are right, while everyone else is wrong."

                                Genuine evil qua evil in the here and now is a very abstract concept until and unless you face it. Spending time at a murder scene or having the occasion to talk to a serial killer and the Nephandi don't really seem all that hard a concept to grasp.

                                                            I agree with Ajax. While it would be all well and good to argue about a version of the Nephandi that are a persecuted, misunderstood minority, that is not a version of the Nephandi that is supported seriously in any Mage: The Ascension text. We have no alternative to the idea of the Nephandi being categorically evil presented in the 20+ years of material printed about them. If that seems one-sided and weird, I don't know what to tell you. Except that this is how it is.


                                                            On another note, I want to challenge the proposition that targeting Widderslainte for termination is morally wrong. Not because I don't agree that it is bad. But I see it less as "reprehensible", and more "unfortunate". Regardless of whether a Widderslainte can truly be seen as responsible for their evil actions, they still engage in evil actions. Indeed, the fact that they can't choose NOT to be evil makes it all the more important that they be eliminated. To paraphrase Isaac Asimov's robot novels, a Nephandus is Logical, but not Reasonable. Which is to say, they may have the ability to think abstractly and plan and other things Sapient beings do, but they do so according to value judgments completely antithetical to the values of humans. It just "feels right" to a Nephandus - and especially to a Widderslainte - to do evil things.

                                                            When you have a being like that, their responsibility for their actions - or lack thereof - becomes a moot point. They NEED to be taken care of, because they don't play nice with the rest of humanity. They are a threat, and bald, simple pragmatism cannot permit them to run unchecked. Any more than one can reasonably allow a rabid, angry dog to run in the streets. It's the same reason I personally advocate the Joker be executed, if not by Batman then by the state. He may be too mad to truly be responsible for his actions, but his actions are uncontrollable, uncontainable, and unconscionable nonetheless. After a certain point - after the Joker has escaped Arkham and gone on a killing spree for the 100th time - you just have to throw up your hands and say, "The clown can't be cured, and is too dangerous to be left alive". I'm surprised an angry mob in Gotham never tried to rip him from a squad car and string him up from a lamp post, because they were simply sick of dealing with him.

                                                            With the Nephandi, it's the same, but on the scale of entire factions. You don't think regular Mages have tried to reform the Nephandi? That they haven't attempted to listen to their side of the story? The Fallen have been a thorn in the sides of the Awakened for thousands of years. The other factions don't call for the Fallen to be annihilated because they've rushed to a prejudiced judgment. They call for their destruction because they've been burned ALL THE TIMES, and have given up trying.

                                                            Fool me once, shame on me. Fool me for thousands of years, I'm fairly comfortable shooting you on sight. While morality may demand that one keep trying to save someone until it gets done, people are weak, and have only so much patience. And all of this while the Nephandi have spent every waking moment doing horrible things, consorting with Actual Demons from Actual Hell (and other places), and trying to get good people to go bad.

                                                            Even if the Nephandi ARE misunderstood, surely you can forgive the rest of Mage-kind for thinking otherwise.
