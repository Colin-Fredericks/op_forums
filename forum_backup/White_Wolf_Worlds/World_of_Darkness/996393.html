    Using modernity/realistically obtainable items which can unballance the game.

    10-31-2016, 10:06 PM
    So I wanted to know what people's opinions about this were.
    My storyteller wanted my neonate, a brujah on paradox, who runs a small-town motorcycle club with his ravnos friend in a solo game, to defend his territory from a werewolf pack (which he tells me is derived from vampire stats rather than werewolf stats, as if that makes me feel better) . The most logical thing to do (other than leaving or falling into huge debt) would be to acquire some large caliber guns for 'big game' and a few silver rounds for trusted officers. I don't particularly feel like this is me trying to powergame, I think this is just a logical character trying to equalise himself against vastly superior opponents. If you're going against 9 foot tall, hulk of fur,fury and claw, you want a big gun. But, that said, I think if you're a human going against resilient vampires, big guns often seem like a safer choice than swords and superstitious nonsense.

    Now the issue is that the game suggests rifles (the most powerful ranged weapon on the table with shotguns) do 8 damage. With the listed rifle firing 30.06.
    Now, even throwing aside 'silly' weapons like anything that fires a .50 bmg, there are several popular civilian rounds that do significantly more than a 30.06, such as a .300 or .375 or a .338 magnum or... I'm no gun expert, I just did a casual bit of research and cross compared weapons with the more extensive tables of mage. In the USA, there are commonly legal civilian arms that should do upwards of 10 dice of damage. freely available for folks who want to hunt bear or other large predators. My storyteller rather dislikes the idea of using anything that threatens ballance, despite the game world being somewhat set over the real world. Meanwhile, Hunters the hunted points out the uses of science and crafts in making stuff you're not supposed to make, or using connections to get a hold of things you're not supposed to get a hold of. It's hard to balance with that mentality, but I'm a strong man for story first, mechanics second.

    I tend to blame and rebalance supernatural powers like fortitude not up to the task of defending against the modern arsenal, whilst he does the other way and dislikes anything that isn't ballanced for supernatural players (which typically means smaller guns, and using the werewolf section in the X book rather than werewolves from the familiar W:TA) I think we've all at one point had a player that asked for incendiary rounds on a vampire hunt. I think it absolutely makes almost common sense that, if you're expecting to fight vampires, werewolves, or anything else with a considerable soak pool,and you're perhaps something far weaker who can't afford to get close: big guns are common sense

    I don't hunt, but 30.06 seems to be the middle hunting round for north america (and would be considered inadequate for some hunting in africa.)
    The dice system isn't accurate, and it'd be a pain in the ass for everyone if I tried to make a precise system based on calculations from how many dice example rounds get. It's guestimates to work out how much damage in dice something should do based on measured impact force or how much it penetrates.

    9mm rounds do 4 damage
    30.06 does 8 damage
    .50 rounds do 16 damage (it's in mage)

    Pistols/Submachineguns. 2-6
    Assault rifles 6-8
    anti personal snipers, battle rifles both bolt action and semi auto- 6-12
    Anti material guns 12+

    Hunting rifles:
    Varmint rifles 2-4
    deer/sheep 4-8
    Elk/small bears 6-10
    Moose/Large bears 8-12
    Elephants,Rhinos,hippo,. 10-14

    .50 calliber bullets are legal in the US. They're probably the guns we think of when we want to go for 'big'
    20mm guns, the cut off point between anti-material rifle and cannons, are also available in the USA for civilians (in at least some states) In comparison, a .50bmg is 12.7mm.
    There are no stats for 20mm guns. However, a 30mm cannon suggests 15 dice of damage, half armour protection (I don't know what that means outside of mage, since mages are more often dependent on armour to soak in comparison to the various less human characters who more often rely on natural/supernatural resistance rather than 'traditional' armour)
    Giving more dice isn't the answer. A limb lost to a cannon isn't going to be more deadly than a limb lost to an axe, there's only so much damage you can do to one place before damaging it further stops being relevant to the rest of the body.
    possible solutions could be to increase the difficulty of soaking. Reduce soak dice by X, exchange some dice for levels, or reduce the damage difficulty.

    This isn't just about weapons. We could include armour, the crazy things you could come up with using 5+ resources or 5+ influence, or anything in mage.

    Say armour. Not anyone can get a hold of the technocracy's latest, but Anyone can do themselves a Ned Kelly. Someone with supreme capabilities, say a vampire or formor, is not limited by what a human could possibly wear, and they are usually well off enough to make special orders. Hundreds of layers of balistic fiber, A thick tugsten variation of italian white armour, a scaled up set for a guy in crinos... Mechanics as is, a vamp or formor could have greatly increased dexterity to deal with penalties of 4+ (logically, this'd have far more to do with strength, but that's even easier to obtain)

    Honestly, such armour in vampire is more practical as it keeps the masquerade somewhat better than increasing stamina to 10 before a firefight, rather than in it's own right as protective gear (though plenty of armour and the grossly underpowered fortitude can together be good enough to survive a garou swipe,maybe... If the lupine is an unlucky pup-theurge of the ronin and her enemy is a great 4th gen ravnos methuselah, who had nine fortitude on her chimerical clone)

    Back to the main question What is your opinion on using the imbalances of reality in a game setting that uses the real world?

    Signatures are for terrible people who want to be caught.

          I just gave my players three nuclear warheads. Literally. They know better than to think that's a good thing. Basically if you give them something make sure that there are consequences for using it. If you have a signature weapon it makes you traceable, the bigger the easier. If you use non-street weapons openly like automatic rifles, grenades, claymores, a 1 gauge shotgun etc. you'll get the FBI, CIA, MI5 or others on your case. Your players aren't experts in forensics most likely.

          If you use legal weapons that are rare and powerful you will leave an identifiable trail and no weapon in WOD works well against everything. Plus enemies will adjust their tactics. Shoot .50 cal? Do you have any idea how complex long-range sniping can be? What are the odds you'll take out innocents with a stray shot when it can go through walls like they were butter? Shooting phosphorous rounds? What are the odds you're going to start a fire? Bombs? Do you even know how to safely handle those in all conditions? Are you aware enough of your chemistry to keep your dynamite cool and protected so it doesn't start to 'sweat' the ultra-dangerous contact explosive that is nitro? Can you wire a remote bomb without blowing your own hands off?

          And then there are the mitigating factors. Got a big gun? Great, they're just going to burn down your haven, use remote powers or jam your gun with movement of the mind at a safe distance and then wreck you, or get a fortitude tank that can run through your bullets like they were a cloud of annoying bees. Going up against a 5th gen Malk? They just used deny and your gun doesn't exist in their reality anymore. A Lasombra? They just jammed the barrel with shadow matter. A Tremere? They just locked your hammer, put your safety on or straight up ignited the bullets causing a misfire. A Setite? They just cursed your weapon to break down. A Ventrue? "Miss me". There is a reason most ancients see guns as a cute novelty. Mages have similar outs and tricks they can employ, and Garou? Well, Garou hunt in packs and keep going until you're shreds. Sure, you can kill a couple but after that? Good luck.

              There's a lot to this question, and I'll have to cut short my answer, but I'll do my best.

              First, I think you guys kind of didn't get the game fairly well. You're looking for balance in the wrong side of town. This game in particular isn't balanced at all to begin with, but I digress.

              Just don't look for balance in the things the characters can get their hands on. In this game your sheet is about what you can do for yourself, or your ability to amass resources, not about the specific things. The specific things are setting-determined, and they aren't meant to be balanced.

              What makes it work are the three Cs: Context, Consequences and Countermeasures.
              Context means that the characters live in a limited condition, not in the Matrix White Room. To acquire any given weapon they need time, resources, access, and it isn't so simple to have them. Yes, you can try to use a cannon with silver bullets so big that targeting isn't very hard. But is it available? Where? All the money in the world won't do anything if no one can actually get the gun and put it on your hands.
              Consequences, or Costs, mean more than the amount of resources you have at hand. Ok, so you got your big gun. You have all the papers? At some places it is really easy to get permission on such material, but isn't necessarily instantaneous. And even if you got, what it means? You have the gun registered? So it is traceable back to you. The gun leaves a kind of signature on the bullet. How will you explain the bodies of people dead by your personal big gun that should be used only on big game? And what your neighbors think of that crazy thing? You can get your gun, but the world will react accordingly. Hide it, show it, buy it, steal it, register it, keep it illegal, every decision about this acquisition will have its own results.
              Countermeasures is the simplest of all elements. Just remember that your enemies live in the same world as you and have access to the same stuff. They can call the police. They can get their own big guns. They can bring a truck and try to put the entire building down. They'll face their own Consequences and do it in their own Context, but they can as much as you. It is actually one of the themes of the setting: the old monsters are powerful, but also out of touch with technology. This is considered a weakness because it is.

              If you guys want the best out of the setting, think outside the box. Supernatural powers didn't evolved to deal with high technology, and isn't balanced with it. Nor it should. It presents its own benefits in the way it can't be traced the way technology can, it can't be dismantled in the way technology can, it is usually far more available. Because claws aren't nearly as deadly as a Tommy Gun. But you'll draw a hell of a lot of attention if you casually walk down the street with a Tommy Gun.

              And I should point, the kind of situation your ST want to throw you in, is the kind of situation that demands preparation and lateral thinking. You can't reasonably fare against an entire team of anything. But you can try to outsmart your opponents in several ways, and I think Big Guns are a fair option, if not the only one.

              On more mechanical issues, your math isn't half-bad. You could make a lot of research about fights and weapons, and you'll see a lot of common lies about a lot of things, but it doesn't have to be a thing. Just go for what works for you. Be it more damage, or less ability to avoid said damage, don't think too much about reality, and more on how it'll work for your game. Believe me, when modeling injuries, reality is a far weirder place, were people can die of falling on dirt and babies can survive dozens of stabs, because injuries work a way a world apart from our what the system can manage.
