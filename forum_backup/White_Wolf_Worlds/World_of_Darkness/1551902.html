          I don't think it was by design. First, because nowhere in the text is said that the powers of the Fallen work in a fundamentally different, "weaker" way, and second because everything indicates the Angels work under almost the exact same rules (the three distinctions being that they don't necessarily have Torment, they have an infinite source of temporary Faith, and they don't feel the pull of the Abyss). Everything seems to indicate that the difference between a regular Fallen and an Angel should be just in the raw number of dots.

          There are other possible considerations, but they cut both ways: Earthbound have extra empowering mechanics, but it isn't clear if they're something Angels had before. It isn't clear if the Generals of Lucifer use the same rules as other Earthbound or not, it isn't clear if they're weaker or stronger than the average Angel, or even than the top dogs of the Angelic Hosts. Lucifer himself appears a bunch of times (more than any other Highest-End monster in the WoD) and seems to follow the same rules as everyone, but maybe not, and he didn't suffered most of the things that could have weakened other Fallen.

          All things considered, while the interpretation that the Lores are weak by design is possible, I don't see real, consistent evidence. And they're not consistently weak, either, they're all over the place. Some are quite strong, and more than a few have at least one capability that is completely (or almost) out of reach to anyone else (like how by the description of the Lore of Awakening, objects animated by it aren't just animated, but effectively alive, whatever that means).

          What it does seem is just that the Lores are badly designed. Partly as a result from being hastily done, partly because it was the first-and-only edition of the game, so there was no feedback to inform changes. An actual design choice for them being weak would be more clearly stated and consistent.

          Now, this isn't necessarily terrible as far as balance goes, as laughable as trying to balance splats is within WoD. Demons have some ludicrous advantages: they're better than even Mages at finding out things and seeing through concealment, they're virtually unkillable without ST intervention and completely immune to mind/emotion control, not to say that Faith is ridiculously easy to replenish in comparison with any other supernatural fuel.

                  As far as I know, nope. I can't say I've seen everything, far from that, but I did seen some discussions on this topic before. All the attempted solutions I saw stick to the 5 dot ladder system that is a staple of the WoD (even Werewolf uses it in a sense, as you still rank Gifts from 1 to 5).

                  Most try to do one of three things: redesign the Lores within the same structure; use Mage's Sphere system, with or without tweaks; use the rules from Devil's Due and ignore DtF (they are the same beings, just not the same set of rules). None is particularly what you're looking for, although you may like DD and pick it (I personally don't like it).

                  Originally posted by Metrere Oil View Post
                  How so? I do remember Demons having the ability to sense all supernatural stuff going on in miles wide radius, but how would they be better at gathering information in general?
                  This sense is the main reason, but not the only one. Remember, though, that it is a skill roll with information scaling to successes, so a Demon may learn a lot about the power being used. And well, they sense every single power in miles. Seeing through concealment adds to that, as their particular way of piercing illusions completely ignores the ability and power of their creator, and work on any kind of concealment.

                  Add to that what they can do with their Names. While a True Name is obviously a secret to be kept, a Demon may spy on anyone that says their Celestial Name, and that's not something other denizens of the WoD are aware of. Dropping your name around is somewhat risky, but can let you witness a lot of conversations of interest. In fact, one simple move you can make is to tell a Thrall your Celestial Name (which you're probably going to do anyways for other benefits) and let them be interrogated later by your target. When the Thrall reveals the name of their master, you instantly get a VIP access to the interrogation. It isn't without risk, but being creative you can do a lot with that.

                  You can also leverage your other abilities. You can make your Thralls better information gatherers, you can make suicidal recon missions knowing you'll come back in a new body, you can simply use the fact you're a divine being (albeit a Fallen one) to convince people to tell you things, even to convince them that you already know anything you need. This isn't something only Demons can do, but in my experience it is generally easier to talk your way through getting good info when you're a Demon, even if you're just talking BS.

                  Oh, and I forgot to mention early: Thralls are possibly the most useful and easy to make kind of "Supernatural Retainer" in WoD.

                      I experimented with an alternative power system for Wraith. A while back there was a discussion about whether Arcanoi count as magic/sorcery which somehow led to speculation about Dark Ages Wraith and in that context, how Arcanoi would look like before the formalization (and honestly, gatekeeping) by the Guilds.

                      The TLDR of the discussion is that Arcanoi are more like Abilities that extend mundane Talents and Skills to the metaphysics that Wraiths operate in - it's more like learning to drive a car than channeling mystic energy to wrap reality.

                      I tried to capture that idea with a more dynamic, less linear Arcanoi system.

                      You get an additional stat block that are basically keywords for Wraith specific Knowledge: Shadow, Psyche, Fetters, Passion, Pathos, Corpus, Tempest, Fate etc.

                      You get dots 1-5 as usual, with each level representing the extent of what you can do with these things:
                      1. Perceive/Evaluate
                      2. Manipulation (self)
                      3. Manipulation (other)
                      4. Mend/Damage
                      5. Transform/Enable/Disable

                      1 Pathos cost per level
                      Roll Attribute + Ability

                      For example, if you wanted to shape the Corpus of another Wraith you would need Corpus 3 and do a Dex + Craft roll.

                      I never fleshed this out. You run into unclear use cases a lot and there would probably be many situations in which the descriptors of the power levels don't fit.

                                      Yes, the answer stands.

                                      Basically, those attempts at correcting Lores don't change the basic mechanics too much, they're still Disciplines with added Torment versions.

But yeah, those attempts didn't even deviate that much, they just reworked the Lore's powers for whatever the author thought to be more balanced than the book's version.
