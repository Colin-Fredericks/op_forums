    Little things to flesh out the WoD

    01-02-2016, 01:52 PM
    What little touches to you add to your games to distinguish the worlds from reality? I mean, the big stuff is obvious, but little things that might be subtle reminders to players and whatnot that it's not our world.
    I was thinking things like extra gargoyles on buildings, maybe RICO never was passed so more organized crime, that kind of thing.

      At the start of a new story, I usually have a handout with fake Weekly World News articles that talk about (in veiled terms, of course) various goings on in different supernatural factions mixed in with "probably-not-true" stories. Every so often when there is a television near where the coterie are doing stuff, some news story will mention a crime that sounds suspiciously like [insert supernatural thing here] activity, though whenever they end up investigating matters it's some crazed mortal/mortals causing the problem.

      Public works issues always seem to stall out and take forever. New York City is still cleaning up from 9/11 and Hurricane Sandy, stalled even further from the '08 economic crisis to the point where a good portion of the residential sections of the city are made up of dilapidated, abandoned homes. Emergency services, in every major city, are overworked and take forever to address any emergencies. This works out well for the coterie when the police fail to investigate some trouble they cause, but bit them on the butt when someone's haven was burning to the ground.

      But the biggest little thing is the five to ten minute "cutscene" at the beginning of every session, where someone's ghoul or childer is out doing some errand they were tasked with and I describe the general ambivalence of the world. Muggings happening right in the middle of the street with people just crossing the street to not get involved, some clearly deceased drunk laying against a building that nobody bats an eye at, car alarms (complete with audio files on the laptop) always going off somewhere, boarded up businesses... These are the norm, always mentioned in some iteration or other. It reminds the players that this is a world where hope is dying, if not dead already.

          I call it the Johnny Fontaine rule after the character in the Godfather who is obviously based on Frank Sinatra, but not called that. If I run a historical chronicle, I will use similar analogues so that characters can interact with those NPCs, but which won't bother me if they kill them, embrace them, or otherwise alter the timeline. If the PCs interact with them in any way, I use a fictional analogue.

          I typically raid other historical fiction and cinematic universes that do the same. The work of James Clavell is one - Japan was ruled by the Toranaga Shogunate instead of the Tokugawa, and Struan's exists instead of Jardine-Matheson. There is also the Big Kahuna Burger franchise from the Quentin Tarantino movies. I include references to Project: Tallhat during discussions of conspiracy theories from the stories of Laird Barron. Stephen King's US Department of Scientific Intelligence (the Shop) also exists and is part of Project: Twilight.I also take concepts or names from Call of Cthulhu supplements like the heavy metal band God's Lost Children (GLC). Typically if there is a phrase or idea from a book or movie that sounds good off the tongue, I will use it even if I don't use it as it originally existed.

          Some things grow more organically based on conversations with the players during game. Therefore, "Chicago style burritos" exist in the chronicle in the same way that Chicago style hot dogs and pizzas exist.

          I also like to emphasize obscure or failed companies so that they are much more prevalent. The Hudson Bay's Company is a large retailer in the United States, and A&P is still the largest supermarket in North America. Nissan cars are still known as Datsun.

          I also use those elements of the official setting that I like. Horatio Ballard took the place of Philip Danforth Armour in history; so there is Ballard Square in Chicago, not Armour Square, and the Armour & Co (which became Dial Corporation) doesn't exist, but the Ballard Corporation does (as does Ballard soap). Black Dog Game Factory exists instead of White Wolf as does a few other Pentex subsidiaries although I never address the subject of if they are Wyrm tainted.

          If I created any kind of major organizations as part of the background of my own PCs in WoD games, they usually exist in some format in my home chronicle.

          I also add lots of local folklore - which is probably the most Gothic thing I add.

          I try to include just enough to show this is not "our" reality even though most of it is familiar. As you can see, they tend to be subtle more often than not.

              I've never run my own games as an ST (though I'm running a few plots in my local MES games), but there are a few ideas I have (a few of which I've used in my plots):

              Warehouses and warehouse districts are almost everywhere (I almost said "werehouse," which would be an especially unusual type of shifter...); if there's not a warehouse nearby, there's some kind of abandoned building - a fast food place that shut down, an abandoned church, a closed down supermarket. This is less prevalent in downtown areas, but definitely a thing on the outskirts of town or in the suburbs

              Obviously for Vampire, games aren't going to be happening mid-day anyways, but for most games, they never happen at or around noon (unless it's a larp happening around then, and it's a bit too much disbelief to suspend); even if it's not night, the sky is always darker from being earlier or (more often) later in the day, and often there are clouds to dampen the sun.

              There are a lot more highly ornate gravestones and mausoleums. Regular headstones still exist, but the really iconic statues and structures are more common features. Graveyards also are always surrounded by tall, wrought iron black fences.

              Unless the geology is absolutely against it, there are all sorts of subterranean structures under the cities (this is one I'm using in a plot I'm running in my local W:tA LARP). Sometimes, these are ancient ruins holding treasure or bound monsters; other times, they are warrens for the Nosferatu; and still others, they are well-maintained secret lairs (not a cWoD example, but I always picture the Lucifuge headquarters as a giant subterranean cylinder, topped by a dome, under Milan, with bookshelves wrapping around the walls).

                  Flourishes of Gothic architecture, like gargoyles, flying buttresses, and stained glass even when it might not be structurally appropriate, and mostly in disrepair or decay; police often wear riot gear, even when just on patrol, and even security guards wear very obvious vests; public parks often neglected and overgrown, potholes, every third streetlight broken; myriad garish neon signs, mostly advertising bail bonds and fast food or liquor; at least one condemned building or house on each block, usually fenced off with rusty chainlink or boarded over... and all this is in addition to what the book describes.

                  These are just off the top of my head - I'm sure I'll remember more when I get home and can look at my notes.
