        Right?
        We've already covered that the Imbued are anti-monsters. They've been tapped by some forces representing life and death to take some form of action relating to monsters. What makes them arguable more human than mages is that they're trying to preserve what's valuable to them and prevent the world from crashing down around them completely, which involves staying with the lives that they had rather than sitting in fortresses of solitude and working ancient rituals to some esoteric effect, or parking their butts in Horizon and forgetting about the world from which they came from a few centuries longer than they would have been otherwise able to live. In fact, they have only the most fleeting access to the spirit worlds, so they are completely incapable of fleeing the world of flesh and matter for another. This is far more significant than possibly being able to light up an object to hit someone (a point which the books waffle on constantly and which have been argued about more than a few times on the internet), because it keeps them grounded in the business they were occupied with before the Messengers chose them for another job. Hopefully, hindsight will endow the next iteration of H:TR with mechanics to encourage not abandoning everything and moving off the grid. Only one Creed really has an excuse to do that, and not all of them should, in spite of being blatantly labeled Hermits.

                                If the Embrace destroys the soul, what do vampires claim when they commit diablerie on each other?

                                Anyhoo, Hunter the Reckoning's biggest problem (imho) is that it lacked enough variation or sustainability as a major gameline. It was perfect for something like Orpheus, which was a limited run. However, HtR has to deal with the fact that its only purpose is to act as a foil to the other lines. Even Mummy and Kindred of the East (which were supplement lines originally, as they required Vampire to run) had their own internal struggles that sustained their own purpose. Sure, they both dealt with Cainites, but they didn't have to. If a Hunter doesn't hunt the monsters, (s)he isn't a hunter. Hunters Hunted (either edition) was a supplement book that contained different kinds of Hunters, with differing abilities and origins. Demon Hunter X (while terrible) was also a proper supplement book which acted as a foil to its respective arena, and neither it nor Hunters Hunted required a complete game line with their own splat books beyond the core.

                                HtR could have used Conviction as an additional means to fuel willpower, like True Faith, with the belief in oneself rather than a higher power (instead a higher purpose). Rather than a wonky resource for angel (or alien depending on mood) powers. The Sight could have been a complete constant ("you cannot unsee the hidden world!") or a flicker that appeared when needed, or not, with an entirely unknown origin. Either could have better served the capacity for storytelling better than "I spend my bloo... err, Conviction to activate my superpowers, do I see any weird shit?" Now, I will admit the Messengers are an awesome fluff piece for the story. I just didn't (and still don't) think that HtR needed the hunters to be able to create force fields with their minds or summon flaming swords to fight monsters. Hell, just being immune to their control powers is a huge damn edge to fighting them anyone, as well as the ability to see through their illusions. Could have even extended the Angel innate abilities further (without Lore or Theurgy nonsense) that they have by giving them more, or even all, of the innate abilities from Demon the Fallen. The ability to pattern sense even if they don't understand what they are sensing is an awesome one.

                                Oh, and yes... Vigil is better. That isn't even a subjective opinion. It clearly has more options, totally internal problems to deal with, doesn't require anything but the corebook (including the WoD one) to play properly, and it has the Lucifuge. Everything is better with the Lucifuge.
