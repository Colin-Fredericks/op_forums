                A darker Union isn't representative of scientists; it's representative of technocrats:
                Technocracy: the government or control of society or industry by an elite of technical experts.

                The argument isn't that the Technocracy would deliberately inflict suffering on people — although with the Technephandi metaplot, just might — it's that they're likely to be more interested in “not letting a crisis go to waste”: there are things you can do during a crisis that you wouldn't be able to do otherwise. Despite the video that CTPhipps linked to (which leads heavily into the Fallen Technocracy), I don't think that the Union would be doing nothing to stop climate change, the pandemic, or whatever other crisis you wish to bring up; it's that, at the very least, they're likely to Be multitasking, with one of those tasks being to find solutions to the crisis while another being to use the opportunities that the crisis presents to extend their influence.

                And it's disturbingly easy for a technocrat to develop tunnel vision; moreso even than their desire to be in charge, that's probably their single biggest flaw. In this regard, those who see crises as opportunities to push their agendas often lose track of the fact that resolving the crisis is itself a goal: “we've accomplished so much since this started; just think of how much more we could accomplish if this were to go on just a little bit longer…”

(By the way, credit for my preferred view of the Technocracy goes to Malcolm Sheppard, who did a far better job explaining this about two or three forums ago.) One of the reasons I push for not presenting the Technocrats as deluded wizards is that that gets in the way of a far more interesting take on them.
                            I'm a bit iffier and think it really depends on your Paradigm (*rimshot*) and what the themes of the campaign are. Which is a very mage thing when you think about it. The question of what the Technocracy is depends, I think, on what the chronicle is about. Which is something that the M20 supplement handles well.

                            1. Going with the "deluded wizards" version, I actually had a very solid chronicle with a friend where the theme of the campaign was the pointlessness of the Ascension War. The conflict was between the Technocracy [explitcly wizards who knew how to do magic] wanting to uphold the Masquerade-equivalent and work coincidental magic through science.

                            They knew exactly what they were doing and were trying to exterminate the Traditions because they felt that if you screwed with a Sleeper's brain too much, it would collapse. The Traditions were fighting brutally against the Union because they all wanted to do their own kind of coincidental magic and hated that the Union was the dominant paradigm. The campaign emphasized the sheer selfishness and wastefulness of the conflict that they were all fighting over what, magically, the equivalent of "Mac or PC"?

                            It was very much a Revised take on the subject (before Revised) because the answer was that the Union and Traditions should have been teaming up to help humanity or at least fighting off the real threats.

                            2. Conversely, you could also do the "Technocrats have no understanding of magic" which is an extreme in the opposite direction. The idea that Unionists really have no idea what Traditionalists are doing and all their theories are grossly incorrect. They see themselves as basically inside a Call of Cthulhu game where a bunch of people are using science like the Ghostbusters and Doc Savage against horrifying alien weirdness. This could be used to emphasize that their obsessive devotion to their paradigm has actually made them ignorant of the true nature of reality.

                            3. Then there's Seers of Thrones-lite where the Technocracy isn't scientiffic at all because they CREATE the laws of reality. They define what the laws of physics are as accepted by the Masses, medical discoveries, and progress of technology is according to the Time Table. Whole rules of the universe may be enforced by their control and they aren't so much all-powerful as still wielding control beyond what most mortals could imagine. You can't be a scientist in such an organization because there's nothing to discover. If you want there to be a new planet, you submit a form, don't build a telescope.

                            All of them are equally valid takes, IMHO, but they create dramatically different settings. I've always felt this is one of those things that overlaps with the Sons of Ether and Virtual Adepts. A lot of people have just as different views on them as the Union depending on how you think science functions.

                                        That's it in a nutshell. That said, my personal take on it tweaks things slightly so that there actually is some justification for their claim that they're not doing the same thing that mages do. And while the changes that I make do change the letter of what the game claims to be true, I will argue strenuously that they keep the spirit of the game.

                                        Brucato just released an STV supplement called Mage Made Easy. I'm still reading through it, but what I've gathered thus far is that it's essentially his advice on what he thinks Mage should be like. There is a point that he makes early on in the book to the effect that the point of the game is people who can change the world. Everything else is second to that. Even “through Enlightened Will” (with the emphasis on Will) is secondary to that. To my mind, the divide between the Technocracy and the Superstitionists is that the latter tend to put the focus on the will, while the technocracy puts the focus on the enlightenment.

                                        Put another way, a Mystic changes the world through force of will, with his belief, practices, and instruments focusing that will. Thus the term “Willworking”. But to a technomancer, the key ingredient is enlightenment — a gut-level understanding of what the real rules are and what can be done with them.

                                        In my head-canon, this distinction between Willworking vs. Enlightened Science is what's behind the rules divide between mystics and technomancers, both in terms of the different schedules for discarding instruments and also in terms of the personal investment that most mages need in order to make Talismans vs. the Union's ability to make them without that personal investment. They both do things that change the world; but they do so in two different ways that have practical differences, with each being capable of something the other isn't capable of (Working Without Focus and discarding instruments in the case of Willworking, and making Devices without personal investments in the case of Enlightened Science).

                                        So no, technocrats are not delusional wizards. They're very close cousins, but they're something slightly different.

                                                            The key difference between Ether Science and the Enlightened Science practiced by the Technocracy is that the former rejects reductionism while the latter embraces it.
                                                            Reductionism: the practice of analyzing and describing a complex phenomenon in terms of phenomena that are held to represent a simpler or more fundamental level, especially when this is said to provide a sufficient explanation.
                                                            In the Technocracy, this manifests as the belief that there is one best way to do things, and that the alternatives are inferior, dangerous, or both. At their worst, the Technocracy has taken to trying to “simplify” reality to conform to their models of how it should be. This includes increasingly isolating the Umbra from Conventional Space so that the latter can be explored and exploited without the danger of interference from the Umbrood; but it also involves attempting to modify humanity so that it's more predictable and subject to their elegant models of human behavior. That's something that would be insanely stupid in our world; but when you can literally change the world, it's actually a viable (if morally questionable) strategy.

                                                            By contrast, the Etherites are anti-reductionist: they can and do explore multiple ways of doing things; and their strategy for Mass Ascension is to expand the scope of things that the Consensus will accept until the Consensus will accept anything, rendering all Ether Science (what their comrades call “Magick”) Coincidental. The Union also wants to expand what the Masses can do on their own. But at best, they see the Etherite approach as being overcomplicated; and at worst, they see it as actively detrimental, risking a paralysis of the Masses due to an overwhelming excess of options.
