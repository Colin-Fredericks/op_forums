    How Do I Do an Exorcism in Mage

    01-31-2018, 04:32 PM
    Dear Sleepers and Awakened (well, maybe not Sleepers),

    In my M20 game this coming Saturday (first Chronicle, second session), the cabal will be approached by Rachel (a friend of a friend) whose husband (Erik) has recently become very unpredictable, with bouts of anger and violence. Upon further investigation, the player characters learn that Erik became possessed by a malevolent spirit when he handled a Native American artifact at the university museum where he works. Rachel and Erik's parents ask the PCs to help since they seem to know what is going on and because they really don't know where else to turn. I want to run this something like the typical horror exorcism plot (The Exorcist, Emily Rose, Vatican Tapes, etc.), and I would like your advice.

    The PCs are just starting out, this is our second session (straight M20 character builds and they got 3 XP the first session).
    Here are my questions.
    1. Are PCs with a max of three dots in their spheres capable of handling an exorcism?
    2. How do I handle this mechanically, within the M20 rules?
    3. Any ideas on how to involve other PCs (6 players total) not directly involved in exorcism?
    Thanks in advance - You folks are a wealth of knowledge!!!

    Jim

      M20: How Do You Do That? sets Exorcism as requiring Spirit 4, or dots in the True Faith merit. And while I don't always agree with HDYDT, on this point it seems reasonable. Prime 3 could be used to bribe the possessing spirit with Quintessence, which could either decrease the difficulty of an Exorcism attempt, or be used to entice a spirit to leaving of its own accord. Of course, giving up a mage's own Quintessence - flavored, as it is, with the mage's own spiritual make-up - is potentially dangerous, as it could make it easier for the spirit to possess the mage herself. And bribing without Spirit 4 would require mundane bargaining, and no assurance that it would work, or that the spirit wouldn't go back on its word or try to re-enter the possessed person.

      If your cabal doesn't have anyone with sufficient Arete and/or Sphere knowledge, or True Faith, it might be better to bring in someone else who does. If the PCs have some dots in Spirit, they could collaborate with the NPC to strengthen their Effect.

      Re: How to involve PCs not directly involved in the Exorcism. Keep in mind that while this Exorcism is going on, the spirit itself doesn't need to be idle. Both the Exorcist and spirit can cast other effects on each other (assuming the Exorcist isn't too involved with the ritual to cast other magick, of course, or if the exorcist is an unAwakened person with True Faith). In this case, the spirit is going to use and all spirit Charms it has to disrupt or aggravate the exorcism. The other PCs will likely have their hands full dealing with that nonsense. Levitating objects, visions or illusions, projectile vomiting, called animals, and even spirits summoned by the possessing entity to defend it.

      Magestuck: The Ascension

              Spirit 3 allows you to harm spirits. The Spirit being in a body, well you would also need Life 3 to harm the body. True Faith could drive out spirits in a religious ritual like in most movie exorcisms. Also, that character with Spirit 3 could cross over to the other side of the Umbra and find a Spirit with the ability to exorcise the bad spirit. Spirits have a whole hierarchy, so there's problem an opposite number to the evil spirit.

              M20 has a pretty short list of spirits in the main book. I'd look for something more demonic from say, Infernalism: the Path of Screams (Mage: The Sorcerer's Crusade book).

              Other PCs can also be doing research around the location of the exorcism. For example, perhaps they find out what object the spirit broke free from and then make sure to bring it to shove the Spirit back into the original object.

              It's Mage, so while mechanics matter, it's more a game about asserting will than a game with very set spell lists. If an idea is interesting to you and your players and won't do great harm down the road, go with what feels interesting instead of the mechanics.

                  Originally posted by DrunkenUraak View Post
                  Thank you so much Bluecho for responding. I’m not sure where this HDYDT thing you sited is. I would like to know though.
                  How Do You Do That? is a source book released for M20, similar to Book of Secrets and the upcoming Gods, Monsters, And Familiar Strangers. The book talks about how to run various Sphere effects in a mechanical sense, as well as just explains certain mechanics that otherwise couldn't be fit into the main M20 book (like, for instance, Possession). You can find it on Drive Thru RPG.

                  While the book and I have a love-hate relationship, some of the material contained therein can be useful. The section on Exorcisms is one of them. If you do get the book, though, take everything you read in it with a grain of salt. The writer, Phil Brucato, loves to overcomplicate aspects of Sphere magick that didn't need such complications, especially in light of previous editions.

                  Of the group of PCs, one of them has Spirit••• and another has Spirit• plus the True Faith Merit. Additionally, they have a contact which could have a book or other objects to aid their Spiritual needs. With that, I think I could make it work, but it will be challenging for them.
                  The character with True Faith could do it, though as you've said it might be difficult. It's debatable how other people can help a character running on just True Faith, unless they also happen to have True Faith (and if their faiths happen to be compatible). At least with Spirit, anyone with dots in that Sphere can lend a hand during the casting.

                  If the PCs belong to Traditions/Crafts, they could potentially use that organizational structure to call upon a more experienced mage to help them (e.i. one with Spirit 4 or higher, or one with higher True Faith). This might involve the cabal trading favors to the NPC, as bringing a mage out from whatever they were doing previously is a big ask, even if the NPC agrees with the task.

                  Of the spirits listed in the M20 book, which would you recommend to use as the possession? Something challenging but doable. Being this is my second M20 session, I’m not too sure about what is challenging yet. Of course, I know I can always tell the story as I desire, but I typically like to keep things as forthright as I can.
                  I would personally just craft my own spirit for the task, using some of the sample spirits from M20 as a template for stats and Charms. If the players are using beginning characters, it's best to pit them against a minor spirit.

                  HDYDT presents the act of Exorcism as being a contested Willpower roll between the spirit and exorcist (both difficulty 8), with each trying to roll a number of successes before their opponent. For the spirit, they need to roll successes equal to the exorcist's Willpower + Avatar, plus 2 successes for each dot in True Faith (if applicable). The exorcist, meanwhile, needs to roll enough to match the "general power" of the spirit, says the book. It lists a graduating number based on overall potency, with minor spirits requiring 10 successes, and going up from there. Since the PCs are just starting out, making the spirit "minor" is a good idea, unless the characters bring in a particularly potent NPC to help (in which case, increase the success threshold appropriately to the NPC's ability).

                  As for spirit Charms, the spirit can have any powers suitable for its nature, or you can make up whatever ones you like for dramatic purposes. If it makes for an interesting exorcism scene - and isn't too great for the beginning characters to handle - throw it in. If it's a minor spirit, it will probably only have one or two such Charms it can employ (in addition to the Possession Charm needed to even be here). And it'll only be able to use those Charms so many times before it depletes its supply of Essence, so look at the sample spirits for an appropriate Essence score. This turns the exorcism into a battle of attrition as much as wills.

                      One more thing: Since one of the PCs has True Faith, the spirit should probably be of a nature that makes it an ideological enemy of that character. While any malevolent spirit will likely make it appropriately anathema to most characters with True Faith - a spirit that does wrong by people will tend to be opposed by most characters who are not themselves evil (like an Infernalist or Nephandi with a dark True Faith) - making the spirit be of a worldview that is consistent with the PC's faith will help make the game internally consistent. At least to start. You can venture into "stranger" types of spirits later, as the characters get more familiar with the wider world. Keeping it consistent now will cut down on complications in the immediate sense, as well as help validate the player's choices in character creation.

                      If the cabal ends up getting an NPC mage or True Faith exorcist to help, it might be more appropriate to match the spirit to them, instead. As if to say that the characters are bringing in a specialist to help. It ultimately depends on how the players go about preparing for the exorcism, and what their characters end up doing. If the players lean on doing the rite themselves, make the spirit appropriate to them. If they decide to get help, make it appropriate to the NPC, if there's a qualitative difference (if it's the True Faith PC that's calling for help, the NPC will likely be of the same cultural tradition anyway).

                                  As said further up, Spirit:3 can allow you to harm the spirit; it can also allow you to attempt to lull it, which while not directly exorcising it (which would require Spirit:4, reversing the channeling concept that also exists at that level) can at least mitigate its current influence until a more permanent solution can be reached.
