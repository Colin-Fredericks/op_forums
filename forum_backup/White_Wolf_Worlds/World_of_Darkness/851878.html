    A World not so Dark

    03-23-2016, 02:08 PM
    Does anyone else have any recommendations for how to run a less heavy WOD game. I love the deep narrative and adaptable mechanics that pretty much allow you to do anything(Even from other franchises, I've run more than one technocracy games modeled after SCPs and xcom), but the "True art is angsty" and "the man is keeping us down" tropes it relies on heavily in straight WOD make me think of bad fanfiction sometimes.

    Anyone have any tweaks, story and mechanics wise, that can mend this somewhat.

      Remember that all monsters have lives. Sometimes you just gotta go eat a hotdog.

      A lot can be done by just playing to the slice of life mentality, creating tangled knots that don't need cutting, using setting problems instead of character problems, talking to players and establishing the tone and direction.

      THe single most epic Mage game I ever played spent it's first half more or less following the lives this little drug-dealing cabal. We did stuff like invade a Mormon temple, go to a recovering drug addict's party, hosted a small mage convention at the club, find a way to send a whiny little brother back to Portland, stuff like that. Even our encounters with other drug crews often ended up as "Run the Fuck away" or "Hey, instead of shooting each other in the head, you wanna talk things out like reasonable adults?" It was good fun, idle days with things going on, but nothing too heavy. All this without actually hacking the setting or mechanics

      The second half turned into the usual emotional torture fest/stop the world from ending thing you might expect from WoD, and it was more potent because of the first half.

      So, really, it's largely in how you run the game. Sometimes, you just gotta go eat a hotdog.

                                  My WoD games cling far more to a 2nd Edition feel than anything else.
                                  The world is definitely 'dark' but has never been as completely and utterly hopeless as Revised painted it to be.

                                  Yes, you can have friends, those that you can trust your life with and you aren't worried about sticking a knife between your ribs as soon as you turn your back.
                                  It's still morally abhorrent to suck the life blood from a mortal, but taking just enough from a would-be lover whilst wrapped in shadow in the corner of a busy nightclub, then leaving them with memories of a passion-filled night plays more into the gothic imagery than the out-and-out horror dynamic.

                                  There's nothing pretty about my WoD but I've always refused to indulge in some of the nihilism Revised edition paints the setting with.

                                  I've played in games where every NPC answers you with a curt reply and snidey smirk, where the world and his wife is out to screw you over and almost everything your character does is met with failure and loss.
                                  It gets tiring very, very quickly.
                                  Games are meant to be fun. If you come away from your session feeling frustrated or depressed there is something wrong going on.

                                  I had a conscience once. Alas, I appear to have misplaced it.

                                          When I played and ran chronicles I found the best games to be mixed with good and bad, too much of one without enough of the other can make the game frustrating and dull.

                                          Ask your players what kinda game they want, find the things large and small that they like and want and put it in. Add more with your ideas and slight twists to their requests.

                                          A good example is before I started being a ST. The ST at the time wanted a stark contrast, light and dark. So another player and I did a most unlikely romance in the backdrop of a Four Horsemen apocolapyse. So ontop of the usual intrigue, we had her Tremere ex-Archon and my Caitiff elder Scourge (told ya ) meet and and become paramours as they were feared and excluded by most of the other Licks. They also were the focal characters fighting to prevent the doom, as others worried about their own unlives, ours simply didn't want to lose the one good thing in their lives.

                                          Paramour is also a merit, I learned. Nice to know that even the Kindred can try to kindle flames.

                                          In the end he broke many a rule and by the luck of the dice he succeeded . And I'm talking the stuff of legends, I think he really wanted us to fail. They saved the day...then the Prince tried to exile him. The twists and turns continued but we always had something to work for, to keep that light burning bright in a dark world.

                                          Ultimately we changed the tone of the game by doing a unusual romance. Others seeing our enjoyment of this new dynamic started adding their own personal twists and on the whole the game added a deep personal involvement within the wider Chronicle storylines.

                                          When I took over I did player run scenes where they submitted ideas they had and I would let them run them on their own. I'd play as a character and assist if they needed help. I also ran some unreal scenes that belonged in a action or thriller movie just to spice things up.

                                          It is a World of Darkness but darkness is darkest when a light burns bright within it. I hope this helps!

                                                      ? I don't really see a challenge in running a non-dark WoD game with the rules as-is. Althugh the WooD/CoD books like to remind you that this is a SERIOUS game with SERIOUS psychological exploration etc. in the TEXT every chance they get, the RULES rarely actually grow up to this promise. It is very easy to end up with a "dark superheroes" game even unintentionally if the GM does not make that extra effort to add the angsty content him/herself.

                                                      Obviously, you could try to remove all the "you're screwed either way" mkechanics if you wanted it to make super light. Golconda? Why, half our vampires have achieved that, it's not that hard! Frenzy? Nah, it doesn't trigger when yiou don't want it anymore. Masquerade?
