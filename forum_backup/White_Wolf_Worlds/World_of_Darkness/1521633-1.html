        Well, at this point I’m pretty much certain I’ll be dropping maneuvers as written. Before M20 they weren’t even in the core rulebook, so I think we probably can get by without them.

        The problem I’m seeing, at least as far as the game I intend to run is concern, is that these maneuvers put the decisions at the wrong times.

        The first aspect of this is how maneuvers are used in a combat scene. They require the player to say what move their character will attempt, then roll dice to see whether that move succeeds. But… that’s not how a fight generally works. In a real fight, the 3-10 seconds that is a WoD action turn is a hell of a long time. Nobody competent goes into that fight planning to deliver Spinning Kick, and if you can’t to do nothing at all. Even if you have a general hope of landing a spinning kick, a fight is very fluid, and if you’re got skills (enough to warrant giving the character Spinning Kick in the first place) you’re going to be adjusting to make use of whatever opportunity arises. In under three seconds, that intent to deliver a spinning kick becomes shoving someone into a bar stool.

        But then there’s also a longer-scale aspect to this. Once a character has Spinning Kick, there’s no point to Punch, mechanically (as has been noted by others). The character opens the fight with a Spinning Kick, follows up with Spinning Kick, and finally pulls out the big finishing move, Spinning Kick. Sure, we might make some different choices to make things interesting, but that’s what the system incentivises. And really, this is setting the character’s combat choices, to a large extent, in character creation.

        This flow - both the short and the long term - is backwards to what I want from combat. For a fight to be dramatic, it needs to be dynamic, with players making significant choices in the moment, and those choices revealing things about the character, adding to the story.

        So that’s what I don’t want. What do I want instead?

        On the round-by-round scale, I want our play to always proceed forward, description leading to mechanic which leads to more description. Rather than saying exactly what move their character hopes to pull off - “I’ll do a Spinning Kick” - I want a more general statement of intent - “I want to harm the bruiser in front of me.” Then we roll Dexterity + Melee, and we check how many successes came up. If it’s a lot of successes, then we might describe the dramatic spinning kick, with a bunch of extra damage dice being rolled as this extremely powerful blow lands on the bruiser’s jaw (or maybe a precise nerve strike, or a kidney punch, is more this character’s style). Because we have the guidance of the number of successes before describing the detail, we can match our description to the outcome - no describing a Spinning Kick, only to have to back out with “But the bruiser dodges, and it hits only air” when the roll is bad.

        None of that means we can’t see tactical choices being made, where players make trade offs with the mechanics. We’ve got that talk up-thread about how Kick is just a poor choice compared to Punch, giving no real increase in average damage, so why would anybody choose Kick? Well, the answer is that nobody should choose kick, but a similar mechanic describes something else - a character who is accepting a high chance of not landing a blow (that matters) on their enemy in exchange for a heightened chance of doing a particularly high amount of damage. (-1 diff for +1 damage die possibly doesn’t actually do this, but -1 for +2 might, or -2 for +2. I’d need to run some numbers.) That tells us something general about the character - what their appetite for risk is at the moment, how desperate they are getting. (And then we get the detail of what they actually do after the roll.) And those sorts of trade-offs are general enough that it doesn’t make a lot of sense to buy them during character creation. You’d buy high enough skill to allow you to have risky strategies pay off more often, but you wouldn’t be buying the specific maneuver.

        And that brings us to character creation. We already have a 0-5 rating in our choice of Brawl or Martial Arts representing general skill, we shade that to an extent with Athletics (for the dodge), the physical attributes, and similar. But we also have specialties, and we can let them play more of a role. Rather than buying specific maneuvers, I have plans to allow characters to buy specialties in Throws, Damaging Blows, Immobilising - the name of the specialty doesn’t matter; a player can have a specialty in Judo and we agree that this means that 10s count as two successes for throws.

        And obviously this means that I still need a mechanic for throws, and a mechanic for grappling, and some other things. But those can be generalised a bit from the very specific maneuvers the book gives.

        The one thing missing from all of this is the ultra-special named martial arts move - the One-Finger Death Strike or the Thunder Crane Kick. The thing that a martial artist hunted for a master for years to learn, said to be truly unstoppable. A staple of old pulp martial arts stories. But the thing about those moves is that they really seem like something properly magical. For that, we have rotes.

        Despite the length of this post, I really haven’t hit on some key mechanics that I’m settling on, but I’m tired and have typed enough for now. Maybe later, when I have time and a hand that isn’t aching.

            1) Fights are fast, but that doesn't mean you can't plan what you gonna do. It's not all instincts. And I'd say the more professional you get, the more you plan and wait for the right opening. Good MMA fighters spend a lot of time just checking, scaning and flintching each other before landing a blow. I don't see the issue with a player (and character) thinking "I'm gonna do a shin to the tights now".

            2) There are reasons for punching: you dont need much room to throw a punch, while a spinning kick requires the right amount of room around and distance from the target. You can punch while fighting on the ground. Spinning kicks gets you tired way faster than punches. Just to give some examples. The thing is, each maneuver is different and should have their own complications and perks. Don't treat them just as numbers.

            3) I feel that vague statements like "I wanna bruise someone" it's way worse for roleplaying than "I wanna do a spinning kick". How exactly you want to bruise the target? It's a punch? A knee to the stomach? An humiliating teep to the face? The answer will also say how the target reacts and then the combat gets dynamic.
            If you are limited by "bruise","dodge","attack", it becomes a game of pure numbers.

            4) Deciding what is done by the result of the dice removes freedom for the player and also undermines roleplay, in my opinion. I prefer detailed descriptions, like "I land a right cross to the liver". Doesn't matter how much successes you get, if you hit you will hit the liver, not the face. I believe better descriptions add realism to the fight.

            5) The best and "special" maneuvers, like the death strike, are not even close to be as good as combat magick. Also, vampires have fangs, garou have bites and claws, I find it very cool for mages to have martial arts as their option for mundane combat advantages.

            And again, why remove them completely? The weak/bad maneuvers will probably not be used by players anyway...

            All that are *my* opinions. It seems you kinda have your mind made here, so go for what you believe will work for you.

                                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                Have you looked at the Storypath combat systems? It doesn't do a lot with martial arts techniques, but it seems like a structure you'd enjoy more than the default Storyteller system M20 uses.

                                The basic reason I bring this up, in in Storypath, you do actually just declare that you're attacking someone, with only your Skill + Attribute selection as a the decision you make at that point. After you roll your attack dice and account for their Defense, you have a pool of successes you spend on various options.
                                Yeah, Storypath is not far off where I’m settling. Not that I’m planning to buy another system to tell me how and then try to integrate with M20 - more work than it’s worth. But the basic concept is about right.

                                We already have that in Storyteller, conceptually. It’s just hidden. When you attack, you roll your Dex+MA, subtract successes on defence (which defaults to 0, but dodge, parry, and the like give successes that subtract from attack successes), then your first success buys Strength dice of damage, and every success after that buys an extra die of damage.

                                If we just add an extra option for spending those extra successes, and let the players decide how to spend them after the roll, we’ve basically got Storypath.

                                And that’s pretty much where I’ve landed. There’s a few effects that I want players to be able to apply in combat - immobilise/hold, knock down, forcibly move, outmaneuver, Violence Enhances Magick, maybe a couple of others - and I figure each of them can be applied by spending successes. You could achieve combination effects - say, knock down with injury - by having the player fully state their intent and adding up the number of successes required, then having the player roll to see if they get enough successes, but…

                                … I’m thinking, why bother with that much detail in the intent? If the player doesn’t roll enough successes, the cool move doesn’t happen and what does happen usually falls flat by comparison. Instead, I’ll just get something brief and general as intent, and then if the player rolls a lot of successes, they can be pleasantly surprised, spend the successes for some desirable combination of effects, and describe a cool move that they think suits the degree of success rolled. I’m pretty sure I’ll hear more cool moves described this way, because the players don’t have to worry about what happens if they don’t pull it off. And more varied cool moves, because nobody has their initial expectations set by that Spinning Kick on their character sheet; they have more opportunity to adapt to the situation.

                                And if what the player wants is a lot of damage and the character’s signature Spinning Kick, they can have that too.
