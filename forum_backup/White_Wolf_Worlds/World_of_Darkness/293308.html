                              Early Werewolf was clumsily written with little research and contained the old Fianna and Get of Fenris Tribebooks. Later Werewolf had people who started doing research and, starting with the old Silver Fangs Tribebook, had the Silver Fangs be natively present over a far wider stretch than Russia, with far more diverse rolls among Garou royalty in North Africa, the Middle East, and most of Asia (including India and on into Indonesia).

                              Unfortunately, the Silent Striders still ended up shackled to the Robert E. Howard vision of Set on into W20. But V20 Dark Ages made some changes on that front (as opposed to V20), so perhaps in time, that can get shifted around to make sense under legendry not distorted by one of H.P. Lovecraft's friends.

                                                          Originally posted by Sergeant Brother View Post
                                                          Really, if you don't like camp, then Werewolf is not the game for you.
                                                          neither is roleplaying in general. Seriously, the whole activity revolves around a bunch of people playing pretend, throwing dice and acting like it's high theater. it doesn't matter if you're running a game with a lot of campy themes like dragonlance or a relatively low-camp one like Legend of the Five Rings; just the act of roleplaying is, itself, pretty damn campy, pretty equally across the board.

                                                          I mean, a Captain Planet
                                                          Either you've never played Werewolf or you've never watched Captian Planet. Seriously, this comparison is so banal and ignorant, and its repetition doesn't make it any less so. it's based on the superficiality of "captain planet thinks pollution is bad; Garou think pollution is bad. Garou are like captain planet!" Yeah, Garou think pollution is bad. So does everyone else in the world, I imagine (even the people doing the polluting don't want to be anywhere near the stuff, right?)

                                                          Thing is, Captain Planet never actually hurts anyone. I don't think he even punches another living being through the entire run of the show. The show presents pollution as bad because it's ugly and the villains like it, not because of the actual harm it causes. Except to Captain Planet himself who is weakened by pollution, which is a stupid weakness for a pollution-fighting hero (but how else are you going ot inject even a little tension into a series otherwise utterly devoid of it, I guess). At the end of each 20-minute episode, everything is at rights again. The Captain has cleaned it all up, the Planeteers are back at home base, wheeler cracks a joke, they laugh, roll credits.

                                                          Werewolf... isn't like that. First off, obviously, werewolves kill things - very often those things are people. Very often those people aren't even really the bad guys, they're low-paid mooks who just happen to be in the way at the moment. And very often, the Garou just don't care. Pollution isn't te only "villain" the Garou face, either - and when they do, its full effects are not only shown, but magnified. What doyou suppose Three Mile Island looks like in hte umbra? werewolf will show you, Ted Turner sure won't. No, Garou face problems from all over - the slow, griding erasure of their culture, as well as, very often those of their kinfolk for example. How often does Captain Planet deal with cultural genocide? Probably not often. Actual genocide? Probably never. However, these are defining conflicts for several werewolf tribes. How about Rage? When the Planeteers summon Captain Planet, do you think he has to consciously restrain himself from cleansing the earth of them, along with the rest of humanity, leaving a pure and pristine world for his patron? He doesn't. But give a Garou the sort of power that guy has, and see what happens. (THough, here's an interesting article about if Captain Planet DID have that going on... http://www.slacktory.com/2012/06/cap...ant-planeteer/ )

                                                          And of course,there's no happy ending in werewolf. Okay, you blew up the pesticide plant... Now what/ Does everything go back to being hunky-dory/ well no, now the town is out of work. The people there get desperate. You traded one problem for another. Oh, and of course all that burning pesticide created even more toxic problems... Good Job, Heroes. But wait, you don't have time to try to fix it; one of your sept elders thinks you need to go help another allied sept on some bug-hunt, for political reasons. So that polluted, desperate town will have to wait until you get back... assuming of course, you don't die. Which you very well might.

                                                          mega-corporation that wants to pollute the environment
                                                          Most coirporations do pollute. Many of them quite greviously. You don't need the Wyrm involved for Endron to do what Endron does; just look at our world, the stuff Exxon does, or how Shell behaves in Nigeria and Sudan You can run chronicle after chronicle of werewolves dealing with the after-effects of corporate behavior, and never once, not once use the Wyrm except as a periphial threat, much less pentex.

                                                          all Pentex does is create a unifying plot hook; Instead of just wasteful, harmful businesses, now you've got those same wasteful,harmful businesses that happen to be woven into a conspiracy. At its most basic, Pentex is just a very small cult of very wealthy businessmen who invest in each other' companies.

                                                          and sells demon possessed toys to make kids more violent.
                                                          As noted, this has gone far more low-key in W20; it turns out the actual way toys are presented - categorically gendered, with "boy" toys emphasizing violence and strength and "girl" toys being bland and basic and often reinforcing poor body image... are already corrosive enough.

                                                          Know why there's the occasional bane slipped in? Again, plot hooking; your character probably doesn't even think about the Anorexia Amy doll his neice is playing with, until he happens to poke into the umbra and see a starving little imp sitting on the same shelf, in the same place... It's a hook, usedto lure the player character into exploring what is, actually a pretty complex issue that - again - has no clear solution or resolution.

                                                          Nazi werewolves, feminazi werewolves, hippie werewolves, drunken Irish werewolves, martial arts werewolves, high tech urban werewolves, the list goes on.
                                                          sort of like the Captain planet thing, these stereotypes are cheap, easy, and wholly inaccurate, and reflect someone whose knowledge of the game amounts to "I glanced at a book once." it's the equivalent of thinking that becuase there are dwarves and elves in D&D, then D&D must be about fixing shoes.
