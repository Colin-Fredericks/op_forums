    The West: Other Supernaturals in the Wyld West

    04-14-2015, 01:09 PM
    Since today is the 155th Anniversary of the Pony Express. I'm expanding on what the Wyld West books suggested. How do you run the other settings in the Wyld West?

    Here's my thing.

    Vampire: St. Louis is about as far west they want to risk going to. They stay on the eastern side of the country at all times. Those who risk heading west has it coming to them.
    Mage: They combat the Storm Umbra in their own ways. The Sons of Ether are on the rise and about to split from the Technocracy.
    Wraith: Necropoli spring into existence as fast as the boom towns fall. Fights for resources and to live against the Third Maelstrom rages across the world.
    Changeling: European Fae and Nunnehi battle one another for territory. With the rapid expanse from the east, the Nunnehi are on the verge of experience their own version of the Sundering.
    Hunter: No Imbued Hunters at the time. Those who find out what goes bump in the night gather posses to hunt down and kill what's out there. Some to protect their families, some for profit.
    Kuei-Jin: They start to claim Dragon Nests in California, battling Garou and Magi for their claims. The Kuei-Jin find out quickly how the Sunset people operate.
    Mummies: None of have risen. Their loyal cultists seek out new territory and places of power for their respected master (either Egyptian, South American, or Asian).
    Demon: Only the Earthbound are available. A couple of reliquaries are discovered in the West, one close to the city of Deadwood, another by a miner party in Colorado where the only survivor is tried and hanged for cannibalism.

    Onyx Path Kickstarters I back.
    W20, MtC, CB20, DtD, MtAs20, BotW20, Wraith 20th.

                          Originally posted by Chattnos View Post
                          Depending on the time period you are talking Voltarian Order or the Electrodyne engineers who don't split off until 1904.
                          I think both favored forces but not sure. The Etherites are also have strong solfacti component so thats where they get the matter specialization.
                          The big experiment that pissed off the Electrodyne Engineers didn't take place until 1887. So there are effectively two periods in play for those-who-will-be-Etherites in the "Old West": 1) between the end of the Civil War when the mass expansion to the West really took place where the Engineers are probably as happy with the Technocracy as the other Conventions and 2) around and after Michelson-Morely and ether being "disproved" and they start getting tetchy, though not so tetchy that they are not still "playing adequately well with others" since it takes about two decades to actually defect.

                          The Voltarians were renamed to the Electrodyne Engineers in the foundations of the Union in 1851, which is a little before the early end of most conceptions of an Old West era game.

                          But there aren't any actual Sons of Ether in the West as such, and their predecessors would have tended more to the Union than the Traditions. The Traditions don't have any real bent toward technomagic at this point. Even being kinda-sorta technological is frowned on, e.g. the Order of of Hermes kicking one of the founding Houses to the curb, for being, ya know, blacksmiths.....

                              I had a portion of one of my Mage games take place in St. Louis in the 1870's. (The PCs were traveling home backwards in time from the future and overshot the mark.) I remember writing up a short "position" paper on how the Eight Traditions were different to hand out to the players. (Which I still have around here somewhere.) Stuff they would pick up on pretty quickly. Plus one player had left his character in the future, so he made up a new 19th century character that was going to join their cabal. They were intensively involved in the supernaturals of the city, so I also backdated each of the supernaturals they had dealt with previously. Some of the wraiths they knew were still alive. The vampires were under the thumb of the previous Prince and the modern Prince was a member of the primogen. They had met the City Spirit and the ended up meeting it's earlier iteration. I had dropped enough Easter Eggs throughout the game that a lot of mysteries were revealed. "So THAT is why that NPC acted in that way!" and "So that's why this wraith acted like he already knew me when we first met!" I was a ton of fun. Made me want to do a whole period piece game. I've toyed with going back and doing a Victorian/Wyld West one based on fond memories, but we've settled on the Roaring 20s.

                                                  Originally posted by Matt the Bruins fan View Post
                                                  I have some roaming in the American West among Sabbat attached to Indian tribes in the Great Plains and Diné territory as part of my vampire character's backstory, though I've never actually played him in that setting. I'd imagine having a decent proficiency with Protean would be crucial for vampires on the frontier.
                                                  You could have sedentary vampires pretty easily as well. Even though rifles and horses had moved many/most of the tribes out onto the Plains there were still some very sedentary ones and, with some of them, having Protean would get you found out and killed a lot faster (Skinwalker! Sorcerer!) than just hanging around sleeping in the day and only coming out at night (living in a dead man's house that has been sealed up, being the guy who lives in the kiva as its keeper, etc.)

                                                  It would be difficult, but not too difficult to get a vamp with a coffin or other container out west either. The trails would have worked. You could definitely fit a full size coffin on a Conestoga wagon. People certainly did. A lead-lined coffin full of brine was used to take bodies down the Oregon trail. And repeatedly opened (while hymns were sung) to show the body to the Indians (who then let the very weird people pass through their territory ASAP). Going by steamboat up the rivers would have been easier. And once they are where they want to be, they just set up like normal.

                                                  The density, like the population as a whole, would be pretty low. It would hard to have a whole coterie most places. But you could definitely have vamps.
