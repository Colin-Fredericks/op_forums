    I was interested but am hesitant to jump in because of two reasons. One is time constraint and the other is the genre. IMHO since WoD is an RPG, then an RPG is the best fit.

    Allow me to voice my thought. At the bare minimum I suggest you have: a programmer and an animator. It seems you have a music (and sound) guy, that's nice.

    Project Manager
    Ideally there should be a project manager. He decides the direction of project, create continuous detailed step-by-step progressions and whip everyone's butts to keep on track. Basically he's who everyone hates but needs. Most hobbyist programmers lack the experience and skills to collaborate (and play nice ) with others.

    Programming
    It makes or breaks the project. Decide fast on platform, technology, testing methods and stick to them!

    Platform: Windows (XP+, 7+, 10+ so on), Macos, Linux, Xbox, Playstation, Andorid, Windows Mobile so on. The most widespread is of course Windows (version XP is no longer supported, 7 and above has the most share, 10 is up and coming but not too widespread yet). I recommend Windows 7 and above.

    Technology: simplest explanations is 2D or 3D. For a new team with minimum experience, go 2D. It simplifies a lot and still looks good. As a bonus, it has much lower system requirements which translate as wider audience.

    How much is the budget? When is the deadline? If you have money, to safe time and lots of pains, license some ready made engines. If minimum budget then the programmer needs to build some new engines from scratch. Which means lots of time, lots of bugs, lots of headache. If you have a programmer who is proficient in C++, then you're in luck. C# is okay for simple hobbyist projects and Java has sub par performance. Forget about other languages.

    Animation
    Unless you're creating a text only game or have static graphics, you need an animator to create dynamic graphic (thus, more lifelike). How amazing do you want the graphic? Depends on the programming.

    Audio
    Same as visual, depends on programming. Avoid voice/speech for first project. It requires more time and equipment, harder to change the lines, more i18n l10n works.

    Testing / QA
    Most newcomers neglect this. But you really need a good QA. Can't stress this enough.

    Documentation
    Prevent headache later on by paying attention on this from the very beginning. Maintain a good docs.

    WoD is a very complicated game. Thinking of translating just the core manual alone into code gives me a headache. So start small. Don't refer to professional games, especially those made by highly funded studios. Look at independent games made by small team.

    Maybe build a 10-hour RPG with a linear story with a few NPCs and one fixed PC. Trust me, even 10-hour gameplay is a lot of work for an RPG! (I'm talking about a good story-based RPG here not a button mashing action "RPG".).

    If you do start this project, persevere! A project is always the most fun before it begins. It goes downhill fast and only becomes worse and worse. The grueling hard work, expenses, emotional distress, real life distractions, unreached expectations can all be too much too soon.

            Jungle Wars:
            I like the idea of the Career. Also, you could have Representatives, groups you manage from afar to gather help from the world around, or that were sent by others to your army, and now you have to deal with "not-quite-a-spy-but-kind-of" guy.

            Balams could work for a more Tactical Action game with a single unit or very few of them, given how they usually work. Mokolés can make for a very distinct game play, with Mnesis working as a source of advanced information, and deployment being a far more logistics-heavy decision, but each unit is an entirely distinct set of options by itself.

            Corporate Wolf:
            Could be expanded to Corporate Wars. Instead of just Garou trying to tackle Pentex, it could be each faction in the WoD that deals with the Corporate world trying to establish their power bases and accomplish their individual goals. For a casual game, you take a noob in the business world and vie for control of a city, or at least a city's aspect important for you. You don't necessarily need to actually wipe out others, but they can get in your way or help you by chasing their own goals. For an Ultimate Campaign, your final goal is as world shaking as the Apocalypse itself, from Garou accomplishing to force balance to the Wyrm (and maybe starting the Time of the Weaver), to Augustus Giovanni finally gathering the resources and knowledge needed to claim godhood.

            Red Carpet Fighting Club:
            What about a progression in the story mode, going from an all-human league to a Ghouled league? For the Fighting Game, it could open for the player to buy Disciplines, customizing the fighter, and other benefits from a Vampire sponsor. Also, we could add a Sponsor Mode, where instead of taking care of the entire league, you play one vampire that concentrates on bringing fighters. A character that pretty much needs a sheet similar to the one you though for Corporate Wolf, because it defines the means the character have to find, get, train and empower the fighters.

            Caern Builder:
            Adding other factions to this can be simpler than it seems while the focus stays on the general management. On some level, Caerns, Domains, Chantries and Free Holds work similarly. And they share the same World of Darkness. They could add another level of possible interactions, and the player be given free reign to decide on uncommon relationships, like allying with vampires or even with a specific sept of BSDs, to see what happens. I would add elements of The Settlers, because I love that game.

            For now I'll rest a little, but latter I'll add some of my own ideas. As soon as I can, I'll start to discuss all of this with the programmers to get the first feedback.
