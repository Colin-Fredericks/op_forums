    Originally posted by BloodArmor View Post
    Pertaining to the Liches of the Tal'Mahe'Ra, "Lich" is used as a title rather than the literal form. To quote V20's Companion:

    Lich (Tal’Mahe’Ra; 4-point Title)
    The Liches of the True Black Hand are what remains
    of puissant and evil mortal mages who have sworn
    themselves to the Tal’Mahe’Ra’s cause and employed
    soul-excoriating rituals in order to preserve themselves
    in the state of hellish immortality conferred by
    vampirism. Although the Embrace extinguishes the
    spark of cosmic enlightenment that allows true mages
    to impose their wills on the world, it doesn’t destroy
    the knowledge that these wicked creatures accumulated

    From Dirty Secret of the Black Hand, it was the same:
    The Del'Roh is served by three powerful Kindred, called the liches, who used to be Euthanatos mages. they serve as judges and representatives of the Del'Roh. They are cruel, powerful and zealous, making them the greatest threat to the secret factions within the Black Hand.

    In V20's Children of the Revolution, there's also a character named Roderigo Al-Dakhil, Lich of the Dead City. He's a Kindred all the way.

    I didn't even think about the Tal'Mahe'Ra stuff from V20.... Thanks for that.

            I have been playing Mage/Ascention and Vampire/masquerade for many years. Pretty much since they came out. In the Tremere revised clanbook, It kind of elaborates on the fact that, Tremere will watch those who show magical promise, sometimes for quite a while. They will only Ghoul/blood bond those who appear after many years to not be able to awaken. Because they still have magical potential they are sort of the creme de la creme of potential Thaumaturges. Even then they only blood bond the promising, the less promising are ghouled and put into service of the clan in lesser more demeaning roles. A Tremere will attempt to befriend and potentially blood bond (and by blood bond, I mean the, you get three drops and feel a supernatural affinity for the vampire, not ghouling) an awakened mage, and usually only if that mage has "hermetic" house Tremere like abilities. They might befriend a cultest or an etherite just because hell why not, it's cool having an awakened ally. Plus, depending on the mage themselves, they might view the Tremere as a sort of hedge mage ally. In the original "Book of Shadows, Mage players guide", There are merits for having a vampire/werewolf/changling companions. I believe they are all 4 point supernatural merits.

                (That's what I get for not checking my messages properly -- somebody linked me a message about this comment and I didn't note it until just tonight... been busy with business and travel...)

                Bold: Much like with Liches, this is less true. Mummies *do* have their Avatars -- they call them Sahu(s). They are the aspect of the old Egyptian mages that made them mages. Their Sahus/god-selves, however, are restructured by the Spell of Life into a different metaphysical purpose. This is similar -- though of course also markedly different in several ways -- to how a lich's Avatar is (at least partially) reworked to be bound to their undead selves. (Sidenote: as conjectured by a number of mages, werewolves and certain other living supernatural beings may also have "avatars", but their god-souls are reworked/repurposed in a different manner, and are not Avatars(tm) in the sense that mages understand. Much like with garou, etc., the (A)vatars of mummies are integrated and "cemented" into their metaphysical identities more tightly than even with mages and their Avatars, so they don't have the open-ended connection to the raw power of the universe the same way mages do... though mummies' particular paradigm -- backed by the metaphysical link of their "united" god-selves along with Horus and Ra -- *does* go a long way to compensating for this by making the magic they do possess very potent within its delineations.)

                This is rarely, however, an important metaphysical point for the vast majority of circumstances. Only in very specific, very select moments would the metaphysical point actually have any important bearing.
