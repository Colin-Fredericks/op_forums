                What best suits me is to be an Orphan or multi-Tradition mage with a paradigm that combines aspects of the Order of Hermes, Verbena, Akashayana, and Sahajiva. I am surey going to stick with the Traditions but I am challenged to pick only one among these four options. The other Traditions' paradigms have little appeal for me for various reasons. I have nothing but scorn for the lowest-common-determinator authoritarianism of the Technocracy, and little respect for the narrow-minded perspective of the Disparates. The boundless solipsistic freedom and resistance to Paradox of the Marauders registers as a very serious temptation to me, but on the other hand I fear the self-inflicted limitations and lack of clarity of Quiet. The nihilism of the Nephandi feels petty and incomprehensible.

                More precisely, my preferred Paradigm is a combination of Creation is Divine and Alive; Everything is an Illusion; Might is Right; Transcend Your Limits; We’re All God(s) in Disguise. Preferred Practices include Animalism, Bardism, Chaos Magick, Crazy Wisdom, Elementalism, Invigoration, Martial Arts, Psionics, and Yoga. Preferred Instruments are bodywork; dance, gestures, and movement; energy; eye contact; language, music, and voice; meditation; ordeals and exertions; sex and sensuality; symbols; social domination; thought forms; True Names; weapons; writing and inscriptions.To a lesser degree, also blessings and curses; cards and dice; elements: math and numerology; prayer and invocations: wands.

                No matter how much Satyr Phil can wax poetic with tracts about the unspeakable evil and danger of sociopathy, personal experience tells me that high-functioning sociopaths exist that can put their personality to constructive ends and not be Nephandi in training, abusers (by a non-woke definition, anyway), or a threat to anyone but their enemies. Therefore, mine is a benevolent or at least enlightened Left-Hand path as befitting my nature.

                A key feature of my mage self-insert is paradigm goes out of its way to minimize dependence on physical tools and ritual, and emphasize reliance on non-physical foci that can be easily used in the field. Any physical foci that get used have a secondary role and are easily carried and replaced. That's because my playstyle preferences for wizards mandate magic to be done with the mage's body and mind only or nearly so, and be fully accessible in combat or equivalent situations. Magic that needs tools or ritual for any but the most outlandish and overwhelming effects is not real magic, just a pathetic travesty of mundane tech. As you may guess, I do not get along with technomancer or ritualistic paradigms (I do love the OoH, but this element gets excised from my version of their paradigm. Less circles, more Enochian), and it is one more reason why I cannot play a technocrat at gunpoint. This playstyle preference obviously becomes a core paradigm feature in a self-insert of mine.

                                                            Originally posted by Penelope View Post

                                                            Interesting but scary. At least your characters are honest about their worldview.
                                                            It is a fact that high-functioning sociopaths have to act a certain degree of empathy and morality all the time that they are unable to feel, understand, or respect in order to get along in society. It can be done without excessive difficulty up to a decent degree but not so rarely the burden of wearing a lifelong mask feels as tiresome and suffocating as it is necessary. I suppose the experience is not so different from the one of being a spy, a supernatural under a Masquerade, a super with a secret identity, or a LGBT person in a closet. The Dexter TV show kind of depicts how it feels even if it unfortunately endorses the Hollywood toxic stereotype that all or most persons with a psychopathic personality are (would-be) serial killers.

                                                            Amassing political, social, and economic power is not for everyone and at best is a partial escape from the need to feign since even members of the elite in modern society ultimately need the goodwill of society to thrive. Misbehave enough, and you'll lose your assets and even your freedom. In our peaceful and enlightened age, the social niches where you can drop the mask and let your inner asshole surface are few and far between, such as being a professional criminal, a warlord or mercenary, and the like. If anything, it seems society is headed to narrow them even further, and the roles where sociopathic traits are adaptive (e.g. being a warrior or hunter) have shrunk. By the way, this is one reason why I am a sworn enemy of woke ideology and cancel culture. Its victory threatens the likes of me with the existential nightmare of deplatforming, ostracism, bullying, celibacy, unemployment, and poverty as the penalty for not performing an IMP extreme level of empathy for strangers that I am unable and unwilling to feel or act. Posing as the normal nice guy all the time is hard enough.

                                                            Superpowers, however, would truly change the picture. Get a sufficient degree of them to be independent from society and able to fight off anything it can throw at you, and you can really drop the mask and let loose your inner jerk and murderhobo without any real consequence. Being able to RP that kind of experience is one of the aspects of gaming I find most fulfilling and liberating, and why I get incredibly upset if the possibility of playing evil characters gets restricted or punished too much. That's one reason why I generally find natural and congenial to play selfish and amoral OP characters, and why I usually find the moral paragon or underdog kind of character too alien and frustrating to play. On the other hand, my enlightened-selfishness and idealist side is also important enough that it carries through in gaming. Therefore, rather then the malevolent kind of villain, I often find preferable to play the sociopathic but noble and/or well-intentioned anti-hero or villain that ends up acting heroically, being the savior, and/or making the world a better place for reasons completely different from altruism, morality, or empathy. Of course, if the character is supposed to be a self-insert, this line of reasoning gets magnified to the Nth degree.
