      There's a bunch of different ways, really it depends on what you want to do.

      You could literally just build mundane skills to the point where you are the perfect vampire slayer. Get access to any sort of fire based weaponry (Dragon breath shotgun rounds, explosive incendiary rounds, etc [These are hard to get but some points in contacts/allies/make them yourself is a way around it, all mundane]) At that point you can use your magick to just make yourself better at what you already do. Entropy for aiming/dodging/hitting weakspots, life for healing yourself. Maybe use life/mind/something to have a 'snap out of it' effect trigger if you get bit and your willpower isn't high enough to resist. Forces 2 to increase velocity on bullets for damage, or just do forces damage in general.
      You can try using a crossbow to stake them but the DC is kinda high but using magick you could lower it and pretty easily sling some stakes into some hearts. I think the DC is default....8? 7? So with a max of -3 you're shooting at DC 4 and need to do like 3 levels of damage. Not impossible but not nearly as good if there are multiple vampires. (Unless you use like, correspondence 4 to make the stakes hit all vampires at the same time but that isn't a starting character. Now you could probably use matter or some shit to make copies of the stake mid air but this is getting complicated so lets move on)

      You could focus completely on magick. Arete 3, Forces 3, Prime 2. Bam, fireball. If they don't frenzy they are dead next turn because of all the damage they just took (Your follow up attack could basically be anything, as the vampire will be fleeing. If they aren't fleeing they will be so weak their dice pool is fucked. So if they aren't fleeing, just go curb stomp it to death. If they are, just shoot them or something. Getting in their direct path means they can attack you. Any ST who has a fire frenzy vampire rush towards you is doing the frenzy rules differently so this doesn't apply.)

      Problems include: Celerity + dodge will fuck you up. Because fireballs are dodged by using the arete roll, that means every dodge success they get nullifies some of your potential damage (I swear to god if ya'll start screaming at me about some house rules about using wits + occult to aim fireballs I'll fireball this metaphorical building we are discussing in. The default rules for 'magick attacks without a weapon directing them' are pretty shitty.)
      You can help nullify this by using a weapon of sorts, as then it becomes a matter of doding a dexterity + weapon roll, which makes the likely hood you get atleast one success higher. It also doesn't matter if you get 1 success to hit with a magick damage attack or 50, the damage is the same.

      After that...it's really just flavor/what you feel like doing. No starting mage can really make sunlight out of nothing (Requires some shit like forces 5 + maybe optional spirit 5 if your ST hates you) or requires like Correspondence -something- and Forces -whatever- to teleport sunlight to you, but it's a lot of effort for something you can mimic with good old fire.

      Mind effects are worthless if your ST uses HDYDT don't even try. The Difficulty will be far too high.


      For added help:

      Take some combination of flaws + merits and work with your ST to establish you have no blood in your body and are basically immune to the kiss. Immortal of some variant would probably do the trick.
      Familiars of some kind would be helpful, especially if it's an elemental that can't be bit. ESPECIALLY IF IT'S FIRE.

      Werewolf allies is like a 3 point merit.

      Kinfolk is a merit.

      Kung-fu/Do is decent if you have it so you are doing lethal damage with your hits. If you are doing bashing, I don't recommend it.

          Arguably the best way to "beat" a hostile vampire is to avoid them entirely. Keep the leech at a distance, where their powers are less effective. Although when I say "distance", I mean less in the sense of physical distance (although Correspondence can work wonders with the right conjunctional Spheres), and more in the sense of avoiding a fight in the first place.

          First and foremost, the first dot in every Sphere is sensory in nature (barring some exceptions like Mind), and being able to pick out the vampire before the vampire notices you is important. Luckily, Mages "read" as normal mortals to vampire senses, save when they are actively using magick (and then only if the vampire happens to be using Aura Sense at the time). Whereas a vampire sets off magickal senses in multiple different ways. If the two don't already know about each other (by face and/or name), and you plopped them both down in a busy club, the Mage would notice the Vampire way before the reverse. Giving the Mage time to do whatever they needed to do to prepare, whether that means setting up an ambush, creating distractions, siccing some other party onto the vampire, or escaping/hiding. Simply having that much advance warning can be critical.

          Indeed, that's sort of the advantage Mages have against Vampires in a nutshell. Give a Mage time to prepare, and they can accomplish just about anything.

          From there, it depends on 1) what Spheres you spec into, and 2) what your goals are in relation to the vampire.

          If you want to destroy the vampire, Jihelu has already suggested many options. Magick that enhances mundane weapons/abilities. Out-lasting the vampire with Life. Good old fashioned FIRE. Other options include slowing the leech down with Time (or speeding yourself up), teleporting in yourself and/or weapons with Correspondence, turning the environment into hazards with Matter (air to acid, entangling their clothes, impale with concrete spikes, etc). Spirit can get Umbrood on your side, which can make a vampire's unlife an unliving hell, while Mind can forge illusions (you can also use Forces) or protect against certain vampire powers. Prime can also be used as a blunt, Vulgar method of getting vamps off your back; either "Rub the Bones" (Prime 2) or outright attacking with Aggravated damage weapons of pure Quintessence.

          If you just want to avoid being destroyed (with or without counter attacking), Forces can make you invisible or forge illusions to trick the vampire. It's also great for erecting walls of fire, that make most vampires consider a mage more trouble than they're worth. Assuming the ST doesn't make Mind useless against Night Folk (thanks HDYDT), you can erect an "Ignore Me!" field that makes the Mage of little interest to the leech. It could also make the mage attract mortals, keeping a thick wall of people between the mage and the vamp (rendering it dangerous to risk the Masquerade or police action by attacking openly). Time can let the Mage know when the vampire is coming, or where they've been, so the two characters never need be in the same place at the same time. Forces, Mind, and Life (and arguably Prime, with Body of Light) give the Mage options for appearing like someone else, so they can walk around even when the vamp knows their face. If all else fails, the mage can always teleport away with Correspondence.

          What can be trickier is when the vamp doesn't attack personally, but instead uses intermediaries. Using Influence and mind powers to "attack" the Mage through institutions, or sending Ghouls or mind-controlled assailants to rough the Mage up. The Spheres can still help, just with an eye for the indirect moves. Anything that lets a Mage avoid being found by a vampire can work on a Ghoul, and Aura Reading (as supplied by Mind 1 or Spirit 1) can still pick out Ghouls by the pale patches in their auras. Entropy can be used more abstractly to make the systems of government and business work more efficiently in the Mage's favor (a bureaucracy is just a machine made of people and paperwork, so Entropy 3 should arguably apply).

          Going away from the Spheres themselves, the Arcane background can work wonders at making a Mage harder to find, by basically everyone. High levels can be a double edged sword, of course, but even small amounts can help a willworker squeak by unnoticed by all but the most dedicated and perceptive of undead assailants.

                                                      M20 does suggest being able to do an awareness roll to dodge an attack that isn't exactly noticeable, but the rules are vague. Is the awareness roll the actual dodge or do I roll awareness + some other mental stat DC -whatever- and the shit ton of successes I get basically count for nothing because I just need 1 for "Alright you have permission to dodge". So it's either: I get 4 successes on an awareness + stat roll to notice they are casting a spell that will beat my ass and those successes count as my dodge, or I get a weird reflexive roll that only needs 1 success for my ST to look at me and go "HMMMMM you may dodge".
                                                      Not sure of a solution.

                                                      If you're thinking 'how would you dodge X' the example the book gives is literally someone spouting off a death curse and someone using awareness to notice something fucked is about to happen and dodging it. I can't think of anything more vague than 'Alright he's saying mean words, I've got a bad fucking feeling about this. Imma do a sick dodge to the side to hopefully dodge this'.

                                                      There is also the issue I mentioned before with dodging attacks with no attack roll. I think the base assumption without rules is you roll against their arete roll, which means attacking without an attack roll sucks. If you use a laser gun compared to a staff and shoot at a vampire, and they get three successes on their dodge, we get a scenario like this:

                                                      Laser gun: 8 (3 successes) Aggravated damage, 4 successes to hit.
                                                      Vampire: 3 successes to dodge.
                                                      Results: Vampire takes the full damage.

                                                      Fireball: 8 (3 successes) Aggravated damage, no roll to hit.
                                                      Vampire: 3 successes to dodge.
                                                      Results: You subtract the dodge successes from the fireball, potentially making it do no damage or very little.

                                                      Conclusion: Use a laser gun or house rules (Occult + Manipulation is common as fuck) to avoid this kinda scenario.

                                                      The above is how I'm pretty sure the scenario works, but it may not be. I don't think I've ever seen a FAQ or developer give an explanation for how the fuck, in M20, an attack roll works for a magick attack. I think it just hits and it's up to them to not be shitty and dodge. (I mean in regards to an attack roll with no discernible 'weapon', like a lasergun. That shit is easily in the text)


                                                      Also a vampire surviving a fire attack is a win win no matter what. They either

                                                      A: Frenzy, and run like hell. Leaving you free to beat their ass from afar.

                                                      or

                                                      B: They didn't frenzy, and they are now hurt as fuck. Their dice pool is shit as hell and I don't think they can spend blood to nullify it (Can they?), meaning I can beat their butt.

                                                      Or

                                                      C: They dodged my attack and I get my ass /beat/
