    [Fiction] Initiation

    02-19-2014, 07:14 PM
    So, having just finished reading Of Predators and Prey (I know, slowpoke.jpg), watching season 8 of Supernatural, and getting the email about the in-print OP&P, I thought I'd share the short-story I was inspired to write. There've been lots of hunters hunting in my entertainment for the past two months, so, enjoy!

    (I didn't know where to post this, so mods, feel free to move it into the proper forum.)

    Cheers!
    __________________________________________________ __________________________________________________ __________________________
    He had peed himself.

    He hadn’t meant to, and it definitely wasn’t part of the plan, but he had. It had happened hours ago, evidently, since his pants were only slightly damp, but he could smell the sour mix of coffee and energy drinks. Still tied to the chair, he tossed his head back and breathed through his mouth. He tested the restraints again, and nearly choked. The rope tying his hands was connected to the one around his throat, and the more he fumbled, the tighter it drew. He struggled for a moment before he gave up, gasping. Bitterly, already aware that it was useless, he tried to move his legs. They were lashed down to a wooden plank, from ankle to thigh, that was set into the floor. As he expected, it was just as tight as before. He had hoped that they might have slacked after being re-tied, but whoever had come up with this system had perfected it.

    Probably through trial and error, he thought darkly.

    He tried to remember the exact moment he had lost consciousness, and realized it was probably after they had fed him. The three of them had come into the basement from the storm doors, carrying plastic bags of groceries and take out. They had joked and laughed, like kids on their way to a party. One had taken the groceries and gone to the old beat-up refrigerator in the basement, putting items on shelves. He had lifted a six-pack of cheap beer by the plastic ring, and said excitedly, “Man, I can’t wait. I haven’t had a beer in so long!” The other two had laughed as they walked in front of Oliver, one sitting on a rolling office chair and staring straight at him, the third pulling out one of those standing tv dinner trays and placing cartons of fragrant Chinese food in front of their prisoner. The one setting up the food was dressed completely in denim - pants, shirt, even a jean jacket. Alabama tuxedo, Oliver thought wildly, recalling having heard the term before but not sure where. The other sitting and facing him looked like some kind of yacht-club kid, pleated pants and polo shirt, but with a cruelty in his eyes that was beyond human. The one moving around things in the fridge dressed like one of those freaky kids from the mall, with pants so big you could make a normal two or three pairs out of them, and some obscure tee shirt with a giant grinning face asking, “U mad?”
    Oliver couldn’t believe it when Alabama started untying him. The restraints around his wrists fell away and the noose went slack. U Mad raced over, exclaiming, “Ooo, I love this part!” while Alabama pushed the tray of Chinese food closer to their guest. “Ah haven’t had Chinese ina long time,” he said slyly. U Mad looked confused, then blurted out “But Gabriel! He’s not Chinese, he’s white!” Alabama tuxedo - Gabriel - scowled at the other. “Shut up, Terry,” he growled.
    Yacht-club said nothing, didn’t move. Gabriel and Terry stood, silent and grinning, their eyes on Oliver. After a tense moment of nothing happening, yacht-club leaned forward, staring Oliver full in the face, and simply said: “Eat.”
    Furious, Oliver swung his fist at that smug, prep-school face, only to find his fingers digging into a carton of noodles. Viciously, he shoved them in his mouth and took another shot. His fingers jabbed into the noodles again, then into his mouth. Angrily, he ate, as if his brain had mixed up the signals he was trying to send. Every punch he threw, every kick, resulted in him cramming more and more food in his mouth. He coughed and sputtered, spraying rice and vegetables, then shoveled more. The grease was up to his wrists and his face was a mess, he ate to make himself sick. The three around him watched in disgusted fascination. After a while, yacht-club leaned in again, and said, “Okay, that’s enough. Sleep.”
    Oliver fought it as long as he could, the rage plain on his face. He had murder in his eyes, but his eyelids were threatening to put that out. When he didn’t immediately nod off, Gabriel said, “Impressive.” Yacht-club snorted. “Stupid,” he replied.
    Still Oliver fought, the seconds dragging to minutes. Every time he blinked, it was a battle to open his eyes again, and at one point, he simply didn’t. He could hear the three around him, but the sounds were getting distant, fading.

    He had no idea how long he had slept. There were no windows in the basement, and the single bare bulb they had neglected to turn off made a poor clock. He strained to listen for anything other than the hum of the refrigerator, but it was as quiet as a tomb.

    Bad analogy, he thought. Don’t think about tombs.

    Oliver was beginning to go crazy between boredom and worry when he realized there was a stiffness and stickiness on the side of his neck. Panic set in as he thrashed and panted, willing his eyes to see into that impossible spot. After a few minutes of desperate and futile struggle, he gave up, strangely fatigued. He wasn’t slow putting the pieces together, but all the signs forced him to admit something he would rather not.

    They had fed on him.

    Or, at least, one of them had. It had probably happened while he was sleeping, and he couldn’t help but wonder if he had urinated before or after the feeding. He hoped it was during, and that yacht-club got it all over his pants and shirt, though given his position, he knew that wasn’t realistic. Still, the minor act of imaginary defiance made him grin.
    He kept that grin until he heard the basement door from the house open, and footsteps come down the wooden stairs. It was the mallrat, Terry, still wearing yesterday’s clothes. Oliver briefly thought about feigning sleep, but decided to instead level a penetrating gaze at his captor. There was a moment of silence when Terry took the last step and looked at Oliver. They stared at each other, until Terry said, “You didn’t taste like Chinese.”
    Oliver was caught off-guard by the comment. He did his best to hide his confusion, but some of his bewilderment slipped through. Terry laughed, then shuffled over to the fridge. Hauling out the six-pack of beer, he spun the rolling chair over next to Oliver. Oliver could smell the week-old dryer sheets, a faint whiff of hair product, but… no sweat, no smells associated with a living human being, nothing one would assume came from someone who slept in their clothes. He realized that Terry’s chest didn’t contract and expand unless he was talking, also.

    Oliver suppressed a shiver.

    “So, what’s your plan?” Terry set the beer on the floor at his feet. “I may be crazy, but I’m not stupid. You haven’t screamed or begged or cried since we grabbed you, even though we haven’t gagged you and Donovan hasn’t told you to be quiet.”

    Donovan. Oliver would still think of him as ‘yacht-club.’

    Terry spun the chair again and sat straddling it. After a few minutes of Oliver’s resolute silence, Terry leaned back. “I could make you talk, you know. Not make make you, like Donovan can. But like… ‘Ve haff vays ov makink yoo talk, Meester Bond.’” Terry smiled, showing a mouthful of normal, if dirty, teeth.
    Oliver didn’t like the implications of that. He had heard about the crazy ones, the ones who sliced the eyelids off their victims and sucked out their eyes, or cut their captives’ stomach open and made art from their guts. Or, even worse, mutilated and twisted their prey only to keep them enthralled as servants and bodyguards, not even allowed to die. Terry seemed on the edge of being one of those. Oliver opened his mouth, trying to choose his words carefully.
    “I-i-i-initiation,” was all he said.
    “Wha-ho! He speaks! And he stutters!” Terry leaned in close, and Oliver could see all the slight perfect imperfections that gave lie to Terry being human. His skin was too flawless, if a bit pale, with small pores Oliver’s mother would have (figuratively) killed for. Terry’s eyes were too glassy, like a doll, and just as cold. “Or does fear cripple his speech? Well, which is it, sir?”
    Oliver couldn’t take his eyes off Terry. “B-both.”
    “Ha! ‘B-both,’ he says!” Terry reached down and pulled a beer off the ring. He opened it with a pop-hiss and held it out to Oliver. “He’s smart enough to be scared, but stupid enough to be honest.” Terry looked down as if wondering why Oliver hadn’t yet taken the offered beer. “Oh. You probably need a straw or something. Unless you want me to untie you!” Terry laughed as he stood, heading towards the fridge. “We’ve got one of those cool aluminium straws, you know, I think it’s been washed. We keep it in the freezer, so it stays cold and keeps the beer cold, and what was that?!”
    Oliver strained to hear, but couldn’t make out anything over the hum of the refrigerator. Terry stood stock-still, one ear cocked up towards the ceiling. Oliver held his breath, and just when his chest started to burn, he heard the creak of a floorboard above him. A second later, the door to the basement opened, and Oliver recognized the gruff voice that called down to him. “Oliver,” it whispered harshly, “Copectic?”
    “T-there’s one down-” was all Oliver was able to get out before Terry had a hand clamped over his mouth like a steel vice.
    “Check.” And then, louder, “Attention vampire. We have eliminated your buddies. We are coming down to rescue our friend. Any resistance will be met with extreme force. Are we clear?”
    Oliver could feel Terry weighing his options. The hand across his mouth spasmed twice, almost crushing his jaw, and his captor made a low snarling sound. Then Terry’s hand moved to Oliver’s shoulder, and he heard Terry say, “Fine.”
    The two men that came down the stairs were as much alike as they were different. In the lead was an older man, his pot belly stretching the buttons on his military fatigues, trailed by a younger guy in a patrolman’s uniform. They had the same hair, and the same face only twenty years part. Oliver could feel Terry’s thumb dig into his collarbone at the sight of them, even though their weapons were holstered, and he wasn’t sure if Terry even knew he was making a slight hissing sound. They stopped at the bottom of the stairs.
    “We just want our friend,” the older man said, raising his empty hands, “That’s all.”
    Oliver could feel Terry shift behind him, then the hand was gone. A look of surprise flashed on the cops and the soldier’s faces, and a loud boom told Oliver that the storm doors had been thrown open violently. From behind him, Oliver heard a grunt then a strangled cry, followed by a thump. A moment later, Oliver heard another voice come in from the back.
    “Bastard ran straight into me,” it said.
    There was the sound of light skipping, and a girlish voice joined the others. “He ran right into him! Just boom, crash! And I got the razor wire over him right as he tried to run, and - sslliicchhh! - done! It was AWESOME!”
    A girl about Oliver’s age bounded into his line of vision, followed by a burly, trundling stereotype of a Hell’s Angel. She stopped and looked at Oliver, and let out a whistle. “They got you tied up pretty good,” was all she said.
    The biker started loosening Oliver’s hands. “Trussed up with no place to go,” he muttered, deftly unraveling the bonds.
    Oliver felt the noose slide away, and the older man said, “All right. Sit-rep. Three?”
    The cop nodded. “That makes all three.”
    The biker finished untying Oliver’s hands and reached down. He lifted up the open beer, and said, “Hey, look at that! They got us brews!” He drank noisily while the others smiled.
    Oliver rubbed his wrists. “W-what took you so long?”
    “Ah, don’t be mad, College, you’re one of us now.” The old Army man grinned. “ ‘Sides, we left Anabeth almost a week for her initiation.”
    The young girl pouted. “Jerks,” she said.
    “A-assholes,” Oliver corrected. She favored him with a smile in return.
    “Hey, my step-daughter's tougher’n she looks,” the burly biker said. He pulled another beer off the ring and handed it to Anabeth. She flashed another smile as she took it.
    “All right, we’ll have a beer and then finish up. Clint, Jonah, you’re on deck,” the older man pointed at the biker and cop. “Electrical fire. Anabeth, drag whatever’s left of that bloodsucker in here.”
    “W-what about me?’ Oliver started picking at the restraints still holding down his legs.
    “College, you got the best job. Get out of that mess, get back to the van, and drink some beer. Your work’s done.” The old Army man grabbed a beer for himself. “Me, I’m gonna see what we can scavenge. We’re done in fifteen, people.”
    They each took a moment to congratulate Oliver in their own way, no pomp or ceremony for this crowd. Clint the biker just muttered, “Good job, College,” as he walked past, and Jonah the police officer held out his beverage and nodded, smiling. The old man shot off a quick salute before turning and heading upstairs, while Anabeth, the one Oliver was secretly and hopefully waiting on, just gave him another smile and placed her unopened beer in his hands. When they all went about their business, Oliver cracked open his drink and began untying his legs.
    He took a sip and said, “Assholes,” but he said it with a smile.
