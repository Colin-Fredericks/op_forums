        When its a game of mostly non-Mages, for that one player who wants to run a mortal spellcaster I prefer using Sorcerer rules. This avoids most of the many crossover rules that have to be dealt with. None of the characters would know the difference between Hedge Magic and True Magick, so using the Traditions with Sorcerer's as Mages works just fine.

        In my home Changeling campaign there are Sorcerers of the Order of Hermes (House Merinita) who are closely allied with the local Changelings. These Sorcerers must be enchanted, have Faerie Blood (4pt Merit) or have skill in Oneiromancy to directly interact with the Dreaming. In such stories, I treat all such Sorcerers have a starting Banality of 3. Most would be considered madmen by today's reckoning. Also, I treat Awareness as Kenning in terms of detecting Chimerical reality.

                        (Just jumping in -- glanced over some responses, but didn't read previous pages in any detail...)

                        I like Sorcerers, especially as a variant counter-point to what Mages are presented as. In the DA/Ravenloft-hybrid game that I've run on-and-off for some years, I have a fae-blooded (S)orceress PC (the player was/still is new to the WoD, and the Mage game mechanics can get heady; Sorcerer is easy for someone coming from DnD -- as with the player in question -- to adapt to conceptually with little muss/fuss). In-game, there often isn't an immediate differentiation between Awakened Mages and Sorcerers; meta-narratively, the difference in applied metaphysics adds both a different slant on how to approach a character/situation as well as conceptualize how a given character's magic works... or doesn't....

                        As far as the game mechanic/thematic differences, I don't have a problem with Sorcerers and Mages running around in a story: some people may have issues with them, but I've never had one.

Brewing can always be fun to bring some ace in the hole or to mess with the occasional fluff-abusing player.

                                Amongst the groups I've played in, they're quite common. Most GM's I've played Mage allows a Mage to take a merit to give a few dots in Sorcery. I've seen one Order of Hermes Mage who had like 12 dots in Sorcery, most in Hellfire and Weather Control, mix it with his True Magick and wreck a Technocrat base. He just walked in and started tossing Hellfire and supercharging them with Forces 4. He ripped through Hitmarks and Enlightened Agents. The Sorcery made the True Magick not vulgar because he was disguising it as Paradox free magic. Which no one thought of doing before in the Order since Sorcerers are looked down upon with disgust. Don't mess with House Flambeau...

                                We customized the Sorcery rules too. Basically you could power spells with Mana (which was just another name for Quintessence) or Willpower. Also made it so Rituals were just basically Rotes that were better in power than the normal spell level. Also made all Paths have Rituals. So really limited free form magic like Mage, but with a few written spells to keep that Spellbook vibe.

                                Check out my Sorcerer 20th homebrew and my update to Highlander: the Gathering for 20th Anniversary edition.
