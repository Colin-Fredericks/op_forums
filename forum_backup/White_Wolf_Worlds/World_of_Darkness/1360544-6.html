                    If you insist on using Foundations and Pillars, then most of them map to the existing ones. The Subtle Ones are the Ahl I Batin just with all the Islamic elements stripped out so that they can carry out the Doctrine of Unity without issue in medieval Europe. Gardeners = Old Faith; Keepers = Spirit Talkers; Voices of Mercy (and Sword) = Messianics. Memento Mori can probably adapt the ones from the Black Hand, sort of. You're pretty much on your own for the Sacred Passion.



                    I've just always treated the Spheres as an objective fundamental truth of how Reality and the Tapestry work, (ultimately reflecting the ideal that people are more alike than they are different) with how each faction treats and interprets those truths/rules as being the cornerstones for their overall philosophies and paradigms. Just personally, I feel that these things should be something that requires a degree of thought and reading, as opposed to just a bunch of totally separate rules. To me, the "why" has always been more important than the "what". (I also tend to more strongly emphasize the use of Rotes as specific "spells"/rituals that are passed down through different traditions and reflecting their cultures and beliefs.)
                    On top of this, I just really don't have the time or inclination to try to work out five totally new ranked powers for every faction I decide to include, especially if I decide to have a game involving the Web of Faith or some place outside of Medieval Europe. While I'm fairly well read in my old age, I don't consider myself enough of an expert on Sufi asceticism or medieval Hinduism or Bantu culture or so forth to develop something that actually reflects what those people would believe. Compounding this is my long running desire to keep things as simple as possible in terms of mechanics so as to keep the game's story and character roleplaying flowing as smoothly as I can without getting bogged down in mechanical issues.

                    To be perfectly honest, I've long considered Dark Ages: Mage to be a game that had no reason to exist other than to show off a shiny new mechanical system the writers and developer had come up with. As written, nothing is really going on that drives the setting the way the War of Princes does Dark Ages: Vampire, the start of the Ascension War drives Mage: The Sorcerers' Crusade, and the Storm-eater and "Second" War of Rage drive Werewolf: The Wild West (or how current events drove the modern games). And it's a new system that's inherently limiting. You can't play medieval members of the Cult of Ecstasy, for example. And even some of the Hermetic Houses are impossible to do with the existing Forma. You can - finally - kludge your way into playing Mediterranean proto-Thanatoics using the rules from the V20 Black Hand sourcebook, but if you want to play a medieval Akashic, your only hope is to try to dig up a rough draft writer Malcolm Shepherd put up on the internet nearly 20 years ago.

                    Voltaire, "Tolerance" (1764)

                            Because it came up in another thread, I tend to organize the Cult of Ecstasy a little differently than what you see in the revised tradition book (or the list of factions in their revised write up). Rather than organized factions, such as what you see in the the Akashic Brotherhood for example, the cult has four broad groupings generally based on the kamamarga/paths of ecstasy favored by the sects that fall under each banner. While those sects may have leaders, and may or may not get along with others who fall under that banner, these broad factions have no centralized leadership. In fact, trying to lead the Cult is akin to trying to herd cats, and is usually only accomplished via incredible reputation and force of personality.

                            The Seekers of the Divine
                            Striving for unity and sacred congress with higher cosmic powers, spirits and forces, they touch the divine World Pulse and channel the powers of gods into reality.
                            • The Dance of Shiva - One of the oldest sects, originating in India and now strongly tied with Shivaism. Using left and right hand Tantra, meditation, and powerful yoga techniques, they channel and harness powerful male and female energies in pursuit of destruction and rebirth.
                            • The Pythian Mysteries - Descended from the Oracles of Delphi and their attendant cult of Apollo. Famed prophets and seers who channel the spirits of their god through ingestion of herbal, alchemical, and - in more modern times - chemically created mixtures.
                            • The Darwushim - Mystical dervishes of Central and Southwest Asia and North Africa, with ties to both the Ahl I Batin and Akashic Brotherhood. Sufi ascetics who seek unity between the material world and the divine by stripping away the illusions of life and touching the power of Allah.
                            • The K'un Lu - Chinese practitioners of Taoist alchemy and Tantric yoga. They alternate cultivating and purifying their own Yin and Yang energies with the ultimate goal of uniting the two to achieve enlightenment, immortality and Ascension.
                            • The Silver Bridge - Christian ecstatics devoted to pursing the Nine Sacred Passions as a path to divine revelation and communion with the Holy Spirit. Methods differ according to denominational influence, but includes the use of sacred icons, music, dance, fasting, and taking up serpents.
                            • The Serpent Path - Originating in West Africa and from the syncretic faiths of Brazil and the Caribbean, especially Haitian Voudoun. Members use ritual song, dance, sacrifice, and pain to summon and bond with the Loa in sacred congress and take their power as their own.
                            • The Moonchasers - Modern Wiccan-influenced ecstatics. They use chanting, ritual sex, hallucinogens, and sacred masks to channel the Triple Goddess, the Horned Man, and nature spirits in order to cultivate their own inner divinity.
                            The Bringers of Joy
                            Chasing after pleasure and joy as a path to Lankashim, they work to spread ecstasy and uplift individuals, communities, and the entire world through the simple pleasures of life.
                            • The Asparsas - The "Daughters of Pleasure", an all female sect of Hindu Shaktists. Dakini mistresses of Tantra, yoga, and the Karma Sutra, they are skilled ceremonial ritualists and users of Ars Cupidae. They also act as quieters of the restless dead and as divine shield maidens of the demon-slayer goddess Durga.
                            • The Fellowship of Pan - Inheritors of the ancient traditions of Dionysus, revitalized by the Romantic and Bohemian movements. Devoted to lives of wine, women (and men and ...) and song, intellectual and philosophical discussion, music, art, dance, drama and poetry. They channel the powers of ancient gods, the Muses and Graces, and nature spirits in pursuit of the ideal of Arete.
                            • The Feather Flowers - Originating with Mesoamerican Mayan fertility cults, the handmaidens of Xochiquetzal blend ancient shamanism with Catholic mysticism and bruja arts. Members act as healers, midwives, matchmakers, and protectors of children while also comforting the restless dead and trying to calm societal violence.
                            • L'Desir Archane - Born out of the 18th century French Libertines and their associated British Hellfire Clubs, they hold that Pleasure and Pain are two sides of the same coin in the quest for Lankashim. Specializing in bondage, S&M culture, sex, Tantric ritual, and philosophies of dominance and submission, they're also known for summoning dangerous spirits - including demons - for the purpose of sacred congress.
                            • Schaffensfreude - Perhaps one of the least organized sects, members focus themselves on the simple joys of creation and craftsmanship. Individuals pursue their own preferred pursuits, ranging from gardening and cooking to motorcycle mechanics and fine art, trying to strike a balance between the rational worldview necessary to master their craft with the romantic joy of creation. Through this balance and the pleasure they derive from it, they are able to touch Lankashim and often infuse their works with magical energy and effects.
                            • The Wild Boyz (and Girlz)- Developing from California's post WW2 subcultures of motorcycles, hot rodding and surfing, today they are a collective of extreme athletes, daredevils, thrill seekers and ravers. They use danger and adrenaline to "get high on life" and "into the zone" of the World Pulse.
                            • Klubwerks - Ultramodernist techno-ecstatics who evolved from 19th century Terpsichorean music hall performers, Weimar era cabarets, and 70s discotheques. Heavily involved in the underground club scenes of various cities, they use high tech dance music, light shows, and designer drugs to create mass rituals of consciousness expansion.
                            The Sowers of Discord
                            Rebels and revolutionaries using chaos to break down the barriers erected by modern regimental society in their quest for esoteric enlightenment and psycho-spiritual freedom.
                            • The Maenads - An ancient and slowly dying cult of angry and often violent women. They engage in monthly full-moon rituals in which members work themselves into a violent ecstatic frenzy through sacred wine (and sometimes harder drugs), loud music and dancing, culminating in an animal sacrifice being town apart with bare hands and its raw flesh eaten. Sometimes this sacrifice is a human - usually male - who is judged to have committed unpardonable sins against the cult or womanhood in general. The rest of the month, members work as activists and crusaders against the Patriarchy, working magic via psychedelic vision quests and sexual ritual.
                            • The Hagalaz - Nordic seidr witches and Loki worshipers devoted to spreading chaos and undermining the order of society. Militantly anti-Christian and often accused of ties to various neo-Nazi groups, they are currently heavily involved in Northern Europe's various biker cultures and heavy metal fandoms. They are skilled illusionists and masters of mind control, as well as shapeshifters and necromancers.
                            • The 5th World Tribe - A mix of Southwestern peyote shaman and 1960s Hippy acid-tripping anarchists advocating a more "natural" and spiritually oriented lifestyle. Trying to subvert society through personal example, most of them live off the grid in pre-digital or pre-industrial communes or nomadic bands. They reach Lankashim via music, dance and vision quests, and are also fairly famous for their handcrafts (especially fetish talisman).
                            • The Dissonance Society - Intellectual anarchist philosophers and radical libertarians, they originated in the salons of the French Enlightenment and came to fruition with the 20th century Beatnik and academic culture. Members fight the Ascension War in the realm of ideas, writing articles, books and manifestos on various sociopolitical topics while also engaging in various psychonaut experiments involving biofeedback, LSD, and sensory deprivation.
                            • The Lightbringers - Modern Luciferian Satanists advocating absolute personal freedom, objective morality, intellectual clarity and iron-clad self-discipline while breaking societal taboos in order to escape the chains of group think. Members use the Black Mass and other calculatedly offensive materials in their ritual chaos magic and demonology.
                            • Bongo's Rangers - Punk rock renegades founded and inspired by 1970s radical performance artist and musician Depak Bongo. Modern members pursue high-tech anarchism through wild outlaw raves, digital terrorism, and chemically engineered designer drugs that tie users brains in knots and break down the rational underpinnings of reality.
                            • The Chronoknights - A small but fanatical group of chaos technomancers in the United Kingdom. Borderline Maraduers who believe that linear time itself is an unnecessary chain upon the mind and society that prevents enlightenment, Lankashim, and Ascension. Through use of chaos magic ritual and cutting edge theoretical physics, they summon strange and powerful time spirits in order to tear themselves out of the flow of time and subvert the laws of cause and effect.
                            The Destroyers of Flesh
                            Extremists seeking enlightenment and power through obliteration of all moral and physical limits, pushing themselves to near destruction in an effort to extend their own breaking points.
                            • The Kiss of Astarte - A female mystery cult originating with the ancient Bronze Age worship of the Mesopotamian fertility goddess Astarte (aka Ianna, Ishtar, Cybele, and Aphrodite) and her torturous journey through the Underworld. Members preach a doctrine of female empowerment through tribulation, revenge, and sexual power. Their rites include rituals of sacred harlotry and the castration of male attendants.
                            • The Eight Chains - A sect of Hindu Shivaists derived from the Aghori ascetic traditions. Members believe that the soul is one with Shiva, but is bound by eight fetters which must be removed in order to be free of the cycle of reincarnation. In addition, as Shiva is all good and all things are of Shiva, nothing is inherently unclean or taboo. Members use left-hand Tantra, sexual rituals, associate with death and the dead, self-flagellation, the taking of animal forms, and other methods to destroy their own sense of shame, fear, lust, greed and other chains. This will often involve behavior that many would consider antisocial, including rudeness, vulgarity, casual cruelty, and violence. Ironically, their rejection of taboos also means that they are willing to openly associate with and aide society's stigmatized and neglected, including street children, those with diseases such as leprosy or AIDS, the mentally ill, and other unwanted and Untouchables. More than a few help operate medical clinics, orphanages, and other projects.
                            • The Studiosi - A radical offshoot of the medieval Gnostic Cathars and flagellants, fanatically devoted to the destruction of the modern Rex Mundi that is the Technocracy and all of its fronts. Members work to overcome the prison of material existence through extreme fasting, scourging, near-death experiences, and other forms of self-torture. They are also extremely effective terrorists in the Ascension War.
                            • Los Sangradores - Mesoamerican shaman who preserve ancient Mayan and Toltec bloodletting rituals, using their own blood and pain as a sacrifice to the gods, enabling one to commune with the spirit world and move outside of time. Over the centuries, members have begun to incorporate various aspects of Catholic rituals, mysticism and trappings into their rites so as to more easily move among mundane people.
                            • The LeMarchand Club - An extremist offshoot of L'Desire Archane founded in 1970s Los Angeles. Members no longer differentiate between the sensations of pleasure and pain, using magic to torture themselves and each other to extremes that would otherwise be impossible to survive in an effort to experience the ultimate in ecstatic bliss. Most are completely addicted to the sensations they experience.
                            • The Heart of the Void - One of the strangest and more obscure sects. Members strive to reach Lankashim through the complete absence of sensation. To this end, they experiment endlessly with Zen and transcendental meditation, biofeedback, sensory deprivation, and self isolation. It's rumored that some who master this path simply vanish from reality.

                                    In a similar vein, I prefer to recast the Euthanatoi as two Traditions: one (the Chakravanti) that's similar to the Tradition as published, but with the necromancy stuff largely excised: they still include the “Good Death assassins” notion, though that's less a matter of what they're all about than what they're known for (in truth, it's just one aspect of what they do; but it's such a controversial aspect that it's all anyone else ever talks about); but actual interactions with the Underworld and the dead isn't in their bailiwick any more than it is for nearly any other Tradition. Factions include the eponymous Chakravanti, the Aided (geasa can be about death, but are generally about so much more), a Greco-Roman sect that takes its cues from the Fates, and the Valdaermen.

                                    The other Tradition (the Euthanatoi) is all about necromancy; but other than that and the name, it has little in common with the published Euthanatos. These Euthanatoi are more of a “Wraith crossover Tradition” that deals with the Dark Umbra and its denizens. Its views tend toward ancestor veneration; and where the living are concerned, it tends to focus more on the notion of the Wisdom of the Ages and learning from what has gone before. Its factions tend to correspond to the various Dark Kingdoms that are featured in Wraith: the Heirochthonoi roughly parallel the Dark Kingdom of Iron; the Madzimbabwe similarly parallel the Dark Kingdom of Ivory; the Bata'a correspond to Les Invisibles; other factions correspond to the Dark Kingdoms of Jade and Obsidian (China and Mesoamerica, respectively), the City of Delights (India), the Kingdom of Clay (Australia), and the Sea Which Knows No Sun (Polynesia). There's also an Egyptian faction that draws upon the same mythology as Mummy, and other factions corresponding to other cultures. Each faction has its own take on the nature of the Afterlife, as detailed in the various Dark Kingdom write-ups.

                                                    So, the Celestial Chorus.

                                                    So, my take on the Chorus has long been that they are not synonymous with any real world religion. Instead, their belief is that The One and his/her/its relation to real world religions is akin to that of the Akashic Brotherhood in regards to Do and its relationship with real world martial arts. Which is to say, the Chorus believes that it is the original prototype ur-religion and that mundane/real world faiths are "Gateway Faiths" to The One in the same way that mundane/real world martial arts are Gateway Arts to Do.
                                                    Primarily, these Gateway Faiths have tended to be monotheistic ones, especially those from along the Indian Rim and eastern Mediterranean Sea. This includes certain forms of Hinduism, Zoroastrianism, Judaism, and Atenism, and later various faiths that have grown out of them, such as Sikhism, Baha'i, Mithraism, Manichaeism, Islam, Christianity, and Gnosticism. (In more than a few ways, the Chorus is very similar to a Gnostic mystery cult.) It also finds expression through the faiths of the Yoruba, the Algonquian, and others. In addition, there are a number of syncretic faiths that also serve as Gateway Faiths for the Chorus, including Seicho no li, Cao Dei, Rastafarianism, and certain interpretations of different African diaspora religions. But all of these are ultimately just seen as starting points to a "higher truth" about Reality and Creation.

                                                    The Sun is a major symbol of The One, being as it is the source of light and life just as The One is. Fire is another symbol, both as light and life, but also for purification. And then there's the concept of sacred sound/language. This is the Song of The One. It is the universal ohm, the Word of God, the language which spoke Reality into being. When members of the Chorus use magic, they are tapping into that power.

                                                    The ultimate goal of every member of the Chorus is to reunite with The One (ie, Ascension). Because all human beings are the creation of The One, all are equally worthy of love and respect. And those that are hurt or otherwise fallen away from the light must be helped back into it. (Although sometimes this means tough love or accepting that one must be purified through judgment in the next life.) They also strive to bring human society back in tune with The One and its Song. Hopefully, this will ultimately bring about the Sacred Shining City, the Chorus's ideal utopian society in which everyone is free to hear the Song of The One and strive for divine reunification. In stark contrast to the Society of Pure Thought (and its modern incarnation of the NWO) ands its idea of all humanity singing the exact same note, the Chorus believes in a, well, chorus of endless voices, singing the Song of The One each in their own way but all still in harmony.

                                                    In spite of the difficult relationship the Chorus and the Verbena have had over the centuries, my own head canon is that Valoran and Nightshade became friends during the Mistridge Tribunals and especially from his kindness in the wake of William Groth's death.

                                                    And then there's the various internal factions.

                                                        Factions of the Celestial Chorus (pt 1)

                                                        The Sacred Ministry - The political and theological leadership of the Chorus, they are priests, preachers and prophets working to guide the world back in to harmony with The One and establish the Sacred City. They favor a mix of high ritual and fiery charismatic presence.
                                                        • The Golden Throne are the elites of the Chorus, many of them holding positions of power and influence within both the Tradition and in mundane society. They are bishops, Brahmans, and deacons advancing the creation of the Sacred City through business, politics, philanthropy, and media. Most members are skilled in mundane and mystical social arts (including Ars Cupidae) as well as sacred oaths and powerful rites.
                                                        • The Burning Bush are radical social activists focused on the middle and lower classes. They call their followers to live lives of righteousness, social justice, and compassion. They are powerful orators and able to work charismatic miracles to foster hope and change. However, more than a few are also prone to flights of demagoguery and zealotry.
                                                        • The White Doves are the Chorus's formal diplomats and heralds among the Traditions. They serve as missionaries and apologists while advancing the Chorus's interests and furthering the creation of the Sacred City. In addition to Ars Cupidae, they are also well known for their travel and defensive magics.
                                                        • The Song of the Goddess is an alliance of feminist voices advocating that The One is a feminine being/concept and force. They promote what they see as matriarchal teachings and values in both mundane society and the plans for the Sacred City. In addition to Christian Mariology, they also include those who incorporate various African beliefs as well as those who follow modern Wicca.
                                                        • The Sacred Blood is a quasi-secret society claiming to protect and husband a number of sacred or divine bloodlines. Most of these are found in Europe, India and South America. Their hope is the creation of modern messiahs who will help lead the Chorus to victory in the Ascension War and help establish the Sacred City. Most of them have some skill in Life magic.
                                                        • The Gabrielites are a small and cliquish society originally founded by members of the Sacred Order of Pure Thought who were given sanctuary by the Chorus after Technocratic purges. They still advocate the idea of One World, One Faith and the idea that a global theocracy devoted to The One is the best way to lead humanity to the Sacred City. Obviously, they tend to be frustrated that very few people listen to their ideas.

                                                        The Aegis Kai Doru (Shield & Spear) - The guardian orders of the Chorus, Paladins, crusaders, and sacred guardians sworn to protect and defend not only their Tradition, but also the innocent and the helpless against the depredations of the Technocracy and of the forces of Darkness. All are trained warriors and skilled in martial magics.
                                                        • The Sons of Mithra are a fraternal fellowship that traces their origins to both the Roman Mithraic mysteries and to the earlier Persian Zoroastrianism. Dauntless warriors of truth and light against the forces of deceit and wickedness, members live by strict codes of personal and group honor. They are renowned as skilled diplomats, healers and monster slayers, and for their signature gladii and maces.
                                                        • The Sisters of Gabrielle are a society of warrior women founded in Hellenic Greece by their namesake. Members strive to master both martial and spiritual arts in order to safeguard the material world from threats beyond the Gauntlet, including hostile spirits, demons, and the Nephandi. They are as famous for their skills as exorcists and seers as they are for their combat ability.
                                                        • The Sword of Vishnu is an ancient and sacred order of Hindu Vaishnavist Kshatriyas. Each of them has a sacred dharma to battle wickedness and evil while striving for unity with the universal ohm of The One. They train in both ancient and modern forms of warfare, while also studying mystic yoga and the healing arts.
                                                        • The Carriers of the Shield are a Shi'ite sect of sober Sufi mystics and scholars. They work to protect and guide their communities as teachers, lawyers, healers, exorcists, and - if needed - warriors.
                                                        • The Knights of St. George are a secretive order of Gnostic-Christian warrior monks originating from the Crusades. Keepers of the hidden Goetic Gospels, members learn how master their own sinful urges and to combat the sin of others. They have gained a reputation as slayers of vampires, werewolves, and other monsters.
                                                        • The Ksirafari are an incredibly secretive order of spies, undercover agents, and assassins. Their origins are unknown to outsiders, but are believed to be from the ancient near east. Members invoke the names and powers of the darker angels of the Heavenly Host, as well as practicing Ars Cupidae, sacred alchemy and technomagic.
                                                        • The Septarian Militias are a loose alliance of prepper and survivalist groups striving to separate themselves from the modern government leviathan. Members and their acolytes live off the grid in self sufficient compounds, pursuing lives of simple righteousness while amassing sizable amounts of modern and mystical firepower.

                                                            Part 2

                                                            The Alexandrian Society - Scholars and philosophers who have tirelessly worked since the Hellenic age to discover and preserve ancient knowledge and divine wisdom, as well as to seek out and explore new manifestations of The One in the modern world. While perhaps the most bookish members of the Chorus, their sacred calling requires them to journey out into the world and face whatever dangers stand between them and the lore they seek.
                                                            • The Librum Sanctum is the oldest and purest faction of the Society, created among the Library of Alexandria. They recover, restore and safeguard ancient works of history, philosophy, theology, science, and magic, and well as collect sacred relics and artifacts. Not just librarians and archaeologists, but teachers and smugglers who believe that humanity's past and its ideas are a vital cornerstone of the Sacred City.
                                                            • The University of Light originates with the ancient Roman Collegium Praecepti and has almost as ancient ties with the lost Ahl I Batin. They believe that science, technology and philosophy - both modern and esoteric - allow one to study Creation and through it gain a better understanding of The One's grand design and purpose. Members are famous as kabbalists, theurgists, alchemists and astronomers, as well as for their skills in modern science and technomagic.
                                                            • Dues Ex Machina is the tongue in cheek name for a collective of ultramodernist coders, mathematicians and engineers seeking the Song of The One in the Digital Web and modern technology. In addition, they work to use the Internet, telecommunication, and counterculture media to advance the creation of the Sacred City and spread the Chorus's message of harmony.
                                                            • The Collegium Solomon is an elite society of theurgists and demonologists following the secret teachings and lore of King Solomon the Wise. They collect the True Names of various spirits and other beings so as to command them in the Chorus's name and bring them into accordance with the will of The One.

                                                            The Hands of Mercy - Healers and peacemakers advocating The One as a source of succor and of universal brotherhood. They believe that mercy and compassion must be the foundation of the Sacred City, lest it be a castle built on sand. They view their calling as being society and spiritual as well as physical, and so are often skilled in Mind, Spirit and Life magic.
                                                            • The Nashimites originated as a dualistic heresy in the Middle East. They believe that if enough of Creation turns to darkness and evil, The One will fall into darkness with them. Because of this, they are devoted to doing all within their power to rescue and redeem even the most fallen soul (even the Nephandi if possible). In this way, they hope to bring healing to the world and make the Sacred City a place of redemption.
                                                            • The Black Madonnas originated as an Egyptian-Roman Isis cult and adapted to devotion to the Virgin Mary. Skilled healers, confessors and exorcists, they primarily focusing on helping the Restless Dead find peace and return to The One.
                                                            • The Chalice of Succor takes inspiration from the legends of the Holy Grail. They combine faith, prayer and spiritual healing with modern medicine and psychology to help all in need, especially with the hidden hurts that Western medicine often overlooks. Foremost among their beliefs is in the sanctity of human life.
                                                            • The Grey Doves are a rogue offshoot of the White Doves. Diplomats and missionaries, they believe their calling is to help end strife among the mundane and the Awakened. To this end, they work to improve inter-Tradition relations and atone for past mistakes. Members are famed as fair and honest arbitrators dedicated to fostering peace and understanding.
                                                            • The Children's Crusade was founded in the 19th century to safeguard children and rescue them from abuse and exploitation. Members work around the globe to help child soldiers, orphans, runaways, slaves and victims of child trafficking.

                                                            The Anchorites - Monastic ascetics and hermits seeking perfection and purity of the self while avoiding the sins of the modern world which distract from harmony with The One. While many of them try to avoid the Ascension War, it will often find them regardless.
                                                            • The White Robes are a sect of Jain-influenced ascetics who have totally devoted their lives to non-harm to all living things. They are rarely found outside of India, where they are often protected by their less pacifistic Tradition-mates.
                                                            • The Children of Albi originated among the medieval French Cathars and managed to survive that sect's destruction. Members reject modern society and technology, preferring to live in small isolated communes in Western Europe and the Americas. They are universally vegan and pacifist as they quietly pursue spiritual unity with The One.
                                                            • The Breath of Life is a small movement of hermits who dwell alone in the wilderness. There they tend to the native wildlife and nature, seeking the Song of The One among the plants and animals.
                                                            • The Invisible Clergy are radical ascetics who choose to dwell in abject poverty among society's lost and forgotten. There they serve as shepherds to those often seen as untouchables.
