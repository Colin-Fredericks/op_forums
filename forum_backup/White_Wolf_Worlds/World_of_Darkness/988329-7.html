            Oh boy... You've picked a pretty large couple of subjects there.

            Combat: Take a look at page 445 onwards for all the combat tables. There are a lot of them, so take a look at the ones you think are relevant to you and print them out. You might even want to copy and paste some of these tables onto another sheet of paper to cut down on how much you need to print. But they'll provide more than what you'll ever need.
            My personal opinion is these tables are overkill. If you need this many tables to run a game, then perhaps the rules are starting to get in the way of the story. But to each their own.

            Now, the Umbra is its own special little headache. Page 85 onwards of M20 gives a pretty good almost all encompassing guide to the Other Worlds, though I'm guessing you already read it and found it lacking.

            The books that detail the Umbra specifically are:

            Book of Worlds :- reads like a travel guide written by Mages, and can be a bit obnoxious at times, though there's a good list of systems and rules on the back pages, if somewhat incomplete. It goes through everything, the Three Worlds, the Dreamlands, Paradox Realms, Horizon and Deep umbra locals. Though it's 2nd ed, so no storm, and Diossetep is still in tact. This book goes through how sphere magic is affected by the various realms, as well as how paradox may manifest on each.

            Infinite Tapestry :- reads like a cross between a spiritual enlightenment guide, and a textbook on astrophysics. If you're getting into this one, be prepared for a lot of thinking. It deals with the Spirit world post Reckoning, and adds a few new features such as Disembodiment, as well as a few tricks to get around the Avatar Storm such as Astral Sojourn. This book does NOT look at the Middle or the Dark umbra at all, but instead focuses in incredible detail on the Astral Umbra. The book will also talk about Horizon and the Deep umbra, but it seems to me as though it's written under the assumption that you've already read Book of Worlds, make references to locations described in greater detail there.

            Other than that, you'll have to go to different game lines for more details.

            WTA: Umbra :- This goes into detail on the Middle Umbra, also known as the Spirit Wilds. While Book of Worlds details the realms of the Middle Umbra adequately and tells you all the Mage relevant parts, the Werewolf book goes into much greater detail, and has a lot more information on the Penumbra than any other book I've read before it. Bare in mind that this is a Werewolf book, so it won't give you any information on Sphere magic or Paradox, and some of the realms aren't really accessible to Mages normally (like Wolfhome or Pangea). But there's more than enough in there to warrant giving it a quick look.

            W20 Umbra: The Velvet Shadow :- This one is much like the other Umbra book in terms of focus, Middle Umbra and Penumbra stuff abound, but it goes a lot deeper and more detailed than its predecessor. It even has details on the Astral Realm and the Deep Umbra, though not much. Again, it's a Werewolf book, so nothing on magic, though plenty of Spirits if that's your thing. Formatting isn't nearly as tight as it is in Umbra though.

            WTA: Rage Across The Heavens :- This gives you the Werewolf take on Horizon and the Deep Umbra (a distinction that Werewolf doesn't actually make). It serves as an alternative route through the planets that only Mystic Mages would have access to. Again it's written for Werewolf games, so all it can offer you is ideas. Not as useful as others, but still a fun read. Loads of information on spirits and Umbral Navigation though.

            Wraith 2nd Ed :- This one is essential if you're planning trips into the Dark Umbra. The first 3 or 4 chapters are dedicated to explaining the Shadowlands and the Tempest well enough to start. Every expansion will add a bit more to the realm of the dead, whether it's the Shadowland citadels, Stygia, the Yellow Springs, or the Labyrinth that tickles your fancy, there's a book on it somewhere (or not, Wraith was discontinued prematurely, but oh well). Most Mages never see the Dark Umbra, though it could be worth looking into for your Euthanatos. Also gives a deeper context to why they see the Good Death as an important rite, it prevents humans becoming Wraiths.

            Changeling: The Dreaming 2nd :- If you you want to deal with the Maya, also called the Dreaming, then Changeling will give you a wealth of inspiration to draw from. The Dreaming is the least static of all of the other worlds, so you can get away with making a lot more stuff up and having it be just as good as canon. The only Mages who can voluntarily explore the Dreaming is, unsurprisingly, the Dreamspeakers.


            Ok, that should give you an idea on just how vast the topic of the Umbra is if you look at everything. So I'm going to ask a few questions to help narrow it down for you.

            Is the Avatar Storm in your game?

            What kind of mage is your Spirit 3 guy?

            What kind of Chronicle are you running? (Themes, plot, setting, etc...)

            Do you use spirits in your game, and if so what kind?

            Do you want to set whole stories in the Umbra for a while?

            Are you happy with your mages going to space? (It was all the rage back in 2nd ed)

                Okay, Magical Items...

                The Revised Storyteller's Companion (not the Handbook) has a chapter on Wonders. The material there is fairly useful, and includes a few Rotes adding onto it (like how Matter 3 can be used to make an object resistant to most mundane damage, something the Sphere description never really touches upon).

                But the most important source on magical items is Forged By Dragon's Fire, an entire book devoted to the subject. It expands on some (but not all) of the material from the above book, as well as giving new stuff (like Grimoires and Primers).

                                That list of books is intimidating, but Umbra isn't at all. It's just an excuse to toss anything you wish into the game. Spirits are representations of ideas, events and things of our world (and possibly some other worlds). The places they live in are shaped by what they are about. Yes, among those you can find all possible mythological beings and religious pantheons - so any idea you have will work.

                                There are two basic layers of the Umbra
                                - one which is just an eerie echo and shadow of our world where night is day and where all sorts of things have spirits that go about their business and seek to improve their power through advancing their poofolio
                                - the other which are the myriad realms and far away places which include mythical places like Avalon, Shangri la, Valhalla and the Land of the Lost or maybe crazy afterlives, godly palaces, fractal planes of mathematics, living representations of planets and Cradles of Life. They can even include past events, future events or even whole alternate realities...

                                The nuances are so varied and with odd interactions that you should focus on what suits you and not worry too much about what books have to say about it. Just see what inspires you and what you might like to use. For instance, how about a group in your New York that follows and praises the Egyptian Gods? As they do, their reverence and rites give power to the Gods (highly powerful spirits) which first reshape the local Umbra into a semblance of their own vision of how a land should look before they start possesing people and bringing their power into the mortal realm...

                                Or maybe there is a chantry into which an ancient egyptian artifact was brought and since it has been brought there, the Chantry has a gateway into a mythical Ancient Egypt where Gods do as they will? Or maybe the long forgotten Geb lives his life as a limping hobo that occasionaly performs strange wonders and dreams of being reunited with Nut, who may or may not actually live in New York...

                                What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.

                                                    Yeah never throw out a hint or red herring you do not want the players to follow, because its super awkward when they do. Players have a knack for latching on to such things.

                                                    As for dealing with vampires you will have to determine how crossover rules are going to work. There are a few different options available, but none of them are perfect. M20, Vampire Storyteller's Handbook, Blood Treachery all have different interpretations how to deal with the two groups together. All of them will require some work from you to make decisions and rules calls. Be prepared with as least some basic stuff such as how Disciplines and Spheres interact (things like can a Mage countermagic a Discipline, what kind of countermagic do the vampires have), what happens when a Mage is ghouled/embraced (trust me dont underestimate your player's abilities to make strange decisions and actions).

                                                    You also need to figure out if you want this to be a limited contact or a plot hook that will pop up repeatedly.

                                                    (Warning Metaplot used ahead)
                                                    As for the Tremere it depends on a few factors. The first is whether the events of the Second Massassa War have taken place, if so then relations between Tremere and Mages are at an all time low constantly simmering just outside of war again. This would likely preclude anything other than betrayal from the Tremere since working with Mages could be seen as an act of treason from either side's leadership should they find out.

                                                    The exception would be the value of this artefact. In Transylvania Chronicles and Nights of Prophecy it is revealed Saulot has succeeded in taking over Tremere's body and Tremere had to flee into Goratrix's (again based on the metaplot in your game whether or not these occurred). This would mean he is currently limited to the capabilities of a 4th Generation vampire which is not how one wins the Jyhad. Perhaps Tremere has been influencing his clan to seek out information on sleeping ancients again so that he might divine the location of another antediluvian to reclaim his former glory. It would not be so open or foolish as to reveal what had occurred to Tremere merely an agenda added to the docket (left vague so that the regents can read into it what they want, "Seek an ancient, perhaps another will be promoted to the council and they wish blood to go with the station" - for example.

                                                    Depending on how the Tremere view this artefact will determine whether the node offer is good or bad.

                                                    If those events have not occurred then it is somewhat simpler as the conflict between the Tremere and Mages has been slowly cooling for centuries. This makes the dealings on the Tremere side probably made in better faith and with somewhat less backstabbing hoping to gain some useful if young pawns.

                                                    Regardless why would the Tremere offer up a place of power? A few things come to mind.
                                                    • They dont own it, they just know it. In this case the node is claimed by another supernatural group and so it is not immediately useful to the group. This will come off like a betrayal.
                                                    • It has some ongoing negative effect. It could be the node is tainted, haunted, or some other bad thing. The Tremere will be quick to point out that Mages such as themselves should have no problem dealing with whats there. It puts the node in hands the Tremere can monitor. Less betrayal, but still comes off like buying a lemon car.
                                                    • The node is dormant. All the signs are there but the node is not active. The group will have to look into how to reawaken it. This could be anything from rousing a sleeping guardian spirit to performing a certain ritual.
                                                    • A safe secure node seems far too good a prize for such a simple artefact and the Tremere might tell them of it but say they the price for using and holding it will require more from the group. Basically a proper no problem node should have strings attached.


                                                    Some things to be aware of.
                                                    • Your players might get it into their head to become ghouled or embraced, be ready for that or how to deal with it or telling them no.
                                                    • Do you want the players to deal with the Tremere again or not? This will affect the quality of their offer.
                                                    • Fire, Forces 2 and a flame available (even as simple as a lighter) will allow the Mage to hurt gouts of Rotschreck inducing aggravated damage causing fire at the vampires. Mages are super dangerous to vampires for this reason.
                                                    • Dominate, Without Mind dealing with Dominated characters can be very difficult and can lead to a disempowered feeling from the players as the vampire gets to command their character like a meat puppet.

                                                        There's a point the above post is neglecting: how do the Tremere know the site in question is a Node? How would they even know what a Node was? Sure, Methuselahs who were originally Mages back in the day would know about Crays, but would a modern Tremere who never was a Mage or had any ties to the Order of Hermes know anything about that? Is it at all likely that a Tremere old enough to remember that would be the ones dealing with a random group of Mages? (In that last case, I have the answer: No, it isn't. Any Tremere that old doesn't have the time to spare dealing with this business. They'll have a younger Clan member deal with this business on their behalf, assuming they know about the deal at all).

                                                        So...does Clan Tremere even know what it's offering? They can't see Quintessence flows, even with Auspex.

                                                        I suppose the Node would be creating Tass on the regular - Tass typically makes up half of any given Node's output. The Tremere could be aware that the site produces said Tass, which could be used as a Ritual component for Thaumaturgy. It may be that the kind of Tass the Node produces doesn't have a Resonance that works well with most Tremere Rituals (blood magic tends to be darker and more twisted than mortal equivalents, requiring ingredients of similar quality). As such, they couldn't get much value from the site...but they know enough about mortal magic to know that the Tass can be of greater value to the Mages. The deal being made is basically the Clan attempting to trade a sub-optimal resource for an artifact they treasure more highly, to a group that doesn't understand the artifact's worth but would gain much greater value from the site.

                                                        All things considered, a good trade for both parties. The Tremere don't really need to double-cross the Cabal, unless they seriously think the Cabal will betray them first.

                                                        I suggest having the deal go off as quickly as possible, with the Tremere exiting stage left as quickly as possible. Given how much vampires love their privacy, this is entirely in character, and not just a way to avoid sidetracking the plot. Further, when the PCs get their hands on the Node site, it could see altogether hunky dory...for the time being. Any number of complications could come to light after the characters have held it for a while. Just long enough to figure out a good story hook from it.
