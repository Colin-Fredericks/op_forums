    Technocrats and The Discarding of Instruments

    03-02-2026, 04:57 PM
    I have a couple of questions after reading the sections about Arete/Enlightment and how its progression relates to the discarding of instruments, especifically in relation to Technocrats:

    1) Does the indoctrination of Technocrats demanding the use of Instruments even in high levels of Arete result in a disadvantage when dealing with mystics or technomancers in combat, or it's not that big of a deal in this context?

    2) The paragraph in the section Growing Beyond the Tools of M20's Corebook, p. 567 kinda has a chilling prophecy for the Path of a Technocrat:

    Technocrats receive so much indoctrination that they never
    break through their preconceptions that way. Unless he leaves
    the Technocracy to pursue a different Path, a Technocrat remains
    convinced that his faction’s perception of reality is the only one
    that truly works… and that perspective demands instruments
    of science, even if those instruments aren’t always obvious. At
    Arete 10, a Technocrat becomes part of the Machine – a focus
    for the Will of technology instead of the other way around.
    Does the Technocrat with Arete 10 just... lose their free will here?

                          Yes, the Technocracy is at a bit of a disadvantage in this regard due to the inability to work without Instruments. At lower levels of Arete, this drawback is shared with other Technomancers: as of Arete 5, mystics will have already specified two Instruments that are now optional, while technomancers of all stripes won't have specified any. And both Technomancers and Technocrats are completely unable to use the Work Without Focus rules of M20.

                          But on the other hand, the Technocracy arguably has an advantage that comes with this drawback: it can be argued that their focus on the importance of tools is what allows them to make facilities that let them produce Devices without needing to expend personal Quintessence or a dot of Willpower in doing so. In short, this drawback is coupled with the Union's ability to mass produce Devices.

                          And when coupled with the option in M20 Sorcerer saying that mages can create Paths using essentially the same rules that exist for creating Talismans, the Technocracy arguably ought to have an edge in terms of techno-sorcery as well, letting them innovate new Procedures and then turn those Procedures into Techno-Sorcerous Paths that their Extraordinary Citizens can learn and wield. A mage creating a Path of Sorcery would need to expend a lot of personal Quintessence on the Arete rolls to set it up, and would need to finalize the whole thing by investing a dot of Willpower into it; the Technocratic counterpart could do the equivalent without either of those requirements.

                              As for Arete 10: recall that the Technocracy has Control. Recall also that the Technocracy isn't really big on the pursuit of Ascension; at least, not personal Ascension. Even among the Traditions, Arete 10 doesn't equal Ascension; instead, high-Arete mages who don't seek Ascension end up with other fates such as becoming archmasters, Exemplars, or gods. I could see an Arete 10 Technocrat becoming part of Control, as the Technocratic equivalent of the non-Ascension fates of non-Technocracy mages.

                              That doesn't mean that Technocrats are blocked from achieving personal Ascension; as I said, Ascension isn't synonymous with Arete 10. The Technocracy doesn't provide any guidance to the Scientist who seeks personal Ascension (though that's also true of most of the Crafts, with the Batini and perhaps the Children of Knowledge and the Hollow Ones as exceptions proving the rule), and may even actively obstruct such efforts (personal Ascension is arguably unmutual); but personal Ascension is, in the end, personal; and whether or not it can be achieved ultimately depends on the individual.

                                      And I do want to stress that it matters whether we are talking about quick spells popped off in the field and big efforts. For the small stuff, being able to work without instruments matters, as does getting the difficulty reduction for working with them after they are dropped. It gives the Traditions and others a versatility that the Technocrats will have a hard time matching. It also means that in the rare situation where they are caught without tools they are at an extreme disadvantage, though between emergency analysis* and seeded technology** they often still have options.

                                      Big efforts, on either side, are usually done with rituals, careful planning, and plenty of preparation. These will typically use the mage's instruments whether or not he's dropped them. And yes, using dropped instruments does make the spell easier, but so does careful study, planning, and preparation. It does mean that you could stop a Technocrat from using a ritual (or whatever they call it) by stealing or sabotaging one of the important machines for it, but honestly they should be able to disrupt a Traditions ritual (say, one Voormas is working on) by messing with the important components too. So for the big stuff the Technocracy is barely hindered at all.

                                      *Emergency Analysis - A Technocrat method of casting without tools. In the right circumstances Technocrat field agents can analyze their surroundings and "calculate" that a convenient event is about to happen (the ice is about to crack, the ladder is about to give way, etc. Think dramatic environmental effects in movies). The agent may or may not realize they are making the event happen, but it works either way.

                                      **Seeded technology - Some of the Conventions seed conventional technology (pun not intended) with small pieces of hypertech so that agents can sometimes use the technology around them for extraordinary effects. So often field agents are closer to their tools than it seems.

                                          A follow-up question:

                                          What intrigues me about the idea of a Personal Ascension for a Technocrat (a topic I already asked about in another post) is how unlikely it seems. I know that Ascension is not defined exactly in the books and the ST has leeway to conceptualize Ascension on a table/player basis and also that Mage is more about seeking Ascension that achieving it. Even then, per the M20 corebook:

                                          As The Book of Mirrors points out (pps. 60-61), Ascension
                                          is not hitting Arete 10 or maxing out all your Spheres. It’s not
                                          about power but about the transcendence of the need for power.
                                          Mere power, in Mage, is a trap. The more a mage pursues it, the
                                          further she gets from ultimate Ascension.
                                          The thing is, everything about the Technocratic Union screams a "need for power". Of course, that power can be used with benevolent intentions, guiding and uplifting the Masses, and all the sourcebooks since Guide to The Technocracy points to a more nuanced scenario than just souless mirroshades. But still, even with all the progress alluded to be possible in Technocracy Reloaded, the same sourcebook points to the totalitarian nature of the Union itself (p.113):

                                          Although the Union has always been considerably more
                                          complex than outside stereotypes would lead us to believe, the
                                          Technocracy remains — despite its current emphasis on reform
                                          — a fundamentally militaristic totalitarian engine of global
                                          control.
                                          How can a Technocrat achieve Personal Ascension, if control (i.e. the need for power) is so integral to a Technocrat's identity?

                                                  Originally posted by Agrat View Post
                                                  A follow-up question:

                                                  How can a Technocrat achieve Personal Ascension, if control (i.e. the need for power) is so integral to a Technocrat's identity?
                                                  Well you need to distinguish between the Technocracy's identity and a technocrat's identity, because they are not the same thing. It is at least reasonable that the former is an impediment to Personal Ascension, so a technocrat who embodies that identity would a) not even seek personal ascension and b) have problems achieving it if he did.

                                                  But the first issue is, does the typical technocrat embody that identity? It's not at all necessary that the members of an organization themselves represent those ideals in all things. I'd say it isn't even that common, even among members who believe strongly in the organization. The principles that an organization works on do not always make for good principles for a person to live their life by. So we need to figure out what the typical technocrat identity gets in the way of personal ascension.

                                                  And the second issue is... there's no such thing as a typical technocrat, in the same way there's no such thing as an average person. However we measure what is typical, normal, or average, that's just a generalization and it is rare to find an actual person who actually fits that measurement. So we need to figure out what parts of the "typical technocrat" identity get in the way of personal ascension and then look at how many actual technocrats have those elements.

                                                      The way I'd phrase it is that a necessary condition of Personal Ascension for a member of the Technocracy would be to decide "am I a technocrat, or am I an Enlightened Scientist?" Technocrats are, by definition, people who believe in the rule of experts, and that they are the experts. Most members of the technocracy are, unsurprisingly, technocrats. Enlightened Scientists cover a wider scope: while they share theTechnocracy's Practices and Instruments, they don't necessarily share its Paradigms, such as Might Makes Right. Navalon is the closest thing to a canonical group of not-technocratic Enlightened Scientists in the game, though they have their own problems.

                                                      As Michael pointed out, disengaging from the Ascension War and leaving whichever faction you;ve been in might be another necessary step for Personal Ascension.

                                                      What may not be required, though, is abandoning your Practices or Instruments. If you think that a Technomancer's inability to Work Without Focus or a Technocrat's complete inability to surpass his Instruments completely blocks him from achieving personal Ascension, I'd just point out that that's the flip side of "you must reach Arete 10 to Ascend": while raising Arete or surpassing Instruments can help achieve personal Ascension, neither is strictly necessary. The actual determination is narrative, not gamist.
