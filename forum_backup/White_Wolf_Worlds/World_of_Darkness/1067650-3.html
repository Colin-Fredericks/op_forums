            Originally posted by Spacecat View Post
            Not exactly that but almost that. Thanks. Something like the same idea from the opposite perspective but that list really does help. .
            So for mortals and nearly mortals who aren't allies of the supernaturals, there are the Year of the Hunter type books — http://whitewolf.wikia.com/wiki/Year_of_the_Hunter

            These guys tend to have fewer clear cut labels than the Year of the Ally counterparts. Also, since they're meant as antagonists, they're a bit more powerful than the allies. They're also hard to describe as evenly as the allies. For this list, it'll be game line, followed by the relevant books, and then a brief description of the subject matter.
            • Vampire: The Masquerade — Hunters Hunted, The Inquisition, Hunters Hunted II
              Hunters Hunted focuses on lone mortal hunters. The Inquisition is about the Inquisition, better known as the Society of Leopold. Hunters Hunted II revisits lone mortal hunters plus most of the other Year of the Hunter groups.
            • Werewolf: The Apocalypse — Project Twilight, (maybe Freak Legion, Possessed, and Subsidiaries: A Guide to Pentex)
              Project Twilights covers government organizations dealing with the supernatural.
              I mention Freak Legion, Possessed, and Subsidiaries because they technically meet your criteria, but they may be too supernatural for what you're after. Freak Legion and Possessed deal with mortals possessed by spirits. Subsidiaries is a book about Pentex, a supernaturally evil mega-corporation.
            • Mage: The Ascension — Halls of the Arcanum
              The Arcanum is a group of mortals dedicated to studying the supernatural, think the Watchers from Buffy or Highlander.
            • Wraith: The Oblivion — The Quick and the Dead
              Ghost hunters, both scientific and supernatural.
            • Changeling: The Dreaming — The Autumn People, C20
              Covers both Autumn People and Dauntain. Autumn People are mortals so entrenched in their mundanity, magic fails around them. Dauntain are probably too supernatural. They're Changelings broken by Banality. C20 is giving them both a major overhaul.
            • Hunter: The Reckoning — First Contact
              Describes most of the above from an Imbued perspective.
            • Demon: The Fallen — Saviors and Destroyers
              Covers people of faith and mortal demon hunters.
            • General World of Darkness — Sorcerer, Demon Hunter X, Mafia, Gypsies
              Of these, Mafia is probably closest to what you're looking for, mortal members of the Mafia who may or may not have a supernatural edge.
              As mentioned before, Sorcerer covers mortal hedge magic. Demon Hunter X covers the Shih and Strike Force Zero, two Asian anti-supernatural groups.
              Gypsies is mentioned only for completeness. It's super racist and should probably be ignored.

                    Many many thanks.

                    Just to clarify, my main concern is not so much to exclude certain groups because of their level of power but to exclude crowd favorites so that players will choose from among the more rarely used and the least used creature types. This incidentally seems to leave us with the weaker creature types. The power level of a creature type becomes a concern only in that creatures with significantly more power will become magnets for players and the last thing i want is for all the players in the entire group to pick the same creature type. It's okay for some creature types to be a bit stronger than others so long as it's not enough of a thing that more than half of the players will decide to play one. This is very relative criteria, of course, but it's the intent.

                                            Well, I do not have any problems with KotE and thought that WW did a tolerable job with the KJ. When it comes to KotEK, you have several problems. Despite being separated by only twenty miles, the Kindred Embraced in Sudan automatically lowers their generation by one and does not have the dice cap from Orun. That is why KotEK was not a good book (plus the treatment of African culture was rather poor).

                                                Originally posted by Spacecat View Post
                                                Just to clarify, my main concern is not so much to exclude certain groups because of their level of power but to exclude crowd favorites so that players will choose from among the more rarely used and the least used creature types.
                                                Ah, my apologies! I totally misunderstood what you were going for!

                                                Hm, this gets tricky. Crowd favorites and rarely used types is going to vary greatly from one player group to the next. It also gets messy since some stuff in some game line are difficult to define as core and non-core. Some even have various degrees of it. It'll also be hard to get a comprehensive list of books since a lot of this information is spread across a ton of places. I'll just stick to the major sources of info to help you get started in the right direction for the stuff that interests you, but understand that for most of these, there's a lot more out there. The focus here will be on things meant to be player characters. I'm not even going to get into things like Black Spiral Dancers or Marauders. Since popularity is so subjective, I won't comment on that.

                                                Vampire: The Masquerade
                                                The most core you can get for this game are the major clans and the Camarilla and Sabbat divide. Past that on the clans side, you have the independent clans and a bunch of bloodlines. There are also other sects such the Anarchs, the Black Hand, and the Inconnu.
                                                Books: V20, Guide to the Camarilla, Guide to the Sabbat, Guide to the Anarchs, Anarchs Unbound, the clanbooks, Lore of the Clans, Lore of the Bloodlines, The Black Hand: A Guide to the Tal’Mahe’Ra

                                                Moving further afield, Kindred of the Ebony Kingdom describes the vampires of Africa. I've never read this one, but my understanding is that they're pretty similar to other Cainites.

                                                Kindred of the East describes Asian vampires. These guys are really their own supernatural creature with no ties at all to core book vampires.

                                                Werewolf: The Apocalypse
                                                Werewolf is a pretty tightly focused game. The spot light is definitely on the Garou. For these guys, you've got W20, the tribebooks, and Players Guide to the Garou.

                                                The game does branch out into the various changing breeds, known collectively as the Fera. For info on them, look to W20, the breedbooks, Players Guide to the Changing Breeds, and W20 Changing Breeds.

                                                Hengeyokai details the Asian shifters, though they're really just the same Garou and Fera with a different culture.

                                                Mage: The Ascension
                                                The main focus of Mage is the Tradition mages. Look to M20, the traditionbooks, and Guide to the Traditions for these guys.

                                                Later into Mage's run, the Technocracy mages became much more playable. M20, the conventionbooks, and Guide to the Technocracy have you covered here.

                                                The disparates are the collective term for the magical orders that don't fall under the Traditions or the Technocracy. Information about them is spread all over the place, but M20 collects them all together.

                                                Dragons of the East details Asian mages. I haven't read it, so this is about as much as I can say about it.

                                                Wraith: The Oblivion
                                                Wraith focuses on the Stygian ghosts and their political factions — the hierarchy, heretics, renegades, and the guilds. You can find information about them in The Hierarchy, Renegades, the various guildbooks, and the forthcoming Wraith 20th.

                                                Outside of Stygia are the various other dark kingdoms whose ghosts work differently. To my knowledge, the only one to be published was the Dark Kingdom of Jade, which covered Asian ghosts. I think some of the other dark kingdoms will be covered in Wraith 20th, but I'm not sure.

                                                The Ferrymen are a special, mysterious faction of ghosts. They're covered in Ends of Empire.

                                                The Risen are ghosts who've reclaimed their bodies to finish some kind of unfinished business. Think The Crow. They're covered in The Risen.

                                                Changeling: The Dreaming
                                                Changeling focuses on the Kithain and their Seelie and Unseelie courts. The Kithain include the major kiths plus a bunch of other kiths spread out between the various sourcebooks. C20, the kithbooks, and the three books of houses cover these guys.

                                                The Gallain are the different kinds of changelings that, to various degrees, don't work the same way as the Kithain. These include the Nunnehi (Native American fae), Menehune (Hawaiian fae), Inanimae (nature and elemental fae), Hsien (Asian fae), and the Adhene (fae that sided against the Kithain in a decisive war). Nunnehi are covered in the Players Guide. Menehune can be found in Shadows on the Hill. Inanimae are in Inanimae: The Secret Way. Hsien are covered by Land of Eight Million Dreams. Adhene were introduced in Denizens of the Dreaming. C20 has all of these guys.

                                                Hunter: The Reckoning
                                                Hunter doesn't have any types that fall outside of the creeds. There are two creeds that are considered failed experiments by the Messengers, but they work mostly the same as any of the other creeds. You'll want Hunter: The Reckoning, the creedbooks, and the Hunter Players Guide.

                                                Hunter Holy War covers Middle Eastern Imbued. I haven't read it, but as I understand, it's the same creeds as the core book. Also, I remember it got a good bit of flack for being unintentionally racist back when it was released.

                                                Demon: The Fallen
                                                Just like Hunter, Demon doesn't cover any demon types that fall outside the umbrella of the Fallen. Demon: The Fallen, Demon Players Guide, and Houses of the Fallen cover the main things.

                                                Orpheus
                                                Orpheus focuses on living mortals who use technology to temporarily project their souls to the lands of the dead. It also has an alternate take on ghosts. Information about them is spread around the entire limited six book run.

                                                World of Darkness
                                                The only supernatural type that doesn't belong to any game line are the Mummies. They started as a Vampire sourcebook, but got moved to a black cover World of Darkness book for their second edition, called World of Darkness: Mummy. They got reworked drastically for their third edition, called Mummy: The Resurrection. I think most folks consider the two entirely different supernatural creatures, although they're in the same continuity. The Mummy Players Guide details non-Egyptian mummies.
