            Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
            That's an interesting hybrid of the Traditions and Technocracy there. I tend to view the Traditions as being for individual paradigmatic freedom, their ideal world being one where every person can determine what works for them. The Technocracy on the other hand is for a unified world with a single coherent way of doing things that works well for anyone. These Librarians seem to want a single coherent world, but one that contains multiple layers some of which are only available to those who dig deeper.

            What would you say their common blind spots are?
            The librarians want humanity to move into the future, but they don't want total homogeneity in reality zones. To them, it's completely fine if a coven of druids have a nudist mystic colony in the middle of a huge national park. They accept that it's a good thing if sleepers agree on a superficial consensus across most of reality (planes fly, cars work, medicine saves lives), but it's also good if people can "supplement" that reality with their own beliefs on religion, mysticism, ascetic practices, weird science...

            STRENGTHS:

            Technocracy: actively engaged with the real world, and looking towards humanity's future.

            Traditions: value diversity in belief and custom, and reject a singular consensus

            WEAKNESSES:

            Technocracy: reject individuality, seek to force their ideas on everyone for the "greater good"

            Traditions: the majority of Traditions Mages spend their lives accumulating knowledge and raising their Arete, but do nothing to help sleepers across their whole life. They take part in the Ascension War at best; cloistered and detached from the human race with some notable exceptions. At the absolute worst the Traditions are like the worst kind of economic/philosophical professors who spend decades locked away in their faculty arguing over the interpretations of Marx-Engels, and in their whole lives barely interact with any members of the working class or help them in any way.

            Arguably the Librarians have the strengths of both and have shed the weaknesses but... well sadly the fact that they have a tiny membership and hold very little power within the sects means that it's almost a moot point.

                            So I've done a few bits and bobs with my players. Letting them choose classes. Mundane vs Arcane EXP seems to be working out quite well, thinking I'll keep the house rule.

                            Players are bonding with characters with similar practices, including eachother. They're working together in ways that would be impossible if they were older and more set in their ways. They don't "know it's impossible", so it's possible basically. Why shouldn't a would-be ecstatic and a norse-style verbena work together on an effect? The ecstatic builds a nice bit of arts and crafts to dangle and show them the way, and the verbena helpfully draws a rune representing the realm of Heimdall onto it. You can see why some in the Traditions love the school, and others find it horribly distasteful to have impressionable children mingling with other mages.

                            One set of two PCs are sharing a sanctum that suits both of them. Another pair have very similar interest in plants but are keeping their Sanctums separate. However they're nearby, and I'm planning that as they upgrade the background they may bump into eachother and run into some conflict. The druid has found an old tree stump from a sacred grove in the hedge maze, along with some pieces of what was once a standing stone, all tucked away and hidden behind a path of thorns. Just over the hedge the herbologist works in his greenhouse with his bee familiars. They could learn a lot from eachother in time.

                            Ramnesis I had a repeating corridor warped by Correspondence. Two PCs managed to learn Correspondence to escape by comparing notes and taking the advice of a 5 point familiar. A small bird brought the artistic PC scraps to make a piece of art with, metal hanging from strings so the metal dangles and spins and points in certain directions. I played around with it and told the Norse PC that he saw Heimdall's influence guiding the other PC as he figured out how to escape this corridor that just led back on itself while staying in a straight line. Since the PCs start with 3 dots in spheres and not 6 I'm going to be handing out some free dots in the first year, keeping track of who's had what so it stays even.

                                                03-07-2019, 07:00 PM
                                                5 PCs, including my own (there is a co-ST so we can both have PCs, which is fun!) visited the tallest tower and received gifts from a strange old man called Hurt. He seemed like he had known they would be coming for a long time.

                                                Legend has it that Hurt climbed up the tower 100 years ago, at least, and has never come down. All first years who visit him may ask for a gift. Those who suit the items he creates (he is a master of Wonder creation) are given one of his many wands and other magickal items. Those who lie outside of his understanding receive items he has collected through various means. Gifts from alumni, mementos from old friends, mysterious items sent by researchers, and items taken from Union operatives, among others. The tower is the tallest in the castle, but it is "shorter on the inside", making it quick to climb.

                                                Oliver, the "false chosen one", received some spiel about both his mother and the wand she received, and how he sensed it had been destroyed "on a terrible night where many paths ended", and a very vague hint about the dark mage he 'defeated' when he was young. He was given an ivory wand called the Tabula Rasa that can erase memories. Hurt had given him several to try, and none had worked with him. Frustrated, he tried the wand our of curiosity. Just to see what would happen. He regretted it, remembering handing another bright-eyed young man such a powerful ivory wand (its twin), and later wishing he hadn't. He wanted to take it back but stopped himself. The twin of the wand, which hasn't been mentioned yet, can create false memories. Bearing the two wands gives a terrifying degree of control. It is only with the twin wand that Erebus was able to create such a large following, through his powerful command of memory, taking control of many Sleepers by giving them memories that inspire total unquestioning devotion, hence Hurt's regret.

                                                Finn, who is connected to plants, was immediately pegged as having "something of Willow (his totem) about him". He received a willow wand that controls plants (Life 2). "Most of my creations are more ordered and well behaved. This one got away from me, honestly. It shall suit you though." He talks about the items like they are his children, and strangely enough very few of the items share his Resonance or Synergy.

                                                Derek, who is a nervous artist, was described as "strange, fae or weird even..." by Hurt, foreshadowing his parentage. He disappeared through a trap door and returned soaking wet, holding a box. Inside was a paintbrush called Helen, which was impossibly beautiful. Golden wood, perfect hair at the tip... and when he experiments with it he will find that his paintings reveal the true nature of the subject.

                                                Ivan, who is Norse, was given a ring that another Seidr had gifted to Hurt many years before. "This ring was given to me by a remarkably brave and bravely remarkable woman, who hailed from a tribe like your own. I know she would want you to have it." The ring is marked by the Goddess Freya and reveals the spirits of the middle umbra, especially those linked to nature, and allows communion with them, and minor command over them.

                                                Alexandria, the prototype of a synthetic human project aiming to design a tactical leader for a group of soldier units (synthetic mix of Matter and Life patterns), was given a pair of Tru-Shades liberated from a Union agent. "Once held by the Technocracy, and now in the hands of the Traditions... I think these glasses will suit you quite nicely, Alexandria" (symbolism!). Since she is a sleeper (she has Enhancements and Advantages to make up for this) their level 1 effects will help her immensely, allowing her to examine Life, Matter, Forces and Prime patterns through vision, as well as giving spatial coordinates (bearings and similar) via Correspondence, although only one aspect can be examined at a time. The glasses are very limited however, in the sense that they can only ever give 1 success' worth of information, and the senses can't be mixed.

                                                The other PCs will get their own items in their next session.

                                                    Interesting session with a lot of inter-PC conflict. A lot of stuff happened that I didn't plan, but it seems to be improving the story in ways that I couldn't have planned!

                                                    - Aisea (cyberbully computer gremlin, self-identified "otaku" who likes hacking things) and Jayden (neglected son of morticians, obsessed with death and subterfuge, has an attitude problem, very edgy) climbed up to see Hurt. Aisea received a sort of wand-remote that allows her to perform a Data 2 rote and connect with distant computer systems, so that she can use her Computer skill on them (or perform other sphere effects). Jayden received a tarot deck and a bag full of dice. The dice have an Entropy 3 effect which manipulates the outcome of random events like card draws, roulette spins etc to suit the user. The dice themselves are made from the bones of a man who cheated at cards, and have a Chinese origin. They're both leaning towards Virtual Adepts and Euthanatos respectively.

                                                    - Aisea discovered that Oliver and Ivan had broken into her and Jayden's den, and confronted them about it. She lied and exaggerated what they had done (claiming they'd smashed up her computer gear) to create more conflict. She then decided to use her new toy to hack Oliver's phone and listen to his conversations. She overheard something quite critical. She also stole some money from his bank account, after discovering he had Resources 5, and purchased a bunch of junk food and computer gear. To respect the PC's backgrounds I've said that the bank can't explain the transactions and refund the missing money. A and J also have a go at cheating on online gambling, and make a few hundred pounds.

                                                    - Derek, Oliver and Ivan are told by their housemaster that they're welcome to read any books in his office, except for those on the top shelf which are restricted*. When he's distracted they take one of the books and put its dust jacket over another book of a similar size (a clever plan I hadn't considered; this means it will take much longer for him to realize the book is missing). The book contains details of a powerful Wonder hidden in the school in the 1400s, and they put together an idea that the housemaster is trying to find the object and steal it, based on the fact that the other book he had on the shelf was a history of the school. This conversation is overheard by Jayden and Aisea, who are delighted to have something to blackmail them with. Alexandria discovers their secret and memorizes the books contents, and they all agree to hide the book somewhere safe so that the housemaster can't get it. Derek and Oliver both have dead parents, so they are very drawn to the story, which details a Wonder that can resurrect the dead.

                                                    *the housemaster has done this knowing that they won't be able to resist being told they can't read something. His plan is for them to discover the Wonder's location; at first it will seem like he has an evil plot, but in truth it's all part of an obscure end of year examination.

                                                    It's all been quite hectic, and the inter-player conflict has been very inventive and fitting. There's this feeling that in the end they'll all be good friends, but for now they're feuding.

                                                            The players had a confrontation with a rather dangerous third year, in which they refused to show him the respect he demanded as a hereditary Peer (for those of you who don't know the UK, our House of Lords has people who are there because they inherited a title) and as a well-respected member of a dynasty of Mages. This culminated in the Norse PC spitting in his face, feeling his own honour was besmirched (I'm a god, I don't bow to children of weak and effete houses), and this led to a fight where the PCs escaped with some bruises, and a deep cut on the Norse PC's face.

                                                            The peer in question is descended from a long lineage of Hermetic/Ecstatic Magi who are strongly linked to the Art of Desire. Debauchery, poetry, art, the Classics... they run the line between the Hermetic and Ecstatic principles. It is rumored that the lineage originated with some sort of tryst between Bacchus, the first Duke, and his wife, resulting in their son being metaphysically blessed by the god of Wine, Death, Carousing and Fertility.
