                            I'd love to see Rathbone's Skeleton Keys explored more thoroughly, and Victoria's specific role as a patron of the Technocratic Union. Since I've been going on a Victorian history kick lately, I have a few ideas on things that could be explored. Forgive any canon errors on my part, as I don't have the books currently at hand, and this is based entirely off of some ideas that I was kicking around a bit over a month ago. I'll start with my favorite Tradition-to-be. I'll probably post more later if I'm not actively discouraged from doing so.

                            The Society Of Ether is still within the Technocracy as the Electrodyne Engineers, but since that link finally dissolves shortly after the turn of the century, we're going to be seeing lines of communication opening up with the Traditions as the Victorian Age progresses further towards imposing an ever-narrowing paradigm on every part of the world touched by the British Empire, and beyond. The Convention (and future Tradition) is likely characterized by enormous levels of intrigue, fierce debate between those who wish to work within the Technocracy and those who think that it's time to forge a different path, and growing alienation from the rest of their Technocratic colleagues as Paradox causes their sky ships to burst into flames, their steam-powered death rays to explode in their faces, and their own patchwork creations to turn upon them in their hidden laboratories. Never the most trusting types, paranoia likely runs rampant through their laboratory chantries. It's almost certain that an Etherite character will be forced to pick one side or another. Of course, it's entirely possible that the character will try to remain above the fray in order to focus on their research, but a moment of crisis is coming, and friends may be thin on the ground for those who go their own way.

                            You obviously have the traditional mad scientist types, steampunk engineers, and Jules Verne's would-be conquerors, but also patent medicine creators, idealistic Yankee inventors, ingenious blacksmiths, and itinerant peddlers of miracle gadgets and electrical toys. More grimly, the Engineers are almost certainly among the colonizing forces, using steam-powered automatic cannons and clockwork soldiers against the native cultures, particularly when "local" magic is involved. The Science Adventure stories of the time, of course, portray all of this in the most heroic of lights.

                            The number of science fiction tropes created during the era are obviously to numerous to count, and I'm not going to insult anyone's intelligence by going into detail on them. Suffice to say that anyone reading this probably has a sufficient familiarity with the material, or at least knows where to look.

                                One element of Victorian society that may be worth a mention is the club system. With a few disreputable exceptions, nearly early every male Londoner of the upper middle class and above belonged to some club or another where he could congregate with people of like status, interest, or occupation, and women began to form their own, similar clubs later in the century. These organizations served not just a social purpose, but also provided opportunities for professional networking, political maneuvering, and information brokering. To the best of my immediate recollection, middle-class Londoners had their own organizations based more upon common hobbies, political leanings, or areas of social concern. Working-class citizens and those below them on the socioeconomic scale usually didn't have the spare time for such things, though informal groups with shared interests would often gather at local pubs or meeting houses for social purposes.

                                While these organizations tended to be strictly segregated based upon sex, there's no reason that a Gothic Victorian club couldn't be notorious (yet oddly alluring) among the moneyed classes for allowing women, non-Anglicans, non-Whites, and--it is rumored--even those of lower social class into its halls. If a Storyteller wanted to run a chronicle based around this concept, with the club itself possibly serving as a Chantry, The Kerberos Club from the rpg of the same name is an obvious source for inspiration and adventure ideas. Naturally, adapting the concept to the World of Darkness would require that the club look more like it did early in the century, when the Strange was mostly a matter of whispered rumor, than it did later in the century, when airships were commonplace and magic potions could be ordered through a steampunk version of the internet.

                                Among the Traditions on the Council at the time, the Order of Hermes seems the most poised to be involved in the club system, with membership particularly strong in those organizations focused on areas of academic and esoteric interest. Some clubs may serve as little more than places for their members to indulge in sybaritic and Dionysian excesses forbidden by both law and society, which would be a draw for Seers of Chronos/Cultists of Ecstasy. Disparates who make up the Victorian equivalent of Hollow Ones could probably be found among the membership of literary and artistic clubs, as well as various bohemian societies. Celestial Choristers and Euthanatoi would belong for their own reasons, likely professional and social ones. As these clubs at their worst tend to represent a culture that is currently trampling over indigenous populations of several other nations while simultaneously destroying pristine woodlands and belching industrial smoke into the air at home, both the Verbena and the Dreamspeakers seem unlikely candidates at first glance. On the other hand, spiritualists and mediums often count themselves among the Dreamspeakers, and the more successful of them would often be accepted into some of the more broad-minded clubs. Verbena who do have reasons to join would likely belong to clubs focused around their profession, particularly those in the medical and naturalist fields, and those devoted to outdoor pursuits and a healthy lifestyle. I have to admit that I'm drawing a blank when it comes to Akashics. If anything, I could see a club member awakening subsequent to joining, but I can't picture one wanting to join a club unless they had to do so for social cover, or as a specific part of a much larger goal on behalf of the Tradition itself. That's not to say that there is no reason, simply that I'm not coming up with one right now.

                                The Technocratic Union is undoubtedly woven in with the club system, and likely runs more than a few of them while exerting enormous influence over many others. The Invisible Exchequer/Syndicate in particular would be in a position to take credit for the Victorian era explosion of clubs, as this was the result of a newly prosperous and growing upper middle class finding ways to associate with with those of similar financial means after having been turned away from the elite clubs for the upper class and nobility. The Lightkeepers (that was the NWO at this point, if I'm remembering correctly) would likely be found just about everywhere one could find useful information or influence the paradigm, and that would cover nearly every club in Mayfair. Those who have the time, resources, and the correct recommendations and prerequisites for membership would likely belong to several. Explorators/Void Engineers are likely so tied in with Explorer's clubs that membership in one or more is an unspoken necessity for advancement and reputation. They'd also probably be found in the clubs for military officers, particularly ones favored by those who have seen foreign service. The Electrodyne Engineers, the Analytical Reckoners/Virtual Adepts, Aesculpian Order, and whatever Iteration X was at the time (looked it up: International Brotherhood Of Mechanicians. Cute.) most likely belong to a few clubs and societies for scientific advancement, with significant overlap between all four Conventions in each of them. As the general decor of Society of Ether chantries is often portrayed as resembling a British club, it can probably be safely assumed that this aspect of Victorian society was of immense importance in their development as a Convention, and later a Tradition.

                                Apologies for the rambling. I've had these ideas rattling around in my head for quite a while, and as I don't currently have the opportunity to game due to other, more vital priorities, I haven't found the motivation to write them down till now. Whether anyone finds the idea useful or not, I'm grateful for the opportunity to finally put these rough ideas out there. There are plenty more, but I'll pace myself for everyone's sake.

                                EDIT: While the history of the Victorian era is rife with various esoteric fraternal orders, occult lodges, mystery cults, and spiritualist societies, I'm treating them as separate in type and purpose from the social clubs of the sort mentioned here.

                                                        IIRC didn't the sphere system itself arise in part from Egyptian learning - so the re-discovery and promotion of those teachings might become a major plot point in and of itself since it's also foundational to the rise of the Traditions.

                                                        FWIW I always wanted to see Sorcerer's crusade as more 'transitional' between what we know of the Dark Ages magical society and its practices and the new. As I recall the sphere system was relatively new and despite being a better approach to magic than foundation/pillars not everyone would have embraced it (or even heard of it) so you could still have plenty of the 'archaic' means of doing magic (I always thought the minor spheres option from the Storyteller's handbook was a good way to depict 'transitional' magic between foundations and spheres. Although I could see using alternate 'mage' systems introduced in games like Werewolf or Hunter as a lesser variation. Or heck, adapting Awakening's Arcana.) it would take some time for the spheres to proliferate through the magical community and become a standard so you could see lots of variations or distortions of the system. Heck, the institution of the systems that become the bedrock of the modern Mage game can be a big part of what happens during Sorcerer's Crusade.

                                                        In that sense it becomes a 'Crusade' to expand ideas and (for some) an attempt to re-take reality from those who have made it more static for the Traditions, whilst the Order seeks to evolve its own goals and eventually grows into what we know as the Technocracy. The latter one actually has the potential to be more nuanced at that, since there are definite upsides AND downsides to the Technocratic Union's rise (the goal to protect and elevate humanity via reason and science isn't bad in and of itself, but some of the methods and beliefs that outcome fosters are dubious.)

                                                        Sorcerer's Crusade also seems a good time for a 'transition' between the the silent Founts and the prominence of Daemons (Avatars). Some will be seeing them, not everyone may understand what they are or what they represent and that can reflect a huge challenge all its own as well. Many may not still hear them whereas others may hear it and realize there is a new 'way' to evolve their abilities, and still yet others may struggle to come to terms with the Avatar and fail. Some may go crazy others may be seen as 'possesssed' etc. Perhaps not all the psychopomps have vanished yet either, and this decline may be noticed by some in the magical community and now they have to grapple with the challenges that disappearance creates and/or attempt to discover why this is happening.
