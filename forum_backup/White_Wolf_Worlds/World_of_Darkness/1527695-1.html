    I understand the desire to want to have more crossover between vampire characters in other games. That can be a fun thing. However, the problem is still that vampires, by their very nature, are predators of everyone else who is living. It's a scorpion and the frog situation. The burden should never be on "OK, you who don't like vampires, why should this vampire not be trusted?" The burden should always be "OK, you who like this vampire, why should this vampire be trusted?" The price for wrongly trusting a vampire is high, while the price for wrongly not distrusting a vampire tends to be very low. The economics of the situation will always prioritize not liking vampires. Regardless of the "Sense Wyrm" situation, this is what is always going to be the default.

    I am going to speak from a Werewolf perspective, as I don't really play Mage or Changeling. While I am sympathetic to wanting a more 1e "crossover can be fun" feel, I also want things to make sense.

    Let's look at the tribes most likely to have some dealings with vampires.

    First, the Bone Gnawers. I will say that you can justify almost any kind of relationship with Bone Gnawers. The tribe, as a whole, no longer follows the Litany in a way recognizable to the other tribes. This changed as later editions made the Bone Gnawers more upstanding Garou, but this was definitely the case in early Werewolf. The tribe has an ongoing relationship with the Nosferatu clan where they trade information. A large part of the tribe is also de facto allies with the Sabbat (!) probably because of the needs of the Central Park Caern in Sabbat held New York. Using that as an example, it would not be surprising if Bone Gnawers elsewhere had similar dealings with local vampires. Whether the Bone Gnawers actively destroy vampires or come to some sort of arrangement really depends on a lot of local factors, so the ST can justify a lot. They might always watch their back, but you can defend a lot of cooperation as an ST.

    Second, Glass Walkers. This tribe would be more antagonistic to vampires than the Bone Gnawers, but more prone to making deals of convenience. They don't like it, and don't trust vampires, but the environment where they work in practically requires it. Otherwise they'd be sucked into ongoing vampire wars and couldn't get anything else done. Unlike the Bone Gnawers, this is less about not giving a crap about the Litany, then about making difficult moral choices in a challenging environment for Garou where many moral dilemmas come up. Since vampires are mainly an urban environment in the setting, these first two tribes take care of almost all real encounters the typical vampire may have with a werewolf. This is going to be about rival gangs defining their turf and having their other side respect that, so only so much can happen. But it does open the possibilities for non-violent encounters.

    Third, Shadow Lords. Individual Lords might make any number of deals with vampires if it suits their purposes. The game even created the Bringers of Light camp in order to explain why this happens. The Shadow Lords obviously have strong enemies among the vampires, but that just creates justification for them to ally with their enemy's enemy and play vampire factions against each other. This is a case where some Shadow Lords may think "this particular vampire or group of vampires can be useful to me" rather than some blanket permission to intrigue with them. And other Shadow Lords might use such alliances against their tribemates. But in terms of storytelling, the ST has some options.

    Fourth, the Uktena and Wendigo, through their kinfolk, have some dealings with the Sabbat - which I assume are Loyalist Packs made up of Country Gangrel vampires of Native American stock on the Path of Harmony. This is probably a dark secret of the tribes and are the least described in the sourcebooks, but it's been there since the early days. This is the Penumbra of the Gray Squirrels as an "ally" mentioned in the original STs Handbook to the Sabbat. It probably does not extend to all the Sabbat as the group they are encountered with is very particular (and in Revised becomes completely annihilated by other Sabbat), but if you hew to earlier depictions there is something.

    Fifth, there may be Children of Gaia who do not consider vampires who do not detect as a Wyrm as not of the Wyrm. They are much more willing to see new neonates as worthy of support or even protection. This attitude may not apply to all CoG, but there may be enough to make it noteworthy. I think it is really only with this tribe (and probably only with certain camps) that you could have a situation where vampires are truly treated as individuals and could even want to help high Humanity vampires in their struggle to maintain their Humanity. Likewise the Stargazers might be very opened when it comes to particular vampire individuals.

    The other tribes are much more circumspect. Very rare dealings with compromised individuals or packs with most being extremely hostile. But the above tribes' activities with vampires are much more documented.

    Another option is with Ronin, Lost Cubs, and Lunatics. They probably share the same fear as anyone else that vampires might feed on them, but all could be very open to cooperation with individual vampires who don't seem like a threat. It's probably similar with being friends with a known drug addict who is currently clean and sober, but whom you suspect is going to go off the wagon again. If you are a vampire ST looking to include werewolves in a Vampire chronicle, this is probably what I'd use, not someone belonging to a tribe.

    You may also have things like werewolves making promises to certain individuals before they realize what they are (or before they become vampires) and then become trapped by those promises because of their interpretations of Honor Renown, or because their pack or tribal totems require them to act with exceedingly high standards. If a werewolf pack under Falcon made certain promises or oaths to someone later revealed to be a vampire, it doesn't mean those promises no longer applies. It means the werewolf has a very thorny problem in how they balance the competing interests and principles of the Litany.

    Individual vampires might also have various Merits that hide their nature from werewolves, either by making them seem more human or by making them seem more like a werewolf.

                I think the biggest obstacle here is vampires being competitive and moderately paranoid in general. It's not to say that they can't interact with other splats, but they'd have to have a good reason. A shared crisis creating a regionally unusual alliance is definitely possible. That said, of the individual crossovers:

                Werewolves would likely need to see vampires as a lesser threat targeted at a greater evil, and even then that's not guaranteed. One of the defining features of the Garou is a past sense of superiority leading to problems in the present and it's all too easy for them to slip back into old habits. That being said, the Wyrm is very much a fundamental facet of reality. Even Wraiths would arguably ping as of the Wyrm because of their deathly nature giving them a strong affinity for decay. Younger werewolves would likely be more polarized to the idea, some would think Detect Wyrm = Detect Evil because they're new to the situation while others would think it's just sensing natural decay when it's somewhere between the two instead.

                Mages would need some kind of precaution for dealing with vampires, but more than that they'd need something that vampires could offer them that would be worth the risk of an alliance. Innate abilities not subject to paradox would definitely be helpful if facing a wider threat, and information from the past would be extremely valuable.

                Changeling ostensibly have the second greatest potential for overlap, especially in 20th. With regards to vampires: Unseelie Fae already have some familiarity with the nasty side of reality as a necessary part of it, and your earlier observation that vampires should be aware of the Dreaming does have some merit. Sharing research among vampire mystics can be difficult between age torpor and the difficulty in moving between cities, but even that much should offer some insight. Banality in 20th is self-repression rather than stasis per se, so that would allow a greater degree of potential overlap; plus the politics of kithain and vampires are largely self-contained so mutual power plays wouldn't necessarily screw things up too much for the other.

                Changelings and mages could get along, depending, especially with the traditions or a disparate craft; they'd likely avoid the Technocracy like hell though. If changelings interacted with any Technocrats, it would likely be Void Engineers due to their occasional break with orthodoxy to ally with other types of beings and lack of programming.

                Changelings and werewolves are already established to have some overlap via Fianna if I recall, other fera with a mystical bent could absolutely work too.

                Mages and werewolves will have some issues because both have a decent potential for egotistical behavior, but neither are innately offensive to the other save for perhaps the Technocrats being agents of Weaver corruption at best and Wyrm corruption at worst.

                Standard caveat: I could be entirely blowing hot air on this discussion, but there's my two cents.

                        I don’t think that there is a single easy answer. It depends on what kind of crossover you’re doing and what sorts of activities or themes take precedence in your crossover. It is primarily a vampire game where, for some reason, a single werewolf becomes enmeshed in vampire politics? Is it a mage focused game where the mages gain a vampire and werewolf ally in their fight against a local Nephandi? What you’re doing with the game is going to determine what setting (or thematic) elements need to be changed if any.

                        I don’t think that you can have a crossover without undermining some core themes of at least one of the games involved. If werewolves get help from a local vampire to take down the nearby Pentex factory, there isn’t going to be much focus on Clan intrigue or declining humanity. If a werewolf is hired by the prince for muscle in a war against the Sabbat, then saving the planet and the spiritual battle against the Wyrm isn’t going to receive much focus either.

                        I don’t know if a crossover can ever avoid those thematic conflicts even if there is a good excuse for the different creature types to get along and the rules were perfectly balanced.

                        If it’s just an anything goes crossover with a Salubri, Sidhe, Glass Walker, Risen, Son of Ether, Mokole, Juicer, and Glitterboy - then theme and setting doesn’t really matter because you’ve kinda abandoned those concepts anyway for wacky adventure.

                            I don't think the themes are that hard to juggle with a big caveat: it can't stretch on too long. One story that's about groups working together towards some goal? You can design that so themes of each line stay relevant. Once you start going for longer though, it either gets forced (because the tricks to keep themes around get redundant fast), or biased towards one set of themes over others. You can try to do some more vampire themed stuff in one story, and then some werewolf themed stuff in the next, and try to share spotlight time as a form of balancing the themes, but it still suffers over time. One big factor is going to be XP, because as characters advance in power, they're expected to advance in their given societies (and that's very necessary for some splats), which will have more drag towards PCs being less crossover friendly even if their personalities and inclinations haven't radically shifted.

                                    It's probably a feature of my nature as a theoretical fan-boy rather than an experienced player, but a lot of my focus tends to be on the bigger "metaphysical" issues of crossovers in the WoD. I was recently re-reading an old Splat-vs-Splat thread for giggles and it got my mind on the fundamental conflicts between the metaphysical frameworks of Werewolf and Mage, to the point where I kind of wanted to throw up a topic discussing them. This thread seems to be focused a bit more on crunch, but crunch and fluff are interconnected to some extent, so I feel like it's still on topic.

                                    It's entirely possible that I am missing some things, especially since I'm just throwing ideas out there mostly without a bunch of fact-checking, so I actively invite corrections and feedback.

                                    But, broadly speaking, it kind of feels like the oWoD throws up between three to five "metaphysical frameworks" which are largely all-encompassing:

                                    1) The "Abrahamic" perspective of VtM/DtF

                                    2) The "Spiritual Hierarchy" (Gaia + Triat) of WtA

                                    3) The "Consensual Reality" of MtA

                                    4) The "Celestial Bureacracy" of the "Middle Kingdom," which manages to blend some aspects of #1 and #2 while also bringing its own baggage to the table

                                    5) The "Egyptian Mythos" (Osiris, Horus, Set, etc.), like #4 in being a viewpoint that blends other pre-existing concepts together while also having its own ideas to contribute

                                    There are plenty of other perspectives; I'm pretty much leaving the basic frameworks of Wraith and Changeling out, for example, because they seem like they are capable of fitting into others without creating fundamental contradictions, paradoxes, or disruption of core themes. But trying to resolve the conflicting lore between these "major" perspectives is often part of the struggle of crossover games. As Heavy Arms noted above, it's less of a problem if crossovers are only happening in the short term. But if your crossover games goes on for a while, you need to know what metaphysical framework you are going with in order to decide the best way to handle certain clashes, at least when the "big" questions come up, like when the various "Plot Device" figures in various game-lines might potentially come into conflict.

                                    The best answers to those questions will ultimately depend on the needs of your players. But as someone who is invested in big WoD crossovers, I'm always interested in trying to find the "golden mean" that does the best job of giving each game-line its due while ultimately creating a comprehensive framework that can guide you on how to fit all the various game-lines together.

                                    While I do try to do my best to balance things, I do feel like my preferred solution ultimately comes out a bit Mage-centric. Not in the sense that Mages are the most powerful things in existence, but rather in the idea that there is no Sacred Truth that makes any given being or perspective more valid or desirable beyond the purely subjective opinion of the observer. So, for example, Gaia is very big, and Gaia's continued survival is of the utmost importance for anyone who isn't a Wyrm spirit that is willing to roll the dice on finding a better option somewhere in the chaos of the Deep Umbra. But Gaia is ultimately the spirit of This World, not All Nature In Existence. To the extent that Gaia and the Triat are fundamental aspects of the universe, they are like gravity, not morality.

                                    I don't know if it's impossible to at least mostly protect the themes of each game-line in a crossover-friendly setting. But I do think that something which perfectly balances the needs and perspectives of each game-line is pretty close to a Holy Grail. I like my solution for the most part, though I haven't settled all the answers for myself by any means, but I can see where it would probably not appeal to other folks who want something very different from their crossover games.

                                                            If you accept this proposition it de-values W:tA so heavily that it might as well just throw it to the side. It's about actually caring and unironically feeling it. Cutting and running is not part of the plan, or any plan; there's nowhere to run, and you're going to be making your stand here.

                                                            I remember someone describing the idea of abandoning Gaia like that as a very "Elon Musk" maneuver, and I always really enjoyed that framing because it felt like it perfectly sums it up. Both in the sense of how it involves a massive denial or abandonment of any moral obligation or responsibility to the Earth or the majority of people who are going to be left to die, and in how desperate and dangerous such a plan ultimately is. But if it's the only option realistically available? I think that creates an interesting tension to explore, the kind of story where the right answer will at least somewhat depend on the opinions of the PCs.
                                                            As noted, it was never available. That's completely out of theme, and you'd have to hack out an entirely new game to cover it being a possible route, rather than what happened in canon, where all the alternate spirit-questing Wagnerians came back and said "No other Gaias, fam," while meanwhile a bunch of Nuwisha returned to Earth heavily wounded and near-death croaking out warnings of only death waiting in the furthest reaches of the Umbra before expiring.

                                                            You can run, but you'll only die tired, because after the Midnight Shadows (see Apocalypse) are done with the coyotes, they're going to sweep back for you.

                                                            I almost forgot, but a related idea that my brain has gotten onto is the relationship between Gaia and the Triat in the "spiritual hierarchy." As noted above, Gaia's particular nature is left purposefully vague, and even whether or not she is equal to, or outranks even, the Triat is left ambiguous. Part of my interest in diminishing Gaia's importance probably partly comes from my personal canon leaning towards the idea that the Triat are the Bigger Fish in that scenario. But this conversation has gotten me considering alternatives. Initially, the fact that the Shenti are found out in the Deep Umbra inclined me to see the forces of the Triat as operating on a scale bigger than the Universe of the setting, whereas Gaia would be, at most, that one Universe. But I have started to ponder the idea that the Shenti may still be loosely "attached" or "connected" to this reality, somehow, and that perhaps different balances of spiritual forces might be found in a different Universe.
                                                            The Shenti aren't anywhere nearly distant enough to qualify as other universes, at least not in W:tA, and especially not by the time the Apocalypse is revving up and Malfeas moves from the Deep Umbra to the Near Umbra (which is also where Flux is; I'm not sure where the hell Autochthonia is supposed to be in W:tA, it doesn't get mentioned very often, but if it's in the Aetherial opposite Earth, then it, too, is in the Near Umbra and not the Deep Umbra). The Mage metaphysical trinity is also not the same thing as the Triat, because the MT doesn't actively do much, but the Triat surely does act, with each Supernal of it acting to further their ends, possibly except for the Wyld, which is frequently characterized as just there, doing its thing to the best of its abilities regardless of its surroundings.
