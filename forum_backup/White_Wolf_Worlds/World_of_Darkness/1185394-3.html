    Some examples:

    How old are they?. Were they founded in the Romanticism or they're even older?, It's there an example of this Tradition existing before Neville just like the Order of Reason existed before the Technocracy or it's just mentioned that they did?. All this "we've always been here" needs to be fleshed out if you don't want it to sound as a brag so empty as when ItX claims to have invented fire.
    As I read it, there wasn't a pre-Hollower organization, but there were individuals and smaller groups with the lookout the Hollow Ones see as their predeccessors. So yes, the Tradition itself, technically is around since the 1800's, but they didn't came from nowhere, or got founded just on the zeitgeist of that era.

    All the Traditions (including the Holowers) have SHOWN that they recruit from "the street", even the lofty OoH (arguably the least "street level" of them all) hasn't been above recruiting from prisons and educating in public schools
    But they're doing in wildly differing scales.

    - why would the Holowers be more inclined to do this?. Why a Tradition so inclined to the art of the romantic period, intellectualism, and costly subculture fashion would be more inclined to recruit people that haven't the resources to indulge in these stuff rather than those Awakened from the social classes more known to actively pursue those activities AND philosophy (cultured middle class) .
    Because subcultures and countercultures, including the romantics, weren't really middle class in the past, in the sense of lifestyle and monetary resources. They were instead the rejection of it, frequently.

    It's rather a shame, and not at all surprising, how consumerism steadily eated up the more succesfull subcultures, but they weren't always like that. Goth, for example just recently became (in the eyes of some circles, at least) about pricey designer talored stuff. at the beginning, it was mostly DIY and very, very cheap concerts.

    What I want to say, is that the Hollow Ones are close to the subcultures containing the disgruntled youth (which are prone to getting into bad places), the archetypical "starving artist" and the intellectual with lots of thought and knowledge, but no money. Also, they were/became the outcasts of society, even Mage society, either by choice or circumstances, so they naturally have lot of interactions with other outcasts and they tend to care about them.

    Hollow Ones are, perhaps, more elitist than the OoH: It's plainly stated that Hollow ones don't like to teach people, they don't want "every" person to be Awakened (they explicitly mention the guy serving beer as being 'unworthy' - like banal jobs are beneath them) and demand the newly Awakened to work hard and earn their truth.
    Again, that's not what I read. They don't want to indoctrinate you into a credo and hold your hand during your journey. They want you to find your own way, your own truths, they aren't interested to show you their truths. But they are willing to teach what they know, they just don't teach it as other Traditions do, spoon-feeding a paradigm. They are more likely to challenge you, to show you things, to encourage you to come up with your own ideas. But they do teach.

    Also, yeah, they don't want everyone to awaken. If you have to force an awakening onto you, instead of you finding your own path, then it's basically worthless. That doesn't mean that they won't help or nudge someone who they see as possible to awaken.

    Also yeah, the stereotypical wageslave living the most mundane of lives, showing no interest whatsoever in anything more is not their concern. It's not about banal jobs, but banal people, being contended to being banal.

    Why would they be so close to people with these mundane jobs and lives that they mook?, why would they be interested in the painful but all too repetitive poor's man life?
    Again, it's not about the jobs, it's about the personalities. Hollowers are willing to work as bartenders, shopkeepers, or any menial job. It's not about that. It's about finding the hidden gem among the rubble, because a lot of the times very interesting people are doing those jobs too, precisely because they didn't fit into the mainstream middle-class mold, but have to pay the rents and food somehow.

    Akashicks build dojos to teach street kids how to defend themselves, Dreamspeakers (and most Verbena) die for their comunities, Choristers are all about helping the poor, Euthanatos are nearly always shown hunting criminals and solving problems for the common people. Yet Hollow Ones are the "outcast Tradition whose members have come from the street" - Yeah, sure they are. Why it's their philosophy more "down to earth" than your average Tradition? .

Some of them might work at a local homeless shetlter, some might at a drog ambulance. Some might organize cityscape defenders (sorry, I don't know the right expression, people who are dedicated to preserve the city). Some might find their place at a local library, doing programs for the poor kids and adults in the neighborhoods. It is, as everything about them, varried. Even the clubs, concerts an subcultural scenes they are doing, those are safe places (more-or-less) for troubled youth to be, to find meaning in life and friends. The Penny Dreadful novel exactly started by kicking out a Nosferatu hard from the club and declaring in the face of the Prince that it is off-limits as a feeding ground, but the vamps could come here to socialize, if they're doing it friendly.

They never really seem more down to earth to me, on the contrary, they're always searching supernatural stuff, making poetry, and going to very goth like parties - that's their thing, I don't get where this "we have more connections with the street than you" comes from.

The other Traditions were much more caught up with their war with the Technocracy, they are much more detached from "the streets", much more living in their own bubble. The Hollowers aren't "down to earth" in the sense of being necessarily the most banal and mundane (on the contrary), they are indeed seeking out supernatural stuff frequently and rubbing shoulders with other sups. They are "down-to-Earth" in the sense of being less prone for getting detached from it than the others, living mostly in chantries and Horizon Realms and interacting mostly with mages and spending their lives with warring over reality. Admittedly, the Avatar Storm and Revised changed this and closed the gap considerably, but even then, the Tadbook said that it was the least traumatic for them.

                                            For me, the greatest strength of the Disparate Alliance write-up is the frank admission that it's nowhere close to being “ready for prime time” as things stand, and it may never be ready for prime time. Right now, the Alliance is more of an ideal that the Disparates are working toward (in some cases reluctantly), and the main thing holding it together to the extent that it is holding together is a mutual enemy. And no one has figured out how to change that. So for now at least, the Alliance isn't really a thing even in canon.

                                            Ironically, the Traditions know an answer to the Disparates' conundrum, with their more or less ubiquitous inclusion of some version of “We Are All God(s) in Disguise” (Book of Secrets p.196) in their teachings providing the glue that holds them together. Most Disparates don't really have that, though exceptions exist: for instance, the Batini helped found the Traditions' shared worldview as part of their drive for unity.

                                            Which brings us back to the Hollow Ones. To me, the Hollow Ones have taken the Traditions' shared metaphysics of magick to its logical conclusion. Philosophically, they have far more in common with the Traditions than they do with the Disparates. So the only reason I can see for them to join the Disparate Alliance is if a wedge gets driven between them and the Traditions. Which, in all fairness, is where the fall of Horizon comes in.

                                            That said, in my take on the setting, the Hollow Ones are still on reasonably good terms with the Traditions; it's just that they're also taking on the side project of supporting a mutual defense pact among the Crafts.

                                                The second rote. My personal favorite. Extraordinarily weird, perfect for my vision of the Hollowers. Once again, mechanical critique is welcome.

                                                Elvis Lives! (Prime 2/Life 5/Mind 5)

                                                Particularly silly, even by Hollower standards, this rote allows the individual, through "happenstance", to encounter a flesh-and-blood being claiming to the one and only Elvis Presley, famed rock star and subject of no small number of conspiracy theories post-mortem. This particular "Elvis" is, of course, made to behave precisely as the Hollower responsible for the effect expects them to, complete with memory and abilities congruent with the the given explanation for his continued existence.

                                                Given the high level of magickal power required to pull off this rote, it is incredibly rare. Still, the fact that it exists at all leads one to suspect that there are likely multiple "Kings of Rock 'n Roll" running around at the moment. One wonders what came first, this rote or the numerous post-mortem tabloid sightings of the famed celebrity that seemingly inspired it.

                                                System: This rote follows standard procedure for the creation of a fully formed conscious creature through the use of Prime. The Elvis manifests in the nearest unobserved area in which he will fit. In addition, the addition of other Spheres may be used in order to give Elvis abilities consistent with the given backstory, i.e. psionic talents, cybernetic implants, spiritual abilities, superhuman physical attributes, etc. (Assuming a greater success threshold, of course.)
