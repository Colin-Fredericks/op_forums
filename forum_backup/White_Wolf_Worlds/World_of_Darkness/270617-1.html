                            Originally posted by Phaolan View Post

                            3. I'd like to see a new version of Bygone Bestiary with a proper 'toolbox' ethic all the way throughout. I mean, there's some wonderful 'creature building' work in the original, but the chapter of monsters (the one that feels too close to a Monster Manual for my taste) doesn't offer quite enough in terms of possibilities. Further, the creation process in Bygone Bestiary feels very mechanical (again, a little too D&D 'roll D100 on table of Monster Tail Features' for me), but I dunno if there's much of a way around that. Finally, I didn't like the conceit of these things as playable player characters; this is MUCH more of a 'mileage varies' sort of thing that any of the above, though, as some of us actually DO want that in the book. I'm just not one of them.

                            ...All of the above notwithstanding, I think that Bygone Bestiary is a wonderful addition to the World of Darkness, and I'm glad to have it. That's something I cannot say about every book in my collection.
                            THIS!


                            I think you've hit the nail on the head there Phaolan. We should remember that even though it was a bit of Mage, Changeling and Sorcerer supplement, it was of the World of Darkness Line. The books of that line suffered from not fitting neatly into any single system so sat in between many of them like inadvertent Crossover books. As such they seemed a bit confused in the organization and really specific in some aspects like talking about Mage Familiars, while being really Vague in others. (Water Zombies?... Really?)

                            Much like your suggestion, I would really like to see a newer Bygone Bestiary handled more like a toolbox for porting creatures from either one game into another or from outside sources into your World of Darkness game, but even provide some guidelines on how to use these new creatures in every kind of game. It needs to be universal, not just for games with spirits or access to the Umbra. Bygones need their own little known back door to the physical world of reality. They need their own identity in the WoD not just as extras to come across or defeat if one is in the Umbra. Something not completely spirit and not quite classifiable. What I keep using as a resource instead of the Bygone Bestiary is the First Edition Storyteller's guide for Werewolf: the Apocalypse.

                            The first ST guide had sections with all kinds of fantastic ideas and creatures. From the Crazy Cat Lady, to the Jersey Devil. From Gaia's own version of Formori; the Kami, to the Wyld's own manic creations that really deserve attention as well. I use the closest template for the creature I'm using from Bygone Bestiary or from something from another system and use the ST guide for werewolf with some tweaks in order to use Bygone in my games. The Bestiary book itself does have some cool ideas and uses, but it really does deserve an update to the 20th anniversary world.

                                        Using the term "Monster Manual" is apt to get a lot of perjoritive answers, even though, essentially that's EXACTLY what the Bygone Bestiary was. Including the "how to make more of the same" and "how to use these critters in your game" sections.

                                        Asking for more than the mostly random scattering and sometimes nonsensical templates in the backs of many many scattered books, doesn't sound like a bad request. Scion, which can't ever be accused of having a decent Bestiary (see "nonsensical" above, esp from the standpoint of them being decent antagonists for the characters), had a very concise set up of templates you could run as is for various "levels" of mortal (perfectly ordinary person, thuggish type, trained combatant, expert combatant) that was tremendously useful. It wasn't too hard to extrapolate them into similar vectors for improved social and improved mental normals as well. (Not that mortals are usually very important in Scion games and certainly not past a certain, very early point in the game.)

                                        If you don't have a decent place to get non-character-type opponents, you end up with a game where the characters are going to face mostly other critters of their particular type. Which is perfectly fine if you want that kind of game. It works well for a mostly social in-fighting vampire game. Most every opponent is going to be another vampire, even if it's a combat situation (combat is effectively "aggressive diplomacy" in such a setting). It works less well if you want to play a Mage game with spirits, space aliens, mythical creatures, angelic beings, the talking and dancing color blue, werewolves, etc.

                                        So I don't see why not to just have a Bestiary and other related books that give you things to interact with. Including some examples of Rank 2 Garou so you can run one on the fly if needed, either as is or with a few tweaks. Sometimes NPCs just happen. Ex. sometimes talking turns into fisticuffs and you don't have those stats ready for the character you though the PCs would just be talking to.

                                                        I think there's not only a desire for something like this for each and every game, but a need for it. There is a reason why pretty much every game system out there has one. Even Amber could have used one. Actually, Amber could have used one BADLY, and that is an example of a game that takes the whole dichotomy of "roll-playing" vs. "role-playing" to it's nth degree. (A game near and dear to my heart and one I love beyond death in a wraithly fashion, but really, there is NOTHING out there but other people just like the PCs that have stats, if that). But I do agree Mage may need it more than the others. You can have a vampire game that is only about vampires and the game even encourages that to a degree, but it's not void of vampires vs. something other than vampires. I can see the relative merits of having them for various games as well. Werewolf seems like it would be a hair's breath behind Mage. A wraith game that wasn't Skinlands adjacent might have uses for it (critters of the Tempest, some specters stated out, etc.) And so on.

                                                        PCs are massively unpredictable. I've had TONS of scenes where an NPC that was supposed to be scenery/chorus has fascinated a PC to the point that I needed some idea of rudimentary die pools. I've had NPCs that are made up for a particular purpose with just a set of rudimentary die pools that suddenly needed a much more complete write up (PCs that set out to go fight and then decide to talk, make friends, ask for services from what were planned as antagonists. There are more than a few times where scenery has made the jump all the way to full-blown interactive PC-level character at the drop of a hat. It's not like I haven't, can't and even am not currently winging that sort of thing, but, when running Scion recently, I discovered myself using those mortal templates a lot and I never have used the stat blocks provided in the various core and ancillary books in a similar fashion. In fact, every time I venture to use them, even roughly, they strike me as very unlikely. I would usually turn to someone like you, Von Lyt, to work through the analysis, but the stats they give for the various neonate/ancillae/elder, apprentice/adept/master, etc. just don't jibe at all with the relative power of PCs at those levels. And not in a "PCs are awesomer than mere NPCs" (if that's how you feel.... I can see it both ways, they are the protagonists, but that doesn't mean they HAVE to be Mr/Mrs. Special). They usually overpower PCs or just look really weird and not at all like what you would expect a character with # XP to look like.
