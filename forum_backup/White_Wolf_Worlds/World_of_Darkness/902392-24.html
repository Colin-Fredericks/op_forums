    497) Phantom Tick Tock [Flaw]: A not uncommon backlash afflicting users of Time magick, this Flaw first appeared alongside the mass proliferation of clockwork technology. Many technomancers in the Order of Reason documented experiencing this phenomenon, but superiors were quick to chalk it up to faulty mechanisms, or the character "having clocks on the brain", and such incidences were papered over in official records. These days, Etherites committed to clockwork technology are the most commonly afflicted. The Flaw causes a persistent ticking sound to follow the mage wherever she goes. There was a period where this backlash would cause confusion and/or panic, during the age when time bombs came into vogue, and every unexplained ticking sound was cause for alarm.

    Magestuck: The Ascension

        498) Pinocchio Syndrome [Flaw]: Deception is a depressingly common use to which mages put their True Magick. Whether it's illusions, distractions, disguises, forgeries, lies, or altering a person's memory, all are employed for the purposes of passing falsehood off as truth. With this Flaw, Paradox twists the mage's subterfuge against them, so lies are as plain as the nose on they face. Literally. Named for the eponymous marionette-turned-boy, Pinocchio Syndrome causes the mage's nose to lengthen slightly with each act of deception. Every lie - sometimes even one by omission - makes the nose longer. Some mages have experienced the Flaw taken further, with the increasingly long schnozz taking on wooden qualities, as if they were Pinocchio himself.

            499) The Ebony Tree [Spirit]: Hate, like a cancer, grows. When a mage of a natural bent (Verbana, Dreamspeaker, Bata'a, etc) fosters hatred or evil within themselves, an Ebony Tree can bloom. Just as the mage harbors a darkness in their hearts, so too will a foul vegetable be planted in the heart of their garden or grove. A pitch-black sapling, watered by the mage's grudge and sins until it grows into a large timber. It doesn't need to be in a natural place, either, though it is often most devastating there. When the mage has no growing place of their own, the Ebony Tree can sprout in a wooded area nearby, or even burst through the foundations of their home and take root inside. An Ebony Tree needs no sunlight, good soil, or water. The mage's hollowed soul provides the only watering vessel it requires.

            The Ebony Tree is wholly black - from the bark to the leaves to the roots to the pitch that flows like sap in its body. Its roots burrow deep in the ground, and suck the life from the land. Growing things wither and perish around it, and even fauna (including humans) grow ill and enervated in its presence. The Paradox that fuels the Ebony Tree will even, over time, warp the Reality Zone around it, even if it grows in the heart of the mage's Sanctum. Magick that promotes health and vitality grows more difficult, whereas magick that wounds, sickens, and decays grows easier.

            As the mage accrues Paradox, backlashes go towards promoting the Ebony Tree's growth, to the exclusion of all other Paradox effects. The Ebony Tree is a true parasite. When absorbed Paradox points (including those used in the initial backlash that created it) exceed 15 to 20, the Ebony Tree has become mature. A mature Ebony Tree can attack creatures in its presence with its branches, or from below with burrowing roots that wrap around the feet and restrict movement. Mature Ebony Trees also begin to bear fruit; blackened and misshapen bodies that appear to bear grotesque faces. They can sometimes be heard whispering or screaming, and when ripe fall to the ground and turn into ugly Paradox spirits of their own. Squat, deformed little goblins, who act as defenders and agents of the Ebony Tree. Picked fruits, meanwhile, remain inert, but entirely toxic to consume. Some Nephandi, however, know how to derive foul reagents from such fruit, for their abominable concoctions. Such Nephandi scour the world for signs of Ebony Trees, or seek to corrupt mages into making them.

            A truly old and established Ebony Tree begins to warp the Gauntlet around it, until its desecrated grove sinks into a Paradox Realm of its own. Many Verbana travel Europe and the corresponding Umbra, seeking the lairs of bygone witches that were swallowed by their own hatred. The persistent byproducts of the animosity born from the Burning Times, and the early days of the conflict between Tradition and Order of Reason mages.

                    501) The Weightless [Spirit]: One manifestation of many taking after extraterrestrial myths, the Weightless inflict themselves most often on Void Engineers and Etherites. Any mage can attract them, though, if their magick manipulates gravity. The Weightless take the form of blue, hairless humanoids with gangly limbs. Their heads are bald and bubbled, like a jellyfish, and their unblinking black eyes stare out above round, toothy maws. Their fingers are tipped with suction cups, allowing them to cling to and crawl over walls and ceilings. This is good, as the Weightless are, true to their name, entirely unaffected by gravity. They float freely, and navigate their environment by clinging to surfaces, or by pushing off and sailing around under their momentum. This zero-gravity activity makes them rare threats out of doors, but potent foes in enclosed spaces. True three-dimensional thinkers, the Weightless come at mages at inconvenient angles, and their soft bodies allow them to hide in tight spaces or travel by paths otherwise off limits to man. Their primary means of attack are with grappling and biting, though they sometimes appear bearing hand-held energy weapons. These weapons can double as methods of travel, as they can fire strong bursts to propel themselves around. Many will float towards their foes, only to discharge their energy weapons, sending them sailing out of harm's way again.

                    Even when killed, their bodies remain unmoored by gravity, making their cadavers and blood sought-after Tass.

                            503) Mind Your Own Mind [ Paradox Flaw | Mind, Prime and Mind, Mind and Correspondence ]

                            This Paradox Flaw affects Mages who treat their knowledge of Mind Magick merely as a capability that allows them to view, check, influence and change the minds of other persons as they please. It does not matter whether the Mage is arrogant, lightly humorous, or coldly clinical about this - what contributes towards and causes this Paradox Flaw is the Mage's choice in her or his decisions and actions to treat Mind Magick exclusively as a tool that allows her or him to know the thoughts and influence the minds of other persons ; to the point of practically not considering at all how a Mind Magick Spells can affect the Mage's own thoughts and feelings.

                            The affected Mage cannot cast Spells and Rotes involving Mind Magick on other persons, at all. If she attempts to do so, her attempt fizzles and fails as she is suddenly beset by intense feelings and ruminations such as : dumb amazement at the self-reflecting aspect of thinking, paranoid terror of mishandling a part of her feelings, or hopeless, deep shame at not knowing how exactly she will be feeling during tomorrow. For the same reason the affected Mage cannot participate in any group castings involving Mind Magick that would be cast on persons other then the affected Mage.
                            Those sudden feelings, realizations and ruminations last between tens of seconds and around four minutes ; as part of those mind states, during these periods of time the Mage is at the very least slightly confused, and very inclined to not pay much attention to surroundings and other persons ( unless something very important or needing immediate attention is happening ) .

                            The affected Mage can cast Spells ( both alone and as part of a group ) of Mind Magick if she or he is the target.
                            This Paradox Flaw does not affect actions involving Mind Magick other than casting Spells, such as research or tutoring other persons.

                                504) Dashed Wishes [ Paradox Flaw | Matter and Prime, Prime, Correspondence, Correspondence and Matter ]

                                This Paradox Flaw affects Mages who let arrogance, pride, hesitation, and laziness - among other factors - get in the way of paying attention to their best friends, lovers, and strangers alike. Another factor that can cause and contribute towards this Paradox Flaw is a Mage not following her or his previous intentions, abandoning plans, and not acting according to her or his given word.

                                The Mage's voice becomes a fading whisper at most when speaking about items that are important to her, and also when speaking about items she would like to acquire in the future. The affected Mage does clearly hear what she is saying, but she is alone in this. Other persons don't hear anything said by the Mage in regards to these topics ; even if they are aware of this condition of the affected Mage and try to hear what she is saying.

                                Mundane recording devices won't record the Mage's words said in regard to these topics.

                                    505) Nope, Still High [Quiet]: There's a reason that the Cult of Ecstasy cautions its members to seek, always, to transcend mind-expanding drugs. Aside from the dangers of addiction, long-term health problems, or simple complacency, drugs present another potential obstacle. That being the inability to "come down" again. Or, at least, the inability to discern mundane reality from the mind-expanded drug reality.

                                    With this Quiet, the mage comes down from their high in the physical sense, as normal, but thinks overwise. To them, they remain in the hallucinatory haze created by their drug of choice. They gain no magical or mechanical benefits from this; they are merely delusional, and/or suffering from persistent (yet less helpful) hallucinations. At first, it is merely an impression that things are not as they seem, and the occasional sensational quirk (a blur, a distorted sound, a phantom touch, etc.). As the Quiet grows, their "false high" persists longer and longer after normal chemical modification has elapsed, and the disbelief and hallucinations intensify. The mage begins to doubt their reality at all times - not in the normal way for some Awakened, but in an all-encompassing manner. They glide through life, variably denying real experiences and putting great stock in purely false ones. How can they possibly take their surroundings seriously, when they are clearly tripping balls? How can they care about anyone, when a given person might not even be real?

                                    Obviously, the wisest in the Cult of Ecstasy know that nothing is, strictly speaking, "real". But even these sages understand that a certain amount of care must be taken within the bounds of humanity's shared hallucination. And that just because something is part of the Great Illusion, does not mean it isn't important. A person deep in this Quiet cannot acknowledge any reality as true or important, save the ones solely in their heads. As the Quiet tips towards Marauderdom, the hazy, drug-induced Hobgoblins of their tripped out minds are made manifest, and the world around them starts getting...wibbly. The mage is now less in control of their rejection and manipulation of reality, not more. Experienced Ecstasy Cultists see such a state as ultimately a dead end, in terms of spiritual growth. An obstacle to be overcome, if the mage is truly to shape the Great Illusion to their liking.

                                    Those that cannot - but tip over the edge - inevitably trip themselves out of the material world, as the intensity of their Marauder Quiet becomes irreconcilable to normative reality. They float - weightless - in a perpetual high, given over fully to the sea of colors and impressions, believing none of it and all of it.

                                    Magestuck: The Ascension

                                                508) Reflected Hubris [ Burn | Prime and Correspondence, Time and Correspondence, Mind and Correspondence, Life and Correspondence ]

                                                Mages who are assured of their Spells success because of their skill in Correspondence to the point that they, for example, mock the possibility of failure or the persons they target, might suffer this Burn.
                                                The affected Mage feels an icy grasp on his throat, and he is unable to breath for ten to twenty seconds, and parts of her neck and head might be chilled and paralyzed. At the same time starkingly white-yellow thin bands of energy appear and clasp around his wrists ; the bands' temperature rises over the next ten to twenty seconds, until they start burning the Mage's wrists, and the affected Mage bleeds from multiple points at his wrists. After twenty to thirty seconds from the beginning, the icy grasp, the chills and the bands around the wrists recede and dissapear.
                                                The Mage suffers bashing damage from the icy grasp on his throat, and lethal damage from the bands burning and cutting his wrists. Though very painful, this Burn is not likely to cause serious or long-term injury.

                                                    509) Haunted By Sacrifice [Spirit]: Mages that incorporate animal (or human) sacrifice into their magick will suffer this backlash. The spirit of their sacrificed victim haunts them as a ghostly Paradox Spirit. They are usually harmless, but they are provided a voice with which to remind the mage of the blood shed by her actions. Such a spirit will linger for a while, depending on the severity of the backlash, periodically reminding the mage of her sins. A common variant involves the spirit of an animal (or human) killed as a result of the mage's negligence when using magick. The result, however, is the same.

                                                        510) Elemental Discharge [ Paradox Flaw | Prime and Matter, Prime and Forces, Time and Matter, Time and Forces ]

                                                        This Paradox Flaw affects Mages who make mistakes or include in their castings contradictions in regards to the elements or other concepts they associate with Forces and Matter Magicks, while casting Conjunctional Spells. A Mage might associate lightning with movement, motion, and intent ; when she includes Prime while inherently associating it with stability and neutrality, she might already be including a contradiction into her casting. Mistakes can be as simple as a Mage using inks of one or two colors for working with Matter Magick, while using a mixture of tens of different inks as her Foci for Time Magick.

                                                        The affected Mage is haunted by very slight manifestations related to the element(s) which was involved in the Backlash that caused this Paradox Flaw. Pages of a book she reads might become singed, worn down by specks of dirt and ground appearing between the pages, or wet. Many peebles or stones might appear in the Mage's clothing or among her personal belongings. An item vulnerable to heat might become damaged, and as the Mage grasps she notices it's so hot as if it was placed in a fire. After picking up items the Mage might see that there is a patch of water at the place where an item was. The Mage gets no indication when or in regards to what those manifestations will happen.

                                                            511) Late for the Party [ Paradox Flaw ]

                                                            The affected Mage acquires several separate tendencies that combine to just make her unable to put things together to arrive on time for any group meetings she has to travel to. ( including serious councils, short exchanges of information, unfriendly stand-offs , hostile confrontations, or reunions ) . She will adopt a light and carefree attitude to the time she has left and the time she needs for the travel, and any tasks she needs to do before going out ; every task taking five to twenty minutes to complete is considered to take "at 5 minutes" , time spent walking is not added to the travel time while planning, routine actions like packing up or tidying a room seem like the take practically no time at all. This all will result in her most likely going out five to thirty minutes after the planned time. She will misplace, lose ( or think she might have lost items, and spend time checking if this is the case ), or forget about essential items - for example keys, notes, wallet, identification. She will also adopt a tendency to space out and go into introspective ruminations prior to group meetings ; for example making plans for the next few hours or the next few days after the meeting, going over the most recent events in her life, or thinking about the meeting.
                                                            In addition to all of this, the affected Mage develops a slight - at first - tendency to be carefree and dismissive about her being late to group meetings - "Seems I'm late for the party... or am I ? " .

                                                            The affected Mage can overcome this Paradox Flaw with personal effort or with the help of other persons ; but it will not be easy or obvious for the Mage to change her thinking and how she will act.
