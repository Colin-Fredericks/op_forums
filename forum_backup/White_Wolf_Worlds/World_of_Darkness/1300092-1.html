        House Rules:
        Seekings - No challenge aspect, always succeed. Three options:
        Leg work - player helps me do something as an ST like map an area or come up with a cabal of NPCs
        Written - player just writes out their seeking
        Group - Sometimes, we'll have part of a play session be a seeking where playing characters take on an aspect of the character's past

        Botches - Botches are always complications. They make things worse but never result directly in just damage or failure.

        Buying spheres is done by halfs - first half is rote access, second half is full access. So someone can pay 14 points towards their affinity sphere of Matter to get a fourth dot (28 XP). The character can use matter 4 rotes from tomes and from mentors and teachers at this level. Upon paying the remaining 14 points, the character gets full access.

        XP Costs - I knock a few points off of arete or generally make grimoires available.

        I'm generally loose on having characters who work together a lot work together to make effects.

        Avatars - to me, Avatars are a part of the Mage but still have their own agendas and powers of observation. Characters with high avatars may have to make a willpower roll to do something against their avatar's nature. On the plus side, the avatar can do very minor magickal effects a little below one dot effects. An avatar can indicate that something may be magical but it's on the character to do investigation from there. They also serve a kind of magickal spidersense and allow the character to do counter magick in many cases.

        High Umbra - The high umbra geography is something like:
        The City - Contains all fallen epiphanies regarding human activity like the tavern of the four winds, and the fortress of government. Outside of this, it's more or less the Overworld from Scion. The forks of the river of language have areas between them dominated by a spire each containing the pantheon court of some faith or another court . Spires access all epiphanies related to that world view.

                                Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post
                                - EXP gets split into Mundane/Arcane EXP. Mundane buys Abilities, Attributes, Willpower, Mental/Physical/Social Merits, backgrounds that Sleepers can have. Arcane buys Arete, Spheres, Supernatural Merits, and backgrounds like Totem and Sanctum.

                                The beauty of this is balanced growth is ensured. If you give 2 M and 1 A each session, for example, people develop the Abilities of their mage's practice over time, investing in the WAY their mage does magick, not just in dots in Spheres. People feel empowered to invest large amounts of EXP into things like Etiquette, Expression, High Ritual, Finance and Politics, because they know that they're not "falling behind" in terms of raw magickal power. They know that everyone's got the exact same amount of EXP to put into spheres/arete, so they don't have to sweat the details or worry about the risks of not min-maxing in a competitive game with opposing PCs, for example. I'm seeing the PCs just... flourish, basically, under this system, with loving care going into little details like how they train their abilities and what they plan to do in the future, and even people spending points on frivolous things that don't feed into their magick directly. Plus Attributes and Willpower are so expensive in terms of EXP that I can't imagine PCs investing much in them otherwise, when they can get so much more bang for their buck with magickal stuff.

                                Essentially the reason I love this change is that it makes sure that magick is more than what spheres you have, and what arete you've reached. It helps direct the attention of the PCs towards how their abilities feed into their magick, and ensures that the abilities that form their practice get some love. And on top of all of that it means you can give loads of EXP without being scared of the repercussions, and without having to carefully curate what your PCs purchase. Mundane EXP is safe (short of ludicrous amounts), Arcane EXP is potentially dangerous if you give too much, so you can keep that in mind when balancing what you give.
                                Decided I should do an update on this:

                                - a Hermetic Harry-potter-like PC realized that his character's paradigm and approach to Magick wouldn't accommodate Life very well. So he began sketching his owl familiar's wings, and the skeletons that his familiar collected. Through continued effort and investment in Art (using Mundane EXP) he reached a point where he felt much more comfortable integrating Life 1 into his character's practice (using Arcane EXP). This has continued to blossom into the PC being something of a Renaissance figure, sketching living beings and prosections (a professionally performed dissection left for people to examine) da Vinci style.

                                - the awkward artist PC decided to get into sword fighting, and has become a bit of a reluctant warrior. They're incredibly soft and gentle but they're a big guy and want to be able to protect their friends.

                                - the farm-boy PC found some orphaned baby bears and invested a HUGE amount of mundane EXP into Survival and Animal Kinship (at least 15), learning new skills that he didn't have before, just so he could look after these bears and teach them the things their mother would have. These skills are *useful* obviously but it's a great kind of character growth to see a Mage learn new proficiencies just to be kind to animals.

                                - the basement hacker PC has started learning tea ritual etiquette and costume design/repair - yes this is motivated partly by them being a huge weeb, BUT they're skills that the player would never have put on their sheet at character generation, and now she's proficient in them.

                                There are lots more small examples, but splitting the EXP has been a HUGE success. Best house rule I've ever implemented. Player characters are growing as people, not just Mages, and it's feeding into their Magick in exciting ways. And it leads to much cooler interactions with NPCs - the hacker PC just wants to learn hacking, say, so they interact only with their own computer and maybe a Virtual Adept mentor. But with Mundane Experience liberating their ability to invest, the hacker PC might now decide they should do something about their CRIPPLING social anxiety, and learn some Expression and Leadership - well that might mean interacting with someone with very different views and techniques. And before long it does, inevitably, influence their magick in some way.

                                Another advantage I didn't predict is that Arcane EXP does allow for some interesting control, so long as you control when Seekings are possible. If you aren't open to seekings for a certain number of sessions, the Arcane EXP keeps piling up. And that means a PC will end up investing in a new Sphere they've never used before, like Life or Spirit or Time. And that always comes along with them developing a new sense of their character's place in the world, which feels like... you guessed it, perfect training for getting to a Seeking! Rather than PCs just trundling along until they can go "yep it's been long enough, seeking please" they're actively seeking (heh) new ways to grow the whole time, opening up new abilities and Spheres and learning new ways of approaching problems. Which is exactly the sort of lived experience that makes sense as the trigger for further Ascension.

                                Because the PCs are so young, I've also put a soft cap on high-leveled Abilities - most of the PCs have one ability at 4, which does make sense... it's strange for a young character to have a highly qualified professional level of ability in one thing, let alone two or three. So the kid who grew up in the wilderness has Survival 4 (Woodland), the kid who was neglected and explored the city has Athletics 4 (Climbing), the kid who spends all their time hacking and posting has Computer 4 (Hacking). But because of that cap, the PCs are encouraged to invest in the Abilities that they have 0 to 2 dots in. Objectively speaking Computer 5 would be an incredible investment for a Mage whose whole paradigm depends on computers, but I'm not happy with that happening for story/authenticity reasons. So instead all that EXP can go towards random stuff like learning the basics of Ninjitsu or Funerary Rites or Whittling (yes, these are all real examples).

                                So, try using this if you're starting a new game. It's good. Trust me.

                                            I started using arcane experience and have had interesting results with the players. One thing that always bothered me at other games was the incessant need to limit the use of magic. It was as if mages were penalized for being mages, and it came to the absurdity of tables that players almost never used magic for anything, just social internacional in fear of paradox.

                                            At my table the players cannot spend experience with Arete, only arcane experience. This experience mod I took from CoD, so that in my game every time players learn something new about the supernatural they get 1 xp arcana point. Of course with certain limitations, such as it cannot be repetitive, It cant be from something they already know, It may come from effects that they didnt known possível or the usage of multiple sphere as long as they have time to undersntand, notice or study It. On nightfolk, said players can learn a lot by even focusing on a singles kind of creatures, like: if you research all cainite base disicplines you would get 1 arcane XP for each=12. This would make said player experts on a certain supernatural subject, which I love the concept of "research"

                                            It was interesting to see the players capture a vampire for interrogation and experimentation.

                                                            I run games where all the players have busy lives - sometimes a player will miss a few sessions in a row because of illness, or travels, or work problems. It's not a competition, and it's nice and relaxed. If you're completely unavailable for a month it's nice to come back and find you're still on track - you've been missing, but your character has been up to all sorts of things. So we fill in the gap and create a great story. Finn was off caring for some orphaned bears he found, that sort of thing.

                                                            And at the end of the day my players want to RP, want to succeed in character, want to find things out. They want these things as their own reward. Mechanical incentives like a cost break are just a useful way for me to recognize the effort they've put in - I can see they just enjoy playing the game and don't really need to be "paid" with EXP. I've had zero complaints on everyone getting the same EXP, since the players mostly work together regardless.

                                                            So my personal house-rule is occasional cost breaks, which I keep track of for transparency and to ensure that I remember who has had what.
