    The Arcanum (Updated)

    07-04-2025, 05:24 AM
    I seem to be very much in the minority in that I’m fond of the Arcanum. I even had a Mage character some years ago whose concept was “the first Awakened member of the Arcanum.” They’re punching above their weight class in just managing to survive the attention of the various supernatural factions in the World of Darkness. But they’re not just surviving, they’re thriving. Slowly but surely, they’re putting together an increasingly clear picture of the supernatural truth of the world. So, I can’t help but root for these underdogs who are underestimated by nearly everyone.

    However, there’s no single sourcebook for them. They’re in bits and pieces all over the place, so trying to assemble a coherent picture of them was an exercise in research (and purchasing numerous sourcebooks hoping to find even a further tidbit of information about them). This is an excerpt from my House Rules Document, which provides a unified overview of the Arcanum as I best envision them.


    The Nine Founders

    These are unchanged from canon. It’s still not known just who (or what) Benjamen Holmscroft really was. He’s almost certainly still out there somewhere, however.
    • Benjamen Holmscroft, of London
    • Jebediah Brown, of London
    • Roger Corwin, of Salisbury
    • Peter Harker, of London
    • Stewart Heath, of Manchester
    • Winthrop Murray, of Kent
    • Liam McPhee, of Dublin
    • Jonathan Kelvin, of Boston, U.S.A.
    • Massimo Linarelli, of Rome, Italy

    Structure

    In canon, their structure seems to vary a bit by which book you read. As such, after reading all of sourcebooks that deal with the Arcanum, I present this simplified organizational structure, which is (roughly) modeled after a typical university.
    • At the top is the Board of Governors…otherwise known as the White Monks. Their identity is a closely-guarded secret and they’re the ones who appoint each Chancellor. A small and secret group of dissidents who style themselves “The Red Monks” oppose them.
    • The “CEO position” is the Chancellor, who has global authority over all Chapter Houses.
    • A Director is the head of a Chapter House.
    • A Dean is the head of a College (these are divisions of interest and study of a particular subject; e.g., ghosts, vampires, magic, history, etc.).
    • A Headmaster (or Headmistress) is the head of a Colony. They’re promoted to Director if the Colony graduates to full Chapter House status.
    All of the Chapter Houses report directly to the Foundation House in the UK. Disregard all references in canon books to a “primary” Chapter House for a given geographical region. That said, when a Chapter House does communicate with the “home office,” they’re not likely to get through directly to the Chancellor unless it’s a matter of great importance. In short, the “middle managers” are all at the Foundation House so as to better coordinate with each other and the Chancellor about Arcanum business as a whole.

    Another change is that they’ve become a bit more “family friendly” in recent years. That is to say, they’ve noticed that the few kids raised “in house” by Arcanum parents have typically gone on to become accomplished members themselves. Accordingly, they now encourage prospective parents to raise their children within the organization in order to “promote from within.” There are also unofficial rewards for Arcanum couples who have children in order to further facilitate this. This is ameliorating their declining membership problem, but at the cost of making them a more insular society. [The Social Flaw: Isolated Upbringing is slowly becoming more common among Arcanum members.]

                          Originally posted by Arcanum Scholar View Post
                          [FONT=Calibri]I seem to be very much in the minority in that I’m fond of the Arcanum.
                          I think one reason why the Arcanum is not heavily used is that STs don't know what purpose it could serve in their chronicles and therefore how Arcanum characters can be used. Every time it gets mentioned, there is always a line about how this organization should be avoided because of the Masquerade, Veil, Protocols, etc. Therefore, PCs need to avoid them. It's very much like a plot using hunters, but without any physical danger. There's only so much you can do with such a plot. Of course, PCs could always choose to disregard these prohibitions and make contact, but what would be the corresponding reward versus the danger of the appropriate authorities (archons, sept elders, masters, etc.) punishing you for it? The risk/reward ratio isn't enough.

                          Contrast to the Talamasca which is the most similar organization in Anne Rice's books. Their members get involved ALL the time despite their organization (not those of vampires or witches) telling its operatives not to do so. They also tend to be very useful to the protagonists, providing all sorts of information (exposition) and helping them in ways.

                          So if you want the Arcanum to be useful in your chronicle (the PCs interact with its members), you need to come up with a way to get past this block. Have Arcanum scholars want to contact PCs (or at least interact with in certain social circles). That's fairly easy. The more difficult thing is internal considerations for your supernatural splat so that such contact is not something that will get your PC killed.

                          So instead of a blanket prohibition against contact, instead it should be something more like, "Of all the mortals/kine/flock/sleepers/etc. you may get involved with, interaction with the Arcanum may be the least problematic because 1) they already know we exist, and 2) they are sworn not to reveal it to outsiders. Interaction should still be considered dangerous, but not inherently a breach of the Masquerade/Veil/Protocols/etc. Be wary and use common sense."

                          That opens up more possibilities for interaction. Arcanum members can then become part of a character's Backgrounds like Allies or Contacts. For STs, they could make a good "neutral" scholar for whatever plot exposition is needed. Why go to the treacherous Tremere for information on the Ritual of the Bitter Rose when an NPC of the Arcanum could provide that information? Need guidance on dealing with characters of another splat? Use the Arcanum as a way for the PCs to learn that. Then at some point once the PCs owe an Arcanum scholar for past help, that character can be used to introduce new plot.

                                  Originally posted by Arcanum Scholar View Post
                                  But I like the more interaction idea. Though that does mean the Arcanum's knowledge base would grow rapidly. In canon, you can see that they've uncovered some truths, but also come to some wrong conclusions as they're only seeing part of the picture. More contact with the various supernaturals means that those wrong conclusions will get corrected and they'll sooner rather than later assemble a more full and accurate view of what's going on the World of Darkness. What happens next is anybody's guess.
                                  I would suggest that the Arcanum should probably "know" the high level basics of vampire as it is in the original 2e hardcover corebook. They "know" the main clans and a rough description of each akin to their two page blurb. They know the two main sects as briefly described in the corebook (which confuses the Black Hand with the Sabbat). They have a general knowledge of the major Disciplines. Anything found in the Players Guides, Clanbooks, and other sourcebooks is mostly out of scope unless you, as the ST, wants them to know it specifically.

                                  I would stress that the Arcanum's job to figure things out is MUCH, MUCH harder than what we as players know. Players know what is objectively true from the sourcebooks we read. The Arcanum must shift through a lot of bullshit, personal bias, and the incomplete information provided to them in whatever capacity from any actual contacts. They won't accept things uncritically. They need to piece things together themselves.

                                  Players tend to assume a LOT more knowledge for their characters than they should because once they read something in a sourcebook, they know it. Even if there is no reason their character should know. Just reflect on how much characters were not expected to know in the original Chicago By Night compared to now. Basic things like that primogen existed, who served the Prince, the very concept of diablerie, and other information were "secrets" not known to every character.

                                  This is how I would handle certain things.

                                  In the rare chances of an Arcanum scholar interviewing vampires, they are not going to be info dumped on everything in the corebook or the White Wolf Wiki. They are scholars who need to verify all details and try to be as objective as possible. They are going to be critical with every piece of information given. How much time will any individual vampire really give an Arcanum scholar? Vampires have their own agendas, time restrictions, and interest.

                                  Furthermore, how much does any vampire character actually "know"? Not only in the sense of the above example with "secrets", but in the sense of "I was there. I am a witness." versus "This is what I was told by another vampire." The first makes it a primary source. The other is a secondary source which is much less useful.

                                  What does a vampire gain from talking to the Arcanum? For most, it would be nothing which means they would have no interest in telling the Arcanum anything. Each vampire who does has their own agenda for doing so. It's likely not to be nice and provide this group of mortals free information in the interest of truth and justice.

                                  Also, the Arcanum expects vampires to be liars. Not only may other vampires have lied to the vampire being interviewed, the vampire being interviewed could be lying. Everything needs to be taken with a grain of salt and compared against other verifiable information. Maybe they had one informant who was secretly an archon, and he fed them complete misinformation that only seems true. Not all vampire informants are equally credible. The Arcanum needs to be equally skilled in spycraft as it is in scholarship.

                                  The Arcanum will also be wanting specific, objective information which they can use to form the basis of their own scholarly opinions. They won't want summaries of events that the vampire wasn't part of. They will be much more interested in a vampire reciting his own lineage than some eighth hand story about the Anarch Revolt. Doesn't mean they won't tape record everything, but that story is of dubious scholastic value even if they hear it repeated by multiple people. (Think of how many people "know" that the Europeans thought the world was flat before Columbus. That is totally untrue. They knew the world was round, but had disputes on its exact size. That "knowledge" is a result of Washington Irving's "biography" of Christopher Columbus and became popular knowledge. The Arcanum will know simply because many people report the same story, it doesn't make it true.) The petty details - which are usually completely ignored by PCs because they aren't "fun" to bring up during the game - will be far more important because those petty details is what that vampire actually knows as a witness.

                                  Arcanum scholars also need to be very protective of themselves and of their "informers". Things can easily grow wrong. Vampires are very dangerous creatures. In canon, the first time they made contact with a vampire, it lead to the destruction of the Boston chapterhouse. There is going to be a lot of intrigue involved. Likewise there is still some danger to the vampire informant even if this isn't an instant kill situation. Individual archons, princes, or random vampires may not like any Kindred telling them anything. There should still be some level of danger. It's just the exact extent in that domain is up to the ST. (And different splats may even be worse.)

                                  Lastly, remember the total number of vampire "informants" all depends on you, the ST. Maybe they only had four vampires in history willing to tell them something other than the one PC in your chronicle who is willing to talk. Or maybe they had twenty vampires talking to them, but sixteen knew almost nothing valuable. After talking with one Anarch, they had to completely revisit all the information they had previously "known" was "true" about the Camarilla. It should be rare enough that any time a PC wishes to become involved in the Arcanum that should be a BIG opportunity for the Arcanum.

                                  When portraying such Arcanum characters in the games, you should be taking into consideration how scholars like this operate and what kind of information they really want. Roleplay them challenging the PC(s) actual knowledge, as opposed to what they think they know. (This might be a good way to get players to question how much their character actually "knows".) They are going to act like a combination of scholars, spy handlers, journalists, police detectives, and prosecutors towards the PC(s). Even if they act nice and credulous to the PC(s) to keep them happy and talking, they are going to be a lot more skeptical and objective in their reports to the Arcanum itself.

                                  The final extent of the Arcanum's knowledge is up to the ST, but I would argue they would probably "know" a lot less than you might expect them to know even in a scenario where they do have more interaction.

                                          In a sense the Arcanum, by its own nature, is actually likely to know more about some things than most vampires (not most players), but that isn't to say much. Most vampires don't really know as much as we think (much less with any reliability).

                                          Originally posted by Arcanum Scholar View Post

                                          If that changed, and interactions became a more common occurrence, their knowledge can only increase.                                           Yes, but not as much, and definitely not as fast.

                                          Everything told about Vampires still holds about everyone else: they're unreliable. Extremely unreliable. It comes back again to how we perceive the lore, because we know for sure most truths, but the characters don't.

                                          Not only that, keep in mind that they get contradicting information, and that new data may put old data in check. How much the Arcanum knows for sure is probably very little, and most other things are just suppositions and unreliable data. It may take years, even decades, to confirm anything at all.
