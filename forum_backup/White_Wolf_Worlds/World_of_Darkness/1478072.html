    Tremere becoming vampires makes no sense if Pillar magick is taken into consideration

    04-05-2022, 01:47 PM
    Dark ages mage is a supplement released by whitewolf that sets the mage game in, surprise surprise, the dark ages. It created a new system of magic to replace spheres called pillars, with each faction having unique pillars that align with their foundation, which is another word for their paradigm. I wont talk too much about it since you can look up what you need to know from the wiki, and since most of you are veterans within the field.
    ​
    The Tremeres reason for becoming vampires seems pretty weak when we take into account that the pillar Corona (Command of mind) at level 5 allows you to astral project even after your physical body dies, and take over other bodies whenever you want like a ghost. Anima (Command of life) allows you to alter the body. This pillar could potentially make a mage immortal, and could even create a body out of nothing, but it will be an empty husk without a spirit to possess it. Corona solves that issue.

    Both the Pillars can be made to seem coincidental as well, if you are using Anima to be immortal, just change how you look. Make it seem like you are older or younger to fool those around you, and when one persona dies, create a new one. Corona is going to be coincidental 90% of the time since no one will be able to notice what you are doing.

    Besides the books being published at different points of time, was there a legitimate reason for the Tremere to turn to vampirism when the powers they had in the dark ages was already pretty coincidental? As long as you didn't throw fireballs, there shouldn't be an issue right?

                                              “Coincidental” vs. “Vulgar” isn't a thing in Dark Ages: Mage. All you have are Backlashes, which occur whenever you botch a casting roll.

                                              The Storytellers Vault Mage Style Guide provides the following Game Design Advice for any M20 Dark Ages: Mage material:
                                              Because of the mechanical differences with the original Dark Ages: Mage you will need to make a choice on what rule set you used to develop your supplements. It is possible to utilize the rule set for Dark Ages: Mage in other edition settings as well, but be clear with your product if you choose that route. Supplements created to be compatible with the Mage 20th Anniversary rules and the setting of Dark Ages: Mage will have a different feel their those using the system for the original game. You are welcome to include elements of either or develop an updated ‘20th Edition’ version of the Dark Ages: Mage Foundation and Pillar system.

                                              If I were going to do something like this, I would want to go the latter route, revising the Foundation and Pillar system both to eliminate problems like the one presented above (e.g., the Order of Hermes' Forma should be predecessors to their equivalent Spheres, and at best should be as capable as their modern counterparts, not more capable) and to open up possibilities that Dark Ages: Mage didn't allow for:

                                              • the Craftmasons demonstrate that a Fellowship need not be locked into precisely four Pillars; and I'd allow the Dark Ages Order of Hermes in particular to have different Houses teach different sets of four Forma each, with the cumulative effect being that the total number of Forma taught by the Order comes closer to nine than to four. It needn't be restricted to the Order of Hermes; it's just that the Hermetics are best set up to support this option.

                                              • Using the Idran as inspiration, I'd explore the possibility of the Foundation and Pillar system being usable beyond the Dark Ages setting. My ideal would be to provide an optional take on the Disparate Alliance, where each faction has its own Foundation and Pillars; along with more robust guidelines for creating your own custom Foundation and Pillars (with the Hollow Ones having a knack for “do it yourself” Pillars instead of having a preset list). I might even suggest Foundations and Pillars for each Tradition, for use in Chronicles where that Tradition abandons the Council of Nine and its “all Magick is one” philosophy and either goes out alone or joins the Disparate Alliance. Though doing all of that might be a bit much for one supplement.

                                              • One tweak that I'd make to the Foundation and Pillars rules would be to introduce something akin to the Subtle/Uncanny/Extreme setup found in Victorian Mage and Mage Made Easy instead of the current “Backlash on a botch” system. I might implement it along the lines of using the existing “Backlash on a botch” system for Subtle spells; allowing a Backlash to happen for an Uncanny spell if more 1s are rolled than successes, with the severity depending on how much the 1s outnumber the successes; and allowing a Backlash to happen on an Extreme spell if any 1s are rolled, regardless of how many successes get rolled (more successes still means weaker Backlashes, because every success rolled is a die that didn't roll a 1). On Uncanny and Extreme spells, the successes aren't nullified by the 1s: if you roll two successes and three 1s, you get a two-success casting, no matter what. But if the casting is Subtle, there's no Backlash (a single success prevents it); if it's Uncanny, there's one point of Backlash (your 1s outnumber your successes by one); and if it's Extreme, there are three points of Backlash (you rolled three 1s).

                                              • I'd also go for more clarity on how many successes are needed for a casting; though I'd build it around the notion that Simple and Complex spells only require one success while Extended and Ongoing spells need more than one. The guidelines would be for how many successes the latter two would require; and I'd borrow Feats from the regular Magick system for that.

                                              My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                      Originally posted by Hello View Post
                                                      Besides the books being published at different points of time, was there a legitimate reason for the Tremere to turn to vampirism when the powers they had in the dark ages was already pretty coincidental? As long as you didn't throw fireballs, there shouldn't be an issue right?
                                                      It was never a question of being able to do those things. It is still perfectly doable to become immortal through coincidental effects, indeed with the exact same effects if you want, with Sphere magic. Life 4, arguably 3 even is perfectly fine to make your body immortal, you can live outside it with Mind at similar levels and so on.

                                                      Even the Tremere were perfectly capable of doing so. The Inner Circle of the House was entirely composed by people with at least 3 centuries of life at the time of their transformation. That was never the question.

                                                      What the setting works with is something else entirely: this effect is possible, but is objectively becoming harder and more dangerous to pull as time goes on, and the Tremere wanted an answer to the conundrum while they still had time to do something about it. There was, from their point of view, a very real risk that delaying a solution too much would have them incapable of applying it by the time it was found.

                                                      Before DA in the official chronology Paradox wasn't a thing. It wasn't something you got with Vulgar Magic or something you got at a Botch, it simply didn't existed. Some years before the Tremere started their search Paradox appeared. It was rare, but slowly increasing in frequency and intensity. And it made some types of magic particularly harder, specifically immortality magic.

                                                      That is what the Tremere were trying to counter. They were already immortals and would remain so for decades at the very least, as would many others. But something was changing and would put the very effects you describe at risk later on. And they already knew that older immortals would be affected first, so they were quite worried about themselves.

                                                      You may take the Revised rules for magical immortality as a starting point to understand it. As stated, you get 1 Permadox every so many years (I don't quite remember if 50 or 100) starting at 100 years, on top of any Paradox incurred by your methods or vulgarity or anything else. The only thing that can circumvent this is to leave for the Umbra altogether and never come back. While Revised was particularly keen on making the presence of Masters and Archmasters on Earth as hard as possible, this rule mostly codified what the setting has always proposed as truth.

                                                      One thing to understand is how it all looked like from their perspective at the time. Magic was changing and immortality was already suffering more than other forms. This was a relatively new development that no one could entirely predict where would it stop or how fast it would advance. They didn't knew for sure for how long they would remain capable of doing something about it. They were looking for other methods to counter it, but faction politics also played a large role on which methods got to be tried. Finally, they didn't knew it would backfire. The Tremere didn't chose to become vampires, Goratrix developed a concoction based on vampires to achieve some sort of intermediate state. Loosing their Avatars in the process was a blunder.

                                                          Alternatively, if you don't like MRev rules for immortality Permadox (I try my hardest to pretend they never existed at all) and immortality spells becoming too dififcult, there is a handy alternative explanation for the Tremere issue. The Tremere leaders made a blunder (on top of losing their Avatars and becoming vanilla vampires instead of an intermediate state, that is). They got a vague prescience of the coming rise of the Technocracy and the effects it would have on the Consensus and Paradox, misinterpreted the omens into assuming immortality would be impossible or too difficult, panicked, and overreacted. If you ignore MRev Permadox rules and bits such the immortality elixirs of the OoH starting to fail (it does not make sense in terms of the overall magic system for various reasons), which are blatant and tiresome attempts by the authors to justify the Tremere metaplot, it all fits together.

                                                          By modern times, immortality spells became mostly vulgar, but they otherwise continue to work fine, and if you ignore the MRev metaplot, there are still plenty of immortal Masters around. The Tremere made a couple nasty mistakes and paid for them. The Lich effect from Dead Magic II existed since the Iron Age, and although far from perfect, it gave much better results as it concerns being ageless and keeping your Avatar; the Tremere were perhaps trying to reverse engineer or imitate Lichdom, but made a blunder. In total self-fulfilling prophecy fashion, the big irony is their mistake paved the way to the rise of the Technocracy. Without the major distraction of the Second Massassa War, chances are the OoH would have wiped out the Craftmasons.

                                                          Since I loathe this part of the WoD backstory and metaplot, and I want immortality to be potentially feasible for elder mages without too much fuss in DA:M and M20, this is how I choose to deal with the issue. As far as I can tell, it works, and you don't have to bend the rules for Sphere magic, Paradox, Foundation, and Pillars out of shape to justify the existence of Clan Tremere.
