    Meeting other supernatural

    10-13-2021, 03:56 PM
    Hello everyone,

    I would like your opinion on something.

    One of my player is mage who likes to socialize in museums, archeological clubs and occult-related meetings (mostly the academic ones, not the underground ones)

    Most of the time, these events occur in the evening and in big cities (whether it's NYC, Chicago or LA).

    Without trying to hard, what are the odds that we will cross path of another supernatural citizen?

    Likely ?

    ***

    Logically, a vampire comes in mind (possibly a Sethite, Tremere or a Toreaodor) ?
    A fera ?
    Or even a Mummy ? (centuries old egyptian urns exposition)

    I'll take any ideas or opinions

      As the storyteller, you decide on splat-to-sleeper population ratios. Crossover is a bit more complex. for me, it's fairly simple. I am running Mage with distant possibility of including Wraith and Changeling NPCs. So, I only really have to concern myself with Mage to sleeper ratio. For me, this is 1 per 100,000 on average and 1 per 10,000 where the plot happens.

      The two basic criteria you start with for a single splat Mage campaign are:
      1. Preference: Do you want to emphasize the important role sleepers play in shaping reality and give your PCs more room for empire-building by having very few Mages? Do you want them to be under intense pressure from the opposed faction's numerous Mages and having to fall back on their own faction's Mage NPCs?
      2. Realism: Objectively, what do you think the odds of a sleeper awakening should be? What special qualities or experiences are necessary for a person to have in order to have the chance to awaken?

      Then, each splat that you are including has a separate set of questions:
      Vampire:
      1. Preference: Do you want your Mages running into egomaniacal vampires who try to blood bond them every time they hit a night club? Do you want the special needs of Vampires to be an opportunity for favor trading? Messing with blood-bonds can be fun.
      2. Realism: If Vampires can reproduce at will, how is the Masquerade able to continue functioning? How would you handle a stealthy player (or NPC) using a Mind 3, Entropy 3 effect to cause a Vampire to frenzy in a crowded place, on camera, as a strategy for affecting the local Consensus?

      Werewolf:
      1. Preference: Do you want some beefy allies or terrifying enemies for your players?
      2. Realism: If lycanthropy is hereditary and recessive, how often would Werewolves turn up?

      As to the question of how often you would run into other splats in specific professional associations, there are a couple of things to consider and I will start with an example. I have a NWO - Ivory Tower and an Order of Hermes both being professors at a university. They refrain from waging a high-Minded war of influence at the expense of the student body, because they both believe in the institution they are working to improve.

      If a conflict occurred, it is likely that the institution would be damaged and that only one would be left. So, in your WoD, what do splat NPCs anticipate the result of an encounter with another to be? If they are powerful, do they confidently claim a position in the institution's hierarchy? If they are a fledgling Mage/other, do they carefully scout the place and avoid becoming too prominent? Also, does everyone scan the aura of everyone they meet?

      More specifically, a Mage who likes to socialize in museums, archeological clubs and occult-related meetings... That greatly increases the chance of encountering another supernatural. However, I would go with Preference over Realism. What kind of NPC do you want to introduce, if at all? If there is no competition, can the PC just start building an information network, recruiting Acolytes, resistance-free?





      Thank you for passing time with me in conversation. My Hacks.

          I would recommend you to first weigh in what you want for your game, before thinking on likelihoods. What do you want for the other splats to represent in your game?

          Second, if you really want to tackle this from a "canon" perspective, learn more about any splat before even considering them. Why would a mummy want to see this exposition? It have little to do with how WoD mummies are, unless this urn is a plot device.

          Third, consider the actual characters you want to use. Other supernaturals are as varied as Mages and some people will be interested while others not so much. Also they are usually small populations in comparison with mortals, so not bumping into one is the norm most of the time, unless there is a particular character which similar habits to the Mage PC.

          Forth, consider what you mean by meeting. Your character may even be close friends with other supernaturals and be completely unaware of the fact and vice-versa. All supernaturals in WoD keep low profiles. Everyone prioritizes not being perceived by common Sleepers, and preferably also not by potential enemies. Everyone try to perceive others first and preferably not being perceived in return, with this second part frequently taking precedence. So what will trigger them noticing one another for that to be a proper "supernatural meeting"?

              As a rule of thumb, supernaturals of all types are rare enough that a chance encounter with someone not involved in your character's supernatural culture stands a very real chance of never happening. So, like HorizonParty2021 and monteparnas have said, you should approach it less in terms of what the statistical probability is and more with what the story-based pros and cons are. As an example, I personally have very little interest in vampires or werewolves; and I only have a passing familiarity with those lines; so don't expect and vampires or werewolves (or wraiths, for that matter) to be making any appearances in my games. Changelings, on the other hand, stand a reasonable chance of showing up somewhere, as Changeling is my second favorite of the World of Darkness gamelines, right behind Mage. That said, if the game extensively features necromancy, I'm likely to bring wraiths into the mix.

              My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                      I agree with others that this should be more a storytelling based decision rather than some mathematical formula.

                      Because I like to include all elements of the WoD in my chronicles as sandbox elements, I try to keep in my head a general idea of how other supernatural creatures interact in the setting I set my chronicle in. I don't spend too much time detailing them, but I like to have a general idea of who is present and what do they do.

                      Garou typically are not found in cities and even the Bone Gnawers or Glass Walkers can't be everywhere. So it is easy to not include them if you don't want. Only if you think there's a reason they should be there - like you think a caern makes sense, or something in a city's history indicates there should be werewolves present.

                      Vampires will be present in some capacity in most large cities. So maybe assigning a Prince, a general idea of the major clans, and where they meet for Elysium, hunting, or other reasons would tell you that there is potential to meet a masquerading vampire in this place, but not that one. There's probably a limited number of places most vampires congregate in any city. If you don't want to deal with them, they can safely be elsewhere. If you have a story you want to tell, you can place them in a place your PCs regularly go to. If you just want them as a sandbox element, figure it out in advance and be prepared to run a possible event if the PCs choose to go there.

                      Usually you can ignore ghosts entirely unless you have a list of specific places that are known to be haunted and want to play that up. Otherwise you can usually safely ignore the entire background of Anacreons, Legions, Renegades, etc.

                      While urban fantasy is big in many Changeling games, that's another group I think most urban based games can easily ignore. Mankind/banality tends to shoo them away. Unless you are inspired that a particular place should have Changelings, they can safely ignored.

                      Mummies are relatively few. If you want one (or more) in your city, that's fine. If you don't, they aren't. Simply because a local museum has some Egyptian artifacts, or is currently displaying some on tour, doesn't mean you must have a mummy be present. If you do have one, it should be because you want to tell a story involving one.

                      Such an approach does force an ST to spend more time on world building, but you only need to figure out a general big picture and not worry about the details. Only if it seems it'll be a story or event you want to run as an ST do you need to spend any time detailing JUST what you need for that encounter.

                          Going by CtL's RAW Changelings get tricky, because unless you do very, very limited interactions, they're all or nothing. Either the Mists apply and anything more involved becomes a vague memory, or you get Enchanted and you're almost completely drawn to their setting, even if as a juxtaposition. Not that you can't find ways to get around it, but again, going by RAW, it is kinda hard, too much for a casual "Encounter Table".

                          Vampires and Werewolves are the easier ones to bring without further commitment. They're "everywhere" (well, Changelings and Wraiths also are), focused on their own problems, and likely to avoid too much interaction. Changelings and Demons are meddlers by nature, and where Changelings bring the full brunt of Chimerical Reality with them, Demons are the harder supernatural by far to get away from, as their innate abilities alone are excellent to find you behind any disguise. Mummies are too rare, so the mere presence of one is indicative of something significant happening.

                              Contact and relations between Nightfolk of different kinds / splats are mentioned relatively often in WoD books, so these chance meetings do occur with regularity. Two reasons for it being that some Nightfolk do tend to stand out from mundane humans (especially if one is observed for a long amount of time), and the detection and perception abilities of Nightfolk being relatively common. There are also existing networks of contacts to consider - a Vampire could come to know about your player's Mage from another person.

Cult of Ecstasy Mages are noted as respecting the Toreador, Chakravanti are mentioned as being in contact with the Giovanni Kindred, and the Tytalans do have some links with the Tremere Vampires.
