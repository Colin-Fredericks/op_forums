        Since Crowley and Karavolos were posting their custom Demon factions...

        For my games I've created two groups. They aren't factions as much as they are Elohim who have rallied around a particular idea.

        The Gardeners are made up mostly of Devourer Reconcilers, with some other Houses mixed in. They have decided that the first step to fixing the world and setting things right, will be to return the humans to a primitive state. They intend to move slowly, so as not to cause large-scale death or disruption, but their end goal is a world without cities, without technology, where humans are few in number. Some even insist that the humans must give up their ill-gotten consciousness (like the Lightning Tree in the D:tF novels). To this end, the Gardeners are activists for voluntary population reduction (and support groups like VHEMT), but are also looking at ways to sterilize vast swathes of the population, and are even researching advanced forms of brain surgery and lobotomies to return humans to their "Pre-Fall" mental state.

        The "Kill-the-Buddha Club" takes its name from a (misunderstood) Zen koan. A pair of Luciferans were discussing the human idea that the Creator had taken human incarnations throughout history. The idea appeared in many human faiths, and the Luciferans had noticed that there seemed to be some power that came with that faith. What if, they wondered, the Creator had actually split itself into "fragments of divinity", or really did put itself into a vulnerable human form? If that were so, then the Luciferan faction could strike a real blow against the Creator, winning the War! For this reason, the group is making plans to kidnap, study, and assassinate major religious figures. Anyone who claims to be or is believed to be divine is a valid target, from monarchs who claim divine ancestry to well-known cult leaders. Since its initial formation, the group has since been quietly infiltrated by Raveners who see an opportunity to strike back at the Creator before destroying Creation itself.
