    Retconning...how do you deal with it?

    08-02-2014, 01:27 PM
    Sooner or later, a lot of RPGs has elements of its canon retconned -often with the arrival of a newer edition- and CWoD game lines did not escape it. Sometimes it's minor things, sometimes it's pretty major.

    For exemple in my case I kept the Hellcats. In my universe the Ceilican did dance the Black Spiral and are now gone; represent one more Victory of the Wyrm against Gaia. I like the Garou-Bastet Symmetry and the tradegy it brings. Another exemple for me: is the whole Old Clan Tzimisce/Vicissitude question. Although not a disease, Vicissitude is not the original discipline of the clan in my universe and OC Tzimisce are the really older the "Sabbat" Tzimisce with the appropriate tension that goes with it.

    So, as a ST, how do you deal with it? As a player, how do you deal with it? Especially when it affects or nullify your character concept.

    Obviously here there's no Right or Wrong here. I'm curious to see how other STs/Players deal with it. What get's accepted and what get's dropped

      As a ST? Whatever I want to make the game I'm running interesting and fun, though I make sure to communicate these sorts of things as clearly as possible. Making sure to note what books are in use and what books aren't, or even partial usage (such as just mechanics, or just fluff).

      As a player, I don't make a concept that relies on these things without talking to the ST first. Either I adjust the concept, or I play something different.

          I see new material as providing more options. Even if I prefer the old stuff, it's my (and my friends') game, not Onyx Path's, and not the Internet's. The only thing that can invalidate a character concept is if it's incompatible with the specific game the ST is running. As an ST, I'm fine with making adjustments like "in our timeline, the Sabbat Tremere haven't been wiped out"* if someone expressed serious interest in playing one, and it didn't otherwise conflict with the story.

          I'll usually default to using the current edition's setting material, and cherry-pick elements from previous edition or my own invention if there's something there I like more. For my current game, I've slid around exactly when things like the Gangrel leaving the Camarilla happened, and skipped Gehenna, but basically use V20.

          As a player, I accept that every ST is going to add their own person touch to the setting, and have character creation guidelines in place to make sure that their chronicle works. I'm fine with that. I'd rather come up with a character that will be fun for the current story than get attached to a concept that doesn't work in every game.


          *One thing I have vowed to be better about is communicating my policy of flexibility. I actually had someone who wanted to play a Tremere antitribu, but didn't because he didn't think it was an option. I only found out once the chronicle was well underway it was too late to change things.

                      From an ST POV: it's a non-issue. Any changes in continuity are either incorporated in-narratively (the change is "discovered to be true" in the next gaming session -- if directly influencing the ongoing stories, and I *like* the changes/updates -- or, if it's something that never came up in the game/ongoing story, then it's again a non-issue: whatever is "true" comes up when it comes up, if it comes up at all...) or meta-narratively (either via the tried-and-true soap opera device of simply not having "x" be referred to in the game for some time, and then when "brought up" it simply is as indicated by "whatever", and people proceed from there, or alternately I go "Hey -- I like "x" better than how it was presented before, so let's go in that direction, changing any salient details as needed to make it work"). My players (over the past 30 years of running/gaming) have never had an issue with that.

                      From a PC POV: it's a non-issue. Just adjust accordingly to whichever direction the ST wants to go in. Histories can just "float", as needed, or be altered with ease, to incorporate any changes. In the case where certain salient points greatly interfere with a character concept of an ongoing PC (*not* simply that the *player* is "annoyed" with the changes, but that said changes *truly* impact on the PC, as he has existed/been run), then you just make the PC be a "snowflake" exception to whatever changes allowing him to exist (though perhaps mitigated in some manner, as appropriate to whatever narratives or meta-narratives are now in effect). Example: a player two decades ago had been running a Tremere Antitribu for several years; his character was in torpor (in a lost, Shallowing part of an African jungle) when the Antitribu purge took place in the late 90's. His character was legitimately not found (and, due to the arcane nature of the Shallowing, thought dead even by his fellows' mystical scanning) and -- when he awoke and managed to make his way back to civilization -- was an exception to the by-the-book narrative. Of course, his days were numbered once his existence became known, but he managed to stay alive through to the Gehenna-end of CWoD. No muss, no fuss.

                      I'll be honest, most of the time I simply don't "get" why so many players/STs are so hung up on continuity changes: you can either accept them, ignore them, partially incorporate them (via certain bits and pieces, or over a long period of game time), or create wholly alternate version that utterly deviate from the published materials. Just change course/vectors and continue playing. >shrug< It's no big deal (and in the occasional instances where it *is*, you can -- per the example above -- simply incorporate and move on...).

                      PS: Of course, it goes without saying that if running for a group of people unfamiliar with you/your game(s), and you deviate significantly from the books, then the simple mention of "Much of what you, the players, know is not going to apply to my game -- I'll inform you as we go where things diverge/are completely different..." should have everybody in the mindset to just take things as they come until they become more acquainted with the world they're in now. Again, simple -- no muss, no fuss...

                          Here's what I do....
                          • The Gangrel are still part of the Camarilla
                          • There was no Week of Nightmares
                          • The Stargazers are still part of the Garou Nation
                          • Tremere Antitribbu are still around
                          • Vampires aren't emotionally dead inside nor are they necessarily inherently evil
                          • Soulforging does not exist in my World of Darkness
                          • There was no Avatar Storm
                          • Changeling: The Lost replaces Changeling: The Dreaming completely
                          • Kindred of the East, Hunter The Reckoning, and Demon: The Fallen are not part of my World of Darkness, East Asia is dominated by Cainites with their own unique customs instead of Kuei-Jin

                          • The fan-game Senshi: The Merchandising is part of my World of Darkness canon. Yes, you heard correctly. Sailor Moon is part of my take on CWOD.

                              As an ST I tend to only loosely pay attention to the specifics of the world I'm in anyway, preferring to take the 'guts' of it, and using the provided details to save me having to come up with them. But I don't feel the need to necessarily include everything from the current edition of the game I'm playing, let alone any newer editions that might come out. And besides, that's a new edition, which in my eyes makes it a different game, which means it's no more relevant than anything I might read in any other game. And as for extra splat books, well, they're splat books: they're meant to be optional anyway.

                              As a player, it's never come up.
