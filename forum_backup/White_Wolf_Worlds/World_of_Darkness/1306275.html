                              Vampires are greatly disadvantaged during the day, but you're typically going to encounter Vampires at night (unless you're actively pursuing one via Correspondence or other tracking methods, which is simple for some Mages and hard for others). It's almost like saying "I know this character has 15 dice to dodge at difficulty 3, but that's not going to do them much good if I attack them while they're asleep." All defenses are situational.

                              As for blood mage countermagic, if you don't want it to work on magick then just say it doesn't work. Or say that it only works in specific instances; Tremere countering a Hermetic could work, but their thaumaturgical countermagic will be useless against a Dreamspeaker or a Akashayana.

                                  As an ST everything that the players get it something you've permitted. You're the final arbiter. If you don't like a PC having a certain path, you can say no. I find "No, but-" is a strong tool for a storyteller. Give them something else (that they pay for with EXP and freebies of course) which runs along similar lines and lets them enjoy playing.

                                  "No, you can't have countermagic since it's so powerful in this context, but I'd be happy for you to have a Mage Retainer, so long as you put enough points in and it's all RP'd properly; I'm thinking a power-hungry infatuated apprentice who is generally loyal but not exactly "obedient"".

                                  This is far more interesting for a story than "I block spells well". Now you're got a traitor on your side who is connected to both sides of the Massassa War, who can protect you from magick if you manipulate his blood bond correctly.
