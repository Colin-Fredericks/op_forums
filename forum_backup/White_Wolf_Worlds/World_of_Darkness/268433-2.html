            That's part of the problem with the Houses and so much focus on them.

                                Neither of this are universally true or false.

                                I'd highly advise reading Fool's Luck; which talks a lot about the Commoner reactions to the Resurgence. Lots of Commoners are split between their feelings of betrayal at both their abandonment by the Sidhe for centuries, and their atrocious actions taken during the Resurgence beyond the pale of simply being Dreaming empowered snobs, and their inherent reaction to the Sidhe's Dreaming empowered right to rule.

                                The Sidhe didn't violently steal Freeholds because you can't. But that didn't stop them from persecuting Commoners horribly for not handing over the keys politely. Yes, the Commoner Freeholds weren't bastions of progressive politics, the vast majority left the old school feudalism behind in favor of things closer to modern constitutional monarchies ruled far more by their oligarchic councils than their figure head kings/queens. Modern political thoughts are something that explicitly have started to work their ways into the Commoner Kith mentalities as they've had to live amongst humans for so long.

                                There's a reason why the Resurgence lead to a war. Because the Commoners didn't just kneel down and accept the Sidhe as a whole. The Commoners, after the war they lost, might not see value in trying to overthrow the Sidhe now, but that doesn't mean there isn't a deep resentment amongst many of them for how the Sidhe Resurgence turned out.

                                                If the Houses are the primary political factions the conflict between Commoners and Nobles, Sidhe particularly but this take also involves the Commoner Nobles, just intensifies because most Commoners will see those of their kind who swear to Houses, even if they do so in an attempt to get political influence to weaken the Sidhe, as joining the enemy. Also it does nothing to remove rebel Commoners from building a political power base of their own, again escalating the conflict, by taking control of Free Holds and swaying the people, which is naturally difficult as the Sidhe have considerable natural edges in this latter endeavor, and rising up to stand against the Houses or forming a "house" of their own demanding a voice of their own.

                                                The Sidhe by the way are right, they are born of dreams of nobility, rule, tyranny, leadership and beauty and so they truly have been born to rule. The Houses just underline this as does the fact that the Sidhe require a higher amount of Fae Realm to affect as well as their inability to be made the fool through magic not to mention their unbearable and commanding beauty. The question you should be asking is about whether or not such qualities and birthrights alone are enough to make a good leader and whether it is better to be lead by those who think it their right or by those who think it as their privilege. There is a valid reason that the Commoners find the Sidhe leadership hard to bow under just like there are valid reasons for them to rebel.

                                                So to matter, even politically, one does not need to swear to a House it is just easier that way but when doing so you are selling yourself to the Sidhe. A thought that should spark a rebellious fire inside any commoner who yearns for personal power, freedom or just does not wish to bend knee to anyone.
