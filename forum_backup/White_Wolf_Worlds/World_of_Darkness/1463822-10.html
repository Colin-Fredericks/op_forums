    I have no doubt that the X5 lines are all borrowing ideas from the Chronicles of Darkness. However, my suspicion with H5 is that there's more to it than that. Phil Brucato mentioned that back in 1999, when WW was designing Hunter: the Reckoning, one of the disappointments he had with it was that he envisioned it as a book about ordinary people dealing with the discovery that the world was supernatural, and wanted it to focus more on that aspect; but the bulk of the developers went straight to “what kinds of powers should they have?”

    If the folks at ParaWolf are more like Brucato in sentiment than the original design team for HtR, it would make sense that they're attempting to de-emphasize the “Imbued with power to fight the supernatural” side of it.

    If that's true, the parallels between H5 and HtV, to the extent that they exist, are more of a coincidence than a direct borrowing. Well, maybe not “coincidence”; more like, if you're making a game about ordinary people hunting monsters, it's hard not to have parallels to HtV: you'd have to actively work to avoid them.

                                                            Sorry; the Buffy bit was over the line. I do get what the original HtR playstyle is like; and aside from a handful of agency preserving Merits from the Bystanders section of the Player's Guide, it's not really my thing. But I shouldn't have taken it that far.

                                                            Also, “HH style” is perhaps the wrong way to phrase it. More like “Year of the Hunter” style, except that the Year of the Hunter books focused mostly on hunter organizations (the Inquisition, government agencies, the Arcanum, various ghost hunter factions) rather than individuals. When HtR was first announced, that's what a lot of us were looking for: regular people who were made aware of the supernatural threats around them and decided to do something about it. The Imbued, being supernaturally empowered to the last of them, were not what we were looking for.

                                                            What I've been seeing of H5 so far is a lot closer to what I wanted to see the first time around. If they could incorporate something like the aforementioned Bystander traits (HPG pp.51–52, 56–60), I'd have everything I want from the original game. The rest of it was, as I see it, merely an attempt to help sell the then-brand-new Exalted game by planting hints that the Imbued were somehow the former Solar Exalted reborn into the modern world. That required the Imbued to be more than just ordinary people who had been alerted to the presence of the supernatural; it required them to be supernatural beings themselves. Because so were the Exalted.

                                                            That said, I would not object if each Creed were given an optional Edge that brings the HtR Edges into H5 — optional, so that those of us who prefer something more like the HH style don't end up having to house-rule the game to get rid of them. That, combined with the Bystander traits, would allow for the Imbued style of gameplay for those who want it. I do hope that they include something like that in H5; I could even get behind the notion that the Messengers are still around, bringing monsters to peoples' attention but no longer Imbuing them, and the “Bystander” traits can arise as a result of that. Just as long as encountering monsters on your own is also an option for becoming a Hunter.

                                                            But (other than the Bystander traits, particularly Hardened Spirit, Resistant to Supernatural Fear, and Resistant to Mind Control; the agency-preserving traits) I also won't be disappointed if the Imbued are not there.

                                                            Or there's another option: do True Faith as the H5 version of the Imbued, with Virtues stacked on top of the Drives in a manner similar to how Dark Ages: Inquisitor replaced True Faith with a set of Superior Virtues, and those Virtue dots entitling you to Creed-based powers based on 1e's Edges. The idea here is that throughout history, the Messengers have always been around, empowering mortals with sufficient Drive and Religious Devotion (HPG p.97) with divine guidance and assistance; and that has traditionally been represented in the game by the True Faith mechanics. But when the Reckoning happened, they “broke protocol” and extended the scope of their empowerments, resulting in them Imbuing individuals who wouldn't have qualified for it before, when only the faithful were Imbued. After the crisis passed, they mostly went back to their old MO. As such, most of the remaining Imbued are true believers (in some sort of higher power; not necessarily the Christian God), though a few outliers still exist.

                                                            There: you get your Imbued, complete with a place within the new structure of the game, and I get my mortal Hunter. Heck, I might even find some interest in playing this version of the Imbued.
