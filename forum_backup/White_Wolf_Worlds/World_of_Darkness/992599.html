    Orphans and magic

    10-24-2016, 06:47 PM
    Why would an orphan think to use spheres?

    In mage the sphere system is a mutually agreed upon system developed by both the traditions and technocracy(after they stole it from the freemasons, who stole it from egypt, etc.). If you take dark ages mage as canon in modern mage games, Why would an orphan use spheres rather than a system that fits with their own paradigm?

      Not every Tradition uses the Spheres. The game system is one thing and should be separated from the in-game story concepts.

      Myself, I do not use the Technocratic Spheres for just that reason. The nine Spheres are enough to describe the reality as a whole, which is sufficient to make them a magick system for everyone. But in the story, technocrats do not use them, neither do most of the Traditions. I guess Orphans would not neither. Well... a baby learns the langage you speak him to. An Orphan that never heard of Spheres will not describe magick in Spheres terms. The Orphan would have to experiment, probably make mistakes and often die because of them. But if he does not, he will explain his magick in accordance to his paradigm, as all Mages do.

              You mean Primal Utility, Data and Dimensional Science ? It's an optional rule.

              I make a difference between the game system and the in-game story. With my players, I insist on the description of their practice precisely because of the differences between their paradigms. In-game, the Spheres is really something of the Order of Hermes. If I had Technocract players, I would let them role-play Data, Primal Utility and Dimensional Science, but as for the system, they woud still use the nine Spheres (hence Correspondance, Prime and Spirit). This is a choice of mine, of course. But to answer the original message, that is why in my opinion Orphans would not think of using the traditional Spheres. The players would use them, not necessarily their characters, know what I mean ? Not sure I'm clear there...
