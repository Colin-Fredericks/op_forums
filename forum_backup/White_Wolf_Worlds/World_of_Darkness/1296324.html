                          Let's get cracking! A few conjuration and forces parlor Tricks:


                          - Apocalypse-Twinkie (Matter 2 + Prime 2): You can conjure a twinkie in your pocket. Really, if bad comes to worse, you won't need to go on a quest to find the last one. Bill Murray will thank you. Bonus: You'll always have food. Except it's only Twinkies. Or a breath mint? Really, the type of candy is yours to pick.

                          - The loaded hip-flask (Matter 2 + Prime 2): Notice how certain characters in movies always have that handy small hip-flask in their coat pocket to calm the nerves? Notice how it rarely ever gets shown as empty? Now you know why. Cheers.

                          - Think Fast! (Forces 2): On the more elegant side of forces tricks, this allows you to always catch whatever's (with non-hostile intent) tossed at you - in style and with a smug grin on your face.

                          - Spoopy Camera (Forces 3 + Prime 2) : Ever notice how cameras creepily fail just at the important moment? Especially when you thought you *just* saw something move on it? That's your shtick. Timing your trick right, a camera you've spotted will lose its picture quality for just a moment. Classic VHS tape seeking problems, or sudden digital artifacts on modern cameras..they happen just at the wrong time, and for but a moment, but it's enough to get you by. The problem? You need to actually spot the camera first and time it right to paint the short error onto the image taken in as you slip by quickly...don't overuse this either.

                          - Goddamn Tinitus! (Forces 3 + Prime 2): Yeah, there's that person in the room, and you just don't want them to hear you as you mutter the Important Passphrase to your friend. You *know* that darn person is evesdropping. Except...there goes that goddamn tinitus. For just a moment, there is that familiar (short) high pitch that only they can hear. Annoying. And gone again very soon, but it might be the thing that just have stopped them from hearing properly what they shouldn't hear...


                          These spoiler'd ones are denied after discussion, because being either actually potentially paradox-without-witnesses, or combat related (Parlor Trick pretty much says 'non-combat in general):
                          - Who's there!? (Might actually be outside Parlor Trick range - see posts Below. TL;DR: Potentially paradox): The bad guys have you in their sights. Or you are trying to avoid those sights? Orrrrr... they already captured you. Things look grim! If only they weren't paying attention to you as you reach for your strikingly yellow utility-belt.... And suddenly, the nearby foliage rustles! The sounds of steps come down that other corridor! A sound of something in the next room falling on the floor! Whatever you have specialized for (and remember, you gotta pick one of them..), it is not real, but they don't know that - and it's just the distraction you needed.

                          - So clumsy, Jackie would be proud (Denied, Parlor Trick says non-combat): Akin to the certain magick-enhanced martial arts moves explained in HdydT - except the only thing you can do is fall down. You have mastered the art of slipping and falling just in time to dodge the hurt, giving you just the extra speed you needed to get out of that dead aim. You can't pull this off often. You can't do it while lounging leaned against a bar or on a rough carpet. But during a sudden rush of panic-action, these subtle forces might be the only thing giving you that little extra edge to get out of harm's way. Bonus: You will, of course, land ruffled but unharmed. Don't stress your luck. (Hey, the Parlor Trick entry only said no damage-producing...)

And I don't think any of those are too much . The only one would be the "telekinesis" one - and that's only because it's three things in one. Each parlor trick should be a single effect - it can be a bit flexible (like the blowing bubbles example), but it has to be a singular, definable effect (one sentence without semicolons or independent clauses, is a good rule). But any of those individually would be good ones.

                                      Though... be careful of the flame one around Infernal-wise vampires and werewolves... turning it green could end your life in a hurry.

                                                      - Windbreaker (Forces 2): For those who think the ol' "lighting the cig without a match" is skirting it, here is a convenient alternative. Everybody hates it when you try to light something and there is just that darn gust of wind that keeps on putting out the lighter or match. Well, no more - just when you need it, the flame stays safe in just the right moment.

                                                      - Don't spit *and* don't swallow (correspondence 2 + Life 2 and/or Matter 2) : Perhaps you are forced to take a drug to stay 'undercover'. Perhaps you are forced to take one, period. Or maybe you are just a specific kind of Cult Of Ecstacy member that doesn't want to ruin an expectation. Ahem. Either way, instead of ingesting whatever enters your oral cavities, it ends up in your pocket or nearby bag instead. It might be worth the later inconvenience.

                                                      - The lucky screen protector (Forces 2): Okay. You *are* actually clumsy, but after paying way too much in hardware repairs over time, you've practiced this simple effect. Again and again.. now, if your favorite phone drops towards the ground from a normal height and you notice in time, it's safe. Lucky you! What brand *is* that?

                                                      - *Bang Bang* "Yay!" (Forces 2): There it goes again, that darn vending machine keeping something stuck. And banging on its front or side (usually) does not help. Except in your case. Whatever gets stuck, it always drops - and because you are extra lucky, you sometimes get a bonus.

                                                          - *Click* (Forces 2 or Matter 2): Just what kind of Mage *are* you? Well, apparently the one who likes to play Russian Roulette. Literally. We're not even going to ask what crowd you are hanging with, but at some point you should feel bad about that thing never firing when it's your turn. Ever.

                                                          - God Of Gamblers (Correspondence 2 + Matter 2 | Forces 2 (depends on the game): A subtle turn of the dice. Always that luck with cards. That bumping the roulette ball does at just the last moment. There is that *one* game you specialize in where your odds are just better than everyone else's. WARNING: Repeated use can be hazardous to your health and/or result in bans from casino chains. Do not specialize in bowling unless you want to make The Dude sadface. NOTE: Forget about modern slot machines. Those are pimped with electronics and computers controlling everything.

                                                          - 360 NoScope / "AAA - 99999pts" (Forces 2 + Time 1 (Highscore) or 2 (Competitive)): The Virtual Adept variant of the above, and more aimed at competitive or HiScore videogames. Actually a subtle application of Forces (keyboard/Mouse/controller/arcade controls momvement - you have to pick one) and Time instead of anything electronics related. You darn cheater. Get ready for the 'LAME' brand.
