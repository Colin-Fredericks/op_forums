        I think how well the idea can work depends a fair bit on how one chooses to interpret the word "shaman," but I think there are at least two ways to handle this character concept:

        When saying they want to be a "shaman," your player might simply mean "a Mage whose magic is centred around summoning, interacting with, or binding spirits." If that is all, then a member of the Hermetic House Fortunae might actually work really well. As posts above have mentioned, the concept of "capitalism" is a bit abstract for the Middle Umbra. However, it seems rather appropriate for an entity of the High Umbra, and summoning Angels from the High Umbra has been a component of the Hermetic tradition for a long time now.

        The more specific connotation of the word "shaman" goes beyond just summoning and interacting with spirits, however. It also tends to include reverence for the larger system, not simply a desire to exploit it for your advantage, along with a role as a mediator between the community and the spirit-world. If that is the sort of thing you're looking for, an "Urban Shaman" Dreamspeaker inspired by the Glass Walker material cited above would likely be more appropriate. "Pure" capitalism is a bit at odds with the idea of a shamanistic character in this sense, but someone who played the role of "mediator" between people and "the market" might try to achieve "balance" through things like regulation, non-profit work, etc.
