    As someone that gripes plenty about CtD, and vastly prefers Lost (I have issues with DA Fae and Mage that are somewhat separate), I do bring this gripe up a lot when it comes up. Part of the problem is that it is such an immediate impact on play. When you crack open the book and read and get lots of ideas of what you might want to play, but then get to the character creation part and find out that your Kith options are incredibly limited... it kind of stops you in your tracks. You don't even get into all the other issues the game has because most of those are ones that come up in play as you go.

    That is, barring the age old tone/art issues that are another big gripe about CtD.

    I honestly don't see how it's any less restrictive then having to choose from Kindred Clans or Garou Tribes.
    The biggest issue is the way they're handled. The Frailties in particular are a big culprit here. A Clan, mechanically, is a set of powers you get cheaper and a weakness. The Clans have a very big spread of options there, and you have to work pretty hard to come up with a concept that really falls flat with any of them.

    Garou Tribes are an example of how a few variables can vastly increase your options. Tribe only defines a fraction of your character's mechanics. Auspice and Breed also have a large impact. As long as it fits the fluff of WtA... it is a really big stretch to come up with a concept that you can't slot into a Tribe/Auspice/Breed combo of some sort.

    The problem with CtD is you run into really basic concepts that get cut out very quickly. You want to play a wise Owl changeling? Not going to happen. If you go Pooka you have to accept all the trickster and lying stuff that you don't care about (and is contrary to being the wise sage font of knowledge). If you go something like Boggan or Slaugh, the Owl part is lost.

The base splat (Seeming) is very broad and open to interpretation. And you get a free list of things you can pick from to support your interpretation. And the list isn't exhaustive. Making your own if none of the list fits is something you're encouraged to do.

                                    Well, end of the day, C20 will be, at the hardest, a revision of Changeling: the Dreaming, not a total re-write (and thank goodness, the last thing the game needs is a fourth version - 1st and 2nd edition really are that different).

                                    Arguing that it needs to be like Dark Ages: Fae is like arguing that M20 needs to redo its magic system to that of DA: Mage. You might like it better, and i have no opinion either way on that... but the gamelines do have a story, and that story says that magic is harder now than it was then. paradox beats Mages across the face now, and banality restricts the Fae. Could even say it's the Dreaming's punishment against the changelings for having broken their oaths to not mingle blood with mortals, if you want the historical tie-in.

                                    That said, the kiths COULD use a little re-working. 2nd edition is very... second-edition-y, because of how it goes "this kith is always like this." Right down to what members of the kith wear. That's very easily corrected, of course.

                                    Some level of customization wouldn't be amiss - not full-out modularity, but something to make the kith more archetype and less stereotype. For instance, Boggans are a "household spirit" archetype, the genius locii of fields, hearths, mills and such. Why would a Boggan hailing from the dreams of a welsh bwg be identical to the boggan derived from a Swedish tomte or a Russian bannik? Very similar, sure, enough that they would consider each other brothers... but not identical. whether it's fluff about different Kithain "tribes" (similar to how Corax do) or full-on crunch, works for me.

                                    Second... Can we get a few more Arts, or some variety on what's there? Maybe even a few kith-specific arts (yes, we have Chronos, Infusion, and... and... urk, hurp, Talecraft). It was always a little bewildering that the supposedly most magical of the WoD's inhabitants... actually get the least variety in their magic. Much of the variety that is there is behind locked doors (These arts are for Nobles only. These Arts are Shadow Court only. These arts are mer only, etc.)

                                    Also... bring balance back towards Seelie / Unseelie. First things first, present the Seelie and Unseelie takes on kiths as equal, rather than the trend of one or othe other being "anomalous" - there's no reason in the world for trolls - trolls, for crying out loud - to be so seelie it makes your teeth hurt, with the Unseelie among htem a mistrusted minority. Make the pslit between the two "no big deal" for the commoner kith - they've been living this way for centuries, after all. And while the books do say "seelie is not good nor is unseelie evil," in practice it doesn't pan out; the seelie are depicted as good guys, the unseelie as bad guys. Part of this is the legacy system; Seelie legacies are all clearly positive or at least benign, while Unseelie legacies are... anything but. An idea I pitched to my changeling ST, was to make [i]each[/] Legacy have both a Seelie and Unseelie face.
