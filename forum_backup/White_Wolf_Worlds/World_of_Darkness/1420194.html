                              See, this is what I'm thinking. My picture of the prehistoric era is one where the proportion of Mages to Sleepers was way different than in a world whose population is approaching eight billion. In contemporary WoD, there are no natural predators left that threaten humans, at least not any that wouldn't die of paradox in an attempt to hunt openly. Apathy rules, according to the books. Apathy is soul-eroding. Sleepers in default WoD, it could be suggested, have weaker Avatars. It sounds rather judgemental, but so is saying that apathy rules.

                              In a world where a prehistoric man uses the blessing of a Verbena sorceress to cross the territory of an Umbrood predator to return to his young love, Sleepers' avatars would be stronger. If you advance that to the era before Order of Reason got going, the parallel world would still have Sleepers with stronger avatars, more mages per-capita.

                              Since it is rather hard to bring armies of sleepers through a Sidestep Time or Spirit effect, there might actually be a balance that would prevent the Technocracy from invading the parallel world. Add to that the fact that not all mystic creatures were predators on humans. The Traditions would certainly have some dragons (a la game of Thrones) and giants who are their allies. I realize I did not discuss conflict between the parallel worlds' ruling Mage factions in the initial question. It is something I am considering in relation to population and capacity to raise defenses.

                              Remind me again. Isn't the order of hermes based on mystery cults, cloistered in their towers?

                              Seems like they aren't the kind of guys who would push 'disseminate magic to the common man'.
                              They are described in all kinds of ways, because they go all the way back to Ars Magica. If each person is supposed to discover a special word that represents them, it might be hard to teach every single person how to use Magick according to their unique individual perspective. It's also about passing difficult tests. They were competitive.

                              Mage has an offense-default. If you meet another Mage and you can't easily identify them as being part of your faction, at the very least, you will want to cast an effect that stops them from shredding your pattern first. The Hermetics had the Parma-Magica, a spell that temporarily cuts you off from casting, but makes you immune to that of another Mage. That's why they became so dominant, compared to other Traditions.

                              The invention of the printing press makes mystery cults difficult to sustain. The UK government used to have Freemasons throughout it. This interfered with the normal operation of government, because knowing the rhyme-scheme gave you a guarantee of passing the selection interview when someone more competent did not. Newspapers blew that wide open. The British public wanted a functioning government that served citizens equally, and so Masons were purged from the ranks of the civil service. Now, books about formerly secret rites of introduction can be borrowed from almost any public library.

                              Appeal by exclusivity still draws people into that sort of thing. It is just that, using Masons as an example, why would most men want to be part of a group that places demands on your time, where there is no chance of meeting women? Whatever downside a group would have previously been able to hide from potential recruits, the printing press ensures will come to light. If you can get the last mystery from the library, there is no reason to regularly sneak off to the lodge and debase yourself. We assume that Masons in WoD would have an overlap of Hermetics, NWO and others competing to run the organization.

                              The Verbena use a degree of appeal by exclusivity. At least there may be dating prospects - sex magick, for example. Returning to the subject of population growth, maybe sex-Magick would be essential to that goal. Druidic rituals to bless warriors before confronting the rampaging giant. Hit it with lightning to soften it up. Sacrifice the giant to promote fertility.

                                      Originally posted by Accelerator View Post
                                      Question.

                                      ..... Ok, its been a long time since I read the books. Is there any philosophical or mental or just plain ass exclusivity needed?

                                      In the eyes of an akashic, verbena, or hermetic. Do you have to be 'special' in any way to use Magick?
                                      It's all about the Avatar. The avatar is a special gift. Mentors teach Focus to Mages who have already been visited by their Avatar. There are some other ways things can happen.

                                      1. The avatar hasn't appeared yet, but is destined to arrive and the precogniscient mentor eagerly recruits the soon-to-be Mage.

                                      2. With great effort or good luck, a mentor turns a sleeper who would not have awakened into a Mage. The teaching creates the circumstance under which the otherwise-absent avatar shows up.

                                      3. Let's say FACADE just shipped a vat-grown human over to The NWO's Mind-crafting facility. They craft a personality and reference-memory for the new person. They are not awakened or gifted with visitation from their avatar. The Operatives need awakened Mages right now. In this case, the mentor does something drastic to force awakening to occur. The avatar shows up in response.

                                      Besides having an Avatar, there are all kinds of ways a person could be Special that don't guarantee a chance at Awakening. The easiest criteria to use is Intelligence. If the person has lived in a Consensus that excludes a particular kind of Magick, high intelligence could make them harder to be trained into awakening by a well intentioned mentor. They have internalized the Consensus Paradigm and their high intelligence makes them a better skeptic, preventing the mentor from imparting wisdom. When filling out a Mage character sheet, as long as you have determined this character is going to be a Mage, there is no minimum number of dots you need to add to any stat, beyond what the rules prescribe.

                                      For the convenience of running a game, all the players are recruited by mentors of the same faction (Technocracy/Traditions). The awakening happens and it is within one faction's territory or they receive a call to go to a special place. The players work together.

                                      As a storyteller, I see it on a timeline where the character's Avatar visits them for the first time, initiating a Seeking, a sort of mission. The experience opens them to understanding that they can change things with their will. The Questing Avatar might only need to appear once, but has the highest single demand, "Save the world", "Build a space colony on Mars". It's not the end goal, but striving to achieve this that makes the Mage advance in his Arete and Spheres. Pattern, I think, shows up when you are done the last Seeking to give you a new one built on the achievements of the last - well ordered, achievable goals. Dynamic - change the world around you, make a difference in the life of someone stuck in a rut. Primordial, you are an incarnation of a concept, so go represent that in the world.

                                      The character may have done some Magick-type things before being aware of their avatar. They survived a car accident that should have killed them, having unconsciously or reflexively used an effect.

                                      There are ways that you can make a Special thing about the person a driver of the Awakening.
                                      Akashic: Early adversity breeds future success.
                                      Verbena - Gardeners of The Tree: They have a hereditary gift for Magick.
                                      Hermetic: A gift for language or math.

                                                  Extraordinary Citizens. They're capable of using Wonders (and, in MRev, were also capable of learning Linear “techno-sorcery”), but lack the fully versatility of Enlightened Science.

                                                  Arguably, the trick that the Order of Reason hit upon was the ability to efficiently distill Enlightened Procedures into Wonders or Paths that could be used by their Extraordinary Citizens (or whatever they called them back then), and then to distill them even further to the point that they were fully integrated into the Consensus and could be used by anyone simply through the application of appropriate Abilities and gear, with no need for Spheres or Paths.

                                                  Whether or not that's restricted to the trappings of technology depends on whether the Storyteller thinks that the Technocracy might be right and that Enlightened Science takes the forms it does because it's building on the foundational truth of reality. If not, then there's no reason in principle why the aforementioned distillation from Sphere to Path to “low magic” couldn't be applied to the Hermetic Focus.
