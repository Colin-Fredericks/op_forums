                Originally posted by mark View Post
                what are the rules for embracing supernaturals?
                The basic rule is: Prevent Power Creep!

                Like many, I plunged into the World of Darkness like a Sperm Whale preparing to hunt squid at the bottom of the ocean. There I reveled in its Epic story. That epic story, weaving together all the facets of Creation, Preservation, and Destruction (Wild, Weaver, and Wyrm) from the formation of the loom to the intricate details rendered by the multi-colored threads is the story I enjoy the most. But there is a problem when you play in the games of gods - it is world wrecking, paradigm pummelling, universe vaporizing power. And what can destroy a universe can not only end, but destroy a story.

                In the original game the greatest representation of this Power Creep was a man named, <drum roll please>: Samuel Haight. He was designed to be that really OMG NPC that everyone could really get behind hating. His background combined knowledge from and stolen power of Garou, Vampires, and Mages. He was a kinfolk-ghoul-sorceror who through an artifact acquired an arete rating. His obliteration was a necessity of the game, albeit the PCs were "mystically" able to render him neutralized.

                It is not impossible for crossover characters to be created which carry the flavor of their origin, by maintaining what I will call their genre. For example, the Kiasyd are a fusion of vampire and fae - but they are vampires only. The Gangrel are vampire skin changers so to speak. The Tremere are an example of what mages could become. And if a wraith or geist could be imbued into vampiric form, it would be some deviant bloodline of Cappadocius no doubt with appropriate necromantic powers. However, only one template can be dominant as a means of preventing more Sam Haights. (Oh, the horror of conceiving him in a plurality of being.)

                So, there are ways to handle cross over characters, but the new Template dominates. All powers inspired from the old genre must be performed inherently with the supernatural advantage and style of the new template. This means changing their description entirely to something appropriate to the new genre and balancing it in both story and mechanics. As a werewolf changes shape because of the innate qualities of what they are, a vampire changes shape through harnessing the Curse of Caine in their blood. The newly turned werewolf must embrace that curse, fall further into the corruption they were born to hate, in order to advance their disciplines. What effects will that self-loathing descent into madness bludgeon upon them? In developing rituals to emulate effects similar to previously held gifts - what corrupted form will such a ritual take so as to starkly manifest as a mockery anathema to what is Garou?

                The story of one embraced (against their will?) - becoming something they would not otherwise be, is a wonderful opportunity to explore. But what is the purpose in the exploration? If it is only to be a powerful baddy, then I would scrap it and do something else. If it is to divine the moral culpabilities and brilliant but brief exposures of the good and the better, then tell the story in those merits. Afterall, it is not the powers which make the story, but the character and the crucible; The other is just CGI on the brain.
