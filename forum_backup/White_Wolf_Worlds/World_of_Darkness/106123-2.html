        I actually liked the Milton-inspired outlook on Demon but there were some things I disliked both in the Fluff and in the Mechanics.

        Fluff, I absolutely hated that DtF tried to steal the big guys of other lines for themselves, like both Charon and Nhudri. I also think a mistake was made in ignoring the Umbra altogether. Also since they went the Milton/Abrahamic route they could still have kept it a bit more open ended and mysterious, as in God could easily have been presented in a way that makes It to be a being composed of Stasis, Entropy and Dynamism aka Weaver, Wyrm and Wyld balanced, naturally in a away that it can be interpreted either way. Also I hated the fact that they basically lost almost all memories of themselves under a flood of human memories when possessing unless they had heavily invested in Legacy background. They are the creators of all things, even if the Faith to empower their work did came from a God, and should not be so easily suppressed.

        Also while I did like the Abrahamic take it did make it undesirable for some, even players who happily played In Nomine on both sides, so obviously it did something wrong even if I can't really put my finger on what.

        Also rather importantly the Fluff describes them as rather powerful beings, nearly on par with Quantum 6 or 7 Aberrants at the highest and even in the lowest at least on par with basic PC of that awesome game, able to create mountains, to destroy species or actually be the Sun etc. Unfortunately the Mechanics are totally unable to come anywhere near what the promises that the fluff makes. Yes, I get it. They are without their unlimited Faith supply and fresh out of imprisonment but still the Lore's ranged from kinda nearing awesome to being kinda sad.

        Which leads us to mechanics.

        Lore's were like said on many parts rather disappointing and on others quite nice indeed on a normal WoD scale that is but they still lacked the feeling of playing as one of the creators of our world. They really did not evoke that kind of might to me. They tried to bring that kind of might back on the table in the Earthbound book with the Mastery Background but that was only usable by the Earthbound and while it did make it possible for the m to level cities with one evocation it still only acted to boost the existing Lore's not to expand them or make them more evocative of the fluffs promises. So while it did what it was meant to do with the Earthbound, made them extremely powerful and dangerous, it did nothing to help your average Fallen to feel more angelic or demonic.
        Also the aforementioned Legacy background was a horrid invention. Basically required to take unless you wished to play a Fallen who did not know that it was anything other than a Human with fancy magical powers. It was mentioned earlier in this thread but Demon also forgot to handle 6+ stats and High Faith in general with excess incoming Faith apparently just lost. An enterprising Demon can easily get a rather nice income of Faith but there really was little point in doing so since most of the time it was all just wasted, which kinda screwed over whole of the Faustian agenda of gathering pacts and so being able to channel huge amounts of Faith. And as a final Mechanical problem, they were kinda squishy like someone else already mentioned, but at least they could be summoned back or could just switch Host.

        Switching Hosts was also something left a bit open ended by the way. Your starting character has his abilities and attributes coming mainly from the human being possessed so what happens to your non Lore, non Faith stats when you switch host? Logically fluff would suggest you learning the abilities and gaining the attributes of the new host which basically means almost completely different character, especially if your low on Legacy in which case you now remember always having been the new host with bits and pieces of previous existence. Still it was a matter kinda glossed over even though it was an important part of the game. All of this even more true if you didn't find a suitable host and fell back into the Abyss and were re-summoned.

        All in all while I liked DtF and wanted to like it even more, and still use some parts of it in NPC's and other things, I still ended up preferring Devil's Due which with a single book beat the whole DtF line. DD had potential if only it had gained a few more books. It took a bit different approach to Demons, in general Demons were a bit more Demonic in it, and tied your memory to your Torment and along with your memory much of your power. If you couldn't remember being able to do something you weren't. Of course it too had it's own problems, it's Arcana were on par with Disciplines and such powers and again did not carry real Demonic oomph but it still worked and had a nice Demonic tone to it, but at least the Arcana were meant to be customized and be modular in general so play groups could easily come up with greater powers if need be, and they were still somewhat squishy. Basically it was a whole new edition of Demon in a book that had almost half of it's word count dedicated to Infernalists and Demonologists instead of being mainly about Demons themselves so it was a truly epic achievement.

        The Theme difference between DtF and DD can be summed up to be that the former is about Fallen Angels and the latter about Demons. And yes even though both are technically the same thing the mood difference is huge.

        A second edition of DtF would be nice especially if it took into heart the lessons learned from DD.

                Disclaimer: DtF was the only WoD game I really liked, a brilliant games setting! So my opinion IS subjective....

                Points of criticism:
                • The Judeo-Christian / Miltonean interpretation of the demons put of many people. If you are bothered by it, for whatever reason, it is not your game. If you want multiple culture interpretation of demons, you won't like it either.
                • DtF is best as a standalone game. It does not play well with other WoD lines. The reasons are many, the point is it plays best if it stands alone. You can try to incorporate it with other lines, there are many threads around about the hows, but I found it not worth the trouble. Forget the "unified" Wod setting.
                • DtF mechanics are not elegant. The DtF tried to implement a similar currency/moral system as the other WoD lines, but it did not really succeed. Even after the Player's handbook errata, many problems remain. Like the Torrent system. Also the moral framework with the three Virtues felt forced for me. There was a disconnect between the rules and the fiction.
                • Lore are limited. I understand that the Fallen are limited in power, especially compared to their original being. But limiting Lores is anti-climatic. Only one of the seven Houses can open a lock (Lore of Portals, first power). Why? And why is it so difficult to learn them. I understand that some houses are experts on in some lores, that is ok. The limits felt very artificial.
                • While the setting is a blast, some minor details are missing. Like the demon uses his hosts physical attributes, but what about the intellectual ones? Not a big deal, but still.
                • "WoD habits" are bad for DtF. Like the demonic courts, mirroring the vampirics ones. Well, it is quite bland and does not really fit well and is best omitted. Also the four political groupings are a bit bland, lacking detail.
                • Metaplot...'nough said. Drop it out of the game, it does nothing to improve the game, nor the setting.
                • Rushed supplements. The supplements are either great or meh. Most of them feel a bit rushed, with page XX in them, "because of space limits we could not...", etc.

                That is all I can think of. None of them are really crippling though. Hope it helps.

                                                            So a lot of people here are citing the "Miltonic" aspects of Demon: the Fallen, but really D:tF is more a Byronic misreading of Paradise Lost.

                                                            The Devil isn't heroic in Paradise Lost. He isn't even anti-heroic as Byron put forth. He's the straight up villain, as anyone who's read the first chapter of the poem can attest.

                                                            Milton did a fantastic job in writing the part of Lucifer. The Devil is so persuasive that we find ourselves caught up in his rhetoric, to the point that we view him as a noble rebel rather than an insolent traitor. You have to remind yourself that in the context of Milton, Lucifer is the prince of lies. He's manipulative, scheming, and you're hearing his side of the story.

                                                            There were two main objections I had to D:tF: the first stems from this fundamental misreading of Milton, the conceit that the Fallen were somehow moved to rebel out of love and compassion for humanity rather than arrogance and scorn for the divine (Lucifer was motivated by the sin of Pride in Paradise Lost). It's a novel idea, and I don't mind that they put a little twist on things, but it gave Demons a degree of moral righteousness that frankly didn't mesh well with the core concept of the game (playing a tormented fallen angel - it just loses a lot of it's punch if you were cast down because God was a dick.)

                                                            The second issue I had was with Hell itself. Hell was just plain boring. Hell was nothing - a void - literally a bunch of fallen angels all packed in a dark room for eons and eons all forming cliques and arguing over what happened to Lucifer. Anyone who's read Dante's Inferno knows how rich a landscape Hell can be - and they phoned it in with Hell in D:tF.
