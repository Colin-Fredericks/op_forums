                                                            And I think that's cool, but also understand why the orthodox might complain.

                                                            About the "Narrative Mechanic". The very concept of such it's anathema, because any mechanic can be abused by its technicians . WoD was created with the absolute and undisputed power of the Storyteller in mind. You can use to set your players free, to chain them, or to have a middle ground (say, by tailoring Ur-Shulgi to the players. If the players are expected to have Time Stop magic, Of Course that Ur-Shulgi has created a ritual that protects against that. You are protected by the fact that the guy hasn't a "normal" character sheet, until you give him one).

                                                            Of course that the players haven't solid rules (that they could abuse) to give them "Narrative Powers". If the Storyteller wants, he can give an Artifact or something to "empower" the players into doing the impossible. The Storyteller has many tools to make any sort of number crunching useless. I think than that's the point (because I've read such reasoning in many of the books. It may be a wrong reasoning, of course.)

                                                            And all of this isn't to weaken the simulationism:

                                                            Helios it's the supreme spirit of the Sun, necessary for it's existence, and the basic book of Werewolf states that destroying Helios should be as difficult as destroying the actual Sun, if not more.

                                                            How many "life points" has the Sun?, how big has to be your stick to destroy it?.

                                                            THAT might explain why the ortodox might find more than a little wierd to defeat such a power with the "average" damage that can be caused by a furry, or by a guy with Strength 10 or so. If you preffer to make spirits much weaker than that, it's OK (specially in Demon, that it's a different game, with different asumptions) For Werewolf, however, it's more unorthodox than playing a Salubri with three heads.

                                                            I always took the "(Blank celestines existence is necessary for (blank) physical objects existence" with a grain of salt. Sure destroying luna may be as hard as destroying the actual moon, sure destroying her may collapse the entire "lunar realm, sure it may alter the flow of prime around the moon so much it becomes a "no magic" zone, but I don't believe the rock floating in the sky somehow needs a cosmic caretaker to survive. That's like saying if a random spirit attached itself to the concept of harry potter and died, then harry potter would have never existed(and yes I do realize the "In a universe with magic.." arguement, but we don't have cosmic caretakers in RL so why are they necessary somewhere that functions nearly the same?)

                                                            then again I personally believe that celestines and above are just spirits that are so powerful they've become a spirit "Realms" of sorts(the wyrm Is malfeas, Gaia Is the penumbra, etc.) and celestines are just in the process of turning(like a tree thats roots reach deep into the ground, making it unable to move, but hasn't yet truely become a "part" of it.)
