      Kinda?

      The problem is the circumstances where it matters are kinda limited in either applicability or in fun.

      If you're running a game set in Rome or Byzantium in the classical era, food and how people interacted with it in their daily lives would have been very different... but also not. There are just certain practicalities to urban life even in ancient times that cause food history to rhyme if not repeat. Once you have cities, you have lower class people that need to eat but aren't agricultural workers that feed themselves along with paying their rent to their local ruler(s). Cities are trade hubs that bring in cuisines, staple foods, and spices from a much wider number of areas than was previously available. However, that's also a level of immersion a lot of people don't want to have to remember in play, just like they don't want to have to study the languages of the time period either.

      In many regards eating customs are more important than specific food items because food is a big part of human social interactions (and there is a noticeable psychological impact of human customs with how taste is perceived).

      But yeah, sure, for the tiny handful of characters in the WoD that are both long lived enough to have experienced multiple waves of different cuisines dominate a given area, and actually been focused enough on food despite probably not needing it if they're immortal on that level, it could be an interesting quirk/hobby for the character to indulge in and give them a bit more flavor (pun intended) in what sort of things occupy their time when they're not doing important plot stuff. In a modern day chronicle, this is rarely going to be the PCs, so it's probably an NPC... which means again, the players need to enjoy this particular character's love of food history and epicurean adventures.

              Even for the Wraith, you're in trouble if your Passion is "comfort foods from my youth," but less so if your Passion is, "exploring culinary experiences."

              But even then, there are foods that humans have been eating for as long as we can prove what people actually eating. Otzi was found with jerky in his stomach. So humans have been making jerky for at least 5,000 years. It also gets really dependent on how specific you are.

              It's kinda like the Ventrue feeding flaw in that sense. In theory it could be so broad some Ventrue barely notice it, while others have such specific tastes they probably don't last long as vampires. But most PCs are going to be some reasonable place in the middle of the spectrum.

              -----------

              While I rarely recommend reading comments sections, the third video has people from Germany and Austria pointing out that all of those so-called "lost" foods are common place in their equivalents of farmers markets. They might not be popular in franchise supermarkets, but there's enough people that buy them for farmers to make sure that they keep growing them on a small scale basis alongside more profitable crops.

              Things like the Romans eating Silphium into extinction is extremely rare. Most foods keep being grown and eaten even if they fall out of fashion in mainstream culture and finding it gets harder; it rarely gets impossible. It usually means the foods go back to being local food items instead of things grown/traded over large areas because they're popular in other cultures.
