                Originally posted by Psicose View Post
                Look, I already saw that I moved the wasp's nest a little more than I should, so, unless the ADM releases me to speak, I better stop here because I don't want to be banned.
                Uh huh.

                The thing of the matter about this is that I don't think it's impossible to talk about the way modern sociological forces can probably have a negative effect on the way something like World of Darkness performs. Ripping off the bandaid you want to dance around, I think it's fair to say that "the woke left", particularly the more casual/less critical declarants of that movement, probably aren't going to be as interested in World of Darkness as they were in the 90s. Paradox's mishandling of the franchise asides as playing to those forces go asides, it's fair to say that the general attitude that made World attractive to progressives have shifted-a lot of the coding, accidental or deliberate, no longer plays as strongly in an era where a lot of what was previously demonized in the 90s are now much more openly accepted modernly, if still fiercely and virulently fought against.

                When you have queer people and persons of color taking the active heroric lead in shows and movies and books and games, when you have fictions that explore socialist themes far more openly and neutrally, when the space for more united globalist interests are being taken seriously rather than just a prop, it can be easy to understand why that audience may not want to play games that lean into the monstrous and complex morally grey as representation. Tastes in fiction, for that audience, has hewn towards having more unabated heroic takes alongside their interests and concerns, rooted in the power fantasy-maybe not uncomplicated power fantasy, but power fantasy all the same-and towards friendlier, laid-back community building.* All of this can be argued as a reason that that portion of the media-buying audience probably wouldn't come in droves to buy World of Darkness.

                But leaning as far into it as you have overestimates a number of excesses that need to be at play in order for your worry to happen. World modernly just isn't large enough to pay attention to, and the majority of the "woke left" paying attention are largely people who discovered their political grounding from these games back when they were teens and World was the hot and edgy new kid on the block, and a good number of those who don't are still pretty quick to pick up on the humanist and empathetic elements of the game. Even if there was a reactionary blowover from the particular portion of "the woke left" you're trying to point at, the worst that'd happen is that some socials would probably just have to be quiet for a bit, or otherwise issue an explanation. It'd be business as usual from there-and honestly, such a debacle would only just open how much ideology and exploration of politics there are already in the games**, that would honestly just lead to it being an increasingly attractive option for those in that political spectrum.

                The reason you're worried about getting a ban openly talking about the political forces that factor into what may or may not happen with World going forward is rooted in the same things that has overblown your reaction reaction to "the woke left" in this situation, in a such a way that can only be communicated through marked demonization. It's fair to admit that the political landscape of the modern day has an impact on the buying habits of the roleplaying community, but it's another to basically just go "The woke left would cancel World of Darkness if they even got a wiff of the Sabbat." That's just not looking at the whole picture with an eye towards fairness and giving the due diligence to the humanity of the people who are in the political forces you're wanting to talk about.

                Anyways, all of this is to really say "If you don't want to have to dance around a subject, maybe don't even try to." If you wanna free your demons, call them by their name, if you don't, then just stay away from them.

                *Meanwhile, the right side of the spectrum, to give due diligence, has had their room for the sort of experiences they'd like from fiction already expanded by a lot, so there's less competition, and in the tabletop sphere, while DnD has only gotten more "woke", the biggest game in this hobby is still pretty good at facilitating the desires and needs of a politicially right-leaning audience (and there's still more attractive options than that!), and the portion that had their shit-kicking boots on for stuff like this couldn't even really make much of a difference when the franchise had an active murderer invested in World of Darkness and otherwise spinning up bad PR. Their Hot Coffee moment has come and passed, and odds are they'd never really be able to kick up another one-though notable, they do still look for such.

                **and again, due diligence to the right, there's also a lot that's attractive towards right-leaning audiences in World of Darkness, and in the hypothetical we're addressing, there'd be a marked interest in selling up the game in that way, which would likely also see increased sales. World of Darkness is no stranger to having intense internal political discussion, even if the long-term audience tends to hew left to any number of degrees.

                Kelly R.S. Steele, Freelance Writer(Feel free to call me Kelly, Arcane, or Arc)
                The world is not beautiful, therefore it is.-Keiichi Sigsawa, Kino's Journey
                Feminine pronouns, please.
