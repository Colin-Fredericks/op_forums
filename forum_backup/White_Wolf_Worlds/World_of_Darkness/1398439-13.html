                Originally posted by Pleiades View Post
                me, I just wish they'd focus less on the umbra and tried to make the mundane world less mundane,
                maybe have some interesting urban mysteries, hiding in plain sight
                I like to have the umbra and the realm cross over to explain some of the weird stuff. A reminder that the spirit and material world was once one, and sometimes is again. Just enough to be weird while allowing the evidence to go away if the police ever show up (a common trope in horror, anyway).

                Typically all the cool stuff happens in the place where the borders between worlds are weak - caerns, haunts, nodes, freeholds, etc. I just have more varieties of them. And don't always make distinctions in my explanations to the PCs, or use the mechanical terms. I like to use euphemisms and alternate explanations.

                    The high fantasy awesome of the umbra doesn't quite have the human element of the material world. You are dealing with caricatures that represent concepts, not people*. You don't have complicated, realistic plots with flawed characters, you have morality tales. I think one of the strengths of Vampire is that the umbras are peripheral; never centre stage. They are there for a rare few characters, and perhaps for one story of the week. Most mage characters shouldn't touch the umbras; they don't have the spheres for it. It's only werewolf where you should actually spend significant time in the umbra; and even then, only half.

                    *There are a few places in the umbra that do have realistic people. All the ones i've read up on are hells.

                                    I didn't like WTA. The books were mostly fine, but each session I played the pen & paper felt just odd. Perhaps eco-terrorism / raging / tail wagging / packs / Alpha / Beta / Metis / howl to the moon / Grinous didn't mix well. But even worse it got when you saw them as part of the World of Darkness in modern times. I think the whole setting might perhaps work in a pre industrial time and without Vampires & Mages. But now? To fit them in imho. the Glasswalkers were created, the tribe which leads the whole WTA theme Ad Absurdum.
                                    ---
                                    I have to admit that Mages don't mix in well, too; but with them it wasn't (mostly) a problem of fitting in or not, but the balance of powers. Ok, the Technocracy might keep Tradition Mages low and Paradox also helps with it. And perhaps the Technocracy hasn't made Tabula Rasa with all others because they really² don't like the use of vulgar magick. *Cought* Iteration X & Void Engineers *cought*. But then there are still the Nephandi & Marauders; and if you really think about it (or if you have played enough mixed WoD games with VTM, WTA & MTA in the same scene) one really, really has to wonder why the world does not go to hell or awakens in pure Chaos on a monthly basis. I can't even say who was more overpowered.. Marauders against any Mages and Nephandi against any others?
                                    btw. balance issues.. this brings me to Baali and any Clan able to use (Dark) Thaumaturgy.. So if we go the dark path anyway let's get some pacts with Demon Lords for good eh I mean bad measures. Still not overpowered enough? Then let's go to the True Brujah and Temporis. The explanation why there is still a balance at all is in most cases the same. Because there are only so few (left) of them. Because so many will (try to) destroy them.. Uhm.. okay, that sounds "very plausible", especially when survival of the fittest is one of the main settings in the game. Temporis controls the time itself. To fight a being which might control time, even in limited ways, is in the best cases difficult. And while all other Clans multiply these with the really powerful disciplines don't, because...?
                                    Manipulation of time and how to not be able to loose fights if you do it brings us back to Mages.
                                    Mages against Vampires or Werewolves.. oh my.. As good as any Sphere might be used as an absolute killer for both. Ok, there are some which are not direct final deadly; but with these you just have to be a bit more creative (like Life against Vampires; go against their meals instead). Against Werewolves it's even worse. So the trick for both might perhaps be the good old principle "kill the Mage first". If Mages could not also learn manipulation of Correspondence and Time.
                                    ---
                                    Where I have to disagree with most people here is in regard of the Week of Nightmares. I both liked it and saw sense in it; at least if you also see the events in the world of Wraiths. The WoD is a slightly changed mirror of our reality. The reaction taken by the Technocracy are just a logical step with technologies which do exist. Sun mirrors & Nukes. I don't really get why the storm which shielded the Antediluvian could not be stopped with any other Magic means (like: Freeze him eternal in time. Teleport him with Correspondence into the center of Earth or into the Sun. Put some mega-tonnes of indestructible matter on him or just transform his lifeless body into anything you like (have you seen my chick Antediluvian chair yet ? Use entropy to impend massive doom and soak any defense he might have (AFTER the roll). Use Prime to draw the power out of the magic/disciplines which power the storm. And these are just ways which first got into my mind.. Oh I forgot, use mind to just let him sleep again. Yes, as an Antediluvian he may use counter magic. But we are talking here over an concentrated effort of the whole damn Technocracy. And if Tradition mages sometimes help out in fighting Marauders & Nephandi, guess what happens if the oldest of the Suckers wake up? But okay, if you also look what happened on the other side of the Shroud with the Wraiths it begins to fit at least a bit better and it also leads to the last trilogy, which is one of mine absolute favorites of all trilogies, both in the WoD as well as without . The only very bad thing was that it also leads to the end of my most loved RP world.. But what an end it was. In good books some or even all Characters die. And in some of the best the Earth or just anything has to die therefore. Never was the saying with one crying and one smiling eye better fitting. But I digressed.
                                    ---
                                    Clanbook Baali.. I can't say I liked it, but it had to stand out of all the other horrors to be "extra evil" and that's not easy to do. Nevertheless it was bad enough that I did not read all the stories, which in other Clanbooks were the part I most enjoyed.

                                                Clanbook Baali was good. The intro was a little juvenile and i recognise some people can't deal with that sorta stuff and it's not entirely necessary even for a black dog book (it does set the tone rather well), but it's the best writeup they got.

                                                I really don't like the idea they've pushed later on which suggests Baali are the vampire equivelent of nephandi or bsds. They might be a bloodline of evil and attrocity, but they don't have inverted souls, it's a very human, religious evil they practice. The embrace is not a caul, apostacy is not a caul. You don't get your soul inverted because the wrong bloodline embraced you. It's a nice in-universe perspective to believe all baali are irredeemable, fundamentally deranged monsters hated by God and damned for all eternity upon embrace regardless of who they are, but it should never be the truth-of-the-universe just because you're uncomfortable with the idea of nuerotypical religious extremists who practice a very foul faith.

                                                (right, yeah, I'm not going to make the claim that they're all neurotypical, most Baali are going to embrace people who'll fit in, IE high functioning psychopaths, but the Baali also target very moral and faithful people to see them slide, kinda like nosferatu attraction to targeting beautiful people, and people deal with trauma differently. If Baali could just make nice priests evil upon embrace it'd be very shallow and ridiculous. It's far better to have that priest struggle against his new Baali reality as the ordeals he faces take their toll. ) .
