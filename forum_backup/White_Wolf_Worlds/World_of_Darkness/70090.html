    Here's what bugs me about the WoD...

    02-11-2014, 06:40 PM
    In another thread,

    Originally posted by Baaldam View Post
    I know it is a Masquerade game you are working on but Requiem for Rome and Ancient Mysteries are two modules that bring up some very interesting ideas when it comes to changes in kindred society through different periods or cultures. Check it out if you can.
    Nightstalkers, for Hunter the Vigil, discussing hunter groups through the ages and their varied interactions with the undead is also very much worth a look.
    That sorta seems like a common thing in the WoD now - a sort of "let's steal from nWoD!" (Or vice versa, I suppose, but I don't like nWoD, so I don't care what they do over there.) Anyway. I don't have a problem with any specific suggestion to borrow from elsewhere, per se, so much as the pervasiveness. Is it a good idea? Maybe. Hell, I'll even say probably! But isn't it sometimes just better to say, "That's how the nWoD would do it, sure, but let's go this way." It just seems like it encourages a sort of creative and intellectual laziness that bothers me.

    Okay rant over. I'd like your thoughts on things, though.

    Edit: I don't mean to call you out specifically, Baaldam - your note just happened to be the one I was reading.

                          It's a ridiculous complaint because mining other games for ideas is a valid, if not good, suggestion unless there is something specifically problematic with the game referenced. Suggesting people read FATAL for good ideas is... not going to go over well; and justifiably so because that book has serious issues on almost all levels. Suggesting nWOD sources on a thread that starts with a disclaimer of the OP's utter hatred of the nWOD and absolute lack of interest in it is rude.

                          The idea that the nWOD gets suggested at lot as something to mine for ideas for cWOD games is inherently problematic? That's pretty much ridiculous. They're related systems, with related themes, and the nWOD has gotten to explore a few concepts that the cWOD didn't. There is no rational reason to have a problem with cWOD threads being about, or including, mining the nWOD for ideas. Doing shit just because it isn't the way the nWOD does it when we're talking about developing content for our own personal game-play experiences is flat out bad advice. Maybe the nWOD was is good for what you want, maybe it isn't. Going the other way on general principle means you are putting dislike of the nWOD over what might be best for your game. Which is fine if that's a personal preference, but it is ridiculous to consider as a larger scale problem.

                          VtR, for example, has a supplement that has some pretty big support for being a great supplement even for non-WOD games. Damnation City is seen by a chunk of people as a wonderful city building source book, even after you cut out the minimal Vampire specific parts of it. It is only game-line specific book I would flat out suggest to anyone running the nWOD because the content is just that applicable if you want a book's help on developing a city for your game.

                          And to address your last question:

                          Requiem is heavily informed by Masquerade. Though that goes in a few directions (and has changed a bit in expression over the past decade). Requiem has aspects that are clearly hold overs that the designers thought were worth maintaining from Masquerade, and things that were rather blatantly changed because of how they worked in Masquerade in application. On top of new ideas that came from other sources.

                                              Originally posted by Big Brother View Post
                                              My problem is that with VtR you're taking VtM, mangling it, increasing the price, then re-selling it.
                                              If that's how you feel about VtR, then that's how you feel about VtR. But that's also your personal opinion. It doesn't really extend well towards you initial complaint that there is a problem with a "trend" of mining the nWOD for cWOD games.

                                              If/when you start threads for ideas on inspiration for stuff that VtM doesn't cover you should feel free to express your distaste for VtR and disinterest in suggestions from VtR. People should respect that. But not everyone shares your views on VtR. Some people like it better. Some people don't care. Some people like both equally for different reasons (esp. more recent VtR culminating with Blood & Smoke; as it has taken VtR a big leap forward in stepping out from behind VtM's shadow at least on a design level). Some people haven't read it and don't know if they like it or not. For anyone that doesn't have a negative opinion about VtR, suggestions to mine from it aren't problematic. So the concept of there being a problematic trend doesn't materialize.

                                              Also, because this one point tends to irk me a bit: VtR's increased selling price is basic inflation. It was a $5 increase in price for a book of the same length, size, and comparable cost factors (well, actually, VtR has slightly higher base cost factors because isn't a purely B&W interior whether you consider the inclusion of red ink in the first VtR book a worthy artistic choice is pretty subjective). As a comparison, I pulled a 2000 softcover printing of a novel in a series. It cost $7. A 2004 novel in the same series that was the same length was $8 for the paperback.

                                              Most people don't realize that the Revised era cWOD books were actually priced well below what they realistically should have been given increased costs on the production end of things. WW chose to keep the costs closer to 2e costs to avoid sticker shock even as production costs went up. When the nWOD came out, they upped the costs to better match the production costs of the day; and like most RPG books charge way less than comparable books in less niche (or other factors like text book price controls) markets. It was really that simple.
