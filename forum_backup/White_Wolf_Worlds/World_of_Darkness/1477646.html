    Adjusting the Dark Ages setting to prevent the rise of the Technocracy

    03-29-2022, 05:49 PM
    Recently (here) I posted a topic to discuss the scenario of a time-traveling group of mages (and possibly other allied supernaturals) that travel to the past to prevent the Technocracy from existing, since in the present (setting hack) it intensified its persecution of Reality Deviants and expanded it to the other supernatural splats, unleashing an Apocalyptic war between Triatic uber-factions that threatened to destroy the world. I am interested to discuss the same scenario (changing the Dark Ages setting so that the rise of the Technocracy is averted) from the perspective of Dark Ages characters. This might be the result of time-travelers subtly changing the past without an overt intervention, or DA supernaturals following a similar path by a natural timeline divergence. As far as I am concerned, this primarily concerns Tradition mages and fae, although it may also affect vampires and werewolves as convenient.

    As far as I can tell, there are a few key flashpoints that may be good for this purpose. One obvious possibility is to make the Craftmasons at Mistridge lose and be crushed. In all likelihood, it would cause the faction to be disbanded or destroyed, and without their role, chances are the Order of Reason never gets organized. Another possibily is to bomb the 997 Gathering of the Square that set up the Craftmasons and their nascent alliance with other proto-Order of Reason groups in the first place, although that seems less easy to accomplish.

    Yet another option is to have House Tremere destroyed before or soon after it turns to vampirism. This might happen because their attempt to destabilize the Order of Hermes with the Schism War is discovered and they are singled out as the rogue House instead of the Diedne, and Marched. From my PoV, this would also have the significant option that the Diedne thrive and stay an integral part of the OoH, leading to improved relations with the Old Faith, other 'pagan' Fellowships, and the fae. Alternaitvely, their turn to vampirism is discovered before they had a real chance to adapt Awakened magic to blood sorcery (or Tremere to Diablerize the Salubri Antedeluvian), allowing the OoH to destroy them with ease.

    In all likelihood, if the OoH does not have the face the major distraction and attrition of the First Massasa War, they would notice the potential threat of the Craftmasons and crush them with relative ease. OTOH, a Cainite society without the Tremere Clan may evolve in ways that I find difficult to fathom.

    Not directly related to the Technocracy issue, but it might also be good for Tradition mages to find ways to tone down inter-Fellowship hostility and achieve some kind of cooperation between Fellowships not unlike the Council of Traditions even without the Order of Reason threat as a unifying element, devise appropriate magitech replacement for advanced technology, and prevent Commoners from becoming a serious threat to supernaturals. For the purpose of this scenario, supernaturals staying or becoming the ruling elites of the world is supposed to be a neutral to good thing, within reasonable limits of course. Something like a repeat of the Impergium is bad, but having a functional magocracy or necrocracy is fine.

    As it concerns the fae, I suppose preventing a restart of the fratricidal War of Seasons is likely warranted. Getting rid of the Technocracy likely does away with the rise of Banality, although it may not suffice. I am not sure what vampires or werewolves may need.

    Yet another potential flashpoint to wipe out the Technocracy may be to destroy the Convention of the White Tower in 1325, although I am concerned by then the nascent Order of Reason had already got too momentum by then for its rise to be disrupted.

    Opinions? Suggestions?

      As mentioned in Guide to the Traditions, keep Genghis Khan alive for another year or two, and the Mongols will likely wreck most of Central and Western Europe, derailing everything.

      Alternately, House Tremere's efforts to frame House Diedne for infernalism in 1003 fails, instead causing the Tremere's vampire experiments to be exposed and the House renounced and mostly destroyed by the rest of the Orders. This means that the Order will not be distracted by war with the Tremere and other vampires in the early 1200s when the Craftmasons use the turmoil of the Albigensian Crusade as cover for their attack on Mistridge.
      If you also wanted to try to head off the Albigensian Crusade (or at least try to delay it a few years), you could try preventing the 1208 murder of the papal legate Pierre de Castelnau by one of the Count of Toulouse's men, and then work at keeping the Count and Pope Innocent III's relationship at least non-hostile. Two side effects of this are that the Kingdom of France is less able to extend its influence into the south, even if it still defeats the House of Plantagenet, and likely cuts back on the activities of the Papal Inquisition. But how much the latter impacts the activities of the Shadow Inquisition - ie, the groups in Dark Ages: Inquisition - and the Cabal of Pure Thought - what I refer to as the Illuminated Inquisition for clarity - is anyone's guess.

                                  Ironically perhaps, I feel like the creatures most able to detect any such breach in time would be among the least able to directly fight back. Those creatures being vampires. The True Brujah would certainly notice any ripples in time, and the Toreador, Malkavian, and Tzimisce would be likely to notice that things aren't going the way they were supposed to. But Vampires can't go back and fix things, so they'd be more likely to try to set things up to stop people from coming back and undoing all their hard work. Imagine a Convention lab getting blown up by a Vampire who died 600 years earlier, or losing all their funding because someone had set up a sell off from the stock market hundreds of years ago. Ultimately I think the Camarilla and Sabbat would still exist, largely because they are the result of ancient god-like beings trying to make them happen.

                                  I forgot to mention, I believe the elder vampires of the dark ages would likely view the time travel as a bad thing, even if they knew why it was being done. If you notice, the Technocracy actually furthers the goals of the Camarilla, so the powers behind the Camarilla would not necessarily want to see the Technocracy go, they would simply be trying to shape it into a better tool.

                                  Since people have been talking about the Tremere a lot, allow me to note that many vampires seem to be strangely okay with the Tremere killing one of their siblings and becoming vampires. There's long been some suggestion that Salot wanted or at least accepted the Tremere diablerie. It makes me wonder if some Vampires might have wanted the Tremere to become vampires, specifically so they wouldn't be mages who could mess with vampiric plans in ways the elders would have trouble fighting against. There are a number of ways I think one could go with this, for example if the Tremere becoming vampires was already a plan to stop the demonic corruption of mages (or slow it down). Perhaps there is a reason that killing the Tremere wouldn't have worked as well (for example, Tremere Wraiths would have worked with the Giovanni to bring about the end times earlier, when the world couldn't have recovered.)

                                  As far as Changelings go, The technocracy actually has less to do with the rise of banality than you may think. In fact, it was the dreaming starting to float away from the mundane world that caused the increase in banality which led into the paradigms of the early technocrats becoming strong enough for those technocrats to become the most influential will workers in the world. This means that if one could find a way to stop the Sundering, the Technocracy would never rise to ascendancy. However, there are many stories about why the sundering occurred, and no one really knows why it happened when it did. I personally believe the Sundering was a way for the dreaming to protect humanity from a demon invasion through the dreaming. In that scenario, imagine you stop the rise of the technocracy by stopping the sundering, only to begin suffering demon invasions. Really though, that wouldn't be ideal for the demon invaders either, as Fae are immortal and super-powerful spirits themselves, and the dreaming is notoriously difficult to traverse, any demon who actually make it past the Arcadian fae would show up at random times and places.
