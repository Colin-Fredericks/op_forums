                          That is impossible the way you wrote it.

                          If you release the fireball AND want to make it invisible /before/ it hits the enemy, keep in mind the rule of 1 Arete roll per turn. The fireball will hit the target the same turn you casted it, unless you keep accumulating successes with +1 to difficulty penalty per additional turn of rolling, and that way you'll waste turns + your enemy will already see the fireball, clearly.

                          Imagine it like this: you make fireball, release it and it flies to enemy. It will hit the same turn you made it. There's simply no time for you to do another Arete roll and cloak it, because rules disallow that you make more than 1 Arete roll during turn (exception: Wonders).

                          Now, if you do plan to use Wonders, attacking with them still takes an action usually. Why? Because you need to aim your spell regardless, unless you want it to fly off randomly of course.

                          However, it's all about wording.

                          You cannot make separate Arete rolls for that, but you can split successes for that and basically do conjuctional magick (which people often forget about, oddly). But good luck with that, because you'd need at least --

                          1. 2+ successes for fireball, which would be only enough to affect it (affecting something or somebody else requires 2+ successes, as per M20). It would only increase difficulty to see your fireball by +2 (rules of invisibillity in How do you do that?)
                          2. Attacking successes, in case person will try to dodge + soak on top.
                          3. If you do extended rolls, cloaking is kinda useless. They've already seen it.

                          Not really likely to happen in a single turn.

                                          What do you mean example of ritual?

                                          You have amount of rolls available equal to your Arete + Willpower.

                                          IC-wise, 1 Arete roll equals 1 IC hour of time. I personally see no need to wait OOCly as long as you elaborate what happens and that your character has spent time, but hey, I ain't the one to tell how server time stuff gonna work.

                                          You can do amount of rolls equal to your Stamina before you need to take a rest.

                                          You can spend Willpower point to put off ritual for later, take a rest and continue on (refreshes your Stamina). Difficulty to "pick up" the ritual from where you left is equal to base difficulty of the ritual (example: you want to ward something, the base difficulty was 8 before applying modifiers, that's your difficulty to pick it up again). You roll Wits + Esoterica to pick it up again. If you take a break again and then come back a second time to the same ritual, the difficulty to pick it up increases by +1, and it stacks endlessly for each time you come back to it.

                                          If you fail, that's it, you simply get no successes that turn, though you get +1 to difficulty for further rolls until you take a rest.

                                          If you botch -

                                          Botched Rolls

                                          If you roll a botch during a ritual, you may spend one turn,
                                          a temporary Willpower point, and one previously rolled success
                                          in order to keep the whole thing from blowing up in your face.
                                          At this point, your mage is holding the ritual together
                                          through sheer determination. You can either stop there or
                                          else keep going with a +1 increase to your difficulty. A second
                                          botch, however, spells immediate disaster… again, see Rituals
                                          and Paradox.
                                          As for Paradox: if the ritual is vulgar, you get Paradox as usual, however for each additional roll you make you get another +1 to Paradox. If the ritual ends up botched or failed, it all comes down crushing on you. If ritual succeeded, you only take Paradox as usual, as from any other spell.

                                          If you're helped by unAwakened assistants (any human or other supernatural), you check "Cult" background for amount of people you need to get bonus dice to Arete rolls.

                                          If you're helped by Mages:

                                          • Equal Collaborators – that is, mages who are more or less
                                          on the same level as the primary caster – can roll their
                                          Arete and then add their successes toward the success
                                          of the ritual as a whole. The Effect’s normal difficulty
                                          and modifiers apply equally to all such collaborators.

                                          • Enlightened assistants – that is, mages with less knowledge
                                          and experience than the primary caster – add one
                                          automatic success to the Effect, up to a maximum of
                                          five successes, total. These successes, however, DO NOT
                                          APPLY to cancel out a botch. If the caster botches the roll,
                                          then those assistants’ successes disappear. The additional
                                          successes count only to enhance a successful Effect.
                                          Only one option may be used at a time – not both.
                                          All Spheres must match if Mages are of less expertise. (example: you do warding with Correspondence, Life and Prime. There's two mages, one with Correspondence/ Life/ Prime, and another with only Correspondence and Prime. The one with three Spheres just like you can help. If their Sphere ranks are equal to yours, see "Equal Collaborators"). Mages who try to help you must have matching Paradigms and Focus, else they get penalties (or are unable to help at all).

                                          If you're interrupted during ritual (fight occurs, someone tries to tackle you down, that sort of thing), you must roll Willpower at difficulty of 8. If you fail - it's all gone and treat it as botching magick, Paradox blows up in your face. If you succeed - treat it as botch during ritual, aka you get +1 to difficulty until you take a rest and next botch or interferance will put it down for good.


                                          That's pretty much all you need to know about rituals.

                                                      Picking Up Where You Left Off

                                                      After casting an imperfect Effect or falling short of her initial
                                                      plans, a mage can pick up where she left off by making another
                                                      roll at +1 to the original difficulty. In story terms, she grits her
                                                      teeth, regroups, and gives it the old (wizard) college try once again.
                                                      If you ever want to stack successes, here's how it goes down:

                                                      1. You do a ritual and follow the rules of ritual. (Usually, it's 1 hour per 1 Arete roll. Definitely not for combat or quick action)
                                                      2. You do turn-to-turn rolling, but each new turn you keep rolling you get +1 to base difficulty, which you cannot modify and thus reduce. Surely you won't stack as many successes as you could with ritual, but it's good for quick action.
