          I now had time to browse over the book myself and yes, it is Hunter the Vigil ported to the classic WoD.

          The Imbued and Messengers aren't even mentioned. Were they now retconned out of existence?
          The capital-H Hunters are said to have Creed, Drives and Edges that separates them from lowercase-h hunters, like members of the Society of St. Leopold, FBI or what not. But then the Edges are basically fancy bonuses to things that should be Backgrounds - and an Edge is basically that a Hunter character has access to this on her own, while a hunter character would have access to this through he organisation he works for/with.

          They have a nice little mechanic to get extra dice, but if one of them shows a '1' the player has to decide to either have the role fail and get a 'Despair' condition that has to be resolved by role-played events, or to succeed in the roll (shortterm) but have a longterm setback represented in an increase in "Danger" for the current "Hunt".

          All in all the Hunters are normal humans and the supernaturals have about half a dozend moster qualitites but no clear ties to VtM or the upcoming WtA 5th edition.

          All in all, what this book tries to go for is probably already accomplished in d20 modern, spycraft or .... well... Hunter: The Vigil.

          It was nice that they incorporated Orpheus, though. Most people forget that it is a cWoD game aswell...

                                          Wishing for an H20 instead (which will never come).

                                          I think this book has quietly crawled into a dark corner and died. There was initial excitement online, then it was announced there were no ties to OG HTR, and it became tumbleweed.

                                          I've seen a few threads start and fizzle out quickly. Maybe this isn't the best place for fans of H5, though -- maybe they congregate on Renegade forums or something?

                                          Both the threads I've seen that were very long were mostly people trying to work out who it was appealing to, with half a dozen liking it, and one or two zealously defending it (to the point that they were glossing over racism and other stuff which felt icky).

                                          Of course, such threads also threatened to tip into edition wars a couple of times, which got uncomfortable, but in the end, they turned out okay and nobody got hurt (I hope).

                                          I think some people will use the book for its refinements of the V5 system (Drive Fields could be applied to vampires to make Hunger add bonuses to certain in-theme actions, thereby replacing both Blood Surge and Discipline dice bonuses, for example), some will use it as a resource for NPCs for V5 (but even then, it's quite thin on the ground), and some will use it for ST-lite one-shots. But I can't see it gaining the same passionate fanbase as the HTR of yore.

                                          However, maybe we'll see HTR stuff on STV that resurrects the Imbued, so there's that.
