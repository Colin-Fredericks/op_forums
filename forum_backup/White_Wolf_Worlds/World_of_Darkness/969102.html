    Mythological Crossover questions

    09-14-2016, 05:30 AM
    IF you use crossover, how do you resolve the differences in mythology of the different cWoD games?
    I do realize that the views of the individual supernatural species when it comes to creation of the world, cosmology, mythology and that kinda stuff can be biased, different interpretations of the same thing and several other things but how do you handle it?

    I talked about that a bit with the guy who has been a storyteller for games I play in for a few years already (and he has been a player and storyteller for much longer already) but he is mainly a Werewolf guy, not really about Crossover so much.

    Personally I have no idea about Mage, Wraith, Mummy, Hunter and so on but have played Werewolf and Vampire quite a bit and am also quite big on Demon: the Fallen even though I doubt I'll get to play it anytime soon (and haven't even read all the material yet).

    My friend told me his main problem with Demon is that its cosmological/mythical background does not fit with the rest at all while for example Werewolf and Vampire can work together quite well. I'm not so sure about that though.
    I mean Demon kind of gives an explanation for different points of view (in short on different plains of perception ALL of them are true and the same thing) but fails to include anything about other stuff like large parts of the Umbra. That might be because, if I remember correctly, Fera didn't even exist at the time when the Fallen were banished into the Abyss. That still doesn't explain the connections in the background though.
    Vampire and Werewolf don't include anything about the Fallen of course since the game wasn't around when their material was written.
    Werewolf, as far as I'm aware totally ignores God/The Creator while in Vampire background its pretty central that God cursed Cain and that is how Vampires came into being (and Demon mentions that the Fallen learned the concept of killing other beings (something totally alien to them beforehand) from that same Cain when he was observed slaying his brother).
    Seeing this I actually think that Vampire and Demon would be the easiest to combine while Werewolf seems the total outlier of the three (and I don't know about the rest of the games).

    I guess it comes down to the question of who/what you take God/the Creator to be compared to the top "players" in Fera mythology.
    As far as I know different Fera and even different Werewolfs have different opinions if Gaia is a level higher or lower than the Triat (Wyld, Weaver, Wyrm). Then what would God equal in their background? Gaia? the Wyld maybe as that's the principle of creation (though much more chaotic than God appears in Vampire and Demon)?

    Any important pieces of info I'm missing? Any insights?

      Originally posted by Hexer View Post
      IF you use crossover, how do you resolve the differences in mythology of the different cWoD games?
      It's...hard. Putting all the gamelines side-by-side equally, things do not mesh well, and directly contradict at times.

      As all the origin stories and world mythologies are conflicting more or less, the only way to really resolve it is to decide on one gameline that you label as 'Correct' for your game, and then take the other gameline's explanations and make them work in that context.

      Demon *tried* this of course, by being the one gameline that says 'All the others are wrong, this is how things really are', but in turnabout it was completely biased on the Demons.
      Which is fine. It essentially did what an ST has to do for crossovers without ending up with a mess, so it already served that option in the book. But it basically simply is the 'Demon is correct' option.

      So, basically,
      1) Decide on true gameline origin story for your game
      2) Weave the others in in that dominant context.

      The characters of course can believe whatever they want.

      Vampire and Demon indeed are the easiest to combine. Mage can *explain* everything, but does so in a grand 'reality is what humanity makes of it' manner, which can strongly take away the significance of the other mythologies. Werewolf brings the whole Gaia/Triad origin story with it and forces everything else to quite bend and warp in order to fit and somewhat make sense.

      Pick your poison

      EDIT:
      Or, you know, if it simply plays no role in the plots you run, just ignore it all as long as it doesn't come up.

          Part of the problem is the nature of God. The God of Abrahamic religions did not really exist until after the Babylonian Exile, when the Jews, influenced by the exposure to Zorastrian ideology, rejected their Pantheon to create a two diety religion. YHWH became the 'good' god while Lucifer became the 'bad' god. Instead of having two equal gods, however, the Jews made to 'good' god superior to the 'bad god, which has led to over 2500 years of argument of why the 'good' god lets the 'bad' god do bad things. Presumably, the eldest vampires remember the change and the Mokole can access Mnesis to remember the change.

          Werewolf possesses the broadest, the oldest, and the most internally logical mythology (though individual groups have their distortions and their biases). Even so, there is no evidence that any of the Changing Breeds have left the Solar System, so their mythologies are decidedly local. I like to think that Gaia is merely the local manifestation of the Universe while the Triat are local manifestations of the corresponding universal principles. Further, I like to think that the Solar System is experiencing a quarantine from the outside to prevent the corruption of its local manifestations from spreading. Just my two cents...

                          My take on things is this:

                          Every story is, or was, true. When Chaos, Order and Entropy war one upon the other, time and history become weapons. The tiny things on Earth only perceive the barest hint of the Outer Darkness waging war on the light. This is for the best: seeing too much of the conflict's true scope would annihilate the sanity of even the strongest non-divine being.

                          Entropy destroys; so Chaos creates anew. Order tries to halt the process entirely. Every time this iteration plays itself out, changes to space/time inevitably occur: the past is mutable. It changes.

                          But Entropy is slowly winning, and so, rather than fade away, the shells of previous worlds collide with ours, leaving imprints of truths no longer valid in the current timeline. As the wheel of ages grinds to a final close, the past burns and now clear history is impossible to discern.

                          It's all true, even if it no longer true.

                                                          It's not that hard to come up with solutions for Demon/Werewolf/Vampire. Gaia is God [which Days of Fire does actually seem to hew in the direction of, at least on a vague thematic level], God created Gaia and the Triat and then died/left, or that "God" as comprehended by Demons was the entire system of the Triat and Gaia perceived as a harmonious whole, the engine of Creation that Demons once knew as their master but which has since so fractured and decomposed (alongside the concomitant splitting of the world into Umbra and material reality) as to be unrecognizable. The Demon ST guide even proposes a tenuous link between the Changing Breeds/Garou and the Malhilm warrior-angels manufactured to wipe out the Fallen during the end of the War in Heaven. Things do get kinda screwed up when you try to figure out how many years ago exactly things were supposed to have happened, mind you.

                                                          Demon, Vampire, Hunter, and Wraith are pretty trivially compatible with one another, generally speaking. Werewolf can be tacked on as above (you mostly have to go with Werewolves labeling Oblivion's touch or aberration against nature as 'Wyrm', rather than everything sort of bad being literally a spawn of the Wyrm- i.e. 'Sense Wyrm' does more than just sense Wyrm, but that's what Garou know it as and they will correspondingly label other things in accord with that understanding). Changeling is awkward; DA Fae might fit a little better if you use them for your changelings, although you pretty much need to have the Fae be either some degenerated/mutated permutation of angel or just delusional cogs in the machine of Creation in terms of their history.


                                                          The exception is Mage. Essentially nothing is particularly compatible with consensual reality in a way that doesn't trivialize everyone else's narrative. You pretty much have to throw most of it out (conceptually and metaphysically Mage never really felt like it fit very well with Wraith/Werewolf/Vampire and later Demon- fighting in space ships in the Umbra and consensual reality and magitek power armor really just could not be further away thematically from the other gamelines).
