          Yes. All of them. The Players searched for a possible Holy Grail in the deepest catacombs beneath Prague and encountered an old atomic bomb, chained to the "grail".                       I don't remember any actual time I would have just killed off all my players, individual players I have but usually always after a few serious warning signs. But I have had the misfortune of being a player in a few games where we all were just killed and it does leave a really bad taste in your mouth most of the time.

                      Two different examples one which I still enjoyed even if we all got killed, apart from one player who just lost an arm, and another which left us feeling a bit bitter afterwards.

                      1.
                      AD&D game with a rag tag group of adventurers including a mage, warrior, kobold thief and a bard I think and a I cant remember the class of the last player, it's been a while. It was a long adventure during which some artifact were found and all such basic fantasy stuff. There was three major events though that lead to the unfortunate ending we got. 1, the Kobold got himself a cursed dagger that while powerful whispered murderous thoughts to him, and being a kobold he had hard time not seeing eye to eye with the thing. 1, in a battle that might have cost all our lives the Mage in desperation called out to his Gods and received a blessing, magical energies whirled around him like a small scale whirlwind pulling the kobold into the whirlpool of energy. The ST covertly noted the HP of the kobold and gave that number as an energy reserve for the Mage to use as direct damage dice against our enemies. He used most of that power to zap lighting all over the battle field nearly killing the kobold. This left the Kobold with a grudge and mistrust towards the mage which lead to him trying to literally backstab the mage a few times at the urging of his cursed blade. These murder attempts lead to the rest of the group deciding it would be better if the dagger would be taken from him, which did little to endear the kobold to the others. 3, at the end of the chronicle in this weird fortress in a different dimension, I think, a dragon of great power offered a deal to the kobold if he would betray the rest of the group, this dragon was of course the being the group was there to kill. Considering his options the Kobold agreed to the deal, after all the others had taken away his greatest treasure and harbored a murderous mage that had without provocation tried to kill him.

                      So when the final confrontation with the Dragon began the Kobolt tried to backstab the Mage and this is where things went down in flames. The kobolt stuck his dagger into the mages back as another player who saw what was going on tried to help the mage and attacked the kobold with his sword, connecting, just as the mage turned and released the power he had been gathering as a lighting bolt to the kobold, who still had his hand on the dagger that was in the mage while himself having a sword in his side from another player, sufficient to say we all were electrocuted to death. Except for the one player attacking the Dragon at the time, he realized all was lost and sought to escape trying to carry the swordsman with him, still barely alive from the lighting as the Dragon attacked killing the wounded and ripping of an arm from the one escaping. Still he manged to get out alive even if badly wounded.

                      It was a fun game altogether and since the deaths were not unfair so to speak it was a cool if somewhat cruel ending for hte chronicle, although the ST did play out a small epilogue with all of us getting into where ever their own Gods take those dead loyal to them.

                      2.
                      The game that left a bad after taste was a sci-fi game called Blueplanet RPG. In it our ragtag group of mercenaries took a high profile assassination job on Earth with huge paycheck waiting alongside a ticket off world. After a long somewhat long series we finally got our job done and swiftly took all the money we made out in cash and disappeared with the first ship to leave this mudball. During the luxury spaceflight, no weapons on board cause of intense security checks, the ship was attacked by some sort of space pirates I guess, never really learned their identity. So we were forced to improvise, few players raided a sex shop of hte ship for handcuffs and improvised weaponry and others went to cleaning supplies to get some chemicals to make imrovised weapons. Against all odds we managed to fend of the attackers and me and another player, we got separated, left for the command bridge now armed with small submachine guns taken from our attackers. The other players meanwhile were caught and surrendered. We got to the doorway of the bridge as we were caught as well but instead of surrendering we decided to trust our dice and fight, and lucky us we killed the four armed soldiers that had surrounded us, managed to get what these attackers were after and slip to an escape pod. From there we negotiated freedom for our fellows and ourselves and were allowed to use the pod to escape the ship towards the Blue planet. At this point my char was the only one who still had all of his money, in a briefcase that never left his hand, even during the aforementioned fighting in which it was used as an improvised weapon.

                      We crash landed on the planet waking up on a shore of an island, me to the sudden realization that all my hard earned and harder fought for money had been lost into the sea. This on it's own felt a bit of a bastard move since I had risked life and limb, literally, to escape the ship with the money. As we searched the island we found a small cabin in which we rested for a bit only to realize that somesort of special forces had surrounded us and demanded that we surrender. Again everyone agreed to surrender two at a time we walked unarmed away from the cabin, except that me and the other player from earlier had concealed weapons with us and no intention of surrendering. There were actual valid IC reasons for our hardheadedness in this whole surrendering matter. Again the dice favored us and we managed to shoot and kill ourselves through the ring of troops. But this time were unable to rescue the other players, yet. So we ran and hid. And started to slowly search through the island, those troops had to come from somewhere. This finally lead to us two, and a few native mutant creatures or something my co-player somehow managed to be-friend, to attacking the facility we found. We did get wounded in this battle, I pretty badly in fact, but we were winning at which point the ST just declared that fighter bombers flew over the island leveling it back to stone age, killing us all.

                      That was a really, really, shitty way to end a long chronicle and left a bitter taste for the two of us at the least.

                          “Don’t cross the streams!”

                          That was a hard lesson I learned about doing PC/NPC Crossovers.

                          My troupe’s Sabbat pack:

                          Another pack had been turned to ash and the troupe’s pack was asked to check it out. After following up on leads, the troupe discovered the [now-deceased] pack’s ductus had been involved in some very aggressive “urban relocation programs.” They figured it was some dispossessed juicebag-turned-hunter.
                          After some subtle [read: violent] detective work and a series of gentle [read: traumatic] shakedowns, the pack found the responsible party and moved to confront [read: murder] them. When the pack drew their weapons, the trio they confronted shifted into Crinos….

                          The plan was, after the pack emerged victorious, to lead into a series about (what I was calling) Daytime Conspiracies. Basically, I wanted to get the players thinking about who/what ran amuck in *their* city when the rest of the Sword was asleep.

                          I created the three werewolf NPCs using WtA rules, inspired by Glass Walkers and Bone Gnawers. IIRC, I used the basic character creation as a guideline plus 20 additional Freebie Points (35 FPs total). I figured that was a fair challenge. The pack had been around awhile; the characters weren’t fresh neonates, so I figured they could use a little challenge.

                          Nope.
                          All four characters died.
                          Quickly.
                          It was horrible.
                          I was flabbergasted.
                          The players were pissed.

                          It was the last time I used crossover rules for creating NPCs.
