                        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                        On the matter of getting rid of the Imbued, I don't think it'll be sending then wherever may be, but really ending the whole Imbuing, with any surviving Imbued either becoming NPCs or losing their powers and joining the other hunters, maybe forming new organizations.
                        And from what I've read about it, all of the necessary game systems to reintroduce the Imbued are already there: what 1e called Virtues appear to be called Drives now, and Edges appear to be more like HtV's Endowments, in that their organizing principle is not the Creeds (which are still a thing), but appear to be more of an extension of how the Numina are organized (we explicitly have Psychic Phenomena and True Faith among H5's Edges). But even with those changes, the basic skeleton is already there: Conviction, Drives, Edges, and Creeds.

                        What appears to be missing is the lore, where the hunters are the Chosen of the Messengers and Imbued with Edges that echo their Creeds, in a last-ditch effort to stave off the end of the world. That could easily be reintroduced as a type of Edges (call them Imbuements) which are granted by mysterious supernatural benefactors and are organized by Creed; and things like the Sight and Hunter's Code could be recast as effects of the first Imbuement that everyone with Imbuements gets. No other new game systems would be needed beyond what H5 is already providing.

                        The only thing lost is the “imminent end of the world” aspect; as monteparnas suggests here, it may be that there were Imbued for a time, around the end of the last millennium; but when the world failed to end, their benefactors essentially said “oops; we didn't need to do that after all” and stopped Imbuing hunters. Reintroducing the Imbued would then theoretically involve justifying the Messengers returning and taking up the practice again. In short, they'd need to be recast in a way that makes sense without the “latter days metaplot” that dominated the World of Darkness from 1999–2003.

                        But even if they were to be brought back, I'm fine with them no longer being central to the game. As I said in my first post to this thread, my personal play style for HtR was to go with Bystanders (and then only because of the game benefits of Bystander-style Conviction and access to a handful of Merits designed to help them maintain agency in the face of Delirium/the Fog/the Mists); the “Buffy the Vampire Slayer” vibe was never my thing. I liked the Conviction/Virtues/Creeds stuff, and regretted that Bystanders didn't tap into any of that. I'd be okay with the Imbued being reintroduced as one option among many, for those who like them; but I really like how it looks like this will make all of the things I liked about the original HtR available to me without forcing me to take on all of the things I didn't like.

                        BTW, is there any news on how this is progressing?

                                                        Agreed. To me, there's a very simple explanation for them no longer being around: the world didn't end, and the existential crisis has passed. Sure, there's still some danger that matters will worsen and disaster will once again loom; but that's only a possibility. So the Heralds and Messengers withdrew the Imbuing.

                                                        Just how important was it that the hunters were Imbued by Messengers with Creed-based superpowers? From the preview, it looks like you literally have everything else that made HtR what it was: humans from ordinary backgrounds fighting monsters and getting in over their heads; Conviction; VirtuesDrives; Creeds; and Edges. In 1e, the Messengers played a role in the Imbuing; but after that, they were entirely hands-off. The Imbued we're left entirely in the dark and on their own in terms of what the Imbuing was and what they were supposed to do. So exactly how is the game any different if they're in the dark and on their own because there are no Messengers empowering them?

                                                        Frankly, the only thing from 1e that hasn't been confirmed for 5e that I'll miss if they don't show up in some form of another were the “agency-preserving powers”: the ability for hunters to ignore the likes of the Delirium and the Mists; the ability to continue to think rationally even when other mortals would be supernaturally compelled to behave irrationally. I don't need Cleave, or the Sight, or the Code, or the supernaturally shielded Hunter-net; just keep my hunter from becoming a lunatic the moment a shapeshifting beastman appears.
