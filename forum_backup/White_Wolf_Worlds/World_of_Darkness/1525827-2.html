                                        To the general concept on "generations" as a viable concept:

                                        Generations aren't about people. Not macro-trends, or individual visibility. Generations are about media consumption. There's a reason why the Western generation labels don't go past the Lost Generation. The Lost Generation was the generation that saw the the radio and telephone become a practical devices; they grew up in a world where instantaneous long distance non-line-of-sight communication wasn't a thing, and then it was a thing in their lifetime. The Greatest Generation grew up with the idea of telecommunication as a fact of life, but during its infancy. The Silent Generation was when in-home telecommunication became part of normal everyday life; they were effectively the "radio" generation. The Boomers were the television generation. Gen X is a combination of the portable media generation, and like the Lost Generation, the one on the cusp of the digital revolution in telecommunications. Millenials are the World Wide Web generation, where the Internet became part of daily life but was still shaped by various limitations even if those were solved faster than the early wireless generations did. And Gen Z is the Streaming generation where the Internet isn't just park of live, but media consumption is available on demand.

                                        Everything else about how we treat social generations as groups stems from this. For contemporaneous narratives largely driven by marketing firms trying to ply their trade now that their messages can get beamed into people's homes, to later stereotypes built around media consumption habits and the bias of what media stands the test of time, the underlying factor is how those broad groups got that media in the first place.
