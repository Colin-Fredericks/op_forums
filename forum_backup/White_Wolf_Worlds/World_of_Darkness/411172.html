    Are Single Gender Splats Bad?

    02-19-2015, 03:33 AM
    My wife and I have been working on a home-brewed creature type for the Old World of Darkness. This will just be for use with our gaming group(s) and maybe to post online for feedback. One of the splats was going to be (almost) exclusively female, something there is some precedence for in the OWoD with Black Furies, Ahrimanes, Lamia, Daughters of Hippolyta, and so on. This would be an important part of the theme and flavor of the splat. Upon giving the idea a bit of reflection, I wonder if such limitations might just be a bad idea as it limits the options of players to have the kind of character they desire.

    Does anyone have any thoughts or experiences with this issue? Would those canon splats be better or worse for opening them up to characters of any sex?

                  From a stereotypes/expectations standpoint, it's interesting how differently a "male-only" vs. a "female-only" splat/society/creature type/club is taken. And the level of expected exceptions to the rule.

                  Personally, I don't have a problem with the idea. I do tend to like to see distaff counterparts to whatever single-gender splats exist, but it's not inherently mandatory to avoid them being "bad." The trouble with them is when they seem to "try too hard." When they're too good, powerful, morally upright, or whatnot than the coed splats, they tend to come off somewhere between preachy and insulting. But they don't need to, and they can be done just fine.

                      Originally posted by Sergeant Brother View Post
                      Upon giving the idea a bit of reflection, I wonder if such limitations might just be a bad idea as it limits the options of players to have the kind of character they desire.

                      Not all groups in life are inclusive. Limiting the possible character concepts is something every single splat, regardless of gender or ethnicity or whatever, does to some degree. I don't mean this to sound offensive, but when I think about that as a reason for revising it to be more open... it seems a lot like a non-issue.

                      If a player really doesn't want to play a female character, then maybe he shouldn't try to shoe-horn his way into an all female group. And if he does want to play a male in the female group, then he should expect to play the part. If instead he wants what he wants, and doesn't give a damn about the story elements, then why should you (as the story teller) bend over backwards and ignore the story to placate his unreasonable wishes?

                      EDIT: The other option of course is that if you are making this all female group unquestionably cooler and/or more powerful than everyone else you are already taking his options away. In that case instead of opening it up to men, maybe you should just come up with a counter-balancing sort of splat equally "cool" for men to join.

                          One thing that I'll point out is that the term 'gender' and 'sex' are not the same thing. That is, when you consider the Daughters of Cacophony from Vampire: the Masquerade, all of the signature characters we saw in books were biologically male, but they presented as women (to varying degrees) and hence didn't make waves in their bloodline.* At my table, we take pretty seriously that distinction, but mileage varies, of course. There is no choice involved in the sex we are born with, but we exert more influence on gender. Those vagaries are not something everyone wants to consider, much less play in a game, but they exist in our world and within my troupe. However, to the point at hand...

                          I don't see these as being bad always, but like mentioned above, presentation counts for a lot. In the 'real world,' only men can be Catholic Priests, and only women can be nuns. In fiction, the Bene Geserit were all women, and as rad as Paul Muad Dib and Miles Teg were, they didn't get into that club. It happens, and the 'good' or 'bad' is ultimately up to you to assess. In and of themselves, I don't see a problem with Black Furies or Lamiae or the name Sons of Ether. In fact, those kinds of things can enhance drama, and THAT is something I always want in a game!

                          * Sure, there were some 'non-canon' versions of Sons of Discord and whatnot, but that goes beyond the scope of this topic.

                              Gender bias within groups of supernaturals is fine, in that it is realistic, for better or worse.                                                   Originally posted by Coridan View Post

                                                  I actually agree with this. I'd like to see a few male only splats as well. I always thought they should have gone with Templars being a male only craft for Mage. In this particular case, the concept wouldn't really work as male only.

                                                  I suppose I should give a few more details about what we're making to put this into context. The creature type we're designing are cambions, descendants of demons. The general concept is that there are these insular inbred families descended from demons, with scheming within and between families and the struggle to maintain a presence in the world of normal humans as opposed to the bizarre subculture created by the cambions themselves. These families tend to be pretty dark and nasty, perhaps reminiscent of the Addams family but played straight.

                                                  Anyway, one of the families is essentially ruled by the elder (hundreds or thousands of years old) males. These patriarchs kill any male child born into the family, draining its life essence to strengthen themselves. At the same time, the patriarchs of the family sire the next generation, making their demonic blood stronger than other families but also making the family more inbred and inhuman, which comes with its own set of disadvantages. There is the possibility for males from this family, those who were spirited away by their mothers to prevent them from being killed, which might be an interesting character concept (and the mother too) as he would be hunted by the elders of his family.

                                                  I was thinking the default for all of the families would be loyalty, but really this isn't at all a necessity. In fact, the family's set up does seem like it would encourage a lot of players to want to make rebellious characters. Though there would be major disadvantages to being hunted by this family, it could also be an interesting hook to explain why the character could be hanging out with members of other families or participating in the activities that player characters often find themselves involved in.

                                                      It's entirely possible to have single-gender splats that aren't misogynist/misandronist in nature, inherently, particularly among the long-lived undead (who, after even a few centuries, come to the realization that any gender views they have -- in one direction or the other -- are at least somewhat flawed, relative to their mortal lives). With more standard-lived splats (humans, skinchangers, etc.), whatever prevailing cultural/societal/familial views regarding gender will likely hold sway, particularly in historical settings, but even then it doesn't have to be as paper-thin, in terms of stereotypes, as many people make it out to be. A few friends have been watching, for example, the Spartacus series from a few years ago and were commenting on how although it was, of course, a male-dominated society on the surface, day-to-day level of things, women still held a great deal of social power/influence, albeit having to be utilized in a more subtle, less-overt manner. And, despite the show being of course geared for entertainment over being a historical documentary or treatise on gender roles in Ancient Roman Life, it still did a good job of conveying in an otherwise realistic manner how gender-specific groups/roles could be played in a "mixed bag", as it were, from a narrative POV: not everyone has to be involved in the same scenes nor perform the same narrative functions as everyone else, but the gender-divide actually provided for scenes/subplots that might not have played out the same way in a more "egalitarian" setting. Same holds true for most other time periods. While I am male, the characters I have played over the past 30+ years have been about a 50/50 split male/female, and in historical settings I've been perfectly fine with playing a period-appropriate woman of "x" culture/region, without having to interject any "modern sensibility" into the character/storylines (e.g., having the "warrior woman" fight alongside men, or be "surprisingly skillful" with weapons when it really would be unlikely for her to be so, or have a period-inappropriate level of political clout -- on the overt, day-to-day level -- that smacks of a 21st century relative equality, etc...); on the flip, I've also played males who weren't typical of the pseudo-historical perspective many people have (not all Viking men were warriors; not all Asian men know martial arts; not all men, period, are dominant in their societal standing/subjugate others, et al...), which in many cases is an overlooked historical "bias" that people miss out on.

                                                      As far as creating a gender-biased splat, it's certainly fine to do, and very appropriate to many cultures and historical settings. The most important aspect, however, is to keep in mind the narrative appropriateness and relativism; that is, not creating "x" splat "just 'cus", but rather because it has a sound premise and reason for continued existence. The WoD Amazons are a perfect example: the mages and werewolves of Themiskyra are, of course, all going to be female, and having one or more of them venture out into the outside world of the DA setting, for whatever time/reason, is perfectly acceptable and a good way to interject a degree of mythos into a game/story. Same thing with the Lamia, or the Cult of Mythras (whether for the vampire Mythras, or the Messianic men who still hold the old Sun God in esteem). It's really when a group comes across as "just for the boys/girls" for "just 'cus" reasons that the gender-divide becomes annoying or contrived, therefore often leaving a bad taste in everyone's mouths...
