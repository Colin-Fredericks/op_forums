                          When I get stuck, I look at what the Storyteller plans for the game and what the other players are doing. I'll often be the last to put in a character, as I take a role that the group needs. (Of course, I'm normally a Storyteller, so helping Storytellers and other players is part of my approach.)

                          For Wraith: The Oblivion, someone with Passions and Fetters that connect some of the other PCs and some major NPCs could be useful - Wraith characters can often get caught up in their personal drives rather than interacting with the group much. To use an example from an article in White Wolf Inphobia issue 50 about building character connections in Wraith, let's say one of the other characters was murdered by a street gang - if your character was robbed by them, or was a member of a rival gang, or has a building in their territory as a Fetter, this would give you a reason to work with her, at least for a while.

                          Another way to look at it is to take advantage of Wraith's unusual character creation and concentrate on the Shadow first. What does the character hate and fear, and what does the Psyche do about resisting the Shadow's desires to act on those violent feelings?

                          The heavy psychological option to look at your life and imagine what you would be passionate about if you became a Wraith tomorrow can yield some interesting results, but might not be fun to play.

                                  One way to look at character inspiration is to consider it from the perspective of the gaming table. During a typical session, what do you want to do?

                                  Are you exciting about solving clues, talking your way out of situations, stirring up shit? Do you want a character who's got a strong person mission that they'll drag others along for, or someone who is innately inspired to collaborate and help other PCs? What kind of personality might it be fun to roleplay? Are you going to be boldly impulsive, or careful and reserved but come up with convoluted plans?

                                  The answers to these questions don't amount to a fleshed out character on their own, but they can give you a spark or scaffold to start from.
