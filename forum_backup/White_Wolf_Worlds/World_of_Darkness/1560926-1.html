    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

    1. A godling wronged by Creation.

    I said evil is tangential, not core. Power derived from Torment doubles down on the idea of the character as a manifestation of Evil rather as a creator deity.

    2. Dealing with war trauma.

    Torment as trauma leads the character to turn everything into a weapon. Torment as a source of power is something else entirely.

    3. Shattered ideals and lost innocence.

    Devil's Due has nothing to enforce this theme, especially the possibility of rebuilding or build something new.

    Vice as a trait also focus too much on evil and corruption as defining the demon rather than being a state they can outgrow.

    4. Your work, corrupted.

    Lores as a system have the potential to cover a greater range of feats than buying a number of fixed effects, and tie those to the notion of you having knowledge and access to the building blocks of Creation.

    This actualize in play the idea of participation in said creation, and together with Memory the notion that you had a plan or expectation for your part in it, and reality fell short.

    No one here is arguing that DtF's Lores as they were done managed it, but Devil's Due proposal ditches the effort entirely.

    5. Memory of making History.

    Mostly I didn't see in it mechanics to remember the world before and bring it in play. I may have missed something.

    6. Rebuilding life and society.

    DtF has an actual society of demons forming. It is badly defined, but that's the point: a bunch of fugitives/survivors/refugees with memories of a destroyed past trying to build present from scratch.

    7. How to define Redemption.

    You didn't got the theme at all. Demon isn't in it to ask if you redeem yourself according to any particular standard of redemption.

    It asks which standards you build. What counts as redemption, what must be done or suffered to fit the bill and clean the record.

    Not just for you, by the way.

    8. Faith as Responsibility.

    Devil's Due default to Thralls and cultists as the medieval idea of Devil worshippers. They get a Torment trait as they're corrupted by you and what empowers the Pact is their decision to go ahead.

    In DtF the Pact is defined by faith, by intimate, confident belief in the power of a covenant, but without fixing the nature of this covenant. You define how you go for it, what you'll be for your Thrall and how you'll fit or subvert expectations and guide their future outside giving orders.

    9. Overall how you choose to change the lives you touch.

    DtF doesn't prescribe what your powers or your presence do to people. You have potential in as many directions as possible. You're not Evil by nature, your "nature" is a blank slate.

    Your choice isn't a binary of letting Torment go forth or block it, it's an open question of which paths you see and what you choose to make of them.

    10. Judge yourself and the world.

    Again, you're not seeking redemption unless you want. You're not at fault to begin with. You are passing judgment and all the standards are up to you.


    Overall DtF needs revisions, no one denied that, but it has the basic structure and narrative in a good direction. It's also open for interpretation and exploration.

    Your main point is the very problem with Devil's Due in all of this: feeling more "demonic". This is subjective as fuck and thematically narrow. Devil's Due don't invite you to explore what you think a Fallen is or can be, it assumes a given notion of "demon" and roll with it, love it or leave it.

    It subordinates all the questions above to this conceptualization of what a demon is instead of making it one option among many, while DtF works to ask you how you conceptualize Fallen Angels and what you'll do from there.
    1: Well, you partly hit the nail on the head with the biggest problem with Demon: The Fallen. It's not about demons, it's about angels. You say that evil is tangential to the themes, and that's an inherent problem in a game called "Demon: The Fallen," IMO.

    2: What about the Lore system in D:tF is BETTER at embodying these themes than using Devil's Due? You keep saying "Devil's Due does nothing to enforce these themes," but you haven't told me what Fallen does to enforce these themes.

    3: Why is my point subjective but yours isn't? My point is that I think you can embody those themes and also make the demons feel more demonic by taking away some of their mortal certainty about their reasons for falling. I don't think them being certain they didn't do anything wrong is a major thematic flaw in the game line, and one that the game line itself struggled with.

    Here's my question for you: What do you think D:tF's problems are?

        For context, this is how I would redo Demnon:

        1: As mentioned, use the Devil's Due power system instead of Lores.

        2: Add Angel player characters, using the lores from D:tF as their power system. The backstory is that, after the fall, some angels decided to stay on Earth to try and fix it, knowing that the choice would ban them from ever returning to heaven. In the milennia since then, they've devolved into different factions that ideologically disagree about how to fix the world. with the "mainstream" faction having a policy of no direct interference in human affairs, while the more "extreme" faction are an illuminati style secret society trying to bring about the apocalypse to cleanse the earth.

        3: I'd give the angel factions a complicated relationship with the demon factions. The mainstream angels would have a Cold war relationship with the Faustians and a hot war relationship with the Lucifarians, while the extreme faction would recognize the Luciferians as the "official" opposition and exist in a Cold war with them where they actually sometimes work together to attain shared goals, while they view the other demon factions as faithless vermin.

        4: I would remove the certainty about the nature of The Fall. It's been too long, and the Celestials have spent too much time melded with the minds of humans to recall with any true certainty. The official story is what most demons believe, but there's disagreement, and the angels have their own stories.

        5: Everyon hates the Earthbound. That's the one thing uniting every faction, demon and angel alike.

                                                            I do like this idea.

                                                            A way it's to expand their inherent perception powers. Demons can see trough all illusions, and distinguish all kinds of magic for what they are ... but that's the extent of their "lore" about things, barring a specific power that allows to know more.

                                                            Technically speaking you could fool the "demon of earth" that created precious stones with a fake plastic jewel as long as it's not magicaly fake, if they don't have the purely humane knowledge in the area to not get fooled. I'm not opposed to humans fooling demons, but I do think demons should be able to "know things" about their specific portfolio, even if they do not perhaps use the same words to identify it. "Maybe the world changed, but that's not how rocks were suppposed to work"

                                                            On this end, the Apocaliptic Form power of "predicting weather" should be scrapped and added as a baseline for anyone with the relevant Lores. I've known people to leave the game forever when they discovered that they have to spend Faith and access their all-powerfull form that scares and awes mortals simply by existing, in order to say "tomorrow it's going to raaaain!" .                                                             2. Changeling 20 managed to open up the Arts to have a lot more applications through the mechanic of Unleashing. Could that be used as inspiration for a similar way of expanding the uses of Lores?
                                                            It's not a bad idea, but it would need to be thematically different. Lores it's a wordplay because it's knowledge, but it's also that demons are the Lords of Creation. They're not an expression of another world that's alive and has an agenda. Their powers aren't something that can be "unleashed", as such.

                                                            More likely, it's like their Apocaliptic Form, a manifestation of themselves on reality. It could be tainted by their Torment, but that's the extent of it's chaoticness. Magic ain't going to do "as it wills", it's going to do as they will, if a bit twisted (or a lot if they're too far gone, but at that point that's sort of their their will too - most of the time)
                                                            One solution I've vouched for these problems in the past it's to use Lores as Spheres, using the power on each level as an example of what could be done on each level - this solves both issues because Spheres have an in built "knowledge function" on their first level, and they're open ended.

                                                            And Demon does compare the powers of mages with their own saying they're basically Lores, except it also says humans can "reinvent" the Lores (guess it's the privilege of being "made in the image of God"), so maybe that's too much freedom.

                                                            One very common "demonic pact" it's to produce gold and jewels from earth to make someone rich. You can interpret a pact to raise Resources as that. But Demons can't do that for themselves. Only trough pacts can they do this sort of stuff - and this is backed in the lore. Humans allow demons to rewrite reality with their Faith, it's what they have now that they don't have God backing them.

                                                            Now, leaving aside how pacts are very restrictive themselves as a system (being limited by both the faith of humans being very low, and the system not allowing enough freedom to the point it requires another book with a list of powers to do some classic demonic pacts, like longevity), as an idea to expand the system w/o making it just open ended we could try to use the pacts as more than just a battery.

                                                            Like, for option 1), you could use Pacts as a way to access Backgrounds, perhaps more than the ones allowed for Thralls, and maybe other Traits such as Merits & Defects, even powers from other splats (if sorcerers can, why not?). At a cost for the pact (and for the thrall). Like, you could invoke stone golems as Allies, impose a Defect as a curse, and speak to animals. That + Lores acting like Abilities could provide a flexible system.

                                                            For option 2) Could be a way to to limit or access the expansion of Lores, trough Unleashing or whatever. So you don't depend on making the effect too wild or too personaly costly to explain why the secondary system isn't used all the time - it is still something you have to build up.

                                                            One of many peeves I have about Demon it's how the "weapon" Faustians want to make it's never ever defined or even suggested of how could it matter at all. Faith it's just "blood for vampires" - there's 0 purpose of having more faith sources than what you need to be "filled", and the power of thralls doesn't impress enough as to consider Pacts as such an absolute Game Changer. Demons don't even have any power that could matter in such a war - how do you even find Heaven?, how do you affect it?. All you get it's to be able to cast your five-per-tree Lores a lot.

                                                            Mastery it's more impressive, and it's not Pact dependant. Rituals are more interesting, and they're dependant on working in concert more than massive quantities of Faith.

                                                            Yet Faith it's important enough that Demons immediately lost the war when humans saw them for the monsters they were, and the "faith flow" stopped. I don't feel this importance it's sufficently sustained by the idea that they were unable to "cast" as much as they would have wanted.

                                                            But if freely given human faith regulates the mojo then it makes more sense. Suddenly Demons were "demoted" from free Dynamic Magic to maybe Static Magic - that has to hurt ... and On Game, to imagine Faith as a "weapon" that could change the game it's less of an outlandish idea.

                                                            Maybe even allow a limited access to Mastery at some (very high) amount of Pacts, to add punch to the versatility - and suddenly Faustians oppening a hole in Heaven doesn't sound so far fetched​
