    On your point regarding Craft, there actually is a leve of granularity in the system. Upon choosing a craft, you must pick a speciality on what thpe of craft your are doing, be it wood work, scrap metal, glassblowing, textiles, etc. and then this is what your craft experience will be localised to. If you have craft Textiles then you will be unable to use for craft knowledge for something like metalwork or pottery, though anything tabgentle to textiles like sewing, dressmaking, etc could benefit if at a higher difficulty.

    and in regards to Rank 3 locking away dirty manuvrrs for Brawl, this is actually wuite a common thing for a lot of abilities. Awareness 3 is required to read auras, pilot 3 is requires for certain aircrafts, military vehicles require at least Drive 3, hypertech 3 is the minimum to start modifying Devices, Medicine 3 to heal health levels. It’s a pretty consistent thing that certain uses of an ability don’t unlock until higher ratings are achieved.

    I gave examples straight out of the core book. Increasing your physical Attributes is Life 3, increasing your damage is easiest under Entropy 1 via Spot Weakness, you can increase you Initiative by increasing Dex with Life and Wits with Mind 1 (though the forum has had discussions of other methods since it seems like you should be able to do that more directly), and reducing the difficulties of your mundane rolls with magic is something described in the general rules and the combat rules.​
    ok, so, persistent Life or Mind effects to increase attributes can invoke pattern bleed, and one could argue that turning it on and off every morning doesn’t get the body sufficient recovery time if done for a pronged period. Pattern bleed being loss of health levels without the expenditure of quintessence. So having life effects up to increase stats all the time isn’t always ideal
    Increasing dots above 5, entering supernatural levels for a prolonged period, results in permanent paradox in addition. Which again, is not always ideal. Revised had this as being the case for any and every attempt as such effects, though M20 softened this to attempts to make such changes permanent, but one could argue that if someone is casting these effects on themselves every morning, there comes a point where the distinction between permanent and temporary in how the body reacts starts to blend together. It does get ST dependent, but I would say it is a kind ST who allows their player to skirt the consequences of permanency like this.

    As for Entropy 1, it shouldn’t be a big issue so long as your mage doesn’t try to do any other sensory effects, as the more sensory effects the mage has going at once, the higher the difficulty climbs for Perception based rolls. But this isn’t actually terribly relevant regarding the domino effect, sibce sensory effects do not impact it.

    I bring this up to counter your claim that such a mage would not care about the domino effect and thus would have no reason to prefer mundane martial arts over magically enhanced attacks. It is interesting to note, however, that these examples do not actually replaced martial arts so much as make the mage that uses martial arts more prone to success. The effects you describe here add to the dice pool or reduce difficulty, generally speaking such a mage without martial arts vs such a mage with martial arts would see the mage with martial arts have the upperhand generally speaking.

    my examples instead were an attempt to get around the need for martial arts by using magic, as describe in the martial arts section of HDYDT. Matter being used to damage armour as stated in the basics, or Forces to direct the winds as written under Chi-field push. If one were to advocate for magic to replace the need for martial arts, then it would be these kinds of effects which would be better suited to this, rather than effects which would make martial arts rolls better. Magic that only increases attributes and lowers combat diff could even be argued to rely on the martial arts ability in order to achieve the sort of feats displayed in the matrix.

            Not quite. There is still the possibility of options that allow for similar tactical decision-making. We would just decouple that tactical decision from the specific moves.

            To an extent, such mechanics already exist. The Targeting a Location rule allows trading off difficulty for extra damage (although the +1 version of that is exactly as effective as using Kick instead of Punch - which is to say, only very debatably useful), and this lends itself to being generalised a bit - it becomes reasonable to apply that same mechanic to any time of high-risk, high-reward combat tactic. The choice to take a full-pool attack itself is a tactical choice, as arguably it is more reasonable to assume that combatants by default split their pool between attack and defence; so really a typical combat round is already asking a character to make a tactical choice between Desperate Defense (everything into defense, so much so that it rolls more dice in total against multiple attacks), all-out attack (no dice in defence), or a balance between attack and defence (with some form of split). Martial artist mages, of course, also have the choice of how to allocate their Arete; Magick Enhances Violence can easily lower either attack or defence difficulties, or damage can be enhanced in several ways.

            These are real tactical choices that the player is being asked to make. And compare that to the scenario that keeps coming out in defence of Kick: “What if the character’s arms are immobilised?” Well, if the character can’t use their arms, this isn’t a tactical choice at all. They use Kick because it’s the only remaining option. If they have an enhanced kicking maneuver, they’ll just use that instead.

            Under the standard M20 rules, though, extra successes really aren’t very effective. They just convert to extra damage dice, and half of those will fail to convert into actual damage (even before soak). It’s my thought that if rolling extra successes allows for extra options - especially options more useful than an extra die of bashing damage - tactics that enhance the probability of rolling extra successes become more viable.

            What I would like to put together is a set of options that any character can use, but which become more effective when employed by characters with higher dice pools or traits.

                A quick analysis of what Martial Artist actually gives through maneuvers…

                Without MA, our basic strikes are:
                Punch: our baseline (6 difficulty, Strength/B damage); other maneuvers will be compared to this.
                Kick: effectively baseline with the first level of Target a Location applied, ignoring any special effects an ST might give via fiat.

                Martial Arts 1:
                Strike: -1 difficulty, +1 damage (if hard style: no difficulty modifier if soft)
                Elbow/Knee Strike: -1 difficulty, +1 damage
                Snap Kick: -1 difficulty, +1 damage (only available in hard style)

                So the real effect here is -1 difficulty, +1 damage at MA 1. Note that this is just plain better than Brawl already, not just a parallel approach.

                Martial Arts 2:
                Spinning Kick: +0 difficulty, +3 damage
                Joint Lock: -1 difficulty, no bonus damage but inflicts Dex+MA/L instead of Strength/B
                Vital Strike: +1 difficulty, no extra dice to damage pool but damage becomes Lethal.

                Joint Lock is complicated to analyse, but the basic damaging move is effectively 1 extra damage die on top of Elbow Strike, plus a level of Target a Location.

                Martial Arts 3:
                Death Strike: -1 difficulty, +2 damage, damage becomes lethal
                Nerve Strike: +1 difficulty, +3 damage
                Thunder Kick: +1 difficulty, +3 damage

                Apart from the option to inflict lethal damage, this is the same as Elbow Strike plus two levels of Target a Location.

                I know I’m leaving out all the maneuvers that do something other than inflict damage, but for the purpose of working out how much sheer power Martial Arts adds, just looking at the damaging moves gives the best indicator; at least we compare apples to apples to some extent.

                And the truth is that, in this regard, using MA is just plain better than Brawl, but doesn’t really become more so with additional dots. And if we desire to make extremely skilled martial artists stand out even without magic, this doesn’t really do that, It makes MA better than the same number of dots of Brawling, but 4 and 5 dots do little more than add an extra die to the pool.

                                        Okay, so here’s the gist of my current thinking for my game. Note that I’m not suggesting this should replace M20 RAW for everyone else’s games. You do what you like.

                                        MA and Brawl maneuvers all go away. If I find that there’s any specific mechanic in any of them that the game really suffers without, I can look at reimplementing as a stand-alone rule, but I doubt that since all those mechanics are locked away from many characters anyway.

                                        The Targeting a Location rule gets generalised into a rule that a player can take +1, +2, or +3 on an attack roll in order to get the same number of successes if their attack achieves at least a basic degree of success after defences are applied. I haven’t run the number on this in detail to check the balance. It’s certainly better than getting the same value into damage dice (because extra successes can become damage dice anyway). Players no longer have to describe this as targeting a location; it’s just choosing to accept a higher risk of failure in order to increase the probability of a higher degree of success. As long as you say how your character is taking this risk (before your roll) you can get the modifiers: target a vulnerable (but hard to hit) location, reckless attack, a high-speed flurry of blows without attention to accuracy, whatever. It is possible that I could be convinced to allow higher than a +4 modifier here, which would certainly work well for mages who can use Magick Enhances Violence to offset the penalty, but I’m initially wary of that.

                                        Various other modifiers to difficulty can still exist. I currently can’t think of any that desperately need to be removed.

                                        Of the basic maneuvers:
                                        • Claw and Bite just become weapons.
                                        • Punch becomes the basic attack-to-damage.
                                        • Kick is subsumed into the above difficulty trade-off rule.
                                        • Flank and Rear were really circumstantial modifiers all along, and should never have been called maneuvers.
                                        • Reading an Opponent can become a separate action working pretty much the same.
                                        • The Grappling rules suck, frankly, and I’m not going to try to deal with them here. (Grappling rules always suck. They usually bypass the damage system, and are horribly broken one way or another as a result.) Suffice to say that I’m going to replace them wholesale.
                                        • Sweep and Tackle need to be replaced with a more general rule for knocking people down.
                                        • Disarm needs to be replaced with a more general rule for depriving opponents of their equipment.
                                        To work out exactly what the outcome of an attack is, declare basic intent (optionally declare anything that modifies the difficulty or die pool), roll the dice, then allocate dice to buy effects and describe in detail how your character achieves those effects.

                                        Now, because there’s a risk/reward thing going on above, I think I need to make sure that missing that first success bites. To achieve that, certain effects may cost several successes to buy unless bought with the first success. The first success must be used in line with the intent declared before the attack roll

                                        Effects that can be bought:
                                        • Deal damage: first success, or 2 successes, for Strength damage
                                          • +1 damage die: 1 success
                                          • upgrade to lethal damage: ? successes
                                        • Outmaneuver: -1/success to difficulties of your future attacks against the target, and +1/success to your target’s difficulties for attacks against you.
                                          • This is basically how you achieve a flanking position.
                                          • Generally a symmetrical modifier - if you have +1 against your opponent, your opponent has -1 against you, etc.
                                          • A character can typically use movement on their turn to automatically negate -1 against them - essentially turning to face a flanking enemy, taking a step back out of danger, etc. This can’t produce a modifier in your favour, unless the ST decides that unusual circumstances apply. Negating a greater penalty may require spending successes on outmaneuvering.
                                        • Move opponent: first success, or 2 successes, to physically shift someone who does not want to be moved
                                          • Extra successes can move them further
                                        • Knock down: 2 success including the first, or 3 successes
                                        • Disarm: 2 successes including the first, or 3 successes
                                          • 1 extra success to determine where the disarmed item falls
                                          • 2 extra successes to take the weapon for yourself
                                        Now, what will extra training do for you? Well, I’m thinking that Martial Arts specialties will allow you to count 10s as two successes as long as you are spending those successes on an effect relevant to your specialty. A specialty in a Hard Style means a 10 is 2 successes for dealing damage. A specialty in Throws (or call it Judo if you prefer) counts 10s as double for knock down and move opponent. And so on. And multiple specialties can be bought, per Well-Trained Craftsman.

                                        That’s probably not as strong as some of the top-tier maneuvers for highly-trained martial artists. However, I also intend to allow characters to choose between 1/2E and Revised era multiple action rules (I started a thread about that some months ago) which definitely boosts characters with large pools. That may be enough to make up for the loss, especially given that I don’t want martial arts without Magick to compete with many other supernatural types. (I am not building for a crossover game.)

                                        I wonder whether specialties doubling 10s is enough. However, I’ve often wondered whether specialties really matter outside of combat, too. If this isn’t good enough for martial artists, how is it good enough for a super-scientist or an Olympic athlete?

                                        Players who want to name specific moves their character likes to make can simply build what they want with appropriate modifiers and costs, and then use their chosen name to declare their intent. They pull off the maneuver if they roll the required number of successes.

                                        This is still a work in progress. The list of combat effects that can be bought with successes needs to be expanded, and I haven’t really calibrated the costs properly yet.

                                                        Incidentally, has anyone actually added up how hard the standard M20 disarm maneuver is?

                                                        Unarmed, you’re rolling Dex+Brawl at difficulty 8, with -1 die (which is essentially an application of the optional Weapon Length rule from the sidebar on page 420), and you need to roll more successes than the opponent’s Strength. If you’ve got Dex 4, Brawl 4, you’ve still got over a 75% chance of failing (or botching) that roll. 74.2% chance of failure or botch at Dex + Brawl = 9, 68.09% at 10.

                                                        Martial Arts (at 3 dots) makes it easier, because MA basically reduces the difficulty of everything by 1, but Dex 5, MA 5 is still only a 54.95% chance against that Strength 2 mook.

                                                        Of course, an unarmed disarm should be hard, but don’t tell me anybody is making that a regular part of their character’s toolset.

                                                        If the disarm roll gives at least one success, but not enough to disarm, you do get to inflict regular damage, though - which makes this an existing example of examining the rolled result before determining exactly what the character does.
