    How much should a player know?

    05-30-2023, 09:49 AM
    I am planning my first Mage campaign and have just finished reading the complete M20 book.
    I want to run a story that follows the metaplot closely, but I do not know how much my players should know.
    Condemning them to read the entire thing does not sound enjoyable nor feasible.

    Any thoughts on my small conundrum?

      In a lot of ways, the answer to this question is simply "as much as they want."

      From an OOC perspective, you cannot really control if the players decide to do some extra research on the metaplot and mysteries of the World of Darkness on the side. Conversely, you can't realistically force them to read it either. So as a Storyteller, you should be designing your game with that range of possibilities in mind, unless you know your players well enough to know whether or not some or all of them are going to actively educate themselves. Which means that anything from the metaplot that you consider "essential" knowledge for the players is something you should be prepared to give to them with the appropriate Knowledge/Skill check. Meanwhile, if you know that some of your players are more likely to dig into the background material, you can reward them for it with little "Easter Eggs" that they can pick up on which other players might miss.

      Which leads us to the IC perspective. What a given character should or should not know about the wider world of Mage should be at least roughly represented on their character sheet. If the characters have extra knowledge that they shouldn't simply because their player does, then that player is metagaming. It doesn't matter if one of your players has read every single metaplot book White Wolf ever put out, their character is not allowed to know any of it without a damn good explanation IC. If they don't have dots in Kindred Lore, they can't roll to know any of the intricate details of Vampire society found in VtM.

      Whether or not a given character should or shouldn't possess that knowledge is going to depend on the character. You can create a Mage who just Awakened and has learned next to nothing about the reality of their situation, or you can play a Hermetic apprentice who has been tutored for years in the ways of spirits and the politics of the Traditions. How much knowledge of the setting those characters will have is going to differ, but you should be able to point to that difference in dots on their character sheets.

      Metagaming is complicated, and its almost impossibly to completely separate player and character knowledge. But one option that can help you as a Storyteller is throwing your own twists into the mix. It's harder for a player to take advantage of OOC metaplot knowledge if they can never know when you're going to throw that knowledge out and replace it with something else.

          Do you mean you want the players to take on the parts of major players in the metaplot, perhaps substituting for characters like Madeline Giovanni, Shadow, Prisoner What’s-His-Number, and the like? Or do you mean you want the players to be doing their own thing, but with the Ascension War as a backdrop?

          if the former, be aware that your players won’t follow the metaplot. They just won’t. They won’t even necessarily fight against it, but they’ll make a decision sooner or later (probably sooner) that will derail it, and if your game is worth playing you’ll roll with it and wind up with a different story anyway. (And that’s a good thing. “Our alt history version of the major plot” can be a lot of fun.) And you don’t want the players to know all the twists and turns that are coming up in this case, so that they can make their own decisions. The reason we play, rather than read a book, is to make our own choices.

          If the latter, how much of the metaplot each character knows to start with will depend entirely on the character. Some characters might be newly Awakened and know nothing. (Some players will aim for this, so that they can learn as their character does.) Others might be aware of the Ascension War and be actively fighting in it already.

          Either way, the MtA novels I’ve read (The Ascension War trilogy; I haven’t bothered tracking down the others, but a friend already had that trilogy) were terrible, and I would not advise inflicting them on players who don’t really want to read them already. Truth Until Paradox is much better, and Truth Beyond Paradox much better again… but they don’t delve deep into the metaplot. Also they are spotty, and you may not wish to use all of them for your game. I’ve been reading through Truth Beyond Paradox recently, and letting my prospective players know my thoughts on each story. Like, “if you want to get a better understanding on what an Avatar might be like, or inspiration for yours, read Well Played,” or “Ignore The Theogenesis Gimmick; any conclusions you might draw from it may not be true in my game”.

              I'm assuming that your players are new to mage, if that is the case, my advice is this: let them learn the metaplot you want them to learn during the course of the campaign. Apart from that, give them whatever info you deem relevant for the start of the campaign. That said, keep in mind that very few mages have a complete image of what's really happening behind the curtains, the majority only know bits and pieces mixed with lies and distorted info. The player characters shouldn't know much, and what they know shouldn't be necessarily accurate.
