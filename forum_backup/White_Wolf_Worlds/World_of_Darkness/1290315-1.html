                    The Technocracy believe that they have the moral high ground. That their way - of One Reality - is unequivocally the correct one, and that everyone else are dangerous lunatics. The Technocratic Union believes they, alone, are right.

                    That righteousness allows them to rationalize a bottomless well of sins. They believe they have the Answer, ergo any underhanded act done in service to that answer is worth the price.

                    Not even getting into how their centuries of success have bred both complacency and corruption. The Syndicate, for instance, doesn't even really believe that Mass Ascension is actually possible. They are content to create a world that is merely comfortable and convenient for Sleepers, even as it sinks those same Sleepers into their stupors. If they can make money for themselves along the way, even better. Then you have the New World Order, which actively brainwashed Union members for years, just to prevent them from speaking, acting, or thinking contrary to the Union's official policy. If the ones at the top had their way, there would BE no free thought, for fear of even the possibility of treachery or deviance.

                    Of course the Union isn't irredeemable. They have many good points (just ask a Progenitor about why stuff like faith healing or alternative medicine is dangerous). And, especially in the Revised era and beyond, you see many of the idealistic Technocrats, who operate on the front lines, forcing changes on the system that makes the Union less abominable. (Like, for instance, Iteration X getting more compact brain implants, so they can get some of their brain meats put back in, that controlled empathy and shit). But that was a change that was only necessary because the Union had become so corrupt, and drifted away from their high minded goals.

                    Further, many of those systemic problems still remained, because the Union was founded on the idea that if they crush all mysticism and displace the superstitionists, that a perfect world will naturally fall into place. A belief that not only allows them to justify any sort of activity, but also blinds them to the problems inherent in the industrial society they spent so long building. Blind to the excesses of corporations, the abuses of government, and the new vistas of abuse and depravity that can arise from new technology like the Internet (and the failure of businesses and systems to tackle such problems on an individual level, because it would be "too hard" or cost too much money).

                            Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
                            What is HOP / HAP?
                            Sigh. Okay, let's take it from the top.

                            HOP: Hypothetical Omniscient Observer
                            HAP: Hypothetical Average Observer

                            These are two ways that the rules for Magick can be interpreted, when it comes to Effects being Coincidental or Vulgar. This deriving from the idea that we're judging it according to a hypothetical observer, against whom the mage is trying to fool.

                            With HOP, you assume that the observer is functionally omniscient. They can see into your pockets, have night vision, perfectly understand physics and biology (and can see them in action), and otherwise has no limits to what it can know about what you, the mage, are up to. It's not a stretch to assume that under HOP, basically nothing is Coincidental, because the Hypothetical Observer has perfect knowledge of the situation and how the mage is violating the Consensus.

                            With HAP, you assume an observer that is a normal, average human being, who believes all the most widely believed aspects of the Consensus. The cannot see in your pockets, they cannot see in the dark, they cannot tell water from vodka at a glance (though they may be able to smell the difference), and they don't see a bone-cracking punch as being anything more than a punch. Most people - including the designers behind M20 - seem to agree that this is preferable, as it gives a more interesting latitude for players and storytellers to operate. Because it's actually possible for an Effect to be Coincidental, so long as the mage is clever. It rewards lateral thinking, sleight of hand, and novel interpretations of both Spheres and Instruments.

                                    This is what I mean by Mage Forum Bingo. There are certain topics that have a tendency to run out of control. People who have seen them before tend to brace for impact when they see them, but they also draw people in because there are strong opinions about them. Even now, I have a strong urge to correct what Bluecho said about HOP, even though this isn't a HAP/HOP thread. These topics have a life of their own.

                                    The Technocracy Are the Real Good Guys is another one, because it often involves people skewing what one side or the other is about. Often it won't be too long before you get a small group of people insisting that the Traditions want to take humanity back to the dark ages or that the Technocracy wants to turn the world into an Orwellian nightmare. Neither of those is true. At worst the two are only wiling to risk those outcomes happening.

                                    Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
                                    And I see the points related but I am not an expert in the lore, for me, as an Awakening player the point is/was: findout what is right and do that (the main problem in Awakening is nobody is sure what is right); here it seems that whatever the Technocracy aims end up being (as stated in that paragraph, by the developers) would be considered an improvement, and ties with my (admittedly skewed) idea of what is good for mankind in the WoD
                                    I am seeing some of your issue. While there are some broad similarities, Awakening and Ascension are very different games. The overall conflict of the Pentacle vs the Seers could be loosely described as 'is it better to try to escape the prison or butter up the jailers?' That's not what's going on between the Traditions and the Technocracy. That fight is an ideological one about Freedom vs Security.

                                    You mention that in Awakening no one knows what is right. That's true in Ascension as well. It's one of the themes even. You have phenomenal cosmic power but are no better situated to tell right from wrong than anyone else. What do you do? How much are you willing to fight for your ideals? What are you willing to sacrifice? As Exhibit A we have a faction that sees humanity as the most important thing in the world and wants to see them dominate the world and is willing to manipulate them, stomp out their dreams, indoctrinate them, and take away what makes them human to see it happen. As Exhibit B we have a faction that sees humanity as the most important thing in the world and wants to see them rise to the very heights of enlightenment and creativity, and is willing to let them fall, be preyed upon by monsters, and live in a chaotic world if that is the price. Both the Traditions and the Technocracy are high minded idealists with a heaping helping of moral flaws.

                                    People new to the game often initially see the Traditions as the clear good guys and the Technocracy as the clear bad guys and get surprised when they realize the Technocracy has a lot going for it. They've come a long way from the early days of Mage when they were just reality dominating villains. Just remember, they are also a bunch of well intentioned extremists who are willing to do terrible things if it comes to it.
