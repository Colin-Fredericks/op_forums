    A single gender society has the obvious difficulty of generating new members. The fact is that as members of the same species they have more in common with each other than they have separately. This is not to say their are not differences between the genders. I would propose considering "Nesting Syndrome". Often women nest while expecting. Men do not nearly as often tend to nest. It is not that men cannot clean house, but if she is already doing so - and trying to help only gets one yelled at - why bother? It is precisely because she is already doing so that makes it unnecessary for him to do so. What is a biological difference to varying degrees then becomes a social expectation. That expectation becomes ritualized, customized, and legitimized into gender roles. However, those roles are socially and biologically dependent upon the other. I find gender only societies less likely than gender restricted institutions (gentlemen's clubs or ladies' clubs) which still require the other to perform socially defined gender specific roles. I would propose that a splat which defines a social group as operating on a specific set of gender based issues or agenda should specifically define the supporting roles the other gender is relied upon to perform.

            With supernatural creatures, the reasons for gender exclusivity could be far different than for human institutions. While there are still a number of gender exclusive real world organizations, most of which are long lasting groups which carry with then some traditional cultural or even religious values. This tendency can even be amplified with supernaturals. Vampires are immortal and the elders have great power, so a tradition started centuries or millennia ago could be continued by the very people who started that tradition, especially as vampire elders tend to be conservative. Werewolves could have values which are influenced by spirits, such as Pegasus with the Black Furies. Vampires have the advantage of unlimited asexual reproduction, which werewolves don't have. Mage Traditions can be exclusive, but with the limited number of humans with the potential to awaken, it is likely to be a major disadvantage to reject potential Tradition members on the basis of gender.

            I would like to see some male only splats. A vampire Bloodline would work well for this and I could imagine all sorts of potential male only Bloodlines. That wouldn't really work for the splat I've created. I suspect that White Wolf doesn't have any exclusively male groups and a number of (almost) exclusively female factions is because it would be politically incorrect to have that.

                And that's why you don't see a lot of all-male splats. As offended as people get from the lack of all-male splats to restore some kind of RPG-character gender-equality option, the game line and company is going to get a lot more bad press if it comes out with an all-male splat that is perceived as misogynist. Which, if it's based on some mytho-historical antecedent it's probably going to be without some careful dancing around how "that's how things were, but NOW the reason we're still an all-boys club is..." And the dancing is likely to get ignored.

                There are other subjects that aren't going to get done for the same reasons. We're unlikely to see splats that directly address racial issues in a manner that prefers particular races where modern concerns about race would come into play (e.g. a fiat in Werewolf that all Silver Fangs are Caucasians and members of other races are not allowed). We're unlikely to see another splat that deals with sexual orientation and transgender issues by ascribing them to negative influences (<cough> Wu Keng <cough>), or that portray disabilities in a derogatory manner (like saying autism in inherently Banal).

                To a degree it's something like deciding not to go into the mine field even if you think there is something there you really want. Is it worth the chance things could blow up catastrophically?

                I've seen this close up. I walked away from a gaming company start-up due to the fact that the other principals could not encompass the fact that what few female characters they were writing were all odious stereotypes, up to and including putting a succubus in lingerie on the cover of the first release. The game company in question wasn't very successful...

                                        Responding to the non-questions first:

                                        Originally posted by von Lyttenburgh View Post
                                        Becuase writing all-female splatbooks is seen as promoting "grrrl power" and gender equality, while the mere thought that there might be all-male splat in the working would create a shitsorm of epic proportions even before its official release.
                                        I am reminded of my days in college radio when some news stories broke. One was about an 18 year old who married a 16 year old with the parents' written consent. As the honeymoon was beginning the police arrested him for statutory rape. In another state that same year an 18 year old got pregnant by the 13 year old she was babysitting and was awarded child support. It is a shame that our arts must reflect this same sort of double standard towards masculinity rather than having to avoid even the suggestion that such actions are hypocritical.

                                        Originally posted by von Lyttenburgh View Post
                                        As for me the existence of any kind of gender inequality in the supernatural societies is just silly. Ability to rip out other people's throats before their eyes, IMHO, makes wonder with your self-esteem and other's perception of you, no matter what.
                                        I have never bought into the "gender inequality" line. Frankly, I consider it BS. Social roles exist in response to maximized return for the social organism. Where the society needs explosive brute strength, the role assignment is masculine. Where a society needs social cohesion or pragmatic endurence the role assignment is feminine. In the late 1800s big business attempted to expand competition for jobs by seeking to employ women equally with men. Suffragets took them to the Supreme Court arguing that it was too important for women's strength to be kept for bearing children and women should not be required to work like men. OSHA was created but its rules sloppily enforced until women went to work in the factories for the War effort. Prior to that 1 in 75 men were maimed on the job every year and 1 in 142 were killed on the job every year. Only recently have women started to seek jobs, and in much lower numbers than men, in the most dangerous jobs. Yet, Roman writers wrote of the fierceness and fitness for combat among women of the far north. The most elite military force created by Sun Tzu was from among the Emperor's concubines. During an early failure of Japan to invade Okinawa, the men responded to a feint at the North while imperial forces maneuvered around them only to be defeated by the women of Naha. The ability to kill, supernatural or mundane, has never been limited to gender, and has been celebrated for both sexes. Further, while modern women scoff at the limitations that accompanied the protected status of women in the past, they never recognize the cost on men - for whom the absolute obligation to die in their defense and be responsible for all legal and fiduciary matters shortened their life expectancy by years in comparison to the protected sex. Now as women are demanding the assumption of those same risks and responsibilities once assigned socially to men, heart disease - stress - has become the number one killer of women in identical percentages to men. The consequences come with the option. Out of this milieu is created every supernatural creature in the WoD. Even vampires, whose specific supernatural reproduction has nothing to do with gender, hold it absolutely taboo to embrace a child - someone who is not already socialized to the gender assignment.

Define their point of view as what you need it to be in order to support the direction you are taking your troupe.

                                            By definition a splatbook is one that is published by the company supporting the game line.

                                            Therefore, addressing issues of sociology in a complex manner is going to bow to public perception and response. You can talk all you want about how vampire and werewolf women and men are utterly equal because they are equally proficient in being killing machines, etc. etc., but these games have to SELL. And putting yourself square in the line of fire is apt to get you shot.

                                            Of course, you should play with gender roles in supernatural groups in each and every game. You can completely ignore them. You can emphasize them. We're dealing with that question right now in my upcoming Mage chronicle. It's set in the 1920's and it's going to be very interactive with the mundane environment of persons who do not stalk the night, shapechange, etc. All equations of relative rates of heart disease aside, considering the activities that players have their characters do, the situation for women and minorities SUCKED in the 1920s.... So we are figuring out how to reflect that. Is simply being a woman or a minority worth Freebies as a Flaw?

                                            But the question really goes to what you can publish and sell without the kind of negative publicity that might negatively impact the gameline or company. I venture to say that, considering the observation that White Wolf brought more females to the gaming table (and LARPing), not throwing grenades out there to have to fall on later might have been the wisest strategy.

                                                            The long and the short of it is, for your own creations...do you think it should be something you should be ashamed of making? If not, make it. If you find it offends people, see if you understand and agree with why. If so, then you may need to reexamine your worldview. If not, then don't let the nattering naybobs of political correctness control your life and creative spark. Make cool things. Let them stand on their own merits, and don't worry about whether others approve, unless you find you agree with their disapproval. Your judgment is what matters more than anybody else's in your own creations.

                                                            Yes, that means that even if I tell you later that I think your creation is a stinking pile of dog maneure, you're within your rights to ignore me if you think I'm full of it, myself. Of course, I reserve the right to say so, anyway, if I do think so. But that's the nature of sharing opinions. Sometimes, you hear ones you don't like. (I will, because I find it silly to do otherwise, endeavor to be polite even if I do not like something, however. But that's me, and my own preferences.)
