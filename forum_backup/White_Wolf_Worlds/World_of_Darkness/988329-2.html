    Originally posted by Frozenfishy View Post
    Something that I don't think that I've seen directly addressed (if maybe tangentially) about your powergamers: Paradigm/Focus needs to be enforced. I've seen plenty of people come to forums or the table wanting to build a character whose Paradigm roughly equates to "I see the truth of reality, and thus make my will manifest." Players who know how the Sphere system works, and who understand the Consensus, understand what we call the "Purple Paradigm," and sometimes will try to make that transfer into character knowledge. That's a big no no.

    Instruments (or foci, whatever term you're using) that are more or less "I cast the magick with my mind" should be highly discouraged, or at least regulated. Skipping all the stumbling blocks and restrictions that is inherent Paradigm/Focus removes an enormous amount of depth from the game and is a clear path to powergaming.

    That's not to say that things like telekinesis, telepathy, and other tele- words are out of bounds for mages. On the contrary, they're pretty proliferate in the Society of Ether, and it's been at least "studied" in the halls of the Technoccracy. However, one can't, or shouldn't, simply say "I do it with my mind." Explain it. How do you do it with your mind? What principles are you using? Why can't other people do it? Do you use a point of Willpower to represent using mind power to do it? Do you concentrate on/through a crystal that has a specific resonating property that magnifies your brain power? Do you see where I'm going with this?

    Magick, inherently in the system, has a price, some manner of inconvenience standing between a mage and immediate, unrestrained access to her magick. Maybe it's a dance or a chant, a magick word or a specific thought form, a consumed point of Willpower or a raised difficulty (as per the rules for casting without instruments).

        1) Paradigm is arguably the central premise of Mage. The whole game is all about the idea that Belief shapes Reality, and that all conventions of Reality are simply the manifestation of shared practises and interpretations. In this way, anything magical can work if enough people belief it to work. The only reason Science works the way it does is because enough people believe in the Scientific Paradigm as popularised by the Technocracy. If, say, the Order of Heremes were in charge and most of humanity believed their consensus as truth, then Scientific instruments either would not work, or would highly vulgar magic.

        The reason this matters is because Mage is all about the hubris of paradaigm, and how every mage considers their view of reality to be the "correct" one, and that all others are either perverting reality, or are simply too caught up in their own world view to see things the "right" way. This is the main conflict between the Traditions and the Technocracy. From a completely subjective viewpoint, they are both doing the same thing, but neither one would ever admit that Enlightened Science and Traditional Magick are related.
        The Traditions consider the Technocracy to be suppressing their practices by enforcing their static view of reality on to sleepers, and thus may drive away humanities innate ability to shape the world around them.
        The Technocracy, on the other hand, would say that they are simply out to keep reality predictable enough to understand and control without need of awakening or specialist knowledge, and the Traditions are simply a group of close minded mystics who's "arts" are not only dangerous, but subvert the work of their own scientists in ensuring reality is predictable.

        So too is Paradigm important within the Traditions themselves. Each one comes from a different school of how magic is performed, and thus a different interpretation on what the Avatar is, on what Quintessence is, in what Nodes are, and what an act of magic requires in order to function.

        Verbena, for example, consider magic to be the very essence of life, and thus only by embracing natural things can one tune their mind to real magic. The Avatar is powerful spiritual force which guides you and Quintessence is that mystical life energy that flows through everything, and can be felt in blood.
        Akashics instead would say that all that is magic comes from within. The Avatar is a way of thinking about an internal power known as qi, one that can be harnessed with enough discipline and training, and quintessence is merely the manifestation of this Qi force.
        Etherites, however, would consider magic and science to be interchangeable, and that rather than doing something all mystic and old, what they're doing is taking advantage of long discarded theories to achieve amazing results. To them, the Avatar is more like Genius, a form of inspirational brilliance that few possess, while many have remarkably different theories on what Quintessence is and how to best utilise it.

        Even within a tradition, there are different prevailing paradigms. Order of Heremes for example:

        A lot of Hermetics believe that the words have intrinsic power in and of themselves, and the older the words, the more potent the power, whether that be the ancient tongues, or long dead scripture, these words hold within them secrets from a time when magic was as common as air, and as such invoking the power of these words brings about these powers.

        Another common theory amongst Hermetics is that the world is built upon language, that everything that we ever experience is simply built upon a foundation of thoughts and ideas, not unlike Plato's theory of the Forms. As such, if one can tap into the language of the universe, they can construct completely new sentences. In many ways this is not all that different from the Virtual Adepts belief of the Universal Source Code, but you'll never here a Hermetic admit this.
        There are a number of Hermetics that consider the very act of casting the spell to be the source of magic. It's not so much that the words have power, but that the act of saying these words puts your mind back in the same mindset of those in the past. So by saying the words, writing the runes, and enacting the rituals, you are able to replicate the effects done in years gone by. This idea is not unlike how Sorcerers perform magic, but applied to the Spheres. It has much more freedom than traditional Sorcery, but it's still highly ritualised.
        As one final example, there are Hermetics that consider Ancient Languages to be a direct method of communication with gods or angels, and so they use these methods of communication in order to attain a form of divine intervention. Depending on which belief the mage ascribes to, this could be to the ancient Egyptian gods, the Greek gods, or any of the other gods that make up the High Court of the Umbrood.

        All this serves that point that paradigm is as unique to the mage at the mage themselves, and the Traditions are little more than a way of grouping generally speaking like minded individuals and fostering the next generation with specific ideas to grow from. In many ways this is exactly what happens in the real world with culture.

        So what happens when a mage sees one who is doing magic unlike that which they perform? Depends on what they are looking at.

        A Hermetic from House Flambeau make look upon the charts and diagrams of a Hermetic from House Fortunae and have no idea what it is they are doing. While the former relies on Staves and Wands, the latter relies on Numbers and Charts. They recognise the importance and legitimacy of the other's work, but just simply don't have the expertise to understand what it is the other is doing. Sort of like when a Biologist and an Accountant try to teach one another of their trades. They don't instantly dismiss the other, but they don't quite understand what's going on either.

        This same House Flambeau Hermetic may look at the practices of the Akashic monks and recognise their solitary practice to achieve mental mastery is not unlike the Hermetic's own tradition which emphasises study, practice, and painstaking diligence to adhered methods. So while the methods may differ in places, they share a similar ethos of self actualisation. So he may conclude that, while he doesn't understand their methods, he can appreciate that it's all about Magic through hard work.
        If this Hermetic encountered a Verbena witch, on the other hand, their outlook on their practice would be much less favourable. Their magic is raw, uncivilised, and very messy, often times involving copious amounts of blood, sweat, and moonlight chanting. Their ways are outmoded, outdated, and firmly stuck in an ancient time that the Hermetics have long since developed from. The Verbena's magic is little more than sorcery as far as he's concerned. Sure they are capable of doing amazing things, but so are many other hedge mages. Their methods are simply barbaric and not to be encouraged.
        When faced with a Etherite, they may be curious by the toys that they produce, but at the end of the day that's all they are. At best they are a neat little distraction, and at worst they support the Technocratic paradigm and thus have a role in suppressing "real" magic. If an Etherite's tools have a basis in Hermetic mythology, however, they'd probably look more favourably upon them, though they would consider this scientist to be rather misguided in their approach.
        Faced with Hollow One, most Hermetics look down on them either in pity, or in anger. It takes years of dedicated study and practice for a Hermetic to achieve the level of power they have accrued, and these Goth kids are walking around firing off half-baked effects as if they were mere parlour tricks!? It's an absolute mockery. Get them out of my face!

        The fact that your players all chose different paradigms is actually a really good thing, it gives them a chance to roleplay these kinds of disputes. Perhaps a rivalry will spring up between two contrary factions, or maybe the similarities will be emphasised over the differences allowing the mages to work together. Philosophical discussions like these lie at the core of Mage, and I think they should always be encouraged when possible.


        2) It's not about how many dots in a sphere you have, it's about the highest sphere rate in the effect being cast. If your mage has Matter 3, then they can do Matter 1 effects, Matter 2 effects, and Matter 3 effects. Matter 1 effects, like determining what something is made off, will always be easier than a Matter 3 effect, like causing a wall to crumble into dust.

        3) Extended magic, when not done as a Ritual, has the following drawbacks.

        - The difficulty of the magic increases by 1 for every extra roll (up to the limit of +3 base difficulty)
        - A Botch causes the entire thing to fall down, and in addition to the normal Botch paradox, you get an additional point per extra roll.
        - (optional) Any effect which gets above 10 successes is no longer coincidental, and thus gives the caster Paradox
        - (optional) If you want, you can set a failure limit. So if a mage fails more than the limit, then the effect fails completely.

        Making the extended magic roll a Ritual casting does the following

        - A lot of time is taken between rolls, depending on the complexity of the ritual this could be anywhere between 10 minutes to a whole hour per roll
        - Characters are therefor limited in terms of how much time they have as well as how much continuous work their body can take before collapsing.
        - The difficulty does not increase per roll
        - 10 successes does not turn a coincidental effect into a vulgar one.
        - Depending on what is being done, many more effects may be considered coincidental when cast in this way.
        - A Failure results in a single rise in difficulty
        - A Botch results in the entire effect botching, paradox is given out as stated above.
        - Will Power can be used to turn a Botch into a Failure, but only once per ritual, and this results in a single success being lost from the count.

        - Mages may work together on rituals. The requirements are that each mage must have the minimum Sphere Rank required to perform the spell on their own.
        - Mages working in tandem like this will roll individually and pool successes together. A single botch results in the entire effect botching and everyone getting paradox.
        - Mages who have at least one dot in all the spheres required, but lack the appropriate levels, may still contribute a single success to the pool.

        Extended rolls the quick way may work while in a battle, especially for things that only require 5 or 10 successes.
        But when you're trying to do something that requires 20, 40, or even 60 successes, doing this without a Ritual is pretty impossible. The risk of a botch becomes all too real.

                To expand on Saikou's point, understand that a Focus is not just a set of recipes or tricks. It's a World View, that the Mage must internalize in order to make use of. A Chorister truly believes in the power and primacy of her particular vision of God. An Ecstasy Cultist truly believes that the world around them is an illusion, malleable with but the right change in perception. The Son of Ether truly believes in the existence of the luminiferous ether, and the technological applications of same. These are deep-seated models for reality that Mages hold dear, important not only to their perceptions of the world, but to their self-identity. And it's a World View they've spent at least a few years training in, ever since they Awakened (if not before, in the case of religious persons or former Sorcerers).

                So when a Mage sees another Mage performing Magick in a manner contrary to the way they've been taught or learned for themselves, their beliefs won't crumble just like that. It can give them Cognitive Dissonance, yes, but human beings are exemplary at resolving Cognitive Dissonance. They can simply come up with any number of rationalizations for the contradiction. The Tradition Council (and the Traditions themselves, internally) are an alliance of convenience, built upon a web of such rationalizations, and a polite mutual agreement not to make a big deal about it.

                When you get back to your game, then, take the players aside for a moment and help them understand this fact. That even if the players understand that Focus is a construction, that their characters are fully and completely committed to the World Views that fuel their Magick. Even if they can understand another Mage's point of view, knowing it intellectually and internalizing it are two different things. Then, ask the players what their characters think of their fellow PCs, and what they think each other are doing from the lens of their own Focus.

                                There are many ways to deal with Cognitive Dissonance. One of these, yes, is to abandon one's original beliefs for new ones. But that only happens if the new evidence is too compelling to ignore. And humans have an enormous threshold for what counts as compelling enough to make them reject everything they've believed. Especially if they can think of something - anything - that can rationalize that evidence within the bounds of one's existing world view.

                                It's significantly easier to come up with excuses, twist logic to suit one's ends, or simply reject the evidence out of hand (at which point, because of Confirmation Bias, they will unconsciously selectively forget evidence that contradicts their views, while remembering clearly evidence that supports them). I mean easier from a cognitive energy standpoint, as well as from the standpoint of simply avoiding having to confront the idea that their views may be wrong (a painful proposition, and one people dodge out of whenever possible).

                                Plus, as Resplendent Fire said, the Mage's original world view works. It provides them a framework for performing miracles. This is, from the Mage's perspective, irrefutable evidence in support of their vision of reality. That someone else can perform different acts to accomplish the same things is not a deal-breaker in most Mage's eyes. Is this Hubris? Most certainly. But it's also completely understandable.

                                                This is one of the parts of M20's Focus rules that requires a bit of player and storyteller thinking. Because the idea of Everything is an Illusion, Prison, or Mistake can mean different things for different people. As can the Art of Desire.

                                                The best way to handle this is to ask what that means to the player. If Reality is an illusion/prison/mistake, in what way is it like that? How does their character think the world works? What's their view of cosmology? Granted, this requires one to really think about philosophy, which can be difficult for anyone who has never considered the subject. Really, the various Paradigms, Practices, and Instruments that M20 lists are broad outlines for what should be a more cohesive, more integrated, and more personalized whole.

                                                To try to brainstorm ideas for this particular example, the Mage character believes that what they see is a lie, or a fabrication, or is simply dictated by arbitrary rules because it was never meant to be. Which of these the Mage believes in is very important, as it informs how they would use their Art of Desire to exploit reality's nature. With the Art of Desire, the character uses their Instruments to either give a person what they desire (even if it's a promise unfulfilled in the end), or to embody qualities people find desirable. It's about knowing people, how they tick, and what influences them. In many ways, using the Art of Desire is like using mundane techniques that affable people use to win friends and influence people, but in such a way that they have a supernatural edge. Wearing all the best clothing, waving sweet-smelling flowers around or putting on scents, speaking in compelling manners, employing body language, maintaining eye contact, establishing ties with people, swearing promises and drafting contracts, etc.

                                                It would be much easier to know what the character's Tradition is, as this will inform the cultural framework we're working with. A Hermetic approaches the Art of Desire differently than an Ecstasy Cultist, or a Verbana, or a Syndicate rep. Moreover, they all have their own definitions about what it means for Everything to be Illusion, etc.

                                                I'm sorry if I'm not being very helpful. Once you've figured out the wavelength that Mage is running on, it becomes easier to dig into these broad catagories and make a workable Focus from them. But until then, it's a lot of heady business. Abstract.

                                                    Perhaps it would be better to start with an example Focus. This one is an idea I've been toying with for a while now.

                                                    A member of the Cult of Ecstasy who mixes a vague understanding of Hindu/Buddhist mysticism (namely the idea of Everything is an Illusion, Prison, or Mistake), with the works of Lewis Carroll. From their viewpoint, the world around them is a huge dream that sleeping humanity is having. Going to sleep (or traversing the Umbra) is a matter of traveling into deeper levels of dream, but they are nonetheless still "sleeping" even when awake. There is another level beyond them that is true wakefulness, and that each lifetime a person lives is actually a "night" they spend in slumber and in dream.

                                                    The world is an illusion because it is the fancy of sleeping minds. Mages are simply those that break from the narrative, and become lucid dreamers. As such, all Magick is merely the act of taking control of the dream and bending it to their whim.

                                                    So how does this loony make that happen? Mostly using Crazy Wisdom, but we'll apply a little Art of Desire as well. There is, after all, something alluring about madness. Dressed in Victorian fashion, he swaggers around in a velvet coat, lacy cravat, a poppy blossom in the front pocket, and colorfully painted cane. Hair disheveled but glossy, eyes bobbing from wild to misty. Offering cookies labeled with phrases like "Eat Me". Speaking softly into someone's ear. Reciting poetry that doesn't make a lick of sense, but a manner that is pleasing to listen to regardless. A simply moving and speaking in an airy, weightless way, as if proclaiming that the forces and laws of the local environment are but suggestions.

                                                    All these things not only get into people's heads when they are around the Cultist, but actually gets into the head of the Dream itself, reshaping it.

                                                    As for Crazy Wisdom, the Cultist knows that it's all a dream, so they work to promote dream logic. Symbols are used heavily. Everyday - and not-so-everyday - objects manipulated in strange ways, as a means of getting across ideas more in line with what the Mage wants. Playing cards and dice and other games of chance or skill. Mirrors and storybook illustrations. Fresh produce and kitchen utensils. Fans and cups and items procured from exotic lands. Magick is driven by metaphor, as are dreams.

                                                    Puns serve much the same purpose, bending wordplay towards unconventional correlations, so that they may be used for Sympathetic Magic. Cracking an egg in order to crack a safe. Running wire for summoning fire. An Ace of Spades, for curing AIDS. Or is that facilitating trades?

                                                    Meditation, for a dreamer, is very like daydreaming. So why can't the Cultist sit and spin images and actions in their head, and have them leak out to infect the outer world? Dreams are dreams, and the divisions between levels of dream can blur together. And there's nothing like having a nap and lucid dreaming to jump off into wanderings through the dreamlands. And, from there, to greater and more wondrous Astral realms.

                                                    If nothing else, simply announcing loudly that the world will change in some way or another can be enough to alter the course of the dream. If you insist, the rocks might decide there's no reason for them not to suddenly be butter. Logic and proportion don't mean anything at all, so butter the rocks shall be.

                                                            Originally posted by artuard View Post
                                                            The character in question has chosen and Orphan with several spheres on 2 dots: mind, matter, prime and time. His focus is : Paradigm: Everything is a illusion, Practice: Art of desire, Instruments: Elements ( he chose this one for me to try and come up with a magic effect which is ARGH how do I do it atm )

                                                            Well there's your first problem. They chose an Orphan.

                                                            The reason why other posters advocated starting with a specific Tradition is because it creates an archetype the player can work with as a base. From there, it becomes easier to work the character into their own person. A Hermetic character is a wizard; now the player decides what kind of wizard they want to play. An Akashic character is a mystic martial artist; what kind of kung fu type person does the player want them to be?

                                                            Orphans are difficult for new players because they need to build their concept completely from scratch. And that IS hard.

                                                            If character generation isn't too far gone, you might want to talk to the player about switching to a non-Orphan, and onto a more established Mage archetype. The nine Traditions offer a wealth of potential types to choose from. Witches, shamans, mad scientists, Sudhus and yogis, miracle workers, super hackers, etc. It'll be a lot easier to figure out what Instruments the character would use to perform a particular Effect when popular culture and legend have paved the way so thoroughly.
