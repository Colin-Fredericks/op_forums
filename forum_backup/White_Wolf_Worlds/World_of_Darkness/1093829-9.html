            You're right, of course. I watched adult horror movies in my early tens and most of the times they didn't give me the creep at all. I started reading Lovecraft very early and while I started playing VtM around 16, I actually read novels a lot earlier. I got an informative version of Dracula (a very shortened version, with a lot of pictures and educational content about the whole vampire myth and the original book and pop-culture connections. It is a quite good book, I still have it.) maybe even earlier than 10, I don't remember exactly when, and I was fascinated by vampires and overall the supernatural from that on. What I want to say is, yes there are children who could handle topics not even every grown-up could, but in general, WoD is not for children and I don't want it to be.

            I also agree with the remark that the big 3 cores are more mild than the supplements, although there are "problematic" things in the VtM revised core too, like the picture about ghouls slavering victims and lets not forget the whole theme of patricide in VtM. It's just not children material. I'd also argue that, while most of the "edgier" things were in supplements, those supplements are quite important ones, like a guide to one one of the main fractions of the game, or to the primary antagonist, or even the ST guide.

            Originally posted by Aya Tari View Post
            I guess that I am trying to say that these type of things are not only tasteless, they are bad for business, and will end up dooming the gamelines by driving away the new customers that cWoD needs.
            Tasteless is quite subjective, you'd hardly pressed to do mature content nobody at all finds tasteless and I'd bet it would end up a very sanitized and bland product. Besides, I don't think it drives away the kind of new customers it was aimed at, which aren't children and never were.


            Originally posted by kalinara View Post
            I don't think it's fair to say that the intro fiction in Mage 20 was "about" incestuous child rape. There were two sentences in the entire story that reference a particular incident in a character's past, one line was fairly graphic (described as a sense memory of the survivor) and one much more vague . The story was about the two adult characters reconnecting, having been enemies and allies in the past. The fact that both characters were survivors of abuse was presented in a heavy-handed fashion, but was relevant as a point of commonality. And honestly, it's hardly the first time that rape, abuse, or murder has been portrayed in the fluff fiction of these source books.
            Totally agree. You know, I love people on the internet have the extraordinary ability to pick up one sentence, or picture and make a great fuss about it, like it was the core theme and biggest thing of that product...

            As a cis person, I don't feel like I have the right perspective to say whether or not Avery's portrayal was damaging. I don't however think that we need to shy away from complex and/or adult themes in general. These are games that, while played by children, have always been written toward an adult audience. And honestly, I would rather see World of Darkness make the attempt to include characters who are transgender or abuse survivors, even with the caveat that they're probably going to get them wrong sometimes. I'd rather see them learn to do better than not try at all.
            Agree with that too. I don't want them to shy away from hard topics, or censuring themselves into one specific kind of portrayal. Yes, they'll misstep sometimes, that's a part of it too, but the sky won't fall.

            And I think it's fair to point out that we're talking about a nostalgia product from twenty-years ago, that got its resurgence through kickstarter funding and is solely distributed through a pdf/pod site. This isn't a matter of a thirteen year old finding a hardcover sourcebook in the local comic shop. The only people buying these books are adults. Odds are, the only children who ever encounter these books will be the children of the adults who already play this game. And that's the point when it's the responsibility of the parents to decide whether or not to present this material to their kids and if so, how.
            Yeah, thought about that too, I think you're right.

            At the risk of being pretentious, I think that if White Wolf decides to create their new version of the World of Darkness to cater to those players who would see one admittedly graphic reference to abuse and go play Pathfinder instead, then it's definitely not going to be the World of Darkness that we remember and enjoyed.
            Yup. Noticed the theme?

            What I see is people have personal issues and trigger points they don't want to see in books (or strong expectations about how they want to see them) and differing levels of how much darkness they want to seep into the game from the real-world darkness of humanity. All those talk about "edgelords" and stuff is just the manifestation of that. That's okay, we aren't all the same, I can understand that. However, I think, as I said above, if you want to do a "safe" product, it'll be bland IMO and it won't be WoD at all. Thus they'll get push-back from some people and some outrage, but yeah, what I see outside this forum and rpg.net (and mostly from the same posters on both forums) is mostly a positive reaction toward WW. I'm not saying everything they do is perfect, absolutely not, they'd have serious missteps and V5 might be still a game I won't like in the end, but I don't hate them and actually what Martin said back about handling controversial topics, I fully agreed with that. I honestly think that it'll be too edgy to some people might be actually a good thing, ultimately.

                                    There's a pretty big difference between one clearly misfired attempt at depicting a trans character and the mindset behind the production of the entire World of Darkness: G*psies book.

                                    This is NOT an attempt to defend Avery. But I think it's fair to highlight something Aleph said: that Avery is very similar to characters a lot of us created and played at age 15.

                                    Strictly in terms of "edginess", it's hard to say she's anywhere near the level of past signature characters like Sascha Vykos. But I do think it shows the company's interest in representation rather than tokenism. Hopefully, they do end up incorporating the criticism, and Avery becomes the precursor to the inclusion of other, better developed and less harmful trans characters.

                                    Similarly, their idea of having an actual Asian person as lead developer of Kindred of the East isn't a bad one. I hope they choose someone who either has academic experience with multiple Asian cultures or they include Asian people from multiple Asian cultures on the development team. It doesn't mean that they will produce a good product, obviously. But I do think the emphasis on the points of view of actual Asian people for their Asia-themed product is a good sign that they're starting to learn from past mistakes.
