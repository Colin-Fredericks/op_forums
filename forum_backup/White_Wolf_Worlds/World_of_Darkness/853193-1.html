                        You folks do realize that you can buy autoclaves, PCR machines, DNA synthesizers, etc. direct off of Ebay or LabX, right? Brand to fairly new equipment is a hell of a lot pricier of course (tens to hundreds of thousands of dollars is a typical price range), but good luck getting full price out of someone for stolen, unsupported equipment that you've probably damaged via indelicate handling (not to mention the impracticality attendant in trying to cart off a lot of this sort of stuff).

                        The real value of corporate etc. R&D facilities is in information- drug pipelines and candidates, clinical trial data, prototypes, corporate behavior of dubious legality, etc.- the sorts of things that business rivals or those interested in market manipulation (or criminal intermediaries with routes thereto) might pay very well to get their hands on. Academic laboratories will rarely have either equipment or data actually worth stealing as far conventional criminal avarice goes. I guess you could steal some controlled solvents and reagents to peddle to would-be meth dealers if you really wanted to.

                                    Honestly, the fence might charge a supernatural more. Working with folks who can drag the fence into their complex web of conspiracies and monsters is a tall ask.

                                    That said, I imagine a fence would offer a discount to repeat customers. Both as a means of encouraging them to come back, and simply because the fence can be slightly more sure that the supernatural won't bring heat down on them (or kill them). Supernaturals who have been dealing with the fence for years probably presume to continue that arrangement, which is usually to the fence's benefit.

                                    I think there's something else we need to discuss about fencing: Vampires have the option of sitting on loot for years, or even decades, before selling it. In that case, stealing becomes an investment in the future, trading time now for a less hot product later. And Vampires, as we know, are time rich.

                                    Now I'm imagining a vampire - probably a Malkavian actually - collecting shiny objects like a magpie. Their haven just being filled with swag, neatly catalogued by date stolen (or alternatively thrown on a heap), and digging things out every time they need to make rent. Items might disappear for decades from their owners, until it winds up on the black market years after everyone who cared forgot about it.

                                    Come to think of it, that could make for a great plot hook. Some Toreador finds out one of the paintings they did while alive comes onto market, after having been stolen decades ago. Now that Toreador wants the coterie to not only ensure the painting is returned (cue heist of a black or regular market auction house), but also to track the chain of purchases back to the original thief.
