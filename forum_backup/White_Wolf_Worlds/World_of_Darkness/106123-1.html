    Originally posted by HOD View Post
    No, it's not. The lines all do it, I gave an example from W:tA before, so here's one from V:tM, just to shake things up: Werewolves are the product of an angry, spurned Lilith and the "spirits of the wild". Kinda doesn't fit with the Garou creation myth now, does it ?
    The problem is with your example, these are differences in the in-game mythologies of different groups. Things like the truth behind the Imbued are stated as canonical facts in OOC sections, not just presentations of mythology and unreliable narrator sections.

    What DtF does would be more like VtM fully detailing Lilith, directly stating that despite whatever WtA says she is directly responsible for the shapeshifters of the world, and then later a WtA book retcons that all Garou mythology is a lie and VtM is right.

    I can put forward plenty of reasons for why there is a difference, but ultimately it doesn't matter. VtM's choices didn't rankle people as much as DtF's choices did. It isn't about DtF being a bad game for what it is supposed to be, but that what it is supposed to be didn't hit the target audience as well as it could have. I would say that Wraith had a similar issue in that the quality of the game itself was never the problem, but the premise itself was a hard sell.

                        Honestly I feel that DtF, along with KotE, tends to get hate just for the sake of getting hate. Yes, it has mechanical issues but then what line hasn't? But beyond that I have found both lines to have some very interesting ideas and actually quite good in their own way.

                        In fact one of the things that have annoyed me the most at the table is when people trash those two lines without having read the Rulebooks. I'm not referring to anyone on the forums here it's just that in my offline experiences I've had several encounters with people who those game lines based solely on small bits they've heard elsewhere. One time I got into a debate with a guy about the backstory of DtF and it's Abrahamic origins and how he hated it. Yet at the same time he loves the idea of Caine and the Antes and if asked he will always hold Caine as the source of all vampires. So I asked him why he loved one Abrahamic origin but not the other. He had no answer beyond the vague 'DtF invalidates half the other lines' to which I pointed out that most of the lines' origin stories conflicted or invalidated other CWoD. He pointed out that the Fallen actually knew their origins as they had lived it, to which I replied that while that is true it's also worth noting that they had spent who knows how many eons in the Abyss which drove every one of them into becoming monsters and that we shouldn't necessarily consider their tales as a hundred percent true. Then I pointed out to him that the DtF actually points out that there are multiple similar but different worlds running parallel to each other, something previously brought up in MtAs, and how things can vary quite wildly from each other. For instance the first chapter the Fallen telling the story of their origins points out that in one world humanity evolved from apes, another they were created. In fact the first time I read that I felt that was WW saying 'Yeah, we've made a lot of lines with conflicting origins but that doesn't mean one's right and one's wrong, all of them are right in their world'. And surprise surprise he didn't know any of that cause he hadn't actually any of DtF's material.

                                                I will say that overall I loved Demon. It is one of my favorite games and to date one of only a tiny handful of gaming books I have actually had the patience to read cover to cover. I literally could not put this book down.

                                                That said, it does certainly have its issues which would greatly have benefitted from a 2nd Edition. I think that they are incredibly squishy overall. I've run them in a crossover capacity both as NPCs and in a cross-sphere game and they just cannot take a hit from just about any other sphere. They have no real defensive capabilities at all and desperately need something.

                                                Also, the power scale of the Lores is a bit wonky. In several cases (notably Lore of the Forge and Lore of Paths but a few others as well) the most powerful abilities appear to be the first ones with no real need to ever get more. I think that a few of the Lores need some serious rebalancing (Seriously Lore of Flames? A first level ability that can endlessly scale damage for no cost and stacks with itself?).

                                                I would have really liked to see 6+ abilities somewhere on the scale (maybe as an Earthbound thing only if necessary) but they should be somewhere.

                                                In conclusion, I will add this for all those who haven't actually read the book. Stop bitching if you haven't read it. I stopped counting the number of complaints on this thread that are just plain provably wrong from people who hadn't read it when I got to 20, and that was still on the first page. If you don't want to play the game, fine, don't play it. But there is no need to shit all over something you know nothing about. I care very little for Werewolf, but I don't go around the Werewolf boards shitting all over it because I know I don't know much about it, and have no leg to stand on. This thread asked for specific issues with the game and its setting. Please restrict future posts to that, and only from those who've actually read it.

                                                            Full disclosure: I love DTF. It hits all the right notes for me, and it stands second to Vampire for my favourite CWoD game. So, in regards to setting, the Miltonian stuff is perfect for my sensibilities. I can't comment on the merits of Demon's setting because such a discussion is ultimately too subjective to be fruitful.

                                                            That said, I get why people don't like how the Demon metaplot raises more questions than answers. I think the Metaplot is worth a bucket of...uh, Vice Presidents...as far as mattering to me, but that doesn't mean I don't empathise with people who are invested in it, and who (perhaps rightly) thought Demon didn't fit.

                                                            Personally, I'm closer to the opinion that all the lines are mutually contradictory, even when crossover is explicit and intended (ask me about the death of the author sometime). Demon's sin was being upfront about that contradiction. But? There's a great deal of material in these games that rewards using crossover plothooks, and if one doesn't like what Demon brings to that table, that's legit.

                                                            This is the problem with having game lines existing in the same world yet offering multiple creation stories. Sometimes these discussions just end up being like talking about religion.

                                                            tldr: Metaplot is tricky!...?

                                                            Now, as for what I don't like about Demon? Mechanically, it's kind of a mess. Of the two Demon chronicles I've been in--one of which I played, and one of which I ran, and in two completely different groups--we never, ever got the hang of the rules. We didn't even try. Mostly this came from over-complication. Many of the rules in Demon needed to be simplified. For example, Torment Invocations. The system given for accidentally invoking them is downright bizarre. It got errata'd in the Player's Guide, but it was still weird, and very un-White Wolfy.

                                                            Another core feature of Demon that needed revision was True Names. The Fallen have to be vigilant in guarding their True Names, lest their enemies use those Names to control or harm them. This is (I think) intended to be the major limitation on Demon characters, other than the Tornent morality stat. The game gives you a really vague system for how to deal with True Names, and, in short, that system isn't suitable for an in-depth exploration of that aspect.

                                                            My biggest gripe with Demon is actually less to do with mechanics, though, and more to do with the way the core was written out. The setting chapters, which are the first four chapters of the book, are written entirely in-character. That's a neat conceit...but I found it grated when what I wanted was a bit of outside perspective. It actually all reads pretty well, but it made the game a bit less accessible.
