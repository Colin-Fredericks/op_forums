                                    Originally posted by Aya Tari View Post
                                    1. Is human nature inherently good (or, at least, inherently innocent)? If so, how do we know?
                                    This is a very separate question from your others and I won't touch it with a ten-foot pole.

                                    Originally posted by Aya Tari View Post
                                    2. Are human civilizations innately good? If so, how can the despots, genocide, slavery, and war across five thousand years of human history be explained?
                                    Civilization is simply a higher order of complexity of human society. As such it allows for greater good and greater evil than the lack of civilization. It is simply an amplifier of human nature, and doesn't change it.

                                    You could argue that civilization makes it more likely that a given person's basic needs of food, shelter, security, etc. are met and therefore gives them more opportunity to engage higher thought, and you could argue that higher thought allows for more "good"...but I wouldn't.

                                    Originally posted by Aya Tari View Post
                                    3. Does civilization need to be chained to a focus on human rights (including the reproductive freedoms of women) to prevent the rise of despots, genocide, slavery, and war? If not, how do stop such things from occurring?
                                    The reproductive freedoms of women are just one example of an institutional environment making it more or less likely that a given person can choose their future. A woman in a patriarchal society without reproductive freedom is by far most likely to end up married, bearing children, and focused on raising them. A woman in a less patriarchal society who can choose whether to reproduce or not is much more able to choose a different future. Similarly, a black man born into a slave family in Georgia in 1810 has far less choice about his future state than if he were born in Minnesota in 1990. it's just a way the deck is stacked.

                                    So yes, addressing issues like these allows people more choice about their future, which is pretty close to an unalloyed "good". In a truly egalitarian society where everyone has a fair shot, perhaps there's less chance of despots/slavery/all the things you listed. That said, it's not as easy to remove these narrowed paths. For example, being born into a rich family allows more choice than being born into a poor family. So should we communally share all property and equalize all economic states? It's much less clear whether that would reduce the likelihood of despots/slavery/etc.

                                    Finally - your asking these questions implies a fourth question: "Has the Order of Reason / Technocracy's work to build a more complex civilization caused more 'good' or 'evil'? Is it 'good' to civilize humanity?" I would argue this question isn't really related to the other three. It doesn't matter empirically whether civilization produces good - what matters is whether the OoR/Tech's approach has produced good. In other words - who cares whether it's possible to fuck up civilization - did the Technocracy fuck it up in reality? The obvious answer is yes, because the game setting is the World of Darkness.

                                            Originally posted by Aya Tari View Post
                                            Scientific backup? How about the entire archeological record before 5,000 BC where there is no evidence of large-scale conflicts between groups of human beings? How about the entire historical record of human civilization since 3,000 BC, which is a litany of despots, genocide, slavery, and war that kept only getting worse until World War II shocked some sense into people and caused them to start to wonder what the purpose of human civilization was?

                                            I am not advocating a return to a primitive society. I appreciate simple things like air conditioning and toilet paper. However, I do not think that we need to tolerate despots, genocide, slavery, or war in exchange for getting to benefit from air conditioning and toilet paper.
                                            I feel obligated to point out that your entire premise seems to be that because despots, genocide, slavery and war are associated with civilization, that there's a causal relationship between them. You haven't really offered any evidence in support of that notion, making your entire argument undone by post hoc ergo propter hoc thinking.

                                            And that's even if we accept your premise as true, that before we went in an agricultural direction, we didn't have genocide and war to worry about. That's just not true. For genocide, let's talk about the Neanderthal. It's by no means settled how that species went extinct, but one of the many viable hypotheses is that Neanderthal extinction was either caused by or by violent conflict with Homo sapiens, or that violence against Neanderthals played a contributing role to their extinction.

                                            The notion that hunters/gatherers are peaceful is not one supported by the data. Conflict and intra-tribal warfare are ubiquitous features of hunter-gatherer societies, including conflicts over limited resources, such as prey and water according to the Journal of Human Evolution. And even though there's no evidence for this, I consider it probable that sexual abuse and abduction and rape of women that would result from such conflicts is easily analogous to slavery.

                                            In summary, your argument rests on a logical fallacy, but even if it didn't, your premises are flawed and demonstrably untrue.
