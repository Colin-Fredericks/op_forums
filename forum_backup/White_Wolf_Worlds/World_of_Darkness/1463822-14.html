                So the book is in, and the responses seem mixed to negative on RPG.net. A lot of people are pointing out that by scrubbing the imbued, it's turned a WoD game (the selling point is usually the depth of the setting and lore) into a very generic game with some hangover legacy ideas that don't fit the idea of entirely mundane hunters.

                There are three loosely supernatural Edges, so it's clear that these are mostly the Xanders of the world, not the Buffys or Willows.

                Weirdly, the Driven have Drive but there isn't an in-game reason why they have this, why it isn't magic or divine intervention, and why no one else gets it. Hunters with the orgs specifically don't get Drive (though there may be some who do, it's clearly shown this is an exception).

                If Drive is entirely mundane, then in theory, a dedicated teacher should also be able to access Drive. If Drive is supposed to be some sort of Fate-style metacurrency, then it should be open to all character types in the WoD, not weirdly gated for independent hunters only.

                There's also more of the iffy 'monsters are the cause of real world ills' without any critical reflection of this. One of the examples I saw was particularly distasteful but I won't go into more details here.

                                                I wasn't expecting super powers and magic-wielding awesome hunters, I wanted a game as dirty streets as it could, with any superstuff like magic and tech being optional or debatable, and that is what I got and I really liked it. If there was something I would add would be Cell tactics or examples of cells, but other than that the game delivered.

                                                Hunter feeling like they are different and are better than "the Establishment" and "the Man" is pretty much in-tone with the feel the hunters have, it's a game about having enough and putting your hand on the wheel. Even if that sounds a tad bit overrebellious at times, it doesn't actually sounds far off. Although it is weird how Hunters seem so ancap, I don't think they would have Drives and Edges like that if they weren't like this, they would have the power of orgs, which I think are a nice playstyle too, and not actually too hard to adapt. But an unprobeable supplement would be nicer of course.

                                                The Monsters traits for me are better than trying to translate to Edges or things like their multitude of powers, besides, they are more a problem by their surroundings and assets rather than their direct reactions. The urban legend and occult danger were well shown in the example monsters, but what I mainly got from all this is that the Monster are the end goal in the middle of a lot of obstacles and shortcuts. Other hunters and monsters, cops, orgs, the cell itself and its own personal problems and dilemmas, all of this should make the game, otherwise it would be pretty straightforward research, investigation, observation and intervention.


                                                I really liked the Despair and Danger calibrations, and was hoping for more of it since the Fallen London notoriety counter for the SI, that was expanded in the Second Inquisiton supplement. More games should have these game-wide counters so that choices and consequences always have an easy go-to in game for the ST, which gives concrete chokes and releases in the tension.

                                                The Storyteller's part is also very robust, giving plenty of advice, molds and teachings on building the story to be played. I actually want to play and not only ST this game.

                                                Strange... When coincidence seems too convenient, I prefer to call it fate.

                                                            I've been looking more into the book

                                                            The Creeds are very crappy splats with no identity. There is Enterpreneurial, Faithful, Inquisitive, Martial and Underground. They are essentially methodologies you use to hunt and your backstory that use old HtR Creed names as sobriquets. Their only mechanical hook is what situation they get their desperation dice in - i.e. religious creed gets it against the supernatural, militant in combat, investigative in data gathering.

                                                            Now what are the Desperation dice? The game has two public counters - Desperation and Danger. Danger represents how much you have pissed off the authorities and/or your quarry and results in higher difficulties and more opposition. Desperation represents how bad things are going for the hunters. The worse things are for the hunters the more Desperation there is. Now Desperation is ranked from 1-5 and any Hunters (again they insist on the capitalisation as opposed to the ordinary hunters) with a Drive and a Creed can choose to use the desperation dice in any field where their creed specialty applies.

                                                            Desperation dice can never be rerolled, each 1 causes horrible consequences and/or raises Danger. 10's give a potential critical win, which seems to only be an extra margin of success. I got no idea what "potential" means here, I assume they mean you need double 0's? But why call it out specifically for desperation dice?

                                                            Powers! (Edges)
                                                            • Assets are Arsenal, Fleet, Library, Ordnance
                                                            • Aptitudes are tricks your character has - Beast Whisperer (petmaker), Drone Jockey, Jury Rigging and Global Access (Hack the Planet)
                                                            • Endowments are the actual powers - Sense the unnatural, Thwart the Unnatural (ignore powers) and Repel the Unnatural (use cross to drive of vampires) and Artifact (1 bonus die to certain skill)

                                                            In general, the whole section is very sparse. For some reason explosives are separate from "Arsenal" as "Ordnance". Edges can be customized with perks that improve their effects in certain contexts. All of them require activation rolls and most of them have Difficulty 4 to even activate properly. Some of them are downright going to cause issues in the future, I'd like to especially single out Global Access - " ...the Hunter can gain access to any kind of data on any subject or person, in any conceivable system" as something that's gonna be hilarious to interpret once Magic, Spirits and Hypertech exist in this setting. Drone Jockey seems very easy to abuse if you load up on perks and get multiple users (simply due to how action economy works in WoD).

                                                            I still don't get why "Drive" is so special for the Hunters and why it makes them different from other humans beyond marking them as protagonists. For something they spend A LOT of wordcount on to make special, it doesn't really seem so in everything I've seen.
