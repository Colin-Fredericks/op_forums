                            Asmodai As others have mentioned, it takes a bit of American historical context to really understand the situation. There has always been a strain of "American Puritanism," and in the 90's it was focused on "entertainments" - movies, music, and video games (like it almost always is). In the 80's, Tipper Gore and her group had successfully browbeaten the music industry into "voluntarily" placing 'Parental Advisory' labels on "objectionable" music (the standards used were laughable and arbitrary - any references to smoking, drinking, drugs, and swearing warranted a sticker, but songs about beating women, as long as they didn't contain any of those previous mentions, were perfectly fine. There wasn't so much as a "committee" as a checklist). In the 90's, several "Parent Advocacy" and interest groups began rumbling and eyeing the entertainment industry again, most especially the burgeoning video game companies. In a move to head off the possible storm of controversy (and censorship, and court, and government regulation), several sectors began a process of "self-regulation" - it's why the ESRB was created. Other, smaller sectors - like role-playing games - followed suit. White Wolf created the Black Dog imprint to publish things that were deemed "too mature" for the main line - things that may have lead to retailers returning and discontinuing their White Wolf orders (as a special side note, Clanbook: Tzimisce was originally published in a 'censored' format by White Wolf, since it predated the creation of Black Dog, and was later republished uncensored under the Black Dog label). As for HöL, amazing as it was, White Wolf did not want it immediately associated with them and confused with the World of Darkness (as the two were basically synonymous at the time).

                            As for "where is this line and who defines it," well... it's a self-imposed measure. It really depends on the sociopolitical landscape and the personal feelings of whomever is in charge of publishing at the time. So there's no hard metric.
