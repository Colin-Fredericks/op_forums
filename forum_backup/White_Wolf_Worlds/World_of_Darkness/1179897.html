    Help on a Scientology Parody as a Nephandic Cult.

    01-27-2018, 03:11 PM
    I just bought Mage: The Ascension 20th ed and i am reading through it right now. As i do this, I am planning on starting my own campaign of Mage when I am comfortable with the rules, especially the magic system. While thinking up antagonists, a interesting idea popped in my head. The basic idea is a Nephandic Cult that somehow became as large as Scientology. The Cult, which claims to a organization based off Kemeticism that worships a obscure cat deity, is actually ran by a extremely powerful and charismatic Infernalist Nephandi. Can i have some advice detailing this cult. Also, can i have some helping figuring out how this cult hit the big time and how the Nephandi got involved?

      Well first, the charismatic leader of the cult is unlikely to be the Nephandus himself. Filling that role himself just paints a huge target on his back. Rather, the cult leader will be a patsy of some kind. I'm imagining just an Infernal Sorcerer; I recommend picking up PDFs for Sorcerer: Revised and The Book of Madness from Drivethru RPG, when you get the chance. The former explains unAwakened magic users (and "psychics"), the latter about Infernalists (and Nephandi, for that matter; the two groups get separate chapters, though there's some overlap). If the patsy is a Sorcerer, he'll probably have dots in the Path of Fascination, and whatever Paths the character's Nephandi backer and/or demon patron will deign to give him. Plus a few social-focused Investments.

      One idea is giving the patsy dots in the Path of Enchantment, so they can arm the "inner circle" with minor Wonders. They are basically like the Wonders Awakened characters can make, except they invariably have only one Effect they can perform, and get "recharged" through some story-specific action, rather than using Quintessence (which Sorcerers can't really work with directly). Sorcerer: Revised explains that better. Point being, it may lead to the NPCs immediately surrounding this "leader" being armed with minor magical items (the leader will likely keep the most potent items for himself, or for the Nephandus). If the cult is like Scientology, it may use chemicals, so the character having the Path of Alchemy might not be a bad idea either.

      The Path of Divination is also a decent thing for a cult leader to harness, as they set themselves up as a sort of all-knowing oracle or prophet. Their image may need "reskinning" to fit the exact ethos the cult is going for. If the cult has a veneer of being "scientific" (like Scientology does), the "leader" may explain their divination as a result of secret knowledge of probability, or the ability to tap into the collective unconscious of humanity. In reality, the cult leader's "science" may just involve cutting open the belly of an animal (or a human sacrifice) and reading the entrails.

      Keep in mind that while I've presented a number of suggestions for Sorcerous power this guy could have, it's best to choose only one or two. The guy "leading" the cult is, ultimately, just a front. An intermediary through which the real Nephandus is acting, and who can be thrown under the bus if necessary. Sometimes, the Nephandus might possess the cult "leader" if he needs to interface with the followers directly. And they Nephandi might make appearances indirectly as an illusion of a "secret ascended master", if the front leader needs an air of legitimacy. Ultimately, though, the intent behind the cult is to allow the Fallen to escape completely if everything comes crashing down. The best way to do that is to make it so no one thinks there was ever a man behind the man at all.

      Magestuck: The Ascension

              Another thing to remember is that Scientology often used another avenue of attack to defend its interests: litigation. The church of Scientology is notorious for lawsuits, and for having their more unsavory practices be coached or dressed up to just pass as legal.

              If your fictional cult is in any way popular - even at a fraction of Scientology's popularity - have the cult lawyer up where possible. If it can entangle PCs in legal battles, it's to their benefit. Having secret hit squads is good in and of itself, but so is making the law work for them.

                  Social pressure is going to be more effective overall than whacking people. Oh, and there's an episode of Millennium called Gehenna which has one of these make money cults as the bad guys.

                  How much detail you need on the cult depends on how the players will interact with it. If it's not the big bad, just come up with some major beliefs and a few phrases and let the players imagine what's there.

                  A larger cult is going to have to offer a lot more, especially if it is secret, than a small one. The bigger the pyramid, the more the followers need to feel they get something.

                          I'd play up the more negative stereotypes about the cats. Greed, Imperial arrogance etc for your 'cat deity'. Is the cat the actual fallen entity itself or an go between to keep people from guessing what they are actually worshipping?

                          If this cult has a lot of money, and your trying for an expy of Scientology, play up the 'secret society of the rich and powerful' i think for the SOURCE of their money. Celebrities, politicians and Celebrities have joined up, and are paying money to the organisation. Then ask yourself...what does the cut do to make these people want to play? Secret Knowledge is a good one, but its not the only one. I mean how does the cult draw in new people, especially ones that can pay? Perhaps they have their hands in the illicit drugs trade and get people to come to one of their services to get their fix of a custom drug they are pushing. Perhaps they are providing more unsavoury things (like doing whatever it is they want to those people locked in those dungeons you mentioned).

                                  Depends on the characters mostly. You have a college student? Perhaps they heard of this amazing new drug that hit one of the clubs last week, and noticing that all the people are itching to get another hit.                                   Then of course you have your magical contracts, such as noticing that the there's a huge amount of hateful or just plane unpleasant resonance near the big compound on the other side of town.

                                  I like the drug one because it can seem to start small and they realise that this cult is a big thing rather than just one sorcerer pushing a magically enhanced drug, but the massive lawsuits threatening the local news agency, or the cult funnelling money into local elections could be certainly something that catches your character's eyes.

                                                      First off, the Technocracy is a large organization. There's always red tape in a large organization, because managers aren't supposed to do what's best for just their responsibility area. So, there are constraints on any Technocrat's ability to get things done.

                                                      The Technocracy has also been subverted by the Nephandi in multiple sites and at multiple levels in the hierarchy. A powerful cult with a lot of money that wants the authorities off its back will certainly have a few paid informants, problem solving experts (could be called fixers) and a few people paid off to look the other way. So, even if there was not red tape blocking the Technocracy, there could be members of the Technocracy who serve the Nephandi.

                                                      Finally, consider that even if there was a strong Technocratic desire to stop a problem, information on that problem has to be gathered. Cults which are secretive are going to take measures to control information, so the lack of reliable information on the cult could lead to indecision or the wrong decision on the part of the Technocracy.

                                                      The Technocracy is not all-powerful, because it is a coalition of humans working together.
