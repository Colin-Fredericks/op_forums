      Why worry about Changeling the game when you have mythology at hand. They say the Fomorians, a evil race of conquering evil giants, led by the terrible Balor One-Eyed were banished by the early heroes of Ireland. The Fianna even claim they had a hand in imprisoning them. Now the Nephandi seek to free the Fomorians and pledge their allegiance to Balor. The Mages have to find a common tongue with the Garou and possibly some Fae helping or hindering both casts - one group does not want to see the Fomorians back, and the other feels that the Fomorians are just deserts for the terrible, terrible humans. (you really don't have to use Changeling the game to run a fun Mage chronicle with Fae)

                          Immortal Eyes: Court of All Kings is the Changeling sourcebook for Ireland. In C:tD, the Fomorians fill the same space as Vampire's antediluvians. They are essentially plot devices. You won't find stats for them.

                          Asmodai has great advice though. It sounds like you're running a primarily Mage game. Stick with that. Use the mythology and mine the other games for ideas to make an awesome Mage plot, but don't worry too much about the details of Werewolf: The Apocalypse or Changeling: The Dreaming.

                          Onyx Path Freelancer

                                  I would agree on the Don't Worry about Changeling thing. Much as I love it the Mage cosmos is big enough that you don't need it and the Myths and Legends are a far stronger resource if there is no real crossover going on (Werewolf I think it;s fine to stick closer too as there is a lot of cosmological Cross Over between the two lines in terms of Umbra and Realms). To build on the suggestions given, the sacred place of the Formorians is Tory Isle and don't forget they have associations with the Winter and the Sea (Balor originally being the son of Domnu and Mannan Mac Lir, also brother to Crom the lord of Autumn, Rot and Decay). It's also worth noting that Balor was more than just a tyrant with a killing eye, he was a hero of his people and spoke with a bard's blessing. Evil? Certainly, a Cruel destroyer of people? Yes, but still rather likeable in person.

                                              Fragments over the years more than anything. Don't forget that much of the Celtic myth otherworld tilts on an axis of perception. CuChulain after all is a massive hero in one direction and a blood soaked monster in the other. Math is wise, just and great but also full of hubris and utterly inflexible. Lugh is heroic and noble but also inattentive of his wife. Danu herself is magnificent but utterly contradictory and fickle. Morrigan is wicked and cruel and yet also a warning that may be heeded to avoid ill fate.

                                                  Echoing what other people have said: make up what fits your story. Golden rule golden rule. However, if you someday want to revisit this setting and just want to be more familiar, here are your answers:

                                                  -For the Fae and Mage presence (to include locations of canon chantrys) in England, Scotland and Wales "Isle of Mighty" is your sourcebook (starting at page 15, with some special attention on page 21 as it goes a long way towards explaining why there are no more Dark Ages Fae). They do not cover Ireland, I find the Fianna tribe book to be of more help there.

                                                  -For the Formorians see "Denizens of the Dreaming". They were hot shit a thousand years ago but have since been relegated to the Deep and Far Dreaming.

                                                  -Naughty naughty Balor One-Eye left a line of Fae. For information on them, check out the sourcebooks
                                                  "Pour L'Amour et Liberte: The Book of Houses 2" (starting on page 103), "The Shadow Court" (page 63) and "Book of Lost Dreams" (from pages 42-43)

                                                  -"Noblesse Oblige" is a sourcebook on the sidhe changeling "house" of Fiona, who have many ancient pacts and frequently share progeny with the Fianna.

                                                  -Furthermore, here is a listing of how the British fae have divided up their dream lands:

                                                  Albion (England)
                                                  The Kingdom of Chalk: The South of England.
                                                  The Kingdom of Mist: The Southwest of England.
                                                  The Kingdom of Roses: From London to Just below the Midlands, encompassing Anglia.
                                                  The Kingdom of Smoke: The Midlands.
                                                  The Kingdom of Heather: The North of England.

                                                  Caledonia (Scotland)
                                                  The kingdom of the Three Hills: From Hadrian's Wall to just below Edinburg.
                                                  The Tuath of Dew: Edinburg and surrounding area.
                                                  The Tuath of Iron: The Southwest of Scotland.
                                                  The Tuath of Shadow: The Northwest of Scotland.
                                                  The Cridhe: The Scottish Highlands.
                                                  The Kingdom of Alba: The East Coast of Scotland.

                                                  Cymru (Wales)
                                                  Gwynt: South East.
                                                  Glamorgan: South.
                                                  Dyfed: Southwest.
                                                  Powys: middle.
                                                  Clnyd: Northeast.
                                                  Gwynedd: Northwest.
                                                  Ynysman: Island just off of the northwest coast.

                                                  So as you can see, this is a ton of reading. Think on it if you really want to get into canon. Again, if you want to revisit this for future chronicles this would be helpful, but otherwise I just see it as work.
