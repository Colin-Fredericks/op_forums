      The "Dominate" discipline that vampires have is very frustrating for my pack of werewolves, but that's largely because the storyteller's NPC leeches have had very lucky rolls every time they've tried it. It's not so much a horrifying thing to be afraid of, but it pisses the werewolves off when it allows the leeches to escape. The more formidable kinds of mind control, like the higher levels of vampiric "Presence" or whatever mind control or illusion a mage might cook up, would be more disturbing. I'd guess that werewolves would probably be able to cope with it psychologically much more easily than creatures who don't have personal experience with the stranger things happening in the spirit world, and the more "normal" human types, like hunters or human Pentex employees, would have a very hard time coping. The most frightening kinds of mind control would certainly be those with a time-delayed trigger, like what happens with Reggie Jackson in the Naked Gun. I'm not sure how any victim (the used or the targeted) could handle that, but if the person who inflicted the mind control it was identified, they'd have to be marked as enemy number one and put at the top of the list of people who need to be killed ASAP.

          Mages getting co-opted by force and turned against their former friends it's far from unheard off. The life of a Tradition mage in the middle of the Ascension War it's to know that at any time your friends and lovers may get captured by the Technocracy and be mindrapped in room 101. At the same time, Traditionalists aren't always above using mind control themselves.
          This does generate a state of paranoia where checking the soul and mind of members it's seen as a common practice - which it's by no means foolproof, as magick can frustrate such perceptions. Technocrats answer the problem by having their members submit regularly to mind reading and mind control to reinforce their loyalty - which also isn't foolproof.
