                Originally posted by Prince of the Night View Post
                Dominate... can obviously be used on those close to a hunter.

                Think Conditionings effects are impossible to disguise?
                They are. Dominate has a tendency to blunt your subject's capacity for rational or creative thought; repeated applications of the discipline against the same person - which is what conditioning is - has a tendency to gradually turn the person into a mindlessly quiescent puppet. So, yes, if anyone's paying even the remotest amount of attention, they'll notice.

                As for the core question...

                It's ultimately a fairly simple matter to cause someone more pain - be it physical, psychological or both - than they can bear, assuming you hold absolute power over them. Assuming there's nowhere they can go for help, and no place they can retreat to that is safe from you, you can assault their weak points without particular issue.

                It's ultimately quite trivial for any of the supernaturals to casually destroy a mortal's life, after all, and the number of ways in which can be done are virtually infinite. Really, it's not so much a question of "how do we do this" as "how can't we do this".

                Rather than killing a mortal's loved ones, a Kindred could instead blood bond them - force the hunter to watch their beloved child, or sister, or lover, fall desperately and hopelessly in love with an undead horror that cares nothing for them, or simply apply Dominate like the mental sledgehammer that it so frequently is to reduce the consistency of said loved one's soul to paste.

                Simply ask yourself what you would give anything to avoid happening to you, and there you pretty much have your answer.

                Originally posted by Crowley View Post

                I'd be interested to know what the circumstances were that made it necessary for a full pack of werewolves to engage a single mortal couple in full view of their child.

                I do somewhat think that saying "hunters have the moral high ground" is a bit simplistic, though; "hunter" is ultimately an incredibly broad umbrella that encompasses everything from the proverbial lost orphan child seeking the slayer of his parents to the crazed lunatics that will kill all non-humans simply for the crime of being non-humans.

                Does the shotgun-toting religious fanatic pumping shells into the beds of defenceless kinfolk children hold the moral high ground over the staunch Child of Gaia fighting desperately every night to save the world from falling into darkness? What about a man who sees all vampires as monsters plunging a hatchet into a Tremere doctor and biothaumaturge who uses the power of his blood sorcery to save terminal patients who otherwise could never be saved, sparing their families - potentially dozens of people - needless agony? Who holds the high ground there? Murderer or victim?
