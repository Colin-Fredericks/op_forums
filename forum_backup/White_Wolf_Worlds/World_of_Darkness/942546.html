                                  Its kind of a false dichotomy really. An experienced brawler is a martial artist, just one without formal systematized training. To use a crude analogy its like comparing someone with an electrical engineering degree to someone with an electrician certificate. Who is going to be better in a given situation? That depends entirely on the requirements of the situation, their experience, and their context-specific knowledge.

                                  For our purposes its all abstract die-rollers anyway. Martial Arts isn't meant to be objectively better than Brawl in the "real world," its mechanics are just better within the rules. If we wanted to simulate hand to hand combat realistically we'd have to incorporate Athletics as well and include variable bonuses/penalties for weight and reach.

                                  We're playing games with supernatural beings anyway, it isn't a stretch to imagine that the Dim Mak Death Touch and the Five Finger Exploding Heart Technique also work in this reality.

                                      Originally posted by mark View Post
                                      basically the question is "why is martial arts so much better than brawling(e.g kindred of the ease core rulebook and companion i think) when even bruce fucking lee said" an experienced brawler can beat any martial artist"
                                      The problem is that this question is more complex than you're treating it.

                                      I see two core things.

                                      1) Why does the system model "basic" fighting in the form of Brawl and Melee, as well as specialist fighting in the form of various supplemental Abilities (Do, Martial Arts, Blind Fighting, Klaviskar, Ikkaksu, etc.)?

                                      2) Why might some of those things be mechanically superior to the others.

                                      The first is a lot more complicated than comparing costs/benefits. Some of the answers make sense from a design standpoint (Blind Fighting is a specialized skill even among fighters, so it makes sense as something certain characters would develop separate from basic fighting skills), while others make sense from a setting perspective (Do is a magical fighting practice that does things better because magic), and others are more questionable (Martial Arts was introduced more as 90s action than fulfilling a design niche or setting concern).

                                      The second question requires establishing if and when this is even the case, before establishing if it should be the case. Martial Arts in KotE core isn't significantly superior to Brawl, but is definitely superior to Brawl in M20. Do has always been better than Brawl, but it's supposed to be because, again, magic.
