                            I think I see what Saur Ops Specialist means. The problem with Mage is that it sets the Werewolf cosmology as one variable truth among many. Werewolf on the other hand is a game that relies on their cosmology as the fundamental underlying truth. Once you take that away, the game's underlying themes get lost. The Werewolves aren't desperate servants of Gaia fighting a likely doomed War to their last breath. They're misguided fanatics who've trapped themselves within a limited view of reality. It becomes a very different story.

                            You can include Werewolf characters in a Mage game, or Mage characters in a Werewolf game. But I don't think you can give both settings equal weight. You've got to make the call: are Mages just creatures of the Trait with an imperfect understanding of their true place in Gaia's War? Or are the Garou just umbrood with delusions of grandeur who only think that they're dealing with an inevitable Apocalypse?

                                    The "noble cause" is the core of setting, though. Whether or not the Garou are or had ever been worthy of that cause is an interesting question, but that doesn't change the way that Werewolf's objective reality forms the basis of the game.

                                    The tribes and Fera have different perspectives of the Garou's actions, understandably so, but they're generally all in agreement about a few things: the Triat exists. Gaia exists. Garou are intended to be the protectors of Gaia. The other Changing Breeds have their own specific roles. And there is a war, which the Garou are undeniably losing.

                                    (The perspective of elder vampires is a matter for Vampire the Masquerade, not Werewolf the Apocalypse. Obviously, it's something that should be addressed in a crossover game, but that's another matter of the storyteller and players choosing which splat's cosmology should be dominant in the game. It's another situation where the settings really can't be given equal weight, because there are too many direct contradictions.)

                                    It's particularly important when we look at things like the Impergium, the fall of the White Howlers, the fates of the Apis, Camazotz, Bunyip and others. Because it isn't just that the Garou have been responsible for monstrous things, but a lot of these deeds represent failures of their sacred duty on a much greater level. And these failures are part of why the Apocalypse seems inevitable today. (For example, the issue with the dwindling number of Garou might not be an issue at all if the Apis were still around to help matchmake.)

                                    These deeds don't carry the same meaning at all if we're looking at Garou history from the perspective of Mage's paradigms. What was a story about how the Garou's early arrogance and failure of their sacred duty may have led to their undoing simply becomes the story of Umbrood killing each other over their flawed interpretation of reality. We lose so much of the original nuance of the setting.

                                    That doesn't mean people can't have fun playing a Garou in a universe with elements of Mage-cosmology, but it becomes a very different story.

                                        Indeed, Werewolves may be a bunch of murderous, even delusional, fanatics, but that's largely irrelevant to the underlying cosmology of the game.

                                        Werewolves may be wrong about many things. Perhaps Gaia isn't greater than the Triat. Perhaps they aren't the ones choosen to stop the Wyrm. Perhaps the Wyrm has to be released in order to restore balance. But the cosmic importance of the Wyrm, the spirit world, Apocalypse - these are never questioned. because that's the underlying theme of the game as much as the Jyhad it's the underlying theme of vampire.

                                        To say that Werewolf entire cosmology it's just a point of view (e.g: the Wyrm may be a comet, like Etherites say - sure, it's going to destroy the planet but no amount of ambientalism is going to stop it. That's their approach, as valid as Garou approach). This would be like saying that the Jyhad it's just paranoia (a lo "fog of the ages" in Requiem) or that mages are just mad people that live in a fixed reality and don't realize it. It ruins the game for a lot of people.

                                        It's not just a matter of "truth", but of purpose.

                                        You can't very well depict the Ascencion War as "the war to define Reality" if you say that reality it's already defined. That's why a lot of mage fans complain when someone says that science would exist the same without the Technocracy. If the natural laws that science discovered are 100% objective, and Consensus can't change this, then there's no war for reality - there's one reality and a bunch of reality warpers that can't do meaningful chages to the world outside their small reach.

                                        In the same vein, if ALL what Garou have been doing it's just a PoV, then the spirit world looses a lot of it's importance - apeasing the spirits, traveling to the Umbra, figthing the Wyrm, are just your (outdated) way to deal with these issues - you could as well ignore the spiritual side of the game because that's a Paradigm. More so, all the "dark" aspects of Werewolf would be gone because the Impergium, the War of Rage, all the bad things Garou did, aren't really bad. They aren't bad because Garou believe they were necessary and belief shapes reality, remember - maybe they were necessary, if all PoV are equally valid so it's this one.

                                        Since these themes are very important, you need to thread carefully arround them (or just stomp them, with the blessing of your players, but that's not the theme of this thread).
