                                            Originally posted by Lord Revan
                                            Again, werewolves can use spirits that they are capable of controling. Let's not count super motherfucker badasses spirits that won't give a crap about werewolves and what they are asking.
                                            As idpersona said, and I add: it is the same as limiting vampires to the methuselahs they can actually summon.

                                            Werewolf's core book have all the rules you need for spirits for a reason. The game IS supposed to work with them. More than Vampire and Mage was ever supposed to work with their upper-end powers.

                                            Vampire was supposed to work with PCs up to 8th generation. More powerful vampires are a possibility, and completely covered by the rules on core alone, even to some degree the Antediluvian bunch themselves. But that's simply because the core rules are the same for all vampires, what changes is just the Discipline level beyond 5, and even this is debatable, because since such powers are customized, anyway, you can simply make your own all the way up to 10th level. So the core book alone have everything you need for all of those vampires. But they AREN'T meant for the same role. A PC isn't meant to ever have access to Disciplines over 5 unless under the most extraordinary circumstances. Elders are meant as powerful patrons and enemies. Methuselah are meant as godlike beings of legend, to only make an appearance on the background and remain as unfathomable threats and (un)living plot-devices. Antediluvian are meant as the very gods of old, they are the forces behind the end of the world of the vampires.

                                            So we are working, first and foremost, with literary roles here: Protagonists, Patrons, God-lings and World-Enders. In vampire you have the same class of creature occupying all those roles, under mostly the same rules, but PCs aren't supposed to leave the first role easily, and even less to ever leave the second role.

                                            In Mage you have mostly the same rule covering it all, again, with one distinction: PCs ARE meant to be able to cover it all. You could say that Masters are meant for the role of Patrons, while an Archmage of "lesser" power is a God-ling, and he greatest Archmages are World-Enders. Except, first, that the world wasn't supposed to end in Mage. Second, that not only the roles are covered by the same rules, now they are truly meant for PCs. Nothing can stop a successful PC from covering all the way up to Arete 10 and Spheres 9. No tricky Diablerie or anything. What hold Earth's power level currently is a prerogative of setting: in Revised, the game starts with no Archmage and almost no Master on Earth. But it simply isn't a rule, just a momentary hold in the setting. Its just a mater of time until Earth is filled with Archmasters of untold power again. Of course, you don't actually have Spheres beyond Master level in the core, but it follows the same guidelines for Disciplines, and what's more: Mages don't really need them at all. 5 is more than enough for them to break the world apart.

                                            Then we get to Werewolf, and that's the thing: while Vampire and Mage were supposed to work with the same general creatures for every role, the Fera are supposed to work with spirits all the freaking time! The Fera themselves are supposed to occupy the roles of Protagonists and, for the Rank 5 and 6, Patrons. And spirits are supposed to occupy them all. They aren't just powers for the Garou. The werecreatures walk among spirits, talk with spirits, negotiate with spirits, and bargain for spirit's aid all the time. They aren't supposed to work without spirits, nor even to only have spiritual help through their own powers. Heck, one of the most basic plots in Werewolf is the spirit-quest, where PCs from any Rank can find and bargain with spirits far more powerful than themselves. As was said before, it is as if a starting Vampire PC could just go talk with Mithras, the 4th gen Prince of London, and actually expect not only to be heard, but to be granted a boon. It is almost unthinkable (if possible, yes) for a vampire, and exceedingly hard for a Mage, but actually common for the Fera. Like, most Rank 1 Fera will encounter such beings before they achieve Rank 2. A lot encountered before Rank 1.

As idpersona said, you can do whatever you want at this level, pretty much. I would argue that the vampires have the upper hand here, but based solely on the rule of mystic potency I referenced: there are very few Mages at levels 8 or 9 of Spheres, and no one at level 10. So the Antes get the upper hand over everyone else in potency, while the Methuselah have no superiors, only equals.

                                            But there are other factors. One is that vampires only get such dynamism on extremely high-level cases, and only for NPCs. PCs are bound to static powers because, in this case, dynamic powers are acquired with age. Even characters like Tremere (before or after losing his body, doesn't mater) and Augustus Giovanni wouldn't be privy to dynamism, because they are too young for such mastery of untold powers. Mages have dynamism from start, so we aren't talking only about the power-players, but about every single Mage.

                                            Not possible at all to say who would win at this level. Both sides can shatter nations with a thought, continents with a little more effort. Both can do whatever the writer wants them to do. Stalemate, there are no solution, except the certainty that humanity can't survive such powers on the lose with so much blood in the eyes.

                                            Just to be clear on it again, the rules weren't meant for crossover at all from scratch, and actually neither meant to truly cover high-ends. To try a crossover of high-ends is to push them well beyond what they can actually account for.
