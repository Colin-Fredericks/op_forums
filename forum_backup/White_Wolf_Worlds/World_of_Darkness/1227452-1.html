    Modern Magick, Modern Paradox:

    James wants to ruin his ex's life. So he hacks into their facebook or twitter using mundane abilities, and creates a post. The content of the post should be inflammatory in one way or another; a simplified strawman of a progressive or conservative political stance, a racist or homophobic joke, a hot take on a really complex and controversial event...

    He then uses Mind 2 and Entropy 2, along with Correspondence/Data 2, to implant several subtle effects onto this digital foundation. With Mind 2 he implants an impulse to spread the content, along with an impulse which heightens awareness and dampens inhibition. When people see this, this is going to be the worst or best thing they've ever seen. So if it's a racist joke it's going to be shared on every racist and reprehensible platform, and universally reviled otherwise. With Entropy 2 he nudges the strands of the digital web in his favour, helps the post find the path of least resistance. With Entropy 1 and Data 1 he might seek out the absolute best way to make the post, the language and timing, the hashtags, figuring out what will give the best possible result.

    James succeeds in this ritual, and the post goes viral. His ex's life is ruined. The scholarship they had won is withdrawn, they lose their job, friends desert them, and a simple google search will make most employers throw her CV into the bin. This is almost impossible to undo if James were to regret it however.

    But say James were to botch the ritual? The backlash could be quite interesting. A paradox spirit might manifest within the digital web, and kidnap James into it. Judgement Gone Viral. This manifestation of Judgement is concerned with his own online behaviour. His embarrassing porn habits, the edgy jokes he made on deleted chatboards when he was 14, the fanfiction and slashfiction he wrote... all of it is laid bare for the world to see. All of his attempts to undo this, hide it, just make it worse.

        Hmm... perhaps expand on the Reality Zones system, by creating levels. A reality zone has a tolerance for technology and a tolerance for mysticism, represented as two ratings from 0 to 10. 0 in Mystic represents a Revised Mage take; the people here are consumed by mass media and technocratic control. They don't believe in mysticism at all, and so the tolerance for coincidental mystic magick is very low. Sensory effects, and "hidden" effects, such as pulling money out of your pocket thanks to a mystical teleportation spell, are fine, but anything overt will not fly unless you set up a convincing cover. 10 in Mystic represents total acceptance of the weird and the wonderful; the people here have absolutely no pretense of understanding how the world works as a whole, and believe that spirits can upheave reality at any time.

        That rating from 0 to 10 effects the burden of Permadox a Mage might carry, whilst also advising the ST on vulgarity and coincidence, and the extent to which paradox strikes back at a Mage.

        For example, within a Technocracy Lab or Construct (Techno = 10, Mystic = 0) cyborgs with enhancements will have their Permadox reduced to 0 regardless of how extensive their enhancements are. And within a mystical Sanctum (Mystic = 10, Techno = 0) a Mage blighted with Permadox due to their curled goats horns and their fiery burning eyes might experience 0 Permadox.

        Outside of these spaces, and a Universal Ascension, reality zones are in the 1-9 range. The exact mechanics of this... well I have plenty of ideas, but I see huge problems with all of them.

        In essence, the ascension war is a competition to push these ratings up or down across the world. If everywhere accepted technomagick up to a rating of 9, then cyborgs riding jetpacks while firing plasma bolts would be okay. That isn't normal tech, but people in these settings would have fully accepted that "special government prototypes" are capable of such activity, and not yet available to the public.

            Originally posted by Aleph View Post

            I think you're taking the wrong angle:

            WoD simulates reality up to a point, but it's far from accurate. Just see the debates around falling damage and admire how WoD representation of phisics allows normal humans to survive falls from planes and skycrappers without needing to fall on something soft. You don't need to be too realist.

            Computers were always much better at solving certain problems than humans, you shouldn't use the same scale and systems. A NORMAL computer isn't going to roll and risk failing or succeeding each time you want to calculate the square root of phi, that's not realistic - unles very bizarre circumnstances happen (like a blackout) a computer it's going to calculate the value up to the point it was programmed to be able to do so. The difference with a Quantum Computer isn't going to be the "success chance" but rather the "amount": it should give the answer with a greater degree of accuracy, or faster.

            The easiest way to adjust the system it's the same that you use right now to check, in game, the chance of success of a calculator when a player imputs a calculation - you don't roll for the calculator - if anything, you roll for the character (to see if she understands the problem) with a bonus because she's using a calculator. Extrapolating to Quantum, just determine how much time a normal PC would take to solve the problem with the right imput, and then divide by 300. If rolls are necessary at all, just give the character a bonus (it doesn't need to be proportional with the difference between Digital and Quantum, because the factor of failure it's more the user imput than anything else).
            I think the "using the calculator" argument breaks down a bit when you start getting into things like AI, who will be using the memory and processing power of said computer as their wits/intelligence score. When said AI can implement thousands of versions of itself onto as single task that are simultaneously all the same AI with one goal, the situation becomes the same.

            Considering that the process of creating AI is well documented, heavily used by many companies, and is possible for anyone with the know-how, Mind 5: Create AI is already a mundane effect.

                Dealing with real world technology has always required a fair bit of suspension of disbelief, because let's be honest: attacks against the powerful rarely succeed, and even more rarely are gotten away with.                 In other words, games will deal with it the way they always have: by fudging things in the name of a good story.

                                        Just finished reading Magic A Very Short Introduction (Owen Davies) and would like to share with you this closing paragraph:

                                        'Magic cannot be consigned to the past, dismissed as the product of ignorance, or ranked as an
                                        evolutionary stage of human thought – at least not in cultural terms. As this Very Short Introduction
                                        demonstrates, since it was first expressed conceptually back in antiquity, it has accrued a range of
                                        meanings that ensure it retains its relevance in every society today. Magic is far more than a venerable
                                        collection of practices. We need to understand it as a language, a theory, a belief, an action, a creative
                                        expression, an experience, and a cognitive tool. It is integral to religion and yet can be independent of
                                        it. An atheist can believe in magic. Yes, science has undermined the basis of magic as an explanation
                                        for natural processes. It has rendered magic’s role in medicine largely redundant by providing
                                        clearly superior results – for those of us who can profit from them. But while science gives us
                                        explanations and solutions, it also provides us with a choice. It can help explain magic but cannot
                                        explain it away – magic is always an alternative. In its broadest sense, magic is a part of the human
                                        condition. To believe magic will eventually disappear is mere wishful thinking.'
