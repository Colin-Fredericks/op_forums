                            Originally posted by Imthestein View Post
                            So for me it's just tedious anytime the Technocracy gets demonized because after 4 editions it just feels like "yeah, we get it, you don't like them. Ooga booga science and technology bad". And don't get me started on the nonsense that all Science is only done by Etherites. That's an insult to the Scientific Community if I ever heard one.
                            My thoughts on this:

                            The Technocratic Union isn't just representatives of science and technology; it also has an agenda.
                            Technocracy: the government or control of society or industry by an elite of technical experts.
                            If you aren't in favor of an elite of technical experts running society or industry, you're not a Technocrat. This is true even if you favor science and technology. The problem is that Mage doesn't currently include a faction that represents science and technology but doesn't have the Technocratic agenda. So thus far, players who want their characters to be Scientists but not Technocrats have had to be rebels within the Union, Etherites, VAs, or (as of M20) Orphans with a scientific Focus. There are reasons why each of the latter three options are unacceptable to many, which up to now has left players who want Enlightened Scientists who aren't Technocrats with only one option: dissidents within the Union.

                            That's where Navalon comes in, hopefully: if it turns out the way I'm hoping it will, Navalon will be advocates of science and technology without the technocratic agenda, and also without the fringe sciences that the Society of Ether embraces or the mystical elevation of information that defines the VAs.

                            Would I have chosen the name “Navalon” for them? No. Nor would I have put the mythology of Avalon as a defining inspiration for the faction. Their Focus will almost certainly incorporate “Bring Back the Golden Age” (M20 pp.568–569), which I can understand people having a problem with. That said, I personally don't have too much of a problem with it — though my inclination for the “Golden Age” that they want to bring back would be the Order of Reason rather than Avalon. The notion of “bringing back” the ideals that Avalon (or the Order of Reason) represents strikes me as a reasonable rallying point for former Technocrats who have fled the Union.

                            So while I don't think it's exactly what I'm looking for, I do think that it's close.
