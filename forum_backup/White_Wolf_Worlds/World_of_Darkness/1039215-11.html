                                    Nope, the idea that xenophobia is part of human biology is contrary to everything we know about anthropology, and I think that you are confusing human social evolution for human biological evolution (humans have not changed that much biologically over the last 500,000 years, but we have change socially many, many times during that time period). There is very little evidence of violence between hominids in the skeletons that we have found and ritual cannibalism within the same tribal group was actually more common that tribal warfare for most of human existence. In fact, most tribal groups seemed to have practiced reproductive outcrossing with their neighbors (anthropologists find little evidence of inbreeding before Neolithic times), which makes sense if you do not want to be constantly worrying about your neighbors killing you in your sleep and taking your women because your neighbors are either your brothers or your women are their sisters. It was not until you got to the Neolithic era that you started to see evidence of widespread violence between groups, probably due to the social (not biological) evolution of xenophobia.

                                        While opposing them, are the Techno-pros, Unwilling to shift their claim that without Tech there would be no progress, and only stagnation.

                                        It's hard to think back, all those....4 days ago, how did this all begin? A simple comment, an expression of an opinion. an exchange of conflicting views... Then came the Anthropologists and the Historians, experts in their fields, willing to use real world claims to back up their notes. Then the Canon's were brought out, each one firing an impressive display of completely made up but generally accepted an adhered to facts. The carnage was brutal on all sides was brutal.
                                        Throughout the flurry of History lessons and Source book references, a number of voices were heard, appeals to emotion. Cries for the glory that is tech, anger towards the evil that is control. All of which built up a great catalogue of interpretive thoughts.

                                        We are all experts. We are all essayists. We are all the masters of our own downfall. Don't you see? We brought this upon ourselves. For each one feels entitled to their own interpretation, but to justify their own convictions, they must destroy any attempt to undermine our own theories. Our own theories about a world which we created, and we have the power to change because it doesn't exist.

                                        The facts are not facts. The History is optional. The main events are modular. And the masterminds are whatever makes sense at the time. Such ideas cannot be tolerated by either side. There is only one way to interpret this game, only one! Perhaps the only thing either end may agree on is that only one view can stand...

                                        We are trapped in a bitter war that has no end, no real purpose. As discussion continues to deteriorate, the massive walls of impenetrable explanation are being built, and cries of "Do what you're like, but this is why you're wrong" echo in all directions.

                                        What is there left to blame? Will an agreement ever be met? If neither side will back down I can only see one logical endpoint.

                                        Calling each other stupid.

                                        I pray it will not come to this.

                                                    The violence in the Neolitic is due to the advent of agriculture, which started a technological revolution beyond anything before that allowed humans to reach numbers higher than ever. With the agriculture, war was invented.

                                                    The low signs of violence prior to that is because human groups were too few and too sparced. So, your neighbors in a area of many, many miles, were indeed your brothers and sisters; the first groups, being all nomadic, couldn't hold a lot of individuals, so, after a certain size, the group would have to split.

                                                    And, like I said, humans were empathetic towards each other within the same group . Evolution also created this on us. But we have ALWAYS been xenophobics towards outsiders.

                                                    What does that mean? It means that, in a radius of 100 miles, every little group of a few dozens were "members of the tribe". But, 200 miles away, there would be "the others" - a different tribe. And those would NEVER cross the territory of the others - because they hated each other.

                                                    Xenophobia is not an exclusively human merit. It's all over the animal kingdom. Territory is one of the most important matters to many species. And the hominids weren't evolving only in the last 500.000 years. All those traits have been shaped since the times of our common ancestry with apes.

                                                    By the way, apes have that same behavioral pattern - empathy in group, xenophobia towards outsiders
