    Comparisons between the major powers of the gamelines

    10-04-2024, 12:15 AM
    So I’ve been trying to write a novel inspired by the wod but more high fantasy. With that in mind I have a question as to the greater power comparisons between the splats. Not including plot devices like the antediluvians, neverborn, incarne, archmages, and hell dukes where do each of the game lines come together in the greater hierarchy of the world? For instance taking them as a group where would methuselahs rank when compared to garou elders, high faith/torment demons, fae, and master mages.

          There's no answer, except the one you create.

          WoD wasn’t made to really compare in this way, instead each game makes assumptions about the others that work for that game. What are your assumptions? What rules will you use? What assumptions from each game are you willing to contradict?

          As a rule of thumb, the higher ups of each given splat are the most powerful beings from their gameline’s POV. The caveat, of course, is that to top it all they have biased views about each other.

          If I can add relevant considerations:

          Vampires: elders are cited as top-villains in every book. Not necessarily the highest challenge possible, but a high one nonetheless. But that said, they’re mostly feared for their plots than for their personal prowess. Methuselah, on the other hand, are described as above the reach of individual PCs, period, and they’re perfectly attainable within the rules.

          Garou: they’re always described as the absolute stronger splat physically, and their rules do the job. They’re not always known for their magical prowess, and at the high-end most lines don’t describe Elder werewolves as all that stronger than beginner ones. Even their own books frequently reinforce that a Garou should rely on their pack to not be in very bad waters against other splats.

          Nothing here is backed by the rules, though. Werewolves are simply stronger than everything else, they have access to more than enough magic to deal with anything, and an Elder with the right choice of Gifts, Rites, Totem, Gifts and Fetishes, none of those particularly hard for them to get, will sweep the floor with anything the other lines have to throw at them, bar only the most contrived situations, or a very well prepared Mage.

          Mages: those guys do whatever they want. They’re feeble humans until they decide to do some crazy shit. The rule of thumb is that Mages are easy targets as long as you kill them unnoticed and unexpectedly. If you’re expected, then you’ll be noticed. If you’re noticed, you’re screwed. This doesn’t change that much from book to book or line to line.

          Changeling: there’s little fluff about relative power here, and they’re generally disinterested of such comparisons, while the others rarely know anything about Changelings. Rules-wise, they have some crazy powers that can and do make them potential threats to everything else, bar a frontal fight with a Werewolf. On top of that, they’re crazy hard to notice until they want to, being otherwise seen as perfectly normal humans. Few splats, or even WoD players, know how dangerous a fae can be.

          Demons: RAW they’re unkillable, period. Only a Demon can permanently destroy another. While a Garou will have no trouble killing their current form, they come again, and again, and again. It is almost impossible to hide anything from them, as they can pierce illusions, are immune to any mental effect and detect any and every supernatural effect in a radius of their Faith in miles. And they can do some crazy shit through Pacts. All that before their Lores and Apocalyptic forms, which are cool.

          Fluff-wise, demons are demons. They’re less dangerous by themselves and more by their ability to influence and empower others, and then to bind them with Pacts. The worst antagonists at all other lines are Infernalists, and while those people aren’t necessarily serving the Fallen, those who do are just as dangerous.

          Other lines I don’t know enough to talk about.

              Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
              Other lines I don’t know enough to talk about.
              To add to this, wraith/risen, mummies and wan kuei are really, Really annoying to deal with.

              Wraiths are weird. They're difficult-to-impossible to oppose without necromancy or true faith, but are complete pushovers if you have their counter. They're the only splat with what is effectively immunity frames, have a number of powers based around affecting you from the other side of the guantlet/shroud, and the majority of powers they have are heavily focused on screwing with your emotions, perceptions of reality, or straight-up controlling you.

              Wan kuei are like a middle ground betwen kindred and garou. They have easy access to aggrivated damage, innate extra actions, the ability to step sideways, etc, but all of that is limited by their resource pool being split into lots of tiny pools, a limited ability to increase their dice pools, an xp tax on their powers higher than Mage Spheres, and the power potential of their disciplines caps out much weaker than kindred equivalents at elder levels(9-dot bodhisatva powers are often equivalent to 6-7 dot elder powers in vtm).

              If you're fighting a mummy, you're basically screwed. Mummies are immortal on a level orders of magnitude more resiliant than demons, have an innate true-faith equivalent, and 5-dot powers than can re-write reality on a scale even high-powered Mages would be impressed by. One of their powers literally allows them to edit how any one power, of any splat in WoD, functions mechanically across every member of that splat.

                  I don't know how to do spoilers in this so beware of spoilers for Hell-Storm from 96.

                  In the novel Hellstorm the Giovanni of Las Vegas (yes, the mob bloodline in the mob city so you can guess how strong they are) have setup a wraith prison where they just stash the wraiths of all their victims and any wraith they piss off. It's a terrible place full of angry ghosts. Many very powerful Giovanni have worked towards forming this prison and making sure it stands the test of time. A single mage basically decides "fuck those guys" and undoes the entire prison allowing the wraiths to screw over the Giovanni of Las Vegas. So one mage >>>>>> the strongest strong hold of Giovanni in North America. Now you can make arguments that the mage would lose to them in a straight fight or whatever, but truth is, the mage walked away and they didn't. That's the power level between Mage and pretty much everything else in WoD.

                          The only thing that I would add is that the higher ups amongst the Mummies are just as powerful as any Methuselah vampire. And probably someone like Horus is as powerful as an Antidiluvian.

                          As for the Wraiths, the Death Lords, rulers of Stygia, are as powerful as Methuselahs, but beings like Charon, the Lady of the Lake, Anubis and the Jade Emperor, those probably are as powerful as Antidiluvians, if not more so.

                          The thing about Wraith however is that there's no rules about that higher power scale, thus those are left as plot devices assumed by the setting. In Vampire, at the early days, there was the "Players Guide" which gave the disciplines above lvl 5 and all the way up to lvl 10, this showing "yes indeed, these are the broken powers of the Vampiric Gods". But Wraith never had that, so we never had the Arcanoi above 5, so we never got that glimpse of a potential Moliate 10 that Charon might possess. So, that type of comic books stuff that him or Anubis can theoretically do, were left in the air.

                          For Mummies however, we do have Hekai (their "disciplines") up to lvl 8, while that's NOT the maximum lvl, with lvls 9 and 10 being left for the STs as "plot devices". Thus, Mummies too can rank at the same lvl of bullshittery as 4th gen vamps or even Antidiluvians.

                              Originally posted by Hand-of-Omega View Post

                              This reminds me of one supplements' in setting advice from an elder vampire to a younger one: Basically, Mages have all sorts of ways of Knowing Things, so if you're gonna go after one, strike first, strike fast and strike hard, and HOPE you completely take them out! If not, or if they see you coming, then you're basically just fucked, and need to get out of town, change your identity and PRAY that you didn't leave something of yours that they can use to mystically strike at you from long range with!
                              Even then there are no garantees. If we are talking about the pinnacle of power, even excluding archmages, we are talking at least at Masters of several Spheres. A Mage who is a Master in ALL Spheres could theoretically do anything.

                              For instance, let's see: with Spirit 5 and Mind 5, said Master could place an effect that binds his soul to a place. Now add Life 5 and Prime 2, and then even if you throw said Master into the Sun, his soul will just travel to his hidden Sanctum and reform his body. And yes, I just created an immortality effect for a Master - true immortality, not just eternal youth. So, striking first, fast and hard wouldn't suffice.

                              Other than that, catching a Mage off guard is a hell of a feat. Basically anything that the ENTIRE Discipline of Auspex up to lvl 9 at least can do, Mages can also do at the lvls 1 or 2 of Spheres. Time 2, Entropy 1 and Correspondence 1 basically means that unless you have some heavy witchery shenanigans, you won't be able to do it. Add Prime 1 to it and it would be much more likely for them to find you then the other way around.

                              Speaking of which: Every Spheres at lvl 1, Time 2, Correspondence 2, Entropy 2 - The All Seeing Eye - this is a route that I invented. It gives you all the sensorial information needed... Whenever you need it. Entropy 2 serves as an "informational filter". Time 2 is obviously to see into the future (or even into the past), Corr 2 is to see far away places. Now, the thing is that, with Entropy 2, you don't even need to know about something that you should be aware of, the effect will trigger the information for you. Let's say you're distracted in your lab, when a pack of vamps are trying to get into the window. You're not expecting it, you're not aware that you should be aware of it. The effect however will be aware of it for you. It will immediately "turn on", showing you the undead nature of the intruders (assessed with Life 1 Matter 1), their general mental states (Mind 1) and their probable purpose (Entropy 1). Given you add Time 2 to it, you would get that "spider sense" ahead of time. Given Corr 2, you would be aware of the threat even if they were on another town. With Prime 1 you can be sure of their supernatural status, with spirit 1 you'll detect any spirits, fetishes etc they carry with them (so for instance, a Giovanni dragging several wraiths along the journey), Matter 1 will tell you the type of fabric that their clothes are made of and more. To avoid unnecessary overload of information, Entropy 2 will filter it into what you'll need for useful information - so, no need to find out every single illnesses of a target of interest if that would never be a relevant information for your Mage. Mind 1 also allows the Mage to process a ton of information at once, thus there's never a sensorial overload.
                              Add Mind 3 to it and on top of all that, you'll also be able to read the minds of any targets of interest.

                              Put this into Wonder Sunglasses that is always on and good luck to any one trying to jump into one of the Boys in Black of the Union. You won't find them, they will find YOU.

                              A single Wonder of that would turn John Schmoe from the streets into a superhero monster hunter. Even if he is no Hulk or no Flash not Doctor Strange, he would still be ahead of any monster, and would be able to deal with them with fire, sunlight or silver. And that's just lvl 1 and a few lvl 2 Spheres.
