    What's the point of the resource background in mta?

    06-10-2023, 11:41 PM
    Since pc's can simply use magick to help generate wealth, such as using entropy and time to help play the stock market, why would players want to take resources as a background and not invest in something different?

      Not all decisions are mechanically optimal. Some are for flavour, to fit the character background, to give some material to the Storyteller.

      Also, some paradigms are predicated on the power that money grants. It is hard to use that power if you lack the means initially.


      Think of Hypereconomics, it will be hard to be "the man of means and power" without starting with certain abilities and backgrounds, Resources among those.

          Also, if the PCs are consistently generating wealth over time, that would reasonably take the form of Resources dots on their sheets that they had earned through actions in-game, I would think. Even if they did not start with Resources, they would have them eventually. Backgrounds are things that characters often acquire through IC actions rather than spending experience points, though that can be an option sometimes.

          But also, setting aside the excellent point about the role Resources might play in a Focus, characters who convert their magical aptitude into enormous piles of cash are going to draw attention of some kind or another, especially if they start throwing it around. Having access to a mundane source of finances that isn't going to look suspicious can be valuable in itself, depending on your goals.

          A Mage can count on being able to easily scrounge up enough money for a sparse but comfortable lifestyle without too much trouble. But if you want the kind of money that lets you shape communities, then simply magicking it up has a lot of potential to backfire. That's not something you have to worry about if you happened to inherit the Wayne family fortune.

              Also Mages generally avoid being too obvious. By creating plausible deniablity a Mage can hide in plain sight. Someone who owns a large house and has no visible means of support stands out and inspires curiosity. Mages generally need space and privacy. Someone with a large bank account can have an isolated space of their own without exciting any special notice.

              Yes, some Mages live outside of the economy. But many more prefer camouflage. Besides, when a Mage needs to travel among the Sleepers, having a check card backed by a bank account and paid off credit cards with substantial credit limits is massively convenient. The Heremetic living in a large old house at the edge of the suburbs becomes just another old dude with cash when that cash is in the public records. The Verbena Witch becomes just another weirdo cat lady left an annuity by her parents with the Resources background. The Sahajiya jazz man with a small Jazz label is just another Sun-Ra wannabe, falling below the notice of the Technocratic Union.

              Resources are a useful mask and a flexible tool.

                                      Resources isn't just having money. It's the sum total of one's money, physical possessions (beyond what the character is wearing or carrying at the time), and their living situation (housing, access to transport, etc).

                                      If you have Resources 0, you either live in someone else's home, or are unhoused. Anyone who has spent any time without housing knows that that can be a knock-on effect on every other aspect of your life. (Although at the same time, there's people who are fully employed, but still can't afford housing. But that's a problem of our bass-ackwards economic system and housing/renting market). You have to find ways to keep your clothes clean, or people won't give you the time of day. (If they don't just call the cops, which can be as good as a death sentence if the cops or even passers by feel like committing murder that day). You have no car, which is either a debilitating handicap (in America) or a non-issue (most every other industrialized country). You may not even be able to walk into a store or fast food joint and buy something, without your ability to do so being in severe question. (So, if you need a tool from a hardware store to solve a problem, you might not be able to afford it. Same with lunch, which might leave your character hungry during key moments...or most of the time.)

                                      A Mage without Resources must basically rely on either the help of others (like fellow PCs) or constant use of Magick to accomplish goals (which carries its own risks, as established).

                                      At a certain point, the amount of extra problems having no Resources generates would be more of a hassle than just...investing a single dot into the Background. If you're using Magick to acquire money needed to get by, you'll probably end up getting Resources 1 during the game anyway.

                                      Playing a magical hobo is a time-honored tradition in Mage. But unless that's the character concept (or at least the campaign start) you're going for, you're just tying one hand behind your back for a while.

                                      Magestuck: The Ascension

                                          More generally: Resources provides a game system representing how much wealth you have access to. This can be useful in the game in a number of ways: you could, for instance, implement a rule where you can use Magick to temporarily boost a Background; which is essentially what the original poster was suggesting in terms of “just use Magick to play the stock market whenever you need a quick buck.” Just keep in mind that a mage who is constantly using Magick to boost his Resources is in the same boat as the mage who is always using Magick to boost his Strength: yes, you get the boost; but the fact that you're having to use Magick every time you want it is going to have consequences — and likely sooner rather than later. If you want reliable access to Resources, take the Background; just like if you want reliable access to the benefits of high Strength, raise your Strength.

                                          And the flip side of Magick and Resources is that there's at least one Magical Practice where Resources are effectively an Instrument: the Art of Desire, or Hypereconomics. Heck, access to Ventures is only possible for those with Resources.

                                                  The thing about Backgrounds is that most of them are designed to change according to what happens in the narrative. Get a sudden windfall that's going to leave you set for life? You're Resources Background goes up accordingly. Suffer a setback that wipes you or financially? Your Resources Background drops accordingly.

                                                  At least, that's one way to handle it. Another way is to treat the Background as a meta-narrative element: it serves as an indication to the Storyteller of how you want your character to relate to the world, and as such establishes a story of status quo that you're character might temporarily deviate from, but will tend to return to. In this view, someone with no Resources who attempts to acquire a windfall through Magick will succeed, for a time; but events will conspire to burn away the additional money that he has above and beyond his Resources rating. Unless and until he raises his Resources Background to match his newfound wealth, the new funds will always be in some sort of jeopardy until he loses them.

                                                  Conversely, a character with a high Resources score who suffers a Syndicate attack that wipes him out will have to deal with the loss of his financial well-being for a time; but as long as the high Resources Background is on the character sheet, he'll have an easier time getting back to his accustomed level of wealth than someone without the same Resources Background would be able to manage. Play out the story arc, then reset to the status quo. Or, if the status quo is permanently changed to one where you have a lower Resources Background overall, redistribute the Background dots from Resources to another, compensatory, Background. For instance, you lose your wealth, but you make new friends: trade dots of Resources for dots of Allies.

                                                  This second approach works better when you treat Backgrounds as something that you purchase with experience points, whereas the first approach works better when you don't attach xp costs to Backgrounds.

                                                  My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
