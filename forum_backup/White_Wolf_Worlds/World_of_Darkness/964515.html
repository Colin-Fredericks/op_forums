                          Well, it's less boring than a game in which Werewolfs encounter the Apocalypse. What happens after that? Nothing. Can I make a new character? No, everything is gone.
                          Honestly, it would be a game of abject failure.

                          Back to the topic, Ascension is one of those things that a lot of mages talk about, but few actually understand. It's the mysterious ideal that always lies just out of reach. What's more interesting to me would be a game about mages who constantly try to achieve Ascension but always find something is holding them back.
                          A powerful hermetic may think that all they need is to max out their Arete, but doing so only cements them in the folds of the reality they control.
                          An Akashic may think achieving ascension requires perfecting their mind and may work tirelessly to do this by countless journeys into the astral realm, increased isolation to avoid distraction, and the gradual removal of any and all material things, until the only thing holding them back is their determination to ascend.
                          An Etherite may think to ascend is to build the most perfect machine, a verbena may think it's to bring themselves back to their primordial roots, you get the idea.

                          The idea would be to have the focus be on their dogged determination, and how said determination is the biggest barrier to achieving their goal.

                          As an aside, one of the last books released for mage after the reckoning subplot was the Ascension storyline. It gave details on the possibilities the mage universe had for a definitive end and ranged from mass ascension, to Nephandi victory as the world descends into oblivion. So if you wanted to see a few "final" story lines, that would be a good place to start.

                          That being said, the books been decanonised since writing the revised convention books. Turns out giving a final end to the world isn't very good for selling books.

                                                          Sorry if my previous post came off as harsh.

                                                          I'm fascinated by the Ascension concept. The less defined it is, the more mysterious it is. All greater concepts should be less defined if you want to keep the mystery; Antediluvians, Grandmother, Ascension, Oracles, Gods, etc. So the hard part of role playing a game after someone reaches ascension is to make it interesting without defining too much of what happens. That's the thing; Once you NAME something or slap a label of meaning on it, it suddenly BECOMES that, to the exclusion of anything that is NOT THAT. So then you've boxed it in in some manner, giving it definition and meaning. And by doing so, it suddenly means less than it did, or has less potential than it did. Certain groups of Mages are known to do this. Start defining things until they mean something that fits in their world view instead of leaving it Unbound by human comprehension.

                                                          That is the difference between Modern Age and the Mythic Age. One is understood by being quantified, the other is understood intuitively... actually, that's just my interpretation. But I think it's very fitting.

                                                          So to run a fun game where things go beyond Ascension, the POWER of what you are dealing with needs to have meaning. The way to keep that meaning and/or mystery is to not name so much of it, don't quantify it. Keep the explanations loose. Otherwise those powerful beings you now know about (or have joined) seem... somehow less. You don't want that. That ruins the awesomeness when what you have achieved feels mundane like the real world. A Storyteller can easily go wrong in this department and make the Ascension experience feel like another version of our world; Same archetypes, same power struggles, same hierarchies, etc. There IS a reason why these powerful beings are ill-defined. The mystery is what makes them wonderful and the sheer power is hard for people to imagine and cognize, so writers and game lines (myself included) stop describing stuff at some point because there just isn't any real world reference for one's imagination to draw upon. If you're lucky, you can be creative enough and make it work. But, these are not games for everyone just as elder Vampire games and Archmage games are not for everyone.
