        I liked the Week of Nightmares, good and bad, and I loved the metaplot. It's one o the biggest reasons I've always been turned off by the nWoD, really. Even with the later psuedometaplot, it was kind of too little, too late.

                    I have mixed feelings about the week of nightmares. I like the idea of it, but I certainly don’t like the execution. It did feel like one of the Vampire game line’s Big Bads was just offhandedly killed because….Technocracy. There doesn’t seem to be any reason why, if another Antediluvian rises, the Technocracy can’t just do it again. And that sort of precedent is really bothersome to me.
                    Aside from that, I’ve never liked the idea that an Antediluvian with animalism as an in clan was supposedly in frenzy for days on end. I remember there being a post from one of the Devs explaining why they had chosen the Ravnos founder, and it was basically: it’s a big event that shows the End is here, without altering the clan balance in the main Sects. That reasoning bothered me as well.
                    Acknowledging that Changeling wasn’t everyone’s favorite, I still felt that they should’ve played a bigger part (or any part for that matter) considering that Chimesrty was being thrown around a lot. I seem to remember that in one of the fluff parts of the book, it even had a Satyr and the effect that the WoN had on him.
                    I don’t know. Kind of going back to the first point, I think for the giant crossover event that this was supposed to be, more could have been included, and then just having the Technocracy step in and “solve” the event came off as kind of a cop out (imo).

                        To be honest the Technocracy didn't just offhandedly kill it. First of all the Spirit Nukes wounded it, but it could have recovered from that, and managed to kill those destined to really kill the Antediluvian, while dying themselves as well, and the orbital mirror unleashed after the nukes only managed to kill it because of exact timing, managed unknown by the Technocracy by an Elder Kuei-Jin with a Hsien companion who was the one who actually held the storm at place shielding the Ante from any such attempts. The lesson here was that it took members of three splats unleashing all they had to kill a single antediluvian and it still was all about minute timing.

                        It was a carefully orchestrated play where the four battling ancients, the 3 Kuei-Jin and the Antediluvian, were all destined to die but as soon as the Technocracy, Mages that they are, started to twist reality they muddled that fate and it took careful manipulation of circumstances and conditions of the fight to make sure that the destined ending would come about. Also a mere one antediluvian awaking in a frenzy meant millions of casualties and changed how the world worked permanently, Avatar Storm etc., so it really wasn't just about Techies running to help and wiping the floor with the big bad of another line. It was way more than that though all the surrounding circumstances are often forgotten as people only concentrate on the very final blow and who delivered it.

                        As a side note I personally didn't really care for the Week of Nightmares and I absolutely abhorred the changes it brought into the mechanics of certain lines, Avatar Storm I'm looking at you. I thought they also missed a lot of metaplot hooks they had planted over the years, Ravana was said to be researching a way to harvest souls of those who died of nuclear blasts to revitalize Lanka and his own power and now suddenly they blasted several spirit nukes right where he had planned it and nothing. Not a single mention about the matter. And more of such let downs were to follow with the actual Gehenna, and other lines similar books, which ignored almost all the story hooks planted over the years, with Vampire basically ending with God just killing all vampires off.... How anticlimatic was that?
