          The "Time of Judgment" event in 2003-2004 included wrap-up books for many of the supernaturals.
          • January 14, 2004: Gehenna and the associated fiction, Gehenna: The Final Night (Vampire: The Masquerade)
          • February 2: Apocalypse and the associated fiction, The Last Battle (Werewolf: The Apocalypse)
          • March 1: Ascension and the associated fiction, Judgment Day (Mage: The Ascension)
          • March 15: World of Darkness: Time of Judgment was a big all-in-one sourcebook including end-of-the-world material for Changeling: The Dreaming, Kindred of the East, Hunter: The Reckoning, Mummy: The Resurrection, and Demon: The Fallen.
          • March 15: Mind's Eye Theatre: Laws of Judgment did a similar thing from the LARP side, providing material for Laws of the Night (VTM), Laws of the Wild (WTA), Laws of Ascension (MTAs), The Shining Host (CTD), Laws of the Reckoning (HTR), Laws of the East (KotE), Laws of the Resurrection (MTR), and an Oblivion (WTO) adaption of Wraith's final book from 1999, Ends of Empire.
          • March 29: End Game wrapped up the Orpheus miniseries. Not technically part of the Time of Judgment, but fits thematically.

                      Originally posted by Psicose View Post
                      In any of these scenarios, if you can call it that, some supernatural remains, because I only know Gehenna really well and what I can say is that the most likely thing is that kindred ceased to exist as a species.
                      Gehenna definitely tends to lean that way, in the sourcebook, but really "the most likely scenario" is completely dependent upon the background decisions that the Storyteller makes about the cosmology and mythology of "their" World of Darkness. The penchant for the Cainite condition to disappear in Gehenna tends to be predicated upon the will of the Abrahamic God, who tends to be portrayed at least fairly analogous to the Bible version. But if your World of Darkness metaphysics include a God who is non-existent or radically different from the Biblical interpretation, then your outcome is going to change massively.

                      I do think that "supernatural creatures disappear" is something that comes up in some of the printed End Times scenarios, but it's a less common feature than in the Gehenna storylines. Admittedly, plenty of them also include a world utterly ruined, nothing but a war-torn hellscape fought over by the worst monsters that have managed to survive the End of the World. I can't recall them all off the top of my head to provide more details; Werewolf and Mage got three or four each as well, and then there were close to another half-dozen scenarios in Time of Judgment.

                              I have an ongoing setting and series of articles over at my patreon that entertains a world after the assorted apocalypse scenarios. One is an Earth that is crawling back after the various end times forces rose. I called it The Fortean Wasteland. Below is the introduction.


                              SETTING INTRODUCTION
                              “So, then, in general metaphysical terms, our expression is that, like a purgatory, all that is commonly called "existence," which we call Intermediateness, is quasi-existence, neither real nor unreal, but expression of attempt to become real, or to generate for or recruit a real existence.” ―Charles Fort, The Book of the Damned
                              It’s been at least four generations since the End of the World. No one knows the exact number of years since the old world was destroyed by the “Occult Apocalypse” that wiped out Western Civilization. China, India, etc – all gone. Most of planet Earth is empty of (living) human habitation, the exception being the country formerly known as the United States of America.’
                              The major cities of the world have been all but destroyed, with even those cities remaining much reduced in population and infrastructure. Now all communities having fortifications, alert guards and curfews. Most people are at a Wild West level of technology (1860’s). Higher technology such as radio, airships and steam cars are the product of inventors and owned by the wealthy. Transit has been reduced to animals, wagons, and the occasional iron horse. Airships are increasingly popular, as they are a safer way to cross the wastes.

                              GAME NOTES: The different supernatural conspiracies of the (old) World of Darkness are mostly wiped out, having waged war on each other and tore the world apart. Now, the worlds spiritual background is in tatters, and old, primal elements are healing in their way. The many weird and dangerous creatures formerly held in check by Vampires, Werewolves, Mages, Changelings, Wraiths and such are now loose in the world, haunting mankind like they did in ancient times.
                              Mortals do not know all of the details! Rather, everything is filtered through a hodgepodge of data and misinformation. As a general rule, I will be using lore found in Fortean accounts of the world such as Alien Abduction, Cryptids, Conspiracy Theories and similar material to flesh out the world.

                              -------------------------------------------------

                              HISTORIC EVENTS (Classic World of Darkness)
                              This is how the WOD settings contributed to the setting background.

                              Vampire the Masquerade (Gehenna): The Antediluvian vampires rose and waged war on their children. All kinds of hell broke loose here, and a great many mortals died. Efforts to fight these monsters decimated cities and clashed with other supernatural entities (see below), laying waste to large portions of the populace. Most of the Antediluvians and vampires were killed, with the notable exception of the Eldest, the amorphous progenitor of the Tzimisc clan. Now, the great clans are all gone, with only Caitiff vampires in evidence, with most living as feral predators. A few small bloodlines can be found, but these are very remote. Revenant families (ghoul families who produce their own vampire vitae) are what remains of vampire society. They view humanity as a lesser species, suitable only as pets, playthings and food. But they look human, and rule over businesses, petty kingdoms and cults.

                              Werewolf the Apocalypse (Apocalypse): The Wyrm thrashed in its prison, eventually breaking partially free and sending armies of gross, corrupted monsters out to destroy the world. Huge monsters rose out of the Earth and seas to rampage across the countryside and through cities (often battling Antediluvians). Fomori and Black Spiral armies attacked the Garou and other shapeshifters wreaking terrible devastation. Plagues, poison and corruption soaked the land. Now the Garou tribes and the other Gaian shapeshifters are gone, with only the most feral, wild shapeshifters still lurking in the wilderness. Wyrm monsters of many types roam the wilderness, especially Thunderwyrms, Skull Pigs and assorted Banes and other monsters. The Wyld has returned in force in the form of the Fomorians (see Changeling below) and is just as dangerous. Fomori, both wild and organized are a major issue, with cannibal mutants and cunning ferectoi ruling over wild tribes.

                              Mage the Ascension (Ascension): In the wake of the rising Antediluvians, the Wyrm monsters and the discovery of Nephandi corruption in the highest ranks of the Technocracy, the Union collapsed into chaos. The council of Nine fell to their own troubles, and Mages around the world (and across spacetime) fell into chaos. With the tearing of huge swaths of the Pattern Web, the high technology of the previous age is lost. Consensual reality has reverted to superstition and a magical view of the universe (with good reason). Though some true mages may exist, they are exceedingly rare and there are none of the old conventions or traditions. Hedge Magic focusing on Hermeticism, miracles, nature magic, shamanism and necromancy are the most common with Technomancers being more like natural philosophers and “steampunk” gadgeteers.

                              Changeling the Dreaming (The Long Winter): The Fomorians, Wyld beings of the Elder Dark returned, waging war on the Tuathan Dream and the Fae. Along with all of the chaos of the Antediluvians, Wyrm monsters and mortal warmongers, the Fomorians were almost an add-on. However, these primordial monsters are the embodiment of the Wyld, the ferocious power of cataclysmic change, and were greatly needed. The fae courts and kingdoms were thrown down, and the unbridled chaos of Nightmare and explosive creativity was unleashed. Now, the Dreaming is wild and chaotic, more akin to divine madness than anything that should be in a Disney cartoon. The Elder Dark and the fae now lurk in the deep wilderness, shaping new nightmares and feeding off the terror and dreams of mortal, ghoul, fomori, beast and elements alike. Kinain (quasi-fae) are often found as mystics, magicians and go-betweens to the fae. Many fae take the form of alien like abductors, snatching humans for breeding stock and toys.

                              Wraith The Oblivion (End Game): Wraith society was destroyed by the 6th Great Maelstrom and set off a series of terrible natural disasters in the Mortal World, neatly coinciding with those of the other groups. The world is plagued with zombie uprisings, flesh-eating locusts and huge numbers of wraiths, specters and all manner of undead spirits. The efforts of powerful beings such as Mummys, Vampires, Mages, mortal psychics and powerful spirit beings manage to fight off the growing threat of Grandmother at significant cost, sealing the "All-Devourer" away in the Maelstrom once more, though many Malfeans continue to operate in their ancient lairs. Now the Shadowlands are quiet, but the shroud between worlds is thin. Stygia is destroyed, and there is little order among wraiths, with most becoming wild specters or returning as ravening undead monsters and other creatures.
