      For many years I ran cities as multi-clan just like how the books suggested. But over the last 5-10 years I have been leaning more towards the notion of mono-clan cities with unique laws and cultures. The reason for this is that most of the glass ceiling issues that are prepacked into vampire go away when you make this change and most of the context for problems changes.

      For instance, in a normal multi-clan city you bounce off all the power blocks, the old vs. the new, this clan vs. that clan, this path of enlightenment vs. that path of enlightenment. That is a lot of stuff to navigate before you can deal with surviving lupine or sabbat incursions, or just getting your nightly meal.

      On the other hand, a coterie of a single clan type move to a small town or city without other vampires, they declare themselves prince and primogen of the city, they embrace as they see fit, they enforce laws as they see fit, and they adjust the human society as they see fit without ever needing to come out of the shadows. If they wish to allow other clans or bloodlines to setup shop, then they are constrained to a specific section of town to setup their haven with explicit understanding that they are a long term guest, rather than an equal owner.

      Most multi-clan cities feel cookie cutter to me at this point. Clan A is on top, clan B is supporting them in theory, while clan C is outright trying to upend the power structure. It is pretty much paint by numbers, with the only variables being which clan is in which position.

      Conversely lets look at some of the mono-clan cities I have used.

      Ventrue City:
      There is a central blood bank which acts as a blood clearing house for ventrue with specific tastes. You can either get blood packs or get the identity information of the donor that matches your needs so you can fetch them for yourself. All the excess blood isn't ventrue fodder is sold on the world market to hospitals and research facilities. Into the Shadows did a good overview of the blood industry which makes for some interesting vampire ideas. The benefits of this city design is that hunting is practically never done, so most of the headlines or activities that hunters use to detect a vampire presence never happen.

      Nagaraja City:
      Between the need for meat and the obvious teeth modifications, Nagaraja have a hard time blending in. So I based a mono-bloodline city around the concept of Midnight Meat Train. A single ghoul hunts people who won't be missed or who prey upon the community. This once again keeps the public face of the city very clean and orderly. Because you can only fight necromancy with necromancy most other supernaturals avoid the city. Furthermore if there are lean times, the Nagaraja can eat the ghosts of their victims for a second meal. This creates a feeling of a group meal and almost a holy ritual being carried out, rather than each vampire doing their own thing.

      Gangrel City:
      I used this in a historical game, where the pueblo cities in america were actually society experiments set up by gangrels. The people were cut off from the ground below while the vampires ran the cities during the night. When the vampire lords were defeated the mortals finally abandoned the cities. All in all it was a symbiotic relationship, the vampires hunted and provided food for the mortals as well as security, while the mortals looked after them while they slept during the day, at least until they could earth meld.

      Very different cities, very different cultures, and very different views on mortals.

                          Individual cities could have any kind of clan demographics because so much depends on the individual vampires and the history of that domain. Large cities tend to have more diversity in clans, but small cities (metro areas under 500k) tend to be made up of only one clan (the Prince and her brood).

                          However, I do have general world population of the clans in my head. Any percentages are only top of my head figures, not firm numbers. Below is a rough estimate based on the original setting of the game, as I ignore almost all metaplot. Therefore, some of my statements will not make sense to those who accept all the changes or assumptions in Revised and V5.

                          The Brujah are the most numerous vampires because they are numerous in all sects and their nature makes them prone to a lot of rash embraces. They are well over half the Anarchs, one of the larger clans in the Camarilla (though in many domains there is not much distinction between an "anarch" and other vampires who accept the Camarilla other than the "anarch" is currently out with the local Prince. The moment local politics changes that vampire is no longer an "anarch".), and the Brujah anti-tribu are almost as numerous in the Sabbat as the Lasombra and Tzimisce. If you combine the Cam/Anarch into one, the Brujah probably make up around 25% of all vampires. In the Sabbat, they are only 15% or so.

                          The Toreador are probably the next numerous clan. It's probably the second largest clan in the Camarilla, and it still has respectable numbers in the Sabbat though behind the big three. They probably comprise around 20% of the Camarilla and 10% of the Sabbat.

                          The Ventrue are probably next. They are another major clan of the Camarilla, often establish successful broods, and are the largest clan in many domains or countries Having many members being Princes result in siring privileges. But they are very few of them among the Anarchs, and the Ventrue anti-tribu are one of the smaller clans in the Sabbat. Both the Brujah anti-tribu and Ventrue anti-tribu are leaders of the Loyalist movement. They comprise around 15% of the Camarilla, but only 5% or so of the Sabbat (which is a much smaller sect, only a third of the Camarilla).

                          Gangrel are next, although much of their population is "hidden" in the sense they reside in small towns and the wilderness as solitary vampires or in small broods rather than easily documented in cities governed by Princes or Bishops. There may not actually be more Camarilla Gangrel than there are, say, Camarilla Nosferatu, but there are a lot of Gangrel who are essentially Autarkis (except when they visit cities and abide by any Camarilla rules there) or independent in the wilderness, and the Sabbat Gangrel (Country and City plus offshoots like the Ahrimanes) collectively make up a significant part of the sect. They are probably around 10% in each sect plus a large number effectively independent. Many of these independents might be loosely affiliated with the Camarilla, Sabbat, or Anarchs, but merely for convenience.

                          Nosferatu are probably just behind the Gangrel, and like them are probably around 10% of each sect (meaning the Camarilla Nosferatu are around three times their Sabbat counterparts). The Sabbat Nosferatu usually pack/coven among themselves because they have more in common with their Camarilla counterparts than they do with other Sabbat. The Nosferatu are mainly concerned with survival, and their membership in both sects is more like hedging their bets than anything else. This does not mean there are not differences between them or that there are not conflicts, but they continue to share information and secrets. Of course individual Nosferatu always have their own opinions, so sometimes this cooperation is not mentioned to those individuals who are more vocal about their sect membership and are involved politically with it.

                          Malkavians would be next, and are probably under 10% but over 5% of each sect. They are a respectably sized clan, but nothing special. While there are more Camarilla Malkavians, as a percentage of their sect, the Sabbat Malkavians are probably higher because I see 1) the role of Vasantasena in creating/saving the sect after the Convention of Thorns gives the Malkavian anti-tribu a prestige their Camarilla counterparts do not have, 2) their mental problems are probably not considered to be as much of a problem within the Sabbat, and 3) siring is not as controlled in the Sabbat as it is in the Camarilla.

                          Lasombra are next. While they are a major component of the Sabbat, maybe 20% or more of the sect, the Sabbat is smaller than the Camarilla and there are very few Lasombra anti-tribu. The Lasombra clan was gutted during the Anarch Revolt with many of their vampires dying during the Anarch Revolt, and their Sabbat numbers collapsed when that sect was devastated by the Camarilla-Sabbat Wars and the first two Sabbat Civil Wars. While their numbers have recovered, it is mainly a result of new neonates being embraced. Being limited to the minority sect means your overall numbers just do not reach those of the Camarilla. The Lasombra probably number less than a third of the Ventrue's total numbers.

                          Tzimisce are right under the Lasombra and their numbers are similar. Major components of the Sabbat, but almost no numbers outside of that and suffering the same problems of the clan being gutted during the Anarch Revolt and suffering hard when the Sabbat falls on hard times.

                          Tremere are next. Almost all their members are in the Camarilla with only a small number of "House Goratrix" being in the Sabbat. However, even if they are only 5-8% of the Camarilla, since the Camarilla is the largest sect this means they have more numbers than the so-called Independent Clans. In terms of influence and power, they punch above their numbers in the Camarilla. House Goratrix survives because the Lasombra find them useful as allies against the Tzimisce. There are only a handful of chantries, and most Tremere anti-tribu form chantry packs. The Sabbat Tzimisce would eliminate the Tremere anti-tribu if they could so House Goratrix avoids areas where Tzimisce power is high and Lasombra weak.

                          Caitiff are likely next, although their numbers may be higher than I initially figure. In some books, the Caitiff seem to be significant, and in other books their numbers are much lower. This probably has more to do with writers being interested in exploring Caitiff rather than a commentary on their true numbers. Over time this population will likely increase in size. I can see an argument that would present them larger than the total number of Tremere. This includes all types - Caitiff, Panders, Thin-Bloods.

                          Setites are the most populated independent clan. There are several reasons why their numbers are so low. First, they do not hold power in many domains, and therefore lack opportunities to sire in both of the large sects. Their strongholds are few compared to other clans. Second, they do not get along well with one another. Setite priests operate alone with perhaps a childe. They do not sire large broods, but seek to spread new temples far away from each other. When they don't, infighting quells their numbers. Third, they have lots of enemies who wish them dead - many members of the Camarilla, the Sabbat, Lupines, Hunters, and many others. Their numbers are probably expanding a lot in Africa whose population has increased greatly in the past century, with large growing cities, and in an area where the Camarilla's institutions are weak and the Sabbat's presence is negligible. However, Setite clan culture often leads to feuds and infighting due to competition slows down such growth.

                          Ravnos are next simply because both the Assamites and Giovanni have limitations put on them by the Treaty of Tyre and Promise of 1528. In my chronicles, the Camarilla is indeed a worldwide organization so Indian vampires (like Unmada or Bindusara) have always been a part of it from the beginning. India does have more Ravnos than normal, but the traditional clans have always been well represented. A clan of nomads who often irritate the powers that be simply cannot have any substantial numbers. But because they wander widely they are present in many places. They are not as well populated as the Gangrel because they lack the means Gangrel have to hide from Lupines, have enemies in the Gangrel who tend to be better fighters, and lack the political support the Gangrel have in their sect memberships.

                          Assamites are next. When your clan organization is a cult of assassins devoted to an esoteric understanding of vampire history and eschatology, your numbers are low. They have a few strongholds outside Alamut ruled by the Old Man of the Mountain, but not many. Where individual vampire assassins are allowed to stay in a domain, like Badr in Milwaukee, they cover a lot of territory because there isn't that much of a need for vampire assassins at any one time or place. To stay busy enough to earn their vitae, they need to cover a lot of ground. There might be just as many Assamite anti-tribu as there are Assamites. They are just limited in numbers because most of them are members of the Black Hand or prospective members.

                          Giovanni are the smallest clan. Per original canon, they are only allowed three embraces every generation which is about one every decade. So a total of 50 new Giovanni have been embraced since the Promise of 1528. There are probably a few dozen Giovanni from earlier, but that is it. Even if we combine these numbers with a hundred or so surviving Cappadocians in whatever form (Samedi, Lamia, Harbingers, etc.) there's just not much left of the old Cappadocians. The Anarch Revolt and Giovanni purge hit them hard.

                          All the bloodlines are extremely small, perhaps each one being only a dozen or a few dozen at most.

                                  The tricky part about this sort of population explanation is that several other parts of the setting start to fall apart. One of the defining differences between the Sabbat and the Camarilla is that only the former does mass embraces, while most of the clans have criteria for embraces.

                                  Ventrue, Lasombra, and Giovanni want competence above all else.
                                  Tremere and Giovanni are after occult individuals.
                                  Gangrel want survivors.
                                  Toreador will embrace on a whim of emotion, the rest of the time they are trying to preserve an artist for all time, in either case neither are seen as disposable.
                                  Assamites desire combat capable mortals.
                                  The Followers of Set have the morality for mass embrace cannon fodder, but they are very focused on their temple indoctrination, which precludes the strategy.

                                  So really the only clans that have both the means and morality to go the cannon fodder route are the Brujah and the Nosferatu. The Brujah will throw themselves at the enemy or at an point of contention with wild abandon. But given the passionate behavior of the brujah they are just as likely to view mass embraces as morally repugnant as see it as a viable option.

                                  The nosferatu on the other hand have options, when the entire nos population of a city can disappear or reappear somewhere else, you don't need to fight head on thus there isn't a real need for mass embrace cannon fodder. This strategy is made even less likely when you consider the nos spawning pools which provide them with hordes of mutant ghoul animals.
