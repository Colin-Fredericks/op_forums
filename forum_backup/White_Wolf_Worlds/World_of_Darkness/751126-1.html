                            A whole lot of these ideas for "creating from scratch", including all the ones in the OP, are easily compressible into a single, core thing: turn Pattern X into different Pattern Y. In other words, as long as you have some thing you're willing to use as 'raw materials', you can make something else with it, as long as you have the ruling Spheres for both the source item and the result item.

                            Typically the Patterning Spheres are Life, Forces, or Matter, but Mind and Spirit also act as Pattern Spheres for their respective abstracted phenomena (mental constructs and spiritual manifestations). You might even be able to use the glow of your own anima as a source for physical light or other energy (a Spirit 2/Forces 3 rote) similar to an Exalt's Anima Banner.

                            There might even be Patterns ruled by Correspondence and Time which can be sacrificed, and one could conceivably cause something to come into being by corroding organizational structures with Entropy, if you get esoteric enough.

                            Transmuting to and from Prime, of course, is the straightforward "make something from nothing" and "destroy a Pattern".

                                            I'm necroeing (?) this thread to get more ideas on how to do that with Forces 3 only.

                                            So, curse of friction is a rote that let's a Forces 3 mage transform the target movement into heat, making it imobile and dealing damage. It also can turn the target breath into freezing cold, enough to hold him in a block of ice. This shows just how much Forces 3 can indeed increase the energy that is being manipulated.
                                            Another example of energy increase is ik hdydt, where Forces 3 is able to lift about 2 tons just by manipulating forces.

                                            Having said that, I ask you: what are the best options of energies to transform? The more abundant and strong the energy is the better. Gravity and magnetism are practically constant on Earth snd pretty strong. Electromagnetic waves are also present almost anywhere. So there are a lot of options.

                                            However, in a very specific and scarce scenario, like for example in deep space, there wouldn't be Earth's magnetic field nor magnetic field. Electromagnetic waves would probably be present, otherwise the mage won't be able to even see. And thermal energy is definitely present. Otherwise...well... The mage would already be dead in the 2,7 K almost absolute 0 temperature of intergalactic space.

                                            Now, all this have consequences. Transforming a kind of energy into another will make the original kind to disappear. This means that transforming gravity into lightning would make things weightless, transforming light would make the area dark and transforming heat would make the area cold. I don't know if this would have a mechanical consequence, though. It would also last for a fraction of a second, which is the time the electric arc would take to hit the target, so maybe that's no big deal?

                                            So, what you guys think? What you believe it's the best option? And if there is any other that's worth mentioning, please do.

                                                Earlier in the thread you said something along the lines of 'paradigm doesn't matter' but you're using a technocratic, science based paradigm here which makes me think that you're making the classic mistake of Mage players/STs in thinking that the player reality science is pretty much the same as the game's consensual reality.

                                                i.e. you're assuming that consensual reality's rules are science based when they're not. The consensual reality rules are based on spheres - they are in fact the game rules and the game makes a strong point that the paradigm a mage believes in is what really matters.

                                                This is something that a lot of powergamers have trouble with in Mage, they try to use 'science' to blag their way through a spell effect even when thy are using a non-science paradigm. The funny thing is there are plenty of options to powergame spells - try looking at the 'pulling on mythic treads' rules for a start.

                                                To answer your question it _is_ important to know the paradigm as that is what allows you to manipulate reality, most of the previous answers approach it from a technocratic science point of view so I'll try something different.

                                                A couple of points - Firstly, converting invisible forces into visible forces is something that I personally wouldn't allow as there's a good reason why Prime 2 is required, which is to stop blaggards powergaming everything! Furthermore with Forces 3 you can only transmute minor forces in limited areas, M20 p516 lists things that can be transmuted if you combine other spheres so all of those are out of bounds, meaning we are looking at pure force to pure force. Lastly, this is always vulgar and depends on getting a large number of successes on an Arete roll which means it requires a pretty powerful mage or some serious preparation. You should also be prepared for high levels of paradox.

                                                Given all those restrictions the only thing I can think of that even comes close is Aether (Aether (classical element) - Wikipedia) - the Sons of Ether posit that everywhere is full of 'ether' which can be used to power their spells, if your ST is willing to let you blag it without Prime 2 then you could use Forces 3 to transmute the ether into whatever you want.

                                                In a similar vein there are a couple of other aether-like energies although they require quite an extreme paradigm that is laden with vulgar magic and paradox

                                                Vril - Wikipedia
                                                Orgone - Wikipedia
