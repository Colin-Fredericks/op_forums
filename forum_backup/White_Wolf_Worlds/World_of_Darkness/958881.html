    A good system for Armour/weapons

    08-27-2016, 05:03 PM
    So, it pains me, but combat in world of darkness is really only good if your characters are changing breeds and your players know how a fight works. Sadly, most of the gamelines don't involve playing savage naked beasts and your average player doesn't know what a punch is. This problem is further confounded when the developers make stupid mistakes like thinking a warhammer is a bashing blunt weapon, (or in other games, a greatsword is difficulty 5, Strength+6) and a bigger problem, far bigger, is that some armours are -3 dexterity, meaning you need Olympian levels of fitness to wear it, because armour is apparently not supposed to be a practical form of protection.

    Weapons and armour are used by people, and should be ballanced for people/what they would be in real life, to the nearest approximate that mechanics can get to without bogging down. -3 dex for armour... well, that's just unfeasible for humans.

    My friend told me that -3 dexterity is fine cause it's for vampires and they can blood buff and have physical disciplines, and that the example knight in the book had 4 in dex (and 4 in most attributes) so it's ok, but he's the stupidest friend I have, so I won't listen to him. (no seriously, the average professional soldier will have 2-3 dex. You don't make common soldiers Olympians, nor should you drastically different rules to them)Mage 20 is better than most, I think. I use it for other splats. Well, it's not 'good' It just isn't all bad. Sure there's some odd things up there (leather dusters are as limiting as plate? Sure.) But let's try to avoid the claymores and the warhammers and the dusters and punch daggers (and most certainly avoid the Flak jackets and 11th century broadsword) It's more the system than the things in it.
    Armour is by far worse than weapons. A simplistic "+soak, -dex" system. Melee Guns are mostly alright. Melee weapons fall apart with reach.


    I liked that mage, for weapons, had little numbered notes on. Like Special #7- +1 to opponent's block attempts. Keep that. Do that more.

    ARMOUR
    Should be at different difficulties to avoid. A breastplate/vest is at +1 difficulty, while a guy in full plate might be at +3 or +4.
    Should be more effective at negating certain types of damage. A sword slash might have an increased difficulty to do damage against medieval mail, and might do bashing rather than lethal(or aggravated!) if less than the armour value is scored in damage.

    More reasonable penalties for armour use than Dexterity penalties include
    Penalties to initiative.
    +1 or 2 difficulty to athletics rolls. This might double in some situations (like swimming)
    Cost and conceal. Armour, Modern or old is expensive and is a massive sign that you're planning to be in a fight. You could hide a vest under a suit, but you can't hide the thicker military stuff under something so thin.

    I liked that mage, for weapons, had little numbered notes on. Like for a weapon there was Special #7- "+1 to opponent's block attempts". Keep that. Do it for armour.

    Examples of armour.
    Light 'soft' kevlar vest. 3 armour, +0 on athletics rolls. Jacket conceal. -1 initiative. +1 to avoid (vest). +0 to any damage.
    Mail hauberk. 3 armour. +1 on athletic rolls. Coat conceal. -2 initiative, +2 to avoid. +1 to cutting
    Plate. 5 armour. +2 on athletics. No conceal. -4 initiative. +4 to avoid. +1 to cutting and +1 concussive
    Full Modern military . 5 armour. +1 on athletics. No conceal, -2 initiative. +2 avoid. +1 to projectiles


    Totally a crude thing by me. Would work much better laid out in a table. The strengths weaknesses of an armour might get simplified to a few words, or something done in the weapon section.
    Just a stupid example of how this works.
    Sam tries to cut alex with a sword. Alex is wearing Mail. Mail is resistant against cutting, so sam rolls damage at difficulty 7 rather than 6. Sam gets two successes, but the armour value of Mail is 3, so the blade never hit skin, and the damage is bashing. Alex can soak with both armour and stamina.
    Next action
    Sam tries again. This time stabbing Alex. Mail isn't extra special against stabbing, so the difficulty is 6 rather than 7. Sam gets 5 successes on the damage roll, breaching the 3 armour. The damage is lethal, which Alex can't soak meaning only the armour can reduce the damage. Alex rolls Armour to soak


    Melee

    Fighting a longer weapon means -1 dice and -2 initiative. Fighting a much longer weapon means -2 dice and -4 initiative.
    Fighting in an enclosed space with a weapon that's too long can result in +1 or even +2 difficulty.
    Small weapons are +2 to parry with. Heavy weapons are +1.

    Characters can use a dodge at +2 difficulty to get past a reach weapon and have reach weapon rules turned around. The reach weapon user can dodge-backup for +1 to counter this.
    Precision hold. Character's grip is further up the weapon for stabbing weapons (Halfswording, spears). Meaning they loose all advantage of reach, but reduce the difficulty to hit precisely and gain +2 armour penetration.




    Range.
    Caryover is limited to the weapon's damage. a man with 7 successes on a light pistol (4) does a max of 8 dice of damage.
    Weapons have different recoil. One gun might be +0 burst fire and +2 full auto. Another might be +2 burst fire and +3 full auto.
    Shotguns (firing shells) are -1 difficulty to hit, but are +1 to do damage for any form of armour.
    Big guns, like high powered sniper /anti material rifles (or anything that'll do more than 8 damage a shot), are +1 difficulty (or more) to use.


    Or, well, this is at least my rough attempt. Part of me is wanting to simplify it a bit more, another part says it sounds more complex here than it would in a book because I'm rather bad with explaining.

      If you're going to overhaul WoD armors and weapons?

      Take a page from Exalted 3e and throw "realism" out the window as a concern. The WoD combat system doesn't work well for weapon-porn lists upon lists of weapons, mod kits, and so on to fine-tune every equipment choice. Have three basic kinds of each general class of weapon (hand-to-hand, thrown, archery, firearms), and for armors: a light version with the least potency but the least penalties, a balanced medium, and a heavy version.

      Then just write up 'tags' as needed to differentiate weapons and armors.

      It's faster, it's easier, you can make new weapon/armor stats with ease because you select one of three base stats and apply tags.

                  The Ex3 tag system isn't that different from what was proposed, but you can write it out with words (though they well translate into standard mechanical widgets).

                  So, you'd get things like this:

                  Brass Knuckles - Light Hand-to-Hand Weapon. Tags: Bashing, Brawl, Worn. Resources 1.
                  Knife - Light Hand-to-Hand Weapon. Tags: Lethal, Melee, optional Thrown (Short). Resources N/A.
                  One Handed Sword - Medium Hand-to-Hand Weapon. Tags: Lethal, Melee, Balanced. Resources 2.
                  Lance/Heavy Spear - Heavy Hand-to-Hand Weapon. Tags: Lethal, Melee, Piercing, Reaching, Two-Handed (if on foot). Resources 1.

                  Obviously the specific mechanics are a bit meaningless if you don't know Exalted, but I think it gets the idea across of how it works out and most of the tags are self-explanatory in intent.
