    I suppose i should specify a bit how i intended for Magnus to work in relation to stealth, or group combat. Like i mentioned, he's meant to act as an attention sink for the enemy, so if the party will want a more stealthy approach, using Magnus as a distraction is entirely within what i intended, and what he's good for. In combat specifically, he's meant to survive the enemy's attention long enough to get into close combat, at which point he'll shift from simply absorbing, to debilitating, mainly through grapples. Let me lay out what i got so far in terms of basic loadout, because i don't think saying Magnus can't protect the party is entirely fair. I'll go over this in quick, functional terms rather than the whole narrative and thematic for each rote.

    Equipment:
    Kevlar - Relatively easy to obtain, and there's no shame in armor when facing guns. I believe the standard is 3 soak against bullets?
    Ballistic shield - Also relatively easy to obtain or improvise. Extra soak would be nice, but ideally it will work like directional cover. This is meant to be used on approach and easily discarded upon entering melee, as to not hamper grappling.
    Smoke grenades (Optional) - not necessarily a commercial smoke grenade, something as simple as a homemade flour bomb could serve a similar purpose. Meant to briefly obscure vision while closing to melee.

    Magic:
    Biokinetic armor (Life 3) - Allows magnus to soak incoming lethal using his stamina pool.
    Stamina buff (Life 3) - Increases stamina, improves soak when combined with Biokinetic armor
    Kinetic Dispersion (Forces 2) - Disperses energy from hits across Magnus entire body. Ideally is meant to lower soak difficulty, though a flat damage reduction for a set number of hits could work too)
    Adrenaline burst - Allows Magnus to ignore wound penalties for the duration of the scene. Must test willpower for fatigue afterwards
    Synaptic overdrive - Provides a bonus to initiative. Must test willpower for fatigue afterwards.

    Now, a few of these will be vulgar depending on circumstance, and probably if used all together. Ideally, they'll be used selectively depending on the needs of the fight, or as pre-fight buffs if Magnus knows he's going into combat. I suspect i might want a ritual to combine all the effects into basically a "war mode" style buff. However, with just some of these on, Magnus should be able to withstand gunfire from multiple shooters for at least a couple of rounds, and still be healthy enough to make it into melee, unless he's quite unlucky.

    This IS all geared towards mundane-equivalent opponents with guns. Hopefully the ST isn't going to throw Fomori bruisers at us right off the bat. Plus once Magnus does get into melee range, his high strength should give him an advantage when grappling, which will give him options to disrupt enemies. In a party situation, that also means the other members have more time and freedom to do their actions while the enemy is shooting at Magnus. If you have any suggestions for refinements/additions, i am all ears.

    Now in a solo scenario, this is all going to depend a lot on the number of opponents. Mind you, Magnus wants challenge, not suicide. He's smart enough to run away if he's going up against 4 people with assault rifles on his own. That's not cowardice, it's not being a moron.

    On the "avoid combat" front though, i am considering just dropping the Brawl 5 idea entirely and moving some points into intimidate. Even if he's not malicious, an argument can be made that Magnus sheer physicality might dissuade people from wanting to tangle with him.

        ,Looking at your responses, some misunderstandings I got earlier became more clear.

        The main issue, and that's something very clear for those with experience on both games, is that you're looking at it with D&D lenses. You're fundamentally thinking through the "dungeon crawling mindset", where the story happens outside normal life and the PCs are explorers deliberately (even if unwillingly) exploring dangerous areas or situations that are not the "mundane reality" of normal people.

        WoD doesn't work like that, for several reasons. Typical chronicles are about dangers lurking within daily life, when characters are going through their routines or at least walking in perfectly normal, civilized areas. You're not ambushed exploring the woods, you're ambushed going to the grocery store. You live among normal people, doing normal things, and so do your enemies, and everything in this world that's dangerous and/or malicious is hiding in plain sight all the time. Trouble just doesn't happen in distinct, identifiable, manageable contexts.

        It isn't a game with clear challenges and victory conditions, either. It's not that you have other tools than combat to solve problems, it's that most problems aren't about combat at all, and even to solve them through violence requires some lateral thinking. How do you beat your way through an eviction notice? What do you do when the enemy's lair (and I mean it, it may well be a lair) is in a corporate building with top security and legal clout? What do you do when your enemy can be anyone in the streets? Or when they simply know where you live and your routine and can stage an attack for any point in the future? Those aren't just things that happen in WoD, they're the regular state of the game.

        Finally, dealing with consequences is half the fun. In WoD your actions happen almost all the time within the bounds of a society with roles and expectations. You have to worry about evidence, witnesses, the police, innocent bystanders, collateral damage, the law, what your neighbors will think, all the time, and the supernatural society isn't a substitute for this, it's a layer on top of that. The bulk of the game is about navigating this environment while doing supernatural shit, and dealing with the consequences of failing, by accident or choice.

        Our advice comes from this whole difference in game dynamics. In WoD your behavior outside combat matters a lot, and you can be a liability for others and yourself way before any brawl starts. There is no clear distinction between when we're in the adventure and when we're not, never. Blurring the lines is the very point, and you can't treat fight situations as fundamentally distinct from just living your life. The story almost always happen during your daily life.

        Now, there is some more pointy critique to be made, here:

        Originally posted by Ironguard View Post
        From my perspective, you're being adversarial. There's a difference between offering critique (and preferably workable alternatives) and saying "you should play the game the way i think is correct" which is what this feels like. There's a difference between flexible adjustment and radical alteration.
        Heavy Arms has a direct and sometimes rude way of saying things, but it has been far from that.

        Literally no one here tried to change your character, but to help you implement your concept while making something better adapted to the reality of the game we have actual experience with. And if many changes were pointed, it's because you have many options, not because anyone demanded you implement everything.

        But you have been consistently defensive and resistant to change, with few very minor exceptions that help, but do not really address the main problems. You don't engage almost anything we say for real, just justify why you think we're wrong and your character needs no change.

        The others explained well my points on wrestling and strength. You can do a lot of damage with your Magic all right, but that has nothing to do with your combat build. And not only does Magic carries it's own considerations, it isn't an exclusive thing. 99,99% of the population can't do nothing about it, sure, but 99,99% of the population won't be your real problem at any given time. And for that matter, even with Magic defensive effects scale far, far less than offensive ones. And your Strength score won't matter much for your Magic, nor will getting from 5 to 6 be a game-changing combat feat.

        Unarmed fighting has a well defined and much deserved place in military training and self defense, but dedicated unarmed combat isn't a combat strategy. It's an important foundation for armed combat, and an important skill for edge cases (which is what self-defense is about), but trying to solve actual problems by fighting unarmed is just not how it has ever worked. That's why while being an important foundation, it has always been depicted and recorded in every place, by every culture, as either training, sports, edge cases or feats for legendary heroes (who don't need to do logic). Glima is no exception to this.

        Grappling, for whatever it matters, work in self defense because in those situations you're frequently at close quarters with someone who's threatening to harm you, but are not actively doing so yet. Even then any self-defense instructor worth their salt will tell you that the most important things in self-defense are alertness and avoidance, and the best strategy in a confrontation with an armed assailant is to comply and de-escalate, because no matter how good you are, your chances aren't that great. And for every story of a fighter that got away from assault by beating their assailant, there are two or three of fighters who got seriously injured or killed trying.

        More important, though, is how the game systems work it, and WoD is simply a very lethal game where weapons make a lot of difference, as do any extraneous advantage. Numbers and initiative easily decide the outcome, any damage is a heavy hindrance, and no source of harm can be safely dismissed even by the most powerful characters. The idea is to use your resources to have the greatest advantage you can get and treat combat as a scary prospect that can easily go wrong if you don't take control of the situation.​

        Originally posted by Ironguard View Post
        This IS all geared towards mundane-equivalent opponents with guns. Hopefully the ST isn't going to throw Fomori bruisers at us right off the bat.
        This is totally a thing in WoD.

        As it isn't an exploration, enemies aren't silo'ed in predictable points, and as numbers and plans matter a lot and combat isn't a default solution, confronting the PCs with far superior foes isn't at all uncommon. Picking up a fight with something way above your weight category without noticing is a constant risk, and even those way below can cause a lot of trouble if they get advantages like surprise, numbers and/or good weapons (2 out of 3 is plenty here).

        Party situations are far less than a thing in WoD. Unless the group is ostensibly a paramilitary unit, they're unlikely to work as one in any regular basis. It's less that they won't want to help and more that they won't be good at that. Magnus is a fitting bruiser/tank (at least conceptually) for a combat party where everyone has a combat role, but that's rarely a thing in Mage. You're more likely to be paired with a lawyer, a street artist and an old recluse with Parkinson. Characters tailor made for combat either exist within a cabal tailor made for combat or, more commonly, have means to make combat go their way without the cabal, and the wits to actively avoid combat (frequently their main role in a group is to detect and avoid problems instead of fighting it).

        Unfortunately another trait of the system is that it's even more random than D&D when it comes to dice rolling. Harmless head-shots and lethal umbrella thrusts are things that happen with just enough frequency to be a known quantity. You shouldn't rely on average results.

        I did had a Magnus-style PC in my Mage game until recently (the player had to leave the game) as I said before, but this player talked to the group beforehand about the character and was totally aware that he would be a liability, it was the point, he wanted to be a comic relief that causes (minor and funny) problems with his trouble-seeking attitude instead of solving them with combat. It worked because everyone was on board, and the player knew his actual contributions would rarely be through combat (the other PCs were a financial advisor who was frequently throwing money at problems, a sword-aficionado youtuber that didn't expect to use the sword, either, and two retired elders, one of whom dotted on the boxer as a grandchild).

        Not saying that Magnus has to be a comic-relief or anything, but an example of how the game do not rely on some of his current assumptions.​

            Originally posted by Ironguard View Post
            Like i mentioned, he's meant to act as an attention sink for the enemy, so if the party will want a more stealthy approach, using Magnus as a distraction is entirely within what i intended, and what he's good for.
            How, exactly, is he good for this? He lacks the social stats to do anything more than put on the air of a menacing brute. As monteparnas has stated in detail, this is a game set in the modern world that at least on the surface works like it. If Magnus comes up to the front of a secured facility dressed for a fight? The guards are going to put the facility on lockdown (making his teammates' lives harder), leave him to wrestling with a steel security door with his bare hands, and take up firing positions that he can't just walk up to due to cover or height advantages. They're also just going to call the cops like normal sane people would (assuming dozens of people haven't already because a huge guy in body armor and carrying a ballistic shield is walking around with grenades hanging on his vest).

            What does that accomplish for the goal of stealth and whatever investigation/etc. the other PCs wanted to do? Is Magnus going to fight the cops too, or just get arrested and face years of prison time (I'm not aware of any country that's cool with people that build IEDs, even ones meant to be less-than-lethal, and then try to pick fights against normal citizens while carrying said devices; wearing body armor tends to make it worse for your legal troubles as well).

            In combat specifically, he's meant to survive the enemy's attention long enough to get into close combat, at which point he'll shift from simply absorbing, to debilitating, mainly through grapples.
            And he gets into close combat and then dies pretty much instantly anyway. Magnus is, as currently written, built for one-on-one fights. That's the only thing he can manage.

            If you're fighting multiple opponents in close-combat, you take +1 difficulty on your attack and defense rolls per opponent. So, two opponents? +2 difficulty on your attacks, including your grapples, because it's hard to keep your holds with someone else is actively attacking you to help their friend get free. Since you're grappling you can't do anything to stop opponents from circling around to get a flanking bonus, guns get a bonus in close combat, and attacking someone in a grapple also gets a bonus since they can't move around freely.... leaving all those unrestrained opponents with the freedom to take a high end targeted shot to avoid your armor, get +2 damage dice on top of all that, and still be rolling at minimum attack difficulty. Swapping Matter 1 for Mind 1 would help a lot here since you didn't put Matter into any of your proposed effects, and Mind 1 can among many things eliminate the penalties on your rolls for being outnumbered.

            Oh, also, the armor is a -1 dice penalty to all your Dex based rolls, and a ballistic shield is another -1 penalty there, so any Dex based rolls you don't have Ability dots in as well are autofails until you drop the shield, and that -2 is also going to suck for your Dex + Athletics rolls to get to any opponents that aren't standing in easy places for you to attack them. This is a huge problem since the best bonuses from a shield require an active Dex + Melee roll... and you have no Melee dots. Even if you swapped to Dex 3, you'd be rolling 1 die at +1 diff meaning that shield is not going to be all that useful against guns (though you could move at half speed and double the turns of them freely shooting at you if you'd like some Cover).

            When I said you have picked the least mechanically effective way of doing combat in this game, I was not being hyperbolic. You don't need to be worried about fomori. You need to be worried that your ST doesn't just fiat that Magnus takes a bullet to the head, goes into a coma, and then is in the hospital under police supervision so that the group can rescue him before he's removed from the game because he can't do anything from prison for a few years.

            Yes, even in combat, even with all that gear and magic, he's a liability, not a powerhouse. If he's lucky to keep normal human guards busy for 30 seconds before they incapacitate him and he'll maybe injure one of them, how is he supposed to protect other people?

            I'm also going to put out again that Magnus lacks key dots for what you're saying. Kevlar vest and a ballistic shield? Not hard to get but not cheap, especially for a character that can't buy stuff off the rack and needs custom built gear for his size. He's only got Resources 2. Modern ballistic counter measures are not designed to be repaired easily, they're designed to be replaced. It's not ~$750 US (not including shipping or tax) for a quality Kevlar vest w/trauma plates that's made to fit him, it's that price after every fight he has to soak lethal or agg damage because you can't fix Kevlar without magic. Once the weave is compromised, you need to replace it. A homemade shield is going to be the same thing, buying one is going to be much more expensive (though they're more durable).

            Also... he's got no Crafts, no Technology, and no Science... how is he making a shield or building smoke grenades? He doesn't have any Backgrounds related to people that could get him these things. Where are they coming from? His zero dots in Streetwise ability to find black market dealers and not get ripped off?

            Sure, for a mage that knows what they're doing this is trivial. Magnus doesn't know this stuff.

            Kinetic Dispersion (Forces 2) - Disperses energy from hits across Magnus entire body. Ideally is meant to lower soak difficulty, though a flat damage reduction for a set number of hits could work too)
            This is going to be a very ST dependent rote and I wouldn't rely on it being allowed. Messing with damage/soak difficulties via supernatural powers is a very rare effect.

            Additionally, Forces 2 in particularly tends to be a two-way street in its effects. If you bend light around yourself to turn invisible, you also can't see without magic to extend your senses beyond your stealth field. If you alter how a character interacts with kinetic energy it's going to matter both ways as well. Strikes against him would disperse across a wider surface area, but his own attacks would be equally weakened as he can't concentrate impacts on small areas of his body either. Not necessarily a bad thing if you want to grapple and immobilize without damage, but that's not really a winning tactic in a fight.

            Adrenaline burst - Allows Magnus to ignore wound penalties for the duration of the scene. Must test willpower for fatigue afterwards
            Synaptic overdrive - Provides a bonus to initiative. Must test willpower for fatigue afterwards.
            All the more reason to grab Mind 1 over trying to do this with Life 2 or 3.

            ----------

            More broadly on the magic side of things:

            It's important to remember that you take difficulty increases as you stack on more and more effects. Loading up on lots of prep effects makes casting on-the-fly very hard to do, and can even make the last few effects you try unreliable. Reliably having 3+ effects running is always going to be better as ritual magic cast in advance with extra successes put towards duration.

            Magnus has a lot of Willpower, but Willpower points recover relatively slowly. Even with that 9 rating, with this much magic you can't reliably spend WP on every roll, and then you're risking having zero to spend in the actual scene.

            Trying to combine things into a single big effect has a bunch of drawbacks (increased number of successes, rolling at the higher difficulty for some effects that could be easier if cast solo, still being a WP sink), and you have to explain it all in a fashion that matches your Focus to your ST's satisfaction. Once you're facing supernatural opponents this also gets into a severely disadvantageous position of losing all your buffs to a single counterspell.

            You haven't even included any offensive magic in this. Or social boosting magic that could keep enemies aggro on you instead of doing things like triggering a silent alarm.

            That's not cowardice, it's not being a moron.
            That is still up in the air for Magnus. The Code of Honor Merit is one you have to personalize and detail. The Merit is vague not because you just have to act exceptionally honorably by some unspecified standard, but because you have to specify a standard that is above and beyond the norms for both your Sleeper social norms and any norms of your magical associations. Don't expect a ST to let you have the Merit if Magnus doesn't have things he'll be willing to die for rather than compromise on.
