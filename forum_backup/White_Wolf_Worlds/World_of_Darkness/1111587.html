    Keeping the Game Lines Separate

    07-05-2017, 05:04 AM
    It seems like I am always hearing or reading about new and better ways to integrate the World of Darkness, to come up with better crossovers or combine the metaplot to make it all work together. Sometimes I think that I must be the only person out there who likes to keep my game lines entirely separate. When I play Vampire, there is no Technocracy controlling human society and making technology work, there is no Pentex controlling the corporate world and putting Wyrm poison into people's hamburgers. Hell, there may not even be mages or werewolves at all, or they might be much different from how they are depicted in their own game lines. When I play Werewolf, I don't want to deal with the possibility of there being an Abrahamic God who cursed vampires or the idea that Gaia and the Wyrm might just be caused by humans believing this or that.

    I think that the different game lines have not only their own unique flavors and play styles, they have their own settings and cosmologies that are mutually exclusive. Trying to combine them just undermines what make at least one (if not more) of those games special. While I've never been much of a fan of the Ravnos Clan, one of my least things in V:tM was the Week of Nightmares where the Technocracy killed Ravnos with giant space mirrors.

    Am I alone in this regard? Are there other people who feel the same way?

                  I'm actually the opposite and believe a living world needs to show the entire world. So while I would never allow the PCs to be crossover (no groups of werewolves with one changeling, or a group of vampires plus one mage, etc.), their setting contains everything. At the same time, I have a very edited version of canon so I don't include every ridiculous thing out there.

                  I do give a lot of thought about what kind of other game elements are around and could be possibly interacted with. Most of my Chronicles the PCs never encounter the easter eggs I put there, and I never have a major plot element or story where those other game elements are driving things.

                  The main reason is I want to have a sense of "there are dragons out there" in the PCs minds so they realize some sections of the map are out of their control, or no-go zones, or simply have some things be very mysterious. I like a sandbox style game, and part of the sandbox needs to be these other areas. Especially if one or more PC is in a very occult oriented character, I want them to realize that there are many other occult forces out there as well.

                  When I run a game, I try to include the following:

                  A general sandbox that the PCs can run around and explore
                  Elements specific to each PC because of their backgrounds and interests, catered to them
                  One general chronicle wide mystery which they may or may not get involved

                  I think this layered approach keeps the game fun and fresh giving the illusion of a real world.

                          I think maybe I have a middle ground approach. When I run a game, I generally assume that some elements of the other games are still around (there's probably a Camarilla for example, the Technocracy is fartzing around, Garou are protecting Caerns/Nodes and so on), but I don't go into very much detail unless my players specifically want to deal with other supernatural types.

                          I prefer to keep the focus, player characters, and cosmology specific to the game I'm running (no Triat in Vampire, for example), but I like having the hint of a wider universe. If nothing else, it can make for a fun change of pace. If the mages are getting tired of exploring the Umbra, they can end up in a political conflict with a Vampire for a bit of novelty.
