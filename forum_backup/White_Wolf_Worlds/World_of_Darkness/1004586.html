                  Well, as a developer with some past in the video game industry, I thinkt it would be hard to have a good video game for WoD.
                  When I played bloodlines (very recently), what shocked me the most was the simplification/rework of disciplines.
                  Presence was a debuff around you, Auspex was a buff on your character and most disciplines were reworked to have the same functionality as celerity/potence/fortitude, you had an effect for each disciplines and each level increased this effect.

                  At first, I was somehow sad/disappointed, but as I played, I understood that those simplification were a necessity. Disciplines, like Obtenebration/Necromancy/Thaumaturgy, are far too complex to be implemented in a video game.
                  You have far too many different effect with even more in-game application.
                  Domination alone have so many application it's very hard to implement in a game. And it's a discipline that I consider "simple".
                  In fact, when you have a very complex disciplines, it may seems like nothing but it's a lot of work (and I really mean it) just to make it simpler and playable.
                  You need to keep the essence of the original discipline while removing most complexities/effect.

                  Let’s take the example of Auspex in bloodlines. This discipline allowed the player to see NPC's aura and boosted your Wits/Perception.
                  The boost to Wits/Perception was a buff to your character that simulated Heightened Senses while improving your firearm ability in combat.
                  But, being able to see NPC's aura was useless, completely. There was no NPC hiding his nature or something similar that would give Aura Perception some usefulness.
                  So, instead, they've added an effect to the discipline, you can see aura through walls and each level of Auspex improve your vision's distance.
                  When you look at it, this difference was weird, Auspex never allowed you to see aura's through walls so why did they add this?
                  Because it allowed you to see that someone was in a room, which was similar to hearing someone movement/heartbeat in the tabletop version.
                  It also added usefulness to Auspex during infiltration by allowing you to see NPCs patrolling.

                  This simplification of disciplines was necessary, but with it, the game became (in my opinion) somehow tasteless.
                  Disciplines were a little too similar to one another and the roleplaying part was kind of lost. Having a Brujah, a Toreador or a Ventrue would have little to no difference.

                  On the other hand, I loved the first NPC Malkavian you met in the game, the quest around her and her clan weakness was awesome.
                  More generally, the world, the atmosphere you had in it, the NPC's, it was great, very immersive, very dark. Just how I pictures World of Darkness.

                  So having a video-game regrouping World of Darkness would be a tremendous amount of work just to rework (most) abilities for every "race".
                  I would really love to see one but for this to happen, a very serious rework of discipline is required.

                  I think that the easiest way would be to have more an adventure game instead of an RPG.
                  By losing some part of the character customization, specifically the clan choice, you would be in total control of what powers the player have and you would be able to design those powers more deeply.
                  Even if I love the character customization part, I think that WoD is more about horror and story than your clan or some skill, and, focusing on it would be better than loosing resources to adapt a tabletop system into a video-game.

                      Now if you think Disciplines from Vampire were simplified, imagine what would need to happen to Mage's Spheres. Even if we assume loads of functions would be flat-out ignored because they aren't strictly necessary (Wonder/Fetish crafting, Dream walking, time travel, layering different locations on top of each other, etc), the Spheres capabilities would need to be curtailed because some abilities are too difficult to program for as open-ended as they are.

                      That said, this isn't quite as difficult as one might imagine, for one very good reason: Mage Paradigms. Simply make every playable Mage character believe in limitations to their Spheres (or simply not have access to knowledge that would let them push the boundaries) that work within the game's mechanical limitations. Time 2 doesn't give you open-ended understanding of the past or future, but it could be used to give the player flashbacks at certain locations, or for combat increasing their dodging ability (they can see the attacks coming). Forces 5 won't summon a hurricane, but it can bring lightning down on an enemy's head. Etc, etc.

                                                          My bad, I used a shortcut. Of course you can implement on specific discipline into the game, what I wanted to say is that these disciplines require a lot of work both in term of development and gameplay.
                                                          If you have the following system: One Disciple Dot - One Spell as you describe above, you'll quickly have too many spell.
                                                          You'll have to use a spell bar (like in Dragon Age or World of Warcraft), have something like a spell book and allow the user to bind/unbind his shortcut.
                                                          This system, while perfectly viable for PC is hard to use on console because you have less shortcut available.
                                                          You could decide to limit the size of the spell bar to 6, so PC and console player have the same number of spell and the portability for a system to another is easier but somehow frustrating because a player will never be able to use all his abilities.
                                                          Or you could decide to create a PC-only game, but you're also losing an important part of the market.

                                                          Moreover, when I said that Obtenebration was too complex, this is what I had in mind:

                                                          The second ability, Shroud of Night create a gaseous darkness around the player (or its target if you prefer).
                                                          In a game, objects are usually linked to a mesh, a 3D model with triangles.
                                                          When you want to add an "effect", like fire or smoke, you create a particle system.
                                                          Particle systems are quite complex so I'll not explain it here, but what you need to keep in mind is that it creates multiple small particles that moves together.
                                                          Now, with Shroud of Night you allow the user to create a particle system almost anywhere.
                                                          The particles should behave somehow like gas and be "stopped" by a wall for example.
                                                          I'm sure you've already seen some buggy game physics with 3D-objects, well with particle it's far worse.
                                                          Handling collision with particles is horrible, it put a huge stress on the GPU and can require days of optimization.
                                                          And at the end what do you have? Well only the second ability of Obtenebration...

                                                          Additionally, you have to work on a shader for this "gaseous darkness" and shaders are also horribly complex.
                                                          For example, the most famous shader for its complexity is the water shader.
                                                          One could assume that, nowadays with the huge amount of game released, having a nice and smooth water effect would be pretty easy, but it's not the case.
                                                          Here's an article explaining why and here is a tutorial to create a water shader (if you're curious).
                                                          Moreover, shaders have a huge impact on the cost of the video game. They require highly skilled and experienced developer and, as you can imagine, they're not cheap.

                                                          So, when creating your video game, you need to ask yourself where do you want to put you money?
                                                          Do you develop a very complex discipline like Obtenebration that'll be used only when the player chooses Lasombra as a clan or do you invest money into improving the rest of the game and remove the Lasombra clan from the game?
                                                          If you want an example, The Witcher 3 had a development cost estimated at 15 million US dollars, and CD projekt used their own technology for the game engine, reducing the overall cost.
                                                          That's already quite a expensive, and you still need to add 25 million for the marketing and distribution of the game.
