            Oh, certainly; I’m not denying that the Syndicate has a dark side. But the question posed wasn’t about how evil the Syndicate can get; it was about what redeeming qualities it has. And you don’t look to the Syndicate’s Gordon Gekkos to find its redeeming qualities any more than you’d look to the original Terminator to find Iteration X’s redeeming qualities.

            You want to play the Syndicate as villains? Play them with a zero-sum mentality where everyone they help is offset by someone who is hurt, and where the winners are picked by who is the most devious and ruthless. You want to play them as good guys? Focus on “everyone gets something out of it” deals, and play up the winners and losers being determined by who is or isn’t willing to put in the effort.

            Primal Ventures are a key point here: invest in a venture that embodies the spirit of the entrepeneur (e.g., innovative and industrious), and you get a dividend in Quintessence — above and beyond whatever mundane benefits the venture produces on its own. And if those mundane benefits include a monetary profit, well, that’s something you can use to invest in other Primal Ventures. Which means more cash, more Quintessence, and more successful Primal Ventures. That’s such a massive win/win that it would take some serious mismanagement for the Syndicate not to be rolling in resources, both mundane and magical.

                        The Syndicate is fighting for a world where everyone can have what they want, even the enemy. You just have to pay for it.

                        To the rest of the Technocracy, the Traditions are "superstitionists" -- ignorant fanatics pushing false, self-destructive paradigms and toxic ideologies on innocent sleepers. They're bigoted reactionaries standing in the way of Progress and Enlightenment. They not only want to sabotage humanity's destiny, they want to humanity to turn its back on the notion of Progress entirely. There can be no compromise with such deluded zealots; for the sake of humanity, they and their twisted paradigms must be stamped out. They are the enemy.

                        The Syndicate, on the other hand, sees the Traditions as untapped markets.

                        The Syndicate doesn't want you to abandon your existing value system. They want to sell you the things they value. You need ancient grimoires, exotic reagents, sacred relics? Just contact your nearest Syndicate representative. Want to get away from the materialistic corruption of the modern world? The Syndicate can hook you up with meditation retreats, pilgrimage tours, and gated communities where you'll never have to so much as look at anyone who doesn't share your paradigm. Want to convert the sleepers to the One True Way? The Syndicate will set you up with a lucrative publishing contract, a nationwide speaking tour, and your own talk show. These are the guys who invented the Burqini; they can monetize anything.

                        The Syndicate's ideal world isn't a grey, homogeneous dictatorship where all opposing belief systems are prohibited on pain of re-education. It's a rich multicultural tapestry where people of all creeds are free to communicate, socialize, do business, and pursue their own goals in life, without being judged or persecuted. The Syndicate don't want to rule the world. In fact, they're the only faction in Mage that doesn't want to rule the world; the only Convention or Tradition that isn't dominated by ideologues who view even their supposed comrades as "useful idiots." The Syndicate believes that all paradigms and belief systems have value, and they're eager to leverage that value for everyone's mutual benefit.

                        The Syndicate genuinely wants everybody to be friends. They honestly don't understand why everyone else seems to find the idea so horrifying.

                                DSPaul has the basics of it right: the Syndicate prefers cordial relationships to hostilities, as the latter isn’t conducive to good business. They prefer being the suppliers of everyone else’s demands, and aren’t particularly judgmental about the demands that they meet (except to the extent that the nature of the demand influences the price they charge). In many ways, they’re the opposite of the rest of the Technocracy: their dark side tends to take the form of enabling morally dubious behavior and complacency rather than demanding conformity and service. To play them as good guys, you need only pair their “providing goods and services” mentality with a moral compass.

On one hand it could easily be NWO or a Time-Motion manager, but the paradigm was extremely syndicate.

                                                    Oh, it is most assuredly explored! The Syndicate gets a customized variant of Prime called Primal Utility that lets them extract Quintessence from sufficiently innovative and industrious ventures by investing in them. The Syndicate is all about finding people with new ideas and the drive to do something with them and empowering them to do so, which danparadox illustrated nicely in a non-financial way (the ventures they support don’t have to be commercial).

                                                    They’re also about reaping benefits from providing said assistance; they’re not altruists so much as they’re symbiotes — and, on their bad days, parasites (if they take too much Quintessence from a Primal Venture, they cause it to suffer setbacks). Their failings come in two forms: the more obvious is the temptation to indulge their own wants to the exclusion of others (the so-called one-percenters); the more subtle but equally troubling one is the temptation to cater to peoples’ wants without regard for whether it’s healthy to do so.
