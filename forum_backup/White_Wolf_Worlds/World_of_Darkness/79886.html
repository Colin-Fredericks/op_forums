              (For context, I'm female)

              I've done it a number of times. I default to playing female characters, but have no qualms about playing men if there is a reason for me to do so.

              Most of my experiencing playing male characters comes from showing up to convention-style games where the GM has a pile of pre-generated characters to choose from. Sometime the characters are all male, so there's no choice (I have some issues with this, but that's a whole other thread). Other times, the character that sounds most fun to me just happens to male. In either case, I'm roleplaying. My character isn't me, and part of the fun in roleplay is being a different person in a different time and place.

              That said, gender identity is something very deeply entwined within most of us, so I understand when some of my peers are uncomfortable or unwilling to play outside their own gender. Pushing boundaries can be fun in a game, but no one should have to go beyond what's enjoyable for them.

              I've heard people complain about negative experiences with cross-gender play. Some are "I tried it and didn't like it", which is fine. Most, however, follow the pattern of a man who plays a female character just so he can describe her physical attractiveness and narrate her doing things that are sexually suggestive or outright sexual. This might also happens with female players and male characters (or same-sex pairings, but this is the thread about cross-gendered play), but I haven't been privy to the sob stories. Unless the roleplay group has designated itself as "The Sexual Fantasy Description Club" this sort of thing is thing degrading, and disrespectful to everyone involved. It's not a problem with playing cross-gender. It's problem with players acting in grossly inappropriate ways.


              Only once have I deliberately created a male character. It was for a Dark Ages: Vampire campaign, so we were using a historical setting in which gender roles were a big deal. The ST pointed out that this matters far less, if at all, amongst Cainites. That's true, and especially so when the ST declares it, but it still mattered a hell of a lot amongst the regular humans of the day. That means a character's gender is going to be very defining for the kinds of backgrounds they might have prior to Embrace, and how they can interact with mortals. My character concept worked better as a man, so that's what I played.

              It was a good experience overall, and I really liked the character. The only annoyance was pronoun use that the table. Sometimes my character, Severyn, would called "she". I don't think he would've liked that. It's not a big deal, though, and I really can't fault my friends for it. We're very used to using pronouns that match the gender we physically see on the person. Also, during a tabletop game you sometimes refer to the character, and sometimes to the player. Severyn will talk to the constable, but Jessa must make the roll to see how convincing Severyn's lie is. As long as people get the pronouns right most of the time while speaking in-character it's easy to overlook the rest of the confusion.

                      I don't personally do it a lot, however I consider that my limitation. When I've tried to play female characters before, they don't usually feel quite right, like I'm not quite doing them justice. It's very difficult not to fall into stereotypes. I now only play female characters if the story I want to tell absolutely cannot be told any other way, and then I'm very careful and watch myself constantly.

                      I had one ST who ran our games for a few years who had seen so many bad examples of this that he outright banned it. His players were forbidden from playing different than their own gender and sexual orientation because it is difficult to maintain role-play for the rest of the group when one person can't understand their own character. I see his point, but would not go that extreme personally.

                      However, I've seen people pull it off flawlessly. My suggestion as an ST is to keep an eye on it, particularly with role-players you don't know, and be prepared to tell them they need to make a new character if it gets bad, insulting, or disruptive, but if they are pulling it off, then there is no problem.

                                                  For context, I'm a 30-year-old, cis, hetero male.

                                                  I'm currently a player in a W:tA game in which I play a 17-year-old female homid Black Fury Ahroun. It's definitely challenging, but I did it because a) it's something very different; b) it's challenging; c) Critical race and gender theory fascinate me, and I thought it would be interesting to explore many of these concepts anew by trying to put myself in a Black Fury head space. My storyteller is not sexist, but there's definitely a great deal of in-game sexism thrown at my character by NPCs and the like -- and I actually appreciate that. Part of playing a woman, I would imagine, is having to deal with the oppressive structures that come part and parcel with a heavily patriarchal society. If every NPC I met treated me with the same deference and respect as they would a man, I think it would serve as a disservice to the character and story, and further, be an act of sexism of itself (because we, as players, would be asked to believe that sexism isn't a thing in this World of Darkness, despite the fact that it is very much a thing in the real world, and that the World of Darkness is supposed to be a grimier, darker, generally more awful place to exist within).

                                                  That said, my character is vain, and has a compulsion that forces her to look at herself, if only briefly, in every mirror or highly polished surface she encounters (she tries to hide this), and is constantly primping and trying to move this lock of hair just so, or adjuster her outfit, or what have you. In truth, I took this from my own personality (I don't look at myself in the mirror compulsively exactly, but I'm definitely overly concerned with my appearance). I recognized that this was a sort of a dangerous turn to take, as women (especially teenaged women, which is what my character is) are often accused of being overly vain, shallow, and obsessed with looks. That said, Rather than construct some uber-lady who utterly defied every stereotype, I acknowledged that this is a flaw that any human being can have, and gave it to her -- thereby also providing some excellent roleplaying opportunities when someone comments on how the ahroun might be too concerned with how she looks to really get into the fight and do the dirty, awful work required of that auspice.

                                                  The one cheap avenue I took (in my opinion) is that she has a hatred of "slavers", i.e., people who enslave others (per the U.N.'s definition of the same). I say it's cheap because it's almost as predictable as a female character having a hatred of rapists. That said, when another player pointed out that it was cheap, not because it was obvious, but because "where are there slavers today?", I pointed out that they're everywhere, and was quickly able to substantiate that claim by finding some statistics on modern human trafficking -- which are staggering and sad. That said, as I see it, I had a friend and fellow player who was not entirely aware of how large a problem this is, and really only found out when he did because I had to be like, "no, dude, it's a serious problem, even and especially in this country", when discussing this character.

                                                          I have no problem playing female characters -- I've played them for 25+ years now, with nary an issue with other players (only once, with one gaming group I played with for a year, was there an "issue", which only involved some of the players referring to my character by my gender pronouns -- "he", "his" -- they just kept slipping-up perception-wise because their seeing me overrode their sense of my character's gender; I was playing a more subdued, laid-back woman, so the gender distinction wasn't prominent). In fact, I'd say my roster of characters is pretty much 50-50 split between male/female (with a few characters being genderless, due to their innate natures/origins, thrown in). Currently, I am playing two games, once a month for the past two years, with the "A" game being in the morning/most of the afternoon (MSH, where I play a woman; in fact, she's the same heroine I played in the same friend's game from over 20 years ago), and the "B" game afterwards (WoD), where I play a male. (Interesting gender-trait swap: Americanna is a Captain America/Wonder Woman pastiche who is physically powerful and the field leader of her team, while Andreas is a mage who, while physically in shape/above average capability, has no general combat abilities and isn't aggressive or good in close-combat situations. It's interesting playing a female in a "traditionally male" role then afterwards playing a male who isn't the stereotypical combat-monster that can creep up in WoD -- or any game setting, really -- but is more reserved/non-aggressive; it's also fun because the two characters share a few emotional/perceptual traits in common -- though they are simultaneously very different in many other ways -- and it's nice to see those similarities played in very different, albeit subtly so, ways...). I like playing a diverse range of personalities/characters, and gender (for me, at least) is just one more facet of creating/playing a given character. Back in the day, it helped out in some of my earlier groups that I had no issue playing a female because it lent itself to a more credible mix of characters, particularly because I play all of my characters "legit": when I play a female, I don't play a caricature of one (except sometimes in humor games, like Teenagers or Paranoia, where such is par-for-the-setting), but a woman with real, non-exaggerated feminine traits (I've been complimented by a number of players/GMs over the past 3 decades that I do female characters very well, the way a female player would likely do).

                                                          It boils down, ultimately, to how comfortable a player is with playing an opposite-gender character and how accepting/mature the group he plays with is. Per my above early-on gaming: when one has a "sausage-fest" of players (as was very often the case back in the 80's/much of the 90's), it is good to have a guy or two who will play a female character "fo' realz" so as to add a more credible dimensionality to the group dynamics; on the flip, it's cool to have a female player play a male character, as it broadens the range of what she might want to portray/act-out, legitimately, in many settings, w/o having to artificially make "Politically Correct" a given situation (she can play the captain of a WWII military unit, or the baron of a medieval land, sans the post-modern conceit of her *having* to play a woman who "defies all convention" for the time period in question).

                                                          That's how I see it, in any case...
