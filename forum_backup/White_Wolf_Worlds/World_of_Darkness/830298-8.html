                            Originally posted by Asmodai View Post

                            Which is about the same attitude presented by Mage partisans who insist on the Consensus, Vampire partisans insisting on God and Caine, Demon partisans insisting on everyone being clueless but them and so on. All of them are wrong because they should learn to respect the other games. Just like people should learn to enjoy the games thesmelves on their own merits. What's your point?

                            As far as core values of Werewolf... I find the whole sentiment rather off kilter, since the enemy is not Captain Planet and the Triat is anything but simple and straighforward. The one thing that the game does is give you a just cause to fight for, which I would argue Mage does as well not making the game stand out. The message of the game is not even about environment per se as it's a critique of the human condition, and how humans being assholes to each other feed other things that make the world worse and reinforce all the evils that we do. The only valid response to such a crapsack world are monsters willing to push them back. Yet the monsters themselves have a very hard time not being human. So, no I do not see anything simple, straightforward or black and white. You are a holy warrior with a cause, but that does not mean that the world you live in supports your narrative or that you can actually live with yourself (considering what rage does to you, you're shit out of luck finding companions that are not utter bastions of patience).

                            Add to this the rich culture, history, religion and of the Garou, the motley crew and additional complications of the Fera, the clever interplay of mythology and the setting... and you actually have a pretty nice game that stands well on its own two feet. So yeah, I don't see where anyone can get twocolor simplicity or captain planet.

                            Captain Planet is about a guy who cannot punch a person and hippie 90s kids fighting ridiculous villians who seeek profit in random acts of polution.
                            Werewolf is about religious terrorists murdering humans and the monsters hiding among them in their holy war to save humanity from themselves.




                            Considering how much postmodernist solipstistic philosophical manifestos have with witchcraft and religon, or how much Vampire The Masquerade has with actual mythological vampires, I have very little issues with Werewolves being defined by being shapeshifters and then spinning off into something else. I've heard a lot of complaints about this, but I really don't see why it's so hard to take Werewolf at its own merits. Mind you, it might not be your cup of tea, but stop slagging it off and let people have their own fun*

                            * Not you personaly, but this is a statement meant for everyone who just shits all over Werewolf without even bothering to actually figure out what it's about.
                            My point is more that people who see WtA that way and DON'T shit on it, but take it at that level and enjoy the hell out of it or decide it isn't for them, are not being the kind of assholes you are railing against. It's specious to say that WtA isn't all those things, because it is. AND it is something more as well, if you dig in deeper. What you've described and several other things as well (fetish hunting can definitely give a D&D dungeon spelunker vibe). Hell, my favorite all-time WtA experience was a semi-LARP real-time political in-fighting thriller at Gen Con. And the politics was was as deep and nasty as I've seen in VtM (which is suppose to be "that" game.... or was. Not sure under the new vision. Politics would only seem relevant in how oppressive it was and how it increased the suicidal inner urges of young Kindred in making their lives even darker and more bleak and hopeless. When we had finished up our last game, I put a WtA mini-chronicle on the table as one of the options for our next foray, after having included significant Werewolf elements in last mini-chronicle.

                            But I DO understand some of the integral reasoning. At the first look, making Werewolves infection driven 1) makes it seems like a Vampire clone when VtM was the only other kid on the block & 2) it causes a whole host of problems of it's own (the x-risk factor is hard to ignore.... werewolves aren't limited by trying to keep a human herd around as blood sheep, what limits would there be on their spread.) They needed to make Werewolf DIFFERENT and the eco-terrorist thing was definitely a way to go (perhaps even an inspired one). It made sense to picking option B (hereditary werewolves) out instead of A (infection-driver), C (both) or D (other ways from mythology, like skin-changers, cursed by God, etc.). I WILL question the primacy of the Umbra and spirits. That is just an odd add on. It's like someone LOVED the Tremere and just wanted every werewolf to be dripping with magic. But, whatever. It was a weird choice, but, apparently, it WORKED for the values of worked that they wanted.

                            Onto a matter of relevance to the OP of the thread. Your statement about the various partisanship cabals is well-taken. However, what happens to the games involved if those concerns are all resolved by Paradox? How would people feel about playing a Vampire game where the whole Caine thing is actually part of the tale of the Triat? Or Werewlf players about finding out that the Triat was actually a misunderstood tidbit from the Caine mythology? Many Mage players complain that they feel the Triat is forced on Mage's core mythology like a straight-jacket two sizes too small, so how would they feel when that became canon? Or, vice versa, if everyone else found out that their mythologies were just some kind of Reality Zone under the aegis of Mage's Tellurian? Because those are genuine concerns with a stated goal of ONE World of Darkness. It seems like a "Grand Reconciliation" would be in order, and I'm not sure who is going to be happy with that.... (Except for the partisans of the line that gets "mythological precedence", which sounds like it's probably going to be Vampire)

                                        I was kind of joking about Teenwolf and Twilight being the same as W:tA werewolves. Though I think that the similarity exists for a reason. Hereditary controlled shape shifting works well when you want a community of werewolf protagonists in who have their own culture, politics, ideology, and so on. That is the main selling point for all WoD games, that show natural creatures aren't just a set of powers or weaknesses, but they are a culture as well.

                                        I don't think you can do that if all you have are Laun Chaney said werewolves and o got randomly infected and have to shape change during the full moon and only during the full moon. That may make for an interesting movie or maybe even a single character in an RPG, but not an entire role playing games group and especially not a society of supernatural creatures. That is why hereditary voluntary shape changers works for W:tA and for some other works of fiction too.

                                        So, I don't know if I understand the criticism of W:tA that says that it can't replicate werewolf myths. Modern pop-culture werewolves (Old Hollywood horror) don't fit into RPG's much less the cultures that WW creates. Go back before the modern era and werewolf myths are so varied (and in most cases obscure) that trying to emulate them seems a bit pointless.
