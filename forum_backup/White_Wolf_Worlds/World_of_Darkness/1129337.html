    Chimerical Items-Help me out?

    08-15-2017, 02:54 PM
    So one of my players decided to buy Chimera(Item) 5 in character creation. The item in question is supposed to be a dueling saber thats origins are of arcadia, inherited from family. They've asked me what does it do? I bluffed a dirty write up of abilities, like reduce difficulty against formal duels vs. nobles and bonuses to fighting larger chimera.

    I've never really had a player do this, its usually chimera companion 5 in most situations.

    Long story short can someone explain how exactly Chimera Item 5 works?

      Shouldn't this be a Treasure? The second edition corebook, at page 288, gives you a table with Chimerical item values. Perhaps this item could have a 5-dot Art that it can do (though it might still need to require glamour depending on the Art, and maybe an activation roll too). Perhaps the player has a Dueling Saber, which is most likely a sword (only at two dots), along with another item or two (a set of light armor, or mabye Sidhe Plate), as well as the Saber having a 5-dot Art to it as well.

      You could also enhance the stats on the chimerical sword and give it a 2 or 3-dot Art as well, etc.

      Anyhow, I would read that Table on page 288. At the end of the day, this might be best as a 5-dot Treasure. But those Treasures tend to be unique or legendary. I think it's pretty risky for a starting character (e.g., around 15-25 freebie points given in character creation along with other default starting points) to have a 5-dot Treasure. I'm not sure the player could keep it. NPCs could be very interested in taking it, even other well-meaning NPCs who are part of the character's family (they believe they can protect it better, it is actually their birthright, etc.).

          A Treasure is a mortal item embued with Glamour. A Chimerical Item is a crafted or forged chimerical item.

          In Changeling 2nd Edition, page 288 there is a list of the kind of chimerical items that can be bought with Chimerical Item 5. The only rank 5 item is Magic Item (Art 5). So the first thing I would do is look at the various Art 5 descriptions and see what best fits a dueling saber.

          Chicanery 5 Captive Heart - change a person's behaviour
          Legderdemain 5 Phantom Shadows - create sentient illusions
          Primal 5 Elder Form - shapechange person
          Soothsay 5 Fate Fire - manifest a person's fate
          Sovereign 5 Geas/Ban - bestow a geas or ban on a creature
          Wayfare 5 Flicker Flash - travel anywhere

          After looking at the above, I would probably pick either the Sovereign 5 Art or Wayfare 5. Sovereign 5 would allow to to impose a ban or geas, which I see the possessor of the sword doing. It would either be like giving a quest on a newly anointed knight, or used to put a ban on a defeated opponent. Wayfare 5 would allow the person to travel anywhere, so if he ever had an opponent to duel, the sword would always put them in contact.

          Give it a good name and flavorful history.

              First, start with asking the player what he wants! If he just wants a chimerical sword then follow the guidelines in the book as Imperator and Black Fox mentioned. If the player merely wants a cool sword you don't have to give it actual powers and the background of being a family item from Arcadia can just be part of the story without any actual point values. Is there a reason why he wanted to spend that many points on the sword? Did he understand that he didn't have to if he just wanted a sword?

              If he does want a sword with cool powers, I would follow the guidelines for the Treasure background, as Black Fox said. If you have the C20 PDF that was sent out to backers you will have access to some different Arts. Personally I also allow a mixture of abilities up to the point value. So for a Level 5 weapon you could pick a level 2 power and a level 3 power, broadening what the sword can do. And you aren't limited to just the powers listed in the Arts. These are just guidelines and you should be encouraged to come up with one of your own. For example, level 5 Pyretics allows you to turn into living flame. But maybe you want to be able to turn into living water! Or living ice! Those could give equivalent "level" powers but without having to be locked into a particular Art.

                  Actually, not all Treasures have a material, mundane counterpart and not all Chimerical Items lack one. C2e was always muddled on the point.

                  In C20 things have been clarified, so you have to decide whether this really is just a chimerical sword (in which case, it probably won't be more than a two-dot Background--but I've always ruled that chimerical melee weapons do Strength + Rating damage), or whether it's a Treasure (in which case, give it dots in one or more Arts that are equal to its total rating).

                  On p.317, it's stated that chimerical weapons can have a mundane counterpart. Earlier, the book explains chimerae of all kinds can have a mundane analogue (chimerical castles may appear as houses in the real world, for example). So decide this with the player. My guess is, if it's from Arcadia, it probably has no material analogue.

                  On p.323, it's confirmed that a Treasure can also be entirely chimerical or can have both a chimerical and a mundane aspect. So, in that sense, Treasures and chimerical items are the same. But if you do want it to do magical stuff, it's going to be a Treasure.

                  You can read more on Treasures and their Art levels on p.324. A chimerical sword is just a sword made of dreamstuff. It has no special properties beyond perhaps looking cool and inflicting only chimerical damage. A Treasure sword, like Caliburn, might look the same but it also has Art-like powers, and requires the investment of rare or magical elements from the Dreaming during its creation. You still use the normal crafting system, however, but with the added quests and narrative elements to obtain the rare materials (time and risk are the main requirements).

                  In this instance, if it's a Treasure, I'd ask the player to come up with a story of how the Treasure was won or crafted. I agree that it would probably be sought after by other parties if it was more than a three-dot Treasure. If it's just a chimerical sword, it could've been bought at any old goblin market.
