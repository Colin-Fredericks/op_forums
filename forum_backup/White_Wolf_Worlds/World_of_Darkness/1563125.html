    Giving permanence to ephemeral objects

    08-15-2026, 11:55 AM
    As per topic.

    I'm looking for a mechanism or Rote to transport ephemeral items from an umbral realm into the Material and make them stick. If there is one RAW I haven't found it, and would be grateful for a pointer to where it may hide.

    AFAIK anything ephemeral taken from a realm into the Material will only last a short while and then start to fade. Technically all ephemera are Spirits, but I'm not looking for a way to make animate Spirits permanent residents of the Material - actually, I think that should be a separate process and require a lot more oomph.

    I'm also not overly concerned with the transfer of the item for now, as I think it makes sense to look at that as a separate process, too.

    I have a few possible ideas:

    1. Spell: I would clock this as a Spirit 2 + Prime 2 effect.
    This would be imo ok for minor items. Get a bottle of liquid Pathos from the Shadowlands, or a minor Device from the Cyber Realm. Difficult to say where to draw the line - would more exotic properties be more vulgar? Would the transfer rob the item of its properties because it's now in a different reality context?

    2. Consecration: Just as you can make material items persist in the umbra, couldn't you bind ephemeral items to make them stick? Or is that too cheap?

    3. Wonders/Trinkets: You could count transferred items as Wonders, and use those rules (sans crafting process). Ups the difficulty of the process considerably, but could preserve more outlandish properties.

    Any help or thoughts on this are welcome. There's a difficulty in balancing the effect, as well as complications and exploits depending on the nature (and source) of the item. Dreaming up Excalibur and taking it out from there should be theoretically possible, but locked behind reasonable difficulty.

      Spirit directly states what you need to "imbue objects with Ephemeral power" (Spirit 2 + Matter 2), transmute matter into Ephemera (Spirit 3), craft objects out of Ephemera (Spirit 3 + Matter 3 + Prime 2), Bind spirits into Fetishes (Spirit 4), rend the Gauntlet (Spirit 4) and attain complete control (Spirit 5). So we have options.

      But first I'll be blunt: to make an Ephemeral object stay indefinitely in the Material is Spirit 5. No other Sphere and no way around it. Any other Magic to do it will be subject to duration as per the Sphere, it's also very clear about it.

      Adding stuff can make your creation different in many ways, but won't make it Lasting.

      That aside, first study the object you want to bring. Not every ephemeral object is the same within the Material, some items will remain just from being brought, whatever the means.

      If you want the properties of an item, but not the specific item, Spirit 2 + Matter 2 should be enough at least for "physical properties". Take the ephemeral object, a mundane replica, and transfer the properties from one to the other. I don't think it will carry Charms or some other powers, but whatever is "natural" to the item in its home realm should be transferred.

      Prime will help with a few odd cases, but don't rely on it too much. Mostly it will help when the item violates conservation laws too blatantly, like a gun with infinite ammo. But Blessing or Perfecting are things Prime does just for being Prime and won't work differently on Ephemera. The "magical" properties of such items are something else.

      Transmute Ephemera into matter or prolonging its stay upon arriving in the Material should be Spirit 3, maybe with Matter 3. It's the same as crafting an object out of Ephemera, but you're getting one that already exists. This is also just the reverse of Stepping Sideways, so shouldn't be harder. That would be your basic answer, and I'd say Prime 2 here is kind of Sphere Bloat more than anything.

      What I'd say Matter and Prime add to Spirit 3 in this regard is control over the result. With only Spirit 3 you're stuck with whatever happens to the object in the transition, which may include it being intangible or having properties change as you guessed. Add Matter 3 and you control what it's made of, so you can ensure it is solid and has the material properties of had before (even if it isn't a material that exists in reality, you're not creating it, the object is already that even if in ephemeral form). Prime 2 should be enough to make most special properties and Charms transfer seamlessly instead of getting butchered by reality (or change unpredictably).

      Normal rules for Fetishes aside, with Spirit 4 you can try to use an ephemeral object as the spirit. I'd probably let it fly and mostly result in a physical object that can use the properties of the ephemeral item within. But that'll probably awaken the item, or awaken something, so while it would be potentially Lasting-like, it's as unpredictable as any Fetishe.

      You can also rend the Gauntlet with Spirit 4 to bring the item, which may or may not have the same effects as bringing through the Gauntlet, or may have more interesting results.

      Finally, yeah, Spirit 5 and you just do it.

      Now, Vulgarity is a tricky question, because the item itself is not subject to it. Fetishes are sought out by many Mages precisely because the lack of Paradox compensates their less predictable nature. So I'd say most methods wouldn't be affected by the item's properties, only by how they came. The lower Spirit 2 + Matter 2 would be the only exception.

      Everything here bypasses most limits to get an item of power because the two things you said to be a different process are, in fact, a different process and way harder than normal.

      Just because you imagined an item it doesn't means its spiritual version exists, is exactly like you thought, you know where in the multiverse it is, it is in a close by realm or you can reach it with a spell. If you use crossover rules it will probably be Glamour and dissolve into nothing due to Banality anyway.

      Overall you're not talking about an alternative way to get magical items. You're talking ways of bringing an item that may already have given you quite the trouble to get in the first place. And that includes things like harvesting Plasm in the Underworld and stuff, which should be in the easier side of all that spectrum.

              Right, let's go from highest priority to lowest.


              Natural permanence

              That's an extrapolation from the variety of Realms found in the Umbrae. Realms diverge in physics and metaphysics to an astounding degree, including many where things work very similar to the Material, to the point that some or all denizens have full material stats.

              We don't really know what Ephemera is, and if it is really a single substance or class, or just the way otherworldly substances interact with the natives of the Material. When it comes to each Realm nothing is universal. Usual rules apply to "the spaces between" that we call High, Middle and Low Umbrae. Enter a Realm and everything is up in the air.

              Bottom line, feel free to have some objects be more or less stable in the Material. If an object makes sense becoming stable, let it be stable and the PCs figure out why. If it will make for a better story, have a normally unstable object be stable. Realms should play a role, their objects being harder or easier to stabilize.

              EXAMPLE

              In a journey to the Spires, the cabal find a city of the Djinni abandoned long ago. They explore, face some old spells gone out of control, and get their hands in some exquisite ancient swords with Damascus steel patterns and each capable of slicing one particular material that should be impossible.

              Upon return most of them will start to dissolve after a time, the PCs trying to study what they can while it lasts. After a month, though, one of the swords is still there and gives no sign of dematerializing. It can still cut any silver alloy as if it was mixed with butter instead.


              Spirit 2 – Touch Spirit

              If you just want the players to have something from their journey, this is your staple. Objects made of Ephemera rarely can survive for long in the Material before dissipating. But they can and do stay in the Penumbra just fine, adjacent to the Material. And for a Mage with Spirit 2 this is almost a mere technicality.

              An Ephemeral object in the Penumbra won't leave its position unless taken by something, and with Spirit 2 the Mage can reach the place with any sense and touch, so the object is still spatially nearby and the character can interact with little effort. The downside is that creative effects are necessary if you want to use it in other Material things, but the pros are both that the item is usually safer against robbery in the Penumbra and that interacting with it is most certainly Coincidental (if weird).

              Bottom line, a Mage with Spirit 2 can basically treat the Penumbra as an extension of their immediate vicinity and their home. The object is already in their hands, only limited in touch of material things.

              EXAMPLE

              The cabal doesn't take chances with all those swords. After a few tests in the Material they take the weapons back into the Penumbra. The Spirit Mage can help everyone in the Sanctum to see and study things in the Penumbra, and once they're finished the swords rest in their Trophy Room, even if at the Other Side.


              Spirit 2 + Matter 2 – Infuse with Ephemeral Properties

              You got this one wrong, you're not bringing any item with you. But you're bringing their unusual properties, or a close semblance of them. With Matter 2 you can imbue a physical object with Touch Spirit, for it to interact with things in the Penumbra. More importantly, you can imbue it with the properties of Ephemera.

              Usually that just means the object will be able to touch anything there, but with an ephemeral object at hand you can use this particular item as the template for the effect, more effective if the material object is similar to the ephemeral one.

              Bottom line, you'll take a lookalike of the item brought from the Umbra, Magically silver tape the two of them across the Gauntlet, then use Spirit + Matter to turn the material replica into an exact mirror that copies the otherworldly material.

              It's a temporary effect, but ending just means you have to make it again, as both items are in their respective realities anyway, so nothing else changes for them.

              EXAMPLE

              The ancient Djinn swords are like 17th century sabers. Not precisely, but close enough. The Mage buy a good replica from a trusted artisan and uses Magic to impose the reality of the old genie sword upon the replica.

              Slowly the new sword changes appearance to match the exquisite material and pattern in the blade. This transmutation is Vulgar, but the resulting sword likely isn't, despite being made of the strange alloy of the genies.

              But this effect is somewhat unpredictable. The physical sword is now made of the same material as the original, but in a non ephemeral version it's impossible to say how much it will work as it did in the Umbra. It can also touch the Penumbra as if it was in both sides. It's up to the ST which properties will transfer, which will not and which will appear that neither had before. If the swords are enchanted on top of being made of otherworldly substances, those enchantments won't be copied.

              You can't take the Rembrandt or the grimoire with that, as the material they're made of is not what makes them valuable and Matter 2 only changes composition, not form. But the water of the River of Death, Plasm, liquid Pathos and powdered Angst all can be copied into material substances since the composition is the point in them.


              Spirit 3 – Converting Ephemera

              Stepping Sideways converts your body and, at a higher Difficulty, your equipment to Ephemera while transposing the Gauntlet. You can simply reverse the effect when coming back and bring Ephemeral objects with you.

              They will convert into Matter. But not into any Matter. Just as your Ephemeral body retains its physical properties adapted to Umbral places you visit, the object will retain its otherworldly composition in a Material version, likely one that adapts to Reality Zones.

              It's more unpredictable than the previous rote, but it is also more faithful. Now it is the original completely, and you can bring the Rembrandt or the book without loss. Even the strangest properties are likely to translate, even if altered, and enchantments too. Tass is very likely to be just as powerful and keep Resonance, though the adaptation may change how you can use it.

              Adding Matter 2 gives you finer control over the substance of the translation. You can make sure it will remain as faithful as possible, or the opposite, change it on purpose for whatever goal you have in mind. Matter 3 gives you finer control over form, which can be used to hide its nature or create a protective layer.

              Prime 2 in the original example from the book is just bloat, but you can add to legitimately get more out of it, either extra mass without taking from the local Penumbra or some protection over exceptional properties like enchantments and Charms.

              On one hand, this effect is temporary. You have to put successes towards Duration or the item will eventually turn back into pure Ephemera. It won't instantly dematerialize, though, it's likely that it'll be as if it just crossed, and you can reinforce the effect to keep it around. In fact, it's probable that at this point a spell to make it stay will need only Duration, so it isn't as much of a hurdle as it seems.

              Best of all, while Stepping Sideways is Vulgar, the item itself isn't Magic. Unless your Effect changes it to be something too bizarre, what the item was before is just its nature. The Magic to keep it anchored is also Coincidental, as you're not doing anything weird, just ensuring it will stay real like expected of any object.

              Here, the bottom line is that you're fully bringing your target. Reality can change it, but won't undo it or merely normalize it. Well, usually. Add Spheres to exert control over the result.

              EXAMPLE

              Now the Mage can do exactly what you wanted and bring the swords, all of them. Since some are "just" inhumanly good craftsmanship and the material property of imprinting defiance against a substance, they'll likely translate well, but the details may change. Adding Matter is enough to ensure they keep working perfectly well.

              Two of the swords are special, though, even for their peers. The Mage doesn't know, but will find out when their powers – if in an altered expression – manifest. One was enchanted for a Djinn Sultan when made eons ago, while the other accrued strange magics through the life of its original, bloodthirsty owner.

              After that the rest is maintenance. Since the item is already in the Material and its existence isn't by Magic, the upkeep ritual just prolongs the stay of something otherwise natural, even if super. All successes go to Duration, and if the Mage is careful this will be a 5 rolls ritual of Coincidental Magic at Difficulty 3 with 3 dice. Aim at a Month long Duration (3/4 successes) and you're gold, just renew every 3 weeks for safety. You can probably do better, but no need to try your luck. At higher Arete it'll be trivial to make it indefinite and renew every couple years.

              If the Mage wants, they can add Prime even in the dark to protect hitherto unknown abilities. It isn't reliable without knowing what to protect and will raise the target successes, but doable it is, and better than nothing. Won't affect the Maintenance ritual anyway.

              You can bring anything at this point. Higher levels will give you fidelity, ease and scale, but as long as you can hold something, you can bring it with Spirit 3.


              Spirit 4 – Rend Gauntlet and Bind Fetish

              First of all: forget Prime. This is not Prime and Prime is not this. You can't just copy a Wonder and it being Ephemeral won't change this. I can say, though, that using Spirit 3 to build it with Ephemera would be rad as hell, but would still follow the regular creation rules.

              Rending the Gauntlet gives you a more direct and scalable means to bring objects. Instead of traversing with just a few at a time like drug trafficking, you can open a portal and dump Umbral contraband to your heart's content. Fill trucks with otherworldly stuff.

              Go wild. Find the Spire Realm where the Library of Alexandria is standing, but filled with sports almanacs and collectible cards from all eras and places, turn the floor into a portal and let gravity do the work. It's worth it just to see the obscene earnings of those chariot racers from Rome and Constantinople adjusted to today's dollar (it's shocking).

              Since you're not translating anything to Matter, the items are more likely to retain their original properties, but any change is also more wild. You'll need to start Maintenance right away, but with more dice and some other Spheres you can at least prepare the warehouse and trucks instead, which is easier, or try the effect as an area if the ST allows. Maintenance is still Spirit 3, so same Diff.

              Adding Matter and Prime still has the same effects, but is less relevant if you don't want to change the items. The portal is obviously Vulgar and probably very hard to hide, but the item just is, Sleepers be damned.

              Bottom line, Spirit 4 scales and simplifies. If 3 gives you exactly what you want, 4 makes it a trivial thing.

              EXAMPLE

              Why bring only a few swords? The Mage prepares the Sanctum's armory to house spirits in the Material for a year, then opens a portal. If they have enough Correspondence and/or successes, the cabal just enters the city and take anything that isn't stuck to the ground and even some things that are. Whole walls will be used to expand the Chantry, and one Mage is explaining to the others that the Adamantine door of the vault is worth more money than any treasure they may have left behind.

              Sure, the new building will need maintenance with Magic until local reality sets in but is more than worth it.

              Fetishes are their own thing and require other considerations. It won't just bring the item to the Material or replicate its properties, but do something else entirely. A Fetish takes a spirit, one that must be awaken and aware, puts it literally inside a material object, and derives its power from the symbolism of the spirit, not its powers. The creator has some influence on the final result, but so does the spirit and the circumstances, no one individually fine control what the result will do.

              So with Binding you'll play a very peculiar game with the original item. Arousing it is the best starting point, although it may fail as it isn't the spirit of an object, it's an object that's made of spirit stuff. Working or not, isn't as if something can stop you from putting inanimate Ephemera into inanimate Matter.

              While you do it you state more or less what you think should happen, that's a primary guideline. The power of the spirit defines if what you asked for is within reach, and scale down or up by itself after initial input. The spirit "states" its own wants, and that modifies the result either blessing it, giving more powers, or cursing the item, although inanimate Ephemera probably doesn't have a big wish list. The ST adds more weirdness as they see fit (in your case is you). Ignore the actual properties of the item and focus on story and significance, unless you feel like using those powers.

              Long term, though, Fetishes have a mind of their own that will learn and change with their experiences. With time the Ephemeral item within will acquire a consciousness of sorts because that's what full blown spirits do, like those bound into Fetishes. That changes the desires of the spirit and the circumstances, and the powers of the Fetish follow suit.

              Bottom line, you'll create a Fetish, but that's a lot easier than regular Wonders of same power level and the item is unlikely to offer any resistance even if it's powerful enough to be worth using it in lieu of a proper spirit. But it will be a Fetish and will get weird with time

              EXAMPLE

              One of the swords has preternatural ease cutting... Paper. I mean, it is a cool trick at parties and all, the paper barely touches it, but not going to win the day. Or is it?

              The Mage decides to bind it into a small paper cutter blade knife. Since it's still an exceptional sword, it should be able to cut like it even in the humble body of a paper cutter blade. And since it cuts the archetypal medium of written words, they should have the power of metaphorically cutting short words, shouldn't it?

              Since the Mage doesn't know what such an old spirit will be like if roused, they bind it dormant, as an object. All the rules for creating Fetishes are followed, the cutter is small but beautifully made and intricate, the binding is a success. It is now that the ST takes everything and create the actual properties.

              It is indeed an exceptional spirit even if not the most magical of the bunch. If roused, it would be thousands of years old and a sword that no Garou king would be shy of claiming even in its infancy, it saw the history of the abandoned city and knew its inhabitants before humans had learned to write (again). It would be worth the few but solid powers attributed. Or rather, it is, because you can't go through all nine yards of making a Fetish with such potential without considerable risk of it awakening as at least a Lesser Jagling.

              Now that the power is established, the ST decides this small, concealable knife packs the full punch of a +6 damage sword that doubles successes in a 10 for both attack and damage, as long as it's used as if it was a saber. It also cuts words by silencing people with a subtle move that can be hidden or pretended as just cutting paper (that it cuts only as well as a very keen and well kept paper cutter blade). Victims can resist with Willpower against Gnosis 7, so good luck for most, failure is mutism for a day and a night.

              As the spirit awoke, it can also choose its share of effects. Since it was abandoned by its long time people when they vanished from the city, it wants to never be left behind again, and to be properly cared for. Small as it is, it will be with the Mage all the time no matter what they do about it, the knife just teleports to pockets and won't change owner easily. It also cuts the words of the user when it feels neglected for a whole day.

              Finally, it feels its "family", be the other swords, the owner or previous owners (the Djinni). It changes temperature kinda randomly when any of those is nearby, regardless if the spirit knows why or what it thinks of them. If used as part of a ritual to do Staying Maintenance on a sword of the family, it helps the ritual as other participant with its Gnosis 7.

              Everything the sword means was used for the item, while actual form and material properties are only tangentially considered, because what empowers the Fetish isn't the magic in it, but its role as an entity with an identity, a history, a web of relationships and meanings and a place to inhabit in the cosmos. A powerful Wonder is probably a bad candidate for this process as it is stronger by itself, but that Rembrandt can be a solid choice without any power at all.

              Spirit 5 – Create Ephemera

              I said before that sometimes Ephemera just work in a sufficiently compatible way with the Material that it'll sticks for longer, even forever in the Material.

              With Spirit 5 you just make it. Instead of using whatever Ephemera is at hand, you make your own with strippers and blackjack. You change the very Ephemera the item is made of, and with a few more successes it is Lasting: not a permanent spell, but an effect that isn't a spell anymore once done. The game is unclear when we can make those at lower ranks, but 5 is the sure thing.

              Bottom line, Spirit 5 just bulldoze all questions and do the deed. You take the item, decide how it works put it in the Material the way you want and it will stay until removed as anything else in the Material if this is what you want.

              You can even make it anchor to your person, so if it is ever destroyed, as a spirit it will reform close to you, period, and the reform won't cause Paradox because it isn't Magic, it's just a spirit doing what a spirit does.

              The one thing Spirit won't do alone even at 5 is prevent changes to magical powers or sources of energy like Essence, as this is the purview of Prime and other Spheres.

              EXAMPLE

              Upon reaching Spirit 5 the Mage decided to get rid of yearly maintenance and put everything they learned about the swords to use.

              They set apart the Fetish one and prepare the others. The two magical ones will need careful work to not risk their spells. The other swords, at this point they saw what each could do, can do now, how they changed or not, how other things changed or not, they saw virtually every possible iteration of Ephemera conceivable, lots of inconceivable ones, and theorized new iterations entirely.

              So they gather all those swords, decides on the experiments they can execute, develop a huge ritual to anchor them all in the Material and in a week or so they're all permanently tied to this Realm. It could be sooner, but the ritual also do a lot of changes to each one in accordance with the Mage's specifications.

              Changes that almost immediately aren't what they really want anymore, so if no one takes the Master of Spirit out of the lab they'll go for years tweaking the sword collection either for curiosity or whim.


              What must be clear is that "permanent" has a very specific meaning in Mage. Or rather, two ambiguous meanings the game never clarified. You can have an actual sword for your whole life and pass it down for generations. But it isn't eternal, it isn't unbreakable, and if you want "can" to become "did" you'll have to learn how to do maintenance, oil it regularly with the proper options, get intimate with several pieces, and tools to keep those pieces.

              Similarly, you can have magical things in the game, but that doesn't mean "permanent" unless it is "permanent", which is a stupidly high bar as it either means the ongoing spell never ends if not subject to an exceptionally powerful and possibly tailored dispel effect, or it means the effect ceases to be a spell at all and is just reality, undeniable reality.

              You don't need Spirit 5, because "Permanent" is overkill. Spirit 1 sufices to appreciate a trophy room in the local Penumbra with mementos you can later take from the wall if you get there again. 2 to 4 give you plenty of ways to use the things you brought potentially forever. Most of the really important effects for the "stick" part are simple, Coincidental and low in successes, they can literally be the regular maintenance an item would require anyway, not to say that the place or container itself can take care of things.

                  I largely agree with monteparnas on her breakdown of the different possibilities, with one caveat.

                  I think the Short-Lived Ephemeral Object line in M20 isn’t about crafting a material object out of ephemera, I think it is creating a material object out of thin air and converting it to ephemera. Normally creating ephemera is Spirit 5, but creating matter is only Matter 2/Prime 2 and converting it is only Spirit 3. It’s a clever workaround to accomplish a specific effect with lower sphere ranks so Prime and Matter aren’t bloat, but they are there for a very specific purpose that we aren’t trying to accomplish here.

                  Matter 2 and 3 to adjust the translation to something more fitting or stronger is a good optional addition I hadn’t considered before. I like that a lot and the more I think of how the ephemeral presents itself the more I see uses in the conversion process.

                              I always interpreted the Create Object with Ephemera effect to be an alternative to Prime rather than to Spirit 5. It makes a lot of sense as both, though, as Spirit 3 already let you shape Ephemera into an object and then Matter converts it. More dots than Prime 2, but accomplished with another Sphere, though I guess it can be done with Forces, too.

                              Consecration was more "another topic" than "not useful", and I'd add a third use: bypass the Difficulty modifier to Stepping Sideways. Since it's now and "extension" of your Pattern, you're not required to increase successes and Diff to bring those particular items.

                              Abd yes, one of the things with Mage is that no power is meant to exist independently. Instead you'll be mixing rules all the time once you get used to it. So, don't have an effect to keep that item in the Material?

                              - Your Sanctum can do that by itself,
                              - You can ask this from a Mentor, Ally or just pay a Tradition colleague,
                              - Other entities can do that for you that you summon with other Magic,
                              - Some weird phenomenon covers that for you,
                              - There's always a Reality Zone,
                              - Other Spheres can find their own answers:
                              -- Store in Sanctum. Get or stow with Correspondence.
                              -- Extend its time in this world with Time, Entropy, maybe encasing in a consecrated form with Matter + Prime.
                              -- Store within yourself with a Demesne and/or with some judicious use of Mind. Or Life to be more literal.
                              -- Let it in the Umbra, but its presence is represented with a construct of Forces, bending light to make it visible and imparting kinetic energy.
                              - Let your Avatar handle it, some of them are capable of doing this.
                              - A Wonder with the power of anchoring items from other worlds.
                              - Get help from some Nightfolk.
                              - Mix and match, each Sphere isn't just giving you some powers, they're also multiplying the ways you can make all those options work for you.

                              Can't summon Spirits yet? Find a map of the local Umbra in the nearest Chapel, pinpoint a passage to the Underworld. Now you can take a radio or cellphone and use Forces 2, Spirit 2 and Correspondence 2 to send a message to any Wraith you know by directing the effect through the route.

                              Your players will be creative as they learn and get used to the system, and Mage is the most "the answer is probably 'you can'" game in WoD.
