    It talks about Observation and Experimentation not peer review. And the very ideals it espouses makes a mockery of peer review. You can't have peer review in a world where EVERYTHING is true. Peer review, and the scientific method, posits that most things are false and that only a portion of theories are really right. The point of the review is to ensure that the authors own biases aren't corrupting the experiment.

    Only the Etherites will punch you in the face for telling them they are wrong.

They have discarded the models the Technocracy uses because they understand that there is more to the world and more to scientifically study than the Technocratic party line allows for. So in the end as I've already said, their scientific experiments have (as a whole) allowed them to gain a better grasp on the true reality of Mage the the Technocrats have. Unless you go with the purple paradigm where everyone is fully aware be they Traditionalist or Technocrat that reality is consensual and that the Technocracy does what they do because it's convenient for them, in which case they are making just as much an affront to science as you claim the Etherites do.

Because they can.

                                I think that each Mage is attempting to make reality reflect their own beliefs, rather than shaping reality with their will, which is why the Traditions and the Conventions are a bit of a mess. While Mage within each Tradition (or Convention) share similar beliefs, their alliances are based on the beliefs that they reject in common rather than the beliefs that they share in common. In effect, every Mage is insane, it is just that the Marauders are the only ones who are insane enough to gain any mechanically bonuses from their insanity, beyond the ability to shape reality to reflect their beliefs.

                                That being said, I think that there should be rules to allow any Mage could create a tool that would serve as a 'lock' that would store one specific rote or procedure that could be used by Sorcerers or Sleepers (whether it is a flaming enchanted sword or a gamma ray laser, it really does not matter). The 'lock' would function as a focus for the Mage and, in order to store the rote, the Mage would have to spend one point of Willpower and one point of Quintessence. Until the Mage withdraws the rote from the 'lock', they could not use the rote and, even worse, they would accumulate any Paradox produced by the 'lock'. I would suggest that a Mage could support one 'lock' per level of Arete.

                                If desired, a Mage could permanently transfer a 'lock' to a Sleeper. The Sleeper would spend (Sum of Spheres * Minimum Arete) experience points to 'purchase' the 'lock' from a Mage. Sleepers could spend one point of Willpower or one point of Chi (depending on their training) to activate a 'lock' for a scene or (in the case of instant effects) one use. The 'lock', since it is attuned to a Sleeper that accepts its Reality, would not produce Paradox. Just an idea...

                                                    Originally posted by stanlemon View Post
                                                    I find it interesting that the majority of defenses I've seen in support of sleepers not being allowed to use any enlightened science even if it is something as easy as pushing a button seem to be dependent on the purple paradigm. I've said it before that I think the purple paradigm should be forgotten because it (like the sphere system) is biased towards a small group such as OoH where Will is what shapes reality. But that isn't what mage is about. It's about how Belief is what shapes reality and what a mage believes they are doing IS what they are doing. So when a technocrat is using an antigravity propulsion drive to fly then that is what is happening versus say a dreamspeaker who believes they are being carried by air spirits to fly then that too is happening. To just say that they are both deluding themselves and what they are really doing is just using their Will to fly goes against the very core concept of mage of how beliefs change and shape how reality works.
                                                    Mage is not about people having their own divorced perceptions of the world and standing amongst peers with their eyes/ears shut. Mage is about the premise that there isn't one right way to view reality, and that there are benefits to looking at the world in different ways.

                                                    Perhaps the faith of the Choirister can grant strength and bring a community together, even if there is no God.

                                                    Perhaps the Akashics are right that jobs, money, and the pursuit of power distracts us from a spiritual peace that's within reach, even if that comes at the cost of attachments and ambitions.

                                                    Perhaps the Etherites' endless pursuit of fantastical applications for science inspires wonder and lures the masses into their field, even if they can never truly share the creations.

                                                    There is no delusion in the stance that Will is the source of magick. It is not the purple paradigm, for it is not a paradigm at all. Using computers and knowing it is powered by tiny little electrons does not constitute a paradigm except for the most well-versed physicist who can build that into a framework for viewing the rest of the world. Knowing that with their Will they can control reality is literally the first step of becoming a mage, but it doesn't tell the mage WHY the world is the way it is, WHAT is actually happening, or HOW they should use this power.
