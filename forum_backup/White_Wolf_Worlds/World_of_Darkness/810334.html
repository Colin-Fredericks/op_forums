      This probably does not happen too often. Most of the supernaturals in WoD have a lot to lose if they 'come out.' The vampires prey on humans, the werewolves hold to a bloody spiritual belief system beyond humanity's ken, changlings die when exposed to many modern sources of banality, ect. Without some serious in-game handwaving, most of the creatures of the night would have little interest in exposing themselves to dangers and discrimination.

                                      Originally posted by EvilTyger View Post
                                      Laruell Hamilton's Anita Blake books has a ready made setting, to some extent. Vampires are still powerful, but Werewolves are all brawn. The first few books are pretty good, but later ones somewhat turn into a Litterotica story.
                                      A couple of other authors have played with similar ideas:

                                      Carrie Vaughn actively explored what would happen if the Masquerade came down in her "Kitty" series. Catherine "Kitty" Norville was a late-night DJ, and relatively minor werewolf in the Denver pack. One night, though, a local vampire decides to troll her . . .and things get out of hand. Despite her best efforts to shut it down, for three hours, Kitty ends up fielding calls about possible vampire hangouts, whether there are any local werewolf packs, and how to find a reputable exorcist. The next day, her boss wants to talk to her . . . and has a simple question for her. Can she do it again?

                                      By the end of the first book, her new show - The Midnight Hour - is syndicated in every major media market in the U.S., supernaturals everywhere are calling in to the first talk show just for them, and Congress is scheduling hearings. Later books deal with the scientific conferences, the first supernatural reality tv show . . . and an ancient vampire who is very interested in these new developments. The focus is primarily on vampires, werewolves, and hunters - although quite a few others get cameos.

                                      A more vampire-focused series, Chloe Neil's "Chicagoland Vampires" kicks off about eight months after vampires announce their existence. Citing exactly the same reason the original poster mentioned, the master of House Navarre holds a press conference announcing their existence . . .

                                      The first book, Some Girls Bite, was probably the best . . . but the others were pretty good, too. A grad student at the University of Chicago, Catherine Merit was attacked by a rogue vampire, and only a last minute Embrace saves her life. The book shows how her friends, family, and school react to the situation . . . should be some usable ideas there.

                                                          Oh man, True Blood sucks (har har) as an example of how and why vampires should do away with the Masquerade. Synthetic blood is cheap and plentiful? Cool, you undead mockeries have still been victimizing people for the past forever, now everyone is just supposed to ignore that? The fact that the undead can now feed without hurting people means that they would no longer leave trails of corpses in their wake, and they would not have to reveal themselves to anyone. Being a vampire in secret would be quite easy, and offer no downside; no abuse, no vampire hunters, no mistrust from humans... while 'coming out' as a vampire does nothing but paint you as a target. The invention of True Blood does not provide any realistic impetus for vampires to reveal themselves, at all.
