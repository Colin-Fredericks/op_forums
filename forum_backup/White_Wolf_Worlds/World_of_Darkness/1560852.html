    crowd source ideas for the aranta-shadur (Nosferatu scavenger hunt)

    06-22-2026, 05:03 AM
    Clanbook: Nosferatu described a particular clan ritual, the aranta-shadur. It is a game that provides practical training for clan members. The clan elders gather all the ancillae and neonates in the warren. Each participant is given their own list of items to acquire. Certain items are common to all lists while others are chosen specifically for that individual based on their own ability or to help develop a particular skill. Sometimes the exact same item (not duplicates of the item, but a unique item) is shown on two or more Nosferatu lists to spur competition. The people who own such items could be mortal or Kindred.

    The aranta-shadur stipulates that all items must be acquired without the knowledge or detection of its owner. Whichever Nosferatu acquires the most items on her list before the deadline (usually sunrise) wins the hunt. Afterwards, all items are returned, again to be accomplished without the knowledge of its owner.

    It's meant for fun and to help develop the espionage abilities of Nosferatu. Although not mentioned, I assumed there are some kind of "prizes" that act as mementos or prestige, but nothing too serious.

    It's a neat little flavor text for the clan. It's very evocative and potentially very fun for a Nosferatu player. It is also an interesting way to provide potential story hooks to a player. However, it is also an immense amount of work for an ST to do. The ST has to come up with an entire list for a Nosferatu PC (and possibly more than one), decide on possible challenges/scenarios to describe for one or more items, and provide some type of description of what the other Nosferatu NPCs have achieved as well. That's not beyond the capabilities for most STs, but it is a lot of work. We also must remember this is not a one time only event. It's something the local Nosferatu would do at least once a year, perhaps more. So it's not just doing it once, but potentially describing many such scavenger hunts over the course of a chronicle.

    Rather than just giving us two paragraphs, this is an area I really wish we got an old style random table chart with a hundred different items and a half dozen brief "encounters". Not only would it cut down on the work needed, it would also inspire a lot more ideas from an ST. Particularly because regardless of how much time it might take IC, this is probably a scene most STs will complete in less than ten minutes of game time. It just needs a brief set up, providing a PC some kind of list, allowing them to narrate how they complete it, maybe give a specific story hook or clue for upcoming game session, and then narrating a conclusion. Despite that limited amount of time "on screen", this can add a lot to the game for Nosferatu PCs, and even the other players can enjoy the scene as described.

    So I'm hoping the board members can contribute various ideas (of varying difficulty) on how to run this. What scavenger hunt items do you think would be good? Remember the idea is that int he process of obtaining the item, it is achievable, fun, and helps them develop spycraft skills.

    Here are some initial ideas of my own.
    1. Expensive fountain pen of a local CEO (or Ventrue or ghoul/retainer/ally)
    2. Specific jewelry piece of a Toreador or local celebrity
    3. Index card/notepaper containing the computer passwords for a specific individual
    4. Musical instrument of a local musician
    5. A particular DVD/blu-ray movie/TV found in a person's house (from anybody, it's a test more of observation than planning)
    6. A cookie jar or other knick-knack showing a particular cartoon or comic strip character
    7. A particular perfume bottle that still contains the perfume in question
    8. A foreign language dictionary
    9. A used pizza box
    10. A particular kind of living pet (also tests Animalism)
    11. A shot glass with the name of its distiller or bar
    12. A used movie or concert ticket stub
    13. An item that contains an animal part of an endangered animal (like ivory)
    14. A milk carton that has been expired more than 1 month
    15. An old valentine's day card that has been kept for more than one month
    16. A high school prom garter
    17. An old bridesmaid dress
    18. A key to a specific model/make of car
    19. A t-shirt showing a specific affinity group identity
    20. A person's piece of clothing showing a sports team that is not local to the area
    I'm interested in reading your own lists as well as other ideas for the aranta-shadur.

      As a suggestion, for actual play I would probably have a list with filler items and maybe one or two items that are slightly trickier to get.

              One way for an especially … testing … Elder to weed out those destined for a long run from the mere placers would be to list something everyday that sounds as though it should be found in the shops, but is in fact out of production and only found in a local museum (A certain make of tyre or a certain brand of cigarettes perhaps?).

              Not a big world-class museum, more a point of local pride.


              - Those looking to weed out the really meticulous and/or devious minds could list something that can be interpreted in more than one way (‘A coffin nail’ could be literal or it could refer to a cigarette and exactly which a newbie chases after could reveal something about them).

              Another possible example of this would be an older British vampire asking for ‘a half-crown’ (Which might refer to a pre-decimal coin, a luckless tiara or something similar) or a ‘bob’ (Also a pre-decimal coin, but a photograph of somebody called ‘Bob’ or a wig in a certain hairstyle might be equally acceptable).


              - I’d suspect that most of these scavenger hunts would only list articles associated with vampires from other Clans when these are known to be non-Hostile (I.E. Will test the youngster, but not to destruction).

              - it is also a racing certainty that none of these prizes will be of the sort to cause Serious Problems for the local warren (Somebody asking for “The Prince’s Pride”, for example, would probably be asking for trouble in a way their peers would not approve of).

              - Finally, it does seem likely that there will be a mix of straightforward requests and riddles subtle & blatant in the list, but that every item listed must be PORTABLE (Unless the local elder or elders are Real Jerks).

              An obvious point, I know, but probably useful to keep in mind.

                  First let's remember those are vampires: they can work on extended timescales and they can game any tradition for personal interest.

                  I think the system must consider first the layers behind local tradition:

                  1. How frequent it is? 1/week to 1/decade.

                  2. How serious is it locally? Enough to reward or cost Social Backgrounds? To risk life?

                  3. What's the hidden goal? There might be none, but always good to think about it.

                  4. Who are favored or despised by organizers?

                  5. How competitive vs evaluative it is?

                  Frequency greatly affects overall difficulty and prizes. Frequent tough challenges will just tire everyone, and tolerance for skipping will be higher. A once a decade game has everyone riled up for years ahead of time, and the prestige is naturally way higher.

                  Seriousness affect how far they're willing to go for the game. It probably doesn't pay to ask for potential murder just for bragging rights, bad for the Masquerade. But if it is like a religious thing, a rite of passage or there's feeding rights at stake, the Nos as a whole may be willing to piss off the Prince seriously, and participants to face Final Death for even slight advantages.

                  Hidden goals mean one or more organizers have hidden agendas, which means there's plot (even if minor) within the game. Some items may be intended for real, with a cover of deniability. Someone is to be punished or rewarded. Some items are tests of the safety of some areas or some persons. The point is, 1-3 items are actual plot points and the scenes will affect further developments.

                  Favored and despised really just means if the organization is willing to rig the game, and how far. Even if the PC is neutral, this affects both description of results and how hard it is to beat the competition.

                  Competitive vs Evaluative is another kind of consideration: how valuable is it to hit certain milestones regardless of final position? That will greatly affect the strategies of participants as priority changes from absolute to relative position.

                  Notice: if local elders want to ensure the clan is capable at cooperating and put grudges aside for common safety, they'll value absolute position, the game is a place to show progress. If they want fierce competition and to either groom ruthless allies or pit the youngsters against each other, they'll greatly emphasize the 1st place and treat any performance below it as no better than total failure.

                  I have more to brainstorm, but that's it for now.

                          Some good questions. Here are my answers.

                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                          1. How frequent it is? 1/week to 1/decade.
                          This is supposed to be a fun activity that helps the brood bond together and teach them useful skills. I imagine most broods do it at least once a year, perhaps several times during a year. Not so often that it becomes tiresome or a bother, and not too infrequent that it is no longer seen as a tradition or part of clan culture. I imagine it is usually tied to a Hosting with the aranta-shadur being known in advance. There is probably a traditional Hosting known for it on some kind of anniversary schedule (the first Hosting of the year, the one always held in October, etc.) It's supposed to be organized by the elders so the ancillae and neonates participate. If the hunt is not done on some kind of regular anniversary basis, I imagine when word goes out about the next Hosting, it's revealed that an aranta-shadur is planned.

                          For STs, I'd keep it to whatever schedule makes the most for their game. If the PCs aren't that interested in it, keep it once a game year and hand wave it as background. If the PCs have a real fun for it, have it at whatever frequency it makes sense for the ST and players. I imagine for determining the actions of NPCs only, I'd determine something like 1-4 times. Small broods may do it only once a year while large broods may do it multiple times.

                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                          2. How serious is it locally? Enough to reward or cost Social Backgrounds? To risk life?
                          It's supposed to be fun. Most items are on everyone's list with a few individual ones tailored to that Nosferatu's abilities. The clanbooks say when the elders really want to test the younger Nosferatu they simply put the same unique item on more than one Nosferatu's list.

                          So certainly no risk to life or any other cost. I imagine there is some slight "prize" given as an award, and some temporary prestige to the Nosferatu who just won. A Nosferatu who consistently wins the aranta-shadur likely gains some permanent Clan Status/Prestige, but is eventually taken out of competition and asked to help organize the hunt. After all, it is no fun if the same person constantly wins.

                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                          3. What's the hidden goal? There might be none, but always good to think about it.
                          I think the goal is just to test each brood member's stealth, cunning, investigation and espionage in a fun way. The clan wants to encourage these traits because it makes the brood stronger in collecting information.

                          Individual hunts may indeed have a goal for the elders such as most items secretly involve a particular Ventrue's Influence holdings, involve the vampires of the Tremere chantry, or similar. Simply the act of collecting the items may reveal important information concerning something the clan elders want to know more about. That may or may not be known to the participants as a whole. Or it may be part of the test to see if one or more Nosferatu have figured out what the "target" is. For an ST, he only needs to bother with this if he wants that as a particular story hook for the PCs to follow.

                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                          4. Who are favored or despised by organizers?
                          While the scavenger hunt may be sandbagged so that a particular Nosferatu is sandbagged or given an edge, I probably wouldn't both with that myself. I don't think this activity is really about determining the winner. It's to have fun, create a bond and common culture, and encourage brood members to develops the skills the elders want them to have.

                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                          5. How competitive vs evaluative it is?
                          Competitive enough to be fun, but not so competitive that it isn't fun. I imagine the elders don't even mind if some Nosferatu team up to work their lists together (especially when it is a relatively new embrace who needs to be mentored, or a recent arrival to the local Brood). I also think that doing the scavenger hunt in teams could be a good way for some game groups to keep all players involved by handing out some brief NPC descriptions and dice pools to each of the other players who have non-Nosferatu PCs. Every game group will be different in whether something like that works for them or not.

                          I think the descriptions given in the clanbooks provide sufficient explanations for anyone wanting to run one in their chronicle. There is some slight wording differences between the original and revised clanbooks, but nothing important changes.

                                                          The closest thing to any scholarly consensus is that it was cooked up somewhere between what is now Russia and the Fertile Crescent, but none of the Old School will agree with anyone else’s account of it’s creation, it’s exact etymology and it’s precise meaning.

                                                          The version most popular with the rest of the Clan is that the phrase was invented as part of a disinformation campaign intended to spook potent local rivals into thinking Clan Nosferatu were working with really, really Phenomenal Cosmic Powers when they were really just training up their fledgelings to avoid a level of close scrutiny that had previously diminished their numbers (With the phrase being passed from brood to brood to help create the impression the Phenomenal Cosmic Power was being spread throughout the Clan … and also for the amusement of various Nosferatu).

                                                          As a result, it has become traditional for Nosferatu asked about the fiercely-contested meaning of the phrase to give listeners a story told with the strictest historic detail, solemn mien and gory details as they build up the historic significance and deep cultural meaning of the phrase, so that they can deliver their preferred interpretation into an atmosphere of suitable solemnity.

                                                          One recent telling ended with “… and now you are ready to know the meaning of this historic phrase: it means ‘TOO GOOD TO BE TRUE, Sucker!”, followed by the teller laughing his head off.
