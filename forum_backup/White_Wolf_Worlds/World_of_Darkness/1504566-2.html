    It's very easy to overly identify with mages. Vampires are an elite beyond the elite, werewolves are radicals beyond radicals, but mages are just people with ideas and strong egos. It's infuriating that people IRL make a living from these practices, siphoning funds and time from people who need real medical attention, now imagine there's a chosen people that actually can make the BS work. That's maddening.

            There is a tendency in Mage to treat the various factions as stand-ins for real-world politics, something that other gamelines would get pilloried for doing. Part of that is that Mage is about “what people believe”, in a way that no other World of Darkness gameline is. So when the Victorian Mage Order of Reason is said to be behind that era's colonialism, the tendency is to view it as “the 19th century Order of Reason is a reflection of the real-world colonialist tendencies of that era”, not as “hey! Victorian Mage is saying that this magickal conspiracy was the real source of the atrocities of the Victorian era; not cool!” By contrast, consider the trouble the folks at Paradox got into early in the development of Vampire 5e when one of their first supplements essentially claimed that some of the atrocities taking place in… Afghanistan, I think? were actually the result of the schemes of an elder vampire.

            That said, the associations aren't one-to-one. As much as some may think it is, the Technocracy isn't a stand-in for “the vast Right-Wing Conspiracy”; it's a stand-in for an overly authoritarian government. Progressives tend to think of that as a right-wing thing; conservatives tend to think of it as a left-wing thing.

            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                    And ultimately, that's the main point: the single biggest thing that M20 got right was “it's your game; do with it as you will”. How well it follows up on that idea is an open question; but it at least acknowledges the principle that it isn't the devs' jobs to tell you how to run your game. Earlier editions, not so much.

                    That does mean that there will be gaming groups that use Mage in ways that will make the game's writers tear their hair out; but frankly, that's always been true. To the extent that they follow that principle in M20, it's a lot healthier than trying to dictate something that they ultimately can't control anyway.

                                I’m currently of the mind that, when it comes to the Union v. The Council, it’s less a matter of a distinction between left and right and more a question of what the Status Quo ought to be and what our relationship to that Status Quo ought to be. I could also maybe see an argument for a authority-liberty axis being a point of distinction.

                                I don’t think those are a perfect allegory either, because as I’ve mentioned above, I think the concepts of Mage are too concerned with the occult worldview to be readily translated into material politics, but I do think there’s enough of a case to be made in either case. I do tend to think that a simple left-right binary (already a somewhat frought conception IMO) isn’t the best way of illustrating the Union or the Council. As has been noted, both factions are portrayed in a wide enough fashion to contain either left or right elements within them.

                                Ultimately though, I think that even rubrics that are generally appropriate don’t particularly hold up under the scrutiny and the setting as presented. One could argue that the main distinction between the Union and Council is whether they choose to uphold or break with the predominant paradigm (the status quo). However it also must be noted that there have been elements of the Union who are not satisfied with other Union interpretations of said paradigm and how it ought to be advanced, this being an underlying cause for any Technocrat Civil War.
