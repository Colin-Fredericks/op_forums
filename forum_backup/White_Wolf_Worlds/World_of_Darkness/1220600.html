    Mage Combat Blenders

    05-20-2018, 03:06 PM
    Using normal character generation rules, pitch an especially powerful starting PC. There's no need to fully stat them out; simply addressing the powerful points and combinations will do in most cases.

    One example might be an Etherite built (mechanically at least) around the Hands of Daedalus merit.

    This 3 point Merit gives -2 difficulty when working with mechanisms and crafting, and turns all of the items you spend time developing familiarity with into Unique Personalized Instruments (giving -2 difficulty to Arete rolls where they are the instrument). Build your focus around instruments and practices which can benefit from this, from hypertech jetpacks to muscle cars to energy weapons and cybernetics. Invest enough points in Abilities to cover these various instruments (some Drive, some Energy Weapons, Craft, Tech and Hypertech).

    Take Forces 2 (Damage) and Prime 3 (Charms, Aggravated damage and access to energy). Using a personalized car as a focus, for example, the Etherite could perform a coincidental Forces 2 Prime 3 effect to obliterate whatever or whoever they crash into (inflicting Aggravated damage)... Difficulty 5 base, pushed down to 3 automatically. 2-3 successes on average means 6 or 8 points of Aggravated damage; even if our target is a Garou, they're at death's door. The mechanics would be identical if they were using a modified taser... which would probably be a lot more convenient than attacking using a vehicle, which draws a lot of attention and risks crashing.

    This leaves a lot of room for effects outside of "kill it to death"... they still have another Sphere dot to allocate, and Forces + Prime leaves room for damage resistance, flying, weather control, Wonder development...

      Likely a more efficient route; start with "natural" Aggravated damage and focus on applying it as efficiently as possible.

      So using Advantages, start with Aggravated Damage claws. Perhaps your father was Fenrir, of Norse myth. That's a 6 point Advantage. Now invest in Brawl 5, Dexterity 5, Strength 5, and make Fenrir a 5 dot Totem for +3 Brawl (along with advice, a potent ally, some Esoterica and Cosmology)...

      Now use level 1 Sphere effects to lower Difficulty for your Brawl attacks. With 13 dice on an attack, rolling at Difficulty 3 with Exploding 10s (highly achievable), you can achieve 10.4 successes on average. That's 5 (Strength) + 9 (Extra Successes) + 1 (Claws) dice of Aggravated damage. That's 6-7 points of Aggravated damage on a modest roll. Spending a few Freebies or EXP for an extra Spec would make it 7-8 points on a modest roll.

      Combining with Time 3 could make this exceptionally dangerous. A Forces 2 attack can only happen once per turn, but if such a Mage were to take Extra Actions (having cast their difficulty reduction Effect at the start of the night as an extended action) then they would be able to take out several foes in a single turn.

      For example, at the start of the scene they could use Time 2 / Entropy 1 / Correspondence 1 to map out the movements and probabilities of the people in the room, predicting their movements in advance (to reduce attack difficulties). They could then implement a Time 3 effect and strike up to 4 people lethally in a single round (1 action, plus up to 3 actions from the Arete roll).

          I built a rather effective orphan with Life 3, Forces 2, Mind 1 and a blessing of an always on (as in can’t turn it off) perception of and ability to touch (and be touched) by wraiths. They awakened while they were a victim of a mass sacrifice by a death cult, only the death didn’t stick and they came back empowered by the souls of every last murder victim with them (these collective voices are their avatar).

          Their paradigm is that the living draw strength from the dead and the dead from the living (from keeping their memories alive and resolving their troubles). Life 3 grants them the ability to basically be The Crow. Forces 2 grants both stealth and various poltergeist-like abilities (moving objects, flickering lights/electronic control, shadows moving of their own accord, chill breezes, etc.). Mind 1 and the unbondable merit reflect the fact that their head is already filled with a hundred angry ghosts... there’s just not room for anything else up there... multitasking is literally asking one of the ghosts in their head to work on a particular problem instead of doing it themselves. The ‘death sight’ and ‘touch’ were bought as blessings instead of spheres because they were intended to be always on and not under the character’s control.

          They made a mission of putting the restless dead, particularly murder victims, to rest by resolving their issues. Often this involves meting out justice to supernatural monsters that prey upon the innocent.

          Before awakening the character had been part of an extremely wealthy British family. The cultists killed their entire family though so they sold off the mansion and other assets and now lives on a sizable yacht, both because it provides a mobile base of operations and as a respite from the calls of the restless dead (not many can reach them when they’re out at sea).

          They’ve learned to fight from various ghost mentors and they’ve used their resources to pick up armor, weapons and other gear useful to hunting down monsters human and otherwise. They’ve learned that much of ghostly aid they receive is easier for them to call upon when they dress the part of a “costumed” vigilante (a mythic thread in the vein of the Shadow, the Crow, Blade, Selene from Underworld, etc.) and has puzzled out that “ghosts” have an easier time acting on the world when their actions can be cloaked in coincidence. Paradox Backlash, when it comes, is in the form of hostile poltergeist activity near them.

              Be an Akashic Brother, take as much Do as GM lets you, get the one that reduces weapon difficulty. Buy yourself a Sword of Mars talisman from Forged by Dragon Fire - get -3 difficulty reduction on attack and damage rolls. Take Graceful merit from Sorcerer's Crusade Companion, which gives -1 dif on Dexterity rolls. Now you are at -5 difficulty to your stabbing and parrying rolls. Take Prime 2 to have Aggravated damage. Take Life 3 to soak Aggravated damage and buff Dexterity. Buy a tattoo wonder that gives you extra actions. Doesn't really matter what your third sphere is, but Entropy and Correspondence are good picks. Buy Arcane 5 and Stealth 3.

              Start each month with a ritual that gives you 5 in Dex, Stamina and Strength and lets you soak Aggravated. You now have 7 dice to hit things at average difficulty of 1, 13 dice to sneak up on things at -1 difficulty (before magic), soak aggravated damage. Your damage rolls have -3 difficulty and are Aggravated.

                          Originally posted by TheVarulfen View Post
                          I'm pretty sure the minimum difficulty is 3, in any circumstance. There's no sense in lowering the difficulty any further, mechanically. Also, even very trivial things may not go exactly as planned (I'm not saying you botch them, but you may not have as many successes as you really hoped for).
                          Well in that case, you could economize on certain difficulty-reducing traits. You'll almost never need more than -4 difficulty (enough to push a Difficulty 7 interaction down to Difficulty 3). Once you've got enough Difficulty modifiers to always hit Difficulty 3 on certain critical rolls, the best investment is more dice. At low difficulties extra dice are a great investment.

                          I find it interesting actually. Mechanically speaking VTM has a LOT of ways of gaining more dice very easily. Blood pumping and the Physical Disciplines are obvious examples. Mage is filled with Difficulty reductions, but gaining dice seems less common. And there's definitely a useful middleground if you want the best result possible. If you've got Arete 1-3 (or your Attribute + Ability pool isn't very impressive), then reducing Difficulty will only do so much. It makes it much safer, but you won't get the insane results you might hope for. This Difficulty vs. Dice relationship makes traits like Cult and Stamina quite useful, since you can abuse a low difficulty simply by repeating the roll.
