    Why not use the Fomori (Mockeries) in all the Gamelines?

    04-03-2014, 01:48 AM
    Just curious but it seems to me that Fomori (for the sake of the thread I will call them Mockeries because it is a term not seated in a specific culture) would fit with most of the other gamelines as good general Antagonists. You don't even really have to worry about contaminating your game with crossover stuff. Vampires already acknowledges evil spirits exist. Sorcerers interact with them and the Baali, Setites, and Tzimisce have a long history with interacting with wicked spirits. So you don't really loose or dilute anything in a vampire game by having Mockeries.

    Mages ties deeply with the Umbra and evil spirits. They also handle misc supernatural situations and could even include their own setting spins on them with various taints/flaws as signs of Paradox. Nephandi and Umbral Dark Lords would definitely have Mockeries as part of their repertoire.

    Wraith I think would benefit from the mystery added by inhuman spirits, and I'm quite interested in mentioning the souls of those possessed by evil spirits after they die. I would imagine some would have the evil spirit usurp/merge with their Shadow.

    Mummy already has a form of Mockery called a 4 Jar, from cultists bound to evil spirits with a lesser form of immortality.

    Changeling seems the like the gameline that most wants to separate from the rest of the CWOD. But still Demon possessed mortals are always a good prodigal to throw in, and they would have as much to do with the game as mortal ghosts. They also have a tie in with the Fomorians, as it was implied that the Fomorian invasion had Mockeries and Fomorian fae among their Horde.

    For Hunter Mockeries seems like they would be a major simple foe for the Imbued, they rank up there with Zombies and Draculers. In fact I think they are more fitting as foes then Werewolves and Mages.

    The Middle Kingdom Setting already has them as the General Antagonists for everyone as the Yama Kings are everyone's foes and they employ them. So the Wan Kuei, Hengeyokai, Hsien, Mages, and Demon Hunters already all include the local Mockeries (Bakemono) as their General Antagonists.

    Demon has an interesting fit since its the only gameline that says only the Underworld Exists rather thenother Spirit Worlds, which is ironic and terrible planning in my opinion since their background story is the most accommodating to other realms of existence. But Demons already use lesser spirits like making Elementals with Rituals, and Earthbound would fit perfectly with using Mockeries as minions.

    To me it just fits the CWOD in General. You don't need to know Cosmology beyond evil spirits exist and they sometimes possess people.

    In my opinion this makes things like the new Book of the Wyrm that is coming out a good resource for all gamelines. Even when I was just getting started in the CWOD one of the major draws to getting the other gamelines was mining them for NPCs. The Cyborgs and Revenants in the Werewolf Revised Core was an excellent example of including supernaturals without needing to mess with crossover cosmologies. And in my opinion Draculers fit as antagonists in other gamelines because they are mostly athiest blood sucking corpses, they don't really contradict anyones cosmology.

                              Well it serves a good tool to make the sins of PCs come back to bite them in the Arse. A lot of time in vampire games the only negative consequence for doing really horrible things is your Humanity can be lowered, and sometimes not even that. But now say the business partner you Dominated into a broken half person gets possessed because they are little more then an empty shell and in getting possessed they go from tool you are used to abusing to an obstacle in your way.

                              Or even victims of conventional malevolence, you get back at an enemy Kindred by shutting off the water in their apartment, a encouraging local criminals to target the area, maybe after a while all the suffering concentrated in the area draws spirits of violence to inhabit folks.

                              Its something that isn't guaranteed to happen but they are events you can have happen to accentuate the repercussions of players actions. Simplest example is blood is addictive, and ghoul relationships are built on that simple fact. A good way to show the effects you have is to have them possessed literally by a spirit of addiction, perhaps even with powers to master the addiction of the PC who caused the whole mess.

                              It would also serve a nice tool for indicating Sabbat Territory. Sabbat generally sow misery and chaos in the kine populace, and their foot print could be an upspike in random possessions. And like everything you can choose how often or rare you want these in your games.

                              Another example of that is in Revised games in the antagonist section they often talk about revenants, ghosts inhabiting bodies that died of violence seeking revenge. They mention after the week of nightmares this has been happening more often. But its left open, you can run a whole game without bringing it up, but if you choose to use it in a game its no trouble, its just a sign of the times. Or really the same as ghost. Sometimes ghosts haunt places. Sometimes they don't.

                                      They don't do much to build on the narrative themes of Changeling, and can do a lot to detract from them. The big problem is that they're too self-evidently real. It becomes a lot harder to sell the idea that magic is fragile and precious as soon as you start crossing over with any entity that's not subject to Banality, or something similar. The original Fomori, and those Adhene, Thallain and Shadow Courtiers who may be affiliated with them, can serve similar purposes, while still being subject to the overall Changeling themes. Werewolf books may indicate a bigger link between their Bane-haunted monsters and the Elder Nightmares, but Changeling books seem to indicate that the Fianna just remembered the name of the latter and used it to describe the former, and that there's never been any deliberate collusion between them (although House Balor and some Thallain have made contacts on their own).

                                      Of all the other things in the WoD, Mages and Wraiths tend to be the easiest to cross into a Changeling game. Mages, because they're playing the belief and imagination angle from the other direction, and Wraiths because stories about being forgotten resonate strongly with both groups. Werewolves also have a lot of hooks to link up to Changelings, but that works a lot better from the Werewolf direction, sprinkling the dwindling fae in as another reminder of Gaia's decline and the Wyld's peril.
