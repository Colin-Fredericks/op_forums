    ALL THE POPULATIONS

    08-20-2016, 03:09 AM
    There have been a lot of "what is the population (rate) of X" posts lately. It occurs to me that it would be good to combine all the Awakened Being populations in one place. The following are my personal preferences--actually they are my preferences when taken individually, with a given splat assumed as the focus of the game being run. I deemphasize the groups I'm not running at a given time, which usually means they are less in number than what they would be if I were running them. Also, if I'm being honest, I just produce an amount of whatever splat makes sense to the plot as I go along. This was an amusing exercise though.

    Vampires (Cainite)--1 in 50,000. It's a nice compromise between the 1 in 100,000 of OWoD and the 1 in 10,000 of NWoD. It doesn't seem like a lot, but I assume vampires don't bother with towns or cities under 300,000 (where about 5/6 of humans live) and that most of East Asia is kept Cainite-free by the Cathayans. Both these factors drive up Cainite-per-person in the places actually inhabited by Cainites. I like a higher vampire population than given in OWoD because vampires are the main reason the WoD is the WoD. They are literally the reason it sucks so much.

    Werewolves--1 in 400,000. This sounds like a small number and it is. But there is only one actual wolf for every 35,000 people approximately, and it would be weird to me if there were more than one werewolf per 10 wolves. Also Wolves have a limited range in the world, and I imagine werewolves mostly keep to that range.

    Mages--1 in 200,000. This is a pretty small number when you consider that this includes Technocrats who barely seem like mages at all. I like the number being small, though. Mages dynamically warp reality at will. It seems safe to say that their influence is disproportionate.

    Wraiths--1 in 200 not including Drones. I know the books add a zero to the numerator of that, but I can't bring myself to accept such a high number. Even with the Shroud, that many Wraiths would probably have been officially and definitively discovered by humans. 35 million wraiths is enough for them to have the complex and fairly autonomous societies they are portrayed as having.

    Changelings--1 in 10,000. It's hard to come up with a good number for Changelings. On the one hand they are a dying race of otherworldly beings living among people whose very unbelief unravels their existence. On the other hand they have a stratified social structure with fae courts and working classes. 700,000 seems like the minimum required to maintain some semblance of that, assuming a lot of those titles are just bluster. Too much higher than that, and our world would probably look a lot different than the one we live in.

    Demons--Hell if I know. According to Renaissance era demonologists there are anywhere from about four million to about 130 million. Either seems like a lot. But then not only a small fraction of them would have made it to Earth. There should probably be fewer Fallen than vampires if both exist in the same setting. Maybe one for every 5 vampires, so one in every quarter million humans?

    Kue Jin--1 in 300,000. They're only in East Asia and some East Asian diasporas, and I imagine them being less common per human than Cainites because they don't reproduce (other Kue Jin anyway).

          Considering this, I'd put them at 400,000-500,000 or perhaps even 1 in a million humans.

                  Kue Jin--1 in 300,000. They're only in East Asia and some East Asian diasporas, and I imagine them being less common per human than Cainites because they don't reproduce (other Kue Jin anyway).
                  The Requirement to be a KJ is roughly the same as being a Wraith, so while Kindred might have the embrace something like the Killing Fields massively grew the KJ population.


                  Honestly though the X supernatural to number is really kind of a shitty way of dealing with them because like people supernaturals Clump. There may in theory be 70000 or so vampires of all shapes and flavours but there are going to be vast swaths of rural areas that are going to be under represented and tourist towns that are going to be over represented.

                  Werecreatures are going to be focused around Caerns which may or may not have a connection to human life.

                  Mages are going to have a similar setup on Nodes but are also able to more readily spread out and maintain a "society" Mages don't need to have 30 guy in a city to have a Chantry a cabal might be spread out across half the mid atlantic without much issue.

                  Wraiths well they are going to clump at cities.. and points of horror. Gettysburg only has roughly 7000 or so living people.. by the 1 in 200 rule there should be only 35 ghosts including drones, and that would't quite work.

                  Changelings are going to reasonably be focused around Glamour. Places with many freeholds and glens are going to be far more Changeling happy.


                  So nothing is ever going to live up to broad statistics just pick what works for story. I'm personally good with a rule of thumb Wraiths>Mortal+>Changelings>Vampires>Werewolves>Ma ges.

                      Generally, the overall numbers don't matter much, but for world building I use an old Census file I had found online and saved. It listed which numbers were canonically verified and which were wholly made up. The numbers have probably changed in the past 15 years, but it still serves well enough.
                      For my games:
                      Vampires: 1:100,000 (including KJ), 1:50,000 outside of Asia and Africa
                      Garou: 13-14k total
                      Mages: about 11,000 total between all faction and in and out of the Umbra
                      Wraiths: A lot more than everyone else. (millions-billions)
                      Changelings: 1:20,000
                      Demons: I never cared
                      KuieJin: about 21,000
                      Mummies: rare-ish. never bothered with a number (maybe a couple of thousand total)

                      Fomori: 1 per shifter at any given moment (so about 47,000-ish)
