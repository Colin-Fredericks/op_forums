                Kharnov Go ahead, I don't mind tangents. It's not a proper forum thread without a parallel discussion by page 3 anyway.

                Before I go over the description, I wanted to adjust the Hierarchy. Current outline:

                - Changed Rank 1 Sin to one for reflection and re-evaluation. This one needs to be properly explained in the description.
                - Rank 3 "Becoming blood bound" was broadened to allow for more triggers
                - Rank 4 "Succumbing to Frenzy" was broadened and moved to Rank 6, added social friction. Would like a snappier phrasing, but can't think of anything.
                - Bumped up severity of Rank 5 & 6 to Rank 4 & 5 respectively
                - Sins 7 & 8 were very close, changed Rank 7 to something with more internal friction

                1. Failure to seek self-knowledge
                2. Losing Will or Virtue
                3. Yielding a personal freedom
                4. Acting against your own self interests
                5. Failure to treat others as you would be treated
                6. Putting desires of your Beast or another before your own
                7. Forfeiting long-term for short-term progress
                8. Refusing indulgence that harms none
                9. Failing to explore a new desire
                10. Feeling guilt for your actions

                In general, broader phrasing allows more for triggers but sacrifices unambiguity. I hope the altered phrasing is still quantifiable enough in gameplay. Let me know what you think, and also if it's enough added teeth. Personally I'm pretty happy with it, but don't let that stop you.

                    Originally posted by Tjalei View Post
                    Vampire supremacist may be accurate as words go, but if you tell that to a player they will image it as vampire skinhead.
                    If I open with monstrous transhumanist with a dash of body horror it's trivial to follow up with they believe in an evolutionary chain, kine above animals, kindred above kine. And also of course: you don't have to go into body horror if you think it is gross. It's practical. Depth can be added over time.
                    See, based on what you're saying here, it sounds like you aren't talking about Paths needing to fit into a "role." That sound more like a Stereotype.

                    And your own response already demonstrates some of the limitations of that approach. Your "stereotype" for a (white) supremacist is a Skinhead, but the Ku Klux Klan would probably qualify just as well, and there are plenty of distinctions you can make between the two. There isn't any reason to prefer one interpretation over the other, which limits the utility of the expression unless you consider both options equally valid.

                    Your use of the term "transhumanist" actually carries a similar issue, even if I do agree that it's appropriate. In my experience, the expression "transhumanism" tends to be associated more with a scientific/tech-oriented approach to things, folks turning themselves into cyborgs or uploading their consciousness. But Metamorphosis is as much a mystical and spiritual pursuit as it is a scientific one. Your use of the term "transhumanist" isn't necessarily any clearer than my use of the term "vampire supremacy."

                    But more importantly, the fact that "skinhead" is the first thing that pops into your head when I say "vampire supremacy" is completely irrelevant, because I am not talking about a Stereotype of the Path; I am talking about the actual philosophy of the Path of Metamorphosis, and I can clearly point to tenets of its ideology and actual mechanics like the use of Instinct as a Virtue to demonstrate my point. Your first impression just doesn't matter in the face of actual facts, and it demonstrates the limitations of trying to force a complex Path to fit into a neat little Stereotype you can sum up in a single sentence.

                    You point out that there's no reason you can't elaborate and broaden the concept beyond the initial Stereotype, but then why do you need the Stereotype if it isn't going to cover everything anyways? I don't necessarily think it's bad if you can do it, but I think it's a mistake to treat it as a necessary component of a coherent Path. There are some Paths, like Metamorphosis, where it's probably a good thing if you can't come up with a neat little box that the majority of the Path followers can easily be lumped into, because diversity and individuality are the point.

                    You might argue that you can defend your use of "body horror" in the same way, but I would disagree. However, it depends on a bit of clarification: when you say "body horror," are you treating that as synonymous with "body modification"? I would treat those as distinct, but not entirely unrelated, concepts.

                    For example, a Metamorphosist who uses Vicissitude to move their heart to a different part of their body as a defence against being staked is definitely engaging in body modification. But while it might be disgusting to watch, I don't think most folks would consider what amounts to a surgical procedure to be a form of body horror.

                    For more of a grey area, if the Metamorphosist instead opted to strengthen their ribcage by reinforcing it with other rib bones (using the 6-dot power that lets you graft on other peoples' body parts), then that would probably count as body horror from the perspective of the person who probably has to be kept alive while their ribs are pulled out and moulded onto the Tzimisce's chest, while still also being body modification. And the appearance of the Tzimisce after the process may not be particularly "horrifying."

                    And, of course, a Tzimisce who turns themself into a writhing-mass of lamprey-like tentacles has probably crossed over properly into something you could call body horror.

                    Now, usual caveats around personal taste, do what you want at your table, etc., part of why I'm pushing back against your Stereotype of Metamorphosis-as-body-horror is because, in my opinion, that is the Absolute Worst element of the Path of Metamorphosis and how it has sometimes been portrayed. Again, not because it's "gross."

                    It's because it's stupid.

                    The idea of Tzimisce/Metamorphosists going out of their way to make themselves look monstrous as some kind of rejection of their humanity is childish edge-lord nonsense. They're still letting themselves be defined by humanity through their opposition to it. At best, it makes sense as a silly phase that Tzimisce neonates go through before they "grow up" and get serious about Metamorphosis. A better example of what a "mature" Metamorphosist might sound like would be Alucard telling Walter "the trappings of mortal form are meaningless to me." Another good example is how Yorak's "default" appearance was that of an old man, despite Yorak being basically the Paragon of Metamorphosis in their night.

                    A mature Metamorphosist would not let themselves get hung up on "looking like a monster." They would coldly and rationally consider what form will best enable them to pursue their own ideal form of vampirism. A Metamorphosist who makes themself look monstrous because they think making their prey afraid will help them hunt is just as valid as a Metamorphosist who makes themself look as blandly human as possible as a form of camouflage for the hunt. What matters is that they are motivated by their desire to become a Perfect Being, not by their desire to give the concept of humanity the middle finger.

                    In the words of Hannibal Lecter, "No, [body horror] is coincidental!"

                    And, again, while taste and such are subjective, there are clear signs that some of the writers have come around to this way of thinking. Even by the time of Revised, you had various factions and ideologies among the Tzimisce that rejected the "body horror" concept, with groups like the Old Clan and the Children of the Dracon. But it's even more prominent in V20, with the Tzimisce write-up in (IIRC) Lore of the Clans basically being a discussion of how the obsession with monstrosity could be a sign of Kupala's corrupting influence. You can see a similar attitude in the discussion between Beckett and Vykos in BJD regarding the latter's decision to mutilate themself just to make a rhetorical point. Entertainingly edgy, sure, but still childish and stupid, for reasons that can easily be spelled out (pun intended).

                    Again, if you can manage to sum all that up into a tidy little Stereotype you can easily share with someone, great. But I'm not shedding any tears if you can't. And I (playfully) think it's downright disrespectful to try and force the Path of Metamorphosis into the Worst Possible Stereotype you can imagine for it.

                    (P.S. I've talked a lot about Stereotypes, and that term tends to be treated as inherently negative or pejorative. To be clear, I'm trying to use it in a neutral manner here, just as a description of what you seem to be talking about. Stereotypes are a kind of heuristic, and heuristics are useful. But they have limits, and you need to be careful of them.)

                        voidshaper While the seeking self-knowledge part is important, having "Self-deception" as the Rank 1 Sin may make it a bit easier to qualify what counts as a transgression. "The True Will must Know Itself."

                        The act of pursuing self-knowledge may work better at Rank 2, analogous to "Neglecting to alter one's body" on the Path of Metamorphosis.

                        While it needs to be defined more, I think it might be a good idea to replace "self-interest" with "True Will" in the Rank 4 Sin, just because of the common connotations of "self-interest" maybe obscuring the intended meaning a bit. While I understand it's inclusion better, I worry that "Treat others as you wish to be treated" might similarly carry too much conceptual baggage. Even if they stay as placeholders, those particular concepts could maybe be rephrased to connect them better with the elaboration you'll eventually be providing on the deeper philosophies.

                        One idea I had for a "custom" Sin that might be appropriate to the Path is "Thoughtless destruction." It leaves a lot of room for calculated violence, but condemns reckless and impulsive aggression, along with excessive collateral damage. I'd probably slot that into the Rank 4-6 range somewhere, but it's just an idea for you to consider. Similarly, "Acting on impulse" could work well as an option for the higher Ranks, could replace the altered Frenzy Sin, especially if you consider Frenzy as an "impulse," but I think it may also cover the short-vs-long-term stuff you have at Rank 7 a bit more succinctly

                        I do like "Yielding personal freedom," it does a good job of covering a lot of ground while still being well-defined and consistent with the spirit of the Path. And Rank 3 is a nice and dramatically-appropriate place to put it
