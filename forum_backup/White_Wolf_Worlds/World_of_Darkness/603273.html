    Changeling - involvement with Garou that makes sense

    07-25-2015, 07:23 PM
    In the Werewolf forum long ago, I asked about ways to properly involve Changelings with Garou since my players are in a pack with Stag, and its ban is to always help faeries in need. Werewolf doesn't give many ideas on how to make that ban important, and I got a few ideas out of it.

    Now I'd like to hear from Changelings ST and players about the reverse situation. Assuming a typical Changeling court in the region, how and why would they involve Garou. I'm hoping to hear lots of different ideas here. It could be fae making Garou enchanted and then abusing them as slave bodyguards to truly being good allies who reciprocally help the pack and call for their aide.

    Assuming there is no existing tie between them (kinfolk are also kinain for example), how would Changelings become aware of a Garou pack devoted to Stag? How would they approach them? How would they determine they would be safe to interact with? How would Unseelie treat them as opposed to Seelie? If you were Changeling PCs, and the ST made it known their was potentially friendly Garou (a pack is devoted to Stag who is a friend of the Fae), what would you do?

    One of my players is more of a Changeling fan than Werewolf, and while I am not that interested in crossover, I'd like to figure out some kind of situation where we can explore this.

    I ultimately see this Stag Pack of Garou as uneasy allies with the local fae more than anything else, but I'm not quite sure how to get there, or how that could change base on the interaction that happens in game. (I am a big believer in allowing players to change their environment and make fae as their enemies or friends based on their own actions and decisions).

    All ideas welcome. Thanks.

                          The changeling approach to Werewolves is that they're scary as hell,attract the worst kind of attention, and are the long lost cousins that you hope don't show up for Thanksgiving. when there IS Changeling / Garou interaction, it usually takes the form of the two groups boning themselves silly, or the Garou getting the bad end of a bargain.

                          Or both.

                          After all, Changelings are highly political creatures. Them of them as vampires, but without the "sucking wyrm-taint through a straw" feature. If a changeling is risking its neck to be around a psycho death machine, then it inevitably wants something. it could be something as innocuous as some fur clippings (yay, dross!) or maybe it wants you and your pack to be its latest pawns in a literal game of chess on the freehold lawn. Maybe the motley you run into wouldn't mind sending you after their 'corrupt, abusive tyrannical count" - or maybe that count wants to get you on his side to keep the snarling redcaps and rebellious trolls at bay.

                          Worst part is, the exploitation is only one way - Changelings are next to useless in the fight for Gaia. Sure, the Nockers can fire up their war machines, the Sidhe can mount their griffins, and it looks awesome until they smack into the "other side" and everything dissolves because the entropic, world-devouring abominations you fight are Changeling kryptonite (both parties learn quickly that your fomori are not their fomori, let's put it)

                          for a different approach, consider the nunnehi - they know roughly where the garou are coming from, seeing as how they interact with spirits familiar to the Garou, and they are the last survivors of cultures desperately holding on. Some of them are even fairly decent in a fight (though banality is still a problem). They also work well as antagonists, with stronger "werewolf" themes. Nunnehi benefit from caerns much like werewolves do, and some young (stupid) braves might not be adverse to conducting a raid while your characters are on guard duty. Do you fight them and risk ticking off your totem? Or do you let them slurp some gnosis? They won't hurt the caern, but allowing them to do this will disadvantage your sept's resources, after all... And doing anything in retaliation could not only upset your totem... but some other garou tribes.

                          If you're werewolf-centric, then something like a Fianna caern that is near some annoying Nunnehi seems more workable than really trying to dip into the byzantine labyrinth of Kithain society.

                              There are lots of reasons they might have meaningful relationships: defending nature and magical sites, battling Technocrats, joining together to fulfil an oath or quest.

                              The major problem with any game featuring both creatures is that the werewolves are largely cut off from chimerical reality. I find that either enchanting Garou or having them swear an oath to the fae or Dreaming (e.g., to defend a magical site which is both a caern and a rath to a freehold) is necessary to allow them to see the world as changelings do.

                              Once that's done, however, things become much easier. Werewolves and fae can bind themselves to each other, and as both are creatures of honour (usually), that gives them reasons to interact. From there, all kinds of interesting things could happen: fae in the Umbra, Garou in the Dreaming, all manner of mythic adventures.

                              But without enchantment, changelings just appear like any other mortals, and the wonderful world they live in is invisible.

                                      I don't think the Fae are going to know them because of Stag. You see, the same way the Garou are oblivious to the Dreaming, so Changelings are to the Umbra. Tough Stag's Ban does suggest some connetion, the nature of such connection doesn't mean the Fae already know shit about the pack.

                                      Changelings aren't just political, they are highly ritualistic. You can expect a Sidhe Lord in special to send delegations and stuff, hold court and all. So...

                                      An official delegation to invite them to court is likely to occur as soon as the lord knows the Garou can be of some use. No matter what he knows of them, he will first prepare in case things go sour, after all, they're raging werewolfs, not pookas, but as soon as he sees it as profitable and get prepared for the meeting, he will send a delegation with all the luxury possible (at least from the Dreaming perspective. They can be quite ragtag on the matherial view, depending on these particular Changelings) and make quite a show for the Garou.

                                      Fae aren't dumb, and much less eager to thrust. They survived the Autumn, after all. So the first thing any sensible noble will do is to SPY on the pack. As soon as he thinks he can invite them without dooming the meeting instantly, he will do it with all precautions in place. But them, if good for business, he will send messages and call them as early as possible, to stablish good relations ahead of time. Specialy to have an advantage over his rivals. He'll evaluate them and start to buy them to his cause, or at least to his goodwill.

                                      Garou are the closest among the Prodigals, but still Prodigals. Changeling society does have a place for allies, but never the same of a true Changeling, tough that isn't quite the same as an outsider. It is more like a pet. He can be more of your home than your neighbor, you can thrust him more, but in the end he is just a pet.

                                      Working with Prodigals a smart noble will allways give precedence to diplomacy, because things can get nasty. But don't be fooled. At heart all nobles believe in their right to command. That doesn't mean they are all assholes, but most are if pressed. They aren't all arrogant, but the great majority are. But even the most kind and humble will use Sovereign if too pressed, even if to apologise after. And Lords rarely are so humble.

                                      To summon the pack they would probably send messengers, maybe some stationary envoys to take residence nearby the Caern for the Garou to contact quickly and be contacted quicker. Only in some instances would they give any magical communicator, since their magic is also susceptible to Banality.

                                      Require Prodigals to attend court isn't common, but also not impossible. Only if the lord of the Freehold wants from them some kind of social help will this be likely to happen. Or if he wants them to see how he is good and honorable with his people, to later make an appeal for help against enemies. Some oaths can be required dependent on context, but the simplest level of Sovereign is precisely to assure good behavior during court, so it isn't quite a problem, they will just have to accept this single use of Sovereign on them regularly.

                                      Since Stag's Ban requires it, the pack will have to behave like knights in shining armor, for sure! But the Changelings aren't dumb, as I said, and will be cautious at first. But as soon as they sense the pack can help, expect them to reach the pack a lot. The noble have enemies, both outside and inside the Freehold. There are probably conspiratorial commoners. Webs of lies and truths intermingled. The Freehold can change from dream to nightmare fast if the pack thinks those fellas are simple, pure stupid or weak. Changeling politics can be as convoluted as the worst the Camarilla have to offer, and the characters are likely to be drag to it if they aren't cautious. The Changelings will reach them and ask for help, against other Changelings that asked also for help, and the pack can end up wishing they had to help vampires.
