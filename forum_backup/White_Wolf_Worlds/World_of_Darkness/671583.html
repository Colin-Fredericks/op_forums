              Since Hedge-Magic is so large, it basically can replicate near to any effect you`d want, they feature quite prominently in most of my games.
              Many of my Players have at least dabbled in hedge-magic, I use hedge-magic as inspiration for spirit powers, and whatever bad dude they are fighting could be a big scary evil ancient monster... or a hedge-magican with just the right pathes to replicate their powers.
              Of course this goes both ways, and what seems to be "just" an insufferable cult-leader with some minor hedge magic could turn out to be something else entirely.
              Personaly I like it, when my players are not too sure about what they are facing on a metagame-level.

              Scotish looking dude transformes into a wolflike creature and talks to evil spirits? Surely it can`t be anything else than a Black Spiral dancer...
              Or maybe its an awakend mage with a dark nature paradigem, a possessed mortal (Fomori), a Thrall to a demon if not one himself, an old fae from a bygone age, an upstart infernalist
              or: a Sorcerer with either Shapechanging and Spirit-Talk or Illusion Magic.

              This keeps them on their toes and makes sure, that the players don`t know all the answers from the beginning, just because they read the apropiate rule-book.

                                  I can see Sorcerer being a good way to get someone completely new to WoD and Mage in particular used to the game. Their Paths are linear, so anyone coming off a game like DnD, or even someone who has never played a tabletop RPG, won't be overwhelmed like they would with Mage's Sphere system. But the Paths can be open-ended enough in some cases (I'm looking at you, Alchemy and Enchantment) that it can ease them into the ideas that Spheres are based on.

                                  Moreover, playing an unAwakened Sorcerer is just simpler than other character types. One of the things nWoD did right was starting with a book for completely vanilla play, that then formed the basis for all other gamelines. So you could play regular humans, without the ancillary details about playing the various splats that complicate things. So with Sorcerer, players don't need to worry about things like Blood Pool/Humanity/Frenzy, Rage/Gnosis/different forms, Arete/Focus/Quintessence/Paradox, etc. Those sorts of things can be overwhelming, especially for new players.

                                  If nothing else, it'd be nice to run a game of Mage, but without Paradox hanging like the sword of Damacles over the players' heads. Path magic doesn't excite the wrath of the Consensus.

                                      In my chronicles, I typically have various people out there who know some kind of magic that may come into contact with the PCs. If the players focus on the occult, they may end up encountering people who know magic I make up on the fly. Mechanically, all these people are Sorcerers - and I typically limit them to the Paths in the original Sorcerer books, or the various Infernalist Paths in the Vampire game.

                                      I don't use the actual organizations found in the WoD: Sorcerer Book and find a way to tell a story with them. However, I will use the name and concept of the organization for these NPCs if they "fit" what I want to do anyway. If an organization, name, and backstory already exists, it saves me time and gives it a more "World of Darkness" feel than if I created my own.
                                      • The Ancient Order of the Aeon Rites - whenever I need an organization that is essentially the Golden Dawn and Aleister Crowley, I use these guys.
                                      • Uzoma - If I ever need an indigenous African sorcerer, I use them.
                                      • Mogen Ha Chav - if I need Kabbalists with real magical power, I use them.
                                      • Seven Thunders - I have only ever used them as part of historical backstory or lore if the PCs are investigating something's history.
                                      • Dreamers of the Silver Portal - I use them for any plot I run that is inspired by Lovecraft's Dream Cycle.

                                      I have used the Aeon Rites and Silver Portal the most. I have inserted the Uzoma and Mogen in a chronicle sandbox, but they never came up. The Thunders have only been mentioned when the old professor, antiquarian, occultist, or Mentor was giving the PCs an info dump.

                                      I haven't used any of the others.
