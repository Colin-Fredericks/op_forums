    Ok, running through things:

    VtM - It's hard to be surprising here. Vampires are dangerous, and the Disciplines aren't hard to be creative with to make even the non-combat oriented ones things that can be very dangerous.

    WtA - Like vampires, werewolves are all pretty dangerous as a baseline. However, I think it's easier to be surprising her as Tribe and Auspice creates expectations on who's going to be the deadliest. If you want to really catch someone off guard? Child of Gaia Ragabash are exceedingly deadly; esp. once they hit Adren. They're not at their best in a stand up fight (they are Ragabash after all) but they're no chumps there, and you can build one that can easily deal 12-15 levels of Agg damage with a successful attack (and Ragabash sneak is very helpful).

    Totem selection can also really change how characters perform. The right Totem can easily take a splat combo that doesn't seem that strong and make them devastating by WtA standards (if you have a group willing to deal with the Ban, Hummingbird turns non-combat PCs into monsters with ease, for example).

    WtO - Like VtM, it's hard to be surprisingly deadly. The "good at being deadly" options are pretty straighforward.

    CtD - CtD is sort of the opposite of VtM/WtO. While it has some obvious combat Kiths, Arts and Realms are flexible and widely available enough that any changeling can whip out some of the deadly options. Since there's little expectations, there's little surprise to achieve. I've found the most success I've had in that regard was a Childling Satyr (think a character that basically sees Jack Sparrow as an idol), simply because "so crazy and naive about how fighting works," isn't something people expect from that splat combo.

    MtR - Again, the "deadly" options are pretty straightforward. You're not going to get surprised by any of the deadlier combinations of traits.

    HtR - Generally, Mercy and non-Wayward Vision Creeds surprise people. Though really I don't know why, there's plenty of obviously deadly stuff in their arsenals; for Hunters anyway. And you can pick up Edges from other Creeds as it is.

    Orpheus - Again, it's not really that surprising where you're going to find the most deadly stuff, but Orpheus has some surprising things with how Nature, Shade, and Lament interact. I don't remember some of the combos, but you can easily boost Spite or Vitality pretty high with them, letting you use some of powers with much more strength than people might imagine at first.

                                                            For out right lethality from character generation, I would say the most dangerous build I have seen was an Assamite Sniper build who also was trained in demolitions.

                                                            Quietus level 1 means you are a walking sound suppressor that can completely negate the sound of any gun or if you are close enough any explosion. So you can fire an M95 or similar 50 cal. rifle and not make a sound. It also means you can use directional claymore mines to protect your back in a sniper hide.

                                                            Obfuscate ensures that your sniper hide is perfect against anything except Auspex, though since most decent sniper rifles can hit a target over a mile away, even if the target is some how aware that there is a sniper in the area and they have Auspex, chances are that they won't think to look that far away and by the time they do look that far you will have already taken the head shot.

                                                            The 50 cal. rifle ammo also provides a few benefits you don't get with normal guns in cWoD games. They are big enough to use Assamite blood poisons on, especially if you do custom sealed hollow point rounds. Failing that there are many real world specialty ammunition types that can enhance the lethality of such a weapon. Everything from armor penetration that cuts through manhole covers or walls to phosphorus rounds (tracers) to burn undead targets.

                                                            The catch about this build is that both the player and the ST need to have an above average understanding of guns, ammo and sniping techniques to bring out its potential. With such a setup a lone character generation Assamite can solo many enemies that would take team tactics to over come. If you build a coterie with complementary skills and tactics, then they can steam roller most opponents.

                                                            It would be tempting to say that just the player needs to have this knowledge, but in truth the ST needs it as well because such a character forces the game to change tone and scope.
