        You could just ditch Paradox and the concepts of Coincidental vs. Vulgar, Sleepers vs. No Sleepers on Difficulties. Then just make doing magic in front of Sleepers a VERY bad idea socially. Sorta the same way that Vampires don't go "Poof" if mortals see them use Disciplines but allowing that to happen will end up getting you punished if anyone finds out about it. If every time a mage was reported for doing magic in front of Sleepers there was an effing Tribunal convened to try him or her Star Chamber style and excuses like "but if I didn't do I would have died" carry some, but not a lot of weight.... Well, it's a different game but not really all that different. It's just taking out the mechanics of making magic hurt through roll-playing and making them happen because of role-playing.

        Hells, maybe you DO keep some issues of Diff. in play based on Coincidental vs. Vulgar, but invert them. Doing Coincidental magic is MORE difficult but it means that the Sleepers are going to be shaking their heads and wondering why something so wacky happened (and your character doesn't go to jail) while Vulgar magic is doing something closer to the true nature of magic and, therefore, actually easier. Oh, and without having to track the Paradox Pool, you've simplified things quite substantially. And botches just become botches instead of a gateway into a labyrinth of varying effects based on math and situation and, then, ultimately, fiat. Instead of going straight to the botches are handled by ST fiat that goes for pretty much every other game and every other circumstance using the WW game engine.

            This is one of the reasons I'd suggest at least looking at how Awakening 2e handles things if you want to go that sort of route. It ditches Coincidental vs. Vulgar, witnesses only count for obvious magic and have a very small impact regardless, the Paradox system isn't less complex but is always resolved per spell rather than building up with points and there's a lot less fiat involved (plus more player choice), and there's quite a large number of consequences that are more external to the mage to use as social consequence fodder (ex: it's really not hard to avoid significant Paradox while casting a bit fancy effect in front of a lot of Sleepers, but you can't really do anything about the fact that you caused significant psychological harm to a sizeable portion of them... which other mages aren't going to like).
