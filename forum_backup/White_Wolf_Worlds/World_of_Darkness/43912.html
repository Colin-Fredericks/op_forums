                      I'm fortunate in most of the folks I play with don't lean that way. However, I do have an official policy in this area:

                      The games I run are in my world. This is a version of the world in the books, but is in no way guaranteed to be exactly the same as the whole canon universe. Any deviations that you would know in-character I will make you aware of before the game begins. Any deviations in material that your characters wouldn't know about is fair game. For example, you do not know what the True Brujah are, what Disciplines they have, or how those Disciplines works. If I decide to include one, I am under no obligation to keep it canon. You know how Gangrel and Protean work, so I'll tell you in advance if I intend to house-rule them.

                      Further, I'm not an encyclopedia, nor do I own every single WoD book ever printed. If something was written about in a book I haven't read, it probably doesn't exist.

                      Finally, and most importantly, the ST's word is law. To make a Storyteller game work, you make an agreement. The ST will do a crapload of work to prepare and run game, and the players show to get an evening of free entertainment. In exchange for this fantastic deal, you understand that it's the ST's show. If I say that a dude name Carpenter shows up, roll with it. If I say that the rules are going to work a certain way, that's how they work. I'm always open to advice and constructive criticism (and have been known to change my mind as a result of such), but ultimately when I make a call at the gaming table I expect it to be respected and the discussion to end. That's the deal. Players need to give their ST enough trust and authority to run a game, and things break down if they try to revoke that at every turn.

                                                  This rule is in every WoD core book, but I'll quote the one from W:tA Revised:
                                                  The Golden Rule
                                                  This rule is the most important one, and it’s the only rule worth following to the letter: There are no rules. None that you must adhere to, that is. If you don’t like it, change it. We have tried to create a simple system that can be used to simulate the complex varieties of life (and obviously, we think it works fine for that). However, there’s no accounting for the creativity of the human condition. Not all of our rules will work well in your stories, so mix them up. Run a whole session without using any dice. Or spread out the map and run a tactical version of the attack on the warehouse. Do whatever you like. This book is nothing more than a collection of guidelines, suggestions and ideas. The rules are ways of helping you capture the essence of the World of Darkness in a game format. But these rules are only to help, not to restrict. Whatever works best for you is the right way to go. We have given you the tools, now go make whatever you want out of
                                                  them. p.175 W:tA Revised
                                                  For us, not only does this affect the mechanics, but the setting as well.

                                                  We called this "Rule 69" because we were teens, and it was on page 69 of some book once upon a time, but basically it gives the ST the right (and duty) to fuck any rule for the pleasure of the players and the story they deem necessary.

                                                  NOW, I agree that if a player builds a character with certain mechanics in mind, that the ST should inform players that there's a change in those mechanics ahead of time. Also, if there is some major change to the world/story that a character would understand as being a certain way, then the ST should try to make those changes known (The Shadow Lords are the Kings of the Garou in my game, The Sabbat have made a truce in this city, Virtual Adepts have returned to the Technocracy, The Red Star hasn't shown up yet, etc.) However, if the ST decides that the PCs are going to walk into a bar and see someone who the canon says should be dead, and those characters don't know who it is/have association with that?... then it is fully within the STs right to continue to mess with the canon because it shouldn't matter to the story. Holy fuck! Sam Haight's still alive and living on the down-low in Hartford Connecticut? There's probably going to be a fun story to be had then! The ST can change his powers even! The rumors of Haight's demise must have been false! (even though canon says otherwise.)

                                                  I've played with Rules Nazis who can't handle changes in mechanics from the book (especially when their "concepts" don't make any sense but were made to min-max powers), but we've rarely ever played with Canon Nazis because, well, most of our STs have NEVER followed strict canon to any game (even Star Wars.)

                                                  However, if you know that these people are going to be this way in your games, just let them know up front "This will not be a Canon Game, and if you're comfortable with that, then you can play." Otherwise, don't let them ruin your game by being dicks to you.
