                                Huh?

                                Every edition used this same basic layout for the VtM title art. The font isn't 1e, since 1e's had two fonts on for "Vampire," and a different one for "The Masquerade." It's closer to 2e.

                                The ankh is OK, but it's not an obvious replacement. In most fonts capital Q doesn't drop below the line of the text, or only does so a little.

                                From a front page layout perspective, it's actually a really frustrating choice. The ankh isn't centered, but is a very intrusive (it breaks the theme and boundaries of the rest of the image) element that's aimed towards the center of a cover instead of off to the side. It draws the eye to whatever is right beneath it, which looks strange if the cover art isn't picked carefully (including potentially blank space).

                                If it's just V5 cover ... it's not a big deal. They can tweak it to look good despite that. I figure most supplements (like every other WoD product) isn't going to slap it on the front cover prominently enough to matter if at all. Stand alone like a button in the DTRPG store isn't going to matter. Just seems... whatever. It's not a bad direction. It's simpler and at least in that contrast eye grabbing. I don't mind sacrificing the quasi-gothic fancy fonts VtM used frequently in the past. But it also doesn't do much to help with continuity of branding, showing off the fact that it's 5th Edition (like their previous V V thing), so it's pretty meh to me.

                                        I have a feeling most of the people reacting positively to it don't have a few hundred hours of page layout under their belts, and immediately eye something like that for its intended use as a layout element over just how it looks. As well, I'm assuming they're all familiar with VtM, and have see the VtM ankh before to get where the logo is going.

                                        I saw it was off-center immediately. It's pretty much the first thing I noticed, because it's the eye catch of the piece. It's the big thing that's different, so it's the first thing your eyes want to look at. And I've spend enough hours fiddling with millimeter sized adjustments to layouts to make cool looking individual pieces look good together, that I cringe when I see things like this because I know however cool it looks in the abstract, it'll be a pain in the ass for someone to make work once they need it to look good on a cover.

                                        Stressing the new edition here is actually pretty important because it's been a very long time (at this rate it will be the second longest gap after Revised to V20) between editions, and it being new is a big part of the marketing. In a very real sense (however any of us feel about the direction) this is the first proper new edition of the game in almost twenty years. V20 is being counted as an edition, but it's Revised with a few rules tweaks and no metaplot advancement. V5 is a massive overhaul of system and setting. "This is a legit brand new version of your beloved old game," is something you want to things like logos to communicate.

                                        Also, I'm not going to run off to WW's social media pages to say, "I think this is mediocre." I don't dislike it enough to do more than point out why here, and what I like about it not overcoming that is just my opinion. I'd imagine most people that feel like my are just as unmotivated to say anything about it (esp. in places that are more positive in general about V5, if they're even talking about it or this in particular).
