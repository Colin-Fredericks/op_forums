    Originally posted by Matt the Bruins fan View Post
    I don't think it's that the Baali embrace automatically makes someone evil to the core, it's that the vampire doing the embracing is going to regard anyone who doesn't get with the Yay Evil! program pretty quickly as a failure and a danger to themselves and their agenda. Soft-hearted Baali fledgelings probably have survival rates lower than pacifist shovelheads who try to talk out differences with the packs that embrace them.
    Not to mention there's undoubtedly Selection Bias at work with those Embraced in the first place. If your potential sire doesn't think you're Baali material, you wouldn't be targeted for the Embrace to begin with. (Unless the Baali was Embracing you specifically for malicious intent; even then, they'll be working very hard to "correct" your behavior, if they aren't just doing it to get their jollies and then destroy you afterwards). Most of the time, a newly created Baali was assumed to be sufficiently "Baali-like" to warrant being made one, or at least had the qualities that could be molded into a "proper" Baali.

    Obviously, what constitutes "proper" Baali behavior differs from era to era and depends on context. In the past, when Baali were making war on everyone, a person could qualify for becoming a Baali if they simply displayed enough viciousness. (Although, as illustrated in Noah Caldwell-Gervais's review of the game Tyranny, people who are victims of conquest can easily be MADE sufficiently vicious and willing to follow orders, if the conquerors deal with their victims "properly"). In more modern times - when Baali are few and far between, and their enemies numerous and vigilant - a shrewd mind and capacity for subtly are far more important.

    But in all eras, the kind of people Baali tend to go for are not typically nice or constrained by morality.

            Originally posted by Bluecho View Post

            ...But in all eras, the kind of people Baali tend to go for are not typically nice or constrained by morality.
            Except when they do. Baali also have a tendency to embrace people on the opposite end of the morality spectrum, especially religious representatives specifically.

            Problem being, the average Baali isn't going to need to put in much effort to turn a neonate to the dark side, they have the perfect self-fulfilling prophecy for that already: Daimonion 1(Sense Sin).

            I don't think many people have really thought out the full implications of that ability. It gives a person the ability to see all of the worst things anyone around them has thought, their darkest thoughts, and most closely held secrets. All without any of the context or good deeds that would offset the worst perspective you can have of that person. It's giving someone "Detect Evil" in The World of Darkness. It alone is a one-way ticket to slowly alienating everyone around you and quickly degenerating humanity without any help from the storyteller.

            This is also why I think it cheapens the game if storytellers make the baali evil because they're baali or when storytellers mandate random humanity degeneration for having diciplines like daimonion. The discipline is designed to give players and NPCs just enough rope to hang themselves with, something that's much more impactful than "I'm evil now because I got infected with evil-juice #3".

            For the main discussion that's my vote. The worst ideas in WOD are when they take the shortcut of "infectious evil"" instead of more human characters who are/were destroyed by their own faults and virtues.

                    Baali who are not evil. First thing which comes to my mind with this is that everyone defines good and evil at least a bit different; and the definition most Baali had was much more different than most others. It's true that sometimes they got people out of the opposite morale spectrum, but either to corrupt them or because these people were so extreme in their ways that it didn't really make a difference in the end. Chaotic Good people often do many things which might be considered evil by anyone who doesn't have the same believe & value system. Like Inquisitors in the dark ages or Soldiers who really believe in what they do in war for a greater good (or for what they perceive as the greater good),
                    ​
                    I think that Baali should be mostly "evil". or to use a better word for it they should be very twisted in their ways and corrupted. Same with any being who does willingly follow / work for major dark forces / the Wyrm / has pacts with (classic) demon overlords (etc.). Warhammer 40k's decription of the chaos taint / corruption is very fitting for this. In the end "evil" is just an infinite source of corruption and each encounter, even small innocent ones with a force will at least put some small seeds of it in the person.
                    Also the system of mental stability used by Kult & or mental health used by Call of Cthullu might well be used to explain this better then I may do it.

                    So no, directly after they got into the Clan Baali don't have to be "evil". But with the years most of them (like 99.9%) will either get there or be destroyed; most by suicide, because they can't live with their soul getting ever more tainted - and it's not possible to get that kind of taint / mental corruption away. Maybe one is able to suppress it for some years. But it will never go away or even lessen. Also there is the matter of other Baali and their infernal masters who mostly won't tolerate it for a long period of time.. and they ever need more sacrifices.

                                        Bad ideas:

                                        The whole granola-hippie thing on Mages and Werewolves
                                        "Brrr, Science bad, Science makes kills creativity, Scientists hate nature, brrr".

                                        The whole point of the scientific method is to understand how reality works. If tomorrow we could fly by clapping our hands you would have scientists measuring how many claps, how strong the clap needs to be, for how long it works, if its the motion of the hands or the sound, if you need human-like hands so monkeys/gorillas can fly too, if prosthetic hands are acceptable, etc, and THEN you would have people using that knowledge for traveling, for criminal purposes, rescue missions, etc.

                                        It felt like a comic book, it was like what a "creative" person thinks that "smart" people behave, and makes no sense... Like reading that Batman is one of the smartest people in the world but does not use all the alien technology (Kryptonian, Thanagarian, everything in the Green Lantern Ring database) that he could access, or that he decided that spending millions in custom made gear/vehicles is a cheaper/faster way to help Gotham than using all possible money for charity, better police salaries/equipment, improving hospitals/sanitation, etc.

                                        Retconning the Technocracy portrayals as Tradition propaganda, still antagonistic, was very mature.



                                        Consensual reality in Mage
                                        "Most people believe in Science, therefore it works and it is KILLING MAGICK,"

                                        It was not a bad idea per se, but the implementation made the setting lose verisimilitude, "what (most) people believe is reality" makes no sense if you are aware that a vast number of the world inhabitants are superstitious and/or lacking access to technology; more people in America believe in angels and faith healing than in vaccine efficacy; or consider the religiosity of Asia, Africa and Latin America, where the VAST majority of world population is located.

                                        It showed writers with limited world knowledge, (and I agree with a previous poster that the naming in Mexico City by Night was shameful, a high-school teacher of Spanish could have corrected all the grammatical errors).



                                        Inconsistent mechanics and no crossover compatibility.
                                        "Your rules don't apply to me."

                                        I am not talking about power balance, I am talking of things like: a lot of Vampire powers depend on Virtues or Morality, which the other lines do not have; or having potential difficulties above 10, but no clear answer for that scenario (Auto failure? Max difficulty always 10? Extra successes needed?)



                                        Werewolf rank increases.
                                        "I must do what society thinks or else."

                                        I read once that Rank was not a power trait, it was the most difficult Status background to raise.



                                        Attributes for Spirits (mainly Rage but others too)
                                        "Everyone has an attitude problem."

                                        Makes no sense a Rage attribute for a spirit of peace/libraries/factories, far batter implemented with CofD Rank/Essence/Power/Finesse/Resistance values.



                                        WoD: Gypsies, most of Kindred of the East cultural implications, and the usage of the word Métis
                                        Seriously? Even on a decade lacking much in the area of cultural sensibility, those were crass.
