    Well, the core difference between Demons and Mages (or DA Fae/Hsien) is that the latter's powers are systematically broken due to being arbitrarily versatile and in some cases scalable (Mage rituals, etc.). Demons are still loaded with a bunch of rigid powers some of which happen to be good- they don't intrinsically have the ability to invent novel supernatural solutions for any given problem. I did acknowledge that Forge 1 specifically was broken. I don't seem to recall powers yielding consistent stun-locking or one-shotting (other than using the insta-kill chance Slayer power- combining the High Torment form with Majesty as an Earthbound to theoretically kill every living thing within thousands of miles always seemed like the most entertaining illustration of the power they were supposed to wield). Some of the Lores I do recall as being extraordinarily anaemic (the wind-cutting thing, Lore of Chaos 1, etc.).

    I'm also not clear on how you plan to get multiple Faith 3+ Thralls- I don't recall actually converting Thralls being a trivial process, and those with Faith above 1-2 are supposed to be extremely rare in the mortal population if memory serves. Mass-ghouling seems a fair bit easier. That said, endowing your Thralls with +20 freebies or whatever it was per Faith point always seemed like the most broken trait for raising an army- turn every random joe into a highly trained killer overnight!

                        Firmament 4 is some strange permutation of Potence moreso than it is an analog to celerity (you generate automatic damage successes which can then be 'split among multiple target')- the main celerity like aspect is that it can increase movement speed. It certainly isn't analogous to making multiple full dice pool attacks per round (Devil's Due does provide the opportunity to purchase a directly analogous power)..

                        Demonic immunity is worded specifically around mind control. Auspex 4 should still work (and in Devil's Due, the somewhat analogous Daimoinon Sense the Sin power works just fine against demons). The wording of the ST guide in regards to Vampiric mind control is fixated entirely on Dominate, so it's ambiguous as to whether some of the more subtle Presence effects and the like fall strictly under 'mind control' as Demon considers it. Illusion resistance still requires a roll so Obfuscate and Chimerestry still have a finite chance of working (although the 'social superiority' argument is one generally leveraged in Vampires vs. Garou/Mages, not in respect to Demons who have similar faculties and dependencies).

                        Breaking the 'Masquerade' still has consequences in terms of attracting attention from hunters and the wrong kinds of supernatural depending on crossover density. Ease of Reaping/Thrall generation is still counterbalanced by the increased exposure to scrutiny and potential threat occaisoned by any supernatural going for massive exposure.

                                    Don't you lose permanent Faith whenever your host dies? That stuff is not exactly cheap and it can erode down to zero (which I think is indeed game over). You can't just keep exposing yourself with every iteration whenever tactically useful without real risk. Not to mention getting soulsucked by an opportunistic peer after getting backed into a corner, or just the ever-present risk of getting dragged back into the Abyss given sufficiently bad luck for who knows how long (quite possibly long enough for the world to end before you get out again, if you are playing Demon in the "leadup to the apocalypse" context it was originally written in).

                                    But yes, I do concede that Fallen demons can get really terrifying given the right combination of lopsidedly powerful Lores (I somehow did wind up only reading through the Lores that were "cool and thematically interesting but not usually earthshattering on a tactical or practical scale" back when I skimmed through those mechanics...). I mean, the Celestials 4 High Torment Spirit Bomb thing can instantly wipe out or cripple an entire coterie with a good roll... That said, the ability of a Demon to do broken things does hinge specifically on certain particular power/form sets, rather than on something pervasive throughout all members of the splat (a 'vampire' or a 'werewolf' will always have a certain scalability of physical threat regardless of nature- likewise, Magi and Fae have powersets that are universally hyper-flexible and thus will always find a way to do something broken or crazy regardless of the exact combination short of willfully gimping yourself). At the end of the day, like Obtenebration and Celerity in vampire (which do represent a qualitative departure from the typical power level range of vampires in their VtM R/V20 forms), it's feasible enough to mechanically hack and tweak the things that will render older PCs unfeasibly broken if you want to start with experienced characters and are afraid of people beelining the most exploitable abilities.

That said, the actual power level depends heavily on Lore focus and Form design- Demons really lack the core scalability of Crinos's fixed benefits and innate fast healing, the Kiss (Revelation in the corebook is a piss weak mechanic unless you've dumped everything into Faith for months or years of sessions) and blood buffing and guns-as-bashing-which-is-halved, or flexible powers that always give you something to do- it's not like you get a dozen lore dots at character creation, after all and the apocalyptic form design rules impose some painful constraints, so it's quite possible to end up with demons rather mediocre compared to other supernaturals.
