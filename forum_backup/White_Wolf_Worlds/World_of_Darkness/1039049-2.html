        Well, quite simply put, If it isn't a wonder, then it's going to run out of juice eventually. I'd say each success you put into your blanket would be split between potency and Duration. When duration runs out, the whole thing becomes inert. Potency will determine how many levels it will be able to heal in total. Once that runs out the entire thing becomes inert. Then you'll have to recast.

        Also, you can turn Lethal into Bashin coincidentally, just cast it before you examine the wound. "oh look, it was just a flesh wound".

        Mage Revised: pp.168: "Note that when a mage alters or heals a Pattern, she does so to the limit of her capabilities. If a mage pulls off a Life Effect, she cannot recast the same Effect for more successes until the Pattern has undergone some natural change (that is, a change of scene). Thus, the mage can transform herself and then change differently or change back, but if she heals an injury or causes a wound in a given scene, she cannot use that power on the same subject again - she's done all that she can to the Pattern at the time."

        Keepers of the Wyck: A Chronicle I'm running FINALE: Chapter 39: Green Fairy

                Originally posted by tzizimine View Post
                Is that same rule in M20? It looks like it was omitted (sorry don't have a copy of Revised).
                I'm having trouble finding it, but then M20's Sphere chapter leaves a lot to be desired. There is nowhere in the book that discusses how Healing works, and HDYDT assumes that because it's in the Common Magickal Feats table it's not worth discussing.

                However, there is a similar passage in Mage 2e, which leads me to believe that this is an intended limitation of the Life sphere, and its omission from M20 was not to remove it.

                Mage 2e pp.202: "Each attempt at healing a Pattern assumes that the mage does as much restorative work as she is able. Repeating the Effect will not heal more damage. At the Storyteller's discretion, the mage may attempt to fix more Pattern damage after the creature has had sufficient time to heal. Any new damage suffered by the subject can also be treated by the mage. Some sorcerers have been known to cure a little bit of damage, cause more and then try again. Of course, these same mages tend to botch this Effect and cause immense damage."

                I'd say unless you can find somewhere where it explicitly states there is no limit to Heals, that this limitation carries over from the previous games.

                                    Originally posted by Saikou
                                    "Each attempt at healing a Pattern assumes that the mage does as much restorative work as she is able. Repeating the Effect will not heal more damage. At the Storyteller's discretion, the mage may attempt to fix more Pattern damage after the creature has had sufficient time to heal. Any new damage suffered by the subject can also be treated by the mage. Some sorcerers have been known to cure a little bit of damage, cause more and then try again. Of course, these same mages tend to botch this Effect and cause immense damage."
                                    To say that one mage "does as much restorative work as she is able" per each attempt at healing a Pattern doesn't mean that another, more powerful, mage shouldn't be able to heal more. On the contrary, one would assume that a more powerful mage it's able to heal more than a weaker one. In other words, the rule assumes that the mage did as much restorative work as she is able, that's why she can't heal more, not because the Pattern it's "locked" it never assumes that another mage can't do more

                                    One could argue that a mage can refuse to do "as much restorative work as she is able" (you get 3 successes, but decide that you don't want to heal the guy completely. One would assume that mages, who are supposed to be able to do anything, can choose to do this), thus being able to recast the heal (but only up to what she would have healed with 3 successes). To show how those words can be interpreted in a different way.

                                    Since the rule it's careful to mention that the mage that it's casting the effect it's doing so at the limit of her capabilities, and does not state that another mage shouldn't be able to try the effect, then it should be understood that these limits are those of the mage that casted the effect in the first place. Otherwise, yes, you could heal a single point of damage of a fatal wound to impede other mages from healing someone, or increase a mage's Strength 1 point, so the opponent can't increase it further. Which would be a oddly limmiting rule that would seem contrary to the spirit of what's written.

                                    In my opinion.
