                                Originally posted by James_Willoughby View Post

                                But, as I have pointed out before, this makes it into Mithras's story, rather than the players' story, and, really, this is the completely wrong focus for a story or chronicle.

                                One of the interesting developments in the World of Darkness as a publishing phenomenon is a shift, in the late 1990s, in White Wolf's intended audience. Prior to the revised edition of Vampire: the Masquerade, there was an expectation of a broad audience. Lots of players were expected to buy a few books, based upon their interests, and there was no great attempt to build a running narrative. Books were designed to be focused primarily on the player characters; you will notice in the early stories published for the game (Alien Hunger, Blood Bond, whatever) there are basically no big-name NPCs. Even the Diablerie series, which sees the appearance of a powerful Methuselah in each book, do not have much in the way of larger consequences through the survival, or otherwise, of their supporting case.

                                This shifts massively with the revised edition. There seems to be an expectation that there are fewer people purchasing books, but that these are not players, but fans, and they are expected to be buying every book. All of a sudden we have signature characters. We have random bits of plot stuck in the back of books (for some fans, Lucita's defection to the Sabbat at the back of Midnight Siege was a big event; for people like me it was when we stopped bothering to buy books). Above all, published stories became slide shows where the actions of the players were inconsequential, and effectively fans were expected to be excited by witnessing important events in the game. The nadir of this trend is the Baba Yaga adventure in Nights of Prophecy, where the players have not one single meaningful choice. But it doesn't matter; they get to see the death of Baba Yaga, which, apparently, is the important bit.

                                Fall of London takes all the wrong lessons from earlier incarnations of Vampire: the Masquerade. I don't care about what happens to Mithras, and I suspect that very few people other than the superfans do either. I don't even understand why anyone should care, unless we are treating these not as game supplements, but as (bad) novels masquerading as game supplements.

                                One of the best adventures released for Vampire is Clash of Wills, which is arguably the last story published before the trend of catering to fans took off. There aren't any famous characters, pretty much everyone dies, and the events are inconsequential for the wider world. And it's fun. I wish the writers for the game would actually remember to focus on that last bit.

                                            Originally posted by Black Fox View Post

                                            The problem as I see it is that while stories have endings, Vampire the Masquerade was not a story. It was a roleplaying game with the World of Darkness as its setting. The stories that have endings would be the individual chronicles that the gamemaster and players created at their game table. And people will always have more stories to create as long as they and their friends have ideas. Simply because Justin Achilli had one story he wanted to tell and created a metaplot to force down everyone's throats does not mean anyone else wanted that story. Obviously they didn't.

                                            Why wouldn't people who liked playing a game where the protagonists are vampires in 1991, 1994, 2000, 2004, and any number of historical settings not want to play stories set "today" when today becomes 2008, 2011, 2014, 2023, and so on?

                                            Stories end. Settings go on. There's very few stories out there where we know the world itself ends simply because we're on the last page.

                                            There was no reason why VtM or the other WoD gamelines had to become a zombie franchise. New people are entering the hobby and enjoying the genre every year. Most people on this forum, I believe, were not around - perhaps not even born - when my friends and I first played VtM back in 1992. Companies still sell the original Stratego, Risk, Monopoly, Clue, and many other boardgames. They just update the marketing and trade design every so often. And while there are spin offs from some of these games tying them into other franchises (the various Monopoly games that simply rename the street names), it's understood that these are merely collectible versions, not replacements.

                                            The idea that RPGs and their settings are essentially fiction written by the game developers to be read passively by players rather than a game that tells an emergent story collaboratively created by gamemasters and players has been very destructive to the hobby. We have entire sourcebooks that aren't actually meant to be used by gamemasters in play, but passively consumed like it was fiction. I understand some people really like this "form" of fiction, but in my experience it alienates a whole lot more people than it attracts.

                                            Metaplot kills gamelines. Having updated settings just to update settings and try to force people to buy new sourcebooks just pisses people off because there is no value for their money. This does not mean that the chronology of the setting has to remain static. People will always set new chronicles in more recent years. It doesn't have to remain in 1991 forever. But that doesn't mean in terms of vampire society that 2024 has to be different from 1991 in a way that 1991 was different from 1900, 1848, 1776, 1648, or 1525. No reason to start moving clans around sects and other fundamental alterations of the setting that both Revised and V5 have done.

                                            I am willing to carve out partial exemptions to time-limited "mystery bombs" that were meant to go off during the production of the game. The mystery behind the origins of the Devil Kindred, their predecessors, the Antedeluvian they killed, etc. was always something (I think) that was meant to be resolved. So after introducing the Cappadocans in the Giovanni Chronicles and Vampire: the Dark Ages, something like the Hecata was always inevitable. Recognizing that there were many other Cappadocian survivors and their childer than just the Giovanni was OK. Something like the Hecata simply brings the various strands together in a more coherent and universal framework like how the Revised corebook initially just brought EVERYTHING together into one corebook so people didn't have to buy five separate books anymore just to have all the clans and various options.
                                            I have a very different view on metaplot. Forgive me if I go on a bit of a rant.

                                            A bit more explanation but a simple question for any person who wants to talk about metaplot. "Do you give a shit about the characters in your favorite media?" This is the most fundamental question you can ask about any property you have any investment in. Do you care about Spider-Man? Do you care about Mary Jane? Do you have any investment in Iron Man or Captain America or Wolverine or so on? What about Harry Potter or Katniss Everdeen. They are fictional characters and nothing that happens to them matters. HOWEVER, it is a story that you must fundamentally have BUY IN for if you want to buy anything.

                                            I CARE about the characters of Vampire: The Masquerade.

                                            I care about Lucita, I care about Theo Bell, I care about Victoria Ash, and I care about Lodin and Modius. So much so that I wrote an adventure to wrap up the characters in Gary for Storyteller's Vault and have played HUNDREDS of stories involving them with my player characters. They are not as real to me as real people but they are toys that I have played with and I want to see/read/hear more about them.

                                            I don't need rituals, I don't need Disciplines, and i certainly don't need books about new Clans.

                                            I am here because the game world as established in Legacy/OWOD has enough characters that I want to know more about what they've been up to for the past 20 years. I *LIKE* The Old World of Darkness because I like the characters that live in it. I don't play it because of the system, I play it for the world that has been created and the chance to play around in it.

                                            It's why I fundamentally enjoy Beckett's Jyhad DIary more than any other book ever written because it is the book of, "Hey, remenber these guys?" Without metaplot, the characters are frozen and static and the supplements don't provide you anything other than empty rules. It's why I've never really cared about Eberron versus, say, Dragonlance or Forgotten Realms.

                                            "What is Raistlin up to?" is a question far more press than King Boranal. Because King Boranal is the same place he was a decade ago. Because Eberron's timeline doesn't advance.

                                                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                White Wolf/Paradox seem to be going away from heavy continuity and trying to distance themselves from the Old World of Darkness (naming it "Legacy") just like they did Chronicles of Darkness.

                                                This is a fucking terrible idea and I hope they reverse course rapidly.

                                                We don't need a THIRD World of Darkness.
                                                I'm usually a lurker, but I've been wanting to post on this specific topic for a couple days now. What you're saying is part of the reason. I'm going to try and keep my post as short as I can because I could write a whole darn essay on this. Just the important information: I was brought into the VTM fandom via V5, I've never played a game outside of that edition, and I frequent multiple WoD communities to try and get an all-around view of fan opinions.

                                                V5 had me in the first half. I enjoyed the Corebook, played Bloodlines and proceeded to buy every book that was currently out (Chicago by Night was the latest). I went on wiki pages and fell in love with the lore. I know not everyone is going to do as I do, but it's not nearly as bone-crushing a feat like I see it made out to be. Unless you're trying to be a true VTM scholar you can get the gist of a lot of things through wiki-surfing and asking older players.

                                                So that's why I'm always really surprised when people get so frustrated with old lore. The whole "Make up your own thing" is IMO evident in any TTRPG you play, but lore should be there for people who don't want to do that, also, it's much easier to drop something than make something up from scratch. When I first got into the V5 fandom people did dismiss some older stuff, but they were the most commonly dismissed content (ie. the Kuei Jin). still they enjoyed a lot of legacy and waited with bated breath for new releases old material. Then this odd shift started happening where there was, the best way I can put it, hostility towards old lore among some fans. This hostility is very odd to me for several reasons, but the biggest is that a lot of these players seemed just fine with old lore until-well, rather recently?

                                                Paradox distancing themselves from old lore came as a comment in the official Discord server via the pipelines. The exact comment was something like: "The game is imagined as a complete reboot now, if we don't reference something it's not true in-lore anymore." I can't even say this is absolute truth. Like, it might be true technically now, but right at the same time, they released the DoC loresheet. We still had Bloodhunt kicking and dropping old names, and they've continued to reintroduce old (albeit with new tweaks/definitions ie. Gargoyles) powers and concepts. It's very much a "Trying to have your cake and eat it too" situation.

                                                They say they desire to distance themselves, but at this point that's impossible. You don't really get to say "No, nothing matters anymore" when that wasn't and had not been the concept. When you still expect a certain level of old player onboarding with newer players because if lore doesn't matter, why are you name-dropping things that only legacy players have an understanding of? Why aren't you explaining in detail those things if you want to bring them back? No new player cares about Gargoyles, so why did you name a new line of ghouls after them, and then talk about their legacy history in a curtly? Why do I care about Custos being the childe of Beckett?Why did you release the Book of Nod PDF? Because you're using an old gameline's history to sell your current edition.

                                                VTM is VTM because it's VTM. I could have played other vampire games, but chose this one because I was intrigued by its history. My group which also consists of new players feels similar. Hell, my friends who have never played think the choice to remove old lore is odd. V5 is my first time coming across such an obvious attempt at fracturing a fanbase. Ad if embracing the old will ruin your chances for the new. It's confounding.

                                                I still really like V5, but I don't like its current direction. If you aren't going to include "old lore" then write me up a new city book please, because the only reference I have is steeped in the old lore. Do the work it requires for having a "new" line. Give me full explanations and more tools since you don't seem to want me to use the old ones previously suggested. Just my current thoughts as a V5 player.
