    Originally posted by Ambrosia View Post


    While I *wish* it could properly be done this way (It would be awesome and save on so many headaches), you run into a problem you don't get with contradicting religions: Each of the cosmologies in the respective gamelines is, mostly, treated as what actually happened. And if you try to do a neutral crossover, you suddenly get a mess.

    Who created the world: Gaia or God?
    Are Vampires under God's punishment, or are they actually Wyrm creatures?
    If the Wyrm is trying to destroy the world, why isn't God smacking it with a 'Leave my creation alone?'. Or is it actually Gaia's creation, and God is just a bystander to the whole creation shebang? Wait, how does any of it make sense if the Mage's view of 'Reality is shaped by human belief' is true? Or are the Mages wrong? But how can they be wrong, if their whole fight and history is about forcing belief into reality? Did God make the Fae? and how come the Triad decided to let Asia have its whole own 1000 hells? Or did those get created because the Mages are right, and the beliefs of Humans created them all? AAAAAAAAAAA-

    You *can*, with effort, ducttape-and-glue something together that somewhat somehow makes sense out of all these things - but you can't taking each lore fully as-written as a whole and just put them together. :|

    It all can actually make sense if you word it right.

    Who created the world: Gaia or God?
    God. Gaia is Celestial, just like Helios and Luna. In fact, I recall there's even one of the BSD endings to W:tA games where Wyrm has won and BSD actually literally find her, and.. well, bad things happen, including rape and murder.

    Are Vampires under God's punishment, or are they actually Wyrm creatures?
    Under God's punishment. And /as result/ of that, once the "death" of vampires has come, Wyrm the Unmaker has swallowed them - but he discovered that unlike every living thing, they did not die, instead biting the Wyrm right in the gut. Screaming in pain, the Wyrm spat them out, unable to bring their final hour. (It is actually explanation from the books, but I do not remember pages for direct quotes. Though, I can ask my friend who actually has them).

    If the Wyrm is trying to destroy the world, why isn't God smacking it with a 'Leave my creation alone?'
    Because God abandoned Creation and humanity. That's pretty much it.

    Or is it actually Gaia's creation, and God is just a bystander to the whole creation shebang?
    God created it, Gaia is "only" a Celestial.

    Wait, how does any of it make sense if the Mage's view of 'Reality is shaped by human belief' is true? Or are the Mages wrong? But how can they be wrong, if their whole fight and history is about forcing belief into reality?
    *Prepares his mage pants*

    Each human soul is quite literally a reflection of God's soul, as described in the Demon book (Demons and Mages have quite a connection in their lores if you read it right). When it is said that God created humans to be similar to their Creator, it is not implied they're similar in their flesh or strength - it is all about soul, and that's why Demons cannot enslave mortals that easily, and only Earthbound can (which is twisted version of Demon, and not the original Lores). The only exception is becoming a Thrall, to which mortal has to willingly agree.

    But back to original question.

    You see, each human soul represents a God, quite literally. Awakening is said to be "waking up the inner god" for a reason. Humans passively shape reality around them, Awakened or not, and that's why phrase "You Will and You Do" (- from M20) fits this so much.

    Mages are not wrong. Reality is indeed shaped by belief, because each human souls is very similar to God's, as Creator made it like that on purpose. (And that's why Spheres can do anything, given enough Arete and ranks. They represent the Creation itself. Just think about it, potentially you can create a whole new universe with power of Spheres if you had enough time and power, and isn't that what God did?)

    Did God make the Fae? and how come the Triad decided to let Asia have its whole own 1000 hells? Or did those get created because the Mages are right, and the beliefs of Humans created them all?
    Now, I admit, I don't really know Fae lore, but I'm sure there's some explanation to them just like Garou have. God created fundament of Creation, and why is it that Gaia spawned Garou, others could not do something similar? I'm sure there's something or someone to create Fae, if only I had time to read that book.

    As for hells, there are countless of Realms existing out there, and many are holding on just because humans believe in that. Why not let it? And could they stop it even? Or did they care?

    It all can make sense if you actually look at it under right angle. I was sceptical about this as well, but realised that lores are tied much closer than you might think.

        I only run Mage anyways, so everything is just a different manifestation of the Mage cosmology in my games.

        But if I were to actually do other games than mage (shudder), then I'd carefully tailor everything to fit that game specifically.

        Example:

        Werewolf:

        Vampires are feeding mouths of the Wyrm, step by step eating the world slowly, tearing it's life essence into the gullet of the Wyrm through thousand, metaphorical lamprey-mouths. They function mechanically like dead people possessed by Banes, build them like Formori with a bunch of Spirit Charms themed after being vampires and possessing a hunger like fire.

        Mages are mortals who have pledged themselves entirely to the worldview of a single Celestine, such as Weaver-Mages that work their magic through technology and order or Wyld-Mages that work their magic through life and creativity. They are pledged to a very little aspect of a Celestine and generally work for more important people such as Black Spiral Dancers, the Glass Walkers likewise have a few business mages. There is no Technocratic Union or Council of Nine Mystic Traditions, only a bunch of mortal magic-users with Spirit Charms.

        Wraith and Orpheus are perfect as written.

        I don't use Changeling or Demon.

                                Originally posted by Aya Tari View Post
                                Part of the problem is the nature of God. The God of Abrahamic religions did not really exist until after the Babylonian Exile, when the Jews, influenced by the exposure to Zorastrian ideology, rejected their Pantheon to create a two diety religion. YHWH became the 'good' god while Lucifer became the 'bad' god. Instead of having two equal gods, however, the Jews made to 'good' god superior to the 'bad god, which has led to over 2500 years of argument of why the 'good' god lets the 'bad' god do bad things. Presumably, the eldest vampires remember the change and the Mokole can access Mnesis to remember the change.

                                Werewolf possesses the broadest, the oldest, and the most internally logical mythology (though individual groups have their distortions and their biases). Even so, there is no evidence that any of the Changing Breeds have left the Solar System, so their mythologies are decidedly local. I like to think that Gaia is merely the local manifestation of the Universe while the Triat are local manifestations of the corresponding universal principles. Further, I like to think that the Solar System is experiencing a quarantine from the outside to prevent the corruption of its local manifestations from spreading. Just my two cents...
                                Sort of, but not quite. The ancient Israelites never worshiped a "pantheon" and were never polytheistic in the way we would use the term now. What you're referring to is a monolatrous or henotheistic religion, in which the deities of other groups they interacted with were acknowledged as being real, but their god was better and cooler than the others and the only one they actually worshiped. This changes during the Babylonian exile, partly due to Zoroastrian influence as you mention but that is often greatly overstated. The conversation that takes place during this time in Judaism centers on the question of how they could have come to this calamity if they enjoyed God's protection and the gods of other nations were inferior, the theological result of which was the idea that other gods were not real and God was capable of acting through other nations to punish his chosen people for failing in their faith. The concept of "the adversary" does also begin to take shape during this time, but it bears little resemblance to either the Zoroastrian Angra Mainyu or the Christian Devil. The Devil is actually way more complicated theologically and honestly doesn't form into anything we would recognize until the advent of Christianity many centuries later.

                                The problem with Werewolf's mythology is that is incredibly contemporary. It comes directly from a late twentieth century culture marinating in post-modernism, neo-shamanism, romantische naturphilosophie, Western reinterpretations of Eastern and indigenous religions, and an assortment of reactions to modern scientific thought. It works great as a fantasy setting, but it doesn't really function as any sort of believable mythology.
