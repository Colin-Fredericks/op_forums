      (Crossposted from RPGnet)

      Welcome aboard!

      I don't recall seeing any - the creatures in question have stereotypes about Mages in their own books.

      The Kickstarter backers' preview of Mage: The Ascension 20th Anniversary Edition mentions Mummies in passing as among those creatures not counting as mundane witnesses. My guess would be that the Traditions would generally find them very interesting, as the result of powerful and largely lost magical effects. Learning-based Traditions like the Hermetics would like to talk to them, the Euthanatoi would be fascinated by their first-hand observations on the cycle of resurrection... and the Etherites would want to take them apart and see how they work, but might have the sense not to admit it.

      The recently escaped demons from Demon: The Fallen aren't mentioned specifically, just "classic" demons (DtF's Earthbound and summoned demons, not connected to a human life) and the Nephandi, who are referred to as the Fallen as well. The breaking of Hell would be a major metaplot event for a game where demon-summoners are a major enemy, so it seems it is not considered to have happened.

              Originally posted by Lian View Post
              Are mages of various stripes going to see a vast difference between Changelings and The Fallen? Both Umbrood who steal bodies, both feed in specialized fashion on human emotions to gain quintessence,Changelings just seem more fragile.
              Almost certainly . . .for one thing, Changelings aren't Demons. For better or worse, the fae are born of human hopes, dreams, and fantasies (both good and bad). So, in a very real sense, the fae are the children of humanity . . .which means they "belong". That doesn't mean they're all good (there's an old saying that the fae are "too good for Hell, but not good enough for heaven) . . . but they're at least neutral. Traditions like the Verbena have long histories of working with changelings, and there are canon examples of Tradition mages sharing nodes/freeholds with changelings (such as the one on the campus of the University of California, Santa Cruz).

              Demons . . . well, they're outright enemies of God. Many are evil, most still want to wage war against heaven, and technically, they're supposed to be in hell. Groups like the Celestial Chorus are going to have a big, big problem with that. And most of the rest are going to be leery.

              Of course, the Technocracy isn't going to be happy with either group . . . but since when is the Technocracy happy with any supernatural creature?

              And on a side note - only non-Scathach sidhe "steal" bodies. Commoner changelings, and most sidhe of House Scathach, are symbiotes, born when a fae soul bonds with an unborn human one. Fusion, not possession.

                  Originally posted by Jeb View Post
                  . . .There are some traditionalist stereotypes you can find in revised MtA corebook: . . . But what about demons and mummies? Are there any canonical stereotypes, If "yes", where can I find it? In what book?
                  If "no", can you provide your own middle-of-the-road traditionalist views (stereotypes) for the Fallen and Mummies? What Traditionalists know about them? What do they feel about them?
                  Well, to start, mages and mummies have a shared history. It takes a true (i.e. awakened) mage to perform the Great Rite, the ritual which creates a mummy. And while most mummies are ancient, new ones have been created throughout history - the most recent one (known) was a British member of the Arcanum (a sleeper occult society), during the Victorian Era.

                  So, if I had to guess, I'd say the Celestial Chorus and the Order of Hermes are the most likely to know much about mummies. Both groups have strong ties to ancient Egypt, and ancient Egyptian mummies are one of the most numerous groups (Horus, the second known Egyptian mummy, arranged to mass produce more for his war against Set). So its likely that members of those Traditions have bumped into mummies, at some point in history. Heck, some of the (ancient) members may even have helped create a mummy.

                  Beyond that, I'd have to guess that the Euthanatos and Dreamspeakers are the most likely to have run across them. I suspect many Euthanatos (Euthantoi?) would be bothered by the idea of beings that can't stay dead (even if they want to). And Dreamspeakers may well have bumped into some of the Incan or other pre-Columbian mummies.

                  As for how the Traditions think of mummies . . . they probably default to the Egyptian stereotype (which - according to 2nd edition Mummy - annoys the heck out of non-Egyptian mummies). Deceased royalty, cursed tombs, canopic jars. . . assuming they even know they exist. Many Traditionalists probably don't believe there are any real (magic) mummies out there. There just aren't that many mummies, after all - only about a hundred Egyptian mummies, for example - so the average mage is unlikely to ever meet one, or see any evidence of their existence.

                  Are there any possibilities for contacts and maybe even cooperation?

                  Thanks a lot!!!
                  Definitely. For one thing, many mummies were mages in life. And many of the others have developed a strong interest in the occult, down through the millennia. And that's a lot of time to bump into the other factions of the World of Darkness.

                  Plus, many Egyptian mummies believe its their duty to oppose the forces of Apophis. That includes the Followers of Set, demons, anything Wyrm-tainted . . . and the Nephandi So right there is an opportunity for crossovers. Mummy magic isn't as flexible as mage magic. but it is Paradox-free. And being immortal certainly doesn't hurt. . .

                  Also, if a mummy ever wants to stop being a mummy, and proceed to a normal afterlife, mage magic is their only real hope. There's a level five mummy power that allows them to annihilate each other . . . but that's not a good way out. And even de facto immortality can get old, after several thousand years.

                      Mummies are very, very rare in the WoD, and their game line is a very small one with a total of 3 books, one for each major revision era of the Game. They are rarely mentioned in the books, and when they are they usually get a line or two along the line of "mummies, give me a break" in the misc supernatural heading in the splatbbooks. But you will hear the most about them in the Year of the Scarab books (Lost Paths, Cairo By Night, Rage accross Egypt). There is some additional backstory and tales about their fights with the Setites in the Setite Clanbook.

                      I can try and write something up out of my head for later, but you can safely assume that most Supernaturals will have never heard of the Mummies and would most likely make Universal Monster jokes about them.

                      There is a lot about demons in all their forms in all the game lines - devils, demons, banes, horrors from beyond, cthonian monstrosities, maras, asuras, rakshasas... But none of them are the Fallen. Worse yet, the Fallen don't fall into any category of demonic beings recognized by the Mages. The Fallen came in a very strange time for White Wolf and they were written in a way that invalidated the plot and core concepts of numerous gamelines just to gain some extra heat by positing the ultimate explanation of the WoD. Suffice to say that stance didn't really do much for the other game lines, and they never really bothered including them. So... you will be quite hard pressed to find anything official on them, and I personally don't want to waste time on a gameline that makes my gut churn, so while I'll try and cook something up for the Mummies, someone else will have to help you with the Fallen.

                              There are actually 4 Mummy books. Two for the first two editions of Vampire. Then two more hardbacks when it was its own "line".

                              Some among the Wu Lung probably know about the Chinese alchemical mummies. Similarly, there are some Kuei-jin who would be aware of them as well. (And probably have very mixed feelings about them... hate/fear/awe/yearning and a whole ton of jealousy).

                              The Cabiri, the mummies using the Egyptian spell after Horus said "Enough" are going to function like the 2nd Ed mummies compared to what happened to almost all the Egyptian mummies after the LGM. There are a few Egyptian mummies who have ignored the order to join up, the Ishmaelites as well.

                              The Euthanatos view on mummies is probably going to depend on how ecumenical they are. If the mummies are considered in light of their own cycle, they aren't behaving all that badly. In fact, they might be seen as embodying some concepts in a very positive way. Preservation of identity across incarcerations is a sign of great spiritual development. Some might even see them as minor "Bodhisattva" types.

Both the fae and demons are spirit beings from other planes of existence, which have trouble manifesting on Earth independent of a human body. Coupled with the fact that some creatures are neither "fish nor fowl", straddling the line between two or more worlds. . . Orzobal the Griffin (from Freeholds and Hidden Glens), for example, is somewhere between traditional Umbrood and chimera. Originally a condor spirit, Orzobal found the Spanish stories of griffins inspiring, and claimed that form for his own. And in doing, opened himself up to feeding on Glamour, as well as gnosis.

                                  Meanwhile, Plato the Banana Slug has gone the other way. A chimera created by the students of UC Santa Cruz, he's evolved into the zeitgeist of the university. Now a spirit being that cannot be destroyed as long as the university exists, he has literally become the embodiment of the UC Santa Cruz community (faculty, staff, and students).

                                  In light of that, you could argue the point. Neither the fae nor the demons would likely agree . . . but it is a defensible claim.

                                              Another option for a crossover - particularly if you're running a Gehenna/Apocalypse/Reckoning campaign - would be a restored Osirian League. Shortly after Horus (Heru) rose as a mummy for the first time, he assembled a cross-splat resistance force named for his late father. Mummies and humans, of course, but also werewolves and other shapeshifters, mages, and more (even some vampires) participated in the effort to oust Set.

                                              The alliance has kind of fallen apart over the millennia, but in a "Final Days" campaign, Horus would likely try to reassemble the League, to wage one last war for the sake of Maat (i.e. the cause of good). And with something like 8,000 years of life under his belt (Horus is almost as old as the Antediluvians), god only knows what sorts of supernaturals he'd be able to rally to his banner.
