                  Originally posted by Yumlum View Post
                  I've readed some of people opinion on WoD, and it seem that the setting seem to be much more on the power fantasy side than CofD, but is this how the game is represented?
                  It's not how the games are represented... but it is side effect of how the games are as a total product.

                  The intent of the WoD is for the PCs to feel powerful - they are monsters and all - but for the costs to ultimately outweigh the positives to make it something that doesn't qualify as "power fantasy" and instead wants to be an urban fantasy game with powerful characters in it. But the implementation of that has been... spotty over the decades. So the WoD is very easy to play as a power fantasy for people that want to do that, even if that's not what the books were aiming for.

                  One of the whole reasons behind some of the changes in the CofD and why it was originally meant to replace the WoD, was to "fix" that. To make the downsides of being a supernatural sting more so the game didn't slide into power fantasy as easily. There were of course costs to that, which a lot of people didn't think were worth it even if they didn't want to play the WoD as a power fantasy either.

                                      The problem is most of the examples of "high power level" listed are either things PCs can only achieve after a lot of play (as many have in-game requirements beyond just earning enough XP and many of those requirements are in the ST's hands), or are NPC only material.

                                      The issue with the WoD vs the CofD here, is that the WoD can easily accidentally slide into power fantasy without meaning to because you happen to go down the right mechanical path. CofD requires you to be more purposeful if you want to go there (this isn't a bad thing, there's a published 1e CofD book with a whole ton of optional rules in it all about amped up power levels of the PCs in it).

                                      Power fantasy isn't really about the relative power of entities in different settings because it's the power within the setting that makes it a power fantasy.

                                      You can have a power fantasy in a zombie apocalypse where the PCs are all technically humans, even if somehow the ragtag group of survivors are also the most skilled badasses in the world that easily mow down the undead, rebuild society, and so on. The fantasy is the ability to exercise power that IRL you don't have. In video games, there are games where you're playing a normal human soldier that has to be careful because one stray bullet could end a mission as they're "realistically" fragile (they're not when it comes to stabilizing and healing damage, but they make the threat of death a lot higher before that can happen) that are fun in their own way, and there are video games where you're playing a "normal human" soldier that makes Captain America look like a punk as they take on mecha-Hitler and all his goons by himself. Wolfenstein is also fun, but it's certainly a lot more power fantasy than most of the WWII set Call of Duty games are.

                                          High end CofD is cosmic level, and players have a lot of control on what they're doing. Not only that, a starting Vampire in CofD is undoubtedly more powerful in general than a starting Vampire in WoD.

                                          Unless we have a good starting combo.

                                          And that's the thing. With CofD, the Storyteller has an easier time keeping the power level within whatever constraints they want. Which doubles as a better time keeping the relative power between PCs and NPCs, which is the real point.

                                          The question in the end boils down to how much can the PCs exercise power over the setting without facing serious consequences, that is what a power fantasy is.

                                          WoD has a lot of ways you can amass far more power in practice than you should by design, and a big part of this is access to do things with starting or low XP characters that completely undermine the expected power of NPCs.

                                          But again, that may be a problem in some groups, or for inexperienced Narrators. But as long as everyone plays in good faith, it is simple to avoid traps, or correct course.
