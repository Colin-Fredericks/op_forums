    Hints, warnings and Insta kill powers. Can the latter be used on PCs??

    06-13-2016, 03:13 PM
    On most every gaming related site i go to, i ask a question in the realms of
    When do you (as a gm) feel that giving hints/warnings are appropriate.

    Do you feel that a gm is required/obligated to give them?

    How many do YOU give? How many do you think is required?

    Do you feel that a gm should be required to start the 'ball rolling' by dropping a hint, for the players to pick up on and go into?

    Do you feel that it is the GMs responsibility to warn them/give them hints, when something bad is going to happen to them? What about if they have done nothing to 'get that hint; like say they have an underworld contract out on them. If they have not checked with any of their underworld friends, then, realistically how would they get any warning/hint?

    How many hints do you give before you actually have the 'bad thing' come about?

    Do you still spring the 'bad thing' even if they have not picked up on those hints/warnings???

    As an example of why i am wondering this, I present the following (note the stats for the NPC’s mentioned are using the Star wars D6 game).

    Back in 94-95, during a 14th month long star wars game, the players (6 total, which increased to 7 during the latter 8 months), were having 'fun' harassing a major Hutt. After every time that they messed with his plans (like hijacking a shipment of arms, spice etc) he sent a response. This started off with just gooks. Then went to thugs, major thugs and so on.

    Players have foiled yet another plan of the resident Hutt, and even got to his inner sanctum and had him in a standoff. 2 Pcs with grenade belts standing next to him, while 3 of his guards had TD's on the other pcs. One went, they would all go. Rather than do that, they decided to leave. This humiliation IMO was the last straw for the Hutt. He tapped into his personal funds, and contracted out 2 well known and outstanding snipers to take out the 4 main players in the party (main players, the ones who plotted and overcame his machinations).

    One sniper, had like 9d in sneak, and 11d in firearms. The other was 8d/10d, and both had silenced rifles. First time they struck, it was after the players settled into a routine for almost a month on their home planet. I had both snipers roll investigations and kept track of their rolls, when they were made etc. Had 1 strike, player #2, while the other struck player #7 (2 of the 4 main ones). When they both went down, the game stopped due to the players thinking i was trying to f**k them over. WE spent over 4 hours arguing about it, and even with me explaining, showing them the notes, they still felt that a GM should never have the bad guys do things like that....

    None of the players for the last 2 sessions, which i had building up to this encounter, even checked with ANY of their underworld contacts. And from prior experience, even if they had got a warning from their contacts, they would have not acted on it (as they saw it as a badge of honor).

    So looking at that, and to the questions above..
    1) Did I do right, by having the assassin/snipers strike without warning them?
    2) did I not give enough hinting?
    3) should I have pushed hints onto them even though they did nothing to warrant IMO getting any?
    4) Should I have held off having the snipers strike until AFTER they had bothered to check, and therefore possibly get a warning that they had a hint out on them?

      Table Top RP lives and dies on the trust between players and GM. Much of that trust hinges on the idea that, no matter how bleak things get, the GM has the players enjoyment in mind.

      Being sniped by adversaries you don't see coming (and have no reason to suspect) is fucking awful. It doesn't matter that those snipers had good rolls, it doesn't matter that they were hired by a known NPC who the players had crossed, all that matters is that from the players POV you randomly killed two of them. Robbing them of those characters, truncating the stories and making them fearful that other unseen threats could bonk them whenever you please.

      This was one of those moments when the storyteller has to look at the field, ignoring all rolls, and ask "Is this going to be fun/exciting" if the answer is no, something must be done. Personally, I'd have curtailed the first sniper's damage, have them deal a devastating blow, but not fatal, and give them player a life or death scene where they have to escape an unseen shooter. Second shooter would need a different scene, possibly later when several players can get in on the action, capture him, and suss out who hired him. That would have been a story, and a feather in the cap of the survivor.

      TL;DR: Killing characters with unseen forces is the #1 way to lose the trust of your players.

          Its one of those things that's very difficult to gauge on one hand players are expected to help the story move, on the other at some point their actions have to have consequences.

          Personally I have a sort of house rule I call "Are you sure", basically it works like this: When a new player joins the table I explain this to them, If I ever stop the game and pointedly look at them and say in an even, calm, but forceful tone, "Are you sure you want to do that?" I'm giving the player the warning that what they are going to do is incredibly foolish and dangerous and I will not be responsible for what happens to their character; there will be no fudged dice, no last minute NPC rescue squads, no deus ex machina, and no mercy. I have never had to use this, but its there for when someone has a blatantly suicidal plan (that's not mentioned in jest) which the only outcome I can see is their death; generally this is for things like if the newly embraced Brujah decides he's going to beat the hell out of the Prince in the middle of Elysium. The hope is that if I ever have to use this the player will rethink their decision and choose a less immediately fatal course of action and if it doesn't I have a clear conscience over the result regardless of what happens.

          Your specific example is something different as I would not have invoked that rule since their actions with the hutt weren't as blatantly suicidal or stupid as what I'm suggesting the rule for. Really the only thing I would say is you should have probably had one of their contacts drop a hint something was wrong maybe a day or two before your snipers were gonna make the hit; something like, "We've done good business in the past and as I was going through some 'financial records' I noticed that hutt you dealt with a month back just shifted some massive cred, he's up to something in your backyard." If your players ignored the hint and didn't even start looking into it, then by all means actions have consequences and in future games the story of what happened to those characters is now a cautionary tale of why you don't cross that hutt. The reason I say that is because unless I'm missing something or you skipped over something, to the players it looks like a month of relative down time then the character is dead from a bullet to the brain. You said you kept track of dice rolls and such for the snipers, but if they basically all succeeded the players had no idea what was going on (and just assuming the ST is up to something because they are rolling dice should not be the only hint as that is purely out of game knowledge). As an outsider looking in here is what I think might have gone through your player's heads, "The ST is pissed we messed up his hutt NPC so he made these godlike snipers to hunt us down, and because he didn't "fail" his rolls [which I'm assuming weren't rolled with the players knowledge] we get shot with no chance to defend ourselves; might have just as well said rocks fall everyone dies." Basically it looks like you took unilateral revenge against the characters through ST fiat (again assuming rolls weren't made in front if the players) since it seems like all you have validating your actions to them are notes that boil down to you die because I said so and wrote that on these sheets of paper. Also as far as I can tell the players were never prompted to roll dice on their own behalf throughout this tracking and hunting process (I'm not experienced with the system if I'm missing something mechanically please let me know), which is usually a big no-no since it removes all sense of agency from the players regarding their characters, and even being told to roll dice is not enough of a hint that something is wrong since that still relies on an out of game hint.

                  Originally posted by garhkal View Post
                  So even after all sorts of hints, warnings and such were given, just cause it 'squishes fun' don't have it go off, or neuter it?
                  Yes! The job of the storyteller is to keep the game fun for everyone (yourself included). If a roll or decision causes all of your players to become miserable it is the wrong fucking decision. Bottom line. It doesn't matter that the dice were hot, or that they could theoretically have caught onto the plan, because at the end of it all your players didn't enjoy the experience.

                  Now that doesn't mean you have to hold their hands to avoid threatening them, but there are a lot of things you can do to create tension without killing a character. Crippling wounds, getting knocked unconscious or captured, tweaking damage such that it is injuring without being immediately fatal, the many supernatural debuffs and disadvantages that can be levied. Death should be a last resort.

If the ship is older and doesn't/can't have such features, try to figure out a way to save the other characters while letting the fool suffer the fate of the stupid, in a crash the pilot section takes the initial impact whereas the other sections while damaged survive (as to the other players, injured but alive), in the ship blows up scenario you could say that the other characters where thrown clear and the stupid one was not.                                       For your scenario 1, really your situation is that one player is trying to kill other players' characters. If that's not clear to everyone, then you need to talk more about the consequences of his choice.

                                      So strip away the ship and all the rest. Would you let one PC shoot all the other PCs in the head and then himself? If your group's really into PvP and they've been trying to screw each other throughout the game, no problem. This isn't happening because they never thought the PLAYER would betray the group in such a manner, but because that CHARACTER managed to be cleverer than their character. They never had expectations that the group was fundamentally cooperative and so you're not breaking that expectation by allowing this to happen.

                                      For most games, though, players have that expectation, and allowing one player to basically end the game is unfair to the other players. You can rationalize it however you want in the game, but that doesn't really matter, because you guys aren't really cowboys in the Old West, or wizards in the 11th century, or vampires on the streets of Chicago. You're a group of friends playing a game, and that's more important than the verisimilitude of the game itself.

                                      I feel like that question of verisimilitude is at the heart of all your questions here. You're unsure about stopping the malicious crashing of the Millennium Falcon because any way you stop it in the game will be unrealistic and "deus ex machina". You're surprised at the player's objections to the sniper attacks because you feel they are the most realistic thing that would happen in the game world. You don't want to weaken the "cripple maneuver" when it would kill a PC because there's no ingame reason that the bad guy's maneuver would be weaker than normal.

                                      There's nothing wrong with wanting a realistic game, but you need to set those expectations at the beginning of your game and let folks opt-out then if they want. You might also want to play a game where recovering from death is a possibility, like Eclipse Phase, where dead characters just suffer a monetary penalty for being downloaded into a fresh body.
