      Depends what kind of Technomancer they are.

      If they're an "incorporating tech into my mystic stuff" kind of technomancer, like a Hermetic mage that uses a calculator to make their charts an calculations. A Dreamspeaker that uses a flashlight to search for rifts in the gauntlet, or even a Cultist who uses elctrostimulation to give them the high they seek, then their paradigm is pretty much as it always was, they just have a few tech foci. Some of the more conservative members may think this is a bit perverse, but on the whole it's still mystic magic, just updated a little.

      If it's a "Build my effects like a machine" kind of technomancer, like your of-the-shelf Etherite and Virtual Adept, then it's not that they don't beleive in magic. Some may consider mystic magic as outdated, inefficient, and actually quite messy (especially with those Verbena people bleeding on everything, Yuck). Others might actually really admire mystics for doing magic they way that they do, but they themselves don't know how to invoke the same effects, so instead they build their effects the way they do understand it.

      The fundamental point combines all mages is that, at the end of the day, they are all will workers, even the Technocrats. They are all using their own force of will to shape the universe into what they see as "correct" or "obvious". The only difference between the Technocracy and the Traditions is that the Technocracy found a way to do it so well that even sleepers can start to mess around with it.

      When it comes to Techomancer's foci, (tools if you're an M20 reader), the only reason their machines work is because they believe that they do. Anyone else who tries to use them would only be able to work them if they shared the same beliefs as the creator.

      How might a technomancer react if they see their machines used by someone else and simply not working? Simple, they'd say they're not doing it right, they haven't got the settings properly, the safety is on, the power's not plugged in properly, you're jamming it. Here let me do it!

      If the device was a Wonder, especially a Talisman, then that would be different, even Sleepers who understand the lore/science behind talismans can use them without need of being awakened.

      Technocrats have Enlightened Science which functions in the same way as magic. Some will be aware of how malleable reality is, so they will know that not everyone will be able to pull of their procedures without proper "training" first. Others would just consider these procedures too experimental to deliver consistent results, and thus require further development. There may come a point where these procedures become a part of the consensus, at which time they are no longer magic, but mundane.

                          It depends a lot on which faction the Technomancer belongs to, their Paradigm, and who they are surrounded by. Tradition technomancers have a party line to tow, that being that Reality is flexible and everyone's magick (or Science!) works. Many Etherites and Virtual Adepts likely just shrug, and assume their mystic colleagues are just weird, or exploiting weird facets of Science(!) that don't require the normal tools and techniques.

                          Other Trad technos may incorporate this flexibility into their own personal Paradigms. Virtual Adepts believe that Reality is a system that can be hacked. One of them might assume, logically, that the mystic is simply using a different kind of method for hacking that same Reality. A different programming language, if you will.

                          Etherites, by contrast, are prone to championing some pretty bizarre deviations from normal scientific thought. They are also known for being pulp-style adventurers, and the pulps didn't shy away from mixing science(!), mysticism, and raw grit and skill. Back in the pulp days, an adventurer might have trained with exotic mystics, especially in things like Yoga or Martial Arts. Others carry magical artifacts (amulets, statues, rings) that pulse with a mysterious energy. In both cases, an Etherite would likely assume there was some scientific explanation for why breathing exercises enhanced their physicality, why psychedelic drugs give them insight, or why the amulet is linked to some form of energy. These things are mysterious, but that's fine. A True Scientist sees mysteries as opportunities. Perhaps, once they've advanced their experiments sufficiently, they'll be able to develop a working theory explaining all the mumbo jumbo.

                          Certain mystic groups have also given rise to technomancers. Hermetic House Thig, Dreamspeaker Ghost Wheel Society (and other Technoshamans), Chorister Alexandrian Society, Euthanatos Lhaksmists, etc. These groups are easily able to reconcile the miraculous with the scientific, as their practices freely mix the two. For them, what they do and what their more conservative colleagues do is merely two methods of interacting with the same fundamental magical theories.

                          Technocrats have the hardest time of it, simply because their ethos brooks no superstitionist ideas. What the Technocracy does is not magic. There is no such thing. Inevitably, therefore, it causes some cognitive dissonance when a Technocrat agent faces off directly with a mystic mage, who proceeds to do the impossible with no apparent apparatus. When this happens, there are usually two outcomes (aside from conversion to Tradition thinking, of course). The first is that the agent starts asking their superiors and colleagues too many questions. This can be corrected quite easily by reminding the agent that superstitionist flights of fancy are Unmutual, and continued thoughts along those lines will necessitate reporting to Room 101 for correction. Sometimes, the Union will just skip the threats, and report them directly to Room 101. The conflict of ideas is no longer a problem, because the agent no longer remembers there being any inexplicable phenomenon.

                          The other outcome is that the Technocrat does what humans (including you and I) do when confronted with cognitive dissonance: rationalize and deny. The mystics are obviously exploiting quirks in the physical laws that they don't understand, but have managed to "master" through centuries of trial and error. The Technocracy, meanwhile, has the benefit of the scientific method, so are able to understand and iterate on developments faster and more completely than the mystic (who, again, doesn't really know what they're doing). Alternatively, the Technocrat might assume that the mystic is a charlatan, using hidden tools of science to affect the image of a wizard. This, indeed, is a gratifying idea to Technocrats, who can feel a smug sense of superiority in the face of obvious hypocrites and liars. And that only the Etherites and the Virtual Adepts - former Technocrats themselves - are even slightly honest about what they're doing (not that they aren't still doing it wrong).

                          Or the mystics could just be tapping into psionic potential. Which, while annoying in its vagueness, can cover a whole hell of a lot of what mystics do, in the eyes of the technomancer.

                          As a Technocrat grows more experienced and familiar with their primary Enlightened competition, they either become more and more sure of their rationalizations, or begin to realize the truth. By then, however, they've spent their lives working loyally for the Union. They've long since tied their egos and values to the Union, and everything it stands for. So when the Technocrat finally realizes that Reality is mutable, and dependent on belief, they are able to rationalize further...as to why the Union (and its deceptions) are necessary. They've seen what the mystics do, recklessly imposing an unpalatable and dangerous Paradigm (or sets of Paradigms) onto everyone and everything they can get away with. Humanity needs to believe in a rational, rigid scientific world view. It's for their own good. Just as it's for their own good that the agents beneath them need to be kept in the dark about the truth.

                              Originally posted by Lord Revan View Post
                              How can someone fail to pull a trigger? Also, the etherite would see that nothing wrong with the gun as soon as he gets it back. Maybe the etherite wouldn't know why it didn't work?
                              How can my mother fail to keep her VCR from flashing "12:00"?

                              Even "hard science" technomantic devices are the focus of extremely advanced scientific theories - so advanced that they are revolutionary to common thought, as far outside the realm of common human knowledge as mystic sigils and fireballs.

                              I think it's a non-starter hypothetical: It's entirely feasible that you can't make a lightning gun so simple that it's really just a matter of pulling a trigger. There's configuration concerns and dials and gauges; enough complexity that it's not surprising that an unAwakened mind can't juggle all of them at once. It's no different than why most Sleepers can't unlock their chakras or use their latent psychic powers: it's intuitive once you know how to do it, even effortless, but getting someone to that point is quite difficult. If you had streamlined the science and simplified the interface to the point a Sleeper could understand it, it wouldn't be dynamic magic anymore - it'd be an Extraordinary Device or just, y'know, an object.

                              Think of giving a normal rifle - a relatively straightforward device - to a caveman. Intuitively that's a point-and-click device, but you're neglecting that we're from a culture where mechanical objects are common and guns are known qualities. Could Thog intuit that the device was a weapon? Would he grasp it correctly? Could you explain to him why he needs to hold it just so? Simple concepts like ammunition (and that it can run out, and that Thog can't just put more metal rocks in the gun's "mouth"), that it's a tool and not an entity, etc. etc. all hinge on a fair amount of unacknowledged "cultural" knowledge about the world and its contents that Thog just doesn't have.

                              Have you ever tried to walk a grandparent through using an internet browser, pointing out exactly what to do, and met with limited success, despite hands-on, explicit instruction? That's us, with the Ethertie's lightning gun.

                                          Charles Babbage was once asked by a Member of Parliament whether his engine would, if provided with incorrect numbers, provide the right answer.

                                          This is an obviously ludicrous question - to us. But why would a Member of Parliament in 1864 intuit that the device was performing math on provided figures and was not an "answer-providing engine"? When dealing with unfamiliar technology, there are actually an untold number of foundational assumptions that the creator and his or her ingroup have internalized - paradigmatic knowledge, in fact - that a naive user doesn't have and that a skilled user wouldn't immediately recognize as genuine knowledge.

                                          My point in saying "It's entirely feasible that you can't make a lightning gun so simple that it's really just a matter of pulling a trigger."

                                          is that you can't make a lightning gun so simple that it's really just a matter of pulling a trigger. So when you say "the Etherite could make it that way" - well, no, see, that's not what "can't" means.

                                          It may seem that simple to a skilled operator, but that's because they're forgetting several preliminary steps, much as we might forget that Thog doesn't know that the firestick needs to be loaded first, or why your grandmother might be mystified as to why a typo in an email address causes the whole process to mysteriously fail.

                                          So the Sleeper pulls the trigger, produces nothing perhaps maybe a few measly sparks or a mechanical whine because, oops, they forgot to depress this or need to adjust for that or they fat-fingered safety button C over here and by they time you've corrected for those three errors they either have stumbled on operator errors four through six or, if they're anything like a grandparent with an email window, they'll keep doing one or the other because the process is actually more complex than a skilled user realizes and they haven't internalized all the correct behaviors.

                                          Seriously this is not a different concern than an Akashic having to deal with the fact that normal Sleepers can't meditate hard enough to heal their injuries.
