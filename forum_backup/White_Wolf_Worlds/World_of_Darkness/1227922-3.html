                        Originally posted by Muad'Dib
                        Within this idea that you have, would becoming a Nephandi Mage be a decision that can't be turned back, as it is suggested in some parts of what is currently written about the Nephandi in M:tA books ?
                        As Dataweaver said, establishing a parallelism with Marauderdom makes it permanent.

                        However, if I were to write a whole book about them, I would like to play with the idea of redemption. Redemption stories it's a genre that's very much in-paradigm for many Traditions. It shoulnd't be easy though. Damnation it's too strong of a word for something that can be turned back just 'cause you want:

                        *Perhaps there's a middle ground between being a normal mage and becoming a Nephandi - a moment where the Caul becomes possible but the mage hasn't fallen yet, and this moments could seed redemption stories (and if the players botch or don't care the character falls and can't be turned back)
                        *If vampires have legendas about curing The Curse of Caine, there could be similar legends for curing Nephandi - but this would require the Nephandi to be willing to change. Something supposed to be impossible. Perhaps, like Marauders, Nephandi have levels of madness and thus their evil can be ameliorated - perhaps to the point of feeling a little remorse in the lowest level. But it shouldn't be easy. Not any easier than curing Marauders anyway.
                        *Perhaps the mage who chose to fall can't be redeemed but the inheritors of their inverted avatars can, if approached soon enough. Having a twisted avatar could be a Flaw that damns your mage to the Fall unless you can attain this legendary cure first.

                                    Originally posted by Flinty View Post
                                    I'd like Ascension to move away from presenting the Technocracy as obviously wrong and evil. The whole setting of the Ascension War is based on the premise that reality is a clash of differing ideas. Presenting one of the main parties as "bad" invalidates the entire premise for me. Focus less on TU atrocities (though they should still be there) and highlight all the good they are doing some more. At the same time devote some word count to why the Traditions are in their way horrible people sometimes aswell. The base assumption of MtA should not be "here are the traditions, those are the good guys" it should be "many mages strive to make the world better but no two of them can agree how to do it. Where do you stand?". Crafts, Traditions, Orphans and Technocrats should be designed as equally player appealing options, their organizations all having good and bad aspects.
                                    I think they've been doing that for about the last 20 years in one way or another.

                                    The problem I see, for any prospective 5th Edition Mage if they base it on the design features of Vampire 5th, is that they will need to tailor it back a little. This means, for me, only having the Nine Traditions plus maybe Orphans (or Hollow Ones?) in the core book. A lot of players seem to be enampred for playing Technocracy agents. Mage Revised (3rd Edition) tried to tailor it back in such a way, although it had criticism, while also trying to integrate some of the Crafts as factions in the Traditions. There is some virtue in this approach, but ultimately, I think the game will need to be more focussed than the 20th Anniversary (or 4th Edition).
