    Do Inquisitor's in Dark Age actually use True Faith?

    12-12-2024, 10:55 AM
    To preface: I'm going to be biased in this discussion because mind is already leaning on "probably no".

    The mechanics of DA: Inquisitor don't line up with any version of true faith in any edition. They focus entirely on Virtues rather than anything to do with the Willpower accotiation universal to every version of TF, reserve the classic "repel vampires" type powers to high levels despite that being the First power of TF, and inquisitors don't even need to be particularly faithful to have their powers despite TF's classically strict requirements.

    The one thing they do kind of line up with are HtR if hunters had access to a handful of Sorcerer paths and were forced to follow a version of the Road of Heaven from DA:Vampire.

    Question is, if Inquisitors aren't running with TF, what are they using? What are you thoughts?

    Personal Speculation: it could be they're proto-hunters created and managed by Lucifer. It's a thing in DtF and HtR lore that he was active during this time and he Really doesn't like supernaturals.
    The Inquisitors being Lucy's Gourmet version of the hunter recipe also fits his MO. She always does things Big when she's directly involved.

          Yes and no?

          Yes, True Faith is canonically the power behind the abilities of the Inquisitors. They can only do what they do because of the fact that Faith can manifest into real power in the WoD; even if some of them rely on borrowed faith via relics instead of their own powerful faith.

          No, their mechanics don't line up at all with how other books apply True Faith, and yes were largely inspired by HtR despite not actually working like Edges either. There's a lot of reasons for this, but the DA Revised era books were not particularly worried about cross-compatibility with the rest of the WoD. DA Mage and DA Fae also work wildly differently from how the same powers are shown in other books.

                  From a purely Watsonian approach?

                  The Church, having organized faith based hunters on a scale never seen before, studied and refined True Faith into more distinct practices than "wild" True Faith that crops up in the wider human populace or even occasional supernatural entity. As the Inquisition lost power and status over time, it grew harder and harder to maintain these practices and eventually the Society of Leopold was forced to rely on True Faith however it manifested in order to have access to it at all to continue their fight against monsters.

                                                  DAI is also explicit about something: it's version of True Faith is the "full" version where other games use a simplified version for their own purposes, like VtM games using Celerity when stating up Lupines instead of giving them Rage and explaining all the Rage rules. That's the official explanation in the rules for why things are different. But as a design device instead of an in-game explanation, if someone wants to explain the differences when has to best fit in an explanation for the extreme differences that come out of the abstraction of True Faith in the other games.
