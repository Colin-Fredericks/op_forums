    IOC should be different than OOC. Now, there is some ambiguity in every OOC. For example, in VtM, the 3rd generation could be refuges from the High Umbra, banished from their home realm in the High Umbra by Caine after they killed off the 2nd generation, and became the first generation of vampires when they permanently possessed human hosts. The Second City would have been created by the 3rd generation to mimic the First City of the High Umbra. There is nothing in particular in the books that conflict with that interpretation, and it helps to address some of the conflicts between the cosmologies of the various splats quite nicely, since the First City never existed on the Earth in the first place.

                    Originally posted by Monalfie View Post
                    I really think it comes down to intentional splat themes. Vampire has a lot of tools and intent for existential horror and identity crisis. Morality, Humanity, numerous Paths, numerous conflicting sects, various conflicting historical accounts. Turmoil over one's existence and place in the world is a huge thing. To me, Werewolf doesn't question the nature of the conflict, it is designed to be about how one pursues that conflict. I think it'd be inherently unfair to critisize your own lore alternatives because I don't want to put this as, 'this is a concept that would inherently be handled bad.' But to me it is a concept that is inherently unsatisfying in how Werewolf is presented as a game.
                    Giving that a like because it's respectful and it made me see it differently. I feel like I'm seeing Werewolf and thinking "hmm, however can I make this a darker shade of grey". I will say that handled properly, theme of questioning conflict don't necessarily subtract from themes of how to handle conflict. My games have both.

If you question the Setting on that level, it leaves the entire discussion kind of moot because you can just make up whatever you like.

And you're trusting the angry shark to remember things from 10,000 years ago with clarity? Feels like "it's right because it just feels right" isn't going to cut it. People who are under the effects of supernatural emotional control often feel like everything is okay and natural.

But there is something to be said for a world which fundamentally subverts the deeply held beliefs of the players. Take a look at Undertale for an example [spoilers ahead]. A comment on the dark nature of Player Mindset in TTRPG, the gullibility of the indoctrinated and the power of a carefully designed Propaganda State. It would also be a game full of despair, denial and both players and characters rejecting the very suggestion of it being true, rejecting the messenger and killing them even (acting exactly as you would predict, and as people do in real life when their indoctrinated views are challenged).

                                                        Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post
                                                        So, there are two ways to approach Lore. The bit that comes at the start of the book or splat or tribe or clan or set of powers or sect. The bit said in a matter-of-fact tone that creates the world for you, the mythos. One is the This Is True approach. Gaia argued with the first Werefox and frenzied. The Werespiders are the offspring of one of the Weaver's daughters. Each Breed was created to fulfill a certain task for Gaia, you get the idea.

                                                        The other approach is This Is Believed. This is the origin this group thinks is true. This is what 1 dot in "Ananasi Lore" gets you.

                                                        I'm not saying that this is true, or that it should be true. I'm trying to make a comment on the nature of lore and how we should use it. I'm trying to point out why in-universe and out-of-universe skepticism of the status quo should be given more time and space. After all, it would be terrifying if any of the Garou lore was just bullshit spread by lying spirits. That might make them monsters...
                                                        Looking back at my OP, I really can't imagine anything more inoffensive. Like seriously, these aren't controversial or crazy views on Lore. They're a mature approach (the approach most STs end up using in my experience) to dealing with Word of God, foundations of setting and individual origins of different groups. And I don't even think that any of the people commenting negatively necessarily even disagree with me in a general sense, since the responses have been Special Pleading for why it doesn't apply to Werewolf.
