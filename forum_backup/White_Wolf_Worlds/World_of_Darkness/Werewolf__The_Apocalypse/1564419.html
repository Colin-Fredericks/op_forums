    Are there any playable Gurahl or Bastet in England or the surrounding area?

    09-29-2026, 12:00 PM
    As the title says, I'm looking to see if there are any playable Werebear or Werecat breeds from either England, or in the surrounding area (Nordic, Celtic, etc.).

    To explain a bit, I was going to be getting into a rather large WTA campaign with my friends; more specifically, we were going to try to go through as much of the original WTA line as possible. This means going (hopefully) all the way to the final Apocalypse battle, as well as a few custom settings such as hunting down a BSD pack in Poland and, as a personal objective for my character, hunting down a powerful Wyrm spirit in France. My DM has encouraged us to use whatever we like for this, since it's going to be a LONG game, and I did have some ideas for Garou and surprisingly Corax characters; however, I also wanted to check into making either a Gurahl or Bastet.

    As I indicated, my character was going to be an Englishman, of optionally either Nordic or Celtic descent; hunting down that French Wyrm spirit was a personal grudge of his. I was considering running either a Gurahl or Bastet, shaking things up a bit, but I can't quite figure out if either breeds would be possible. For the werebears, they seem to have all been wiped out in Europe, leaving only Native American or Russian werebears left that I know of; similarly, I was considering a Ceilican for a Bastet character, but I've seen a lot of conflicting information whether that tribe is still around or not, even on here.

    So, basically... would a British/European Werebear or Werecat be possible in the WTA lore?

              The answer is yes and no.

              Sticking to Revised metaplot both Breeds are extinct from the British islands and have been for a very long time. It was one of the first places in Europe to extinguish local bears, and the loss of the Ceilican is a sticking point to the Bastet.

              BUT, weird shit happens. That depends a lot on ST preferences, but there's a lot of ways your character could exist as an exception.

              Easier for Gurahl. People underestimate a lot how much humans mix, and a non negligible proportion of British citizens have Native American ancestors without knowing or looking like it. The mere fact that contact with the New World is now common may make the spirit side of this stir and accept a new Gurahl being born from old kinfolk stock. Those are valid rationales for a character that's a very rare occurrence, but still possible.

              For the Ceilican it's harder, but here's the thing: this is fiction. Harder doesn't mean impossible, it means plot. A number of parties have been working in secret to bring the Ceilican back. It may be that something worked, or that an event in the Umbra catalyzed this return.

              You should discuss your ideas with your ST and, rather than arrive with an ordinarily viable character, see if an extraordinary plot could be a good addition, especially for a game that aims to go the whole nine yards.

                  I think there's justification for werebears. The Eurasian brown bear once lived in Britain so there had to be werebears there. While the brown bear was extirpated from Britain around 1500 years ago, a homid line could still exist passing down the heritage. Actual wild wolves haven't been around in Britain for over 300 years (or Ireland for 250), yet that hasn't stopped the game from populating the British Isles with lots of Garou. I believe the European brown bear would map to the River Keepers tribe.

                  I don't remember anything stating that the Gurahl were completely wiped out in Europe (or Eurasia), but only survived in North America. I think there's just statements that most of them live in North America. Just that the Gurahl lost a lot of members and went into hiding - going into long term hibernation, escaping to various places in the umbra or Den-Realms, and otherwise laying low until recently. The lore on the Changing Breeds changes from edition to edition, so it all depends on what your ST wants to do or allow in his chronicle. (In the 1e Players Guide, it's mentioned there are only around twenty Gurahl total in the whole world, and most of those are implied to be ancient survivors of the War of Rage. Later editions seem to imply a much larger number though still small compared to the Garou and some other Changing Breeds.) You probably need to work with your ST to develop the right background to explain it, but there's no reason why it would be impossible. It's just whether your ST will accept your pitch or work with you to find a concept that fits with his chronicle concept.

                  As for European/British Bastet, the options are more limited. Europe simply isn't home to the great cats. As Ramnesis said, Ceilician could be allowed based on the lore, but it depends on the ST. There's a lot of extra baggage that goes with them, and many STs would not want to bother with it.

                  Although the Qualmi are said to be North American only, the Eurasian lynx does exist. I don't know if the creators of the Bastet knew that, or simply decided the Eurasian Qualmi were all wiped out and only the North American variety survived. So while not canon, there is plausibility for arguing that a Eurasian "Qualmi" (perhaps under a different name, but mechanically the same) could exist. Of course, they would probably not be in Britain as the Lynx there was extirpated in the Middle Ages (although some think they might have survived much later). But again, neither do wolves and there's Garou all over the place there. There's no lore to support that, but it all depends on how much the ST wants to depart from canon. (In my chronicles, I usually find myself wanting to keep to the spirit of original canon, but making lots of individual adjustments to suit my tastes.)

                  Bears and lynxs are found in Scandinavia though, and the European wildcat has a wide distribution on the continent. So if you are willing for your character to outside Britain, you can find nearby regions with these wild animals.

                          Indeed, the books are inconsistent on what's exactly the effect of losing all your kinfolk in an area. It's bad, but how bad? And the problem is geography or species? How long does it take to change something?

                          The Bunyip went extinct. But the thylacine was wiped out royally with nothing close to it remaining. And even then there's no indication that their human kinfolk stopped breeding true, the new cubs just have to go to other tribes.

                          None of the original Mokole kinfolk species is standing, and their closest relatives are birds. They not only jumped species, they jumped a major clade within the Cordata. Sure, there was a mysterious magic ritual involved, but still.

                          South America has a small but very present Garou population that's born here, have their human kinfolk here... But we don't have wolves. We never had wolves, not even close. And the things we do have that resemble wolves are not kinfolk.

                          You could arguably have any Changing Breed anywhere simply because nothing really stops people from traveling. And I agree that Indian Changing Breeds shouldn't be too much of a stretch to find in England. Whatever happened to make Portuguese Garou breed true in Brasil should apply for Indians in the UK.

                                      Given how Bastet are said to seek partners, it's within reason to think that all their breeds have kinfolk all around the world. If the ST decides this is the line, then all Bastet should be global.

                                      But the point with Indian Khan has another nuance. The ST may decide blood isn't enough, but social presence is: the Brazilian Garou may have thrived not because some kinfolk came, but because enough Garou to establish at least one Caern came.

                                      If THAT is the line, then the high migration of Indians to the UK (whatever their ethnicity) isn't relevant in itself, but because it makes a higher probability that some Khan migrated, and with that established the spiritual background needed for them to breed true.

                                      We must always temper those observations against the fact that this is not about genetics, even if lineage plays a role. Kinfolk and Fera are spiritual phenomena, so background context can matter in indirect ways.
