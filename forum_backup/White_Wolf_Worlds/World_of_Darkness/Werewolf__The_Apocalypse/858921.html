    Is it not possible to transcend the 5th level as a changing breed?

    04-04-2016, 08:52 AM
    When vampires are 7th gen, Kuei-jin are at the 6th level of their chosen dharma, mages whenever they want to... They can reach beyond the limits of their human form in their human form. We're not talking about how technically a vampire has potence 5 so really that 13th gen is at 10 strength (well, that comes in to play) but rather that Mr Enlightened there has seven points of melee and therefore makes an expert look like a noob.

    Now, maybe it's because renown is an inherently greedy thing, and the changing cultures are poor as shit, or gaia put limitations on her servants, or she saw how vampires and Kuei-Jin were going and thought 'Nope, None of that' (Changing breeds mostly predate Cainites, so I'm not sure about this)

    Maybe it's because they don't live too long (well, Rokea live long, but for every 5 years spent learning they spend another 600 moving forward) (Kitsune sound like they should advance into post 5 with the tails, but I've found no rules on it.)

    Now, some totems can bring one beyond 6 in an attribute, but this is borrowed power, and although I have read that a ridiculous level of devotion to a totem might get you more from it, it's not a permanent or personal improvement.

    On the otherhand, some gifts basically are just improvements. The Cecillian have one that provides 3 dice to any instrument they play. I'm sure that somewhere I read that there was a gift that gave an extra die to all charisma rolls (forgot the name, but sure it's in there). King fish/Majesty adds one to all intimidation/leadership rolls. There's a few more.

    Another thing is that the gifts of the changers allow for superhuman levels of a skill, and could we therenot justify having more than five dots to something as these gifts feel innate? For example, dexterity with improvements such as ballance,Catfeet,Hare'sLeap, rathead, flame dance. Perception with all those new non-human abilities, Charisma with many things, stealth with manythings, brawl with many things. Even just the regenerative properties of the garou is a good help for brawl, as constant regeneration ensures you can go further than the common person in a sparing match. It feels odd to have superhuman ability and be limited by five dots.

          There's no over-arching reasons beyond different games with different design goals.

          WtA has, for a long time, frontloaded "bread and butter" power (essentially the 6+ ratings equivalents), and gated off "story" powers. Higher ranking characters in WtA do have more experience, thus higher stats and more toys, but they've vastly expanded in their powers that allow them to effect major alterations in the course of events (often without distinct mechanics).

          This generally stems from WtA being the game most focused on the PCs starting as violent individuals growing into bigger characters from there. All of the PC character start off being able to easily kill humans even without investing much into combat; with only situations dictating why it's a bad idea to resort to such things. But Rank 1 PCs don't have a lot of power beyond beyond their personal abilities. The game focuses (to use werewolves as a core example) on growing from a grunt to someone that sees the big picture of the war and needs to gain the power to push large scale efforts towards how they feel is the best course of action.

          A lot of the other games are far less frontloaded this way, and characters are more likely to have a lot more "story" powers. Vampires get some nice automatic perks, but without investment/preparation their default package doesn't simple let them curbstomp humans. However they also start with much strong abilities such as the addictive nature of their blood which can be used to dozens of goals.
