    Tribal Membership of Packs

    09-30-2021, 04:36 PM
    Obviously, this is something that will vary to some extent based on the total population of Garou as well as the presence or lack of different tribes in the region, and PC packs are what the group wants them to be. But given sufficient numbers, how often do Garou form packs consisting entirely of members of a single tribe, versus packs with members from multiple tribes. Do packs still tend to be dominated by one tribe with token membership from others, or are they often "melting pots" with diverse tribal membership? Are there certain tribes you see as more or less likely to be in a pack together?

      This depends entirely on why and how the pack was formed.

      Assuming a multi-tribal sept here.

      If it was formed by elders for a Rite of Passage, mixed.

      If it was formed by elders on a SPECIFIC type of Rite of Passage, one tribe.

      If it was formed by tribal elders for tribal tasks, one tribe.

      If it was formed by garou coming together due to skills, mixed.

      If it was formed by garou wanting a one tribe pack, one tribe.

      Basically, reasons are as varied as the garou.

              It's another "depends" sort of thing.

              While the game focuses on lasting packs that will keep going until they get destroyed for fall about on their own, that's because it's focused on PC packs where the players will be one group for the whole game.

              In the setting, packs are frequently formed ad hoc among Garou that aren't in a lasting pack for specific tasks, and then disband when their job is finished. So many Garou will be part of a few packs while young and looking for a long term pack. While usually buried in supplements that are Rites for forming temporary packs rather than the Rite of Totem, and Rites for breaking up a pack if the Elders really want to do that for some reason (that is pretty drastic).

                  The first thing that would define this would be where those werewolves live. In average NPCs don't move around as much as PCs do, so most Garou are likely to be inducted in their tribe, live and work their entire lives in the same area, bound to the same Caern, and the exceptions tend to be exceptions for a reason. In the same vein, packs will be loosely defined on practical terms and only formalize when they sense it is time to.

                  So, if you ask about the general rule all over the world, then most packs are probably single tribe without even a token outsider. They're local Garou from single tribe septs and doing it otherwise simply isn't a thing in their context.

                  But those septs exist in their own contexts. More cosmopolitan places tend to be few in number, but well traveled and "more eventful" so to speak. Septs near big cities, or important places for several tribes, septs in any area where many tribes share space for any reason, those tend to be the places where mixed packs are possible with real frequency and also where situations that require special action, like forming specialized multi-tribal packs, also occur more frequently.

                      This is an area where each ST will fill in the gaps of canon with their own preferences so there is no one right answer.

                      I am pretty sure at some point the books made it clear that single tribe septs and packs were once the norm, but as the Garou population wanes, the power of the Wyrm rises, and the Apocalypse nears that multi-tribal septs and packs became more common. I can't offhand cite a reference though.

                      When that began to change and how that plays out is up to the ST. While it makes the most sense that septs and packs would be mono-tribal because of trust issues, and that most multitribal septs and packs would likely be limited to a few closely allied tribes at the local level, OOC game needs often dictate septs and packs that can accommodate whatever character concepts you group of PCs come up with. Different STs will balance that in different ways.

                      But I think we can look at examples of what the original designers thought the setting should look like. Rage Across New York is a good example. The Sept of the Green at Central Park, New York is out example of a multi-tribal sept. It is a misfit sept being hope to lots of oddballs, exiles, and other Garou who can't fit because the caern is a caern of Fellowship, and welcomes everyone. It is assumed that is where the PCs will start out because any character concept would be welcomed there. There is an IC reason for it. But outside that sept, the rest of the caerns are mono-tribal or very limited in their tribal membership. The Sept of the Sentinel in the Adirondacks is Get of Fenris only and the Shadow Lords - who previously held it - seek to invade it and take it back. The Sept of White Waters at Niagara only has two tribes - the Uktena and Fianna, but it was previously Uktena only and only let in the Fianna because of a huge service that tribe provided. The Sept of the Hand of Gaia is a huge Rank 5 caern once held in common by the Three Brothers. But it fell to the Wyrm and was only rescued by a huge multi-tribal coalition lead by the Children of Gaia. So the sept is multi-tribal, but is dominated by an alliance of the Children of Gaia and the Black Furies (who were instrumental in understanding the Enigmas of the caern).

                      So the default seems to be mono-tribal septs unless something about the nature of the caern or its history justifies allowing more than one tribe.

                      I think the most important rule is that packs will reflect the nature of their sept. If a sept is mono-tribal, their packs will be mono-tribal. If a sept has two allied tribes controlling it, packs may be exclusively of one tribe or a mix of the two. As the sept becomes more diverse (for whatever reason), the packs will reflect that. The more diverse the sept, the more diverse its packs are likely to be. Even anruth packs that travel from sept to sept were originally from a home sept, and most anruth packs likely cease their travels at some point and stay at a sept they'll fit in at.

                      Two other important things is that packs will attract Garou who are most interested in a specific pack's quest or purpose. The other is that packs will only want new members who work well with existing members. So a war pack will most likely attract certain tribes more than others. A wisdom pack will mostly attract a different sort of tribe. Obviously Garou are individuals, but they are forged by their tribal culture. A Child of Gaia could decide to join a war pack of Boar dominated by Get of Fenris and Shadow Lord, but is unlikely. But that Child of Gaia might decide to join a war pack of Bear whose members are Fianna and Black Furies. Likewise most Get of Fenris won't be interested in a wisdom pack under Unicorn filled with Stargazers, Silent Striders, and Black Furies. Exceptions always occur, but they should make sense.

                      In my own chronicles, I make almost all rank 1 caerns are mono-tribal and therefore so will their packs be. Most rank 2 and rank 3 caerns are either mono-tribal or have two or three trusted tribes controlling them. Sometimes what tribes trust each other or are on good terms reflects local history and may not be obvious. For example, the Wendigo and Get of Fenris are generally viewed as antagonistic with one another. But maybe there is some sept in northern Canada where local Get of Fenris immigrants in the late 19th century helped the Wendigo defeat a major threat to the caern, and as a result the local members learned to trust one another and cooperate. They now share that caern. So there can be strange pairings. However, rank 4 and rank 5 caerns are often larger coalitions of tribes because of the need to gather sufficient Garou to defend them, and the desire for Garou of many tribes to travel there to petition spirits at the appropriate caern level to learn their Gifts. But of course you can always think of exceptions. Just have good and interesting reasons for them.

                          Thank you very much for the feedback everyone! I was thinking of including a bit more context earlier, but I had to leave for work, so I'll add that now!

                          An off-and-on project that I have been working on, mostly for fun, involves updating the Vancouver setting to the 20th Anniversary Edition; I'm a Vancouver resident, and being a crossover-nut meant that having my home city being an unusual crossover setting intrigues me. For the unfamiliar, there is a rank 5 Caern of Cooperation in the middle of Vancouver; with the caern's focus being cooperation, the local Garou created a Compact where the sept is shared and protected by all the tribes. Officially, the over-a-hundred Garou in the area are all members of the Sept of the Great Caern, but in practice each tribe has an unofficial territory and their own unofficial septs. The original sourcebook does not provide any details for any of the packs in the area, so I am having to construct it all from scratch (even if it's just broad strokes in most cases).

                          In the original Vancouver setting, most of the tribes were willing to cooperate under the unofficial leadership of the Silver Fangs. But after the local peace treaty with the vampires that was organized by the Glass Walkers and backed by the Silver Fangs ended up falling apart, I'm currently imagining that the Shadow Lords (who criticized the whole situation just to trash the Silver Fangs) ended up getting some of the more belligerent tribes on their side. Aside from the Silver Fangs and Co. vs. the Shadow Lords and Co., I'm also imagining that the Glass Walkers and Bone Gnawers are kind of working as a coalition, since pretty much everyone else has been rather soured against the whole "give peace with Vampires and the City" thing a chance. A similar situation with the Uktena and Wendigo being pissed at everyone else has the twelve remaining tribes (I'm accepting the canon about the Stargazers leaving the Garou Nation) has resulted in the local Garou population being split into four blocs. Which doesn't include the fifth group of hardcore radicals that would literally burn the entire city to the ground if given the chance.

                          So far I've been imagining that multi-tribe packs might form among members of different blocs, but that mono-tribe packs are a bit more common, especially among the senior Garou since each tribe wants experienced groups loyal only to their interests available. I had also kind of been imagining many of the packs as having something of a hierarchy: each representative in the local Council having a mono-tribal pack as their "retinue," with more mono-tribal packs filling the middle of each tribe's hierarchy, and then multi-tribe packs mostly being treated as a starting point before being brought into the more prestigious mono-tribe packs.

                          I'm not a super-experienced veteran with any WoD line, but Werewolf is definitely one I have less experience with, so I was hoping for some insight on how divide up 100+ Garou into packs without having any pre-existing guidelines in the source material that I'm working with.

                              Originally posted by Kharnov View Post
                              Thank you very much for the feedback everyone! I was thinking of including a bit more context earlier, but I had to leave for work, so I'll add that now!
                              Ok, here's my take.

                              Since the tribes form unofficial septs, then it is certain that monotribal packs are the norm in the city. This is a direct reflection of their culture: they cooperate, but they also keep mostly to their own. If mixed packs were the norm it wouldn't happen.

                              But packs form due to a multitude of reasons and frequently out of some personal connection. A stable pack is like a family to a werewolf, it is an extremely tight bond that shouldn't ever be broken lightly, while temporary packs have a specific reason to exist as they fulfill a function. Not only that, but since they share a lot of things while they exist, even temporary packs have a big impact on their members, especially early ones.

                              Packs also don't quite work like clubs that you want to enter. You don't wish to belong to that cool pack, you wish your pack to become one of the cool packs. Unless your pack have a defined goal and it breaks after completing it, getting you out of a pack because you're invited to a more prestigious one isn't exactly how it works, much less on scale.

                              Your idea of hierarchy doesn't quite hold without much more explanation. Why are they forming mixed packs with their Cliath? What is the purpose of such packs and why are they so common? Why they don't exist at "higher" levels? What if a problem arrive that needs an experienced mixed pack? What happened to mixed packs that existed earlier?

                              In werewolf intruding too much on pack composition isn't normal after the pack is formed. It is like a marriage, and like a marriage you can have arrangements and matchmakers and political shenanigans, or just happen spontaneously, but once it is done it is done. They can break, but outsiders shouldn't interfere too much. And breaking from outside is extreme. But also like in a marriage, where for some social and legal situations the couple is a single entity, the pack also can acquire or lose prestige and face, or face prejudice, as a whole.

                              A mixed pack being seen as untrustworthy is more likely in this scenario than being a standard entry point then broken from outside, but you also need to think about what happened to all that mixed packs that existed before. Which ones stood, which ones broke. Where it left good friendships that can try to bridge the gap, where it left bad blood and open wounds in the Garou society.

                                  Originally posted by Kharnov View Post
                                  An off-and-on project that I have been working on, mostly for fun, involves updating the Vancouver setting to the 20th Anniversary Edition; I'm a Vancouver resident, and being a crossover-nut meant that having my home city being an unusual crossover setting intrigues me.
                                  I want to note that Dark Alliance: Vancouver is a very early WW product, and as a result there are things that don't make sense with how the game was developed. It doesn't even make sense with other early WW 1e products. For example, the impression that I get from the book is that the author didn't truly understand what a "sept" was. Most of the time it seems he is talking about packs when he mentions septs. At other times he might actually mean a sept running an actual caern far away. Sometimes it seems to be something entirely different, not quite a pack but clearly not a group that runs a caern. The book also mentions the term Protectorate a lot, but the term doesn't seem to match the definition used in the original corebook or how it was used in Rage Across New York published earlier. I get the distinct impression that the author was given an early version/notes of the rules so he could create an early sourcebook, and he either interpreted things wrong or things subsequently changed before the corebook was published.

                                  The other thing is that like all early Rage books, the population figures for the Garou just do not make sense. There are far more Garou then later products would imply. There's something like 368 Garou in all of BC, 159 of which are in the Greater Vancouver area (over 40% of all Garou in BC). (This doesn't count contradictory information in the text. For example under the Get of Fenris description we're old there's around 45 total in BC. But later we're told 30 are here plus 40 here and another 30 plus presumably the 15 in the city which means there are well over a hundred Get.) Later sourcebooks would say a Rank 5 caern usually has 25-40 Garou. Even if you keep the rank 1 Smiling Buddha caern of the Glass Walkers, that's just another group of up to 10 Garou. Granted these are merely guidelines the game later gave, not strict rules, but DA:V can't be reconciled with them.

                                  Now you could rule that "Greater Vancouver" covers a lot more than the sept that runs the Great Caern. The Vancouver Compact was clearly agreed to by a regional Concolation so it's likely multiple caerns/septs in the region are part of it. That would make the sept of the Great Caern the dominant player among the septs of the Compact, but there would be other septs in the city (like the Smiling Buddha caern originally) and farther away that are part of the Compact. But that still would be a hell of a lot of septs/caerns to get to 159 Garou.

                                  So I would encourage you to use DA:V more as an idea mine than playing the setting as is. It simply has too many contradictions with future assumptions of the setting, a legacy of being such an early product. Use whatever information you like as you update it, but be prepared to ignore a lot of it. So if you don't want 100+ Garou, don't worry about it. It's OK to have much lower numbers if you want.

                                  Originally posted by Kharnov View Post
                                  I'm not a super-experienced veteran with any WoD line, but Werewolf is definitely one I have less experience with, so I was hoping for some insight on how divide up 100+ Garou into packs without having any pre-existing guidelines in the source material that I'm working with.
                                  Personally, this is how I would handle it.

                                  First, I'd have multiple septs/caerns in the setting. The Great caern is merely the most important one, and therefore its sept the most important to Concolation politics that involve the Vancouver Compact.

                                  Second, you need to decide whether Smiling Buddha caern still exists. Rage Across the World, revised edition stated it was destroyed and Roger Daly dead. You may want to go with that, or somehow keep the caern active. If so, you can dump a lot of the Glass Walkers at this caern.

                                  Third, I'd reduce the numbers of the sept of the Great Caern to be more in line with later products. You probably want members from all the tribes there, but some tribes will be quite low in population. You can probably ignore the Striders and Stargazers entirely. You can always have a lone member show up to visit whenever you want. But that still leaves eleven tribes which means an average between 3-4 members per tribe. That is probably not enough, so you'll want to make some hard choices as to the Great Caern itself. Since the Bone Gnawers will be in the city, they'll likely need to be here. And if you don't have the Smiling Buddha caern anymore, the Glass Walkers will need to go here as well. But having decent numbers of those means a lot of tribes won't have many members. So I suggest many tribes simply aren't members of the Great Caern. Instead, they'd be members of their own sept/caern that is still part of the Compact.

                                  So what tribes should be members of the Great Caern sept based on the tribal descriptions of DA:V? I would include the Fianna, Shadow Lords, the Silver Fangs, Uktena, and Wendigo. That's a good enough mix of Urrah, Eurasian, and Pure One tribes for some lively politics. You can always have other tribes appear at the Great Caern as visitors or when Concolations are held to review issues related to the Compact, but otherwise it's those six or seven tribes which allows you to have around 6-7 members each on average.

                                  Fourth, all the other tribes get exiled to some other sept/caern (and don't forget most of the tribes at the Great Caern will also have caerns of their own elsewhere in BC). These will be monotribal caerns, but part of the Great Compact. So members of these other tribes will still have a reason to visit the Great Caern on occasion. DA:V name drops enough "septs" and "protectorates" that you can probably select one or more of them to be the site of such a caern/sept. I suggest getting a map of BC and drawing where you want these caerns to be located so you can cover the area you want.

                                  Fifth, you may also want some caerns/septs in BC to NOT be a member of the Compact because they are too far away. So feel free to mix things up.

                                  So that gets us to how to do the packs.

                                  First, the monotribal caerns will be easy. The packs from those will likely only be members from that tribe. So the tribes not represented at the Great Caern will default to mono-tribal (unless you have a special idea you want to do). Black Furies will be in their own pack as will the Children of Gaia, Get of Fenris, and Red Talons.

                                  Second, what do we do with the packs at the Great Caern?

                                  Well, it makes sense that this is a "leadership" pack comprised of one member of the tribes there probably under a trusted Totem of Respect with the pack purpose of ensuring cooperation between members. Members of this pack will fill the sept leadership positions. This isn't necessary. You may think of alternate arrangements. But it is the one to my mind that best leads to coherent leadership and cooperation. All the tribes that make up the sept get represented, and as its members will likely by an elder from the tribe, each will have sway when the pack internally deliberates.

                                  The other packs will likely default to either a monotribal pack, a pack between tribes that are trusted allies, or a true multi-tribal pack based around a compelling pack purpose and totem (not all tribes would need to be part of it). Purely for game design purposes, I probably want at least one truly multi-tribal pack and the rest be a mix of one or only two tribes.

                                  Now there are lots of ideas that can make sense. But one idea is that the multi-tribal pack be based around Raven. It's one of the core pack totems, and Raven is the caern totem. Raven is also a totem that many different tribes are known to respect. So it's an excellent excuse to bring Garou of different tribes together. Raven is a "live off the land" kind of totem. So I'd include Bone Gnawers who can dumpster dive or panhandle and live off the land in the city itself. I'd also include a Wendigo and Uktena because the Pacific Northwest Indians have strong cultural associations with ravens. And I'd include Shadow Lords because they too have strong connections to corvids. Therefore, they all have easy motivations as to why they want to serve together. They could be a troubleshooting pack that roams all over Vancouver.

                                  The other packs would either be monotribal or combos of two tribes (Uktena & Wendigo, Bone Gnawer & Glass Walker, Fianna & Silver Fang). Some other combination make make sense for you. Or maybe you want another multi-tribal pack of three or more tribes because of an idea you have. But I think in terms of internal sept politics, a mixture of single tribe packs, double tribe packs, and mixed tribe packs will prove interesting.

                                  And any time you want to bring in more tribes, have a pack visit. Or hold a Concolation - just have a real agenda for the Concolation. It'd be at the Concolations (which you may want to be held on a regular basis since so many septs are invested with it) that the real tribal politics happen since they form a kind of super-government that governs all the septs that are members of the Compact.

                                  ​These are my initial ideas. Hope some of them can be of use to you.

                                      Thank you very much for your excellent feedback!

                                      monteparnas

                                      Thank you especially for your points about packs not being "clubs" with more or less prestige, along with the points about (permanent) packs not being broken up willy-nilly. Questioning my own thoughts leading in this direction was one of the reasons I made this topic Regarding the purpose of "mixed packs" of cliath, it was essentially along the idea that individual tribes might not have enough cliath to form a mono-tribe pack, but don't want to admit an inexperienced Garou into one of the more "prestigious" mono-tribe packs, or ruin the "1 Garou per Auspice" tradition in some cases. But if that whole line of reasoning is in error, then I'll have to approach it from a different perspective

                                      Black Fox

                                      Fair point about DA:V being an early product with some rather rough edges. The topic I have going over in the VtM forum already covers some of that. I didn't generally find the use of the term "sept" confusing, because I could usually put together the context of when they were talking about "official" septs further away versus the "unofficial" septs that just seem to consist of a single tribe occupying a particular neighbourhood or region in the Metro Vancouver area without (necessarily) having a caern; being a local helps. But there are some ambiguities for sure; for example, the local Get of Fenris sept (or "sept") they describe is actually about 70-80 miles from Vancouver (Abbotsford/Chilliwack). I'll also need to dig into the meaning of the term "protectorate" in WtA terms a bit more.

                                      Regarding the confusion about numbers, I did notice that, and I've mostly parsed it by assuming that sometimes they were talking about the Garou population only, while the larger numbers given discussing individual septs might include Kinfolk. But that tendency for the tribes to form unofficial territories even while officially being part of the same sept was part of what led me to suspect that mono-tribal packs might be the norm, since that would make less sense if territory was being claimed largely by multi-tribe packs.

                                      I had originally trimmed down all of the local tribal populations by five or so, mostly intended to represent attrition through the conflict with the local Kindred that was set up in Rage Across the World and the Syndicate Convention book. But thinking about the fact that it's been over 25 years since DA:V was published, and declining population has generally been depicted as a problem the Garou are facing, I can probably whittle things down a bit further, get it more in line with recent source material.

                                      I want to try and keep the idea that every tribe is represented at the Great Caern, the Stargazers being the exception because they're no longer part of the Nation. But I can probably parse that by having most of the local Garou not officially part of the Sept of the Great Caern, but represented within it. As for the Smiling Buddha, at the moment I'm trying to decide whether or not I want it to have been taken over by Black Spiral Dancers; Pentex/SPD was involved in clearing it out, and it's right in the middle of the Downtown Eastside. Other possible local caerns is something I've been pondering; the Smiling Buddha was supposed to be one of a handful of "lost caerns" from the pre-colonial times, the only one rediscovered by that point, with Glass Walkers and Bone Gnawers looking for the others. I also suspect that the Fimbulwinter Sept of the Get has their own caern, as far from the city as they are.

                                      Regarding my ideas for multi-tribe packs, so far they have mostly consisted of groups of younger Garou from allied tribes, but there are a few cases of multi-tribe packs among the local leadership. Specifically the Silver Fangs and the Fianna, as well as the Black Furies with the Red Talons. In both cases, the local membership is so low that they can't form a 5+ pack on their own for their leaders, so they team up with an allied tribe. Other multi-tribe packs could definitely be put together for particular purposes, but I'd have to consider what those purposes would be.
