    Garou Nation vs Humanity

    09-23-2015, 02:26 PM
    One of the most compelling aspects of Werewolf: the Apocalypse, in my opinion, is the inherent antagonism between the werewolf race and humanity; as well as the idea of werewolves hunting people. One of the function of the Garou, according to their beliefs and culture, is to act as the immune system of Gaia, eradicating corruption, yes? People in the setting of Werewolf are not always, but often seen as tainted by the Weaver or Wyrm; and most tribes have some sort of issue with humans as a whole.

    And, of course, there's the Red Talons, who are pretty open about their disdain of all things human.

    (Disclaimer: I realize there's a vast difference between the game advocating misantrophy and racism - which clearly isn't the case - and the fictional werewolf race, in character, harboring collective resentment directed against humans. This isn't a thread about Werewolf supporting racism, it's a thread about the Garou culture and morality - pretty dark stuff - and how to use these things in the concept of an actual game).

    What also comes to mind is the idea of role reversal, that I believe is explored in the books in great detail - werewolves treating humanity the way humanity treats nature and animals; where becoming a werewolf means switching sides on the Man vs. Nature conflict.

    Now, the reason I started this thread is to get advice and to discuss how to deal with this particular theme in the context of an actual game. What sort of stories and plot points evolve out of these aspects of the game?

    Thoughts?

          Originally posted by Christian A View Post
          Now, the reason I started this thread is to get advice and to discuss how to deal with this particular theme in the context of an actual game. What sort of stories and plot points evolve out of these aspects of the game?
          I think that really depends upon what your players are like. You can go to some very ugly places with this particular issue, so it really depends upon what the players as a group function like. If they're okay delving into very squishy questions of morality (like was genocide against whole cultures during the Impergium actually morally defensible), then go to town with it. On the other hand, if they like things more clear cut "Good Guys vs. Bad Guys" and don't want to go much deeper than that, maybe this isn't the best theme to play upon, since if you look at some of the threads, this can get complicated.

          I for one really bristle when somebody makes the statement that the Garou have some sort of divine right to do pretty much anything they want (including outright murdering innocent people -- because of the "crimes" of society, "they're at war", or they're justified because they're predators). My view is that justifying such behavior as right and proper because, y'know, Gaia... is something that strikes me as incredibly shallow and reprehensible. If I were playing in a game where that's the default view -- I mean, like the "moral of the story" seems to reinforce that viewpoint, I very quickly wouldn't want to play anymore.

          On the other hand, I tend to hold that much of the problems that the Garou now face (and endlessly b**** about) are practically direct consequences of the psychological harm done to the humans during the Impergium, and to a lesser extent, their dismissive, contemptuous attitude towards the human race in general. I'd love to play in a game where that theme gets some play -- but there's all sorts of people on this board who clearly would feel that the Garou being morally responsible for the current state of affairs in the World of Darkness (and by extension, will be responsible for the Apocalypse whenever it comes) is thematically, the equivalent of running your nails down a chalkboard.

          So in short, I'd advise actually talking to your players about how they like to see the Garou nation and its morality vis-a-vis the Impergium, War of Rage, etc. before delving into the bottomless pit.

                  The old debate of are Garou heroes or monsters always comes back to this. From my perspective, the people who keep shoving left wing politics into the game setting keep trying to make the Garou heroes and keep attributing to them their version of politics. People who aren't as obsessed with left wing politics seem to be fine with allowing the Garou to be monsters as well as depicting positive attributes to their culture.

                  Personally, I like the tension between the positive aspects of the Garou and their monstrous aspects.

                  The main thing to remember is that Garou, like vampires, have a Beast, and it comes out as well. They are predators and have the instincts of predators. It's just that unlike vampires, the Garou are meant to be predators, and therefore the game is not about werewolves "losing" their humanity. If anything, it is about them accepting their predatory nature, but finding a balance with their spiritual side (Rage and Gnosis) and doing what they are supposed to be doing.

                      I find the way the Impergium is described in revised is unsatisfactory. I can see how humans have been culled (and more on that later), but there it is implied that garou ruled over mankind - terrorizing it. Such behaviour I find atypical for the garou, for there is no serious and logical reason for them to have done so. If there was, why wouldn't it have been passed on in oral garou culture to today. If it was instincts, why aren't the instincts in the garou today?
                      Culing, as with the agricultural term, makes sense. Not that the garou had any traits they wanted to be removed from the gene-pool, like breeders today do, when they cull their flock. But, to enforce some kind of Darwinism. Garou, being a force of nature humans had to deal with - not not really evil overlords oppressing them. That said, any village wouldn't be needed to constantly checked upon in the Stone Age. Coming to check on the humans every other year to kill the elderly and sick would probably enough.
                      Also, the Dawn Times already had the Litanei and it forbids to hunt humans for food, so that couldn't have been a reason to kill humans.

                      So, there is not legitimation to mistreat humans, besides culling in a responsible way, on the garou's side and there is no explanation why such a behaviour would have been told as what had happened in the Dawn times, until today. Also, not in a way where you still try to be proud of your Ancestors and a part of the people can even channel the ancestors.

                      It is said, that there were metaphysical reasons to stop the culling - and it has been said, that Children of Gaia wanted the humans to be educated so the would not grow unchecked. The Pure Ones seem to have had that method working, while the other tribes failed massively in that undertaking, and brought their failure to the Pure ones...

                      ----

                      So, in my opinion, the Impergium should just be a time where humans were killed by garou when old and weak. The same that happens to any other animal - but with the humans it had a special meaning, since they weren't prey. It shouldn't be depicted as a garou tyranny.

                      ----

                      That said, the Impergium was only ended in one regard - the total number of humans. Besides that, the garou still go around and kill humans that were sickened by the Wyrm (mentally or physically).
