    Shadow lords

    05-22-2022, 10:31 AM
    I recently have finished reading the Shadow lords revised tribebook and I liked it. They resemble in many ways the scorpion clan from Legend of the 5 rings, they are extremely loyal to Gaia, but also capable if any means to achieve their goals (exitus acta provat), even dealing and using the wyrm. As characters, they could be fertile ground to certain kind of problematic players, though. Their tendency to manipulate everyone and using even their packs is a narrative highway to hell, only appropriate to mature players and storytellers. What are your experiences with this tribe? Have you played them?

              I pretty much like all the tribes. As long as they provide enough concepts for STs and players while remaining true to their theme, I can use them in some fashion.

              I am not a big fan of any the Revised era sourcebooks because 1) they have a tendency to ignore the moral complexity and failings previously established in favor of a more black and white high fantasy metaplot, and 2) related to the first, they utterly ignore or retcon any prior canon that gets in the way of their new story to tell.

              Much of the extremely dubious dealings of the Shadow Lords were ignored. The secret society of the Masks is basically dismissed as irrelevant because of their new favorite Mary Sue NPC, the Margrave. Their "fear is the path to power" is just forgotten as a setting element. The Society of Nidhogg? Also completely eliminated once again by Margrave Mary Sue. The Lazarite Movement, which seemed an attempt by Shadow Lord metis to build their own personal power base among the Metis, also no longer exists. The Margrave is just that good of a leader!

              The Bringers of Light still exist, but curiously the book seems completely unconcerned about a camp whose main purpose is to justify Shadow Lords dealing with Wyrm minions. Despite repeating that many have fallen to the Wyrm, this doesn't seem to present any problems. Since the Bringers of Light philosophy is basically "I'm going to prove I can't become an alcoholic by drinking as much as I can", I always took their claims with a big dose of salt. Now I always liked this camp because it was an easy way to justify a Garou working with vampires to some degree. That is a good NPC in both Vampire or Werewolf, as an ST can do lots of things with it.

              Then we have the Judges of Doom, the star chamber vigilantes of the Garou Nation. Justice among the Garou is essentially sept based. So a self selected group of Shadow Lords who decide to take legal matters into their own hands is a very big problem. It means they are extra-judicial killers. But the revised tribebook just ignores the moral problems by stating the Margrave supports them so it's OK. I wonder what the other tribes think about this? Because many tribes handle their justice pretty severely, I imagine the Judges probably kill mostly Bone Gnawers... so maybe the other tribes don't mind. Maybe the Children of Gaia do, but they are probably the tribe next in line for the Judges' killings.

              There are some other issues with the revised sourcebook as well. It has the Typhon totem there. Unfortunately, the original Typhon totem was introduced in Rage Across Russia where it is explicitly a defector totem to the Wyrm so it can gain more power and escape the harsh demands of Thunder's rule, and it is slowly corrupting the Shadow Lord packs and septs that have it as as their totem. This is completely ignored in Revised as if it never happened. Is this not brought up in the tribebook because it is oriented to players, and they don't want to spoil the truth? Or is it another case of the writers simply ignoring canon to present the tribe in a much better light? Probably the latter.

              Lastly, I have a complaint more about omission. No mention is made of the Hakaken cults among the Shadow Lords, which is a thing we know from the Book of the Wyrm. I always thought this was a fascinating concept especially because Hakaken was supposed to be one of the greatest Garou heroes ever before he foolishly danced the Labyrinth. The story of Hakaken and his lingering cults among the Shadow Lords are an intriguing concept. Lost opportunity here. But given the general white washing of the tribe here (which happened to every tribe during the Revised era), it's not a surprise this was ignored.

              In many ways, the Shadow Lords were intended as one of the designated bad guys in early Werewolf, so I welcome a more nuanced approach to the tribe that show its loyalty to Gaia and the Garou Nation. But Revised went way too far in the other direction. They either outright eliminated the more villainous camps in the tribe, or simply pretended problematic ones presented any issues at all. By doing so, they made the tribe much less interesting. As an ST, I can do a lot more using some of these secret societies and camps than I can with "the Margrave is just so awesome and has taken care of all of these problems for you!". And as a player, the conspiratorial nature of the tribe is one of the reasons people like playing them. Getting ride of these secret conspiracies just makes the tribe more generic.

              It's perfectly OK for STs to disallow certain camps or concepts for PCs if it'll be too problematic for the game. But the wholesale elimination of them by an offscreen NPC, or ignoring them entirely, was not a good solution.

              The Shadow Lords were right proper bastards that you hoped would be on your side rather than against you, but you prepared accordingly. And I think that's how they should be.

                      Black Fox I actually think the heroic tone works better for the Lords. Because if they were straight-up token bad guys of the tribes, a lot of the nuance they gained in the revised would be lost.

                      Especially when you take them in the context of what they do to protect Gaia vs what they have accomplished. Shadow Lords are right bastards, they torture, tip-off vampires, maul, make deals with Wyrmy creatures and are extremely cutthroat among each other.

                      Yet, it is -these- garou who have managed to do one of the bigger reparations to the Fera. Not the Glass Walkers, not the Bone Gnawers, not the Children of Gaia and very much not the Silver Fangs These manipulative, power-hungry, litany tap-dancing torturers, who have actually succeeded in part-way redeeming a Wyrm totem. Because they never make the same mistake twice.

                      That to me creates a lovely question of ambiguity. What are the Lords doing that other tribes aren't? Why is it that brutality and manipulation have born fruit where compassion and kindness have not?

                      (And I prefer that Children of the Bat are legit. Because if they were just covert Wyrm-tainted, it would rob the situation of its entire point.)

                      Stuff like that is delicious moral ambiguity for me that I love to explore.
