                            So, there are three factions in this first book outlined thus far—four if you consider the unaffiliated Red Talons and Silent Striders.
                            • Western Concordat —Your garden variety Garou Nation outlook, traditions, and organization. Their interests are primarily centered around the usual song and dance of direct action in opposition to the Wyrm, a largely antagonistic perspective on the Weaver, and a very Calvinist kind of purity philosophy concerning social politics. They are much more attuned to understanding Harano and believe unity is what will drive it off. They think if the Tribes unify, Gaia will reawaken. They are all about reunification and reinforcing the traditions that have always stood. They're facing the reality they can't entirely afford to use Cliath as the cannon fodder they once were; there just aren't enough nowadays to do things the old way.
                            • Eastern Concordat — Highly humanized and rapidly coming to embrace the trappings of civilization—with caveats. They believe that if they heal the world and the destroyed Caerns, Gaia will follow and heal with them. They are best attuned towards rebuilding and reconstruction of the wreckage left by the Storm Eater. Many of their efforts are centered around building and nourishing Shard Caerns and covertly converting the population towards being better stewards of the world. In short, their society is in a 'post-war rebuild' mindset. They think a corrupt human society started it, and a healed and Gaian human society will fix it. They embrace Bitten far more than the other factions.
                            • Dawn Tribes — The revivalists of Turtle Island and Abya Yala hold a widely varied number of stances, but at its core sits the belief that this is not the first Apocalypse but one of many, having occurred to many societies and civilizations in the Dawn Tribes and that the end has come for the Garou Nation as well. To them, this is the age of the Fifth Sun. Unlike the other factions, they believe all three of the triat are vital and must be disentangled from one another. Of all the factions, they are all the most aligned towards spiritually decolonizing the nations and their homes. They were handling the Wyrm perfectly well before the Wyrmcomers arrived, and want a return of their precolonial traditions. They embrace their kin and crinos more than the others.
                            When push comes to shove, all three will go to war when the Wyrm shows its face and will aid one another (in each tribes' way, good luck convincing the Balam to do it any way other than their own.) They all follow The Litany, at least by their interpretations of it. Regarding the Council of Tribes and the higher ranks, you can find much room for cloak and dagger kind of cold war conflict. I think that the higher the Garou rank, the more entrenched they are in a social conflict between factions, but Damian has it right when push comes to shove. Packs that were together before the nation fractured are still together, and they're like:

                            "My dad said we're enemies now."
                            "Yeah. Wanna go kill this Black Spiral pack?"
                            "Sure!"

                            I'd think of it a lot less like a cold war between countries and more of a messy, ongoing divorce between people who still love one another; they argue over pets and cars and housewares, but what they really want is eachother. If you want to know some direct inspiration, I listened to the new Sufjan Stevens album Javelin while doing the primary writing for the factions. Eastern Concordat is "Shit Talk," Dawn Tribes are "Goodbye Evergreen," and Westcons are "So You Are Tired." People understand their hands are tied mainly in tribe Politics, but everyone is still willing to work together, short of their main differences. The Westcons are unlikely ever to treat their kin and crinos entirely the same as the other factions and still hold a more conservative view of charachs. There will be dynamics where faction influence ought to have a major say in any given sept's overall policies and disposition. When the unified fight ends though, many of the old wounds still remain. An excellent approach to viewing the factions here is that the things that motivate them to action are a strong defining point of unity.

                            Once thing to pay attention to when you do read the chapter on the tribes is the opening passage for each of them, which I'm calling their Call to Action. That is something that, at its core, defines what the tribe does both in its faction and abroad. It is sorta like the 'verbs' but in a more pathologized format. The same goes for Gifts too. What your character stands for more defines the Gifts they'll have access to than their XP or freebie allocations, breed, auspice, tribe, or even the faction they're a part of.

                            Rage asks the Killi "When Will You Rage?"
                            Gnosis asks the Killi "What Do You Stand For?"

                                            Yeah, its more or less as Ana Mizuki puts it, and you're right about the striders staying adamantly neutral to keep the lines of communication open. The idea of the Red Talons being organized feels too human to me (and the idea of them in prior editions deferring to a kingly figure feels silly.) This isn't to say they don't have septs or sept roles, but when seen from the outside, they're almost indistinguishable from a Yellowstone superpack, minus the shapeshifting and violence that often follows their revels, and propensity for fighting the Wyrm. It wasn't that they refused to join the council of tribes or any one faction; they've no emissaries capable of speaking for the entire tribe.

                                            I've written them as a Tribe that doesn't even entirely identify as a Tribe, at least in the same way the other Tribes think of it. They are better seen here as an autonomous collective of mostly unaffiliated all-wolf septs. I've written them presented as a tribe that didn't come together. Instead, Griffin sought out, handpicked, and convinced them of his politics long ago, spreading and coalescing a "You and I are wolves, but we are not the same." mindset throughout these previously uncontacted lupus septs. From there, it spread to known septs, and the Talons began effectively taking Lupus from other tribes, taking off in greater numbers around the Dark Ages (see Red Talons and Older/Younger Brother.) A tribe that grew memetically through shared unexpressed grief at a world that is increasingly shrinking, along with their families and loved ones. A significant reason the tribe persists is the continuation of the tradition of Griffin convincing lupus of other tribes to join his cause. Their extreme position is one they arrived at through desperation rather than an organized decision.

                                            I tweaked their history a little to make them a little more playable and a lot more relatable. They never even named themselves Red Talons, nor did they agree to the accords, but rather, a single lupus that didn't claim membership to anything called a tribe showed up, declared "I am a wolf like you, we are all wolves." When pressed to name their tribe, paired with increasing frustrations between the exchanges, the Garou finally lashed out and was like, "You want to know what I am? This is what I am!" and clawed the other Garou, meeting the demand with three bloody gashes before leaving, earning wolves like that one the label of Red Talon. Gifts, rites, and traditions remain more or less the same here; otherwise, they are just a bit more tragic and narratively sensical for an all-wolf tribe. In WtE, they start out of the running gates with being the one tribe out of them all with no more caerns of their own, having lost them at the apex of the Age of Apocalypse. This puts them into an existential crisis of facing extinction for learning how to compromise and work with other Tribes to learn and start building their own Shard Caerns, creating divisions within the "Tribe" itself.

                                            Additionally, with homids facing a declining population for the first time, this has created a talking point for the two to relate better to one another. This unknown position they're in and the direction it goes is ultimately for the tabletop to decide. In the long term, I'm hoping it steers in the direction of 'growing pangs towards something greater,' but not all tables are likely to play it like that.

                                            When the Nation split, they initially joined the Western Concordat. When the Glass Walkers quit the eastern concordat and joined the westcons it so thoroughly alienated the Red Talons that they dropped what tenuous affiliations they once had altogether. This outcome is one that's driven mixed opinions between tribes. On one hand, they are losing potential allies. On the other hand, with the Red Talons out of the Western Concordat, the faction is a lot less likely to trend towards extremism, leading some to think the Glass Walkers scoffing at the Eastcons' acceptance of the Hearthbound and joining the Westcons may have been a political maneuver catalyzed by the Shadow Lords to shift the Overton Window away from extremism.

                                                        The old Caern-building rites have effectively become bane-summoning rites from start to finish, making the creation of new caerns (called Great Caerns in this setting) more or less impossible. A shard caern is so-named after its heart, which is little more than a spiritual sliver taken from the heart of one of the 13 remaining Great Caerns. They are weaker and far more vulnerable to attack but retain the same abilities and powers reflecting the Great Caern that provided the shard.

                                                        Kahaila, the Native American Ao, have returned. With the awakening of Turtle came their reawakening with it, each of them following a dream that jolted each of them awake of Gaia's Heart, a long-extinct gnostic flower believed to bridge the physical and spiritual world naturally. They, in turn, began seeking and awakening Hatha, Native American black bears of the Dawn Tribes who possess the means to heal the land and bring fossilized flora and fauna back to life. The Gurahl understood their duty from Gaia as her healers and began reluctantly restoring communication with both the Dawn Tribes and the Children of Gaia. Margrave Josef Konietzko saw an opportunity to form a new faction, and while other tribes were still reeling from the Sundering of their Caerns, threw the full might of the Shadow Lords' support behind the Children of Gaia to launch a go-for-broke expedition deep into Pangaea to recover a specimen of this plant.

                                                        In short, they succeeded. They recovered the flower, a trillium-like plant called Gaia's Heart, from fossils from the Middle Devonian Epoch, when bones first appeared. This is from a time when Gaia was so powerful; she held an omniscient awareness of her realm, and the entire Gaian Realm was as spiritually charged as a caern, held together in a fine mycelial network connecting these delicate plants. Even in Pangaea, they managed to recover a single fossilized specimen. It was enough. They were able to bring it back to life, and upon planting it on a Great Caern's bawn and carefully tending it with ancient rites, it bloomed and began producing Shard Seeds.

                                                        When planted and cared for in this manner, Shard Seeds produce more Gaia's Heart flowers. When grown in a cleansed and spiritually reawakened site of a former Caern, a Shard Caern is formed. Margrave formed his faction around these Shard Seeds' cultivation (and control), cementing the formation of the Eastern Concordat. With the Dawn Tribes inherently possessing the means of creating and nurturing shard seeds of their own, the power the Western Concordat used to hold over these tribes rapidly unraveled, and the Western Concordat has found itself needing to cooperate and be diplomatic in ways that would have devolved into fighting in the past.

                                                        The process of cleansing and reawakening a fallen Caern results in a Sept being able to be formed, but without a shard seed, the Caern cannot become more powerful than level 1. The area surrounding them is called a periphery, and shard seeds are planted to form a new heart. The elements and phases of planting the seed are similar to old building rites in that you have to prepare the land, clear it of threats, prepare extensive defenses, and then it begins. The ritemaster performs the Rite of the Furrowed Earth to plant the seed. If successful, the caern's old Patron is then capable of being summoned, extensive rites of contrition and chiminage performed. As a final step, the Rite of Acceptance is performed, binding the Patron to the Shard Seed. This part of the rite summons a storm of banes, though at a stage where the Patron can help with the defenses. The shard seed germinates if successful, and a new Shard Caern is formed. The Kahaila believe that with time and vigilant defense, the shard caerns can eventually grow in power to become a Great Caern once more, though being only ten years since the battle of Crescent Moon, none have yet grown to this point.
