    Simba Gifts

    Submit (Kneel)
    Armour of Kings (Luna's Armour), Heart of Storms (Might of Thor)
    Coup de Grace, Wrath of Kings (Wrath of Gaia)
    King of Beasts (King of the Beasts)
    Obedience

    Note that King of the Beasts (the exact name varies between W20 and W20CB) is adjusted to impact all animals within 300 feet, rather than a single animal within 100 feet; it is naturally bumped up from Rank 2 to Rank 4. Unconditional absolute power over every animal within 300 feet is potentially extremely strong, especially in Africa. Kneel is only Level 1 for Simba, instead of Level 2. For Obedience make sure to check out the Shadow Lords entry in the Garou thread.

    Majesty (1): +1 to Leadership and Intimidation. Pretty handy for Simba, and for only 3xp it's cheaper than buying +1 to both Talents.

    Sun Claws (2): Spend a turn, and 1 point of Rage, activating this Gift. For the rest of the scene your fangs and claws deal +2 dice of damage, or +2 levels of damage against Vampires. A neat bonus, and worth activating if you have it and can prep before the fight with razor claws and so on.

    However the turn economy of the power, along with the fact that it makes it harder to regenerate (bumping the Stamina roll from difficulty 8 to difficulty 9 if you try to regenerate without resting) does make it harder to use.

    Swara Gifts

    Blissful Ignorance, Impala's Flight (Speed of Thought)
    Sense the Unnatural, Walking Between Worlds
    Messenger's Fortitude, Speed Beyond Thought

    Note that while Sense the Unnatural is normally Green, an argument could be made for downgrading it here. The Gift is amazing, just not necessarily on a Bastet, who get an improved version of Sense Wyrm, and Sense Magic too. Sense the Unnatural does give some budget 'Scent of the True Form' type abilities, sure, but it's not impossible for you to acquire that Gift either. All in all Sense the Unnatural remains good, but if you get a chance to buy Scent of the True Form for 5xp, Sense the Unnatural arguably becomes 100% redundant on a Bastet. Swara also uniquely get to Step Sideways at Rank 2.

    Ghost Caress (4): This Gift lets you send sensations to a distant target using a 'focus', another person who you touch directly. This can't truly harm the recipient but will probably upset them.

    Spend 1 Willpower, roll Gnosis, difficulty 7 for a short distance and 9 for for 50 miles. Whatever you do to the focus is felt by the target instead.

    I'm gonna say it. This power is weirdly sensual, and the write-up is all but explicitly sexual. I'm not necessarily a huge fan of that in this context. For some reason at Rank 4 Werecheetahs unlock "Sex Crime: The Gift", at least the way this is written. Naturally use of this Gift could be consensual, you could give your ally a really nice back massage while they're in a meeting or tap a message to them through morse code, or play a prank on a friend where you suddenly give them the sensation of being dipped into ice water up to the neck when they aren't expecting it.

    You'll note that none of these feel like things a Rank 4 Fera will be doing. If this were a Level 1 Gift it might rank well, but this doesn't sit well at Rank 4. For the utility of driving a Pentex rep in a meeting crazy by repeatedly waterboarding the focus... I'll give this Amber. Just keep it PG13 people. Well, each to their own, every table is different.

    River of Blood (5): Spend 1 Rage, roll Manipulation + Primal-Urge difficulty 7, or difficulty 5 if you're in a place where significant blood has been shed (recently or historically).

    After 3 turns blood coalesces from the soil, taking forms of your choosing. Strength and Health Levels of any structures you make with blood are equal to your successes x2, so with 5 successes you could make a constricting tentacle with a Strength of 10 and 10 Health Levels. Unfortunately since these structures don't have any other guidance on how they work, it's hard to say how this plays out. Ultimately the 3 turn delay is the real problem here.

        Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
        I think the Changing Breeds suffer from saturation.
        I agree. I don't mind those breeds that clearly exist in folklore - almost every human society has a werecreature based on the local apex predator in their legends. However, some breeds are clearly more derived from high fantasy than the real world mythology and legends that the World of Darkness is based on.

        I'm a big believer that things not included in the original corebooks don't need to be part of any game. So I go with an ala carte approach and feel fine picking and choosing what additional elements work in my chronicles, rather than feel compelled to include everything ever published.

        Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
        I love the Khan and the Simba, but rationally I feel that one feline breed was enough.
        I can see why there are multiple batest. Most of the big cats don't overlap except in a few places. If you are playing in North America, you really only need to include the Pumonca in your setting if mountain lions are present locally. Only if you really want to explore things like this would you expand into the Qualmi. The other Bastet don't need to be present at all.

        The Balam, Simba, and Khan likewise work well as the sole species in the places they are from. The Bagheera and Swara can easily be ignored unless one is running a Bastet only chronicle and you set it in India or Africa.

        The Bubasti and Ceilican can easily be dumped, and the setting won't lose much. These are very minor creatures that exist more for crossover and to be "special" than any real purpose.

        Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post
        I'd say the thing that makes me question the setting's cohesion the most is that the Ratkin are extremely numerous, outnumbering the Garou many times over. Similar thing with the Ananasi. Things start feeling crowded.
        Ratkin and the Ananasi are the two breeds I think are least defensible from a perspective that the World of Darkness should be based on some kind of real world lore (or at least favorite gothic horror tropes). They seem high fantasy creatures to me. I know some players love them, but they don't do anything for me. So I don't use them in my setting at all. I do have some creatures like them, but they are fomori horror inspired enemy instead. And the only reason they exist is that when I was diversifying the kinds of Wyrm factions that the Garou could fight, a quick glimpse at the fomori powers already showed these archetypes were available. So after cobbling together their fomori powers, I just need to think up of some justification why these fomori exist - usually some Wyrm cult worshipping some strange Wyrm Incarna in Malfeas.

        I feel the same way about the Lost Breeds that perished during the War of Rage, but because they became extinct in the Stone Age that explains why modern people have no legends about them. So I am OK with these having once existed.

                    I really don't mind the WoD just making stuff up; it's Urban Fantasy where they take a kernel of human folklore and blow it up into something mostly unique to the setting. Part of what I love about WoD is explaining things and getting responses like "wait, like Cain from the Bible, that Cain", or "what do you mean space isn't real" or "...so 9/11 brought them back?"

                    So I don't mind weresharks, wererats, were(every type of cat)s, or fictional ancient Egyptian cats which are enslaved by the Setites as Ghouled pets, or were-fae-cats which might be related to wild Scottish cats or perhaps the cats that wander the lands of the Sidhe, and I don't mind hacker Werewolves and homeless Werewolves and mutated werewolf-werewolf offspring. If you removed everything in the WoD which didn't have at least one foot in folklore, you'd lose a lot. In the context of the WoD, there are human legends in existence which don't exist IRL, and there are also supernatural factions which went undetected or who were heavily misenterpreted in history.

                    I run into this problem when I'm talking to someone from a random country and they go "well in our local folklore all vampires can do fleshcrafting-y stuff, so it makes sense to me that 100% of vampires in my country would be Tzimisce-" No! Your knowledge of your local folklore is great, awesome, but I don't get the logic that human myths based on possible medieval sightings of what might have been vampires should dictate modern politics. Maybe the most famous Masquerade breach to ever happen in the country was based around a Tzimisce. Or maybe the area is like 33% Tzimisce, or was back then, so when Masquerade breaches did happen the tales tended to include warped bodies and alien forms. Or hell, maybe those weren't vampires at all.

                    CTD has the greatest responsibility to get this stuff right, since it's ostensibly largely based on human beliefs; for everything else ordinary humans are looking at everything through a keyhole into a room full of smoke, interpreting the noises and shadows as best they can.

                    That said, the number of Ratkin and Ananasi in existence just makes me laugh. The simple fix is to make their kinfolk exceptionally numerous but make the werebeasts themselves very rare. I don't like the idea that Nosferatu bump into Ratkin or are aware of their existence; they should be rare enough, and in big enough sewer networks, that they are realistically 100% under the radar.

                        Miscellaneous mechanical details.

                        By paying 3, 4 or 5 Freebie points you can free yourself of 1, 2 or 3 of your Yava via a Merit. Given that some of the Yava are utterly lethal, I think it's worth getting rid of at least those. The explanations offered are a blessing from a spirit (good plot hook) or simply that you're a 'strange prodigy' which I like less. An advantage to the 5 point version is that if you are totally free of your Yava, that means enemy won't be able to take advantage of the mystic benefits of knowing your Yava. This manifests as -2 difficulty on supernatural rolls in one direction and +2 in the other direction, which is pretty significant!

                        For Frenzying, Bastet are much like the Garou but use a fixed difficulty of 6.

                        Crinos inflicts Reduced Delirium, Chatro inflicts Full Delirium.

                        Bastet get access to the Sex Appeal Merit, Cat Magic and Step Sideways Merits. Step Sideways is particularly interesting since it lets you cross the gauntlet 2-4 Ranks early depending on your circumstances (Rank 4 for most Bastet, Rank 3 for Beast Courts, Rank 2 for Swara). Cat Magic could be anything or nothing, it might let you learn Sorcery or mimic Gifts more easily or create your own Gifts/Rites with relative ease.

                        Bastet also get access to the Secrets and Den-Realm Backgrounds, but they usually can't take Ancestors. They tend to have a personal Totem rather than a pack Totem.

                        A Bastet's Pyrio is like a much less extreme Auspice, and it grants an additional means through which you can recover WP while slightly influencing your personality.

                        Bastet use Cunning, Ferocity and Honour. They Rank up much like a Ragabash; you simply need to reach a certain total.

                        Finally, the Bastet use Rites very similar to those of the Garou (though they prefer one-person Rites, such as the majority of Mystic Rites), with one noteworthy addition.

                        Cat Calls allows a Bastet to put a message into a certain type of movement; say they focus on the movement of traffic, or a group of cubs frolicking, or the blooming of a flower. The information contained in this message can be up to a minute's speech, or equivalent, and any Bastet who happens to see a similar movement (traffic or cubs or flowers elsewhere in the world) will have a chance to pick up on the message by making a Wits + Enigmas, difficulty 5-8 depending on proximity. This is pretty great, especially for a Rank 1 Rite, and it helps to explain how the Bastet keep eachother updated despite being solitary.

                            Final Verdict

                            So, how good are the Bastet? Let's list pros and cons using Garou as a baseline.

                            +++ Great Gift lists including an excellent sensory setup for finding Triat strength, magic, special items and so on
                            +++ Forms tend to favour Dexterity, which is excellent for killing, survival and stealth; Chatro is often a very strong form
                            ++ However the Storyteller handles Bastet 'stealing' or 'copying' Gifts, chances are it will be a significant advantage
                            ++ Able to play a character with a high starting Rage without limiting yourself to a non-mystic role or wasting Freebies
                            + Able to inflict Reduced Delirium if they wish, which might be helpful to minimize chaos at times
                            + Pyrios grant an additional means of recovering Willpower
                            + Even a 1-dot Den-Realm (a house or square mile) is a significant advantage, even once you learn to Step Sideways naturally

                            - Yava can be a serious threat if you don't protect yourself against them with a Fetish or Merit
                            - No Pack structure by default, though this will be overridden in a game with a core premise of a pack forming under unusual circumstances
                            - Unable to Step Sideways unless you spend 7 freebie points or 6/8/12 xp at Rank 2/3/4 depending on your situation
                            - Generally unable to access Ancestors
