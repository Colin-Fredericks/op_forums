    Rite of the Shrouded Glen

    01-30-2022, 10:35 PM
    So this level 4 rite renders the interior of a bawn permanently invisible if 10 gnosis is spent upon the casting... but what exactly is the benefit? I can see how it could be useful to prevent scrying, but beyond that...?

    So, a couple of questions:
    • What does invisible mean, exactly? If somebody scries, do they see nothingness, indicating something is hidden? A penumbral reflection of what the land would look like without the caern being there? Or...?
    • Does the rite prevent people walking up to the bawn from seeing what lies inside?
    • Is the Sept invisible to just creatures of the wyrm, or anybody that's not part of the sept, or..?
    • Other then the Rite of Luna's Eye and the Uktena gift of scrying, what other scrying abilities/gifts are there that this gift would foil?
    Sorry if these questions are obtuse. Just excited about the rite, but a little confused about the particulars. Thanks!

      This is a good question. Our minds think alike. I think one reason I respond to your posts is that often I had to figure these things out in my own chronicles merely 6-12 months before you ask them for yours.

      Obviously the Rite somehow protects the caern by preventing enemies from easily figuring out there is a caern there to raid or invade. After all, we know Mages, marauding spirits, and BSDs would want to despoil them. But first they would have to find them. But to your point, what does that mean in practical terms?

      The mere existence of this caern rite logically implies that a caern is easier to find or detect without this rite being performed. So what should that look like? Well, we're not given descriptions to that effect as far as I can tell. But I can think of some things that might be noteworthy.

      First, caerns often have large concentrations of Garou or other Changing Breeds during their moots. "Hey, it's the night of the full moon, and I can see there's a crap load of Garou over there. Must be a caern there."

      Second, Garou often open Moon Bridges in the Umbra. A huge moon bridge popping up would be an obvious sign a caern is present. It's like how if you hear jet engines taking off, you're probably near an airport.

      Third, caerns are homes to a lot of spirits besides the caern totem. The spiritual energies of a caern often awaken or attract Gaian spirits. Congregations of large number of spirits is usually because there's either a domain present or a caern is present. So seeing lots of spirits might be a sign that a caern is present.

      Fourth, a caern's penumbra is supposed to be much more integreated with the physical world than elsewhere. It does not mean an exact relationship, but it should at least be identifiable as to where it is. Even things that aren't physically the same, should be spiritually reflections of such (such as the Grave of Hallowed Heroes being obvious as to what it is). Someone who knows the physical world's layout and has some knowledge of caerns, should be able to identify a caern.

      Fifth, we have the caern heart. The caern heart is always identifiable as self-contained area, easily seen as what lies within it and outside it. And inside the caern hear, the physical realm and spiritual world is one and the same. In Garou septs, this is almost always guarded by at least one character like the Warder. So again, this is easily identifiable if one knows what one is looking for.

      There may be more things that this, but currently this is what I can think of.

      Therefore, I reason the Rite of the Shrouded Glen must eliminate these tell tale signs. By making an area of the Umbra invisible (and since this is a caern rite, I assume it can only be done at a caern) it creates a "bubble" where if you are outside it, you can't see these things. Instead, you only see what would normally exist without a caern (Garou and other people are invisible, spirits can't be seen, moon bridges don't appear, none of the distinctive tell tale signs of a caern show, etc.). You only see what the penumbra would be like as if the caern did not exist. Only if you enter the "bubble", would these things appear. And of course, since everything inside the bubble can see outside of it, they (Garou, the caern totem, allied spirits, whatever) can take action if they see something approaching that shouldn't. They can arrange interceptions, deceptions, ambushes, etc. as needed.

      The Rite of the Shrouded Glen therefore works best against foes who don't already know that there is a caern there. You have strategic surprise in your favor. It's less effective when your enemy already knows there is a caern there from prior knowledge, but even then it still provides tactical surprise for the sept's Garou. If the bad guys approach a caern, they still can't see inside the bubble until they reach it. They have no idea if the sept is unwary, or if they are completely armed and ready for them. They may not know the strongpoints and defenses established internally. All they would know is a vague notion that somewhere in this vicinity there's a caern.

      To some of your questions, if the Rite completely made the area completely blank/invisible, it would defeat the purpose of the Rite. "Hey, there's just a blank white space there, so it must be a caern." It'd be self-defeating because more often than not, you're drawing more attention to yourself, not less. A level four Rite should be very useful. It should be a cornerstone of any sept's defenses.

      To a certain degree, I think it's similar to how the land of Elfwood is "invisible" in Disenchantment.


      That's how I'm running it right now. Open to change based on interesting ideas and advice.

                          Originally posted by pwtucker View Post
                          The Athro is going to call in favors to have the Rite of the Shrouded Glen cast, but seeing as the large BSD presence in town is already aware of the sept's location, what else could you see a new Grand Elder implementing in terms of security measures?
                          Who you pick as the Warder and Guardians will be important since those will likely be the only Garou on hand when the BSDs attack. Since they could very well die defending the caern, you want experienced Garou, but not the people you want to be future leaders who will need to survive.

                          You may also want some kind of actual defensive structure they can retreat too on the caern heart (if possible) so they can prolong the combat as long as possible. Something that prevents the BSDs from using their numbers to their advantage, and which would allow wounded Garou to retreat and heal up before going back to the frontline.

                          Other than that, there are some Fetishes that should always be useful.

                          Sanctuary Chimes will prevent any unfriendly spirits materializing.

                          Guardians of the Caerns has several fetishes designed for sept defense. One is the Sentry's Voice, which is a horn that only the sept members can hear. Another is the Watchspear that warns of Wyrm creatures in the Umbra when they approach within 200 yards.

                          But you don't want to leave behind Fetishes that can't be allowed to fall into enemy hands. You don't want the sept's only Klaive to be there. You want it in someone else's hand who can escape.

                          It's also good to have certain Talens stockpiled before hand they can be used extensively like Bane Arrows, Gaia's Breath, Nightshade, and Wyrm Sign. That'll be good for the Garou stuck in their little fortress trying to fend off 50 BSDs.

                          You will also want good scouts that can patrol the Bawn or even spy on the known locations of BSDs. That might mean use of talens like Chiopteran Spies. Local spirits already living on the Bawn. Spirits you bound into service or have given chiminage too who. That may notify you in advance of an attack so which might put enough people on alert that the counterattack can be quicker. Likewise, some of these spirits could be used for actual combat.

                          Originally posted by pwtucker View Post
                          In-game, I've had the BSD's ignore the caern until now due to their being pre-occupied with their own business (cue long term machinations to summon an Incarna of the Wyrm) but they number around 50 or so in Detroit (multiple hives) and could steamroll the caern if it ever properly caught their attention.
                          Yeah, that large a number of BSDs would certainly overrun a caern. That being the case, it would behoove the sept to have planned for it to fall initially given the number of Garou they have at their disposal. Instead of sacrificing everything in a futile defense (although some guardians would need to sacrifice themselves on principle to maintain the sept's honor and to defend the caern totem), what the sept needs to do is have organized a counterattack with reinforcements to occur in a time period before the BSDs can corrupt the caern. In other words, have the army fall back into the Bataan Peninsula and activate War Plan Orange (note, this actually didn't turn out well for MacArthur in the Philippines so even good plans can fail).

                          That means the Gatekeeper (the sept's diplomat) sending out her Moonwalkers and making alliances with those septs willing to commit to this caern's defense and send overwhelming numbers of Garou to take back the sept. Given every sept wants to ensure their sept is defended first and the population decline of Garou, this isn't necessarily easy. But in Under a Blood Red Moon, the fall of the Fanum caused a large number of Garou to assemble to take it back. So ultimately I think "delay the enemy, and wait for reinforcements" will need to be the primary defensive strategy.

                          That means lots of preparations. Work on establishing those alliances, and have certain packs stay behind to harry the BSDs in a guerilla war and collect intelligence that will be useful when the counterattack occurs. For example, rather than have the sept's packs who aren't Guardians come rushing into the caern, where they might be slaughtered, they may want to have a predetermined assembly point, and someone who can perform the rite of the Badger's Burrow who can ask questions like "Has the caern fallen yet?", "How many Black Spiral Dancers are attacking?", "What other Wyrm creatures are with them?" and the like. Given Garou's Rage and culture, this might be easier said than done, so it's important good leaders are selected who can command the others, and they follow a flexible, but prepared plan.

                          As General Eisenhower said, plans are useless, but planning is indispensable. The sept's members should have a clear idea on the general strategy and what they need to do when the attack comes. But be prepared to be flexible since they can't predict what the BSDs will actually do. This is where the importance of having good Adrens and Athros come into play to be the leaders that the cliaths and fosterns need.

                                          Question: how long would it take a bunch of BSD's to corrupt a level 2 sept? Is there a rite they must perform? Is it a gradual process, or something they build up to and then execute?
                                          The only place I know that addresses the issues of corrupting or reclaiming a caern is the second edition Players Guide to Werewolf. That has a lot of information that does not seem to be mentioned anywhere else, but since it is not a Revised product, it often gets overlooked. The relevant pages are on page 110-111. There are probably very few games that actually use these rules. So while I am summarizing them for you, it doesn't mean it's something you must use. But even so, it might be good for inspiration.

                                          We're told several things. First, the Black Spiral Dancers must perform the Rite of Desceration. The first half of the rite is to bind the caern totem to the physical realm. It may take as long as 36 hours for the first half of the Rite to be performed. The ritemaster must make Manipulation + Rituals rolls (Diff 7). He needs to gather 50 successes on extended rolls while not making more than 18 rolls. If he botches at any time, the rite fails and the caern totem escapes. If the ritemaster is disturbed at any time, the rite fails and he takes 5 aggravated health levels from the angry caern totem. Presumably, if the ritemaster cannot complete the ritual in 18 rolls, the rite also fails and he would take 5 aggravated health levels and the caern totem escapes.

                                          Simultaneous with the ritemaster making checks, there is a second portion of the Rite. Some other BSD (a chosen champion) must duel the caern totem. Only if this Dancer defeats the totem AND the ritemaster makes all the successes needed, a new Wyrm totem appears and the the defeated caern totem is forced to serve the new Totem.

                                          THEN the ritemaster must make one final Charisma + Rituals roll (diff 5 + caern level) to transform the caern into a new corrupted caern type. WP cannot be spent. The # of successes is the number of ranks the new Pit has. The new type is some corrupt variant of the old type. If the ritemaster fails in this roll, he may be shunted into Erebus (!) and any other BSD rite participants take five aggravated damage.

                                          (The next pages after this section then has the way that Gaian Garou can reclaim a corrupted caern, so this is not a one way thing.)

                                          This provides us a lot of information, but it leads to a lot of questions, and there are aspects of the rite I personally don't like. We're not told the level of the Rite. It seems to be that each roll of the Rite takes two hours to perform, but this is not made explicit. I also presume that should the caern totem escape, then the ritemaster can't perform the final check to corrupt the caern. But again, this is not explicit. What happens if the other BSD defeats the totem, but the ritemaster fails? Can he try again and start anew, or is it over? What happens when the caern totem flees (as caern totems are not supposed to be able to leave the caern), does it mean the caern is rendered inactive, destroyed, something else? (If so, can it be reactivated, or would it need an entire new Rite of Caern Building). What happens to a defeated caern totem serving a Wyrm spirit if the second roll fails? What happens if more than one BSD attempts to fight the caern totem? Presumably that causes the rite to fail. As written, there is no connection between the rank of a Gaian caern and the corrupted caern. You could end up with a Rank 5 Pit in place of a Rank 1 caern. Does that make sense? Maybe you want to limit the corrupted caern rank to be no higher than the level of the former caern.

                                          There's also another issue. The Rite of Desecration is to corrupt a Gaian caern into a new BSD Pit. They don't need to do this if all they want to do is pollute or ravage the caern so it can no longer function in its purpose. Just ruin its appropriateness for whatever type of caern there is. Presumably the BSDs also have a variant of the Rite of Caern Building to build straight up Wyrm pits instead of corrupting Gaian caerns. But I don't think that's ever mentioned.

                                          So despite the wealth of information provided in this section, it is also insufficient. An ST wanting to use the 2e Players Guide rules will still need to make a lot of calls.

                                          So what's the purpose of the BSDs here? It's probably not to just maraud and damage the caern. Belle Isle is in the middle of Detroit, not an isolated part of the wilderness. They just can't truck in toxic waste and dump it. It'd cause too many issues with the humans. If they want to do or some other means of spoiling the caern, they'll likely have to prepare things so they can do it - which should give Garou time to respond assuming they spot these preparations. Presumably, they do want to corrupt the caern.

                                          But corrupting the caern will take some effort. They will need a chosen champion who can defeat the caern totem in a ritualized combat. And they will need a highly skilled ritemaster who can collect the necessary number of extended successes. If those two Garou participate in the attack, they'll need time to recover. If they are held back until the battle is won, then they could theoretically begin the Rite of Desecration early. But remember the BSDs will need other participants in the rite, and will probably want as many members as they can get to reduce the difficulty. They'll also need other BSDs to protect the rite group. The Ritemaster certainly will not want to start the Rite until he's sure he can do so without interruption as it could easily kill him or ruin the rite (this is one area where ongoing harassment by sept members could be beneficial - it could buy time for others to arrive in force. Even a suicide mission by a small group of Garou could be successful in disrupting the rite for one turn and causing it to fail).

                                          This could also be used to explain why BSDs don't attack the caern. They don't want to prematurely attack it before they completely their own preparations for the caern. Maybe they are awaiting for a highly skilled Theurge to arrive, or there are disputes among the Hive as to who will be the chosen champion to duel the caern totem, or they need time to arrange a terrible "accident" to occur at Belle Isle shortly after their intended attack and conquest. It's not just a matter of attacking and sept and killing Garou. They need their own plan to accomplish their aims afterwards. If it means taking over the caern, it means being able to defend the new Hive. Maybe they need to build their numbers up even more?

                                          Rather than running the Rite of Desecration as is, you may want to simplify it for your needs. If you want to make things urgent, limit it to 24 hours before the Garou need to counterattack. Or maybe you want to give the BSDs some time to recover and pacify the area before performing the Rite. That would give outside Garou more time to respond (and this is realistic, it takes time to summon and assemble enough Garou to defeat 50 BSDs). And it would allow a stay behind group (perhaps the PCs) to conduct harassments of the BSDs so they keep delaying performance of the Rite (perhaps even indefinitely). Or you may want to establish a set period of time you think is just right - like 48 or 72 hours or whatever number you like.

                                          Or go your own way using the things you like as inspiration.
