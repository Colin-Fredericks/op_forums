    Rage in Scotland

    06-29-2023, 07:32 AM
    So I've been running this game in Scotland (white howler renewal) and by reading many posts here and documenting myself I'm starting to slowly flesh out the country on the supernatural level. Here is more or less the things I've thought about, or starting to think about implementing.

    I like the idea of Scotland being a sort of liminal country, thinking of it a bit like the Egypt of the UK where the supernatural is pretty much alive in the thoughts of people, especially locals like Highlanders that would for example keep putting some milk outside their windows for the fairies and such. They consider everything "magical-ish" to be fairy or ghost related. Umbra habitants have an easier time crossing the veil/gauntlet everywhere in the country.
    ​

    I've created a map where I document everything that could be of interest so: all pictish stones sites, stone circles, some burials sites, radiocative wastes/spillage, faery/ghosts sites. I also added official mentions of caerns, pits and other WoD things. Though since I don't really read a lot of non-werewolf books I may have missed a lot of information except for Changelin/fae since they have a specific supplement for the british isles.

    Werewolf: The country is mainly populated by Fianna and Fenris, most caern are mono tribes caern with a few exceptions. Every caern is in serious distress with the BSD campaign to get back their country, a few caern have been lost over the years. I only 5 caerns for gaian garou, against 8 pits (some being ancient caerns). I may be looking to add one or two more gaian caern for the fianna and/or fenris. I don't have much in the highlands, and I'm thinking of the feroe islands for the fenris since the shetland is an absolute wyrm hell with the whole pretrol thing in sullom voe.
    The big idea is to keep the werewolves here under constant pressure, they don't know when they will be hit, they just know they will.

    The "belt"on the south with glasgow and edimbourg is a very active place for BSD, with the caern of gloom and sorrow that fell, the Demborough/Damburrow location that is supposedly located around. I've read a bit about the pretanic order and I'm thinking about taking them back to scotland.
    Fera: Most mention of Ceilican in old books are how they got decimated and/or turned to the wyrm with one of their abandonned/fallen taghairm located in the highlands. I haven't thought too much about it, but since I want them to be present in the game I may give them back this caern and/or add another one somewhere. I'm thinking that the fay-cat could be a way to introduce not only a local fera (and we don't have much of those in EU) but a way to introduce some changelin/fae (some of my player are slowly turning their head in this direction) elements as well.

    Wraith: I really want to give Scotland a special touch with ghosts, so I've adapted some things from rage across Egypt. Sometimes a place become "touched" by death and anyone being there or walking there can have a hint of the shadowlands and it's ghosts or worse end up there (and die mostly), it also work the other way around allowing ghost to pass while it's open. It's a phenomenon that is random and can appear anywhere though most often in places that are "predisposed" (so related to death by folklore or where people died and such).
    - I plan to use the Cororuc ghost as a bridge between the pcs and wraiths if they manage to find him and persuade him to be an helper of their cause.

    Fae/Changelin: They are supposedly all over the place I guess, I definitely have to look a bit more into it. I know that the old and super powerful faes were gone, and then kinda returned and I wans thinking to exploit this so my player could get more context of the old Scotland/caledonia and maybe get some informations to the white howler tribes for creature that have actually know them (not necessarily on good terms though).

    Vampire: I read here quite some interesting information about the Dunsirn (I think it was initiated by Blackfox) and I want to add them in my scottish setting. My player don't spent much time in cities, so I initially didn't think too hard about vampires, but I have a few very filthy rich characters (one that actually have wealth from the scottish petrol industry, haha) so the Dunsirn could definitely come into play at some point.

    Mage: I don't know much about mage so I probably have missed a lot of things. I've read some things here and there from supplement like VDA british isles but I don't have much for my contemporary setting. I know wyrm-affiliated mages are doing shenanigans in the shetland (sullom voe) but that's about it more or less, and the shit going there seems really huge.

    Hunter: I really don't know much about those but with such a "supernaturally active" country, it has to have some real hunter there.

    Don't hesitate to tell me if you have used Scotland, or scottish related things in your game, or if you things that could be added !

      A few thoughts:
      The difference with Scotland could be the presence of the Lochs, most of them have monster legends (not just Loch Ness) and one of the native supernatural predators is the Water Horse or Kelpie, which could well be a Fey Nightmare from the Dreaming.

      The Lochs themselves could be the source of a lower gauntlet (essentially large portals) as well as the Highlands being difficult to settle, it’s one of the largest wildernesses in the UK.

      There are around 10 Ley lines that run across Scotland the Sickle Ley running from Tintagel through Stonehenge and then up into the highlands, so the place could be abundant with places of power (smaller fonts, glades and wells as well as Cairns).

      The lines could lower the gauntlet or could be a result of the gauntlet, but with this much power it would explain why a largely remote area is of interest to Mage, Fey as well as Garou.

      Mage would like be Druidic or pagan, with more hermetic or technological in the southern cities, local nephandi might well be derived from BSD kinfolk with unpleasant alliances.
      However if their is a contest for fonts or the Ley lines there may be rival Faustian nephandus who serve demons rather than the Wyrm.

      This dosen’t help the Garou it just puts them under more pressure as they have competing forces trying to claim their caerns.

      Formori could be common from the BSD pits as well as other wyrmholes, the Romans used armies of formori to annihilate the Howler kinfolk so you could have some ancient breeds lurking in the Highlands.

      Similar to Nordic countries where they still leave offering in faery homes outside homes and beside roads, older Scot’s couldn’t help but be affected by the lower gauntlet on a subconscious level let alone staying in at night.
      So they’d definitely leave offerings and observe small rituals just in case.

      Ley Lines were also called Corpse Lines by some paranaturalists, so they may also affect the Shroud, this makes sense as Scotland has a huge number of haunted locations with Glamis Castle being one of the most haunted places in the world.

      BSD activity would be high if only to keep an eye on lost Howlers, in my mind Scotland has lots of old families and bloodlines so it’s a likely place for a child of lion to emerge from a lost line.

      Finally for all of the above it could also be a stronghold for Seelie and Unseelie Fey, if it is a place of power there would be ancient and still strong freeholds (Fey caerns) hidden from mortal eyes, if mortals still have a semi belief this reduces the weavers affects on the Fey (being creatures of magic), plus you could have a fey true fey tucked away who didn’t cloak themselves in human flesh to escape the approach of science.

                  My thread on the Dunsirn can be found here if you only remember it, but don't know its location.

                  With the relatively small size and population of Scotland, and with the large presence of Garou relative to the people, I'd concentrate vampires almost exclusively in a few cities. If you want to keep to canon, the Toreador are the dominant clan and have some allies among the Brujah because they oppose the Ventrue in England. Ventrue would be one of the rarer clans. And I'd probably limit the Tremere to one chantry in Edinburgh as explained in this post concerning the Gehenna cult known as the Ninth Wave. You might have a few Gangrel or Nosferatu hiding in the Scottish wilderness, either keeping their head down to avoid the lupines or being some kind of hag known in folklore (the Cailleach is suppose to dwell somewhere in Argyll and Bute, particulalry around the mountain of Ben Nevis).

                  My recommendation for Changeling is that unless your players really want the specifics of the Changeling setting, it is just better to go by the actual folklore and fairy legends of Scotland and the British Isles. I am not a big fan of the Changeling setting since it is the worst of all the gamelines at emulating the source material (either folklore or pop culture). It's really is its own thing which I don't think fills the expectation of most people (though I recognize that game does have its own devoted fans). If you want to do research for inspiration, I can recommend this blog. For the purposes of a Werewolf game, I'd treat them like they were their own special type of "spirit" in a section of the Umbra all its own who frequently materialize in places strong in the Wyld. With the decline of the Wyld in the world, the "inhabitable" places of the world for fairies have greatly declined and will continue to do so.

                  If I was going to include hunters, they'd be inspired more by Solomon Kane than the Catholic Inquisition because of Scotland's Presbyterianism.

                  If you like to read, you may want to check out the Terror Tales series, which is a British series of new horror fiction short story anthologies set in different regions. They have two books set in Scotland, the Lowlands and the Highlands. I personally find the actual short stories hit and miss (typical for any new fiction anthology), but each book has multiple stories (typically two pages only) of actual local myths, legends, and folklore before each story, and they are fantastic. They'd be a great way to spice up your setting.

                                                      In the modern era, besides the modern British Technocracy, the most prominent faction is likely to be the Verbena, both of the traditional Celtic type and the Nordic influenced hold overs. (What in the Dark Ages would've been the Old Faith and the Valdaerman, respectively.) Also the more modern Wiccan and New Age types.

                                                      Those same Celtic and Nordic influences also produce Euthanatos. Mainly ones concerned with issues of fate/destiny, sacred oaths, watching over certain communities/villages/towns, and eliminating threats.

                                                      The Order of Hermes has a long presence in Britain, including parts of Scotland. House Diedne was one of the founding Houses, mixing Druidism with Hermetic Arts, but were wiped out by the rest of the Order during the early Middle Ages (mostly because of Tremere shenanigans in order to distract from their vampire experiments). Some of them may have escaped into the Fey realms. In some of the Ars Magica books, House Ex Miscelania was presented as having a very strong presence in Scotland, especially with those incorporating "witchcraft" and "shamanism".

                                                      A lot of the Cult of Ecstasy activity in Scotland likely originated with the Romantic period, especially with groups like the Fellowship of Pan. Then there's the more modern types, especially in the major cities.
