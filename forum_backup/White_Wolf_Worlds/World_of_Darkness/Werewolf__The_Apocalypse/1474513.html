    Brainstorming how SL's could assassinate an athro GW ahroun ally mid-battle

    02-12-2022, 04:19 PM
    In short:

    There are 6 weeks till a huge BSD battle, wherein the new GW athro Grand Elder will lure the BSD's into the caern, retreat, then return with an overwhelming number of allies to crush the BSD's before they can corrupt the caern.

    The former GE, a SL adren ahroun called Erases the Foe, is planning to use that chaos to remove the GW athro so that he can return to his position of power. He has 6 weeks to come up with a plan, along with the aid of his adren theurge SL packmate, and possibly a SL elder theurge from another sept who is sympathetic.

    Could you guys help me brainstorm ways that sufficiently motivated SL's could try and get away with this nefarious deed? 6 weeks is plenty of time to enact some serious plans, but I've never played high level games, and am at a bit of a loss as to what they might do. Find a way to isolate the GW athro and then summon an incarna of Grandfather Thunder? Find a way to ensure the athro is overrun by the BSD's?

    Any suggestions are most welcome.

    (Note: 1/2 the PC pack is firmly allied with the SL's, the other 1/2 reluctantly allied with the GW. I'm foreseeing a chance for excellent rp if they have to decide in the heat of the battle which faction to support.)

      I think there are too many factors to have a true plan as too many things can go wrong in six weeks. Simple plans are the best, not elaborate ones, especially in combat. But in general, getting rid of rivals in battle like this fall into two typical categories.

      The first is the tactical retreat from battle at the moment when the target is at his most vulnerable and needs his "allies" to enter the fray. This allows the enemy to overwhelm the target and hopefully kill him. This is probably the safest option... unless the target (and possibly others) somehow manages to survive and tell everyone he was abandoned. But depending on circumstances, it might be too late for the target to save his crown since he's now seen as a loser or might not be believed. In any case, the betrayer needs to have a good story that fits most of what happened (why he left the battle).

      The second is to get a patsy... I mean assassin, who will kill the person at the right time in battle, hopefully in a way that hides it was an assassination because of the confusion in battle. Or perhaps before the battle when the target is alone, and then put the blame on the enemy for somehow sneaking into camp. This is much harder to pull off, but if the right time is found, it's more likely for the target to be killed - provided it can be done very quickly. (This would be a terrible option to do to a PC because most players will think it unfair, but is possible with an NPC).

      I think either could work for your purposes, it's just what fits in best with your plans. One thing you may want to do as an ST is to keep both options open, and then use whichever one works best for the scene as it plays out.

      There are lots of things you could do provided you're open to it. For example, maybe the SL adren ahroun knows a vampire who has Dominate. He makes a deal with that vampire. He arranges a trusted ally of the GW athro to meet with him, but it turns out the vampire is there and puts him into trance and implants a trigger that goes off in middle of battle (I don't think this technically can happen with the Dominate rules as written, but it is the type of thing Dominate is supposed to emulate). Or if you want a different flavor text, maybe the SL adren prays to Hakaken for aid, and the corrupt totem somehow aids him in exchange for some other service or ongoing worship. Then the packs of BSDs - subtly or directly influenced by Hakaken - make killing the GW athro the priority instead of things they would normally do to control the caern. ...because they know Hakaken will end up corrupting the SL adren and they'll eventually get control of the caern anyway. Or maybe get a bane to possess an ally (not turn him into fomori, just possess and use his body) and do the deed. But whatever it is, it deflects attention away from the SL adren ahroun as it is someone else who does the deed and clearly has no connection to the SL. More prosaically, you find an ally of the GW who is ambitious or greedy, and you pay him off some kind of bribe. Or maybe they offer lots of chiminage to a Pain spirit to wrack the GW athro at the right moment, crippling him in combat at precisely the worst moment so that he'll quickly fall in battle?

      What you prefer is going to be a matter of taste, and what makes sense within your chronicle.

      There are obviously other scenarios. The Shadow Lord faction might wait until after the battle is won, and then challenge the GW athro since he "foolishly" "lost" the caern originally, and attempt to kill him in a klaive duel. Or descend into a coup/civil war after all the allied Garou leave to go home (except for the SL elder theurge and other allies). This is more of a straight up civil war than betrayal.

      What gives you the kind of resolution, or choice for the PCs, that you want? Do you want the SL adren to be seen as more honorable? Less honorable, but still loyal to Gaia? Ambitious to the point he will make deals with enemies - vampires, Hakaken cultists, banes, etc.? Do you simply want to force the PCs to take sides? Have an ongoing mystery for them to investigate afterwards? Allow them the opportunity to see something "wrong" with an ally of the GW before or during battle (or that their new Grand Elder has been abandoned) and have a chance to intervene heroically (or choose to let him die)? Something else?

      I think once you know what kind of thing you want for the PCs, you'll be able to brainstorm something that puts them into that situation. And certain ideas might allow for multiple instances where the PCs might impact things.

          Excellent post as always. Lots to think on/chew on. Here's a bit more information on what I'm hoping to achieve:

          Due to lots of great RP beforehand, the pack is split in their support of the ex-Grand Elder and the new one. They've all agreed to follow the new GE, but two members are close allies of the SL's and willing to help them return to power so as to back their long term vision for the Sept. As such, I want this betrayal moment to involve them and be a crucible for their loyalties both to the competing two factions and to their own pack.

          The SL adren was raised a Silver Fang but went SL at his Rite of Passage, and as such still shows elements of his nobility and integrity - but his deed name is Erases the Foe, and I want him to show why at this critical point. I want him to reveal his plan to the two PC's that support him at the critical moment during the fight, and force a crisis in the pack as they suddenly have to pick a side. (This desire for a crisis within the pack plays into visions they've had of their fate, and comes on the heels of many other RP-driven decisions they've made.           The SL however would only remain a viable option for his two supporters if he doesn't go full Sith Lord on them; any plan on his part to use fomori, vampires, or other agents of the Wyrm would lose the PC's support and make it an easy choice for them.

          As such, I'm hoping to devise a plan that's clearly dark and twisted (killing a GE for supposed long term gain) but which doesn't use the Wyrm if possible. Hence why I was wondering about summoning an incarna of Grandfather Thunder, or... yeah. Not sure. Further complicating this is the fact that the GW athro ahroun is infamous for surviving impossible fights at the cost of her allies, and thus it would be very 'IC' for her to survive being stranded against numerous foes or other deadly situations. It would need to be an overwhelming strike that even her incredible abilities couldn't withstand.

          Perhaps something like the elder SL Theurge crafting a fetish that the PC's deploy at a critical moment to cripple her or trap her in some way? Force her into a uniquely crafted pocket realm prison? Something that the PC's could help the SL's craft over the course of the 6 weeks without realizing what they're working on? (One of the PC's is a theurge apprentice to Erases the Foe's SL theurge adren packmate.) And then, since the SL adren wouldn't be trusted to get close, they ask the PC's to deploy it instead?

              There's still plenty of options for an SL that remains firmly in the Gaian camp. I personally would not summon an incarna of Thunder. It's kind of overkill, and I don't think Thunder would want to be used to do the dirty work of one of his servants. It inverts the relationship he wants which is to be the one who dominates others. Thunder would want his children to come up their own methods. Plus summoning any kind of Incarna can go quickly wrong.

              I would suggest making use of a Pain spirit, an epiphling or semi-epiphling. They are part of Grandfather's Thunder's brood, and Pain-spirits are the listed teachers of such Gifts as Whelp Body (level 4 Ragabash) and Open Wounds (level 4 Shadow Lord). So it's adequately Gaian. It's detailed in Axis Mundi (also in the Ratkin book). It has a special charm Agony, that doubles wound penalties. It can't materialize in the physical world, but you can use various flavor text to get around it. Maybe you need to have the target wear something that, unknown to him, allows the spirit to use its Charm across the Gauntlet on the person who has it. And use one of the PCs to convince the GW to wear it, like offering it as a good luck charm. Or create your own unique Pain jaggling that can do more, but keep the flavor text. Or the SL NPC must ask the PCs to do things to perform the chiminage such as finding material correspondence, inflicting pain on themselves, agreeing to perform some future service, or anything else you need so the PCs are critical to the scene.

              The plot is basically to maneuver the Pain spirit in battle to strike the GW at pitch battle so he falls in combat much quicker against the BSDs than they otherwise would. Of course, your battle may take place in the Penumbra as well, so it's possible the spirit can be spotted. But perhaps it can be disguised or hidden, or presented in a way to make it seem that the BSDs summoned it. Or that it was simply attracted to such a horrific battle to oversee the merciless killing by the two sides.

              Or the Pain spirit is fashioned into a unique talen which is then given to one or more of the PCs to use against the NPC when it is the right time, so they need to stick close to the GW and "help" him. Tha talen would then do whatever combination of the Agony charm and/or the aforementioned Gifts, whatever works for you.

              It gives something for the elder SL Theurge to do (summon the right Pain spirit and properly prepare the chiminage, or perform Rite of Binding beforehand). So instead of just being random, he is essential to the plan.

              Pain Spirits are suppose to exist in order to warn people of their own mortality - the lesson of bodily pain. By respecting Pain, a mortal learns his own limitations. They hate people who deny their own pain as such people ignore the wisdom the Pain spirits want to teach. A GW athro who somehow survives impossible costs and lets others die as a result, might be someone who is need of their "education". If the GW has followed the lessons of pain, those other Garou would be alive. What a terrible waste due to this guy's arrogance. You can present the scene of chiminage to the Pain spirit in the aspect that this is balancing an offense to the spirit world, and therefore appropriate to Gaia. But it's still a nasty betrayal and a terrible way to die while being wracked in pain. You could really twist the proverbial knife into the PCs consciences by presenting the NPC's death agony in a way that shows how awful it is.

              This is how I would approach the scene right now since it seems to hit the major points you want. But you and others can probably come up with lots of great ideas on your own.

                                              The OP already discussed the issue of how long it may take the Spirals to desecrate a caern in the other post that Heinrich linked to. He's using a variation of the rules for corrupting a caern found in the second Werewolf Players Guide, and he is using that as the ST to determine what is an appropriate response. You can come to a different conclusion in running your own chronicles, but he has already thought about this issue and decided what's appropriate using all the details going on in his chronicle.

                                              Basically the situation is this. The PCs sept in Detroit is heavily outnumbered by a BSD hive in Detroit. They just don't have the numbers to survive an attack. They'll be wiped out whenever the BSDs decide to attack. Nor can they succeed by attacking the Dancers first for the same reason. Simply standing and fighting just gets all the sept's Garou killed and the sept falls anyway. So instead, they have worked out a plan ahead of time to rally Garou from additional septs who can compensate for the greater BSD numbers. They know how long the BSD's rite of desecration will take in his chronicle setting, and the arrival of reinforcements is suppose to occur within the time period where the caern will still be uncorrupted. Is it risky? Yes. But there's not an alternative because the sept just can't magically create an entire army of new Garou to occupy the sept waiting until the BSDs come. They have to work within the limits they are under in terms of numbers and resources.
