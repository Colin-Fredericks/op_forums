    Its easy to dismiss possibilities in favor of preferred interpretations, and to ignore the possibilities brought by crossing elements, even without a crossover game proper.

    Set being so strong is too stingy for Werewolf because Set is a Vampire? Werewolf have already things like "Fomori" with as much power as an Incarna. The Hydra have quite a bunch under her control, and under Werewolf cosmology everything is ultimately linked to the Umbra. Why not apply that? The Antediluvian can be Banes or Fomori of such power as to become equivalent to the Tribes's Totems. Maybe Set's rites are ways to commune with the Wyrm itself, in order to protect its most daring feats from interference from the Celestines.

    Heck, it could very well be true in a Vampire game, why not in Werewolf? To downplay the other games power-players is certainly fair, but a bit of lacking in creativity at some times. If you want to incorporate them (if not, it is all irrelevant, anyway), it is better to adapt them to the actual cosmology you're using.

                                                    Even as a rumor, I wouldn't call it zero examples. It is, at the very least, an idea. My main point, by the way, isn't about Sobek being real (it is just a rumor), but being an idea that can be real or not. And the idea is canon, even if its veracity isn't stated, specially because its falseness isn't, either. But I digress.

                                                    The Laughing Stranger, the point on the Gnosis roll is that it definitely IS an oversight. And a very acceptable oversight. This discussion is about a very remote possibility of a specific ritual to get rid of a stated rule. Even considering it possible, still remains as very, very far from the default working of the game, and an actual means to make it happen wasn't ever presented. It would be really strange for them to go so far as to address an eventual loss of Helio's protection. If it had been done, then the possibility would be a given and this discussion pointless, because them a way to do so would be a given. So, yes, this roll is absent on the rules, but it means nothing, really.

                                                    About reasoning it, there are several ways. As SOS pointed, you could simply say that Helios drives the corpse of the "Abomination" to be a rampaging Beast for some hour before destroying it with pyrotechnics. It doesn't need for the Mokolé to be alive, so the whole should make the Gnosis roll moot, and that isn't the same as non-existent.

                                                    But, again, if someone can get rid of Helios' blessing, the Gnosis roll shouldn't be so much of a problem. Even Gaia being a greater spirit, it is also a fairly non-interfering one. If it was so hard to deal with, then Gaia wouldn't need warriors to begin with.

                                                    Just to finish, a thought I had for some time in this discussion. I find it poor to write down the possibility of said event based on Helios' standing, or Gaia's. Forget about Set, said ritual could be performed by any BSD or whatever, drawing power of any Wyrm or Weaver-aligned entity, with the help of any McGuffin. The possibility of a unique event deconstructing any set piece of the setting is a staple of fiction and a source of options to the ST. It isn't the same as saying that it happened or whatever, and the very impossibility by default means gives it significance. But to simply state that impossible is actually completely impossible by any means serves only to cut on sources of theme and drama.

                                                    If Helios and Gaia are too powerful for you to have a chance at circumvent them even in a single case, so the changers can go home. The war is pointless, the gods can take care of themselves. The game is, though, ultimately based on the premises that the gods need their subjects to deal with shit.
