                                            Originally posted by Cain Loup-Noir View Post
                                            So basically Vitae and ananasi blood point are not actual blood flooding is the system of their body, it's rather a game representation of the "energy" it contains. If vampires could suffer hermorrhage they would not bleed their pool but simply lose health as ghouls and ananasi.
                                            My misconception come from those power that destroy/steal vampires blood pool like "blood cauldron", "theft of vitae" or simply the fact that vampire can drink it directly from the neck of their peers like it was actual blood
                                            That is mostly correct, the explanations that followed missed important bits in the Vampire Core books (any edition), plus some analysis in Mage.

                                            Vitae is the energy the Vampire Curse creates out of blood. The process is unknown in details but they're for all purposes Thaumivores: they drink blood as a medium to absorb the victim's Quintessence, and Vitae is the particular form of Quintessence they store.

                                            Ghouls digest the Blood completely as normal. Their Pool is just energy. There's no such nonsense as you sipping Vitae along with their blood, you simply can't take the Blood Points, you cause damage as usual.

                                            Ananasi have no clear indication of what their Blood Pool is. The type of Gifts and effects that use it and the anatomy of an actual spider do suggest it being more about protein, but the effects still are, in the end, mystical, and they shouldn't need blood specifically for that, so take from that what you will.

                                            Kindred do store the Vitae in the blood within their bodies, though the relationship isn't so direct. As far as pure matter goes, a vampire has approximately the same volume of blood in their body as a normal human of their mass. Low Gen vamps concentrate the energy in less volume. But bleeding a Vampire will indeed take Blood Points out, not because it is blood, but because it is tied to blood in their bodies.

                                            But another complication arises: there's no such a thing as a vampire hemorrhaging unless it is mystically induced, because they're not mammals. Instead of a circulatory system, the undead transport blood through controlled osmosis, which is weird as hell. For this reason they don't ever lose Blood Points through open wounds, they don't bleed out enough at all. But taking out a chunk of flesh can do the trick if they don't wish that chunk to be out of blood first.

                                            Spiders don't stomach solids well. The idea of the Ananasi sucking blood is because spiders do look like doing that. But they actually inject the victim with acids to liquefy everything. The Ananasi have an innate difficulty to stomach solids to represent this as well, preferring soup and similar foods. So:

                                            Ananasi sucking Vampire: will take out Blood Points because draining blood doesn't cause damage to Vampires. For the werespider it's an awful experience, but that's their spiritual nature refusing undead Blood. If drinking from old human blood, for example, they'd still get whatever normal nutrition that could provide.

                                            Ananasi sucking Ghoul: funny taste, maybe, but nothing else if that much. No effect on Blood Pool. Causes damage. Same if sucking most other supernaturals.

                                            Vampire sucking Ananasi or Ghoul: causes damage, doesn't touch the Blood Pool. The ichor of an Ananasi isn't much better than sucking Ananasi insect or horseshoe crabs, it'll probably equate to any food ingestion plus bad taste.

                                            Ananasi sucking another Ananasi: causes damage, doesn't touch the Blood Pool, don't provide Blood Points because still isn't blood, but provide normal sustenance. Spiders are, after all, consummate cannibals by nature.

                                            Special – Ananasi sucking a Changeling: that's tricky because they're not affected by mystic properties of the blood the way Vampires are (basically it's the Vampire condition that cause the werewolf blood to have extra effects), but Changelings can do that on their own. If one is bitten, they can spend Glamour to cause their own blood to Enchant the drinker. So the spider is not really safe.

                                            Abominations: this name is better reserved to the obtuse rules around them than the creature proper.

                                            What you get is a weakened Garou: seriously their regeneration is their most impressive innate property. Being locked from ever raising Gnosis again while being susceptible to loose it sucks royally, and constant depression that halves your dice pool is really bad. The benefits are the endurance and blood healing of Kindred and access to Disciplines to replace not getting new Gifts without insane effort. Not really that great. Oh, probably never raising in Rank again, too.

                                            The result is seriously just unplayable. Which is a shame, because the concept isn't totally bad, but early authors were decided to veto cross-splating not by banning it, but by making it as brutally constrained as possible.

                                            My suggestion: remove some of the drawbacks. Really, for Werewolves they're not a big deal. They'd need a lot of development in both aspects to really be that powerful, which ignores that things still cost XP. I've used them in play a few times, and IME they're not any more powerful than other Werewolves even if you remove constant Harano and heavy blocks on advancing Werewolf traits. Keep the loss of Regeneration, ditch the rest. Advancing Rank and getting Gifts will be naturally hard and demanding if possible at all.

                                            In a Vampire game they're more of a problem, but actually less than allowing a normal Garou, so take from that what you will.
