    I like the idea of Pure Breed being most effective within the tribe, but I think that it should also be effective with different tribes. Perhaps 1/2 as effective with historical neighbors and a quarter with others.

    Garou and human interaction less face facts the average Ahroun can not blend into society very easily this is not a bad thing but plays hell with having a character that wants to have a job of some sort. The ability to work means resources, and unless you're going to create a multi-national company that provides a stipend to packs this creates a wealth of problems.
    Ancestors/Past Lives clarify what it does, characters that channel an ancestor should also probably be given a social bonus within the Tribe. Eliminate some of the tribal background bans. I can see the loss of mystical backgrounds or even physical ones in some cases. But not every tribe needs to have restrictions on Allies, contacts, resources, etc. Yes Bone Gnawers are the bottom of society in the Garou Nation but this should be reflected in a social penalty in interaction with other tribes not oh you are so poor you eat soup cans and if this is the case then give the Gnawers a stronger social network poor people in the real world develop strong social networks usually among families that aid each other. This could be done by requiring Bone Gnawers to have 3 dots in either Allies or Kinfolk.
    I have no problems or little problem with most of the combat mechanics I did away with the whole declare actions thing years ago.

                                                The Mage: the Ascension Revised Convention Books were (as best as I read) an interesting setting.
                                                For the Mages, Time of Judgement came... and went. Yes, Umbral Storm hit, and the Void Engineers are fucked up with most of their bases lost and those who can navagate through the storm that still rages come back with some crazy tales and war stories.
                                                Meanwhile, for everyone else, things got shaken up... but the world didn't end, but many of the factions are still recovering.

                                                There's a brief mention of the Werewolves and the Vampires, saying that "For them, their end times came and... nothing happened."

                                                Now, this could mean a few things:
                                                1) In the CWoD setting, the Apocalypse/Gehenna were adverted (somehow) with the results mitigated in such a way that the Mages just aren't aware of the cosmic evidence/happenings.
                                                2) The Time of Judgement actually SPLIT the spheres of the CWoD into their own realms, completely severing the influence of one over the other (the CWoD games had the ability to, but weren't really designed to be played together, and their production/development was not intended to be balanced/influenced by the themes/balance of the others. It was like different roads that SOMETIMES crossed, or ran alongside each other briefly, but never merged truly into one unified highway system.) The paradigms just aren't that compatible as one main, flawless setting.

                                                It will be interesting to see how V4 handles the timeline and greater CWoD as a setting. :-/

                                                However, for Werewolf, the End Times certainly felt more massive than they did for Vampire. In Werewolf, we weren't talking about powerful figures rising up and bringing the end, but rather the fabric of the universe being squeezed and rotted until everything finally gives way and existence is rent asunder in a physical and spiritual way. We may very well be IN the churning, grinding Apocalypse, like falling into a black hole. The very idea that the Garou are fighting to prevent a thing that is already happening is part of the great story for me--they fight a losing battle because it is their duty, it is their destiny, and it is the Good and Right thing to do. Those who succumb to the fact that it's a lost battle and they have already lost either become lost to depression, or turn to the other side to join the "winning" side and enjoy the view as the world burns down around them--why fight it?
