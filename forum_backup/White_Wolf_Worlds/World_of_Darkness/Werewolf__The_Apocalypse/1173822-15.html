            Originally posted by Fat Larry View Post
            Rather than make a new thread for this, I figure I'd ask it here: how would you go about using Pentex(or any of its subsidiaries) during a game set in the Middle Ages?
            Well, unfortunately(?), most of those industries don't really exist before the Industrial Age, if not the 20th century. Even the really old trades like woodcutting, mining, and farming/livestock probably wouldn't be on a scale that would support a big multi regional conspiracy like PENTEX. I think the more likely avenue for this sort for this sort of thing in the Dark Medieval World/Dark Ages is going to be various Wyrm cults (which is another subject I'd like to cover at some point).

            I mentioned in the Head Canons & Home Brew thread that my own PENTEX set up is a little different than canon, and that several of the companies were founded by and run cults devoted to various Wyrm spirits. Smith & Young I tie directly to the Red Circle, mainly because as I've mentioned, the P&G logo myth just amuses me to no end. Other cults might find certain period suitable expressions, such as That Which Devours All Flesh (Logan Meat Packing Company) being the power behind a secretive clan of cannibal bandits similar to the (probably mythical) Sawney Bean clan. Treerazer (Good House International - and yes, I took it from Pathfinder) may be somehow tied to medieval charcoal burners. The Thirsty Ground/The Ground Which Thirst For Blood (Yale Cane & Fletcher Agriculture) may be tied to rural villages or even fief lords conducting human sacrifices to insure bountiful harvests. And the whole Dagon/Deep Ones stuff I tie to Hallahan Seafood can work with any isolated seaside fishing village along the Atlantic or Baltic.

            But the best bet for a Europe-wide equivalent to PENTEX with its fingers in various courts, the Church, merchant guilds and the like, is to look at Dark Ages: Inquisitor's House of Murnau and use it as the basis for an extended and widespread noble family (like what the Hapsburgs turned into) that is pledged to the Wyrm.

                                            Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
                                            Avalon Incorporated

                                            So, toys.

                                            Avalon is one of the companies covered in Subsidiaries: A Guide to PENTEX and, in all honesty, I've always felt it was the weakest chapter of the book. (It comes across as an attempt to try to force fit a Changeling/Mage crossover into a Werewolf book. The example toys in the first edition of Book of the Wyrm are way creepier.) That aside, the toy industry is at times a rather fascinating and outright weird place. Hasbro and Mattel are two of the more famous American companies. A few books on the subject include G. Wayne Miller's Toy Wars: The Epic Struggle Between G.I. Joe, Barbie and the Companies That Make Them, Jerry Oppenheimer's Toy Monster: The Big Bad World of Mattel, Sydney Stern and Ted Schoenhaus's Toyland: The High-Stakes Game of the Toy Industry, Eric Clark's The Real Toy Story: Inside the Ruthless Battle for America's Youngest Consumers, Roger Sweet's Mastering the Universe: He-Man and the Rise and Fall of a Billion Dollar Idea1, Robin Gerber's Barbie and Ruth: The Story of the World's Most Famous Doll and the Woman who Created Her, and Orly Lobel's You Don't Own Me: How Mattel v MGA Entertainment Exposed Barbie's Dark Side.

                                            Modern Marvels has done an episode on Toys (season 10, episode 85). Also, the documentary Plastic Galaxy: The Story of Star Wars Toys (2014), and the Netflix series The Toys That Made Us (2017; only 4 episodes, unfortunately). You can also find a number of toy-themed channels on YouTube made by hardcore fans of various lines and franchises. From a more fictional angle, there's the 1992 Robin Williams film Toys, which is not all that good but may offer a few ideas. Also, Small Soldiers (1998) and the "Lisa vs Malibu Stacy" episode of The Simpsons (which is were the clip I posted earlier in the thread came from).

                                            When it comes to the idea of toys - especially dolls - coming to life and attacking people, there's a long and often enjoyable history of the subject. The Twilight Zone's "Living Doll" episode (season five, episode six) is a classic. Stephen King did a variation on the theme with his short story "Battleground". And, of course, the Child's Play series. (The second film is especially interesting for Avalon purposes as the opening shows both the manufacturing process for the Good Guy dolls and the company executives in full CYA mode in the wake of the first film.) One suspects that Avalon has long made dolls that talk to children and encourage them to do bad things.

                                            Magic kits are a long time favorite of mine (not that I was ever able to get any of the tricks to work right). Harry Blackstone Jr. was the big name when I was a kid. Chris Angel appears to be selling his own kits now. Such things offer the possibility of introducing kids to Wyrm-craft or possible possession of the psychic banes that create Odyssey subjects.

                                            Boardgames are a big part of the toy industry, and Tristan Donovan's It's All A Game: The History of Board Games from Monopoly to Settlers of Catan is a good overview of them. Parker Brothers and Milton-Bradley were two of the major companies until they were bought out by Hasbro. I suspect that Avalon likes to make board games that encourages the worst aspects of society, including ones where you win by backstabbing and betraying others. I'm sure they also like ones that encourage rampant consumerism. I grew up in the 80s, and one I remember was called Go For It, with the objective of trying to accumulate as much extravagant trappings of wealth and status as possible (in the form of sports cars, high end electronics, huge houses, and the like).

                                            McFarlane Toys, the money printing machine created by former comic artist Todd McFarlane to fund his sports memorabilia collecting obsession, is slightly infamous for it's horror figures. Their stuff is really aimed more at adults, but I suspect that most of Avalon's action figure lines look like the stuff McFarlane makes. (Thanks to Fat Larry for reminding me about them.)

                                            I can remember the Collectable Card Game boom of the mid-90s, including stories of people getting mugged for boxes of Magic: The Gathering Cards. One can easily imagine banes feeding on and encouraging such behavior. Likewise using Cute & Fuzzy Cockfighting Seizure Monsters to encourage kids to trap small animals and force them to fight to the death for their amusement

                                            I may as well mention Mighty Morphin Power Rangers: The Movie (1995), with Ivan Ooze's mind controlling ooze as a potential angle for the toy slime that seems so popular with kids (it's allure always escaped me, for some reason). Kids play with it and it ends up taking over their parents, Invasion of the Body Snatchers style.

                                            And, finally, I've previously mentioned my love for Grant Morrison's short bit "New Toys" from the Vertigo miniseries Weird War Tales DC Comics did nearly 20 years ago.
                                            The reveal at the end is that kids toys are being replaced by insectoid like aliens for malevolent purposes. In my own personal WoD, I like to insinuate that Avalon is secretly run by Ramsey Campbell's Insects from Shaggai.


                                            ADDED: I'd nearly forgotten about one of my favorite action figure parodies, The Kronies, teaching kids the importance of all the crony corporate pseudo-capitalism that big-business/big-government collusion can engineer.

                                            Also, I added a bit about the 1976 remake of King Kong under the original Endron post.

                                            And let's all have a moment of silence for the passing of Toys R Us.


                                            (Hope to have a bit about the meat packing industry done for the weekend. Then probably video games/computers, cigarettes, and Project: Odyssey in some order.)

                                            An often Overlooked movie that could provide good inspiration is the film Small Soldiers, which features Toys programmed with chips that turn them sapient, not only a good idea for more comical takes on bane possessed toys, but the Primary antagonists, the Commando Elite (billed as the in-universe toyline's heroes) serve as a great visual inspiration for depictions of Action bill characters. Just replace the Gorgonites with Comically exaggerated Garou and Fera stereotypes for the villains and its a perfect propaganda line.

                                            As Freak Legion pointed out, it doesn't even take much exaggeration to paint the Garou as Villains, a Bunch of Savage monsters bent on Sending humanity back to the stone age or even extinction itself, depending on the Tribe, its not even a slight exaggeration.
