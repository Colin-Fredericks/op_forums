    Entering and altering Chimares

    11-06-2025, 07:08 AM
    I'm interested in whether STs have introduced house rules for their players to interact with dreams of individuals. So far, I have resolved questions narratively when the situation happened but it looks like I'm going to need some crunch because it is going to be 'a thing' in an ongoing Chronicle.
    I have a mixed group, and so far the Oneiromancer has handled most dream interactions (they have the right kit, after all), but I would like for the Fera characters to have a more active role as well without disadvantaging the Sorcerer.

    The books say that to enter the Dream Zone, Fera need to fall asleep in the Penumbra, and I have used that simple mechanic to let them enter a Chimare if the sleeping/dreaming individual is nearby, with a bit of an Inception flavor. However, I feel there should be some natural barrier similar to the Gauntlet that needs to be overcome, especially if the Chimare is something the dreamer crafted via lucid dreaming or a proper Demesne. Do you have some additional conditions for entering/exiting Chimares in your games, or can the characters simply 'walk in'?

    Rite of Dreamtime (Level Two) allows creation and manipulation of your own Chimare (likely aimed at creating a Demesne or Den Realm), and I could see that Rite to be adapted for altering the Chimare of others. The price for changing things inside is a rather steep Gnosis cost though, and it is probably easier to manipulate a dream through interaction with the dreamer Inception style. If you had dream quests like that in your Chronicles, I would be interested in hearing about how you handled dream manipulation.

          I've always wanted to make dreams and dream travel an important part of my chronicles so I have researched this extensively and come up with my own interpretation of it. However, my players are generally uninterested so I've never used them in play so I don't know how well everything works. But this is what I use.

          Chimares are simply domains in the Penumbra. They are linked to the Dream Zone, but the Dream Zone is separate.

          Chimares are just individualized dream domains of a single dreamer. The early editions stated EVERY dream generates its own Chimare, but later editions seemed to walk that back and implied only the most powerful dreams generate Chimares. So pick whatever works best for you and your gaming group. Garou in the Penumbra may enter any Chimare they wish simply by choosing to enter them just like they do any other domain. When Garou are in a Chimare, it's just like any other umbral domain - they can encounter spirits, emanations, and other creatures (like a Wraith who uses the Arcanoi Phantasm). And of course the dream self of the dreamer herself.

          Some STs do want some additional challenges when entering domains like certain dice rolls (usually involving Enigma because that is what is used to navigate the Umbra) or figuring out certain tasks or riddles. It's all up to how easy or hard you want to make it. I generally make it very easy to enter dream Chimares. Any challenges or dice rolls are what happens once they get inside and try to do something.

          The Galliard Gift Dreamspeak allows a Garou to enter someone's dream (a Chimare) although in this case the Garou does not need to be physically present in the Penumbra next to the Chimare to do so. Presumably when the Gift is succesfully activated, the Garou simply vanishes from their current location and appears directly into the Chimare. Although not stated in the Gift description, I assume that when the Garou wants to end it, they simply pop back out of the Chimare and return to where they originally vanished.

          Likewise the Galliard Gift Gift of Dreams allows Garou to craft a specific and potent dream for an individual. In essence, this creates a specific Chimare for that individual to inhabit.

          There are also what I call "independent" Chimares. These are semi-permanent domains that exist independently from dreamers. They are usually formed as a result of many dreamers over time at the same general area, or are a result of powerful dream spirit energy in a location. One example is described at the Sept of the Green when the Wyld aided by the caern totem created a Chimare at Turtle Pond in Central Park. People who sleep there have incredible, inspirational dreams.


          All Chimares connect to the Dream Zone. When the dreamer awakes, any Garou or other entity inside the Chimare, are cast out into the Dream Zone or some sub-realm outside the Penumbra. If you want to directly travel from a Chimare to the Dream Zone you can, but I'd make it so that such Garou need to undergo a prolonged quest to go deeper and deeper into the subconscious before they can actually make it to the Dream Zone. It's usually much safer for Garou to travel directly to the Dream Zone via their own methods (sleeping in the Umbra, the Rite of Dreaming, or certain fetishes) than try to do that in someone's Chimare. Individual dreamers do sometimes enter the Dream Zone directly from their own Chimare, but those incidents tend to be fairly rare. Certain individual people can be more prone to experience such potent, deep dreams than others.

          Individual dreams link to the Dream Zone, but the Dream Zone is also where archetypal, lost, incomplete, or collective dreams reside. Generally if Garou want to explore these kind of dreams, or undergo Dreamquests to understand the meaning of important/persistent/prophetic/powerful dreams, then they'll ultimately need to go to the Dream Zone. The travel to the Dream Zone is when you can create more complications or require real spiritual quests.

          One thing I've done in my own chronicles is have a few of these persistent "dreamrealms" hidden in the Dream Zone described. I include them as a standard ongoing element that I use for the purposes of flavor text and plot hooks. They're inspired by the Dreamland stories of HP Lovecraft, Neil Gaiman's the Sandman, and other horror or fantasy stories that utilize some kind of common dream trope or setting. I have a remnant dream of the Summer Country when I want to emphasize positive aspects of the world or spiritual learnings. There's also a dream of the "Table Rock" where the Garou developed the first version of the Litany. I have something I call the "Vale of Doom" whenever an NPC has prophetic or predatory dreams that will build up to their own deaths. It's meant as a Wyrm influenced dreamrealm so the Garou PCs can undertake a quest to save the NPC's life. I have the "Night School" which provides insights into forbidden occult knowledge. It's another Wyrm influenced dreamrealm that tries to tempt and recruit people into Wyrm sorcery. There is the "Blockula" based on folklore about witches where servants of the Wyrm are able to gather in their witches' sabbaths and converse with other Wyrm witches. There's the fabled city of "Syrannon" which is very similar to Lovecraft's fabled dream cities, and mentioned as the goal for the Zolondondere in the Sorcerer sourcebooks. I add more such dreamrealms all the time as I get inspired or decide it is needed in the game either as a place to adventure, or as a recurring dream element that is common to some group (particularly Garou, but not exclusive to them). These kinds of recurring dreams are rare, but I found that players like them as when they appear it's a sign they can go on an interesting adventure or realize the dream have more than personal significance. They're good as omen (for dream interpretation rolls) or when someone has the Gift Sight from Beyond. I am for a mix of benevolent, benign, neutral, or hostile dreamrealms.

          I also have a very hostile Wyrm Nightmare dreamlord reside in the Dream Zone that is actively trying to invade Gaia's dream and corrupt reality. It's basically a counterpart to Chimera and the other unnamed Dreamlords of Earth. I built an entire faction around it. I've mentioned it chronicles, but haven't heavily utilized it in any chronicles yet. I'm waiting until a group actually wants to have Chimera as their pack totem. (Of course there are also various other Nightmare spirits, but those aren't necessarily anti-Gaian).
          ​
          ​The Umbra sourcebooks talk about the Dream Zone, but not all sourcebooks are equal. The Revised Umbra in 2001 has half a page about the Dream Zone. The 1993 Umbra sourcebook has five pages, and provides a lot more guidance and rules. It's one reason (but not the only one) I find the 1993 version to be much superior to other editions. (One thing it includes is that each auspice has an extra, special ability they can use while in the Dream Zone or Chimares. The Galliard's power is "dream shaping".) If you want to include dreams and the Dream Zone more heavily in your chronicles, I highly recommend you try to obtain a copy. I purchased my PDF online and am very glad I did.

                                  Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
                                  I'm generally trying to keep away from Changeling lore (ie making the Dream Zone and the Dreaming the same thing), but I vaguely recall that being a thing there.
                                  My understanding is that the Deep Dreaming is one of the lands of the Dream Realms, the shards of imagination. Dream Realms, also called dreamscapes, are isolated islands that dwell in a “sea” of imaginative possibility. The Deep Dreaming is the primeval portion of the Maya. Here are massive realms called the Onerae ruled by the enigmatic Oneira, or Dream Lords. Only the comatose, mad, and the utterly fearless voyage here. Even the fae avoid this place, and the few dreamers who voyage here seldom return. Trods are the only way to travel this far. The Deep Dreaming is home to fabled Arcadia, now lost to the Changelings. Arcadia is just one of the Dream Realms of the Dream Lords. It is now cut off from Earth due to the Shattering.

                                  The Far Dreaming is essentially the Arcadia Gateway which fae call the Milderwood or Forest of Lies, and Mages call Hy-Brasil. Potent Dream realms that linger can draw dreamers again and again. Some are even communal and shared by people. Very often these communal Dream Realms are a result of popular fiction or legend, or even the cause of such stories, legends, and myths. In time such mighty Dream Realms can become mythic, and thereby permanently lodge into the Near Umbra. Arcadia Gateway is one such, presumably because the fae interact with humanity a lot.

                                  The Near Dreaming (called the Dreamtime by some such as Dreamspeakers) is the nearest contact of the Dreaming with the Realm. The Fae consider this reality their home. Why the Changeling Dreaming is so prominent of the Dream Realms (as seen by Earth's inhabitants), and the others are not is somewhat of a mystery. However, there are references to the fairy world as being part of the Dream of Gaia, overseen by her Incarna avatar Danu. This might have helped lodge the Dreaming so much closer to Earth than the other Dream Realms ruled by their own Oneira. Likewise we have references to the Incarna of Stag and Oak making mutual oaths to the fairies in the earliest days which cemented ties between our worlds. The other Dream Realms don't have this.

                                  Of course, this is not directly laid out. Bits and pieces of information are scattered throughout multiple sourcebooks, and this is mainly an artifact of second edition. The Revised era, and Changeling never got a Revised edition, liked to eliminate any ties between the game lines.

                                                          Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
                                                          How would you fit the Dream Zone as described in WtA into this architecture? Something in-between the Dream Realms maybe, or just replace it?
                                                          The Dream Zone, as its name suggests, is a Zone which is an umbral term of art. A Zone is an independent reality that is an inherent part of the Umbra, but which is hard to define or explain. (The other two known Zones are the Mirror Zone and Null Zone, but more are thought to exist.) No matter how much Garou or mages known about Zones, there is a mystery about them. They defy rational thought and categorization. Zones are separate from other aspects of the Umbra, but also connected to them. So the Dream Zone, directly connected to the material world by the dreams of its inhabitants (Chimares) is also an incredibly distant and exotic part of the Umbra.

                                                          A general description of the dream realms, and the geography of the Dreaming, can be found in the Book of Worlds for Mage: the Ascension (page 79). So the Dream Realms are beyond the Far Dreaming (aka the Arcadia Gateway in the Near Umbra). Where the Horizon (or Membrane as the Garou call it) bisects the Dreaming, on the other side is the Deep Dreaming (where the Oneiri or Dreamlords dwell).

                                                          So the Dreaming of the Changelings is technically part of the Dream Zone, but the Dream Zone is much, much more than that. Most Garou probably don't connect the two together at all since they are more familiar with Changeling Freeholds (because they can become caerns and also entrances to the Near Dreaming), Trods (the Penumbral domains that connect to the Near Dreaming which Changelings call the Green Paths of Balor), and the Arcadia Gateway (which is the Far Dreaming). Those are much easier connections for them than trying to reach the fairy world in a long runabout way through the Dream Zone. In my games, I have almost every Stag pack totem just know how to enter the fairy realms through well known Trods if their packs ever want to go through them and encounter the fairy world.

                                                          When Garou want to undergo spirit quests to the Dream Zone its because of actual dreams of people. That it is somehow connected to the fairy world is just one of those mysterious things that don't make sense which is why the Dream Zone is classified as a Zone.

                                                          Likewise the Dream Zone also connects to the Dark Umbra because wraiths with the Phantasm Arcanoi also interact with dreams and can create entire special Dream Theatres that take place in the Dream Zone (but they're also part of the Dark Umbra). The Dream Zone in fact links to lots of different places, and there seems to be more than one mention of a link to the Black Labyrinth of Malfeas. But we normally don't think of those places as "part" of the Dream Zone.

                                                          The Dream Zone is just a very weird place.

                                                          When I was compiling my own handbook on running scenes/encounters for dreams, the sourcebooks I pulled from were Umbra: the Velvet Shadow from WtA, Book of Worlds from MtA, Dreams and Nightmares from CtD, and Guildbook: Sandmen from WtA. The first two had the most important information with the latter two only really being used for completion's sake since dreams can link to those two settings. The Book of Worlds also give descriptions of some of the other Dream Realms that exist, but of course there cannot be an exhaustive list of such realms, nor any idea of how "big" or "important" each one is relative to one another.
