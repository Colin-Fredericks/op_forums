    Tribal Gifts! Tribes, now no longer associated with specific human cultures!

    Beginning Gifts (Requires 3 Renown)

    Curse of Aeolus
    Well, that certainly didn't take long. Just like with the Fianna Hart Wardens. Much as with many other Gifts, they skipped ahead to get to some of what the writers presumably considered iconic, but without the capacity to tap into or detect the energies of the Triat, that makes a short footrace to making magic fog. As you might have guessed, it has also been nerfed, as it only covers the size of a single house instead of a city block (roughly 400 by 400 in feet). Awkwardly uses Glory, because of course it's the height of Glory to conceal your actions under a thick fog. Also, instead of being reliable soft cover, it is, much like every other Gift that inflicts a penalty, only a -2 to ranged attacks.

    Tribes also begin to just stretch the availability of auspice Gifts that we've seen. The other two beginniner Gifts for the Furies are Porcupine's Reprisal and... Halt the Coward's Flight.

    A Get of Fenris Gift in the Black Furies list? It's like a joke from a John Biles parody from the 90s that leans heavily on Ranma 1/2. Is anyone else old enough to get that? Google the Year of Projects, maybe it's still around.

    Intermediate Gifts (Tribe requires Renown 6)

    Coup de Grace
    Instead of easily just doubling the damage of an attack, it adds damage... but only if you would already bring their Health down to nada. I think this could have been done far, far better, but that pretty much goes without saying at this point.

    Kali's Scar
    Prevents a wound from healing for a night and a day, or not if the ST decides that the opponent is too powerful. Also allows you to track the target.

    Wasp Talons
    At least this one still works. Also works in any form, oddly enough. The attack roll is a bit odd, but not nearly as odd as it could have been. The damage to your claws is superficial, so it can still be healed quickly to "reload". The one flaw? No range mentioned.

    "Elder" Level Gifts - Renown 9, bucko.

    Gorgon's Visage
    (Puts hand in shape of L) LAME! Bigtime lame. Barely manages to stop targets from moving, can't even turn them to stone, and is so telegraphed that anyone can look away quickly. Authors, chief editor, WTF do you think "elder level" is supposed to be?

    Furies also get Break the Shackles and Drain Spirit at this level.

    Edit: Also Whelp Body, but it can be hard to see given the flow of the text, if you flip rapidly between pages. This is somewhat close to getting Body Wrack, I guess.

    Because Tribes are so truncated in length, I'm fitting in another two.

    Bone Gnawers

    Beginner Gifts

    Odious Aroma
    Gives you a Stink Aura that prevents others from touching you... based on your HONOR! Now, instead of a potential distraction in combat, it's apparently meant to stop combat, somehow.

    The Gnawers also get the previously-described Gifts of Blissful Ignorance, Rapid Shift, and Sight From Beyond.

    Intermediate Gifts

    Face in the Crowd
    Lose people by entering a crowd of humans or canids. The Gift says dogs, but the book knows jack all about wolves or how they look, so I'm going to call shenanigans on that one. Relies on HONOR! for some reason (make Cunning a thing already! UGH!)

    Streets Tell Stories
    Literally put your ear to the street and hear anything going on along it or the buildings adjacent to it. Only living beings can be heard, so you'll only get half of a conversation over the phone. The combination of traits once again makes me imagine Satsuki and Prince Zuko trying to hear something while pressing their ears to the ground.

    Gnawers also have Scent of the Past available. This is all shaping up to be a bit too much emphasis on being the information-gatherers and brokers of the Garou, aka "We read about
    urban werewolves in the dregs of the city and just assumed they would be like the Nosferatu".

    Elder Gifts

    A Thousand Eyes
    Talk about redundant. A bunch of Gifts for seeing and investigating around, turning the Gnawers into one-note Nosferatu clones wearing the "I know secrets" hat. Wait, let me amend that, "I know secrets, and also rant about HONOR! like Zuko" hat, because guess what Renown you need here? Mentions that you can see out of animals' eyes as long as they're cockroach sized, which is a failure of a balm for their patron shift. Basically, just an Animalism power in the Gnawer Gift list.

    Between the Cracks
    This is using the name of a Gift that let you find a temporary bolthole but the effect of Blink, except that instead of shaded urban areas you're literally vanishing where one thing is cracked and exiting where there's another crack. Massively truncated in effective range as this one is, I'd rather have Blink, of course.

    The other elder Gnawer Gift is Defy Death which, as we've established, really should be in quotes for how unreliable it theoretically is.

    Children of Gaia

    Given the Revised Tribebook, one shudders to imagine what cruel and inhuman indignities the Coggies will be subjected to.

    Beginner Gifts

    Brother's Scent
    Yet another Gift for hiding in a crowd! The original explicitly mentioned that your presentation could be way, way off; no real hint of that, here. Only that you have to roll if someone is looking for people specifically. Also mentions the CoG as being an... impostor.


    That's the only new Gift that they get. The others are Mother's Touch, Song of Serenity (does this mean that Coggies are compulsory musicians, too?), and Sense the True Form. They really didn't want to add in anything for variety, did they?

    Intermediate Gifts

    Calm the Furious Beast
    Yet another concept wrecker for the supposed dismal state of the Garou, as you can pull someone out of a frenzy fairly easily. A bit harder to get than a beginner Gift, but still not locked behind Elder exclusivity.

    Reveal Trauma
    Like Diagnostics, but can also affect the living. Now that's some major league splitting and dilution of themes! No Pain Chain for you! While being able to diagnose any ailment or structural strain would sound nice, it only provides a bonus of two dice and not anything else.

    CoG also get Open Seal here.

    Elder Gifts

    Life's Presence
    You can sense any living being, or undead feigning life, in... the area of two-story building. Conceptually, most similar to Song of the Earth Mother, which just blows this out of the water. It's not even funny how limited Life's Presence is.

    Share the Pain
    Actually pretty nice, for once, as you can take injuries for your packmates. You can even change health level damage to Emotional Damage. Goes particularly well with Luna's Armor, which they also get here.

    (Break!)

    Bone Gnawers got pretty heavily shafted here, because the direction was to make them Wolferatu, or as far as they're imagining, Dogsferatu. Nothing like Riot or Call the Rust that take them from being "eyes on the street" to wielding all the powers of the overlooked urban spiritual landscape and the defenders of its peoples. CoG managed to weather the storm rather well, though they still had some stinkers, and surprise surprise, they don't get the capacity to deep six weapons and keep them from working. And, in spite of how very badly the game wants us to imagine otherwise, the Black Furies are quite clearly still invested in Greek myth, and with Gorgon, the Greek myth feminine. I just have might doubts that the writers can pull off clever subversions like "actually, the Greek heroes were terrible", so who knows where that's going to go as time passes.

            Specifically 1e Forsaken style. 2e had facets and different functions by Renown for one Gift, I think. One wonders why they went with 1e.

            Normally, the next three tribes went Fianna - Get of Fenris - Glass Walkers. Feels kind of weird that we're going straight into...

            Galestalkers

            (Younger Brother, except not.)

            Beginner Gifts

            Camouflage
            Told you. Mark that as another stealth Gift that requires HONOR!, though unlike some others, you can spend resources to extend it to your entire pack. Given that you have to stand still, I'm surprised that they didn't also make your cross your arms.

            Lacerating Wind
            Justin Achilli was obsessing over skipping straight to this over the "fart wind" Gift in one blog post. It gives a group of enemies a penalty or acts as a ranged Falling Touch against one.

            Not Younger Brother also learns Ensnare Spirit.

            Intermediate Gifts

            Chill Cloak
            And yet another Gift that improves concealment with HONOR!, with a side of suggesting that some people may not actually know how endothermy and ecothermy work (certainly not the latter, given the spirits identified as teaching the Gift).

            All the other Gifts are repeats - Call the Ridden, Pulse of the Prey, and Wind Claws.

            Elder Gifts

            Blood of the Wastes
            Makes life in harsh climates easier based on your HONOR!, as a passive, permanent effect for every form. Also assists in foraging. I guess it's more versatile than Blood of the North, but there was a reason that the survival-assistance Gift wasn't, you know, this far down, and I don't think it's actually difficult to comprehend why.

            Claws of Frozen Death
            May or may not count as a claw gift. Only takes effect in Crinos, and causes your target to be less able to defend against further attacks. I'd ask why this is here and not earlier, but I think I know why.

            Also Rans: Oathbreaker's Bane

            Ghost Council

            (Elder Brother, except not. It's been... reimagined.)

            Beginner Gifts

            Augur
            Scrying under a strange choice for a new name, as you can remote view anything by a reflective surface, such as water. Even a single drop of water, apparently on the other side of the world, though the prior version didn't restrict range, either... which was why it was Rank 3. That's one hell of a swing on range and power for a chargen-available Gift. Truly, this is just as much all over the place as the W:tA of old, so if they were going for a smoother power curve, they botched the roll.

            Blackout
            Instead of Shroud and its ability to simply just make a ten foot cube darken magically, this snuffs out a limited number of light sources. I suppose it can be used to technically turn off electronics by a technicality if they're hooked up to a light or a screen, but if you had a 1000 cubic foot volume go dark, that would have also done it and then some, providing an escape route or avenue for creeping. This seems to be made low-key to play by some kind of "Masquerade" style that W:tA doesn't bother with and prefers to make people sweat through because it's one of the two tenets of the Litany that everyone took seriously. But of course, there's no Litany anymore, effectively.

            Also includes the previously-detailed Sense Danger. Seems like they've got one less Gift here than some others, but Augur is pretty boss, all things considered.

            Intermediate Gifts

            Serpent's Coil
            This is basically turning Coils of the Serpent into mist-based Obtenebration, since while it couldn't inflict damage from grappling prior to W5, it can now. Given how Blackout works, it doesn't surprise me.

            Hands of the Earth
            Not sure why this got placed here and not as an elder level Gift, as it was an upper mid-ranked Gift in prior editions. It's still fairly effective telekinesis, and even stacks up well against V5's telekinesis, amazingly (this tracks; it had twice the lifting weight as five dots of Movement of the Mind through most of the two games' history).

            Mindspeak is also available.

            Elder Gifts

            Shrouded Aspect
            Invisibility under a different name. Lasts for a pathetically short period of time, especially since it got bumped from mid-rank to something only elders get, but such is the trend.

            Dreamwalk, Take the True Form, and Thieving Talons of the Magpie are also available.

            Glass Walkers

            Beginner Gifts

            Skinbind
            You can store an object as a magic tattoo. So... why do the Glass Walkers have it, and how did it even get past a developer sweep when the developer for some reason thought that ending up nude after turning into another form is somehow an important horror scene?

            That's your only new Gift, your other choices are Animal Magnetism, Gaia's Candor, and Spider's Song, so here's an important question - how is it that Garou are bad at social interaction, again? GWs seem like they can play anyone like a fiddle just at chargen, or maybe with some Renown boosts.

            Intermediate Gifts

            Energize
            One to beam up. You can power and maintain objects that require electricity on Rage, possibly also requiring a paired Trait roll if it's broken. Local Garou too angry to stop for gas!

            Your other choices are Grasp From Beyond and... Snarl of Challenge? The hell? That's almost as weird as Furies getting Halt the Coward's Flight.

            Elder Gifts

            Control Machine
            For some reason, they couldn't be bothered to divide this between an easy "mechanical objects only" Gift and a harder "Electronics now, too" version. Unlike living things, you can't endanger the objects, and also gets more difficult if you go against the "purpose" of the device, like unlatching a lock. The fuck? It opens and closes. I'd have placed the difficulty as trying to get it to close on someone instead, if I were doing that, but I'm still hung up on "animals will die for you, machines will tell you to go fuck yourself". I guess I know where the writers' sympathies lie.

            Doppelganger
            Has an umlaut that I didn't type. Quite frankly, it doesn't deserve it, because it lacks the endurance and effectiveness of the legacy version, but then again, it is a W5 "elder" Gift.

            Recover Memory
            It means from a computer data storage device, primarily, though it can also be used on books and other written material. Which raises another question - how is it that they don't know way more archeology than anyone else? If any civilization wrote down anything, it's fair game. You also need other Abilities to understand what you can feel out, though, so it's actually easier to use it on hardcopy texts than on electronic data, as the later requires a Whatever + Technology roll.

            Three tribes that had some of the most interesting tricks in previous material, and what we get is frequently "adjusted" with the expertise of ham hands. Once again, there's also no consistency in power levels or effectiveness to be found. Simple tricks from prior editions (Control Simple Machine) are changed into brutal challenges for no fucking reason at all, the Galestalkers have some random inclusions that, much as Bugerkrieg noted, make them a lot like Forsaken, but with the touch that it's bad because they're erasing inclusion with the approach toward tribes, and the Ghost Council are turning into the Luposombra. I'd really love it if Vampire didn't take over every spread of culture, but given that they took all of the cultures out of W5, I suppose the Vampire stans in charge just defaulted to what they knew.

                For whatever reason, my method for editing things caught the spam filter yesterday. At any rate, we're getting back into the genesis of this thread, with the...

                FiannaHart Wardens

                (Yeah, we can see through the paint. It started flaking immediately with the Gift section.)

                Beginner Gifts

                Sacred Boundary
                You can draw a line in the sand. It doesn't stop anyone, but you'll know if someone crosses it. Also, it has to be drawn with...

                (...sacred water from a caern, blood, or their own urine)
                This reminds me of off-color uses of Spew, a Mokole Gift. At any rate, you can only have one at a time, and it has to be a circle or placed like a tripwire between two objects. It also only lasts for a day or a night, so you'll have to bleed yourself a lot (or do things the wolf way) if you don't have access to a caern. The area also depends on your Glory, which sounds like a setup for a joke.

                Blessed Brew
                I know I already touched on that, but... yeah, snrk, some impressive reimagining this is. "Fianna is just a word" indeed. A word that indicates how this wasn't originally written as a reboot, reimagining, or anything but straight up continuity.

                At any rate, this is like the photo negative of vampires trying to get you to drink their blood, as this allows you to ward off mental influence. Only usable once per get together for a game, but it works for an entire bottle.

                Also Rans From the Auspices: Crow's Laughter

                Intermediate Gifts

                Territorial Dominance
                You can mark spots on the perimeter of any size territory during the full moon as long as you can reach them. Then you become the Fisher King... for all of a few days. There's a bonus die in it for most rolls, but if anyone dumps trash on it or despoils it, you'll take actual injury, or at least Emotional Damage. The timing and duration sharply limit the effectiveness of this Gift, so I don't see people picking it up if they ever start adding to Gift lists.

                Also Rans From the Auspices: Beast's Fealty, Song of Inspiration

                (You know, when W20 compared the Fianna to bards, I don't think that "caricature of a D&D bard" is quite what they had in mind...)

                Elder Gifts

                Balor's Gaze
                You know, in past editions, this was a rather severe penalty (-5). Here, it's less than half that, and it can only affect one person at a time, except maybe not even then because of the resisted roll. I know it's been something of a running gag for the elder Gifts in W5 to be incredibly underwhelming, but this is getting tiresome.

                The Living Wood
                ...This should have been in the CoG list first. What the hell, W5? I suppose putting in Warp Frenzy would have revealed that Crinos wasn't really the true end-all, be-all of ripping and tearing (and I think that vampire fans run screaming at the prospect of Gift of the Spriggan actually still existing), but it's fairly obvious that the HWs were Fianna first, so they shouldn't have to poach the CoGs.

                Trees that are nearby will grapple enemies, bushes will grapple enemies less well, nothing else matters unless the ST is feeling generous. May or may not also be used to grow plants; it's rather non-committal to the idea.

                Also Rans From the Auspices: Kiss of Helios, Living Ward

                Red Talons

                Beginner Gifts

                Hidden Killer
                Previously, this made werewolf-made wounds look like knives and gunshots to make it look like some kind of illicit deal gone wrong, a mugging, etc. Now it completely eliminates signs of injury, so that corpses look like mysteriously exsanguinated bodies. The text specifically highlights that they resemble the victims of someone who really wanted to get that last Hunger Die ticked off, so they're either going for Red Talons being the most ardent enemies of vampires like in 1e's text for them, or they just wanted to make them act like the Sabbat. I'd say that the tack the writing takes leans toward the latter, but the book is kind of back and forth about Garou interacting with vampires.

                Render Down
                Technically improved in one respect from its last appearance in W20, as it allows you to turn an entire car into a pile of glass, metal, and oil. Not so improved for requiring minutes to work or relying on HONOR!

                Also Rans: Razor Claws (Red Talons are the new Fenrir!), Song of Rage (urgh)

                Intermediate Gifts

                Gaia's Embrace
                Would not have pinned gravity control as a Red Talon thing. This penalizes ranged weapon use and causes flying characters to regret flying, as they experience a little slice of the BattleTech in-universe instructional film for aerospace pilots, "Deconstructive Lithobraking: When the Ground Is Not Your Friend". Of course, if you were flying at the altitude level in BT where you could get this Gift used on you, you were already likely to crash, anyway. Has about a three hex radius, in BT board game terms. Also applies Honor as a straight penalty to ranged attacked (presumably only with projectiles and not energy beams, but since the Technocracy is unlikely to show up, ever, in 5e WoD, we may never know). VTOLs are also likely to crash, but that's VTOLs for you. That armor limit of only two on the rotor is a killer.

                Quicksand
                If your enemy is on soil, you can make them reenact the scene from The Neverending Story that makes everyone bawl. It's only a -2 penalty (ofc) but it also caps movement at walking speed.

                Also Rans: Fangs of Judgment

                Elder Gifts

                Shield of the Wyld
                AKA Shield of Gaia, except not. At any rate, this is limited to reducing a limited amount of damage against constructed weapons that aren't made of silver or those that have a spirit in them. For reference, the original was a legendary Gift that gave the one learning it a choice about which form of technology they could be permanently unaffected by, with the only limit being that you couldn't choose nukes. And from then on, you couldn't be shot if you chose guns, couldn't be recorded or photographed if you chose cameras, etc. But you won't be getting that, because this is W5.

                Also Rans: Feral Regression, The Thousand Forms

                Shadow Lords

                Fatal Flaw
                You learn what ways someone will take agg. This can be used to soft-spot vampires, because "throw them out into the sun" is probably a good indication of that, even if not infallible. Can also give a damage bonus, if you've done your research. Pretty comparable, since the old version could add on one die of damage, but then gave somewhat more abstract definitions of flaws, like insecurities or vulnerabilities, as it was based on the Empathy Ability and all.

                Icy Chill of Despair
                Now, the previous version of this was one par with Majesty, the Presence 5 power. This one... is not, and that's kind of a travesty. It's just a de-buff to spending or regaining WP.

                Also Rans: Shadow Sense

                Intermediate Gifts

                Dire Distraction
                The activation roll for this Gift is odd in that it is a "guess who?" for the origin of the effect. Why one curses with Glory, though, I'm not sure.

                The rest of this level for the Lords is also-rans. I'm getting tired of listing them, so blurry, starey, and scary will have to do.

                Elder Gifts

                Thunderclap
                This one has an odd duration that suggests it knocks people to the ground for an entire scene, but that couldn't be correct. May also inflict a penalty against others. I'm not sure why this is here, and yet, there is that recurring issue with the elder gifts...

                Under the Gun
                Mostly comparable to the old version, which is damning with faint praise, on account of how that was a mid-ranked Gift and not, say, the ability to spawn shadow clones or supernatural compulsion. Basically, the only Gift in this tier that fits is the one that lets them be Lelouch... kind of.

                The reduced scale of these is getting grating, especially since a lot more Disciplines from V5 managed to avoid getting nerfed at all, and inherent powers like blood bonding or boosting Attributes were left more or less untouched.

                        I think in W5 you don't really pick Gifts and Rites based on character-choices. Due to the combination of how weak a lot of Gifts are and how punishing the system can be, the best way to approach this is to concentrate on the resource-management of your Rage and make sure you always go into combat with high Rage and keep it at a low level when you're doing more social/sneaky stuff. And for that reason, the first category of Gifts/Rites you should focus on are the ones that let are the ones that let you manage your Rage-score. The next-best ones are the ones that are about dealing with the Umbra (letting you peek into it, Spirit-stuff). And the third one are all the Gift that give you a die-bonus based on your Renown-rating when you do certain things.

                        The rest you can take it or leave it, I think. Especially because if you don't manage your Rage properly and you end up losing the wolf, you can't use Gifts or Rites. And if for whatever reason, you end up in a situation where you enter Frenzy, you can't use Gifts either. So in that sense, the game almost begs players to focus on "gaming the systems" by focusing on stuff like Song of Rage or the Rite of Rage. If you don't do that, the system will kick your butt and then you won't be able to use the Gifts and Rites you picked instead anyway.

                        And once you're in combat, you're better off just throwing the Crinos Form at whatever obstacle the ST has put in front of you. You're keeping Rage in reserve for Regeneration and hoping you can trigger Brutal Outcomes on your rolls in combat. And you also need a reserve of WP, so that you don't enter Frenzy in your Crinos-Form. This system does not reward characters for trying to get clever with Gifts. And ST are in a bit of a pickle here as well. If they're more lenient with the strength of the enemies to allow players to spend Rage/WP on using Gifts, then the person who's using the Crinos Form as a blunt instrument just smashes the enemy into pieces with Brutal Outcomes and the Crinos Form long before the PC who's trying to employ some strategy using Gifts does anything. If ST creates opponents that can go toe-to-toe with a high Rage Crinos Form, then they will beat the shit out of any PC that's spending Rage on using Gifts rather than reserving it for Regeneration or a lack of WP leads to Frenzy when they're in their Crinos Form and after that, they're forced into playing "Crinos Form as a blunt instrument" anyway.

                                Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
                                And yet they say the STs should make sure the players don't strategize with their Rage XD
                                Ah, yeah, that sidebar on pg 132...

                                It even says: "Likewise, a Storyteller should be mindful of players attempting to game the system by using Gifts or other abilities for the sake of dumping Rage." But, look, what else does the Gift Song of Serenity and Rite of Tranquility do? The same way Rite of Rage is transparently just a tool to increase Rage, those are tools to decrease Rage. And Rite of the Wolf Reborn is just a tool to reverse "losing the wolf" without having to wait for a character to howl at the moon.

                                And I don't really see the appeal in playing W5 in a way where the ST isn't very liberal and forgiving in how PCs can gain Rage. Because a scarcity leads to one of two things: Either you're gonna invest in those abilities that rules-as-written just let you increase/decrease your Rage or you're running a game of WtA where shifting, using Gifts and fighting rarely happens. The latter isn't even about the torment of being a werewolf. If anything it's about the PC being wary of using their werewolf-powers because they're constantly so close to losing the wolf. In that scenario, it's all about the PC having werewolf-powers but being unable to access them. That would be the conflict. Not that being a Garou with Rage is a cursed existence.

                                This also reminds me of this bit on pg 135 where the corebook is concerned about players shifting too often: "Note that too-frequent (or frivolous…) shifting takes a terrible toll on body and mind. Should a character shift back and forth multiple times in a short time span, such as by seeking simply to shed Rage, the Storyteller may realize such massive bodily stress by inflicting appropriate levels of Aggravated damage to Health and/or Willpower. (Let the player know before you do so, the better to inform their decision-making.)​" The only reason you could be shifting so many times in this game is that if you stay in a supernatural Form you have to take Rage-checks at the start of every scene and at the same time you need to be in a supernatural Form to be able to use most of the Gifts. So, this game is punishing you both for shifting to a supernatural Form and staying in it but also for not being in a supernatural Form.                                                         The Rites are also a bit of a mess. First off, the distinction between "Common Rites" and "Social Rites" doesn't make sense. The only way it is a distinction is that the writers bothered to write rules for the first category and not the second.                                                         But those two also bring up an element in W5 I hate and it's "chagrin". Rather than hashing out Renown gains and losses at Moots or some other kind of social gathering, the way Renown is handled in W5 is that a particular Renown-track can be put "in chagrin" (ie it counts as one dot less). And how does that happen?

                                                        "When a Garou acts in overt opposition to a Renown virtue’s creed, and in an especially noteworthy way, the Storyteller may declare that character to be in chagrin. Mark that Renown type on the character sheet as subject to chagrin."
                                                        So the way the W5 corebook envisions this is that a PC does something and if it breaks the tenets of that particular Renown, that Renown type is put "in chagrin". But it's all up to the ST. That puts a huge burden on the ST for how to regulate this and on the player for having to figure out what the exact rules of Renown are. The way I handled it wasn't exactly like it's described in the corebook because I had a situation where one PC got into a fight with the guards as two of them were sneaking into a warehouse. And the second one wanted to move on and leave the other one behind. So I told that player I would put his Honor in chagrin if he were to leave his packmate behind when he's in trouble. And so what we agreed upon was what he did instead is that he announced himself to the guards, the first player who got in trouble told the second one that he could handle this alone and he should move on.

                                                        And removing chagrin is the same thing. The book wants you to talk to the ST and the ST then tells you how you can get out of chagrin. Again, it's all on the ST to make this work.

                                                        This chagrin-"system" also only gets even more confounding when you add the Silver Fangs Ban to it: "If a Silver Fang takes an action that causes them to risk chagrin or losing Renown, they may recover only a single Willpower at the start of the next session, instead of their Composure or Resolve rating.​" (I could almost believe that this is another one of those cases where this was copy-pasted from the draft of the Hunters Entertainment version and that's why this makes little sense in the context of the actual chagrin rules in this book. Because there's no "risking chagrin" in the way it's outlined.)

                                                        If you were to do it as described, I guess the players are supposed to read the mind of the ST to know what actions would put them in chagrin and which ones wouldn't and the ST is supposed to read the minds of any Silver Fang player so that whenever these PCs come even close to doing something that would put their Renown in chagrin, the ST can trigger the Ban.

                                                        And then you get to the Rites which are connected to this...
                                                        • Rite of Shame makes chagrin worse. The problem is that the declaration of this Renown-violation wasn't made by anyone within the setting but rather by the ST. If a PC does something that puts them in chagrin but nobody knows that they did this thing, did the Renown-violation really happen?
                                                        • Rite of Contrition needlessly turns the "removing chagrin"-process into a mechanic. (The nature of this penance depends on the grievance and the offended parties, and is up to the Storyteller.)​" You're already in the process of doing penance in a way that is up to the ST! How does it make sense to add this caveat of "But if you have a Brutal Outcome in this Rite that's all about doing penance, you have to do penance again!"...?
                                                        • Rite of Celebration in this system feels completely neutered. Renown are just dots you buy to get better at doing Gifts and hopefully there's a narrative justification.
