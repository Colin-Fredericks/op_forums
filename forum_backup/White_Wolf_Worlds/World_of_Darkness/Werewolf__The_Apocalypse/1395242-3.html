                I thought I was going to be one of those with the most negative depiction of Garou, but compared to some of the posters here I'm extremely mild!

                There is a power imbalance between Garou and Kinfolk, but I think it is important to remember that 1) Kinfolk have numbers, 2) Kinfolk know a lot about the Garou, and 3) Kinfolk aren't helpless. It's relatively easy for Kinfolk to get access to high purity silver. If living among the Garou really was that bad, it wouldn't be hard for a kinfolk community to pull a Sicilian Vespers and wipe out an entire sept, or sell them out to the enemy.

                In fact, I wouldn't be surprised if that's been done on occasion in Garou history at the most abusive septs. And while I'm sure the Garou took revenge on those specific kinfolk, I'm also sure it's lead to reforms and more protections of kinfolk in local Litanies as a result. Because no one wants to be murdered in their sleep, or see other Garou die to no benefit to Gaia or the Nation. Self-defeating eventually gets expunged.

                I do think terrible abuses occur, but that happens in humanity too. They are more the exceptions, and the Garou themselves identify them as abuses. Sometimes the abusive Garou get away with it. Sometimes they don't. This can be fun to explore in game for PCs. Attitudes and actions help distinguish NPCs from one another. And it allows PCs to take sides, forge alliances, and identify opponents or enemies.

                I think there should be conflict among Garou (as opposed to just external enemies) because it makes for a better game. And I like making kinfolk a prominent element of the setting. I feel too often I, as an ST, neglect this element in my chronicles as a real part of the setting. And I feel other STs and even Players do as well. So a discussion like this generates ideas for everyone on the type of things that could happen at a sept.

                I do think many Kinfolk are raised in a cultlike atmosphere. But I think this is less like Jonestown and more like a Mennonite colony (not to pick on the Mennonites, I just need a more normal example of a community that divorces itself from the larger culture). There's propaganda, conformity, isolation, and strictness, but actual crimes committed against their members are rare. This doesn't mean that there can't be some kinfolk settlements that are like Jonestown. Some septs go bad. That can be an interesting part of the setting. But I don't think it is the standard.

                Part of my goal here is to identify "what is abuse" and "what is acceptable". So I can help guide the PCs of my own chronicles. What is acceptable to a Garou can be different from what is acceptable in a human community. But it should still allow a generic player of the game to play and not leave in disgust.

                        I don't think that the kinfolk could effectively mount much of a resistance against the Garou. Especially since they will be raised to believe that their abuse is normal, not only normal but righteous, because their masters are blessed by Gaia to save reality. People raised for generations to believe such things would have a hard enough time resisting humans, much less the demigods that Garou are.

                        I do, however, think that there is a factor that would heavily moderate what I described above, and that would be the protectors that Black Fox described. Not just protectors, but family protectors. Any group of kinfolk is going to be tied to a group of Garou, who would be their protectors or if you want to be a bit darker about it, their owners. Their lords perhaps. These Garou won't just protect those kinfolk, they will be their literal family, even immediate family. Because of the way that Garou heredity works, a Garou's parents, their children, their brothers and sisters - those are all likely to be kinfolk and those will be the kinfolk who who under that Garou's protection.

                        So if some Garou wants to go and rape a kinfolk, in all likelihood he was be raping his pack mate's sister, mother, or daughter. That Garou would likely be raping his own cousin. That isn't going to go over too well and would cause a lot of fight's and bad blood. A Garou from another pack or sept or Garou group who does so would be unofficially declaring war by raping the immediate family members of another group of Garou.

                        So I think that overt rape would be rare, because of the above reasons. Though there would still likely be a lot of pressure on kinfolk to consent to a Garou's advances and how much pressure does there have to be before the consent isn't really consent anymore? I imagine that every few Garou see themselves as rapists and very few kinfolk see themselves as being raped. The holy one wants sex, you should feel lucky to be chosen, how perverse would be be to say no? Likely this situation would be handled in a more orderly manner, like arranged marriages, than just Garou getting an urge and selecting a kinfolk.

                        I imagine that there would be a similar mindset for other Garou requests. If a kinfolk owns a business and a local leading Garou asks for money, or for them to sell the business, or otherwise do something that hurts financially - the kinfolk would be expected to comply even if there were no obvious fear of retribution. It is as though a devout Christian were just visited by a literal angel, displaying miracles, who asked for money - few would dream of saying no.

                        I think that in all of these cases, that there would be an issue of chain of command. A pack leader or influential elder may be able to make extravagant demands upon kinfolk and that would be seen as entirely justified. Other Garou, though, would likely face negative repercussions if they abused their authority over kinfolk.

                                        Kinfolk fill the same role in Werewolf that Retainers (especially ghouls, but not limited to them) do in Vampire. They both provide servants for the main supernatural character that can act in the mundane world on behalf of their master. But vampire retainers have it far worse than kinfolk. However, that darkness can easily be elided during vampire chronicles. One reason for that, I think, is that while many things vampires do to retainers are abusive, most of them are metaphors for actual real world violations. Feeding on people, turning them into vampires or ghouls, using mental and emotional disciplines like Presence and Dominate, are obvious metaphors of rape and other abuse. But because they are metaphors and not the act itself, it can be easier to digest.

                                        I wonder if there is something similar that can be done to Kinfolk that could be metaphor of certain abuses that if stated directly could be too much for the gaming table. But would make a good stand in.

                                        We need to use proper "source material" as inspiration from actual werewolf legends, folk tales, and popular culture. It can't be something arbitrary and new, but something that would click immediately in people's head as "yeah, that would happen to werewolves". Like something that shows kinfolk as being complicit in any "crimes" of the werewolf. Like eating any bloody meat they bring in (from animals they killed in wolf form), but never being allowed to ask where it came from (as this is a universal taboo in "trained" kinfolk - because it would bring "bad luck" but the real reason is that doing so might inadvertently harm the veil). I think expectations of silence from Kinfolk on various matters might be good. In game, it could be used simply as an interesting taboo that gives flavor to Kinfolk. There are lots of things Kinfolk don't talk about. And they're trained in that. There is a long list of things they simply don't talk about, they don't ask questions about, and they don't answer if other people ask them.

                                        These people don't lie to cover things up. They simply are silent. They talk around things so they don't have to mention it. The taboo is that strong.

                                        It would be a good metaphor because we know victims of abuse are often told/warned to not mention it to people (because the perpetrator wants to remain hidden). Thus in less abusive kinfolk communities, this range of topics is much less. In more abusive communities, this silence becomes more and more present.

                                        In terms of having the right feel, I think this scene from An American Werewolf in London is a good example. If we translate to WoD terms, it's a tavern in a kinfolk community,with obvious signs of werewolfery present - perhaps as chiminage to spirits, a fetish, or as a sign of some kind. But despite it being out in the open, it's just completely ignored as if it isn't there. Two outsiders come in, and then ask about it. But they don't get any answer.
