    Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
    Combine all this with the fact pigs can survive on anything, including human feces, and we've got plenty of data points to start concocting some pig-men action. Hell, the introduction of pigs to the Americas might, in and of itself, be part of the Wyrm's plan to cause mass death. Especially in regions that Garou have presence and/or Kinfolk. Humans didn't need encouragement to bring their bacon with them, but it certainly furthered the Wyrm's interests.
    I like the idea that the arrival of feral swine in the Americas could have been the trigger for the creation of the swine things, and I have a great idea of who was behind it. The Voodoo Pigs. Originally the Skull Pigs were some Ice Age species of North American tapirs and peccaries that were corrupted into monsters before the Pure Ones arrived. These monsters were thus indigenous to North America. Later the writers forgot this part and simply decided to make them corrupted descendants of the Grondr - wrong species, wrong continent, wrong timeline. So if you go with that retcon in your chronicles, this may not work for you. But with the original lore you have these intelligent, magic using Voodoo Pigs who now have access to some new, unknown species creating ecological upheaval. They could have created the first pig men in secret completely isolated from any human contact, and then recruited the first human cultists from any scavenging human survivors of a people wiped out by disease, by criminal exiles, or other social outcasts on the fringes of human society.

    Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
    Through the forgetting-magic, the island might escape notice for centuries. (Who knows? Maybe this has something to do with the "Bermuda Triangle".). The island becomes visited by pirates (vintage or modern) who stumble upon it by accident while looking for hideouts from the law.
    I like the idea that there may have been Thrassus cultists among the Caribbean pirates. Pirates were made up of all sorts of outcasts which is where the cult is strongest. You could invent some sort of pirate lore concerning a particularly awful pirate who performed horrible deeds (actually one of the worst fomori or Ferectoi of the cult). Steal what you can from actual pirate history, supernatural stuff from Disney's Pirates of the Caribbean, and even a legend concerning some "buried treasure" that in fact should never be found. Just need to come up with a great fictional pirate name and spin a whole legend around him. There's a lot to work with. Maybe the Brujah pirate Smiling Jack once knew him or served on his ship?

    Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
    Given that Greenland is mostly a frozen wasteland, you could easily take more than a little inspiration from John Carpenter's The Thing. (Which, funnily enough, is the THIRD part of Carpenter's "Apocalypse Trilogy").
    I selected Greenland because 1) Lovecraft mentioned Greenland as one of those fringe places with a known Cthulhu Cult, 2) the term skraeling has as one of its possible meanings "wretched one" which sounds a lot like fomori, and 3) the disappearance of the Norse settlement of Greenland is one of those things that can easily lend itself to supernatural explanations. So a Thrassus cult that formed among exiles/criminals/outcasts on Greenland wiping out a small and isolated band of Garou and kinfolk fits those ideas.

    Whether the Get managed to defeat the threat at the cost of their own lines and send word to their brethren back east (perhaps a kinfolk fleeing east or the last Get survivor leaving by the last use of a moonbridge from a minor caern now being abandoned), or just have late arriving Wendigo end the threat (or perhaps some other group like Dreamspeakers) is all up to whatever the ST prefers. In any case, I think a doomed sept of Get of Fenris - no matter how small - helps create the idea that Thrassus is an actual threat that can defeat even great warriors of the Garou.

    I do like your idea there is some buried threat under the Greenland ice. That provides nice story seeds that can be used in any number of ways.

    I remembered some Get and Wendigo ancestor spirits mentioned in Axis Mundi that had some early contact while these Garou were alive, and just checked to see if they could somehow be included as part of this. But it turns out that they met in Vinland, not Greenland, and their meeting would have been in the 11th century while the Norse settlements in Greenland would not disappear until sometime in the early 15th century. But an ST could use the family line of Ragnar Red Axe as later involved in this encounter to provide more details on what happened and build a small little history of a minor and forgotten Get of Fenris sept on the shore of Greenland.

    In any case, I hope others provide some more ideas of encounters. The more specific details, the more it makes the menace seem real.

        Been away from the forums for a while, but my curiosity was piqued when I saw the thread title referring to Thrassus Thrice-Damned. I always found the entry in the Book of the Weaver on Exhibit 338 to be fascinating; just its very nature as a transparent container made of "not-glass" holding an unknown viscous substance was something I found very compelling for some reason. Anyway, on a lark some years ago, I tried to do a short write-up on the Talons of the Wyrm and put it in my GoogleDocs in the hopes that someone might find it useful for inspiration. Keep in mind, I tried to find all mentions of the Talons of the Wyrm in W:tF books, but I only found references in Rite of Passage, Rage Across Russia, Caerns: Places of Power and Book of the Weaver. My research was cursory and might be incomplete. Since my write-up is not that long, I'll copy and paste it below. For the record, none of this is canon; it's all just spitballing on my part. I also included a couple of paragraphs on the nature of the remaining two Talons that were never detailed by White Wolf. I just made up the names Vothnaak and Galchus and tried to imagine what sort of artifacts would contain the last two Talons of the Wyrm. In particular the information on the Tain being one of the artifacts stored at the Sept of Bygone Visions is stuff I totally made up. Anyway, to wit:

        The Five Talons of the Wyrm

        Narlthus, Thumb (or First Talon) of the Wyrm: referenced in Rite of Passage. Currently held by a sept of Black Spiral Dancers, led by Azaera. It has not yet been released from its egg-shaped meteorite prison. Reputed to be the Talon of Beast-of-War.

        Koschei the Deathless, the First Finger (or Second Talon) of the Wyrm, "He-Who-Points": referenced in Rage Across Russia. Currently bound in the Soul Egg, a gemstone held by a sept of Uktena Banetenders in Russia. Reputed to be the Talon of Eater-of-Souls.

        Thrassus Thrice-Damned, Smallest- (or Fifth) Talon of the Wyrm: referenced in Book of the Weaver. Currently imprisoned in the Anti-Christ Serum, labeled in a classified storage facility as “Exhibit 338,” location unknown (but maybe in Roswell, NM in Area 51?). The object imprisoning Thrassus Thrice-Damned appears to be a three-foot long, seamless glass capsule (but if the transparent material is indeed glass, it is of a type of glass that cannot be scratched or marred by any man-made instrument) containing a thick blue-green fluid of unknown substance. As the Smallest Talon, Thrassus Thrice-Damned may be the Talon of the Defiler Wyrm, but this is only non-canon speculation.

        The Sept of the Bygone Visions entry in Caerns: Places of Power had no references to an imprisoned Talon of the Wyrm. The only other reference to the Sept of the Bygone Visions mentioning the Talons of the Wyrm was in Book of the Weaver suggesting that since the discovery of the Anti-Christ Serum happened at the same time as "recent struggles at the Sept of Bygone Visions" the two incidents were somehow related which in turn suggested that the Anti-Christ Serum was Thrassus Thrice-Damned. I found another reference to the unnamed Talon held in the Sept of Bygone Visions on page 97 of Rage Across Russia in the same section describing Koschei.

        World of Darkness: Blood-Dimmed Tides makes reference on page 16 to an unnamed Talon of the Wyrm lying buried somewhere in the Arctic Circle. The Garou of the Wendigo tribe believe that this Talon of the Wyrm lies hidden underneath the half-frozen silt, guarded by terrible aquatic monsters and Banes.

        Assuming there are five Talons of the Wyrm, that accounts for all of them. Either the Third or Fourth Talon could be contained in the vault of the Sept of Bygone Visions.

        It seems more than likely that all of the Talons have been imprisoned as their existence would have come to light long ago if any one of them were still free....

        Unofficial write-ups for the Third and Fourth Talons (feel free to use or modify this information for inspiration):

        • Galchus, also known as “He-Who-Scorns” and the Third/Middle Talon of the Wyrm, is buried in the frozen silt of the Arctic Ocean. Long ago, when Galchus was still free and fleeing from the Garou, he was pursued by a pack of Wendigo warriors and Uktena Banetenders. The Uktena had brought from the south with them two artifacts that were to be used to imprison Galchus: a stone sarcophagus of a unique and unknown design (any human archaeologist who tried to identify the origins of the sarcophagus would be utterly baffled) and a simple sewing needle made from whale bone. Once the pack had defeated Galchus, the surviving Garou performed a Rite to bind Galchus within the needle, then sealed the needle inside the sarcophagus and sunk the sarcophagus beneath the frigid waves of the Arctic Ocean. Over time, the sarcophagus sank into the silt, where it has remained ever since. Now, thousands of years later, a number of aquatic monsters and Banes surround the area, as if vigilantly trying to protect the Talon. The Wendigo still tell half-remembered tales of a Talon of the Wyrm sunk beneath the waves in the far north, but Galchus’ name, his location and even the manner of his binding have been lost to history.

        • Vothnaak, Fourth Talon of the Wyrm, is imprisoned inside an artifact called the Tain, which appears to be a very old and strange-looking jewel box. The Tain is one of the many artifacts contained in the Womb, the hidden vault of artifacts located at the Black Fury Sept of Bygone Visions on the island of Miria (known to the modern world as Ecube). The Sept of Bygone Visions do not know that a Talon of the Wyrm is imprisoned inside one of their stored artifacts. Vothnaak’s name, the tale of how he came to be imprisoned within the Tain and even the tale of how the Tain came to be stored inside the Womb have all been lost to time. It is up to the Storyteller to decide if Iona Kinslayer’s vision of a shadow spreading across the Glen of Miria has anything to do with Vothnaak.

        Assumed Directions that the Talons of the Wyrm fled (assuming they “landed” in Europe):

        Koschei ran to the East(-Northeast) to Russia.

        Narlthus ran to the North(west) to Canada.

        Thrassus Thrice-Damned ran in an unknown direction. Assuming that Thrassus Thrice-Damned is located at Area 51 (and not any other undisclosed location), then it may have run towards the (American) Southwest (assuming it was bound near that area and not later transported to Area 51).

        Galchus ran to the North to the Arctic Circle.

        Vothnaak ran to the Southeast to Greece (assuming it got captured and bound near Greece).



        ​I hope someone finds this useful.
