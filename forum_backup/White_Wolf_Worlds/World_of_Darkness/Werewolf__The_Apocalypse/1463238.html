    Lupus Gnosis Issue?

    10-09-2021, 01:32 PM
    I'm an old hat in online games, enough an old hat that I've seen a lot of limiting of the lupus breed in play. While some say lupus are just impossible to play (not true), others say lupus should be limited hardly for their gnosis 5.

    This gnosis 5 is used as a huge stopgap. Enough that I've seen people demand more limits to the breed in addition to the character creation ability restrictions.

    One thing I have almost never seen done is lowering the gnosis score. I myself would prefer lupus to have 3 like the metis, rather than 5.

    What do you guys think?

      Well, in our LARP we have no lupus, because not spending time in breed form is a renown reduction, but since all players are humans in real life, we play homid or occasional metis. A human pretending to be a wolf might work well in Cosplay-circles, but we deem it silly in a way too distracting form the topics we usually incorporate in out game.

      I agree, that it isn't impossible to play lupus. It is, imo, nigh impossible to get people to agree on how a lupus should be played. We all are human and entertain that homids have a huge bandwidth of characteristics, even within the constraining archetypes of tribe and auspice. In my experience, it is, harder for players to be okay if someone else at the table plays a lupus different to what they believe a lupus should be played and that is even more true in games with a lot of players like LARPs or Online games.

      That said, we have an alternate character creation system, where breed doesn't dictate a starting gnosis, but dictate a bandwidth of suitable Gnosis starting values, depending on rank. Since all dot need to be bought with character creation experience points, choosing a lupus isn't getting you 20XP worth of Gnosis points simply because you choose lupus (compared to homid). So, yes, you could create a lupus with Gnosis 3 in our game. Also, envy about this costly feature is removed, by the alternate character creation approach.

                              It was probably a game design mistake to give different breeds different amount of Gnosis, and different tribes different amount of Willpower. Possibly even different Rage for the auspices (although at least the auspice angle has better justification in game). In early days of the game, you could tell who were the cheese players because they brought to the table their Lupus Stargazer Ahroun as this got them a lot of build points for free.

                              A game like Vampire didn't have that problem because other than Clan weaknesses, you didn't have one group at a disadvantage or advantage over another. Traits like Willpower, max Blood Pool size, Humanity, etc. was all based on points spent elsewhere - certain Backgrounds or Virtues that any player could have chosen.

                              If we wanted to go down a similar route, we could make all starting PCs have 1 each in Gnosis and Willpower (and possibly Rage) and then assign a certain number of Tempers points (I recall somewhere referring to this category as Tempers though I can't remember where, rename if needed) that a player can divide up however he sees fit. Maybe somewhere between 4-6 points or some other sensible number that is spent on a one to one basis.

                              Maybe at this level we even state there is certain Maximum allowed per auspice or breed in certain categories at this stage, that can then be supplemented by Freebies. That's how the game handles Abilities restrictions for Lupus - certain skills are disallowed during the Ability 13/9/5 breakdown at character creation, but Freebies can spent on anything if you really want your Lupus to have points in Computer or Drive. Or we can dump any restrictions entirely.

                              That way, no PC gets more build points than any other. That eliminates anyone from getting a build point advantage and would lead to players only picking tribes/auspice/breed based on their concept. We can always state that traditionally this auspice has more Rage than others, or that this breed has more Gnosis than others. And STs can even enforce this as part of NPC stats. But players can build their characters however they want, and no one is at a build point disadvantage than others.

                                              Another thing that I find frustrating with the talk of how lupus "should" be portrayed: time.

                                              Lots of people seem to think the a lupus PC had their First Change, cub-hood, and Rite of Passage all within the last week of history, instead of years to adapt to their expanded mental state, the reality of the Garou world, and so on.

                                              I had a lupus I played for ~10 years in game (longer IRL time), who I created as having had his First Change four years before start. So that was 2 years as a wolf, 4 years as a Lost Cub/found Cub/training/etc. and 10 years of experience as a Garou out in the world. Some people would complain about him not being "wolf" enough, to which I had a simple response: he's been leaving around humans for three (then four, then five...) times longer than he was a ever just a wolf. If you spend four years living in another country with a very different culture, and a very different language, and you still couldn't act like a local even there's obvious tells you're not one... would people think you were even trying to learn how? Because if my lupus can pass as a human, he's doing something he's been practicing how to do for years. Thanks for the compliment.
