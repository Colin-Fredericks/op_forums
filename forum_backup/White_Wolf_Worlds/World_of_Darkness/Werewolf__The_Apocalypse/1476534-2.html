    Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
    However, despite the flexibility of the werewolf, I think people seeking a werewolf game want a few specific things;
    1. Loss of control
    2. Monster inside scaring the person
    3. Painful transformations
    4. Full moon change without control
    5. Violent slaughter without the human side's consent.

    And while WtA DOES offer these, it is not the whole game. Essentially, take Gaia, the Wyrm, the War of Apocalypse (, the breeds, the auspices) and such off and I think that is more what people seek. Essentially a game of dealing with a human and monster side.

    Which is very close to Vampire, which is why WtA went its own direction.
    Actually, I don't want these things in my games, and I don't think to many other people I know are seeking for it.

    As far as loss of control goes, sure, there are players who want that for their characters and as their experience in game. Me, personally, not so much. Still, it is worth mentioning, that there is a difference to loosing control to one's emotions, as opposed to an outside force. There is no submission there, when a garou looses control, the garou is dominating the surroundings, usually.

    There have been players who dwelled in what being a garou meant for their character. Usually players who started out as cubs. Most players don't explore that anymore, once they have some experience with the game. At least not in my experience.

    I never had, to my knowledge, contact with anyone, who wanted the physical change to be painful. Character groth and personality shifts due to emotionally painful events, sure, but that's something else.

    Also, I never experienced any player who wanted to be forced to shift on the full moon. Or loose control due to the moon. Even those players I know, who really want to loose control do want to loose it because of the emotional state of their character, as triggered by the situation they see themselves confronted by, but not by an external trigger...

    Slaughter without the human side's consent also is kind of close to loosing control. In any case, all the WtA I play have the garou as a single person. There is no Dr. Jeckel and Mr. Hyde where the monster has it's own personality and the garou in homid form thinks of the Crinos "That's not me, that's the moster". I mean that could happen, I guess, especially if the Gnosis is so low, that one doesn't remember Frenzy, but even then, the other garou would tell the character, that is what you did, not what some monster did.

    What I want from WtA specifically, as a player, would be something like this:
    1. Power - Just with any other game, I want to be a protagonist that has some ability to change things and possibly is special compared to the average character in the game world. I don't like, for example, a game like Paranoia, where the character is meant to be subject of the computer's rule.
    2. Teamwork - Garou as a sept and within a pack usually work for the same goal. As compared to for example Vampire LARP, with a lot of backstabbing, intrigue and often out-of-game grievances stemming from these, the werewolf game is more friendly. Players are competing for sept offices, who's pack is getting the next mission and so on. There are hostilities, but they do not fill the game and you don't have to be paranoid to survive...
    3. Being the Hero - Garou have a mission and while their means are questionable (by human standards and Ragabashs everywhere) they believe in their cause and see themselves as the heros and feel good about what they do. I want my character to have that satisfaction. I don't usually want plots where victories are only partial or where every win is countered by to losses right away. This can bring a sense of dread, but, like I said, I don't need that.

    So, these are rather general statements. I like WtA for it's Setting, or most of it. That being 2nd Edition and some aspects of revised. That's what we were playing, when I started the game.

        Also, I agree with monteparnas on the premise, that one letting go of the Rage is meant to be a cathartic experience for the garou. In our LARP we always double check our plots, so the follow the guideline "Violence is always an option". It might not be the best option, the safest, but usually it is an option.
        This and a huge part of the nature of the game is that the antagonists are things that frustrate and make people feel powerless IRL. Racism, capitalism, poverty, pollution, global warming, misogyny, etc. The Rage in-game is the cleansing fire that can burn them away but also can destroy something good as it spreads.

        Essentially, it is making pollution a physical target and punching it.

        This is why the Captain Planet insult always feels hollow to me. Because not only are these issues not just things in cartoons, but the nature of WtA goes very much into the punk side of Gothic Punk. It is saying fuck you to how things are and fighting for how things could be.

                                        Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
                                        However, despite the flexibility of the werewolf, I think people seeking a werewolf game want a few specific things;

                                        1. Loss of control
                                        2. Monster inside scaring the person
                                        3. Painful transformations
                                        4. Full moon change without control
                                        5. Violent slaughter without the human side's consent.
                                        I don't know if that is what most players actually want. All of those things take away from player agency. Instead of letting players decide what their characters do, these points would hijack that. And players hate that. It's not fun for them. Even in Vampire which states it is a game of personal horror and wants PCs to explore the consequences of doing evil, 1) the rules that take away agency are used only some of the time, and 2) most players I see downplay the personal horror aspect and want to live out power fulfillment fantasies.

                                        What I do think some players want is to see more of the classic werewolf tropes in the setting. (And the tropes can be varied, actual folklore, movies, and stories about werewolves often do not agree - and that's fine.) Too often, the game ignores them. As you say, the setting actually does have concepts that can fulfill those tropes. They just aren't utilized. And we almost never saw them in setting sourcebooks, so it's easy for players to overlook.

                                        But WtA does have concepts like Lunatics that meet almost everything you laid out above. But Lunatics as a concept is rarely mentioned again outside a rare 1e supplement. But what would have happened, if we saw at least one Lunatic NPC in each of the Rage books, exploring different ways that individual Lunatic impacts the local setting, and how PCs might interact with that person?

                                        We have the concept of Ronins, which would be a great place to explore a Garou who could have a life totally different from the culture of the Garou Nation. Ronin could easily be used to explore alternate takes on the werewolf (conforming more to people's expectations) while not invalidating anything about the setting. And we could have been given more tools a how to incorporate a Ronin PC with a traditional Garou pack, making Ronin + Garou PC party just as easy as a Vampire including a Caitiff with Clans. But the game didn't so many STs wouldn't know how to run a game with this odd PC, so they discourage players from even making such a character. (I remember we have talked extensively about how Ronin can play a greater role in the game on the past ion several different threads.)

                                        There's actually a lot of Merits and Flaws that mirror these tropes too. You can create Garou who are banned or forced to change depending on the cycle of the moon or other things, that have the folklore attributes of middle digits being equal length, hairy palms, or eyebrows that meet together, or even have the Larry Talbot pentagram on their hand, or vulnerabilty to wolfsbane. It's just that we don't see these Flaws for actual NPCs. But what would happen, if there was a noticeable number of NPCs that had these? Or we might see a specific family of Garou consistently show these traits giving a reason why this element showed up in human cultural lore about werewolves.

                                        In general, if the Rage books had a section for non-tribal Garou, and included a few NPCs that matched some of the genre/trope expectations of pop culture, I think it would have strengthened the game. And people would be more likely to see the game as being part of the mainstream werewolf genre instead of something apart from it.

                                        This is what I try to do in my chronicles, and I have had success with it. My players seem to like it. It doesn't fundamentally change the setting, but it reminds the players that this is a game about werewolves, and not some fantasy species.
