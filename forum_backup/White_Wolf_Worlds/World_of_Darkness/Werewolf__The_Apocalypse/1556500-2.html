    Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
    Diaspora of the Weeping Moon could have picked up on lore from an older Cretan society and fused their own into it to make up something new. I think they are pretty compatible with the mix of influences.
    While I am aware of the Enlightened Society of the Weeping Moon, I don't have any Wild West sourcebooks and don't know much about them or how they were used in game as antagonists. However, I agree that groups like this would be a good place to name drop some references to the Cretan Mysteries and minotaur.

    In the back story of Samuel Haight, we're told that he learned Thaumaturgy and lore about "powers even a mere Kinfolk could learn" from a Tremere warlock's library (whom he had destroyed). So references of this could be known to the Tremere, or at least certain secret societies or individual chantries as the ST wishes.

    Haight then travelled around the world learning lots of different occult secrets before learning the Rite of Sacred Rebirth from a cult in India. That perhaps might be a bit far to learn anything that originated in Crete, but it's not impossible. Or it might be an indication that perhaps the Minotaur spirit was more active around the world than was suspected. Of course, we don't have a direct connection between Minotaur and that Rite. That rite came first, and the Skin Dancers contact with Minotaur came later. Yet personally I would lean towards some real connection between the Rite of Sacred Rebirth and Minotaur.

    References to Minotaur or spirit entities that might be Minotaur would be a good way to build up suspense. Moloch isn't described as a bull in the Bible, but it's a popular image to depict Moloch as a bull headed idol. And there are lots of other references to sacred bulls in world mythology that either refer to the Minotaur spirit, or which this spirit might masquerade as.

                        Another thing to do is lean heavily into the idea that this is a kinfolk secret society. There's lots of reason why kinfolk (and not just those of Garou) may resent or be envious of their Changing Breed. They are immune to the Delirium, have knowledge of their society and customs, and are capable of possessing some (limited) supernatural powers of their own. And since they do reproduce their Changing Breeds, there's not much to prevent a corrupt colony of kinfolk to sacrifice their own children in order to harvest skins to be used later. That's obviously twisted, gruesome and horrific, but that is what would make it an interesting antagonist in a chronicle. The PCs find a "lost" community of isolated kinfolk, and they initially think it's a great find - until they realize this village are Skin Dancer kinfolk who have been harvesting pelts for a long time, and their isolation was on purpose, and they see the PCs as a threat for exposing them. Maybe it's a community founded by renegades of multiple Breeds.

                        Kinfolk can learn Gifts. Even though spirits are unlikely to teach a lot of Gifts to kinfolk, once one is taught, that kinfolk does have the option of teaching others that same Gift just like Garou can. That means a kinfolk community of Skin Dancers can pass on these Gifts to each other in perpetuity, slowly building knowledge of new Gifts throughout the centuries whenever one of them somehow picks it up. Of course, the entire kinfolk community does not need to know these, just a select group of elders and "wise" people.

                        You can combine this with some of these kinfolk also knowing one or more Paths of Sorcery that they also pass on to each other. Then maybe even Numina like the Soulstealing in Kinfolk: Unsung Heroes.

                        It's one thing to fight a small pack of Skin Dancers. Another to fight an entire small community of kinfolk who've been following corrupt ways for years, decades, or perhaps even centuries, and have their own sick Litany and mythology.

                                                Looks like this topic may have exhausted itself. But before I abandon it completely, I want to revisit the two possible Skin Dancer caerns mentioned back in Outcasts. Since this information is (mostly) provided IC, there's also mention of doubts whether it is true. I consider these weasel words as just allowing STs to determine the actual factual status in their own chronicles so they don't have to care about players saying one way or another what is canon.

                                                The first is the Dutchman's Caern in Arizona located at the Superstition Mountains. For those that don't know, this is just east the Phoenix metro area. It's very close to Phoenix. It's a powerful rank 4 caern (Enigmas and Wisdom) that is a lost Nuwisha caern with the totem of "Trickster." An image of Sam Haight himself supposedly revealed itself and led them to the caern. If you want to include this in your chronicle, this is a fairly powerful caern and should be a stronghold of the Skin Dancers, helped by ancient magics hiding the place. It also opens up the possibility that Nuwisha may or may not also be involved. If so, I doubt very much this is intended to be a stronghold of the Wyrm aligned Skin Dancers, and may be a plot of Trickster to align them to Gaia. If the ST wants to keep them more villainous, I'd probably ditch the Nuwisha angle and possibly change the totem to something else.

                                                The second is the Cranberry Caern in the pinewoods of New Jersey. This is a weak rank 1 caern (Stamina) with totem of Magpie. The hook here is that this is the caern that Ronin may be gathering at in order to ally with the Skin Dancers or possibly even join them. The sourcebook states that "all four factions" of the post-Haight Skin Dancers are involved here. However, a Rank 1 caern is just not going to have many packs or Garou present. I think the canon guidelines is up to 10, so this may just mean 1 or 2 packs with their members divided on what to do. While it says the Skin Dancers don't let the Garou know they are Skin Dancers, the only reason I can think of why Skin Dancers would tolerate or even encourage Ronin to attend their caerns would be to try to recruit them into the tribe.

                                                So they're fairly basic, but I think they do a good job of presenting options for the ST. In both cases, it would be very easy to move the locations of the caerns or adjust other details about them to better fit whatever the ST wants. As a sourcebook, Outcasts put its weight behind the idea of Skin Dancers as a playable (non-Wyrm aligned) faction like Ronin. Its caern descriptions fit that. If you want them to be more as antagonists and villains though, you wouldn't need to work too hard to depict them differently.
