    Beckett, I think you're skipping a few of the books in your assessment. The IIRC the Revised players guide has a whole section on dealing with how Garou raised among modern religions deal with the world of the Garou. There are lots of examples in the books of Garou that have fused non-Garou religions with Garou culture. It's not something the books spend a lot of time on, because it isn't the focus of the game, but it isn't something ignored.

    The problem is that, as Cogliostro pointed out, the Garou can demonstrate the truth of much of their belief system. It's not necessarily taking the WtA setting as "the one true way," but that the Garou culture and religion is based on things they don't have to take on faith. Think the Tenets of the Litany are just something some Garou made up? Well, direct servants of Gaia and Luna can be called on to confirm that these laws are things the Garou will be judged on. If you violate them, rites to acknowledge Renown and thus gain Rank (which again, have confirmation in observable effect) don't work, but rites to punish law-breakers do work.

    It isn't that a Garou can't have different philosophic ideas about things, but ultimately they're going to have to deal with facts other Garou can present to them, while most of the reasons to doubt the Garou religion are not things that can be easily demonstrated as fact.

                I'm pretty sure the Black Fury books address this when talking about one of their Camps which operates as a group of Catholic nuns who try to influence the faithful to be more Gaian in their philosophy even if they never divert from Christian dogma.

                A reasonable person, even by Garon standards could just as easily use their speaking with angels <spirits> as proof of their Faiths and debunk every Tribe Lore that the setting assumes is true just as easily as the Garon teaching their history and beliefs.
                The problems with this assertion are:

                There's no real world religion for someone to be raised under that actually teaches a metaphysical system close enough to WtA cosmology for this to work. "Spirits are angels and demons," doesn't really make sense unless you completely abandon how religions with angels and demons define them. If Gaian spirits are "angels" and Wyrm spirits are "demons," what does that make Weaver and Wyld spirits? How does a Garou being able to pick up any object and awaken the spirit reflection of it jive with the idea of angels? In what faith does almost every thing sitting around have its own personal angel inside of it that can be called forth? How does it deal with a spirit getting summoned up that says, "Oh, yeah, I'm an angel, but I wasn't actually created by your idea of a supreme deity, I exist because of the collective belief that I should." which is something that can be done? Heck why, in general, would the angels consistently get annoyed that you keep calling them that, and insist that they're not angels?

                What's their proof that debunks the mainstream core of the Gaian spirituality/religion? At best they can just change names.

                The fact that they turn into a werewolf and kick demon ass that have escaped hell, wielding heavenly holy relic swords means they don't have to take it on faith, either.
                The idea that Fetishes would make sense to someone as "holy relic swords" doesn't really pan out. Holy relics in Christianity aren't powered by an angel that lives in the sword that needs to be cajoled or paid for service with spiritual energy gathered by meditating on the nature of the supreme deity. There are real world faiths that have a closer idea of holy relics to Fetishes, but they don't really have the same "kicking demon butt in the name of the supreme deity," angle.

                But, if you look at the same exact setup from a general WoD game, maybe a crossover for example, it's entirely possible the Garon are wrong.
                The Garou are canonically wrong about all sorts of things. However, the reality of their spirituality isn't generally one of them because it is essentially both hardwired into them, and by necessity factually true. The Garou might be off one what exactly Gaia and Luna are, and their role in the greater scheme of things, but Gaia and Luna have to be real for WtA to work; even in a crossover general WoD game. The animist spirits of WtA have to be real, as well as the Garou's ancient pacts with them. Etc.

                Another example, from Vampire, the Canon setting involves Cain<e> (and Abel), so out of game, we know for a fact that Caine, God, angels, etc. . . exist.
                Except we don't. The canon setting says that European vampires (and many vampires that have fallen under the control of them) believe this. OOC we know that this is not a set in stone fact. We know there has to be a first vampire for the kindred. But as you note, there are lots of OOC canon contradictory myths about how that all went down. Nothing necessitates that an Abrahamic supreme deity (and all the other trappings such as Abrahamic angels) exists in VtM, it is just a common belief that send being is responsible for vampires. There's a big difference between the canon mythical vampire creation story, and the canon on its truth. The canon on its truth is only that some first vampire that created all the others a very long time ago had to happen. Meanwhile, with WtA, Gaia is both in-character and out-of-character a canonical truth.

                                This is an interesting thread, to be sure. I think it may have strayed from the OP's original question, which I think was "What are the Garou's attitude towards holidays?" to the question of "What are the Garou's attitude towards religion in general?"

                                For the former question, I'm surprised that no one has yet pointed out the Seasonal Rites section on pp. 43-49 in the 2nd Ed Werewolf Storyteller's Handbook. The Garou have adapted a number of human holidays to use in their own worship of Gaia. I especially liked the section on the Bone Gnawers celebrating Superbowl Sunday.

                                As for the latter question, that's probably alot more complicated. I do remember reading about a Black Fury NPC who basically combined her Christian upbringing with her Gaia worship after her First Change. I also seem to recall that Garret Faithful, the leader of the Children of Gaia in the Amazon War also did something similar, as he likes to think of himself and his followers as following the ideals of Arthurian knights. So the attitude of the Garou towards human religions is...complex, to say the least. But one thing one has to remember is that every Garou can feel the pain of Gaian his heart. The Garou know that Gaia is real because of this. Such a thing is definitely going to color an individual Garou's attitude toward human religions in one way or another.
