                    Originally posted by Aya Tari View Post
                    And to realize that you cannot give up because no one else is willing to fight. The Garou are tragic heroes because, unlike mortals, they cannot escape into the comfort of insanity when they are forced to witness unspeakable horrors. And Garou will witness unspeakable horrors because they will follow Bane and Fomori to the source, whether it is a Fortune 500 company, a child brothel, a death camp, a Fomori family, a lair of a serial killer, etc. And, on occasion, Garou will have to choose between two bad choices because no one else is willing to make the choice.

                    ...that's not quite...I mean you could look at it like...well...ok, I actually have a small problem with this line of thinking.

                    As noble and well intentioned as it is, there is a serious danger to seeing the werewolf condition in Apocalypse as that. Because 1) werewolves aren't the only people who are willing to take action about situations like that, human beings fight all the time against the Wyrm without even realizing it and despite being woefully outmatched, and the idea that the Garu are the only ones willing stems more from Garu tradition/propaganda and the second point. 2) The Garu know that they're some of the most potent killing machines on the planet and therefore they adopt ideas like this to justify their actions, and that has led to a majority of the atrocities the Garu nation's commuted across history.

                    Another way to look at this is the super hero fallacy: Who does more good? A superhero, or a social worker? Sure, one can leap over building in a single bound and fight cosmic evil without being driven insane or being, what scientists officially call, "super killed." But a superhero's purpose in comics and literature is to battle and stop threats that are beyond the human ken, and inspire people to do good in their own way within the context of their own lives.

                    I know that analogy isn't perfect for the Garu, but this notion that only the Garu can save the world crops up again and again. And I think this is on purpose to let the audience question whether this is true, or just more of that cultural inheritance that so defines the existence of most Garu.

                                Personal Horror it should be noted was the Vampire The Masquerade tagline. Werewolf the Apocalypse was always tagged Savage Horror. With that said the personal horror of Werewolf is easy enough to discover. The first case lies in Rage. To demonstrate I shall paraphrase and reassemble from a few sources.

                                First you have to understand what Rage is...

                                Imagine the very angriest you have EVER been. In your life. That mindless, blinding fury that possesses a life of its own and forgoes all reason, that comes with screaming and tears and violence and a need to break something or make someone bleed. Congratulations you've just recalled something that is a tenth of Rage 1. That feeling that stirs beneath the surface of a Garou psyche. This is a weapon and a Curse as is covered by using Rage and the effects of the Curse as given in the rules. But let's expand it. Imagine family and friends, imagine the arguments and fallings out you have with them, imagine what happens when those same arguments can stir Rage, the same Rage that turns a Garou into a mindless nine foot tall killing machine in the throws of frenzy... What happens when you return to sense drenched in mothers blood? Or maybe you keep them safe, because there are other monsters out there who would harm them, use them, corrupt them? How free could they be? What rights do they have if they are part of Garou society? Or do you leave them forever if they are not? Can you afford to do so though? Gaia needs warriors and th Garou need support from those who can sits in the human societies of the world.
                                Free they are an Achilles heel or utterly distanced. Guarded they become pet like. Useful they are servitors and fully equal they'll die on someone's claws.

                                That's family and Kinfolk. Rage can lead to much of he intertribal strife too.

                                Then there's the Litany. You suffer renown loss for breaking it. It's the Nations law to be upheld. How many bad things must you do to protect it?
                                Good cops who discover the wrong thing at the wrong time?
                                Honest free media who are going to print the wrong story?
                                Packmate mates with another Garou?
                                The list goes on and the Litany is full of laws that can make you face some very hard choices.

                                Werewolf is a game of the line in the sand. It's a game of moments, desperate ones. It's a game where existence is on the line in a very real and visceral way. It's a game of fight or die, become wise or lose. It's a war of fang and claw and spirit and metaphor. It's the only battle left to fight and each hour could be the last hour of the last day and it could be your deeds that means it is not. And those deeds may be terrible and yet they prevent far worse. Do the ends justify the means? Or do they just feed the foe you face?

                                In the end only one question remains to the Garou...
                                When will you Rage?

                                    Because it is literally impossible to destroy the Wyrm because you would be destroying the very concept of destruction. The Garou probably only kill a fifth of the Fomori killed by the Fera. Anyway, the Fera probably only kill three million Fomori per year, so the global population of Fomori continues to slowly grow despite the actions of the Fera.

                                    The only way to reduce the production of Fomori is to improve the human condition and the environment, but that is the paradox with current human populations. If you improve the human condition, you strengthen the Wyrm by destroying the environment. If you improve the environment, you strengthen the Wyrm by worsening the human condition. Without a stabilization in human populations, technology cannot catch up, so that is not a solution either. In effect, the Fera (and the world) are playing a game of 'heads I win, tails you lose' with the Wyrm, the the Wyrm being the con man.

"The Garu are the only ones who can stop the Wyrm, but the Wrym cannot be stopped", is one of the enormous fallacies of the game setting and is one of the big morals that Apocalypse is trying to teach. It's that in order to fight for humanity and the world you cannot have contempt for humanity and the world. And the Garu have loads of contempt, so much so that their most noble belief is built upon the notion that the humans cannot resist the Wyrm.

                                            ...rather than the truth, which is that the Garu made the Wyrm the greatest ally of humanity. The Wyrm is kind to those who serve it, it empowers them and allow them to fight creatures that are so impossibly more powerful than they are that their genetic memory screams in terror just at the sight of them. The fact that the greatest force of the Wyrm within the World of Darkness is a human run corporation is meant to showcase that it's not the Garu who will save the world. It's humanity. Humanity must learn to either co-exist on the planet in harmony with the environment, or it will destroy the planet and itself in time. Those are the only outcomes, because like you already stated, the Wyrm has stacked the deck against the other forces of the planet by playing upon their own contempt for humanity to the point where either humanity must free itself from the Wyrm, or the Wyrm WILL win.
