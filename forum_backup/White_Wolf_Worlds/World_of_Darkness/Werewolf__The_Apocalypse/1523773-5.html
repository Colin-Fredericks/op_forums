            A Red Talon tribal template is pretty much guaranteed in a lupus and wilderness focused sourcebook, but it should have been a non-obvious auspice. Showcasing what a Red Talon Ragabash or Theurge would have shown the diversity of the tribe more. But I wonder how much the creators really conceived of the Red Talons as a tribe that could be played by PCs in a mixed pack as opposed to one of the designated bad guys of the setting. Multiple things about the template seem like a bad stereotype. Not only does this character come off bad, even the humans mentioned in it are stereotypes. Very few hunters would ever attempt to hunt while drunk because of firearms safety issues. No one wants to risk killing a friend. And hunting wolves (while drunk) makes even less sense unless we make substantial changes from the real world.

            The character's simple mindset is too simple. As written this is a character as a bully, wanting to establish some kind of lupus supremacist ideology in a group where they are a small minority. Some acknowledgement of the obvious flaws of such an approach, and being in favor of a more intelligent approach could have transformed this into a stronger character that would be more fun to play.

            Most of the traits are broadly acceptable, but not increasing Willpower to equal Rage is a huge mistake. It puts the character in position of failing lots of Rage rolls and succumbing to the Thrall of the Wyrm. That would not be fun for any player. But it is something overlooked by brand new players because they are still mastering the rules set. A template should already be set up in a way so the PC can succeed. As is, this character is going to fail and blow up in the player's face. Bad design choice.

            Like all the other templates, the basic concept though generic is OK, but the details are weak. I think it's obvious that at this point in time, White Wolf really didn't know what to do with the lupus breed and wrote about them in a very perfunctory way.

            Interestingly, I think the example "Lupus of Note" written earlier in the sourcebook do a much better job of showing some diversity in the characters. If they had considered other such characters, but what they would have been like in their youth, that might have created more interesting templates.

                                        One thing Werewolf lacked was the many canonical examples of characters in regional sourcebooks that Vampire had. Chicago By Night not only gave us characters we expected from each clan, but characters that defied expectations. Tremere had Nicolai and DuSable, but also Garwood Marshall. Ventrue had Lodin and Ballard, but also Bobby Weatherbottom and Lorraine. Toreador had the expected artistes and poseurs, but also Sir Henry Johnson who was an English naval captain. And so on. The introductory sourcebook CbN 1e had over 70 characters for 7 clans, meaning on average there were around 10 NPCs per clan. In contrast, Rage Across New York had around 26 for 13 tribes, meaning 2 characters per tribe. So even a small clan like Tremere had four examples in CbN 1e, while the Red Talons had one example only in Rage Across New York.

                                        The lack of seeing many different interpretations of a tribe early on easily lent itself to tribes seeing much less diverse than they could be. I think the "problem" tribes who have seen less like a problem if we saw more examples of them, and how they could fit into the game.

                                        I imagine WtA writers likely assumed any Red Talon PCs would come from the "Anti-Extinction Faction" which the Players Guide tells us is the main camp of the tribe. But we're not given examples of it so it's easy to forget. And I think the writers themselves would forget that as well and this camp would be reduced in stature.

                                                10-26-2023, 05:27 PM
                                                Now on to Rite of Passage. Released in 1992, this was the first Werewolf sourcebook for 1st ed, with only the Storytellers' Screen coming out between it and the core book. This was written by Sam Chupp, William Hale, and Rob Hatch. The template art is by Josh Timbrook.
                                                The book is about 90% introductory chronicle and about 10% sourcebook. These five characters were created with the chronicle in mind and apparently used to play test it. Unlike the premade templates in Ways of the Wolf or the tribe books, these are named characters with more fully fleshed out personality and backstory. The word count for each seems to be a lot larger than that of Ways of the Wolf or Black Furies. And unlike Ways of the Wolf, they've had their freebie points spent. Also, in contrast to every other book that came after, these go from Ahroun to Ragabash rather than vice versa.

                                                Snarls-At-Fleas / Lone Wolf (Lupus Ahroun Get of Fenris)

                                                I'll note to start that this character appears to be male, but nothing in the write up says anything definitive about gender, and because the image is in Crinos form (which tends to down play sex characteristics), this could just as easily be interpreted as a female character.

                                                Their upbringing is a fairly typical Lupus affair. A pleasant life as a wolf interrupted by humans. But the writing here is top notch, with a lot of emotional detail that I really enjoy. Piece by piece, the pack is forced back by encroaching humans, and one by one members of the pack fall. And bit by bit, the Rage grows, until finally they start to Change. The description of the character growing from a wolf's mindset into something more is really good, and something I think anyone wanting to play a Lupus character would benefit from reading. Learning how to think and plan and lure human hunters into traps and ambushes eventually leads to erupting into the full Change and Crinos form. But once the hunters were dead, the surviving pack members didn't want them anymore. They were as much a monster to the pack as they were do the humans, and wandered alone until being found by other Garou and becoming a Get of Fenris. But no matter how much they loose themselves in the fury of battle, they still long for a return to the simple life among the pines with the pack of their youth.

                                                This is what I feel the Red Talon in Ways of the Wolf should have been. This sad and tragic longing for a lost innocence that one can never return to, and a clear motivation to protect and defend that pack that played into the First Change. I actually can't help but wonder if the presence of this character here prevented them having such a thing in Ways of the Wolf. It would be sadly ironic if that were so.

                                                The image is of a Garou in Crinos form. There appear to be some ritual scars on the right shoulder, and they have a klaive. It's a nice image. Nothing about it really stands out, but it's a very solid depiction of a werewolf in Crinos.

                                                Lone Wolf is the concept listed on the character sheet. I tend to dislike this term for non-Ronin Garou. In nature, a lone wolf tends to be a soon-to-be-dead wolf. But in the context of this character, forever isolated from the pack they wish to return to, and because of that perhaps a little stand offish to these new pack mates (non of whom are Lupus if you use the pre-generated characters here), I think it fits. And I confess, I'm head-cannoning the last part, but I feel it makes sense given what's written, and would give this character a good personal arc through the chronicle.

                                                With Attributes, Physical is primary, with an extra dot from freebies, for a 4/3/4 spread. That seems perfectly fine. This character is obviously meant to be the muscle for this pack. Socials are tertiary, with 3/1/2, which makes sense. This feels like a straight-forward type who can be a strong personality when they want to. Mental is secondary, with 3/2/3. As we saw in the write up, they're fairly clever when they want to be. And I appreciate having a character of this particular mix of Breed, Auspice, and Tribe who is presented as not being some sort of thick headed lout.

                                                For Abilities, Talents are primary, again with an extra dot from freebies. Brawl is at 4, with 3 each in Intimidation and Primal Urge, and 2 each in Athletics and Dodge.
                                                Skills are secondary, with 3 each in Animal Ken and Survival, a 2 in Stealth, and 1 in Leadership.
                                                Knowledge is tertiary, with 2 each in Investigation and Rituals, and 1 in Occult.

                                                Backgrounds, of which Get get four in 1st ed, are Contacts 2, Fetish 1 (Klaive), and Pack Totem 1.
                                                It's nice to see some effort give to Pack Totem. However, I'm kind of at a loss as to who these Contacts might be. Other Get of Fenris? In 1st ed, Get couldn't have Allies; in 2nd ed, this was changed to Contacts. (They couldn't have Past Life either, which was just bizarre.)
                                                As for the Klaive, the first edition core book does not appear to have any descriptions for Klaives. They're aren't among the example Fetishes, and the weapons chart just says that knives include Klaives. All you really get is the word's definition in the lexicon, and that's about it. However, second edition made these a level 4 Fetish, and even the step down from that, the Fang Dagger, is level 3. However, it seems like two freebie points haven't been spent. I'm guessing maybe they were meant to be spent on the Fetish background?

                                                Rage has been raised from 5 to 7. Gnosis is 5. Willpower has been boosted from 3 to 7.

                                                Stat wise, this is mostly pretty solid. However, using 2nd ed rules, I'd probably change the backgrounds to Fetish 3 (Fang Dagger), and Pack Totem 2 (because I feel like this is the sort of character who wants a place to belong, even if they're going to be tsundere about it). I would probably drop Rage to 5, that way I'd still have four freebies, so as to buy a point in Alertness and two dots in Pure Breed.

                                                Stat quibbles aside, I really like this character a lot. It's a great back story with a lot of emotional depth and some great potential for role play and growth as part of a new pack. An excellent start.

                                                            Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
                                                            Rite of Passage, in the (ridiculously brief) section about each Tribe's Rite of Passage, it says the Get cull those they feel aren't worthy, either killing them out right or banishing them to be Ronin for the rest of their lives.
                                                            I try to incorporate a lot of overlooked or forgotten 1e and early 2e lore in my version of the chronicle, but I agree that is dumb for the reasons you mentioned. I ended up incorporating a variant of it though - the Get of Fenris include a lot of real danger in their Rite of Passage so that while it is rare, it is not unheard of for a cub to die during their rite of passage. It's not intentional, and it is limited to those NPCs who have very low Attributes (actual 1s in their Physical or Mental traits). In other words, not characters built using the standard character creations rules but reflecting the left hand side of the bell curve. They want it to be a real test to see if the cubs are worthy and ready.

                                                            As we've discussed earlier, these are extremely early products and their tribal descriptions are still in the form of wanting to easily distinguish one tribe from another. So they are using very broad stereotypes that sometimes fall into self-parody. So adjust accordingly if you are using these sourcebooks today.
