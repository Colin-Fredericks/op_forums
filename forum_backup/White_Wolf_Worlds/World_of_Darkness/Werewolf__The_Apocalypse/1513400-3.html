                            Something a player of mine did years before Touchstones were introduced in the CofD that more than adequately addresses the concern of making an animal relevant to the kinds of games the WoD does:

                            As a mortal, his family were dog-breeders and bred [I forget which, but one of the larger working breeds like German shepherds]. While at university he got into political activism and caught the eyes of a Brujah, and was a ghoul during the rest of his education. Completely unrelated to that, the Sabbat rolled into his home town, pulled a mass shovel-head event, and his family was wiped out. He rushed home, and managed to find one surviving pregnant dog. His sire Embraced him as he wanted to be more able to protect people from the Sabbat, and in the meantime kept the dog, and realizing he couldn't be a normal breeder, at least made sure to keep a few puppies so over the decades he could constantly have a living connection to his dead family and the animals they loved so much. Being larger working dogs, they were capable of being part of adventures because they could scent, guard, or even help fight (he didn't like to ghoul them, but he might ghoul one of a generation for that purpose).

                            So, yeah, it's not really hard to tie animal companions into adventure and narrative; even with direct connections and stakes regarding the supernatural.

                            This is even easier with Werewolf... because they can be family with wolves, not just love them as pets. Guarding the fragile wolf population is big deal to them. Wolves are organized social predators that fight to protect their pack and territory. Having a wolf pack Touchstone that helps protect the land around a rural Caern is... basic stuff. We haven't seen Gifts yet, but WtA traditionally has made it so werewolves have access to powers that let them communicate more deeply with other animals than humans and wolves. Why shouldn't a Hart Warden, or Ghost Council member apparently, have both a Touchstone out of a local wolf pack, and a local herd that they help keep healthy as part of keeping the local ecosystem healthy?

                            The new Get of Fenris can come in and try to steal wolf cubs to breed wolf-born Garou for their war, or poach your herd. Human hunters can be threats to both. A dam built hundreds of miles away could cause them to shift into a different Garou-pack's territory. Etc. etc. There's plenty of directly relevant to the game dramatic conflicts to have with multiple animal Touchstones.
