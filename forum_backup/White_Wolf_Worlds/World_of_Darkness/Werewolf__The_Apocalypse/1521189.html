    (W20) Mini Splat: Witches and Witchers

    08-22-2023, 11:47 AM
    What is this? An alternative take on shamans and wise folk who use their relationship with the spirit world to harness power.

    Why is this? It started as a quick way to create NPCs outside the Fera with strong ties to the spirit world, but within the Revised/W20 rule-set. I wanted something for shamans that sets them apart from Sorcerers and Mages, and closer to the framework of Spirits as portrayed in WtA. Something that would still work for a paleolithic shaman, without the formalized trappings of Mage and Sorcerer.

    Who are they? Mortals who have been touched by spirits in some way. Maybe a Spirit took a fancy on them, or they have been introduced by a Mentor, or there is an actual Spirit in their ancestral line. They could be Kinfolk, or distant relation remnants of the lost breeds. Or they could have simply fallen into the role by circumstance.

    Why witches? Because their connection to the Spirit world and how the mechanics play out are very close to folklore around wise folk. Plus the term is not as occupied by canon material as ‘shaman’ is.

    What are they (not)? They are not Sorcerers, Psychics, Shapechangers or Mages. They do not possess any inherent powers, instead they rely entirely on the powers granted by Spirits. They are Spirit Mediums, perhaps somewhat similar to Enchanted from CtD.

    But why a PC splat? I want to be able to offer it as an option for a potential new player. Also, why not?



    Key Traits

    Spiritual Awareness
    All witches possess a degree of Spiritual Awareness. This is covered by a Perception + Awareness roll against the local Gauntlet strength. A single success is enough to sense the presence of a Spirit, while additional successes allow to see, hear, and converse with it.

    Numina
    A Numen (pl. Numina) in this case is a Spirit Familiar, not to be confused with the inherent powers of psychics and sorcerers. Maybe the overlapping terminology hints at the origins of the latter. For witches, this means a close Spirit ally they know and have worked with for a long time. They have at least a cordial relationship, but more often the social bond is closer to a friendship, as far as the strange mindset of Spirits allows. Witches can have several Numina.

    Totem
    Most witches will have a personal Totem. They are very likely the ones who introduced the character to the Spirit world in the first place. Some witches will petition a Totem on their own instead, but getting their interest is not an easy task in either case. This should be something covered by the character’s background.

    To use Pack Totems from Werewolf, divide their cost by two (rounded up) and ignore their Pack benefits. Otherwise you can follow the rules given in W20 for personal Totems. For the replacement of mechanics that are only applicable to Fera, see below.

    Chiminage
    Chiminage is a trackable stat for witches. It represents the goodwill of each Numen towards the character, and is used in exchange for providing their Charms and powering Gifts. The type of Chiminage a Numen prefers corresponds with a type of Renown.

    Charms
    Charms are the native powers of Spirits. A Numen can use these on behalf of the witch in exchange for Chiminage.

    Gifts
    Witches can learn Gifts from Spirits just like Fera, although this is limited to Gifts that do not require the anatomy of a Shapechanger. Because they do not have Rage or Gnosis, they rely on their Numina to provide those for them should the Gift require it. In exchange for Chiminage, of course.

    Rites
    A witch can learn any Rite that does not require specific Fera anatomy. Finding a teacher is probably the hardest part, although some Spirits teach Rites and a few are recorded in folk traditions.

    General Replacements
    Use the following replacements when rules call for them:
    Rage Point, Gnosis Point Chiminage
    Permanent Rage, Permanent Gnosis Sum of the two highest Numen Ranks.
    Renown, Rank* Highest Numen Rank of a compatible Type
    *Renown and Rank replacements only apply when they are called for in general mechanics, never for social interactions with Fera.


    Character Creation

    Mostly standard: Attributes (6/4/3), Abilities (11/7/4), Backgrounds 5, Willpower 5. You have 21 Freebie points.

    Characters additionally get 5 points of Numen Ranks (none higher than 3 at this point).

    To create a Numen, choose what kind of Spirit they are and the Type that matches it. Type is a combination of Triatic aspect and Renown, for example “Weaver / Wisdom” or “Wyrm / Cunning”. You can use the Renown block on a Werewolf character sheet to write those in, together with their Rank and starting Chiminage (Rank x2). A Numen Rank caps at 5.

    For each point of Numen Rank, choose a Charm the Numen has (so a total of 5 at this point). You can note those in the Gifts blocks. Also, choose three Level 1 Gifts that are taught by Spirits who fit the Types of your Numina.

    Freebie Points
    Trait Cost
    Attribute 5 per dot
    Ability 2 per dot
    Numen Rank 7 per dot
    Additional Gift 7
    Background 1 per dot
    Willpower 1 per dot
    Experience
    Trait Cost
    Attribute CR x4
    Ability CR x2
    New Ability 3
    Numen Rank CR x7
    New Numen 7
    Background CR x3
    Willpower CR
    Gift Level x5
    New Rite Level

    Using Numina and Chiminage
    To access the Charms of your Numina, you ask them nicely, and spend a point of Chiminage. You also do this if they need to jump in and provide Rage or Gnosis for your Gifts – each point of those translates to a point of Chiminage as well. If a Numen is in a bad mood or disposition towards the character, that price can go up. On the flip-side, if you encounter something that particularly entices action from a Numen, they could just as easily use a Charm of their own volition without asking for anything in return. Numina are not bound, but follow the character around because of mutual trust and quid pro quo. They are, like all free spirits, their own personality and should be treated as such.

    Regaining Chiminage Points
    Chiminage works just as it does for Fera, the only difference is you pay in advance and gain boons to call in later, represented by the Chiminage pools. Additionally, if the character performs an action that would usually earn them Renown, they can translate this into Chiminage for Numina of the appropriate type.


    Feedback, criticism, and ideas are very welcome. Also let me know if this would be something you would be interested in playing or not, or whether you would prefer a shaman character with RAW rules.

          I can at least give some of the reasons I would prefer it, which are of course personal:

          - I really do not like that existing Numina (the power kind) which deal with Spirits are a power stat of the character and use the same mechanics like throwing fireballs. The powers of a shaman should come from the Spirits, not the shaman.

          - Existing Backgrounds, Merits, and Numina that access a Spirit's power are extremely vague towards the extent these powers can have. Charms and Gifts are well defined.

          - Everything you need to run this is in the Core rules. It plugs into the system instead putting additional mechanics on top of it. I will be honest, trying to make a working shaman with the Kinfolk rules is a pain, and using Sorcerer rules can be awkward because it's not geared towards WtA.

                  Originally posted by Black Fox View Post

                  I think you can get everything you'd want by using the Sorcerer rules (and their various spin offs), but restricting what kind of Paths and Merits and Backgrounds work best.

                  Keep Paths restricted to appropriate flavor like Ephemera, Divination, and Herbalism. Have Merits like Special Friend, Spirit Guide and Totem. Backgrounds like Familiar and Mentor.

                  You can create very strong, targeted mortals with supernatural abilities with whatever flavor text you like by using Sorcerer. The issue is keeping things targeted as opposed to letting players buy whatever the hell they like because they have build points or XP and never mind if their concept and improvements makes sense with their concept.
                  That's how I did it in the past, but.. it's a process. You have to be willing to mix and match various books to get something that is workable.

                  Let me make an example:
                  I will go through a quick character creation process using Sorcerer 20 as a base. My concept for the shaman is an urban eco-terrorist who targets corporate destruction of nature, or ‘if Poison Ivy was a shaman’ so we know what we’re looking at. I will skip Attributes, Abilities, and other trivial stuff for brevity.

                  My targets for the build are:
                  - has a Totem and can talk to it
                  - can talk to and call on Spirits
                  - has a Spirit companion
                  - can use plants offensively
                  - can mix poisons
                  - maybe has a few Rites

                  I think that's a sensible list.

                  Let's start with the Totem. Fireweed seems a good choice:
                  Sorcerer 20 uses the Mage 20 Totem Background. To get a Totem that is workable within the WtA context (infrequent interactions, it can talk to me, maybe helps in a pinch) we want it at Rank 3. M20 charges double for Tote                  m, so we'll need to invest our 5 Background points and one freebie into it. We don't even get the listed benefits of the Totem, because M20 overrides it with fixed Ability bonuses.

                  This is a personal opinion, but I don't think a shaman player should need to dump their complete starting Background points into a trait that is pretty essential to the concept, and Fireweed isn't even one of the big guns. So I can offer using the W20 Totem Background, which cuts the cost in half. Or if I feel super generous, the Sorcerer 1e Totem Merit which just lets you pick any Totem, plus it can teach you Gifts. As a side note, Kinfolk 20 outright bans the Totem Background.

                  Next, interaction with Spirits:
                  Ephemera 2 to call on a type of spirit (Plant). If I want to call on a specific Spirit, Ephemera 3. Sorcerer 20 completely skips over the communication part of the Path, but we will assume it also includes being able to actually talk to Spirits.
                  It's worth noting that the Path got a lot harder to use since Sorcerer 1e. Calling a Plant Jaggling in S20 RAW would cost 1 WP and requires 5 successes of an Attribute + Ability roll against 6. In Sorcerer 1e the interaction is free and only needs a single success. I like Ephemera, I really do. But seeing a player spend WP and fail their roll just trying to reach out to a Spirit felt so bad I house-ruled it away. For a new player I would probably recommend the classic version and treat it as a general Numen, not Sorcery specifically.

                  Spirit companion:
                  The M20/S20 Familiar Background really isn’t usable in a WtA context, since Familiars in Mage deal with Quintessence and Paradox. We will substitute this with the Numen Background from Player’s Guide to the Garou. Our Character doesn’t have Gnosis to offer their companion, so we will change that to Chiminage (see what I did there?).

                  Fun with plants:
                  Nope. S20 doesn’t offer anything in this regard, and I included this just to point out that Sorcerer does not offer a good spread of shaman adjacent powers. To further complicate the issue, if I wanted to pick Animal Psychics I would have to jump through the hoops of combining Sorcery and Psychic Phenomena, which is heavily discouraged. There’s Spirit Chasing from Kinfolk 20, but the author halfway forgot the Path isn’t exclusive to animal Spirits. If I would allow Gifts to this effect, there is a whole package of things to do to make Gifts actually work for Sorcerers.
                  For completeness sake let’s just say I would import Green Path from V20.

                  Poisons:
                  Herbalism is an easy choice. Done.

                  Rites:
                  The default for a Sorcerer are Rituals. RAW we would get a few of them in chargen, and I would give the option to substitute them with Rites. This isn’t explicitly covered by the rules, but imo it should be for shaman characters.

                  ---------

                  So, after all that, can I create a working mortal shaman character for WtA with the current rules under S20? Yes, if I use a dozen other books and spend two hours cross-referencing my options.
                  I just think using the WtA framework for shamans makes so much more sense because the DNA for it is already burnt into the system.
