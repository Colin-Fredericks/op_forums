    Something discussed in another forum, but it deserves to be here as well; W5 seems to treat the garou as amnesiacs to the point where they barely remember ANYTHING about their past. But every other faction does.

    Thing is, there is giving STs freedom and then there is this. W5 isn't giving much to work with, only fragments that don't even work together. The Litany is bad, yet it still has the positive laws and ragabash and philodox exist yet don't seem to play a role in interpreting the Litany.

    Now, if the point of the game was that the information was lost, that would work. But the book doesn't as much treat the information as lost as much as irrelevant. The book doesn't offer any concrete lore to base your game setting on, instead it tells you what lore is WRONG and harmful.

                                Originally posted by Manfr View Post

                                We just got 2 supplements for H5! I'm pretty sure we will have supplements for W5 too
                                I don't think additional books along those lines are really what people think W5 needs right now.

                                There isn't exactly a whole lot of new in the three additional releases for H5:The Free Bonus Material is... just some extra examples of Touchstones, a new monster, and a few play aids, the Storyteller Screen Kit is obviously built around a play aid, the expanded rules are things already available in the V5 stuff, and then it's a bunch of storyhooks and on sample adventure. Lines Drawn in Blood is more of what people would think of as a proper supplement, but even then it's four adventure modules that are capable of being strung together for a larger ongoing game.

                                None of that is a bad thing. It's certainly good stuff to have for H5 groups, and W5 groups would benefit from such things for W5 too. But it's not a more comprehensive monster building guide, nor a better explanation of how to implement the Danger tracker in play. It's not something like HtR 1e's First Contact to more fully explore the line between Driven and Jobber, and how orgs that aren't as inherently hostile to Driven cells work in play; it's easy to understand why government Orgs don't like a bunch of vigilantes running around taking government maters into their own hands, but it's a lot harder to understand why the Arcanum wouldn't be fairly amenable to cooperating with Driven cells

                                W5 needs books that really expand on a bunch of stuff in the core book, not just offer a bunch of example NPCs and some starter adventures to play. Not that W5 shouldn't do those too, but as people have pointed out, there are a lot of topics that are near unusable because of how much W5 wants to leave things mysterious/open to multiple interpretations.

                                I don't think it's an inherently bad thing to try to make the WoD a bit more like the CofD in term of being a toolkit where you pick and choose which elements from the books you want to use. But a toolkit needs to be full of tools... or it's just an empty toolbox. Selling me a bit driver and a single bit just means I have a screwdriver for one type of screw. The fact that I could have dozens of bits for all the different types of screws out there does me nothing until I have more bits.

                                            Anyone think it's strange just how much the Wyrm is downplayed in W5? Having read the book now, while the opening makes a big deal about how it's Garou's purpose to fight the Wyrm and whatever, it seems like every time it's mentioned, it has to be followed along with some kind of note that's like "But is the Wyrm really that bad? It's just a force of nature, after all. Maybe it's the Garou who are wrong.". And while banes and such are still around, the Wyrm has been removed from a lot of enemy factions. Pentex is no longer directly associated, only incidentally working alongside it, and there's a big sidebar that says "oh you don't have to use Pentex there's other real-life evil companies" (which was true in the original game to be fair, but there it was less emphasized and with everything else it just seems to be more of an attempt to distance the game from its original antagonist). The BSDs are here, but they seem to just be a token mention and don't actually come up much. And it feels like everytime the idea of actually fighting the Wyrm comes up, it's always accompanied by the game shrieking that this is a fast-track to Hauglosk and you will be just as bad as the Cult of Fenris. Hell, the Red Talons now explicitly think the Wyrm is the victim so now it's okay to start using its tactics.

                                            But the game really wants you to know that the Cult of Fenris are always there and always terrible. Hell, the sample adventure at the end, despite having nothing to do with them, really tries to push you into shoehorning them into the plot and gleefully encourages you to steal their artifacts and caern.

                                            It's just kind of impressive just how blatantly this game doesn't actually want to be Werewolf: the Apocalypse at all, but just a reskinned V5 the way it railroads you into prioritizing petty inter-Garou conflicts instead of. You know. Saving the world.
