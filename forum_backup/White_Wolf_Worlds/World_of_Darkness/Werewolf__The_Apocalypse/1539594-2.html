                We see that the idea of this section is to describe example paths for how werewolves are inducted into Garou-society. And the "twins"-concept feels way too convoluted to be any kind of model for how this would usually go. Worse than that, it's complicated in some ways and quite bland in others. Like, the idea is that the twins are supposed to be a propaganda-weapon but then all there is to them is just "Look, it's Ahroun-twins who fight good.". No prophecy or some tale of past glories are getting invoked. It's just the evil baron-guy saying "I have twin-daughters who are both Ahroun and fight good.". That's lame.

                And it does bring up the lack of "re-imagination" again. This idea of the twins being a "propaganda-weapon" the Shadow Lords can use against the Silver Fangs: That imagines a type of setting with Tribes that are more similar to the legacy-editions and not this universalist "Anybody anywhere can join any Tribe."-idea from the W5 corebook. Even stuff like the section believing that it's important for the baron to falsely claim that they're his daughters. That hints at how heredity would be the main-factor to undergoing First Change. If it were just the chaotic "Anybody anywhere can be Kin who then later undergoes First Change.".

                So you get these sections where it does feel more like a continuation and the underlying principles are those from the legacy-editions. And it's like that until it suddenly is not as the worldbuilding swerves into this other direction. Of course, it's awfully convenient for the writers to rely on legacy-stuff to this degree without any responsibility to stay faithful to it or be invested in staying consistent. Calling the twins his daughters is somehow meaningful but W5 of course doesn't believe in heredity being a main-factor anymore and Kinfolk don't exist either. Anytime, there's something that breaks with established worldbuilding, they just can bring up the "re-imagination, not continuation"-thing again. Meanwhile, they're doing stuff like "Of course, there's still the same-old rivalry between Shadow Lords and Silver Fangs." or "How are Septs organized? Actually, it's exactly the same way as before, here's the copy-pasted list of all the Sept-positions...". Rather than "re-imagination, not continuation", W5 is more like this shoddy mixture that is neither invested in being its own thing nor in being a faithful continuation. It's a game of chance with whoever the writer is just picking and choosing on a whim.
