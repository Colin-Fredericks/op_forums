    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    In absolutely any RPG there's a huge difference between what a player can do and what the NPCs should look like, specially what should be the default among them. PCs are special by definition and have access to things based on game balance and game needs for special characters. NPCs are story elements, and standard NPCs are setting elements.
    ...

    So, while you can build such characters and they're perfectly valid, as a setting premise they're probably exceedingly rare, unique or currently non-existent.
    That is completely true. PCs are special because they are specifically designed to fulfill whatever need of the player (to roleplay an intriguing concept, to explore a metaplot element, to be a unique snowflake to make them feel special, to live out a power fantasy, etc.) and not something organic that develops like in real life. Ideally, a setting should be a living world that the ST tries to roleplay realistically based on the setting and genre conventions. PCs often aren't representative of the setting when taken as a large group.

    This is more easily seen in LARP than tabletop, because in tabletop games, STs run most of the game world and she can make sure the NPCs portray the setting as a whole. Even if there is something strange and unusual about a sept of local Garou (or vampires, or whatever), she can establish a rationale why this local group is different and have the NPCs farther away react appropriately. In LARP (and online games with many players), most of the characters are the PCs themselves with only a few actual NPCs. And while LARPs are fun, the end result is that most LARP groups of PCs often do not meet expectations of the setting as portrayed by the books.

    So in LARP/online games, what we usually see is every member of the Silver Fangs be decent, capable leaders who display modern or post-modern sensibilities. We'd have no idea that the tribe, collectively, is supposed to be a bunch of inbred derelicts leading the Garou Nation to disaster because of class prejudices and hidebound devotion to tradition. Shadow Lords might have a dangerous edge to them, but we don't see a bunch of underhanded conspirators willing to use lies, murder, and working with Wyrm minions to accomplish their goals. All the Bone Gnawers are competent, loyal Garou who truly honor the Litany (even if rough and dirty looking), not mostly a bunch of demoralized, resentful, screw ups or Rabble that just don't care. Or PCs that actually treat Metis as bad as they're supposed to be treated. That's because those tribal descriptions best fit for antagonists (NPCs). Players usually want to portray more heroic protagonists. (And STs often don't allow certain concepts if they feel it'd be disruptive to the game. They don't want to deal with some jackass ruining things simply because "it's my character concept!")

    Or why a quarter of the game is comprised of Ahrouns with the Huge Size merit. Or why most Kinfolk PCs are special in the sense of having Gnosis, Sorcerer Paths, or some other power. Or how almost everyone has high amounts of Pure Breed despite the setting saying that is dying out. Because individual players want the benefits of that. In a tabletop game, having one PC like that is OK. The character is unusual, but every NPC is usually normal so the setting overall meets expectations established by the corebook. But in LARP/online, many players have the same idea, and it strains credibility that there'd be eight Ahrouns who are all 7' tall and weigh 400 lbs of lean muscle in homid just hanging out at this specific sept. (And they're always Ahrouns. I have yet to see a Ragabash or Theurge who has the Huge Size Merit, because the power fantasy here is to play a huge, bad ass warrior. Not a Glasswalker Ragabash who plays basketball on the weekends and has to go to to the Tall & Fat store to buy his clothes so he can show up to work Monday as a forensics accountant.) So in these games, it just sticks out like a sore thumb. It's just not believable as a setting element. But hey, it's a game, so you roll your eyes and move on.

                                World of Darkness rules in general don’t do epic combat between powerful creatures very well. It’s because usually offensive scales way faster than defense. In movies or other stories when we think of two supernatural creature of great power fight, they fight for hours, each striking blows and inflicting wounds, but continuing to fight. We see this kind of fight described between Mithras and the werewolf pack or between Ravnos and his various opponents. The rules doesn’t accommodate that. Using WoD rules, one creature goes first and gets 6 attacks each one doing 15 levels of aggravated damage and then the victim only gets 8 dice of soak, so it dies on the first round of combat. The winner is the one who attacks from Obfuscate or drops out of the umbra first. Or to take it to the next level, attack during the daytime with the pack or have your ghouls take a bomb to the Cairn.

                                That isn’t as interesting, though, as the epic all night fight. So the fights are described in a way that the rules can’t emulate and almost always this will be in the elder vampire’s favor, since that kind of fight will usually favor the Garou pack over the elder vampire.
