                    Originally posted by Knightingale View Post

                    Some thoughts:
                    • So there are (universal) Native Gifts (don't know if I like the term...), Auspice Gifts and Tribe Gifts
                    • Me neither. It makes me wonder who their diversity consultants were and whether they got them to check things like this. "Native Gifts" just sounds like weird Amerindian fetishism. "Inherent Gifts" or just "Common Gifts" would do.
                    • The Touchstone-rules are mildly better than the H5-ones, although now I would say having one Touchstone that you truly focus on is better than having three.
                    Agreed.
                  • Once per story seems like a huge limitation. The only way I could see this work if the ST really keeps narrative arcs on the shorter end of the scale with campaigns.
                  If they were more effective, it wouldn't be so bad. As you say, it really depends how long stories are.

                  Doing The Fall of London for V5 would be one story, right? But some tables will finish that in three sessions or fewer, while others will stretch it out to 20 sessions or more.

                  My guess is that it's to prevent people spamming the same "Touchstone actions" (for lack of a better word) over and over. But is spamming really an issue? I don't think it would be, primarily because the WoD5 games seem to want us to engage with Touchstones, so we should be rewarding that.

                  I think, if I were to use these, I'd modify them so there are chapter benefits and much more powerful story benefits. You can use each of the chapter benefits once per session, and each of the story benefits once per story.

                  If the game needs more balance for "downtime actions" (to prevent everyone saying "I watch TV with Buddy again to reduce my Rage to one LOL" every session), you could introduce limits for that.

                  E.g., only one downtime action with Touchstones per chapter, or a limit on between session downtime actions so that any Touchstone interactions eat into actions that can be used for other things like learning Gifts, performing rites, etc.

                  It really doesn't seem OP to have people move a point of harano to hauglust (or vice versa) or heal one level of WP damage. Reducing Rage to one is potentially a much bigger deal, but as I suspect Rage is going to be fluctuating a lot in play, I don't think it will be, so that can probably be once per session too.
                • It seems like there's a Harano and a Hauglosk track...? Or it's one track where you're supposed to stay in the center but then that would mean you want Harano-points to avoid Hauglosk, for example... Hmm, wonder how that's gonna work exactly.
                  • Yeah, I read it as two tracks, but you may be right. It may be like Quintessence/Paradox, or Yin Chi/Yang Chi, which would indicate some forward-thinking design as far as future games go.

                  I also think they missed a trick. Touchstones should've been far broader: spirits, kinfolk, wolves, humans, sacred places. Taking those things as Touchstones would reinforce in-character behaviours, themes and setting.

                  Writer, publisher, performer

                                          Yeah, because Chronicle is a multitude of Stories. But this all gets even weirder once you start considering formats like Oneshots...

                                          My guess is that it's to prevent people spamming the same "Touchstone actions" (for lack of a better word) over and over. But is spamming really an issue? I don't think it would be, primarily because the WoD5 games seem to want us to engage with Touchstones, so we should be rewarding that.
                                          What I really don't get here is that both H5 and W5 have talked about this formula of "The more Touchstones you have the more connected to the world you are but it also involves a greater amount of risk.". But neither version has supported this type of risk/benefit-analysis with their mechanics. Spamming shouldn't even be an issue because that formula suggests the whole point is to have higher healing-rates when it comes to Willpower and having more control over the Harano/Hauglosk-tracks due to having three Touchstones, for example, compared to one. It's only in V5 that the formula at least somewhat works due to Touchstones being linked to Convictions.

                                          If the game needs more balance for "downtime actions" (to prevent everyone saying "I watch TV with Buddy again to reduce my Rage to one LOL" every session), you could introduce limits for that.
                                          E.g., only one downtime action with Touchstones per chapter, or a limit on between session downtime actions so that any Touchstone interactions eat into actions that can be used for other things like learning Gifts, performing rites, etc.
                                          At that point, you get into Blades In The Dark territory but there you have the Mission-Downtime-cycle in the game to support that. Although, the way it's phrased does sound like that, the positive effects are always described as "spending a scene or period of downtime with a Touchstone".

                                          And Justin Achilli did mention that his inspiration for the Caern-management-portion of the game was the Lair-system in Blades In The Dark. I guess, we'll see how exactly that will work in W5.

                                          Yeah, I read it as two tracks, but you may be right. It may be like Quintessence/Paradox, or Yin Chi/Yang Chi, which would indicate some forward-thinking design as far as future games go.
                                          I'm really not sure but the main-reason for that is that I really can't picture how this is supposed to work... At least, it's clear now why Hauglosk is "extreme urgency" since it has to fulfill the role of the extreme polar opposite of Harano. Even if it's two tracks... So, the player-character is supposed to be caught between "giving up" and "extreme urgency"...? What even is the "happy medium" supposed to be there when these are the extremes...? And how would that "happy medium" fit into a mid-Apocalypse-setting?
