                        All in all I think there is some form of divide between the 4th/20th Anniversary Editions and the 5th Edition(s). I see many old fans loving, reveling in and immersing themselves in the materials of 4th edition games, not only because they are more compendium like, but are of nostalgic value and have care to be so, focusing more on bringing as much options as they can, revising all of it, and give it a complete experience and possibility. So they acted like a gathering, polishing, some advances to better settle new possibilities based on what 3rd editions were doing. So you get all Clans, all Bloodlines, all Tribes, all Gifts, etc.. And we are still getting books for those editions, W20 is getting Apocalyptic Record, and seems like a confluence of all of W20's might.

                        So I see this way, the fifth editions are about a more tabula rasa approach, while fourth editions were tabula repleta (full plate let's say), giving an approachable game by new players, less heavy in rules and lore, using the best parts (according to the creators, of course, as it is) of the setting, system and metaplot and making a renewed, strong, focused and thematic game. This of course means that what doesn't mesh together with the focused vision they have will have to be taken some way or another. V5 took a lot out, to give more strength to what it remained and/or was added. I for one have the luck of liking pretty much all of V5's choices, even then I go back to V4 to all that I wish in regards of plot and systems that V5 deliberately doesn't cast light on, or that makes vague remarks of. One is a full-pack sandwich of everything the game meddled in the previous editions, like a parade, where Becket's Diary takes vanguard, being a big love letter to all of it. The other is a new generation, fresh and light, trying new things and melting what it came before and taking all it considered more poignant and like what is trying to achieve.

                        It might not be precisely this metaphor, but I do see as good thing, we have a full toolbox in 4th edition, and many new shiny tools in 5th edition. Some might look nasty, crude, annoying, nonsense, or useless to the users of 4th edition, but yeah, better to have the option I think. Otherwise the 4th editions would've ended up redundant or changing too much and not being able to maintain the All-Stars vibe it had. In the end, I just hope as many people as possible can enjoy the game, whatever it is, and take something they like from each new implement; and independently, just be merry with their own game and share what they cherish, and point out what they miss.

                        Strange... When coincidence seems too convenient, I prefer to call it fate.

                        -Blood Omen: Legacy of Kain d=
