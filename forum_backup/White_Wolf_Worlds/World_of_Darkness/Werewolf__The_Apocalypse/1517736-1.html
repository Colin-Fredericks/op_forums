                        Well. Returning mankind to the Stone Age isn't really an agreed upon course for the Garou. It's also not really viable, nor will it stop the Apocalypse at this point in time. Impergium was a solution for a while, but the moral, philosophical and human costs were too high. To shore up against the oncoming Apocalyptic tide that will hit the world the Garou have to:
                        • minimise infighting between themselves
                        • open communication and find ways to cooperate with the Fera
                        • find more allies among spirits willing to stick with them and honour the commitments to them
                        • fight off the avatars and monsters of the Wyrm that are direct threats to the planet
                        • sabotage and disable Wyrm operations that focus on making humanity its worst self
                        • support and communicate with humanity to reinforce them being their best self
                        Of course, fixing humanity may be a fool's errand, but it's the only real way to save the world long term. This is one of the reasons why I love Werewolf - the faults and errors of the Garou Nation are the faults and errors of Humanity, and the issues they need to resolve are mirrors to issues that plague us.                                                 The ​Permian–Triassic extinction event happened 251.9 million years ago is the Earth's most severe known extinction event, with the extinction of 57% of biological families, 83% of genera, 81% of marine species and 70% of terrestrial vertebrate species. (Pinpointing the exact causes of the Permian–Triassic extinction event is difficult, mostly because it occurred over 250 million years ago, and since then much of the evidence that would have pointed to the cause has been destroyed or is concealed deep within the Earth under many layers of rock. The sea floor is completely recycled over around 200 million years by the ongoing process of plate tectonics and seafloor spreading, leaving no useful indications beneath the ocean. But the best theory is the flood basalt eruptions that produced by the Siberian Traps increasing in atmospheric carbon dioxide with other emissions from the Siberian Traps)
                                                The Triassic–Jurassic extinction event happened 201.4 million years ago and profoundly affected life on land and in the oceans. About 23% of all families, 48% of all genera (20% of marine families and 55% of marine genera) and 70% to 75% of all species became extinc (The leading and best evidenced explanation for the TJME is massive volcanic eruptions leading to ozone shield deterioration leading ti increased ultraviolet radiation flux)
                                                The Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event in approximately 66 million years ago caused the extinction of about 17% of all families, 50% of all genera and overall 75% of all species became extinct (caused by the impact of the giant Chicxulub asteroid or multiple impacts; other crater-like topographic features have also been proposed as impact craters formed in connection with Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction. This suggests the possibility of near-simultaneous multiple impacts, perhaps from a fragmented asteroidal object. In addition to the 180 km Chicxulub crater, there is the 24 km Boltysh crater in Ukraine, the 20 km Silverpit crater in the North Sea, and the controversial and much larger 600 km Shiva crater. Any other craters that might have formed in the Tethys Ocean would since have been obscured by the northward tectonic drift of Africa and India.)
