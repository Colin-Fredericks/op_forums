    Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
    And I actually feel this would work for W5, since its supposed to be very low-key in the stakes department. Though, with there being no good way to detect if something is tainted, regular poison would also work. There was a dog food issue here last year where bunch of dogs died or were paralyzed due to potato mush going bad. That needs no WICs to be horror, especially if the company refuses to help.
    Yeah, in that sense removing Wyrm-sense as a way to detect Wyrm-taint was a mistake, I think. Because Wyrm-taint is a nice little short-hand for why a company is bad. Sure, you could maybe make the bad guy some Dupont chemical plant. But then to explain why what's going on is bad you'd have to get into the actual science of what's going on, like with PFAS, forever-chemicals, Teflon and all that jazz. With Wyrm-taint, you can just quickly explain "This company produces Wyrm-tainted products and of course that's bad. End of story." You can use Dupont as the inspiration but you don't have to get into all the details of that real-world-problem.

        Just putting my two cents in. In my mind, Pentex would not be evil in itself exactly, but a Wyrm weapon it can use on the material plane efficiently, I mean, through its subsidiaries you can get a sense of what it does in the world and how it attacks, for example, and forgive me if I'm just stating the obvious: a film, game or advertising company could be used to cause an effect that encourages violent attitudes like a brainwashing process, beyond information control; food and pharmaceutical companies that contain substances that make people more apathetic, weak and contaminate them with the Wyrm, possibly making them fomori later on; biofuel company that isn't make biofuel, but it's lie will give people a clear conscience to use their product; arms company that discreetly delivers weapons to mercenary troops, or violent groups, that will strengthen the wyrm influence and also make profit; construction companies that choose to build their buildings in areas that should be preserved, so on, use creativity, otherwise you can use the subsidiaries.

        Now you may be thinking that Wyrm could simply influence the owners or representatives of these companies without the need for Pentex. I agree that it could, but I think it's better for the story for you to face one big enemy that follows a pattern than several small ones that appear just because it's convenient at that moment and there are also the examples I mentioned above that could be used together if they were in the hands of one bigger company (basically a script resource), apart from the fact that this is partly the WOD's stance of not placing all the blame on the supernatural, in other words, Pentex has its mundane part that only thinks about making a profit, simple human greed, and its supernatural part that uses its resources to strengthen the wyrm and helps the first part to profit by table.

            Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
            I always enjoyed the Subsidiaries quite a bit. First, I liked the idea that most people had no idea what Pentex was. It's sort of like in the real world, few people on the street know YUM! Brands, but everyone knows the companies that part of YUM! Brands. Pentex was able to operate a bit in the shadows because it was always hiding behind its subsidiaries. Importantly, they also put a layer between the character's and the players, like gh0st-fox says. It's one thing to go on a shooting spree through the O'Tolley's. It's a completely different feel when your characters are shooting up a McDonalds. In light of real world problems and issues, the latter feels more in bad taste.

            I understand why Achilli wanted to remove them - the nature of parody and satire innately brings a level of humor into the game that he doesn't feel belongs in W5. Among the old WoD games, Werewolf was absolutely the one with the most humor involved, nearly all of it revolving around Pentex parodies and satire of real life companies. And to some degree, even if you try to play those sorts of parodies straight, they will always inject a tonal change into a game.

            But I don't think that was ever a bad thing though - the humor in W5 was a core part of the game's identity and did a lot to set Werewolf apart, not just from other WoD games, but from other roleplaying games in general. In contrast, W5 feels a lot more generic and lacks a strong identity.
            I think part of my issue is that my first "real" exposure to Pentex was Pentex: Monkeywrenchers and the Book of Wyrm. Later, I really enjoyed Pentex's role in Project: Twilight. The thing is, none of those books really emphasized Pentex as a "humorous" corporation and the later satire of things kind of blew past me.

            For me, there's nothing really any more "funny" or "silly" about Pentex than Arasaka in Cyberpunk 2020 or Weyland-Yutani in Alien.

            It's there to be the Technocracy or Sabbat of the game and provide an immense amount of foes for our heroes to fight with unlimited resources. They're also a lot more interesting villains than Black Spiral Dancers or Nephandi.

            Very few people can understand, "I want to unmake reality" as a motive but everyone can understand, "I want to make a shit ton of money." The irony being that Pentex has aged much better than most groups and with a bit of tweaking you could have them be the Seers of the Throne equivalent in Mage where they'd arguably fit just as well.

                                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                            Indeed, things like Captain Planet in RL were actually meant to shift the idea of environmental protection from corporate regulation to individual responsibility (which does nothing to alleviate actual environmental devastation).
                                            To come back to this, I have to disagree with that. The explicit goal in Captain Planet was that Ted Turner wanted to foster an awareness in kids about the importance of environmentalism, and the idea that just because a problem is happening on the other side of the world doesn't mean it won't affect you.

                                            In quite a few episodes, Captain Planet and the kids couldn't solve the problem by themselves, but needed the help of local communities to come together to help fix the problem, showcasing the importance of community activism. Or conversely, you'd have episodes where at the end (after Captain Planet fought off the magical bad guys) the EPA would show up, because they were contacted earlier in the episode, to help shut down whatever polluting system was going on. Further, at the end of every episode they'd have suggestions on things that viewers (kids) could do to help, and this would often involve things like writing to politicians, boycotting companies that don't have good environmental policies, encouraging older adults who can vote to vote for pro-environmental policies, engaging in local community based activism to help get local pro-environmental changes, put together food drives, etc.

                                            Hell, one of the most reocurring themes was that individuals can't beat corporations on their own. When one of the Planeteers would go of in their own to fix a problem, they'd lose. You can't summon Captain Planet on your own. The show always pushed the need for collaborative activism to defeat corporate maleficence.

                                            Honestly I think the show was incredibly progressive, and I don't really see that it intended to shift the idea away from corporate regulation to individual responsibility, especially as the villains were corporations and there was almost always a point within the show that they were explicitly dodging environmental regulations. I guess there could be a cynical interpretation, but again, I don't think it really holds up too well.
