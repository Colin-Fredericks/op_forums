    W5 videogame/visual novel "WtA: Purgatory" - Review

    07-29-2024, 08:51 AM
    A lot has changed since Heart of the Forest, the prequel to this game, came out a few years ago. At the time of that release, the W5 corebook was still being developed by Hunters Entertainment. Since then, a quite different W5 corebook saw the light of day and in that environment, we’ve now gotten a sequel to Heart of the Forest named Purgatory.

    On the surface some things stayed the same: We follow one Garou-protagonist, we get a visual novel based on narrative choices and managing various stats, the art-style is still the same and the Polish setting is still the Białowieża Forest (at least in part in this game). What this game introduces is a new protagonist in the form of Samira (an Afghani refugee) and the Warsaw Sept.

    The story in this game can be neatly broken down into two routes. Early on, you’re presented with two problems the Warsaw Sept faces and you have to decide which one you follow up on. I think one thing that surprised me during my first playthrough was how short this game is. Because the game essentially bifurcates during that choice. You can only do one route and when you’re finished with that problem, the game ends. My first playthrough lasted about two and a half hours but because of the overlap that happens in both routes, I got through my second playthrough in roughly ninety minutes. So this isn’t a long game by any stretch of the imagination.

    The game also doesn’t exactly follow the script from the released W5 corebook. But at this point, I feel like a broken record mentioning that. On one hand, I’m glad none of the W5 media is interested in trying to replicate the presentation from the released W5 corebook. On the other hand, I think it’s safe to say that there’s no consistency when it comes to W5. Personally, I like the setting presented in this game more than the one presented in the W5 corebook, though.

    The Game



    This screenshot is from my second playthrough. It’s less about the actual text but more about a moment in the game where you can see most of the things that go on in this game.

    On the right side you see some art that depicts what is going on. The art-style is okay, I think. The Bane-Spirits look interesting but there was never anything in the game that wowed me. It works and doesn’t distract in a negative sense.

    Now as to the actual game-mechanics. It’s a bit different from Heart of the Forest. During character-creation you’re first asked to pick one physical Skill and one mental Skill. These are more like specializations, though. And really, what you’re actually picking is options for flavor-text. The actual events in the game are pretty much on rails (more on that later). So the difference between picking either Stealth or Athletics is that you get the option for flavor-text depending on whether you picked the former or the latter.

    The same applies to Tribe. For my first playthrough my Samira was a Silver Fang Philodox and the second time around I chose her to be a Black Fury Theurge. You get this flavor-text that is sign-posted by the Tribe-name like in the screenshot. The plot doesn’t really change. Events aren’t impacted by the Tribe-choice. That is disappointing. Then again, it has to be said that as a purely flavor-oriented thing it works quite well. The reason for that is that these flavor-influenced texts show up quite often. It actually happened so often that Silver Fang Samira was a different person compared to Black Fury Samira. In the case of the former, the text mentioned how Samira dreamed of one day attaining a leadership-position in the Sept and when she interacted with her companion, she was authoritative and decisive, treating her companion more like an underling. Meanwhile, as a Black Fury it talked about how she desired to make a change in the world and she was always impatient with her companion, urging to just make a decision and get back to the action. For example, in the screenshot you see the Black Fury version whereas in the Silver Fang version Samira actively went to the Sept asking for a fake-ID and then with their support went to the Glass Walkers and told them to make one for her. So that works quite well.

    The same applies to the chosen Auspice and Renown. Although with the latter, it has to be said that “Renown” is the wrong word for it (the W5 corebook’s approach to Renown has the same issue). Rather it’s about Glory, Honor and Wisdom presenting various ideals and virtues on how to deal with various situations. And so, you get moments where you can choose how to approach a situation, then depending on what you pick, you either get a dot in Glory, Honor or Wisdom. And then occasionally you get flavor-text based on whatever your highest Renown is that shows how your character analyzes a scene and what has happened. Again, it’s just there to be flavor.

    This worked well for my playthroughs but I can see that you can potentially stumble into combinations of Tribe, Auspice and Renown where descriptions contradict each other. In a way, this presentation banks on building your Samira in rather stereotypical ways. A Glass Walker Ragabash works better in what the game gives you than a Silver Fang Ragabash, for example.

    Let’s get to the trackers at the top. Both Willpower and Health are tracks that just go up and down depending on choices. Losing Health without going to zero means you take a hit in combat or another situation without being badly affected. Willpower is something you occasionally have to spend to pick certain (good) options in a situation.

    Then you got Rage and Harano. There are still options which increase or decrease those trackers but more importantly is that you get flavor-texts based on where your tracker is at the moment. The categories are: Zero (self-explanatory), Low (1 – 2), Balanced ( 3 ), High (4 – 5).

    With Harano it’s pretty obvious: the less the better. How this shows in the flavor-text is that the higher the Harano, the more Samira’s thoughts turn more introverted and anxiety-driven whereas she opens up at the lower end and becomes more empathetic. It’s well written… although it does do the thing of Harano=depression which I’m not a huge fan of. But as a way to describe depression, it’s done well (more on that later when I talk about Samira as a character). Also, I think this system would be something the TTRPG would’ve benefited from. Especially because this kind of categorization already existed anyway with Humanity in V5. Another thing Renegade should probably introduce in a future supplement.

    Rage also works well in that regard as the flavor-text makes quite clear how high-strung and aggressive Samira is at the High-rating. Meanwhile, in the lower ratings Samira feels drained and unenthusiastic. It’s interesting that this game positions Rage 3 (Balanced) as the good place to be. So Rage isn’t positioned as inherently bad.

    The other two things left to talk about is character-relationships and Gifts/Items. Relationships exist on a -3 to +3 scale. Does it matter? Again, it’s mostly flavor-text so the differences you can make with your choices are miniscule. Gifts/Items are one-use-only things – which is completely fine. Conceptually I would take issue with this but this game is so short that it didn’t bother me. For example, in my first playthrough with Silver Fang Philodox Garou I never used a Gift. Whenever the game presents the opportunity to use a Gift/item, you should just do it because there won’t be some other time to use it elsewhere in the story anyway.

    I also have to say that managing all the trackers in this game was easy. That screenshot you see is near the end and as you can see that’s exactly the stats you would want heading into the last third of the game. So, the game’s pretty forgiving and easy when it comes to avoiding failure-states.

    The Story

    But what’s Purgatory even about, you may wonder… Let’s start with the setting.

    (I don’t go over all the details of the story and events in the game but if you absolutely do not want any spoilers, you should probably stop reading here)

    In this game’s setting you deal with three Septs. You got the Warsaw Sept, the Białowieża Forest Sept and the Sept in Afghanistan that Samira used to belong to. Each Sept has a caern and each caern is protected by multiple (!) Garou-packs. In fact, part of character-creation is to choose between three Packs which each have their own dedicated Pack-Patron-Spirit. There’s no talk of “The Garou Nation has fallen” anywhere in the game. One thing I also really like is that two character-relationship-trackers you have in the game are the Warsaw-caern and the Białowieża forest. And the game shows off that these are spiritual entities which can help you! These are conscious entities you can interact with. It’s a bit sad how the W5 corebook downplayed that.

    Also, it should be kept in mind that whatever elements I talk about below have to be seen in the context of a rather short game. In general, a big problem for the narrative is that it takes on quite a lot of things at the same time and due to its short length none get the proper attention.

    Samira

    Aside from the flavor-options I mentioned there’s of course one that’s the most important to the game’s story: Samira is an Afghan refugee in Poland. There are flashbacks/dream-sequences that describe the journey of fleeing Afghanistan and getting to Poland.

    Most importantly, the game starts with the final border-crossing in which Samira’s little brother gets killed. And this leads to one of the more interesting elements in the story: When the first Chapter starts, Samira has already been in Poland for about a year but hasn’t been active in the Sept. And that is because at the start of this game, Samira is in Harano. So, on one level Samira’s story-arc in this visual novel is about her getting out of Harano and becoming an active member of the Sept again. Of course, if the game had listened to the W5 corebook, she should’ve been declared an unplayable irreversibly doomed character the second she fell to Harano (lol)…

    The Afghanistan-element especially suffers due to the short length. While the flashbacks talk about weeks of travel, it really does give the appearance of a short journey. Like, you’re in Afghanistan, you travel to a nearby city, go through the mountains, cross a river and… voila, you’re at the Polish border. Later on, her uncle also arrives and it just enhances this feeling when he writes a letter saying “Hey, I had to leave. I’m coming to you.” And then a few days later, he really is already at the Polish border.

    The descriptions of Afghanistan also don’t work that well because the focus is entirely on the US leaving Afghanistan in 2021 and the Taliban taking over. For one, being a civilian in Afghanistan in the years prior hadn’t been exactly a cakewalk either. And the text makes it seem like the Taliban are these space-invaders that left before and only now in 2021 reappeared.

    What the game also does is completely sidestep the religious angle. Instead, in another way where it ignores the W5 corebook and goes with the old lore, this game’s setting still firmly believes in Garou being mainly a hereditary thing and so Samira is growing up in a family of Garou. Kinfolk is never mentioned but it’s essentially what is described. Her aunt is a Garou and is the first person to move to Warsaw. But part of this is that the game states that because they’re a Garou-family, they didn’t grow up as Muslims and don’t practice those beliefs/traditions (like covering your hair) and instead they grew up believing in the Litany. That’s how it’s phrased. There’s a flashback-scene tied to this where Samira forgot to cover her hair after leaving the home and is confronted by Taliban-soldiers. The game is more interested in sidestepping that conversation than having any sort of take on it, though.

    Overall, this leads to Samira as an Afghan refugee being more about her character being an outsider in Poland who came there as a refugee in a general sense. It’s more about Poland’s reaction to it rather than delving into the experiences and regional history of the country Samira came from. Due to how little this gets explored, you could’ve easily made her a Syrian or Libyan. There’s no discussion of the war in Afghanistan that started in 2001, for example. Thinking about it, I think this would’ve worked better if she had been the younger sibling who as a kid came with the older sibling to the border, then the older sibling gets killed, the younger sibling experiences their First Change. That way you could narratively justify why the Afghanistan/Islam-angle is being downplayed and not explored in detail.

    The Missions

    It starts with Samira going to the Sept and meeting Illona who’s the Sept-leader. On the way there, you encounter a weak Bane-Spirit that flees from you. At the Sept you find out that a Glass Walker Garou had been found murdered. Then, Andrej a Shadow Lord Garou arrives and angrily berates Illona for not taking action. Andrej is a blonde, blue-eyed guy who’s angry and abrasive while also claiming that the increased presence of Wyrm-minions and Bane-Spirits in the city is all due to the increased number of refugees. If you know W5, you can already see where this is going…

    In fact, I was a bit surprised when something was changed here compared to the Demo that came out a few months ago. In the Demo, part of Andrej’s abrasiveness was that he started his rant by dead-naming the Sept-leader (Illona is a trans-woman). That was already a bit of a weird choice (they introduced a trans-character just to immediately dead-name her…?). But the part that bugged me the most is that Samira’s POV had been all about her having zero idea what was going on. I mean, come on! The line from Andrej was “Mikhal… or should I say Illona?”. Also, this should’ve led to an immediate confrontation because it went way beyond mere provocative. So, it’s good that they removed that bit from the released version.

    Afterwards, Samira goes to the Apokolipsa-Bar (the name is a bit on the nose…) where she works part-time. There she meets Pat, Victoria and Aleksander. And you essentially get the two missions in this game. You can only pick one. Pat’s route is about investigating the death of the Glass Walker and Aleksander’s route is about investigating Andrej.

    Remember how I mentioned in the other thread how W5 only knows how to tell two stories? This game falls perfectly in these two categories. Pat’s route is about bad people doing bad stuff (and they happen to be Fomori) and Aleksander’s route is about Garou doing the wrong thing (because they’re too angry and/or sad).

    I mentioned at the beginning how much of the character-creation-options are just about flavor-text and this shows in these two routes because they’re essentially on rails. There’s little that happens where you can deviate from the general plot-structure. Additionally, both routes have the same structure.

    It goes like this:

    Investigation-scenes -->Infiltration/Investigation Site A-->Fight with minion after leaving Site A-->Train-ride to Białowieża Forest (Samira finds out that Tariq, her uncle, is coming to the Polish border)-->Fight with two minions in the trainSmall town scene with protestors against refugees/talk with Journalist/NGO worker-->Talk with Winter Weasel Spirit-->Infiltration/Investigation Site B-->Talk with the Wolf Mother Spirit-->Final confrontation (You either can fight the big bad, his minions or protect the refugees trying to cross the border… and you still save the refugees even if you don’t pick that last option)

    In both routes, these are interspersed with flashbacks/dream-sequences that are tying back to the Wolf-mother/Wolf-father conflict in the story.

    Pat’s route

    Pat is a hacktivist Glass Walker Garou who’s investigating the death of Emyr, a fellow Glass Walker. You do the investigation of the murder-scene which leads to a nearby refugee-center. You learn from the people in the refugees that Emyr was investigating a nearby factory. Then the refugee-center gets suddenly attacked by teen-vandals (they throw rocks to break the windows). After you’ve chased off the teenage-vandals, you follow them and find out that the owner of the nearby factory is paying them to vandalize refugee-centers in Warsaw. Also, the owner is a Fomor.

    You infiltrate the factory and learn about how the factory is producing weapons and cameras for a private military company and you learn that an attack on refugees trying to cross is planned. When you leave, you’re confronted by a Fomor. Then you travel to the forest-Sept. On the train you get attacked by two Fomori. Once you get to the small town, you meet up with Maia (the protagonist from the first game) who is the current Sept leader of the forest-Sept and the trail leads to the mentioned private military-base. You infiltrate that, learn of where the attack will take place and then you basically get to the final confrontration where soldiers led by Fomori are trying to deliberately kill refugees trying to cross the border.

    This was the first playthrough I did and I can’t stress enough just how abrupt the ending felt. You kill the bad guys, protect the refugees and then the story just kinda ends… This one also felt like the less impactful of the two stories. It’s just all about bad people doing bad stuff. There’s a bad company with a factory, there are bad politicians getting bribes from the company who are anti-refugees and then you got the military-base full of soldiers willing to just hunt down and kill a bunch of refugees. And the soldiers are led by Fomori, of course.

    You have that talk with the Wolfmother-spirit which talks about the Wyrm being ascendant but there’s no talk of an “Age of Apocalypse”. One thing I liked about this part is that writing makes clear here that the human evil doesn’t just get exploited by the Wyrm but it pushes the evil in the world to expand as well. This isn’t the take you get in W5 where it’s all humanity’s fault and the Wyrm would be powerless to further any of its evil agenda if humanity were just a little bit nicer. There’s a push and pull between humanity and the Wyrm that goes in both directions. The setting just feels more spiritual when the Wyrm is depicted like that, I think.

    Aleksander’s route

    You can kinda see what this one’s about but it doesn’t play out exactly the way you’d think. So Andrej, the main-villain of this route is a Shadow Lord and the leader of the Wolf-father-Pack. I took a screenshot of how it’s described in the game:



    Andrej’s whole take on the situation is that there is only one reason why the Wyrm has gotten stronger in Warsaw and it’s the refugees. So the investigation starts with checking out the location where supposedly a member of Andrej’s pack has been attacked by a refugee who was also a Fomor. You find Zuzanna at the location and it quickly becomes clear that she lied about the attack because Andrej told her to do so (you later find out that she’s being abused by Andrej as well).

    At this point, I wondered whether this is a setting without the Cult of Fenris. The first place you infiltrate is the den of the Wolf-father’s Pack and Andrej is giving an anti-refugee speech to a bunch of Garou. The whole thing also had the whiff of a cult. But to my surprise when you search Andrej’s room you find a book – which is all about the ideals and goals of the Cult of Fenris. That’s right, you don’t get the Cult of Fenris but instead you get this Wolf-father-Pack which uses this book like a manual to imitate the Cult. It feels odd. So it’s exactly like the Cult of Fenris – except it isn’t the Cult of Fenris…?

    It gets even weirder with the Wolfmother-scene on this route. Because it’s here and only here where Hauglosk gets mentioned. Nobody but the Wolfmother-spirit in this setting seems to be aware that Hauglosk is even a thing. Also, she explains that the Wolf-Spirit has fallen and split in two. What used to be two halves functioning in unity has been disrupted. The Wolf-father-Spirit who used to be about lashing out at the enemies of the pack and keeping the hearth safe has been consumed by Rage and has now turned into a vindictive and cruel Spirit. That actually felt like a better origin-story for something like a Cult of Fenris. And you wouldn’t have a whole Tribe fall as one but instead split into two factions. Meanwhile, the Cult of Fenris isn’t just members of that one fallen Tribe but as the name implies an actual cult that any Garou who has the same ideals and beliefs can join.

    But the ending of this route is also a letdown. Andrej and his Garou-followers are attacking and trying to kill a bunch of refugees that try to cross the border. I thought there would be more to this because Aleksander as it turned out used to be Andrej’s boyfriend. So I picked the route where you directly fight with Andrej. But the fight’s pretty anticlimactic. He’s bad, you’re good, Samira and Aleksander beat the crap out of him (the worst that can happen here is that you don’t beat him outright but get him to flee). If you’re successful, you knock him unconscious, drag him back to the Warsaw Sept where he gets executed.

    Conclusion

    I had a great time during my first playthrough – until I had gotten to the ending. I was completely unprepared for that. First, I thought that after killing the soldiers and Fomori who targeted the refugees, it would go on to be about Samira & co thoroughly taking apart the Fomori led private military and factory. So the ending felt quite abrupt. Second, I imagined that after I would be done with that part, the game would move on to let me play through Aleksander’s story. Neither happened. Instead, you got more of a bland “All’s well that ends well…” kind of summary. And there’s even this line about how the story you didn’t pick at the beginning would just remain a mystery. So, if you’re doing Pat’s route, it said that Andrej’s dark secret remained hidden while if you’re doing Aleksander’s route it said that Emyr’s death remained a mystery. Very unfulfilling…

    The next shock came when during my second playthrough I realized just how on rails the story actually was and how little impact your choices had on the adventure. Your choices were more about what sort of flavor-text the game would give you rather than actually changing the course of the narrative.

    But if you can get past these two issues, the one strength it has is that it nails the tone of a WtA-game quite well. Spirits are important, there are plenty of Garou characters and it’s emphasized that they’re a tight-knit community. Tonally this game is much closer to what I would want out of a WtA-story than what we’d seen with other W5 material.
