                              Fera that should seemingly be commonplace are absent to underline the tragedy of anthropogenic extinction and the overarching great extinction that humans bring with them (yes, it's done by Garou in the setting, but the Garou fucking up is just an allegory for humans fucking up). W:tA is really more of a "did you know how terrible it really is outside?" kind of horror over gothic horror, and the gothic sensibilities, via Vampire, are generally considered a thing that isn't good for the world, and therefore something that W:tA is going to struggle against in tone to its last word. This is why trying to "reimagine" W:tA to be more compatible with V:tM in WoD5 is particularly galling.

                                  Kinda depends how you want to define Gothic doesn't it?

                                  If, for example, one were to look at the rather copious notes compared to his contemporaries that Bram Stoker left on his world building for Dracula... Werewolf is pretty on point: a bunch of white people that read a bunch of books mostly written by other white people about things they didn't have first hand experience of, and then created a massive mish-mash of folklore from across multiple cultures, tossed in stuff just because it was cool, and then made a bunch of shit up on top of all of that to form something that at least feels cohesive. The Gothic authors were far more committed to mixing entertainment with societal critiques than they were about being faithful to folklore or mythology.

So the argument that Werewolf isn't particularly Gothic usually comes from this angle instead.

                                                      Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                                                      The Changing Breeds should reflect the actual real world legends of what animals that people turn into.
                                                      This is my main problem with the changing breeds (and with a fair bit of werewolf lore [and with oWoD over CofD in general]) is how certain and hard a lot of things are. Ratfolk are Rat shifters and not Rat pooka or rodent-like Sluagh nor rat faced Gorgons or Fomor that happen to be able to breed true. Meanwhile despite the many myths surrounding shapechanging pinnipeds or porpoises, unless you count storytellers vault lost breeds or changeling breeds, they don't exist. And while the system allows for a lot of strange one of critters or lineages to exist, the lore rarely accommodates it instead defining things as relatively concrete groups with known or knowable tenants and objectives. There should be boar shifters, in the places where legends of talking boar-kings and wildmen who wear boar skins are told. I would want a line of were-dolphins that perhaps once ranged across the atlantic but are now reduced to a single lineage in the rivers of Brazil. I'd even take it as a Wyld spirit or the like who periodically posesses someone who lives along the rivers perhaps as an echo of a changing breed that was wiped out in the past, comparable to a mage avatar or changeling spirit. I think the setting, in every splat I've played, benefits from the degree of uncertainty that even the wisest mentor the PCs have access to might not know the true nature of the things that go bump in the night, or at least may not be willing or able to accurately communicate such things if they do know.

                                                      I love the idea that theurges can ask the stones of a path to help them track someone but only be told about the strength of footfalls, that inhuman sources have inhuman perceptions and priorities that would make it difficult for them to give you the answers important to you, even if they wanted to. I love the idea that large parts of the Silver Record have been lost forever, destroyed in wars or by foes or even by erstwhile Gaian Garou who wanted some things to be forgotten and so obliterated entire epics and fables.

                                                      For that matter, to disagree with OP, it also makes sense that some of the strange and wild things of the world are gone, lost forever or indelibly corrupted. Like the idea of 'our ancestors wiped this out and it never returned' adds a delightful bittersweet note to Garou trying to act as protectors of Gaia and fight against forces of extinction. And it makes sense that if you kill all of a particular flower, no matter how plentiful your garden, it would take a miracle to see that flower bloom again. So, despite the abundant wild kinfolk of the Grondr, it would take a miracle for a new and uncorrupted line of Grondr to emerge from the ruin heaped on their line. It'd be comparable to the singular White Howler being born from a modern Welsh family except without a Nation looking out for her to try and tell her what to be or not to be. And if traditional genocide proved tedious even for the warriors of Gaia, less traditional methods, such as killing or corrupting the Boar-shifters patron spirits, severing their connection to luna or Helios or whichever incarna chooses them, or the like, are possibilities.

                                                      Alternatively, if you really want Grondr to be out there, in the sunlit realm, with human and wild boar kinfolk, maybe, a bit like the Ananasi or the Naga, they have made some kind of powerful spirit pact that makes it difficult for Garou to perceive or track them. A kind of Arcane background but just for Garou, or maybe shifters as a whole, and the Garou are too arrogant and stretched too thin in modern nights to see through it. Or maybe part of the genocide was that Grondr had some creation rites to prevent them from being too populous similar to corax, or opposite of ratkin, which the Garou disrupted as part of the genocide and and non-skull-pig heritors now use sparingly and in secret, a la the Salubri of VtM, intentionally keeping their number small and well trained to avoid their ancestral adversaries.
