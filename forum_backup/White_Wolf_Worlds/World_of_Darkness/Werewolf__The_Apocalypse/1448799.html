    Considering a Mongolian Tribe

    05-17-2021, 04:18 PM
    Mongolia has a rich cultural mythology (a term I in no way mean to use to suggest "falsehood") around wolves, and a relatively stable wolf population thanks to the cultural respect they are afforded. There's obviously a lot of cultural archetypes to draw on in the fashion that other Tribes do their associated cultures (for better or worse), and I'm considering either adding a new tribe, completely rewriting the Stargazers, or having a major branch of an existent Tribe (like the Hakken are to the Shadow Lords) help fill that setting niche.

    I would love to hear thoughts on what you'd like to see in such a Tribe or branch that both draws on Mongolian history, culture, and legend, without falling into harmful stereotypes (*tries to not eye the Fianna*). Would you rather see a Silver Fang "House", a rewrite of the Stargazers, or a whole new Tribe? As Mongolian tradition sees wolves as messengers of Tengri, is there room in Werewolf cosmology for a Tribe with a masculine view, or even masculine counterpart (since Tengrism also features an Earth Mother), of what the other Tribes call Gaia?

          I've thought of the Silver Fangs as the tribe that belongs to the taiga, and the Shadow Lords as the tribe that belong to the steppe. Garou who live in grasslands don't have much to shelter them from a storm, so it's natural for them to appease Thunder. In my chronicles, the Shadow Lords are the main tribe in Mongolia and Central Asia. (Not the only one, as I don't hold any large territory is the sole protectorate of one tribe.) So all sorts of steppe peoples have had Shadow Lord relations regardless whether they spoke Indo-European, Turkic, Mongolic, or other languages.

          I think it's been a mistake of the game to too heavily concentrate on the human cultures of the Garou tribes' kinfolk as their tribal identity. While homids will have some of that passed to them, it would mean nothing to lupus. And of course, go far enough into the past, and there is no Mongolian culture. It's some other human culture, and before them a different human culture. What should be identifiable as a tribal culture/identity is derived from the tribal totem spirit which will persist as human cultures change.

          That's why in my games, I would have no problem for there to be a splinter branch of Fianna in Mongolia because why couldn't there be Children of Stag elsewhere? Their bastion of power might be Western Europe, but why couldn't there be a smaller redoubt that was established in 10,000 BC and somehow managed to hold on. They would still have lots in common with the Fianna of western Europe, but their homids would obviously be derived from Mongolian stock and culture. "Fianna" after all just means a warrior band, and all the steppe people's have those.

          And of course the Black Furies could also very well be in Mongolia as well, since the Eurasian Steppe is where the Greeks placed most of the Amazons. There's likely a few other tribes who could be located there as well that would make sense.

          Terms like "Gaia" are an artifact of it being an RPG. People should not get held up on it. Developers just ransacked their dictionaries and thesauruses looking for semi-appropriate, but unfamiliar terms to include as game terms to allow players to communicate to one another. They weren't scholars like Tolkien or Barker who invent a whole new language. The Garou Tongue likely does not actually use "Gaia", and all the tribes likely use their human language for an appropriate term for the Earth Mother in order to fit in. But to make things easier for the players, we just use Gaia. As opposed to some made up term for it, or have these people use Jord, Nerthus, Cybele, or whatever. That just gets too confusing - players need a common language for common concepts. Likewise, the Get of Fenris consider Luna to be female even though actual Germanic culture considers the moon to be masculine. Garou culture and human culture are different.

          Cool things can be added to the game through exploring a particular folklore, culture, and mythology. But it all needs to be transformed into something the Garou would understand, and quite often that is different from a human experience.

                                                          Something I workshop for my own game the idea of kind of a sub-tribe that I go back and forth between titles like ‘Wind Riders’ or ‘White Stallions’ or whatever.

                                                          I usually consider the Black Furies to be descended from the same ur-tribe that migrated westward from the Iranian plateau/caucuses area where they with Scythian and other nomadic tribes got settled into the Greek, Balkans, Turkey region. I usually have it that when the Black Furies became a tribe, the pre-tribal garou who would become the furies, with Luna-sent-Pegasus as their tribal patron. So the women and Garou-born who wanted would join the Black Furies and the men and the other Garou-born went elsewhere. Going eastward past the Iranian plateau I have them contact the Children of Gaia and with their stewardship effectively become a sub-tribe (kinda like the Siberakh) with Wind Horse as their patron (an abstraction of a figure from Tengri religion and parallel to Pegasus). This sub-tribe I have occupy around Central Asia and Mongolia with a heavy lean into the nomadic horse-rider archetype.
