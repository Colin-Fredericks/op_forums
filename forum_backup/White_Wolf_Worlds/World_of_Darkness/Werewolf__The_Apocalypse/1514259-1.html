                    I once ran a campaign with Odin as the big villain. I designed the campaign for a player who pretty much only plays Get of Fenris. Ironically he didn't end up being able to play. I changed up Odin a bit from canon. He had been a mortal mage, a necromancer of vast skill with his goal of being able to learn how to surpass death. He would often journey deep into the underworld. At one point he discovered a vast tree that seemed to reach up forever, though as he got closer he saw that there were countless wraiths impaled on it, writhing in agony. Suddenly one of the branches grabbed him and impaled him to the tree as well. The tree was, in fact, a Neverborn that had grown so large it had burst free of the Labyrinth. For 9 days and 9 nights, Odin mentally wrestled with the Neverborn. Finally he managed to win, tearing free of the tree and having wrested countless secrets from the Malfean but was fundamentally changed in the process, far more dead than alive.

                    He isn't exactly a vampire, but he's able to create vampires. His brood are able to pass themselves off as Gangrel (they possess Fortitude, Protean and Auspex). His undead Valkyries search for great warriors, turning them into vampires, or capturing the souls of warriors who had already died and returning to Odin who lives within the Underworld. While impaled to the Malfean, Odin had visions of the future, visions of Ragnarok or Gehenna. He gathers the souls of great warriors and pits them against each other where they fight, die and are reborn. He is preparing a vast army for the Apocalypse. Mechanically I statted him up as something more similar to a Onceborn. In addition to his magical prowess (which has only continued to grow since he was a mortal), he has a number of unique powers and abilities. He calls his underworld kingdom Valhalla but others call it the Dark Kingdom of Blood (due to the blood that is constantly spilled by his undying spirit warriors).

                    Ultimately Odin is somewhat similar to Osiris from Mummy. Odin is largely trapped within the Underworld and sends servants out into the real world to do his bidding. His own power is incredible but it's difficult for him to directly emerge into the world, and he has only done so a handful of times over the centuries. In one of his visions he saw himself being killed by a fast wolf, Fenrir, and so he has made it his goal to destroy the creature and its children. Ironically his vision of dying in Fenrir's jaws is what started his feud and will likely lead to his death.

                                    The next group are changelings. This shouldn't stop discussion of how to use vampires among the Host. But as others have stated, there are a lot of themes present in "Odin" that can be exploited. Fairies are one of them. In mythology, Odin lead the Aesir and later allied with a separate group of gods called the Vanir. Most mythologists place the Vanir among the elves. In my own chronicles, the Vanir is another name for the Tuatha de Danann. The Unseelie sidhe (elves) of House Aesin are their descendants in northern Europe. So a very long time ago, the All High - a powerful vampire even back then - made pacts and oaths with the fae of that noble House.

                                    Changeling introduced the idea that the Get of Fenris had a blood oath against the sidhe. I never liked that as it was a huge restriction introduced fairly late. What if players had a Get of Fenris PC in a pack of Stag? This would completely change the existing dynamics of a game. It would invalidate a lot of existing games. So in my chronicles, I changed it so the Get of Fenris only had a blood oath against the sidhe House Aesin instead. That would allow most interactions between the Get of Fenris and changelings to be unchanged, but simply gave STs an additional tool if they wanted to introduce complications. House Aesin therefore made their oath with the All High because 1) the Get were mutual enemies, and 2) the All High knew prophecies of Endless Winter (Ragnarok/Gehenna/Apocalypse) and seemed to be a useful ally.

                                    Nowadays, this oath is kept by members of the Shadow Court inside House Aesin (and their allies) after the Shattering. A tenet of the Shadow Court is to understand the supernatural world, and make and break alliances as necessary. These fae consider their alliance with the All High as very necessary. I also place within this group a secret tradition of Black Magicians (Dauntain). They trace their forbidden arts to the All High. The ignorant consider them Dauntain, but they reject the idea that they are in thrall to Banality. They merely seek to master its mysteries to use against their enemies - especially the Jotunns (Fomorians) and their allies. They acknowledge it is dangerous lore, but are confidant they can use it carefully enough. Because of their control over Banality, the Black Magicians among the Aesin are critical in maintaining the fae’s relationship with the vampire followers of the All High. They let the other members of House Aesin/Shadow Court (and their other changeling allies) to not worry about any negative impact involving Banality.

                                    There were probably once fae who had blood bonds with the All High in return for mutual oaths of loyalty, but those are long since gone given that the All High has been in torpor for a thousand years and the massive changes to the fae as well. If the All High ever awakes from torpor, some of the might consider renewing the oath (I also see fae oaths being effective on the All High). But that would be only with the All High. The fae would not consider doing it with any other vampire, including the Walkure.

                                        I think having changelings allied to Odin gives certain tools to a Werewolf ST. First, feuds with these fae against other fae could justify involvement by packs of Stag that tie back to stronger Werewolf elements. Second, changelings can make intriguing enemies on their own whether the ST uses actual Changeling rules, treats them as spirits mechanically, ties it into the Arcadia Gateway, Trod domains, or the many other ways faerie stuff is included in the basic setting. Third, this expands the menace of Odin because many Garou with ties to the Changelings are not Get of Fenris. So the ST has additional hooks and perspectives to introduce the plot. Fourth, it can be quite some time before the PCs realize that these fae are even connected in any way to Odin and vampires. This can be useful in setting up long term plots. PCs might see these fae antagonists to be more of a distraction than actual enemies, or attempt to deal with them in a more positive way than they would if identifiably Wyrm minions were doing the same thing. It just gives the ST a lot more tools.

                                        I see the fae as providing an unexpected edge to the Host against their foes. Fae and vampires don’t work closely together. Each group mainly pursues their goals independently. But they are aware of the other and their leaders can communicate with one another as needed. Occasionally one side calls in the other when necessary, and often exchanging favors. See them more like as allies than one single team.

                                        Traditionally, Changeling and Vampire are seen as opposite ends of the World of the Darkness with very little crossover. That makes sense, but there from time to time both game lines make reference to one another. I think an independent group like the Host provides more effective justification and appropriate flavor text than trying to force sects like the Camarilla or Sabbat to changeling society as a whole. I think the limitations added here (House Aesin and the Shadow Court) makes sense, though an ST could always decide to expand things a bit. Even if crossover isn't your thing in playing either of those games separately, I think in the context of antagonists in Werewolf, it can work very well.
