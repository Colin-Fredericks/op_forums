    New W5 Glyphs Released

    10-29-2023, 04:07 PM
    As if some people didn't need another reason to dislike W5...

    As part of the "Month of Darkness" promotion, Paradox has released pack of various WOD5 illustrations for free use, across all three game lines. This includes logos, symbols, and dice designs. The W5 portion includes a selection of new glyphs, many of which weren't included in the rule book. These include glyphs for the five forms, tribal patrons, other major spirits, the four seasons (?!), and a few other important ones. Strangely enough, glyphs for the Cult of Fenris and the Stargazers weren't included, although the BSD glyph was.

    Many of these glyphs are just outlines of what they kind of represent. For example, the glyphs for the five forms, in traditional order, are an open palm, a clenched fist, a wolf-man head and shoulders (including pointy ears), a large howling wolf head, and a normal wolf head. Some of the tribal patrons are no better. Now, the previous editions' glyphs looked liked what they represented as well. The new ones just do so with dashed-line outlines instead of less figurative means. Stag is just a diamond with stick antlers. North Wind is basically just a bunch of wavy lines. I can't even tell what Rat is. It looks more like a ram that anything else.

    Considering how complex the rest of the glyphs in the main book are, it is highly unlikely that the Garou would resort to something so simple for so many other 'words.' Many of the others are a little better, although I can't figure out why some were chosen to be included in this first batch of glyphs, such as Ally/Safety. I would have included the actual pics, but I'm not sure if they would be allowed. Also, I am not that tech savvy enough to be certain I know how to do so.

    THWarted

                                          No, the asset-drop didn't come with any explanations.

                                          I've looked through the W5-corebook to see how the book handles Garou-language and glyphs. The first thing is that it doesn't seem like it's ever acknowledged that the Garou have their own language. Instead, the "language of spirits" is mentioned and other than that it's about human languages a Garou may grow up with. For example, here on pg 45 in the Werewolf Names sidebar: "Garou names may reflect the cultural norms and mother tongue of the Garou’s homeland, or they may become quite esoteric, and even borrow from the language of spirits.​"

                                          The glyphs are both treated as symbols and an alphabet. Like here in the section The Litany on pg 46: "Many — probably most — Garou know the Litany in some form, whether verbatim as presented here, via a series of parables, or perhaps even as a series of lupus- scratched warning glyphs on a pile of stacked stones at the edge of the Schwarzwald.​" (man, that lupus must have done a lot of scratching to get the entirety of the Litany on that pile of stacked stones...). Then there's a bit in the sample-adventure where a test is described whether the PCs are able to recognize that they are in a dead caern that used to belong to the Cult of Fenris (pg 306): "On a Difficulty 3 Wits + Awareness test, the characters find a number of smooth soapstone rocks with runes carved into them buried. Also buried are a number of crystals carved into spear points. Characters who succeed at a Difficulty 2 Intelligence + Occult test recognize the runes as werewolf glyphs and the spear points as serving some kind of Rite purpose, but unconnected to the murder-ritual. A critical win recognizes them as Cult of Fenris runes as well.​" Well, as we all know Tribes in W5 have no connection to human culture or ethnicity whatsoever. That's why the Native American Tribes were completely rewritten. It's not like there's any sort of cultural link here when while discussing the Cult of Fenris the glyphs are called "runes"... sigh

                                          Then there's the Wyrm-Loresheet where the first dot is this (pg 299): "Garou who knows the glyph can represent the Black Spiral, but among servitors of the Wyrm, the glyph itself
                                          possesses nuances that most Garou don’t know. You know how to convincingly make the true Black Spiral glyph, which you might use as bait or to mislead others, but those who know you possess this ability are suspicious of you, if not outright hostile." Here you get some notion that there are nuances to a glyph and somehow most Garou don't know about those when it comes to the Black Spiral glyph. Together with the bit in the adventure about the Cult of Fenris "runes", it seems like glyphs are yet another area in W5 where Garou are a bit clueless for the most part.

                                          Well, anyway, it's really more about "Garou have glyphs and can read them on a successful Intelligence + Occult test.". But there's no mention of a specific Garou-language or any exploration of where those glyphs come from or how they are used.

                                                          The thing about low-Wyrm lore Garou not being able to fully comprehend the nuances of Wyrm-glyphs even if they can more or less recognize the Black Spiral glyph and could theoretically reproduce it (even if this might be looked askance at by other Garou) is actually interesting imo and offers some compelling ideas for things which might be further developed upon, regardless of how one feels about the new W5 glyphs.

                                                          The notion of fleshing out how the Garou can use their glyphs/language, both for practical purposes, but its social and political implications is very interesting imo. It might be something worthwhile to look for inspiration from irl beliefs about the spiritual/magical powers of writing systems. Perhaps the reason why it would be looked at with suspicion to know the Black Spiral rune is that merely being able to read it, much less reproduce it, really does provide an extra route by which a Garou might become the subject of Wyrm influence or molestation. One could further flesh this out as part of what Older Brother/Ghost Council and the Shadow Lords do which make other Garou somewhat uneasy; they learn the fallen tongues and do expose themselves to serious risks in exchange for the knowledge and power which that brings.

                                                          Leaving the glyphs themselves aside, I do think there’s room there for a development of that concept for in-setting uses.
