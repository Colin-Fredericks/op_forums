            I feel the, "You can't make systemic change because it is a HORROR game and about your personal struggles is authoritarian" is a helluva take.

            That would mean film noir is authoritarian.

            But forget it, Jake, it's Fifth Edition.



            Basically, I feel like this dangerously comes close to arguing that non-idealistic storytelling is somehow morally relevant rather than artistically. The idea that you have to be able to make a difference and good to be able to triumph over evil in order to not be supporting authoritarian ideals. Which is nonsense. I could easily make the same argument that idealistic storytelling where good triumphs over evil is inherently fascist because it lies to people that the system can in any way allow a non-corrupt non-tyrannical victory without being torn down first. Which is ridiculous.

            I don't think there's anything wrong with depicting the World of Darkness as a place where there's a corrupt authority in the Garou Nation and systemic evils that the player characters can't necessarily beat. That's basically always been the depiction of the Garou Nation leadership (Silver Fangs are insane/corrupt, the Shadow Lords are, well, Evil) and they're up against Pentex which has always been shown to be so ubiquitous that it was undefeatable unless you totally destroyed Western civilization.

            Werewolf has never been non-Punk. It was always about fighting the incarnation of Western pollution, capitalism, and authority. The big difference as mentioned is that Vampire: The Masquerade allowed you the opportunity to JOIN the forces of corruption and greed while Werewolf never allowed you to be a Pentex executive. Which gives a very different feel toward Werewolf. Werewolf has always been a bit more binary in its morality with Werewolves guilty of things like past genocides and incompetent leadership but no one argues its not better than the alternative.

            There's no, "The Anarchs are children and the Camarilla are the adults in the room" or "The Technocracy is right while the Traditions are Luddite terrorists." Which actually does seriously impact the themes of the game.

            Which are VERY common views in their gamelines. As mentioned I'm a V:TM guy then a M:TA guy but W:TA is still on the list.

                    No offense, but I think you've missed the point here. It's not that people want WoD to become Good Guys vs. Bad Guys, or never ever explore corruption within these groups, or that addressing this corruption is somehow authoritarianism​. It's that Hunter and Werewolf had the aspects that made them "punk" in their own unique ways removed to try to make them more like Vampire, and in the process, they were just left as empty husks that do nothing but tell the player they're worthless and they will never accomplish anything, while actively removing any structure or tools they could have used to actually play the game. It's one thing to be nihilistic or have seemingly impossible odds, but it's another for a supposedly "punk" game to actively tell you that trying to fight the power is useless and stupid. Which W5 does. A lot.

                    I'm speaking admittedly a WtA main, but I've played my share of every WoD line, including a lot of VtM. I've read W5 front to back by this point, and tried to play H5 (had to stop because the mechanics just plain don't work, but that's a story for another day), and for the former especially, there is no more Nation to fight against. It's been gone for a while. The Silver Fangs and Shadow Lords are now just some guys, barely different from any of the other tribes and don't hold any real authority. So who are the Garou supposed to rage against. The Wyrm? Well, no, W5 keeps telling us that the Wyrm isn't all that bad and maybe the Garou are overblowing this whole thing. Corporations? No, the book won't shut up about how the Garou can't actually do anything meaningful to stop them and probably shouldn't even try because hey corporations aren't that bad either. Fighting for Gaia? Nope. Gaia's either unfixably terminally ill or already dead, and you can't go into the Umbra. And if you try to fight anyway? Well, we call that Hauglosk and it'll make your character unplayable very quickly.

                    So what's even left to this game? Why even bother playing it? Like you said, Werewolf has no option to join the bad guys. What's "punk" about being told to sit in one place and guard your singular block until the world explodes? There's nihilism, and then there's being given no point in playing the game at all.
