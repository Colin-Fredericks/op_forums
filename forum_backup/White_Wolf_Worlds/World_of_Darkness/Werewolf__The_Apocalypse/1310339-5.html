                Caveat, I have not read this thread.

                I have some disabilities, though of course that in no way "authorizes" me to speak for anyone else with disabilities, let alone ones I don't have.

                I don't think Metis should be removed from W5 (although given the rumor that stepping sideways is being removed, I may not care at all about W5). I don't mind Metis but I do hate retcons. W5 as a continuation of the metaplot into the 2010's and 2020's is useless if all prior history is retconned. Guess what, Metis are part of that prior history. It would be just as disruptive as retconning away the Lupus, or the Gurahl or the Bastet.

                I do think their name and depiction should change. Yes, the Garou would give them a disparaging name, but that doesn't have to be an OOC slap in the face of a Native American nation, too. And why would the Bastet call Metis by a disparaging name? They don't see anything wrong with Metis, and barely even punish the parents. Bastet Metis get the coolest Gifts, and nobody can steal them. Ajaba no longer care what Breed a werehyena is, although maybe in the past they did. And like the Dalit, maybe Garou Metis have their own name for themselves now.

                Something very important to understand about disability is that being disabled is not inherent to any particular body or brain arrangement. A very short human is only disabled if they live in a society of people who are all taller than them. I, standing 5'4", would be profoundly disabled in a hobbit society just because I wouldn't fit indoors. A tailless Garou does not communicate in the same exact way that other Garou communicate, but that is only a disadvantage if other Garou are unwilling or unable to meet them halfway. A two-tailed Garou doesn't even communicate differently from their septmates. My hypersensitivity to stereos and construction vehicle noise wouldn't matter in the slightest if I lived in a quiet, pre-industrial farming village - in fact, I wouldn't even be aware I was "disabled" if I lived in a quieter society. In a zero-G space station, having usable legs would barely matter. In Africa, albinism is a death sentence both because of horrible skin cancer, and because some people hunt albinos for sport. In the U.S., albinism mostly just means you need to be careful of your eyes in direct sunlight. People with Autism are viewed as incapable of functioning in human society -- because every society is made by and for people without Autism. If Autistic people were the majority, I guarantee you that the non-Autistic folks would be at a profound disadvantage for all the mental feats which come easily to those with Autism and are very difficult for those without Autism. A Metis with three eyes or two tails is just odd-looking, and unable to blend into human or wolf society - they have no actual physical disadvantage. Having no fur doesn't really matter much if you live in a hot climate and are immune to diseases spread by bloodsucking parasites.

                Every single person has things they're good at, and things they're not good at. Whether the latter count as "disabilities" is based on what their society and environment expect or demand of them, not on some inherent inferiority. This is also a truth in evolution within any species: traits that are advantages in one environment can become maladaptive when circumstances change, and must be replaced with new adaptations for the species to keep thriving.

                As a disabled person, I grew up thinking of myself as a freak because of the way I am treated by most self-proclaimed "normal" humans. Then I grew up and realised there's nothing whatsoever "wrong" with me. ALL the problems I have in life are caused by discrimination rather than my "disability". I'm not "damaged" and my differences don't feel like a "curse" from God. The only people punishing me are mortal bigots. My "disabilities" give me amazing abilities that "normal" people don't have -- but they aren't advantages because "normal" people don't value or reward those abilities.

                I don't know how to translate this into mechanics, other than ensuring that the three Breeds are mechanically balanced. But it can certainly be conveyed in the writing (and is already in some Werewolf writing). It isn't disparaging to depict Garou contempt for the disabled because that contempt is a constant reality for real disabled people. It is, unfortunately, part of being disabled. It would only be unintentionally disparaging if the voices and accomplishments of Metis characters somehow disappeared from W5. I don't think any of the Metis deformities published so far make a character unplayable or unable to fight for Gaia, not even the lack of claws or weak immune system.


                I have my own theory for the nature and origin of the Metis in this setting, based partly on in-character opinions I read in Guardians of the Caerns, partly on the different outcomes of "inbreeding" in other Changing-Breeds, and partly on somebody's suggestion that if the shapeshifters didn't depend on their Kinfolk for breeding, they would have wiped out humanity and/or let their animal kin go extinct. I think that Gaia (or Luna and Helios) made every Changing-Breed dependent on their Kinfolk for breeding, not to prevent Metis from existing but to remind the shapeshifters that their Kinfolk are valuable. Perhaps the shapeshifters were created for the sake of the humans and animals, not the other way around? Gaia accomplishes this in different ways with different Changing-Breeds, though. Some have no conception, or a stillbirth, if two shifters breed. The Grondr had sterile, but non-deformed "Metis", who were an entire Auspice and necessary for the wereboars to do their duties. The Nagah have non-deformed, fertile Ahi, but a female Nagah can only birth one Ahi in her whole life. The Kitsune have fertile, non-deformed Shinju children, but normally one of the Kitsune parents dies at the child's birth.

                So Gaia (or Luna and Helios) told most shapeshifters not to inbreed. Then some Changing-Breeds (Garou, Bastet, Ajaba, Ratkin) either screwed each other for the very first time... or started making a habit of it. And I think the Metis were born as a result. Not as a "punishment" but as a warning. They're sterile because otherwise Kinfolk would no longer matter. But why are they "deformed"? There are possible explanations that don't require deformity to be the inherently bad thing most Garou think it is:
                1. Metis have two shifter parents, so they're "closer" to Gaia. Thus, Gaia's pain or injuries are written on their bodies in some way. This is only as "bad" and dishonorable as a battle scar. Maybe originally, the Metis weren't deformed, merely sterile. Maybe in previous ages, the deformities were more obviously not actually disadvantages, merely neutral differences.
                2. The Garou slaughtered the other children of Gaia because they served Her in a different way from the Garou, and they could not tolerate a different way of living. They still fight each other over Tribal differences. The Metis are there to remind or teach the Garou that even someone who is "different" from you is still Gaia's child and chosen. Until the Garou learn to accept their own dang children as equals, they won't learn to accept the other Changing-Breeds. This goes for the Bastet, Ajaba, and Ratkin too, who have plenty of racist attitudes themselves.
                3. The Metis are closer to Gaia, and/or created to help fix new problems that have arisen. They have to pay a price of some kind for their amazing kickass Gifts, otherwise they'd take over and shapeshifter societies would become unbalanced. So far, only the Bastet Metis have the kind of Gifts everyone else envies, but that could be changed. Ajaba Metis also have a uniquely useful position in their Breed's society, as the middle between the extreme personalities of males and females.

                I do think it would help to make spontaneous metis clearly different from real metis. There is a problem with the message that "wrong" breeding, and/or disability, is being associated with pollution and radiation poisoning. Even though pollution and radiation poisoning can cause physical birth "defects", this isn't a message I like to see.
