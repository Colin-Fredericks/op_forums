    Rank 5 Fianna Gifts

    Call the Hunt*: Spend a Gnosis, roll Charisma + Occult (difficulty 8). If you succeed the Huntsman shows up, along with a single hound. Booooo. But you can spend additional Gnosis and Rage to add more Hounds! So with a Gnosis and Rage of 5, you could potentially get 10 hounds.

    Huntsman
    W10 R10 G5 E40
    Armour, Tracking

    Hounds
    W6 R7 G2 E18
    Tracking

    Notice something? Low Gnosis. Gnosis is Perception and Wits. I would, if I were making this guy, swap the Rage and the Gnosis. He also lacks any form of ranged attack, weirdly. A Water Elemental is a better hunter than this dude, with its Gnosis of 10, which improves its Airt Sense, which is what you need to best follow the tracks of spirits and find the way into their realms. Yes, Tracking does let you unerringly hunt your target with a point of Essence, but many of the tricks and traps which smart prey will put down to mess with a hunter would be sensed with Perception or Wits. Poor Airt Sense may mean that while you can easily find your target, you enter the Realm they are in through a poor choice of entrance and find yourself having to fight a lot of enemies to reach them.

    Notably if you summon this guy and he doesn't think your reason for calling him is good enough (the target isn't evil enough, or strong enough) he will hunt you instead. Honestly if I were a Fianna Elder I would boast at a Moot that I could escape The Hunter himself, and then summon him, and hide from his awful Gnosis 5 (though of course this requires inventiveness due to the Tracking Charm). Great way for a Ragabash to farm some Renown, but whatever.

    As written this is unfortunately not as good as the designers probably intended. The Huntsman is a decent combat summon with his physical stats set to 10, but an ally that has 10 to hit and 10 to damage is equivalent to a Dexterity 5 Melee 5 Strength 5 ally holding a giant sword. Nothing to sniff at, but hardly a gamechanger at Rank 5. Yes his soak and his massive Essence pool are great, absolutely, and with Armour his soak will be even better, but anyone outside of his melee range has nothing to fear from him.

    How about his Hounds? They're fast and they're quite mean too. I would wager that many powerful 'boss' enemies could be overwhelmed if you brought a huge number of Hounds, simply because you would completely overwhelm the action economy... so this Gift does have potential to be good.

    All in all if you burn a huge amount of resources on this, and you pass the test, and you're facing problems where a huge mob of spirits can fix them... great! But I would argue that there are Level 4 Gifts which do the same thing but better. There is really nothing stopping you from spending the same Gnosis to summon 5 Fire Elementals! Though that would require five rolls.

    There's also a question of whether you'd be better off not learning this Gift at all, and simply using a Rite to summon the Huntsman, who automatically comes with 9, or a multiple of 9, hounds! I'm gonna call this a wash and say Amber with an asterisk.

    Fog on the Moor: This Gift lets you take on an intangible, ghostly form which can pass through anything but silver. Silver attacks can harm you, but nothing else can. You can't regenerate in this form, and you can pass through objects. Spend a Willpower, roll Gnosis at difficulty 7 (I would rewrite it as local Gauntlet, but sure), and instantly assume a ghostly form. You stay in this form for one one turn per success.

    It only costs a point of Willpower, and the roll to do it is doable. A break of a few turns in which you can flee, drop to a lower floor, run through walls, shout the evidence you want to give without being dragged away, is great. But the result being measured in turns is bad. Children of Gaia get a 'nobody can hit me' Gift at Rank 4, for example. The possibility that you will fail the roll is a little worrying at difficulty 7, not super likely, but a reasonable thing to worry about.

    This is a great option for infiltration, for assassinations, for thieving, for surviving insane explosive gambits, for escaping a huge attack... but like many Gifts it would be far more fitting to its Rank if you could apply it to your Pack for some extra cost. I think this one provides a strong enough effect that it deserves Green, after all it isn't that much more expensive than a Level 4 Gift to which it is equivalent, but it's still a little lean for its level.

    Gift of the Spriggan: Spend a Gnosis and roll Stamina + Primal-Urge, difficulty 8. On a success you shrink to the size of a small puppy or increase your size up to 3x. If you shrink your stats stay the same, including the hilarious potential for being a puppy-sized Crinos. If you grow you gain +3 Strength (or 'strength dice', which I think means the Attribute goes up, but they could just mean +3 dice to rolls based on Strength) for doubling in size, and +6 Strength for tripling in size.

    If your Crinos Strength is 9, increasing it to 15 is pretty great! You become a huge target, but presumably if you're going for this option you're happy with that. Anyways, with a Strength of 15 you can 'hurl a pickup truck', step through walls, destroy buildings with relative ease, and so on. This is the mother of all Veil breaches, but I like it. Lifting 6000lbs (nearly 3000kg) ain't bad either.

    This one is fun and useful enough to get Green, though you do run the risk of drawing the attention of the most powerful spirit in the scene and getting hit with a Blast which just outright kills you. The biggest Werewolf in the fight draws the most attention. Arguably the tiny option, for stealth and surprise suplexes, is far better.

        Rank 5 & 6 Get of Fenris Gifts

        Endurance of Heimdall: Spend 2 Gnosis, roll Willpower (difficulty 6), and if you're successful you double your Stamina for the scene. In Crinos with high base Stamina this could be huge, and there's no reason it wouldn't stack with other Stamina boosts. Part of the problem with Werewolf enemies is that you start running into foes who deal 15 dice of Aggravated damage, meaning that on average having 8 dice of soak against Aggravated still leaves you with an attack which could kill you in one, and so dodging becomes your main option. But if you have a Soak of 16 instead of 8, now you're looking at a much better average outcome, possibly no damage at all!

        Not super inventive or exciting, but very good. You could easily just delete the Willpower roll.

        Horde of Valhalla*: Fenris' Wolves are mechanically identical to the Hounds we see above. And those aren't THAT great. But you spend as much Gnosis and Rage as you like, say 10 points, and you make a difficulty 6 roll, and if you pass then you get a number of wolves equal to what you spent. So perhaps 10+ mighty wolves of Fenris manifesting. The fact that you risk wasting a HUGE amount of Gnosis and Rage annoys me, and the summons just... aren't that great! But there absolutely are battles where overwhelming your enemy with a crowd of summons will massively swing things in your favour, and there's nothing stopping AOE buffs from applying to these spirits.

        The fact that you could have 10 temporary Rage when you use this, and spend 1 Gnosis on it, to get 11 hounds, and then recover your Rage and go again... means I have to give this Green. But just remember that the summoned spirits aren't THAT great, their strength is their numbers, and their ability to track foes when you need to. WoD doesn't really have rules for being forced to engage certain enemies, so whether swarming your foe with weaker threats will make a difference is down to the ST. In theatre of the mind it's very easy to hear descriptions of a NPC just 'finding their way' to where they need to go, since without the strictures of a battle map there isn't anything blocking their concrete, defined list of possible paths. So, buyer beware.

        Fenris' Bite: Strength + Primal-Urge, difficulty of the target's Stamina +3, and on a success if your next bite attack hits it inflicts 3 automatic, unsoakable levels of Aggravated damage. This is roughly equivalent to adding +6 to your Strength or your damage dice pool. This also mangles one of their limbs! Great. If you get 5 successes on the initial test to activate the Gift it severs the limb instead.

        A limb mangled with this effect is useless until healed; if you hit a Garou with this twice they are completely out of claw and melee options and will be stuck trying to claw you with their hind legs or biting you. Hit anything running on four legs with this and you cripple their movement, and a humanoid creature will be very slow with one leg mangled.

        Great, multi-use, and it's boosting your damage output significantly if you get it to work. But a difficulty of Stamina +3 does mean that this is going to be most useful against enemies you could kill in one hit anyways. Think about it. A BSD in Crinos will have a Stamina of 4-8. That's a difficulty of 7+, immediately. If you ambush a target in Homid this Gift could be great, but a powerful Garou enemy who is in open battle with you will be nearly impossible to use this Gift on, as a base Stamina of 3, +3 from Crinos, +3 from this Gift, gives a difficulty of 9 already. Even with your Strength boosted by Crinos this Gift isn't likely to go off.

        So you become absolutely peerless at ripping limbs off of... weak enemies. Hmm. And by spending that Rage, you're doing that instead of a whole bonus attack. This Gift is far from useless, don't get me wrong, and if you face Spirits with a really low Willpower it's great, but it's... sadly not quite there. A fixed difficulty would be much better.

        Call Great Fenris*: This is excellent. You make a difficulty 6 roll (Stamina + Occult, easy), spend a single point of Gnosis, and Great Fenris shows up to fight for you. His stats are absolutely superb and he debuffs every spirit opponent he faces by 2 dice. His to-hit is 15, his damage is 22 base, and with a Rage of 20 he has strength far beyond any individual Garou. This thing could probably crack right through a thick steel wall.

        There is a problem however. If he isn't happy with what you've summoned him for, he eats you. Fine. But worse, even if he's happy, he takes payment in the form of one of your hands before leaving. This is where things get weird.

        Can you heal the battle scar? Mother's Touch allows that, but it feels disrespectful to the Totem. Can you replace your hand with a cybernetic techno-Fetish? Well that will depend on your allies. Missing one of your hands is huge, especially for a Lupus, since now you're hobbling around on 3 limbs instead of 4. On the assumption that you have some good way of handling the loss, whether it's healing or replacing the limb, I'll mark this down as a Green, but it definitely needs an asterisk.

            Rank 5 Glass Walker Gifts

            Chaos Mechanics: This Gift lets you passively use Rage and Gnosis in the same turn with no penalties. So, so many Gifts become better with this Gift. So many Gifts are bad purely because spending Gnosis means you can't spend Rage in the same turn. Now you are no longer held back by that restriction! It's an amazing boost to your abilities. For just one example, with this Gift you could use Signal Rider to instantly arrive at someone's location using their mobile phone, and then take a bunch of Rage bonus actions at the end of the round to kill everyone in the room.

            The utility of Fetish weapons also increases massively with this ability. A Fetish which requires a Gnosis roll to activate its special effect is balanced mostly by the fact that you can't swing it a bunch of times a turn using Rage. Not any more! Arguably the best Gift in all of WTA, though it would be competing with things like Sense Wyrm and Mother's Touch.

            Summon Net-Spider: Once you've used this Gift, even once, your difficulties for checks relating to building, using and hacking computers is halved. So a difficulty 10 test would become difficulty 5.

            Anyways, for this Gift you spend a Gnosis and roll Charisma + Computer (difficulty 8), a Net-Spider appears, and it follows your instructions. Their powers are described as pretty immense, disrupting or destroying almost any system. An obvious Green, easy.

                Rank 5 & 6 Red Talon Gifts

                Curse of Lycaon: This Gift can force a Garou into Lupus form, or turn any human or animal into a wolf permanently. You simply spend a single Gnosis and roll against a difficulty of the target's Willpower.

                Someday I'm going to play a VTM PC who got hit with this power and is masquerading as the Wolf Ghoul of another PC. Imagine realizing that this actual factual wolf is Kindred/Cainite and thinking that someone embraced a wolf somehow. But, do note that the Gift says "normal" wolf.

                The utility of this Gift is insane. It feels like a Level 6 Gift. Many many foes the Garou face simply aren't a threat if they become a wolf. Sure, they'll try to bite and claw at you, but that might be much less dangerous to Gaia than their stock portfolio and investment strategy was. Given that wolves are almost extinct in many places, threatened in many others, an ability which lets you repopulate wolves by glaring ominously at foxes is pretty great!

                There are a million stories you can tell with this Gift, and it feels like it should be mentioned more in the stories of the setting! If ANY Red Talon Elder can use this Gift, I just can't see how they're worried about wolves dying out. Kinfolk? Absolutely. But wolves? It also feels like this Gift could be very useful for any Tribe who wants to fortify the Lupus side of their bloodline. A human Kinfolk could be turned into a wolf, and presumably they'd remain Kinfolk, though the Gift does say "normal" of course. Ethics aside this could massively bolster the likelihood of the Tribe producing Pure Breed Lupus, or at least give them more wolves to work with.

                Hell, a Red Talon who fell in love with a human could petition an Elder to turn them into a wolf. The potential for drama is great!

                Gaia's Vengeance: They didn't bother writing this one. Unrated.

                Scabwalker Curse: Snarl at a target within a short range, spend a Gnosis, roll Manipulation + Survival (difficulty 7). For one day per success the target will take a level of Bashing damage for every turn they are in contact with a synthetic or artificial material. This Gift is absolutely a death sentence against humans and some supernaturals, but the damage can be soaked, so with a Stamina of 2 the target will be taking 1 level of Bashing every other turn on average, so it may take them 14ish turns to die. Given that the duration is measured in days, 14 turns is nothing, but it's a long time to take to kill a single human.

                This Gift could absolutely be a huge bother to a Vampire, Mage, Changeling and so on. They'll have more options to deal with it in terms of better soak, access to healing, and places to escape to.

                The thing is that at Rank 5 I'm not particularly excited about a new way to be really, REALLY mean to someone who isn't a threat to me already. Yeah you can use this on a real threat, like a powerful Fomori or Garou, but is this actually going to kill them? Really? Anything with healing or regen will be fine, anything capable of escaping into the Umbra will be fine, and so on. Against a real threat this power is just an annoyance, to the table as a whole. "Oh yeah, remember he has to roll to soak- oh he soaked it again, fine."

                I would rebuild this Gift so that the allergic reaction to Weaver tools manifests as dice and/or difficulty penalties, which are going to be more relevant than this weird normal-person-killing ability. Yes, this power does let you kill someone in a way which can never be proven, but if you're a Rank 5 Garou you aren't desperately looking for ways to get away with murdering someone, any Rank 3 Ragabash worth their salt can kill someone and make it look like an accident.

                Shield of Gaia*: Choose something within the realm of the Weaver, and become fully immune to it. The examples given are bullets, photography, electricity, chemical toxins. This is a perfectly well written Gift, but very hard to rate. Chances are you'll pick something being used by a major antagonist, or by the faction you are hunting down and destroying. This Gift could be the source of a big legend about a Rank 6 Red Talon, so it's an exercise in creativity and shared Storytelling.

                This stays unrated with an asterisk, but that's simply because it is in fact 100s of different Gifts.

                    Rank 5 Shadow Lord Gifts

                    Obedience*: Spend a Gnosis and roll Charisma + Leadership. Everyone present rolls Willpower. Both rolls are difficulty 8. If you win by 1 success, everyone in the vicinity has to follow orders which don't inconvenience them. If you win by 3 successes, everyone treats you loyally as if you were their Alpha. If you win by 5 success they'll do virtually anything including things which are practically suicidal. The effects last for a full day.

                    Now, imagine you stack this Gift. Pure Breed. Merits which reduce the difficulty. A fitting Leadership specialty. You've buffed up your Charisma with Beyond Human. Ancestors setup. You might be rolling 15+ dice at a difficulty of 5-6 against a crowd of allies and enemies who are rolling 4-8 dice at a difficulty of 8.

                    Basically, if you maximize the potential of this Gift, getting 5+ successes is very believable. The potential to get everyone in a huge battle to obey all your orders is hilarious but also does, sadly, break the story. At its best this Gift is so good that it's bad, annoyingly, and there is nothing stopping it from applying to outright lethal enemies. I would edit it to say that those who are full-out enemies of the user of the Gift can spend a single point of Willpower to ignore the effects, which makes the Gift become much more about inspiring obedience in your Sept, or among rivals.

                    This is so swingy and dependent on your setup, Merits, Backgrounds, and a bit too underwritten, so I'm leaving it unrated with an asterisk. If it were ran RAW it would be Green, with the right setup, but potentially straying into "too good, so it's bad" territory.

                    Shadow Pack: This Gift lets you summon a small army of shadows, which is a bit anime, though I'm not an expert. You roll Gnosis, difficulty 8, only needing one success. For every point of Gnosis you spend, you create a shadowy duplicate of yourself. They have your Attributes and Abilities but can't use Gifts, Fetishes, Gnosis, Willpower or Rage. They have one health level.

                    Let's imagine you're a beast of an Ahroun. 5 in all physicals. You pour 5 Gnosis into this, summoning 5 duplicates. You do so in Crinos, so they have Strength 9, Dexterity 6, Stamina 8. They have Aggravated damage claws and fangs. They have a dodge pool of 6 + Athletics, and a Soak of 8, which will stop random weak attacks from banishing them.

                    Best case scenario, they all stay alive, and you have an additional 5 attacks each round. That's pretty great! One of you could keep a foe grappled while the other 5 go crazy attacking as fast as they can. The difficulty of 8 on the Gnosis roll is very annoying, however, so I worry a little about wasting all that Gnosis! With a Gnosis of 5 your odds of succeeding are only 60%, so 40% of the time you'll waste all of the Gnosis you're trying to spend. Even with a Gnosis of 10 your odds are below 80%.

                    Regardless, the duplicates last for a scene. I really struggle to rate this one because the difficulty of 8 means it simply isn't a safe place to pour so much Gnosis into. The difference between difficulty 6 and 8 is huge, the gap is far bigger than the devs ever seem to realize! And even if you summon 5+ shadows, a single point of damage hitting the whole room bursts them all, which is very achievable for high level threats.

                    I must, with great sadness, give this an Amber.

                        Rank 5 Silent Strider Gifts

                        Gate of the Moon*: Spend 1 Gnosis per 100 miles you need to travel, roll Intelligence + Alertness, difficulty determined by ST, and on a single success you instantly arrive.

                        While you can't bring your allies with you, I like this enough that it redeems the Silent Strider list. It also makes those really DUMB run for hours Gifts entirely pointless. This is just a huge upgrade on those. With this Gift you can appear inside your Caern, yell that your pack needs urgent help at a certain location, then teleport back to join your allies. You can move from Caern to Caern with messages. You can appear in front of a BSD pack to give them a warning before disappearing to rejoin your pack. It's great.

                        The only thing that should give us pause is that the Gift's cost adds up, even if you're jumping within 100 miles every time. If your Chronicle has very poor access to Gnosis recovery, then the * may drop this down to Amber, since the travel becomes much less convenient to use.

                        Reach the Umbra: If I were rating these on names, this would be a weak one. Along with all of the "X of Gaia" ones.

                        You can instantly step sideways with no roll. If you need to roll for some reason, presumably as part of a Gift or Rite, or leading a pack across the Gauntlet, you get a -2 difficulty. You annoyingly can't spend Rage on the same turn you step sideways using this Gift, and you also can't use this ability to dodge. That said, being able to do this is amazing. You can simply blink between the two layers of reality at will, which is a huge QOL upgrade, and exactly what I want Gifts to do/be. Stepping Sideways is core to Garou, this improves on that, which is great.

                            Rank 5 & 6 Silver Fang Gifts

                            Luna's Avenger: Spend a Gnosis and concentrate for a full turn, your whole body turns into silver. This grants a few benefits:

                            - +2 Stamina
                            - +1 health level
                            - all of your attacks are treated as being silver
                            - you suffer none of the negative effects of silver

                            Given that this costs a single point of Gnosis and there's no roll, and that it lasts for a whole scene... this is pretty great. It's especially good against Garou and hunters of Garou, and against silver-vulnerable Fera. But in all circumstances the +2 Stamina and additional health level are pretty great.

                            Socially speaking, this Gift is a bit of a crowning ability for a Garou to have. For any Garou, to see a so-called King turn into a being of living silver would be terrifying and awe-inspiring; you can understand why many would believe that such a creature was a born ruler, especially with 4-5 points of Pure Breed backing it up.

                            Paws of the Newborn Cub: Metis stay winning! This Gift turns you into your natural form with no special Gifts or supernatural tricks. The duration is one turn per success, on a Gnosis roll, difficulty of the target's Willpower. So say a Rank 5 Silver Fang were fighting my Rank 1 Metis Child of Gaia. They spend 2 Gnosis, a big expense, roll Gnosis at a difficulty of 8, and then say they get 1 success. He's stuck with no Gifts, no regeneration, for one turn, and that's... pretty much it. No stepping sideways, probably no Fetish uses, and his claws might deal Lethal instead of Aggravated, but they might stay Lethal. Same question with bites. When in reality, chances are it would be far better to just tear him apart with Rage actions.

                            All things considered this Gift isn't... great. The cost is extreme, and it's Gnosis. Two points. You roll Gnosis, always a bad pool compared to other options, and the difficulty is WP. WP is high in enemies worth disabling. And a duration of one turn per success really sucks.

                            If this cost a single point of Gnosis and on success the target lost all their supernatural abilities and tricks for three turns per success, now we'd be talking! But all things considered this is very meh. Yes, you could stop a 4th Generation Vampire from using their terrible powers, but if you're face to face with them you'd be better off just clawing them to death. That said, perhaps their Dominate or Presence stops that. I get it.

                            Amber, simply because in extreme circumstances it would be hugely beneficial. But it isn't a GREAT Gift.

                            Renew the Cycle: You know how Paws of the Newborn might be useful simply because you might find yourself fighting an ancient vampire you otherwise couldn't harm?

                            Well, that. This Gift is bad. Say you're dealing with a 1000 year old Vampire, which is certainly powerful enough to be a threat to Elder Garou. Why waste 10 Gnosis and a difficulty 8 Gnosis vs Willpower on attempting to turn him to dust when you could just make him helpless with Paws.

                            This is extremely niche for a Level 6 Gift, which is fine, but it should be EXCEPTIONAL within that niche. The time scaling is also off. The Vampires who will be the biggest threats to Garou will be too old for this Gift to touch. More to the point, why isn't this a Silent Strider Gift? If you ever happened to be playing a Rank 6 Silver Fang, you'd probably be better off learning a Level 6 Gift for your Auspice.

Like many Level 5 Gifts this feels like an ability which a sensible Elder will almost never use since it takes a nuke to the problem.

                                There are some concrete mechanics in WoD Combat of all places, but they're kind of underwhelming.                                     Rank 5 Stargazer Gifts

                                    Circular Attack: By spending a WP and rolling Wits + Athletics, difficulty of the highest Wits of your opponents +3 (e.g. if there is a Wits 5 enemy, difficulty 8), you can dodge or redirect a number of attacks equal to your successes this turn. On the turn you use this Gift you can't spend Rage for bonus actions.

                                    This is a pretty neat Gift for use against physical enemies; a difficulty of 8 is achievable, and redirecting a swing of a Grand Klaive to hit a different enemy is great. At this level of play you'll be facing enemies who could theoretically one-shot you, so getting them to hit their allies instead is great.

                                    That said, this Gift is terrible against any Spirits with decent Gnosis. Gnosis = Wits, so a Gnosis of 7 makes this Gift difficulty 10 to use. If I were rewriting it I would specify that against spiritual enemies you half their Gnosis, rounding up, then add +3 (e.g. Gnosis 10 becomes Difficulty 8). Basically if the pack attacking you has a single high-Gnosis spirit helping them, this Gift doesn't work as intended.

                                    All in all I'm happy to call it Amber; it's amazing in the situations where it works, but a passive ability where you can redirect attacks which you dodge would be much much better.

                                    Harmonious Unity of the Emerald Mother: Lupus get ignored once again. This Gift makes it so that you get all of the benefits of Crinos in Homid. Not in Glabro, not in Lupus, just Homid. This Gift lasts for one day and costs a point of Gnosis. How good is having regeneration in Homid? Well, Metis and Lupus get it for free as Rank 0 Cubs/Ronin. How good is having +4 Strength, +1 Dexterity and +3 Stamina in Homid? Vampires can do this at baseline by spending Vitae for Blood Buff, though it costs them a lot of course. It's neat, but is it THAT good? It's helpful when you're stuck in Homid, sure. And if you go Glabro the benefits vanish, you get weaker for some reason.

                                    Sadly Red, but easily rewritten to be Green. Make it permanent, passive, and apply in all forms. Hell, expand it to include the senses of Lupus, so that you have the best of all your forms in every form. Just go "You have Strength +4, Dexterity +2 and Stamina +3 in all forms, Regeneration in all forms, you can soak Aggravated damage in all forms, and you subtract 2 from the difficulties of Perception checks in all forms." Very strong, but it speaks to the Stargazer wisdom of the divisions between the forms being illusory.

                                    Wisdom of the Seer*: I'll be honest, I hate this kind of ability. For starters, there are already Gifts, Rites and Backgrounds which pull this off. But more to the point, "honest, yet vague and symbolic" is going to vary wildly by your ST.

                                    Spend a Gnosis, roll Intelligence + Enigmas (difficulty 7), more successes means more clarity in the answer. I struggle to imagine how questions like "are my pack alive?" would go, since I don't know how to give an "honest, yet vague and symbolic" answer to that. The flower of the wolf yet blooms? To which the player goes "... what?" and I go "yeah they're alive". It just comes off as pretentious!

                                    If it's information the ST wants you to have, it will come to you in dreams and messages from spirits. If it's information that will be useful but you don't need for the plot to work, this power could be helpful. The problem is that this ability is so vague, and arguably so redundant in a game full of spirit dreams and Rites and related Backgrounds and Merits and Gifts etc. Leaving it unrated, I imagine it would often be Red or Amber, but with an overly permissive ST it could be Green.

                                        Rank 5 Elder Brother Gifts

                                        Fabric of the Mind: See the Galliard list. A mess of a Gift, but if you run it as it is written, it'll probably work out as Red.

                                        Fetish Doll: Spend a week building and enchanting a doll. This requires a Perception + Crafts (difficulty 8) roll. You also need something from your intended victim, or something belonging to them. You must then roll Intelligence + Medicine, difficulty equal to the target's Willpower. Every success inflicts a level of Aggravated damage, which is still soakable. After 10 successes the doll stops working.

                                        So, how good is this? Well, it's an absolutely guaranteed way to murder almost any mortal. Untraceable too. It will work on most supernaturals; even a Vampire of immense power might have no Fortitude, or a low value. Against Garou you run up against their soak, which will be impressive in Crinos, but there's every chance you could kill them in short order.

                                        What's neat is that even with an enemy with a Willpower of 10, you'll eventually inflict 10 levels of damage though if you deal them 1-2 points at a time it'll be much easier to soak,. Even if it takes a lot of time and effort and accidentally stabbing your thumb. Against my boy it would be a difficulty 8 roll, so 10 dice would average 2-3 successes each go. In Homid form he'd soak 2-3 on average with a Stamina of 5, and during the attack he'd likely shift into Crinos to better protect himself against this invisible attacker, at which point his Stamina would shoot up to 8, soaking 3-4 points on average.

                                        Basically this Gift is very neat, but you won't kill even a Rank 1 Garou with it, unless they have very bad Stamina, or they're already injured, or their soak is somehow compromised, or they're incapable of shifting into Crinos to protect themselves, and so on. So the best time to use this against a Garou is to play dirty. Injure a rival mid battle. Injure a rival mid challenge. Injure a rival at the moment one of their packmates punches them. Injure a rival while he's drinking something so he thinks someone has tried to poison him, or if he dies the blame will go to someone involved with the drink.

                                        I'm going to go ahead and give this a Green. It's a faff to set up, but the results are incredible, and they reward patience as well as forward planning. This ability is a great seed for plots, for example an Elder Brother promising a Kinfolk of the Fianna that she will win the heart of the man she loves, a young hero of her tribe, and that she could make her a good luck charm to help with it, if she had a piece of his hair, fur, perhaps a keepsake of his. With this Gift you can make people unwitting accomplices in murder, and I imagine a few Garou who helped to free Banes or steal Caerns in the Pure Lands probably wound up victims to this Gift in the end.

                                            Rank 5 Younger Brother Gifts

                                            Invoke the Spirits of the Storm: This power seems to allow you to summon any weather effect relating to water and air, so fog and tornadoes, blizzards, sure, but you can't bring a heat wave for example. Spend a Gnosis, roll Willpower, difficulty is based by how severe the change is, so a sunny day turning to a terrible blizzard might be difficulty 9 or 10, depending on your ST. For every success the weather effect covers 10 miles. If you call for a thunderstorm you can deal damage by spending Gnosis, Wits + Occult to hit, dealing 10 dice of Aggravated damage.

                                            So, how good is a ranged attack of Wits + Occult which hits for 10 dice of Aggravated? It isn't bad at all, but using the Gift at all costs 1 Gnosis, and then the attack is 1 Gnosis a go. And you can change the weather with the help of an air elemental, which any Theurge or Elder Brother would be able to call upon.

                                            As for the general weather changes, you already have a pretty great weather-changing Gift, Chill of Early Frost, which hits all furless creatures with a nasty two dice debuff. All in all this is... well it's pretty mediocre. Perhaps you stand a lot to gain by killing huge numbers of people indiscriminately, as you would do with a surprise blizzard or tornado, but I can't see why unless you were serving the Wyrm, or extremely misled in your ways. I'll call this Red and suggest you just invoke the help of an Air Elemental if you really need school called off or the roads closed. At Rank 5 you will have Theurges subordinate to you, or a friendly relationship with at least one Elder Brother.

                                            Heart of Ice: So there are a lot of Aggravated damage mystical ranged attack Gifts in W20, but this one is sorta special since the damage is unsoakable. Now, the Blast Charm is better, sure, but not everyone is going to be summoning spiritual allies into battle, so considering this as a Gift it isn't bad at all.

                                            You spend a Gnosis, whisper the target's name into the wind (does there have to be wind?) and roll Wits + Occult against a target of the target's Willpower. For every success you inflict a level of unsoakable Aggravated damage, but the damage comes one turn at a time. So with 7 successes you'd inflict 7 Aggravated, over 7 turns, 1 per turn.

                                            What this means is that the power can be a death sentence, especially against someone who is already injured, but if they have a means of regenerating Aggravated damage (very rare) or healing it (less rare, but still rare) they might be fine.

                                            The power is perfectly fine. Enemies who are worth slowly freezing from the inside out will typically have pretty good Willpower however. Call it Amber; I wouldn't take it unless I was in a game with a bunch of failson Elders who have been elevated through circumstance and privilege and whose actual WP is shockingly low, at which point this Gift would be pretty great.

                                                Skin Dancer Gifts

                                                Mostly irrelevant to PCs, but worth covering for completion.

                                                See Past The Skin (1): Roll Gnosis, no cost, on a success you can recognize other Skin Dancers by looking at them. Extremely situational but handy for finding others of your kind.

                                                Mask Taint (5): Spend a Gnosis, roll Perception + Subterfuge (difficulty 8), suppress all of your detectable Wyrm taint for one scene per success. Comes far too late. And what Skin Dancer hits Elder Rank anyways? The high difficulty also makes this pretty mediocre.

                                                Boli Zousizhe Gifts

                                                Technically any Glass Walker could learn these Gifts, so they may be more relevant. The BZ are the Asian branch of the Glass Walkers, and apparently they are more traditional. I'm not enough of an expert to comment on execution of Asian content in WoD, so I'd welcome input from someone who knows what they're talking about.

                                                Sheng-Nong's Eyes (1): This Gift is a little confusing; it's intended to help you to work with two tools at once, including your own hands, granting you the focus for this task. Mechanically you spend a Gnosis to get a bonus action, either for the turn, or for the scene. Presumably for the turn. In addition, for the rest of the scene you can ignore visual and off-hand penalties, so you could strike a near-invisible enemy without penalties and manage twin blades.

                                                This one is... confusing. The wording needs much more clarity. Assuming it does the following:

                                                - On the turn you activate it you get an additional action
                                                - For the rest of the scene you ignore visual and offhand penalties relating to attacking

                                                Then it is... Amber? Let's say Amber.

                                                Fu Xi's Honour (2): If the ST agrees that a member of your character's family or pack is helpless to defend themselves, you may use this Gift. In this situation you may spend a single point of Rage, and add your Honour rating to your Physical Attributes for the duration of the Scene.

                                                This is busted beyond belief. +1 to Strength, Dexterity and Stamina for a Scene is great. +10, which is perfectly achievable in a long campaign even if your Rank is slow to rise to match your Honour, is one of the most powerful Gifts in the game. Green, but so great it's busted. My "much honour upon you, in this honourable family, of honour" senses are also tingling, but again, I'm not an expert on Asian folklore and perhaps this Gift is fine. It just feels like a bad parody, to me a random English dude, that the Gift where you get a buff from your Honour stat is the Asian Werewolf Gift.

                                                Yao's Commands (3): See Elemental Favour. The Gift isn't playable in its written form.

                                                Yu's Endurance (4): See Heart of the Mountain in the Get of Fenris list. Simply adding this to the Glass Walker list is... interesting. Even if you have to go on a trip to Asia to justify it for some reason.

                                                Huang Di's Sacrifice (5)*: Spend 2 Gnosis and roll Stamina + Leadership (difficulty 8). For every success a packmate can heal (successes) damage, including Aggravated. So for example if you lucked out and get 5 successes, your 5 packmates could each heal 5 damage, but with 2 successes you could have 2 packmates heal for 2 damage. If you stack this with difficulty reductions, Crinos Stamina boost, or hell if for some reason you have +10 to your Stamina from being very honourable then this Gift is much better.

                                                On the assumption you stack the roll I'll call this Green, with an asterisk.

                                                    Kucha Ekundu Gifts

                                                    Similar disclaimer; my knowledge of African folklore is limited. These Gifts are learnt by the Kucha Ekundu, who are an offshoot of the Red Talons, and whose animal Kinfolk are the African Hunting Dogs. RAW playing a KE is mechanically directly superior to playing a RT, and realistically you're going to have fewer people going "oh a Red Talon, I know what THOSE are like", so I consider it a win-win. In character you'll have people going "... what? You're dogs?". African Hunting Dogs are misnamed, they're not very closely related to either dogs or wolves, so the interbreeding is Spirit Magic of course.

                                                    Speed of Thought (1): See Silent Strider list.

                                                    Feed the Pack (2): This is interesting. This Gift is better, in my opinion, than the Bone Gnawer, Red Talon and Lupus Gifts with similar deals. Instead of a Gift which helps you find food, this is a Gift which allows you to eat on behalf of others while they sit at home in safety. It's still very niche and IMHO more suited to a Level 1 Gift, but it's a step up from "you can find lots of food". Before you eat something you can roll your Gnosis, difficulty 6 for packmates, difficulty 7 for kinfolk, and on a success you can grant the nourishment of what you're consuming to one other person.

                                                    Now while this Gift is niche, I do think that the ability to keep your packmate who got stuck in The Abyss or Malfeas or who is running non-stop to deliver a message is... handy. It's good enough that I can convince myself that it's Amber, partly for inventiveness. If you have a Ragabash packmate they can always go on hunger strike for a whole month to impress people with their fortitude, or set a formal Challenge of not having any food or drink pass their lips, which this Gift doesn't break. Ultimately there are enough uses you can find for this Gift that I'll give it a pass.

                                                    Predator's Many Eyes (3)*: This Gift is like Territory, but instead of urinating to mark a territory, you mark an animal (Fera, and presumably Garou, are valid targets, with their consent) simply by looking at them. Charisma + Animal Ken, difficulty 7 is used, and the range is sight, which is pretty great. Thereafter you can see through the target's eyes.

                                                    IMO this Gift is best used in two ways:

                                                    - you can currently see an animal, right now, and you want to get its POV, for example you see a bird flying around and you want its bird's eye view; you might return to using the POV later, but your main intent with using the Gift is to check things out from their angle now
                                                    - applying it to several shapechangers, with their consent, so that you can flip through their POVs (i.e. you can see through the eyes of the other PCs, or through members of a few packs)

                                                    Territory letting you simply cast your senses to a location is sometimes superior since with this Gift you might be stuck looking at something boring, or the animal could be asleep! The limitation to predators only is also possibly a bit restrictive. So this Gift is possibly worse at building a network of places you can check like supernatural CCTV. With that in mind there's a decent argument for getting both Predator's Many Eyes and Territory, since Kucha Ekundu technically get both in their Tribal List.

                                                    * Do keep in mind that while this Gift is, in my opinion, pretty clear if you read it carefully, there are also contradictions between its text and the text of Territory. I would argue that these contradictions aren't a problem since the Gift says "in all other respects, as Territory", so if it contradicts Territory, the contradiction is an explicit exception to the way that Gift typically works. That said the Gift could be written much more clearly.

                                                    Clenched Jaw (4): See the Ahroun list.

                                                    Crocodile Pact (5)*: This is a "call for powerful help" Gift, but a particularly cool and flavourful one with good lore. You spend 2 Gnosis, and you roll Charisma + Rituals (difficulty 7). With even one success, you get help within the hour. This could be Mokole from a local Clutch, or powerful Mokole Ancestor Spirits, or some other boon from the Umbra. For me this Gift has the edge because you only need one success, and the result you get is somewhat tailored to the problem. Rampaging Werecrocodiles for battles, ancestral wisdom for problems. There's also no side effect of "if the problem isn't big enough you get devoured by a giant crocodile", which is nice.

                                                    The * reflects that this Gift's utility will be somewhat hampered if there are no Mokole within an hour's journey of you, since the possible results of this Gift become much more limited.

                                                        Hakken Gifts

                                                        If you're a Japanese Shadow Lord you add these to your list. Again, there is no cost to doing this, beyond the buy-in of your character being from Japan and people perhaps mistrusting them as a foreigner to the Garou Nation.

                                                        Dream of a Thousand Cranes (1): You have to fold an origami crane to activate this Gift of good fortune, which takes a turn. When you do so, spend a Gnosis and roll Charisma + Occult, difficulty 6. Every success you get translates to a single die which you can add to other checks in the same scene.

                                                        Immediately this makes you a far better ritualist, crafter, and in fact anything where you roll for a project. In a PBP game, or a game with Downtime, this Gift would be a nightmare to balance. Yeah there's that one Philodox from the Hakken whose katana Fetishes are incredible, yeah his planning and crafting and Ritual rolls to make the sword and turn it into a Fetish get loads of bonus dice from folding cranes-

                                                        This Gift is great, but I wouldn't suggest using it in your games.

                                                        Fair Path (1)*: This Gift lets you wash yourself from battle by touching clean water, and on a successful activation with no cost you add +1 to your Appearance. You can think of this as a free (3xp) way to boost your Appearance by 1, with an activation. If your Appearance is 4, this is much cheaper than buying Appearance 5. Is it worth it? Well does Appearance matter in your games?

                                                        If you have a Gift or Ritual where you roll Appearance, or you're dealing with Garou/Supernaturals/Spirits where beauty and fashion are important... who knows. Let's call this Amber with a *, assuming that Appearance is at least somewhat useful (which is the RAW!) and you care enough about Appearance to pay 3xp to boost it.

                                                        Storm Winds Slash (2)*: Feels extremely cliche, but I like this, much like I enjoy the Gift which lets you stab a target's shadow. This Gift lets you use a melee weapon at great range by spending a Gnosis and rolling Dexterity + Melee difficulty 7. Notably some weapons are difficulty 7 anyways, like a massive greatsword, so this Gift is best with such heavy weapons. The Gift specifies that targets 'take lethal damage as normal for a melee strike' but doesn't make it clear whether you can inflict Aggravated damage if the weapon is a Fetish or similar.

                                                        Targets can dodge as normal.

                                                        Assuming you always deal an ordinary Lethal weapon strike, with no special 'rider' effects, this is a perfectly fine Amber. If we assume the Gift lets you strike at a distance with a Fetish weapon like a Klaive or Grand Klaive, it's Green.

                                                        Dark of Night (3): By spilling a vial of ink you can blind an enemy. Spend a Gnosis, roll Manipulation + Subterfuge (difficulty 6), resisted by the target's Gnosis if they have it (difficulty 6). Every success blinds the target for a whole hour.

                                                        Multiple hours of blindness. This Gift is incredible, even with a Gnosis cost and at level 3. You can gradually blind a whole pack of enemies. The effect is very safe, it isn't a claw strike to the eyes, it isn't acid, it's like mystical ink inside the eyes based on the flavour of the power, so blinding a spirit or Garou or mortal with this power won't cause permanent damage or any long term offense (unless they hold a grudge).

                                                        If you were planning to use this Gift, I'd suggest that the target should be blinded for two turns per success.

                                                        Living Treasure (4): This Gift can convince any object's spirit, whether it's a Fetish or something weirder or an ordinary object, to answer your questions.

                                                        Touch an object, roll Manipulation + Etiquette (difficulty 7), each success gets you a single answer. You can only use this Gift once per object, ever! No cost, however. This Gift is incredible for discovering lost lore of Fetishes and things like Faerie relics. If you were stood at the standing stones of some Garou sacred site you could ask them who put them there, for example, and they might answer 'the fae folk' or 'wolf-men with hair of red'. Pretty amazing for detective work.

                                                        Divine Wind (5): This Gift lets you summon a storm. There's too many of these.

                                                        It costs Rage only, which is nice, and you can spend a load of additional Rage to help boost the storm. Stamina + Enigmas (difficulty 7), every success and every point of Rage gives a radius of 1.6km. The storm lasts no more than a scene and is strong enough to uproot trees and throw cars around.

                                                        This, to be honest, sounds like it will damage wildlife and probably do more harm than good. But it might help you in an emergency, especially in a city, though wild destruction in a city is bad for nature too due to the environmental damage (spills, resources used up for rebuilding etc.).

                                                        I'm gonna call this Amber simply because it doesn't have any 'rider' effect which would help make it useful.
