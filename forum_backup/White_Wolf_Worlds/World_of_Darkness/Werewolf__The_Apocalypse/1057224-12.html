                                                Admittedly Garou becoming Garou by more pop-culture interpretations of werewolves is interesting, but I don't really see how it makes the game fit more into the WoD. We already have a supernatural creature like that, and they're vampires (and despite attempts to show multiple view points on the origin of vampires, these divergent takes come off as clear falsehoods by bloodlines and sects being isolated from the epicenters of Cainite/Kindred society).

                                                There's simpler ways to introduce more of a morally grey setting. Aside from playing up tribal divisions, camp rivalry, general apathy versus fanaticism, and political corruption among the Nation, you can ramp up the much neglected Lunatic and non-criminal exiled Ronin (ripe for Skindancer recruitment!). Also play up more divergent philosophies on the nature of the Triat and Gaia; they've had such views sitting around in each of the core books since the early 90s. You could have the spirit order overall in disarray thanks to the increasing disconnect between the the physical and spiritual worlds, and spirits falling more and more into Triatic divisions. Introduce more tribal and regional specific interactions with the spirit world, use of ritual, etc, etc.

                                                One way I've come to view the conflict lately, to introduce a grayness to the cosmology itself, is looking at the Triat as parts of Gaia rather than alien entities invading her. The Wyld, the Weaver, and the Wyrm are her. SHE is the insane one. The cosmos itself is suffering from a self-destructive multiple personality disorder. Maybe the Garou and other Fera are fighting a war for what's increasingly a memory of a whole and functioning mother. Some Garou think killing all the Wyrmlings will return her to balance, or maybe its the Weaver that needs to be culled, or maybe the only way to forestall the Apocalypse would be an endless war of balancing these three aspects of her "personality". The later fits in better with an animistic world view anyways.

                                                    I like the idea of greater shades of gray in WW, though I wouldn't make a bite infect people. Instead I would place a greater focus on Tribal rivalries and difference in ideology between them.

                                                    You could actually remove the Wyrm as the primary villain. Maybe it is the Weaver. That is more accurate to real world environmental issues - which result in large part from greater development. Then you have some Tribes who like the Weaver and modern technology while others want it destroyed. Some Tribes (or individual Garou) embrace the Wyrm as a destroyer, who will basically destroy human civilization and save the natural world. Of course, that would result in billions of human deaths, so some Garou object.

                                                    Through in this diet of dynamic and the setting becomes very gray. The Wyrm is evil but it is also trying to save the natural world even if it means billions of human deaths. The Weaver has created a world where humans have a much higher standard of life than ever before - medicine, human rights, plentiful food and shelter, and so on. But in allowing humanity to thrive, the Weaver has allowed the rest of the natural world to come under threat.

                                                    What will the Garou choose? It's not so obvious right?

                                                        Lycanthropy caused by an existing werewolf biting you is mostly a function of the Universal horror movies. That is not how folklore depicts werewolves (or other people who change into different animals). That is almost always a form of witchcraft given to a person by the Devil or other evil entity. If someone really wants that as flavor text, the easiest fix is to simply have a Garou biting a pre-Changer in order to awaken the beast or otherwise provoke a change that might not happen otherwise. In my games, it is a common occurrence with Lost Cubs or even Lunatics.

                                                        Most stories that one could tell with a cursed transmission of lycanthropy can already be told by emphasizing existing concepts like Lost Cubs, Lunatics, and Ronin. After first edition, the game really played down those aspects as part of the setting, and I think it was a mistake as those kinds of concepts are what most people, unfamiliar with the WoD version, think of. The WtA lore we know can be a big obstacle for people who might be curious about a werewolf game, but then don't think what the game describes is anything they're familiar with. It's one reason in my own chronicles, I increase the population of Ronin considerably (and most Ronin are not exiles from Garou society, but werewolves who've never entered Garou society, either being by themselves or part of existing Ronin groups who seek a cure for their "disease") and heavily utilize both Lost Cubs and Lunatics.
