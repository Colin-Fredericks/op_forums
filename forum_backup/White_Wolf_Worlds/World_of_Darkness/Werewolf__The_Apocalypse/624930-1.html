    Originally posted by idpersona View Post
    I very much think it's the Totem. Albrecht was rejected by his tribe, but remained a Silver Fang. Falcon didn't disown him.
    To be precise (and by that I mean, what I remember from the book I read ages ago): Albrecht was rejected by his Great-Grandfather, who had every one convinced Albrecht was his Grand-Child (for purposes of inheritance). Albrecht was is proud an joy and heir apparent until the day when he appeared to become to much of a threat to his still reigning Great-Grandfather - the King. He was then banished from the sept and protectorate, but not from the tribe. There is no mention that any Rite was enacted to make him loose his tribal membership. There wouldn't have been grounds to enact such a Rite, which would be a Punishment Rite, in the first place - for even Silver Fang don't throw garou out of the tribe, when they present the carcass of a mighty wyrm beast in the throne room, no matter the stench.
    Albrecht could have gone to another Silver Fang sept an be their hero, but he chose to hide at the bottom of a bottle instead. That certainly is unbecoming for a Silver Fang and might have cost him additional sympathies with his former sept-mates, but that is another story.

    Actually, I as ST wouldn't allow a Pure Breed 4 garou-PC to act this way for a prolonged time span, since their Pure Breed should make the paragons of their tribe and even with with the Silver Fangs mental issues one should strive to be better sooner, rather than later. But, it makes for a nice drama story, so I can look past that.

            Originally posted by heinrich View Post
            Pure Breed is sublime in nature, and it can't be sensed by itself. It being sensed only manifests in social circumstances, where the pure bred person has an edge over those of lesser breeding and equal social abilities and attributes.
            Originally posted by heinrich View Post
            But, there is another thing. Pure Breed means you are a paragon on your respective tribe. The higher the Pure Breed the more you should stick to the archetypes of the tribe and the more you share the obvious characteristics of the tribe, including the physical ones.
            I think these two statements are somewhat contradictory and would be interested in hearing how you reconcile them. I do agree that when it comes to "rolling" your Charisma + Ability + Pure Breed, that the target can't distinguish one from the other, only the effect. However, I think it can be determined by other means than simply the effect of certain social circumstances. In a real sense, Garou inherently know whether another Garou has better PB than himself - I think it is instinctive animal nature that causes a lower PB to naturally defer to a greater one, although the full effects aren't notice until social challenges start being called.

            However, clearly Tribes can make some kind of distinction between the various levels of Pure Breed, even if they don't use the game mechanics determination of ranks 1-5. A big part of the Silver Fangs is that they know Pure Breed is declining and know which specific family lines have more than others. That is why some Silver Fang families practice inbreeding to various degrees, including incest, to keep that level of standard. They may not rank them according to 1-5, but there are characteristics of what a PB5 (for every tribe) should look like in the same way Dog Show judges can evaluate this specific dog is what this kind of dog breed should look like.

            Originally posted by heinrich View Post
            That said, the first garou surely were paragon of what it meant to be garou.
            I don't believe the very first Garou, the first Garou born in the Paleolithic Ice Age who knew nothing about the spirit world and had to earn their hard won knowledge, the ones who figured out the very first ways to make pacts to get Gifts and Rituals, were born with Pure Breed 5, I believe they had zero Pure Breed. I believe what would become Pure Breed was something that was generated as the first generations of Garou produced the heroes of myth and legend - the ones who stole fire from Helios, made the first pacts with Falcon and other spirits, established the first caerns, etc. Their personal characteristics were prized as signs that newborn Garou had that same power and excellence of their forefathers, And this is what helped generate the effects of Pure Breed. I don't think all the first Garou had this. Some ice age Garou were awesome. Some ice age Garou didn't do jack. Eventually, as the best/alphas were distinguished from the worst/omegas family lines over time, then Pure Breed emerged as a real property, supported by spiritual/magical ties. This might have taken centuries or even thousands of years.

            This is how I figure it out anyway. I understand if others choose something else. Most players likely never bother to think about these kind of things because in most games, it doesn't matter.

            For me I am thinking about this because I am considering of running a subplot where a particular SF kinfolk family is now at the point that they can only produce Garou who have Pure Breed 2. Therefore, I'm trying to figure out what this means, and how other Garou will react to it.

                Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                I think these two statements are somewhat contradictory and would be interested in hearing how you reconcile them.
                Both statements relate to different aspects of Pure Breed, but aren't exclusive to each other. Also, I wrote "sublime" when wanting to write "subliminal" - not a native speaker, didn't know "sublime" was a word and what it meant.
                So, the effect of Pure Breed is subliminal to others. They react to it and can't help to do so (except for some Stargazer Willpower boost gift, iirc). But the very core of subliminal is, that they can't wittingly sense it in most circumstances. Exceptions being possible, especially if the difference between you and the pure bred character is HUGE.
                The other statement is what Pure Breed means for the character, who has it. And there Pure Breed is a certain character trait, that has to be represented by role-playing (especially in LARP). It is more than the dots on the sheet. It is a obligation the player has to portrait his character with certain characteristics - for he choose to have these as part of his character. That two can be regarded as subliminal, if you think of Pure Breed as something forced on the normal mindset of the character. But I don't see it that way, I thing it is something that is part of the characters psyche inherently. Just as his Nature, his Auspice (that usually brings some characteristics to the character) and every other part of the PC's characteristics. It is "sharing the tribes physical, social and mental trademarks" with a score, rather than just "belonging to the tribe" contra "not belonging to the tribe".

                    Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                    So you two disagree with the others that it is case where it is the tribe members that reject the cubs. Instead, it is the Tribal Totem.

                    The question is why does a spirit care about Pure Breed?
                    Because a spirit is an embodiment of certain concepts. Falcon is basically "the birthright to rule", whereas Grandfather Thunder is "the fitness to rule". And each one of these will have certain prerequisites when it comes to proper followers of their tribe,

                    If Pure Breed is generated as a result of your heroic ancestors, then the first Garou had no Pure Breed. Why did Falcon accept those first Garou? If you believe all of the first Garou had Pure Breed 5, then how could this degenerate over time?
                    Take a look at the founding myth of the Silver Fangs: Wolf saved Gaia from the Wyrm and died in the process. When he was revived, his fur had turned silver and Falcon sponsored him. He set the standard that modern Pure Breed is derived from.
                    (Since almost nothing is known about the creation of the Garou except for their legends of it, I'd consider it rather unproductive to speculate about them in evolutionary terms as primitive near-apes.)
