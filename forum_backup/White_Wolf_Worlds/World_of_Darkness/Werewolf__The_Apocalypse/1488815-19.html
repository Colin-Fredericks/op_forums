There is no explanation for why the majority of the Clan spent thousands of years treating Blood Sorcery as a more normal Discipline with a different name from what the Clan calls Blood Sorcery, and never bothered learning rituals for it.

    Tell me that you could, though house rules and homebrew, make it work does not solve the problem that Paradox doesn't care to do that work themselves.
    Bloodline and Descendant Loresheets do not count towards the maximum number, you can have one of them and a "plot" Loresheet, per the rules in Cult of the Blood Gods. So, you could have a Warrior Caste Loresheet (or Descendant of the Amr, maybe) and a Leopard of Zion social loresheet.

    Vizier, Sorcerors and Warriors per DA20 all had different Quietus powers, that could be different Blood Sorcery powers.

    Also, Assamite Sorcery is not much concerned with Blood, and Hermetism, as much as War, Spirit Manipulation and Astronomy: Assamite Sorcerors could have developed their own set of rituals, distinguishing them from other Sorcerers and from Clanmates.

    The Schism is still in V5 Metaplot, so despite the obvious incosistencies a power retcon may cause, the problems can be solved with a certain coherence.

                                Originally posted by MrNatas View Post

                                I'm really curious about how a quantify an RPG being 'obsolete.' How is one rolling system any more 'in-date' then another? I can't even concede the point that it's no longer being worked on or out of print since there are still materials being put out, the recent Sorcerer 20th for example, and material stuff being worked on for later release, the W20 Apocalypse record comes to mind.

                                Is it purely because WoD5 being release making all other version no longer applicable?
                                It's more about visibility and what a new player sees. If you have stopped producing all the other games to focus the market on the product you want to move (and for good reason, Requiem and V20 existing are a hindrance to V5's market success in the One World of Darkness paradigm) the default game is the one that you have available in hobby stores and that is getting the tie-in products and the official support of the company.

                                Most people will not have a strong opinion or experience about Vampire beyond knowing there was a cult cRPG and that there was a cool Vampire RPG once. Those people are walking straight into WoD5 with zero preconceptions and expectations of what the game actually is and you can package and sell them anything. Now you can go about this in multiple ways...

                                Wizards said "well, anything we've ever done exists and we will reference it here and there, feel free to integrate it into the game and we won't hit you over the head with the lore, but would you like to try our big megaadventure of the trimester?". This means that they focus on an experience while leaving a lot of space blank with an option for others to fill in the blanks. They still did a solid job at this - I can easily run Planescape, Dark Sun or Spelljammer in 5E straight out of the core books while using the old books for fluff. This is what Outstar claims they are doing.

                                However, Parawolf said "You know all that other stuff? It's irrelevant and unplayable in this new WoD and the new setting will be what we want it to be, you were doing it wrong before, anyway." What I feel that they are missing out is that the selling point of the old WoD was the cool factor of being monsters, the elaborate insane political and social groups that helped create shorthands for character generation and (especially) Vampires being in vogue. Now they assume that just being fledglings should be enough, but anyone with a internet connection starts noticing that the powers used to be cooler, that the politics uses to be more complicated and that the setting had more options, stories and ideas in it than the experience they claim they are selling.

                                So you have a strong attitude from the publisher that they just want one specific kind of game, they want to make sure this is the main game that's being sold and marketed, and that game is limited compared to what came before. And while I can see that V5 has a number of good structural and mechanical ideas, it feels unfinished, and the way it's designed it seems to have nowhere to go. With every supplement we get it looks less "free" and more proscriptive. This is the game the general public is seeing and this is the experience they get.

                                It fills me with utter dread what they still might do with the Apocalyptic Record if they demand excessive rewrites in the last moment, I'd be much happier if that thing was closer to done nowish, and not still changeable by the final edicts of the W5 team. Either way, I don't expect that W5 will be the game I want it to be and a game I will teach new players.

                                                        Originally posted by Nicolas Milioni View Post
                                                        I love wod5,but a new edition don't make the ones before them obsolete
                                                        I think that Justin and his team are taking a note from WotC in their approach to setting.

                                                        When WotC set up Forgotten Realms as THE setting for DnD 5ed, everyone expected a new massive setting book. Instead they released the Sword Coast Guide and a number of adventures, all offering a different glimpse of the setting.

                                                        Sword Coast Guide (but also Van Richten's new Ravenloft book) are not word-expensive metaplot indexes: they do present the rough sketches of the setting, give structure to how to use plot seeds to frame your stories, and then you are explicitly told to expand your game world with pre-existing published material, if you feel the need to.

                                                        This is where the DMsGuild comes into play, allowing both for easy recovery of old material and an endless stream of new player options.

                                                        Of course, numbers and size here are different, but the model seems very similar: and Dnd5 has still had its number of critics for having a slow pace of publishing (compared to standard DnD rhytms) and supplements which are mostly adventures and plot hooks.

                                                        However, it has the distinct advantage of reducing the reasons for Edition warring. I also think it's more ethical and respectful of past legacies, instead of trying to convince you to buy a new book just to check how this minutia X has changed in 10 years.

                                                        We shall see whether this makes sense for WOD: with me it's working even though I'm not a new customer.
