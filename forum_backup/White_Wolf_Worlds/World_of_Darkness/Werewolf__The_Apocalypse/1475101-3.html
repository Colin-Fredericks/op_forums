        If your fundamental problem is with the fact that Garou do not have the same maximum potential for violence and destruction as other splats like Vampires or Mages, then yeah, that's kind of just the way it is. But you are ignoring the other side of the coin, that your average Garou can probably turn 9 out of 10 Vampires into sausage if they just happen to run into each other on the street. Garou aren't shit, it's just that the most extreme examples of Mages and Vampires are like diamonds. And the vast majority of either splat will *never* get there.

        The really low Generation that turns a Vampire into an overwhelming force-of-nature is a) the product of a ""birth""-lottery, being lucky enough to have been sired by an Antediluvian or a Methuselah, or b) a long process of gradually eating your way up the food-chain without being murdered by the vastly more powerful beings threatened by your ambition, which also comes with the chance of becoming possessed by one of the rivals you tried to diablerize. And either one of those options requires *centuries* of struggle in order to hone the powers necessary to achieve that potential. While forum-chatter can make it sound like diablerizing your way from a humble 11th Generation all the way up to 4th Generation is just part of regular Vampire gameplay, it really *isn't.* Most Vampires are just struggling to make it from night to night, and will never rise in power to the extent that they can't be swatted out of existence by one of their elders, or a Joe Average Werewolf they have the misfortune of stumbling across in a dark alley.

        (PS: to bring in an earlier example, Vladimir Rustovich was over One Thousand Years Old. There are probably Maeljin Incarna that weren't even born yet when Vladimir was fighting his way up the ranks. And Vladimir is an example of a Vampire committed to being a *warrior*, more than a socialite or scholar.)

        You could probably write a story about a Garou that achieves incredible power through decades of struggle, by forming pacts with powerful spirits, acquiring potent artifacts, pushing themselves to the absolute limit of their potential. And a Werewolf that could continue that process for *centuries* likely would be able to rival the might of a powerful Methuselah. There are just lots of reasons that story doesn't get told, in particular because the themes and setting of WtA aren't meant to encourage that kind of tale. And conversely, almost any Vampire that attempted such a thing would be as likely to die in the attempt as the Werewolf.

        Also, a Garou could theoretically be empowered by an entity other than the Wyrm. I could be wrong, but I believe that it's theoretically possible for a Garou to become a Kami, though there are reasons why it pretty much never happens. You might argue that a Vampire doesn't need to get its extra power from something else, except yes they do: another Vampire. It's just a question of whether you're being empowered by a Celestine or an Antediluvian, both of which are effectively god-like beings.

        I mean, if it makes you feel better, I'd be inclined to think most Celestines would be able to squash an Antediluvian, if you somehow ended up in the weird situation where they were throwing down with each other in a dark alley. VtM is just kind of unique among the gamelines that it is written with other Vampires as the Biggest Bads in their fish-pond, whereas the Bigger Fish in other gamelines tend to be bigger than a super-charged member of the same splat; Mage comes close, but I think even Archmages are not a proper match for the greatest of the Celestines, but I could be wrong there.

        But to return to the first point, if the thing that interests you most is being able to crush your enemies, then you want to play Vampire: the Masquerade, not Werewolf: the Apocalypse. Specifically, you want to play as a member of the Sabbat. Werewolves don't get to become nigh-unstoppable demigods because their entire story is about fighting a war that's nearly impossible to win, and maybe even that trying to "fight" it in the traditional sense is itself a losing strategy. And yeah, if the only thing you prioritize is a capacity for destruction, then of course the incarnation of cosmic destruction itself is going to be the most likely source of the power that you're seeking. If Ramsay is a better character because he's better at hurting people, then of course the God of Hurting People is the one who is going to help make your Werewolf character "better." And if you really want to tell that tale in Werewolf, it'll probably be the story of some asshole Garou supplanting Hellbringer as the new Maeljin of Violence.

            Part of this is survivorship bias. There's no reason for the games to tell us what Methusaleh level vampires were destroyed in the past by Garou. Vampire books tell us about the vampires that survived and exist in the current day. And Werewolf books are mainly concerned about current threats that are hooks for the PCs, not historical battles that are irrelevant.

            So we know of Methusalehs like the All High, Baba Yaga, and Mithras because they survived until the modern nights. But we wouldn't be told for example that the Nosferatu Methusaleh vampire eating children in the Black Forest were destroyed by the Get of Fenris sibling warriors Hansel and Gretl, or that the Fianna destroyed the Malkavian Methusaleh Sirona (the name of a Celtic goddess) in Gaul around the time of Caesar because that would simply be trivia not relevant to the needs of a current sourcebook. But as fictitious examples, they don't serve your needs. But clearly, all those ancient vampires that were once embraced but didn't survive couldn't all have fallen simply to the intrigues of other vampires.

            We do have one canonical example of Garou destroying a ton of vampires - Under a Blood Red Moon. Now none of them are the level of a Methusaleh, but it did include some old vampires and those of more potent blood than is allowed in character creation.                         I think territorial conflict between vampires and werewolves is not necessarily about land. For garou hunt game or simply go to the supermarket. Vampires need blood. There is no conflict there. But, vampires often try to gain influence within the mortal society, which might bring them into conflict with garou kinfolk and by extension the garou. And, those vampires older and wiser, might correctly realise that once the garou are on the trail of one of the vampires, it might be a good thing to lay low for a couple of years...

                        That said, the garou should see vampires as wyrm-creatures all of the time, certain post-1st-edition materials tell us - but, still they are the lesser evil in most urban settings, for fomori and banes are possibly more common and more in line with the tenet to "Fight the Wyrm where it dwells and breeds".

                        Wewaupet is basically the only immortal garou - or is he an immortal being that originally was a garou - in any case, we can assume that he is able to kill vampires, if he ever wanted to....

                        Besides that, a Legendary Garou can learn the 2nd Edition Player's Guide Gift "Renew the Cycle" and enter into a contested Gosis vs. Willpower roll (difficulty 8 on both sides). If the Garou wins ans pays three permanent Gnosis the Undead is destroyed. This would include Antediluvians or Caine himself. Since an avatar of Helios or Gaia could teach this Gift, one would assume that similar abilities can used by such avatars directly, too - so the Rite of Summoning might bring an adequate result, too, possibly (I generally don't allow to circumvent Rank limitations by summoning a spirit and appeal to him to grant an ability once).
                        The Gift doesn't cancel the Spell of Life, though: Mummies just die, to be reborn later (pre-Web-of-Faith, that is).

                        So, as far as the comparison between rank 6 Garou and Elders go, the Silver Fangs have one specific Gift that might just kill any vampire. In addition to "Mind Block" as an adequate defence against vampire mental abilities...
