                      It's greatest failure, above all the sex, litany and other messes, was to portray the Child of Gaia as ignorant priviledged people who only -want- to do good, never succeeding.

                      If you look at each event the characters in the book are in, there is never any success in their actions. When its just this young pack, it might make sense. But when the Elders of the tribe gather and they -still- all fail to raise a Caern due to BSDs being able to outwit them ( with flaming portapotties, people), it doesn't make you want to play the tribe. Tribebook is supposed to be a celebration of a tribe, not a parody of it.

                      More than that, though, notice that none of our leads ever actually suffer from these events. They are witnesses, they might get hurt, but they always have a retreat to go to. It never feels like these guys are actually risking themselves. These guys don't act like warriors or peacekeepers, they act like bunch of young idiots with no goals who just go to the protests/meetings because they want to.

                      In short, we see all the flaws of the tribe. But none of the merits.

                          Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
                          In short, we see all the flaws of the tribe. But none of the merits.
                          This.

                          Also, it comes across as (and I hate to say this in this way, because there are times when it's okay to be "x" because what "x" does is well-written/plotted/conceptualized/what-have-you) very "I'm a fanboi and I got to write this book so I can say this, that and the other plus make shout-outs to my friends...!". It just... just... it just wasn't simply that it was not well written (plenty of WoD books are guilty of that) -- I could overlook/forgive that, in the broad -- it's just that it was...it was... >breathes for a second and thinks<

                          It was *wrong*.

                          That's the problem.

                          It's one thing to paint a picture of the flaws of the subject matter in question, and it's certainly fine is the picture painted is at once subtle in its indication of said flaws as it may be direct about them, the same as with the subjects merits/strengths.

                          But this book doesn't do that, at all.

                          It's hamfisted in a manner that disrupts the subject matter unnecessarily for the reader. (Hell, for all the shit that the first Clanbook:Malkavian gets for being laid out the way it was, the meta-narrative and in-narrative aspects *jibe* -- like it or not, the presentation made *sense* relative to the subjects at hand, just as the later Revised version flipped on that and went for better writing over meta-narrative presentation.) With TB:CoG, though, the author-insertion of elements (and some poorly researched ones, at that, in several instances) is just sloppy and doesn't live up to the idea that while very early WoD stuff was a bit all-over-the-place, style and editing-wise, it more-or-less had its shit together by Revised... with this book being one of the glaring missteps along the way.

                          What makes it horrible is not simply that it's bad (there are worse), but rather that it's bad *and* mediocre. If it were pure-on Samuel-Haight bad, then we could make fun of it and actually gain something from it in that regard. But I can't even find anything, properly, to "make fun of' about it because it's so both overtly-and-subtly ill-executed it just leaves a bad taste in my mouth (much like, for example, "Gypies" leaves an equally bad taste for many people, though for contextually different reasons).

                          >shrugs<

                          I have been around here for waaaayyyy too fucking long...

                              Just to build on my previous point - lacking a framework for an actual cultural identity for the CoG, I've just made them a multitribal camp at my table, which I find actually better emphasizes their ideology than them being a distinct tribe does (I can always be convinced otherwise by a truly good in-depth tribal writeup, but unlike the other Tribes they've never gotten one). They're all about unity and equality between the Garou, and potentially the Fera.

                              This also makes them look less inept - because in canon the major places in the world where they aren't found in significant numbers are the very places that their dream is realized or being realized: Africa and East Asia. If they're a Camp rather than a Tribe, this makes sense, since Garou who believe in unity equality and peace with the Fera just join the Beast Courts in East Asia, and the Ahadi in Africa. So the Children of Gaia aren't organized there because there isn't a necessity for them to organize toward that end.

                                  You know, I've never really understood why everyone hates this book. Yeah, it had some points that I thought were a little goofy, but thematically I thought it totally hit the nail on the head. Being a voice for peace in the Garou Nation -- you know, the people who did the Impergium, the War of Rage and the War of Tears and are just generally homicidal dicks at the best of times -- being a voice for peace in that toxic soup is hard. It's a thankless, endlessly frustrating slog with far more defeats than victories. Moreover their ideology is so far out of whack with the rest of their fellow Garou, that they don't really relate all that well with them (and vice-versa), so you wind up with really unconventional interpretations of Garou culture, the Litany, etc. The Children of Gaia are (at least ideologically) radically different in outlook from the rest of the Garou, and the book highlights that.

                                  Now, if you don't like the Children of Gaia as a tribe... Well, that's different.

                                                      Originally posted by Uniform Two Six View Post
                                                      Well, first, it was a pair of elders who were investigating whether or not it was even possible to open a caern there (this was specifically stated as being in doubt since the dormant caern was very weak and an abandoned air force base was probably not going to be a good location anyway). Also (despite the fact that that wasn't what they were doing), I would challenge the assertion that "raising a caern" equates to "the simplest thing". Opening a caern is a MAJOR deal, very rare, and sometimes fatal for the participants.
                                                      While it's true that opening a caern is not at all simple, you'd think that raising a caern on such a dangerous location with an entire stadium's worth of human witnesses present would be "the simplest thing" to realize is a BAD idea. The Wyrm is already going to know about the Opening and send as many of its minions as it can to disrupt the opening. And the Coggies knew (or should have known) that the minions were on their way. Opening a caern when there are humans present not only threatens the Veil, it endangers all the humans present because they will inevitably get caught in the crossfire.

                                                      So the Coggies aren't just being inept here, they're being criminally negligent. If the Coggies were this incompetent throughout history, they couldn't claw their way out a paper bag, never mind negotiate the Concord and end the Impergium. That's why the Revised Tribebook portrayal of the Coggies feels so off: it feels so wrong for the Coggies to be portrayed as this incompetent when they did have at least a few very real and large victories against the Wyrm, namely the Concord and the end of the Impergium.

                                                      EDIT: I just realized that U26 said that the Coggies weren't trying to Open a caern at Woodstock '99. Rather than try and re-read TB: CoG again (for fear of having my eyes bleed so profusely that I'd die of blood loss), can someone tell me what all the Coggies were really doing there?

                                                      Originally posted by Uniform Two Six View Post
                                                      Second, uniting the tribes and advocating peace is precisely the mutually-exclusive paradox that the Children face. How do you advocate peace to a warrior culture? Answer: You don't -- at least not successfully. The Shadow Lords are better at uniting the tribes because they get the blood-and-guts mentality of the Garou at large.
                                                      The more accurate answer to advocating peace to a warrior culture is: Make war on the real enemy: the Wyrm. But create peace between the Tribes and between the Garou and humans. You can't go into a war with a split between you and your allies. That inevitably leads to defeat. And nobody said uniting the Tribes would be an easy task, especially when you have to take the Garou's Rage (simultaneously the Garou's greatest strength and greatest weakness) into account. But the only other alternative is extinction.

                                                      Of course, the Garou's Rage is what prevents them from realizing the Coggies are right. It's just far too easy for the Garou to give in to their Rage. That's what makes the Coggies' mission to unite the Tribes so difficult. But nobody said that anything worthwhile was easy....
