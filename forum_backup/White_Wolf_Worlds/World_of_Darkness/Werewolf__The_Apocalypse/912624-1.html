    Steeds of the Gods

    Griffins were the steeds of the gods, having pulled the chariots of Apollo, Zeus and Nemesis. In Dante's 'Divine Comedy', the Sacred Gryphon pulls the chariot of the Church to take the narrator to heaven.

    If griffins are the steeds of the gods, then griffins represent the conveyance of the divine powers to the earthly realm. Indeed, the Red Talons have been repeatedly mentioned as a source of divine prophecy for the Garou Nation. Griffin brings what is in the Heavens down to the Realm, and this is an important function of the spirit. It explains why Red Talon prophets bring word to the Garou glimpses of the future or warnings despite this tribe not being particularly known for their Theurges.

    As a corollary, the Griffin Incarna and its jagglings would be known for their travel between the various Umbras. They routinely go into the High Umbra and interact with beings there. Maybe that is the same for the Dreaming. Although the Red Talons are not known for their ties to the Dreaming, it is easy to imagine members of that tribe being responsible for the Big Bad Wolf characters in so many fairy tales.

    Lupus have higher Gnosis than homids, so lupus characters should be played with that in mind. As a tribe of all lupus, Red Talons collectively have a much stronger tie to Gaia than any other tribe since most of that tribe's members do not have the large amount of Gnosis that lupus have. Red Talons have a much stronger tie to the spirit world than the other tribes, even compared to the Uktena and Stargazers who are probably seen by most players as the “spiritual” tribes. Yet this connection is overlooked.

    Building on this motif, Griffin packs and Red Talon theurges could have a heritage of really skilled umbral travelers, particularly to appropriate Near Realms or even other local penumbras in the Dreaming or Astral Plane. Perhaps they do this in the hope of catching a glimpse of Griffin in his voyages among the gods so they can receive a vision.

    Red Talons should be spending a lot of their time in the umbra. The game makes a point that the spiritual world is more important than the physical. Spirits can survive the death or destruction of their counterparts in the Realm, but spiritual destruction inevitably leads to physical death. Given this, we can see the Red Talons look to solutions to the problems facing them not in the Realm, but in the Umbra. Whatever bad things happen in the Realm have a “true source” in the Umbra. Any cause and effect relationship that takes place in the Realm is really of minor importance. The real solutions lie in the spirit world. If that can be fixed, then the Realm will straighten itself out.

    I think that is a good perspective for some of the Red Talons ideas of war against humanity. Many of the battlefronts of that war don't happen in the Realm, and it isn't fought against individual human individuals. Instead it is often fought on battlegrounds in the Umbra. Maybe it is against Weaver spirits responsible for the wealth and power of human civilization; or against the spiritual forces of the Wyrm that disconnect man from his ties to Gaia; or against malevolent Banes engaged in individual plots. The Red Talons might be seen as spiritual warriors par excellence. The kind of allies other septs want when they need to raid the enemy strongholds in the Umbra.

    An emphasis on Red Talon activity in the Umbra also allows the ST to showcase the Red Talons in an environment where their war with humanity will not disrupt the game. Instead of being an example of what is "bad" with the Garou, it would be an example of what is "good" with the Garou. The ST can emphasize a very deep spiritual, even benevolent side of the Red Talons. Perhaps many spirits show a favorable bias to Red Talons and packs of Griffin as a result of the strong spiritual ties and honest chiminage provided by them to the spirits. It might even shame PCs that they aren't more like the Talons!

                Griffins hate horses, and horses are their favorite prey

                The idea that griffins hate horses can be shown in the original conception of the hippogriff, a cross between griffins and horses. While today a hippogriff is just another fantasy creature, originally the term was used to mean a concept that could not exist. The ancients thought a cross between griffin and horse to be impossible because it was well known that griffins hated horses and would attack and kill them.

                Griffin’s hatred of horses might be a result of horses being one of the important domesticated animals of man. Perhaps horses are considered traitors to the animal world. If so, griffin likely hates all the other domesticated animals as well – dogs, cattle, cats, chickens, pigs, etc.

                Horses were probably domesticated by man around 3500 BC on the Eurasian Steppes. This time would be after the end of the Impergium as the great cities of Mesopotamia built on intensive agriculture were in existence by then. Perhaps Griffin was much angrier at the horse for being domesticated at this time, when man had violated the laws of Gaia (via agriculture), than he was with the earlier domestication of other species (when man was merely a hunter-gatherer). I also think it has something to do with what domestication of the horse did to humanity. Earlier domesticated simply provided man with food to eat. Horses for the first time allowed man to travel faster and gain an advantage over animals for hunting. Man therefore became much more dangerous, especially to wolves and other predators. We can imagine legends of the Red Talons when the Griffin incarna approached the Horse incarna, and they fought over Horse's betrayal of the rest of the animal kingdom.

                This might make good ways to teach Red Talon cubs aspects of the tribe. Send them out in raiding parties to attack hen houses, cattle, sheep, etc. which is all natural wolf behaviour anyway. But what was once only a means to fill your belly, now becomes symbolic spiritual warfare against the spirits who “betrayed” Gaia by agreeing to become mankind's servants and slaves. Spirits of these animals may even be the subject of attack by Red Talons in the Umbra.

                Perhaps some of the stories homids tell of the Red Talons ritual killing of men are actually that of abducted horses sacrificed to appease Griffin. It might be a good way to show PCs the extent of the Red Talon anger and rage without provoking PCs to intervene and stop Talons. It would also preserve some of the bloodier and more horrific Red Talon rituals, but not be as offensive as PCs or create as much difficulty as introducing NPCs into a chronicle.

                We can also imagine Red Talons encouraging domesticated animals to escape man and live in the wild, becoming feral. I imagine Griffin would not have as much trouble with mustangs - feral horses - as he would with domesticated kind. They might become friends with feral cats and dogs, mustangs, pigeons, razorbacks, and other feral animals. Indeed, the Red Talons might lead missions inside towns and cities in order to encourage animals to abandon their servitude among men and leave free once again, or protect such feral animals from dangers. Perhaps some Red Talon Galliards sneak into cities and use Beast Speech to learn news and rumors from such “spies”. Or they come back and tell their septs the stories of such abandoned animals as more proof of man's ways. In Axis Mundi, “Lost Dogs” belong to the brood of Rat so we can imagine many spirits of feral spirits are likewise loyal to Rat. This may make some interesting conflict between Red Talons and Bone Gnawers depending on the exact type of feral spirits involved.

                Maybe Red Talon theurges lead debating packs into the Umbra to find the spirits of domesticated animals and argue to convince them to repudiate their loyalty to man and once again align with the Wyld. Plots that could arise from this might be similar to the story in The Birds (both the Du Maurier book and Hitchcock film) when avians began mysteriously attacking humans. Small scale local phenomonen similar to that might happen whenever Red Talons win such a debate with a local jaggling. There are lots of possibilities for stories that begin with “the animals are acting weird”. A Red Talon plot where they succeed with an Incarna (or come close to it) might be the basis of an entire chronicle.

                I like this because it gives additional ways for Red Talons to fight Mankind without the need of violence that threatens the Veil (which I find makes the ST's job ever harder and often damages suspension of disbelief). Instead, we have plots in the Umbra, and strange animal activity in the Realm.

                                    Griffons Mate for Life

                                    In legend, griffins not only mated for life, but if either partner died, then the other would continue the rest of its life alone, never to search for a new mate. The griffin was thus made an emblem of the Christian Church's opposition to remarriage.

                                    The loyalty of the Griffin to their mate even after death might have echoes in Griffin’s adoption of the spirits of animals that have gone extinct. While the actual living creatures themselves have left Gaia, Griffon may consider it his duty to protect their spirits because of his role as a protector of the sacred spaces of Gaia. Even though they have passed on and their children have left the Realm, Griffin continues to be loyal to them.

                                    Loyalty may also explain the Red Talons continued following of the Silver Fangs despite the obvious decline of the Garou people during the last thousand years. Often this is displayed as a result of the sense of hierarchy among wolves and submission to the alpha, but perhaps this has a deeper reason as a Child of Griffin. After all, in a wolf pack the leaders will be challenged once they get old and weak. A natural wolf instinct would be to depose the Silver Fangs if they seem like they are not hacking it. If Griffin expects loyalty from his children though, this might complicate things for the Red Talons.

                                    In terms of mating, this may also impact the Red Talons. Wolves tend to be mate in loyal pairs (often just the alpha pair, but in some packs another pair may also mate), but if one wolf dies the other often finds another mate. Also, not all mates are loyal. Some males may bond with different females in different years.

                                    However, among the Red Talons they may follow Griffin much more closely. Red Talons may mate with only one partner wolf. And since Garou live far longer than wolves, this would have a huge impact on the tribe's population. In fact, this would have such a huge impact, it is probably not the case. However, it may mean that 1) while their mate is alive, a Red Talon would never mate with another, and 2) some Red Talons may take it upon themselves to only truly mate with only one wolf their entire lives as an offering or sacrifice to Griffin. It would be the equivalent of a Red Talon monk.

                                    A critical question is how do Red Talons interpret their own mating behavior. In wolf packs, typically only the alpha pair mate and the others do not. Is this wolf characteristic carry over to Red Talon lupus as well, or does this not carry over to Garou? I have to imagine that Red Talons do not reproduce wolf behavior here. This may cause them some anguish and feeling isolated from their wolf kinfolk - which is actually quite appropriate for Garou. Hearing a Red Talon lament their isolation from their own wolf kin could humanize a tribe generally known for their hatred of Man.

                                    I think this is some interesting flavor for the tribe. While somewhat based on real wolf behavior, this actually seems more like a human trait. Furthermore, this contrasts sharply with a lot of other mating behavior of the other tribes; some of whom seem to believe to use kinfolk as breeding stock as much as they can. This would portray the Red Talons in a much more positive, even romantic, light.

                                        From what I understand, the alpha wolf phenomenon is a product of artificially formed wolf packs. In a strange turnabout it could be the Red Talons who keep the biggest ties to the mortal world by bringing their families into the conflict.
