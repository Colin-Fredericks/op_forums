                                Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post

                                And why would monsters necessarily follow human cultural trends? It could be that the Get's problem is in making too obvious bloody messes of modern fascists, not that they're siding with them.



                                That wasn't the direction it was moving in during Revised, though. Everything in BNS is from out of nowhere and often relies on prior editions' material, skipping entirely over Revised.
                                They wouldn't NECESSARILY follow human cultural trends (and if that's not the route you'd want to take, it's perfectly valid), but I see a couple of reasons why it would make sense to take that route, and why it's a direction I personally prefer.

                                From a lore perspective, it makes sense. These are creatures who live half in the world of man and half in the spiritual. Who they were before their first change informs who they become. The attitudes of their kinfolk and the other people they see in their daily lives influences their decisions, and the political and social attitudes of the mortal climate creates opportunities.

                                In more meta terms, I like my games to be a reflection of the reality I know, and a way of exploring that reality in interesting ways. Especially in modern games, I look for concepts that reflect my lived reality, warts and all, and I think looking for those kinds of patterns is an inherently human thing, and a core virtue of storytelling in general. If there's a tribe of fiercely female warrior women, I see fertile ground of allegory for the feminist struggles I see in the groups I run in. When I see the Get, I see a reflection of military warrior culture and the reflectively hostile insularity of much of modern far right conservatism (and admittedly, my opinion is probably the outlier here, and I'm not expecting the writers to create a game tailored to my preferences). For me, at least, Werewolf is very much a game about the safety of the past: the threat of a mythical history that obfuscates the troubles that past represented, and the inability to achieve that in the face of an uncertain and frightening future. It's not the major theme for me (that would likely be the dual natured romance and dangers of radical ideology and the questions of moral righteousness when faced with an unbeatable threat), but it's prominent; and, as much as I abhor and pity the rising prominence of vaguely fascist nationalism, I think there's a stark metaphor in those people's apocalyptic view of the future, a future where they see their values, or religion, or racial identity, or whatever, threatened by existential genocide.

                                In regards to the new writers skipping right over Revised, not gonna argue with you there. That sucks, and I'll be honest, I don't find a lot of these ideas, as presented, as very palatable. That said, it's been over a decade now. If the writers decided they wanted to undertake some rather major retcons for the sake of a bold new setting, I wouldn't be adverse to that. But again, these changes don't indicate an edition that's going to appeal to me.

                                I'm curious about your thoughts though. How do you perceive the Get? What's their hook for you? And how would you like to see them develop in 5e? It's been quite a few years, so there's a lot of possibilities for how they might have evolved.

                                    Originally posted by Lunar Falcon View Post
                                    They aren't a tribe about equality or even hating men, they are about solely focusing on the feminine and are a Wyld worshipping fertility cult with a focus on meting out justice for historically vulnerable groups (women and children).
                                    If they aren't a Tribe about equality, why are they wasting their meting out justice for the historically vulnerable groups? We already have the Bone Gnawers, Children of Gaia, and Uktena that have "mete out justice for the disenfranchised," as part of their shtick anyway. There's also a lot to answer for in their old traditions of killing babies for having a penis; kinda hard to mete out justice for children when you kill your male children that so the signs of becoming Garou to maintain the illusion that Black Furies don't give birth to male Garou kids. Their refusal (until the BNS changes) to stand up for gay men, queer anything that's not female, or transgender people doesn't exactly show them as champions of the historically vulnerable either.

                                    If they're a Wyld worshiping fertility cult, why exclude men? Werewolves can't reproduce asexually, so they need both sets of parts to celebrate fertility. Real world matriarchal fertility religions don't show any evidence of excluding men from being active participants just because of their focus on femininity as the core of fertility. If you're out to maximize fertility in the setting, you want male kin to knock up female Garou, and male Garou to knock up female kin. Excluding that second part for just kin-kin pairs doesn't make sense.

                                    The problem is that, like many of the Tribes, the Black Furies were designed as political message first, and the authors have been back-filling and retconning in material to try to have them make sense. They started as a fantasy fulfillment of the late second wave/proto-third wave feminist ass-kickers. This included all the problematic issues with prescriptive femininity simply being moved from patriarchy to matriarchy instead of self-definition, and a lot of TERF nonsense. Everything meant to expand them past that (like the whole Wyld worshiping fertility cult stuff) was added later to make a bit more than the early dull stereotype, however as they added such things, the reasoning behind them being AFAB only diminished.

                                    If you want to play a Homid don't try to be a Red Talon.
                                    Personally, I've always found this to be stupid. But my dislike of the Red Talons is far deeper than this and not worth getting into here. Needless to say, the idea that the Red Talons reject Homids that want to reject their humanity is an entirely too human way of thinking for a Tribe that's all wolves IMO.

                                    If you want to play a white person don't try to be a Wendigo or Uktena.
                                    Kind of a funny statement given characters like Evan Heals the Past being long standing canon characters. It seems like the authors didn't want to exclude, "playing a white person that finds out they had some tiny amount of indigenous people in them and wants to connect with that heritage," as something these Tribes deal with.

                                    If you want a male Fury be a Metis.
                                    You realize this was a change introduced later in the game, right? Originally this wasn't even a possibility.

                                    It takes away from the identity of the tribes.
                                    Does it? I'll admit in some cases it makes justifying all the Tribes as separate entities difficult (but I think that's an issue with a lot of the Tribes overlapping way too much anyway). But one person's dilution the Tribe's identity is another person's expansion of it.

                                    When I hear a lot of complaints about the Furies it's typically from male players.
                                    Would you like me to get my wife to post her standard rant on everything wrong with the Black Furies? You'll have to trust it's her posting via my account, but I'm pretty sure she'll oblige if you really want. Though we share very similar opinions about them, and you seem very focused on one complaint to the exclusion of all the far more compelling ones.

                                    Women have had a pretty shit hand dealt to them through most of human history and the Black Furies are a fictionalization of the concept of "what if?" "What if women could have had a say in their role in society, stood up for themselves, owned their own bodies, were allowed agency over themselves?" You don't have to force men into the tribe for the sake of fairness because it was never meant to be fair.
                                    This never addresses why outright excluding them in the first place was the right idea. You don't need an all female (and worse all AFAB) group to answer the "What if" posed. A matriarchal Tribe that excepts all genders and sexes can still explore these topics.

                                    Of course, it all seems like a strawman to me, because I've seen a lot of complaints about the Furies - both idiotic and well thought out - but "it's not fair for them to exclude men!" has never been one of them.
