    Well, what makes humans special in that regard, is that we care about "why." But why takes a ton of brain power, and slows down learning. Regardless of how an individual leans best, we learn fastest when we know why what we're learning is important (it makes it easier for us to encode learning in long term memory when we can associate it with something important to us already).

    An example from primate studies: Once you show most apes to tap 1 through 10 in order on a touch screen for a reward, they pick it up very quickly. Humans can, of course, do the basic task very quickly as well. It's when you start making it harder you see massive performance differences. Like if you scramble the numbers around the screen, or you only flash the numbers for a second, the other apes will perform pretty much as fast as if the numbers are just listed in order normally. Humans start struggling (many can't successfully pass the "flash for a moment" test).

    The apes have zero brain power invested in what each of those symbols mean beyond "what order to press them in to get the reward." To us, each of those numbers registers a huge amount of associations "1" means more than just "the symbol you press before 2" and that stops us from being able process the input quickly.

    But all those associations are what lets us be "smart" because those associations allow us to handle abstract thoughts. We care about "why" 1 comes before 2, and what that means on a larger scale. That's pretty much our entire evolutionary strategy that lets us purposefully invent tools. In the wild we've observed chimpanzees using all sorts of tools, but there seems to be very little experimentation. They seem to only discover improvements by accident. We cared about "why" the sharp stick is better for stabbing prey, which meant we can associate "sharpness" in the abstract with other things, like rocks, to make stone spears. That's the connection that other animals don't make, that made it worth it for our brains to be so large, and so invested in very very slow learning patterns in comparison.

    --------

    Getting back to wolves and learning: Wolves understand the concept of verbal communication; they have a fairly complex verbal system for coordinating while hunting. It's why dogs can take to verbal commands; they're wired to want sound-based communication as part of socialization. Lupus would actually have very little problem understanding lots of human things. Toilets for example, are no mystery to them. Wolves (like most small and medium non-apex predators) don't like feces building up around their dens because (especially if it's pup feces) it can attract larger predators. The idea of "making it go away" is completely sensible to a wolf (even if the whole flushing thing is a big "why" they wouldn't quite get, but they wouldn't really care either).

    The thing that probably frustrates Homids and Metis trying to teach Lupus is that the Lupus are used to learning direct things. "When someone holds out their hand like this, shake it firmly," is all the Lupus probably cares about, but Homids esp are going to be used to explaining the whole social aspect of it being a form of greeting, and getting into what different ways of doing it "mean" and such that to the Lupus is actually frustrating information overload.

            I think playing a lupus well, while tricky, is easier than people think. Likewise, playing homid well is much harder than most players perceive at first. Warning: this is going to be a long post.

            Having a shared language in the garou tongue makes the transition much easier, I imagine. If the newly changed lupus can communicate with other garou right off the bat, they can start learning pretty quickly. Sure, its going to take a while to learn a lot of things, but I imagine a lot of septs are also experienced with teaching new lupus and kind of have their own systems for it down pretty well. Plus, having more experienced lupus teach lupus cubs probably eases that transition a lot, as the older lupus has a better idea of what is going to be harder to learn, how to explain it in ways lupus would understand, and prioritizing what cubs need to learn based on their own experiences.

            But there is a lot more to this issue, too. The first is that, well... garou are made for this shit. They are designed to be able to perform their functions, and part of their function is to deal with humans. Garou "instincts" go beyond primal urge, or connecting to gnosis, or being able to cross the gauntlet - all of which, in my opinion, any garou could and probably would pick up over time even without training. Their job and duties, the things they were designed to do, are as much as part of who they are as anything else. The spirit part of them isn't passive, its active, and imbues them with a sense of purpose that will urge them towards the functions they are meant to perform, because it is in a sense part of divine plan. That's how I see it; maybe others don't, and that's fine. But I do think a lot of people underestimate this aspect.

            Then you have the fact that not only are wolves more intelligent than most humans give them credit for, lupus aren't normal wolves. The game explicitly describes that lupus are exceptionally smart and their intelligence sets them apart from there kinfolk pack, to the point where they can even kind of recognize that they are different. Now, that intelligence and sentience doesn't reach "human" levels until the first change, but they are already more primed for it. Their "brain" isn't as undeveloped and immature as a human baby, for example. The garou brain is also really weird to talk about, because how do garou brains even work when they can shift size and shape and capabilities? That literal and metaphorical malleability no doubt allows them to form new neural connections way faster than normal. Think about THAT.

            On top of this, not all lupus are completely new to garou. There are much fewer wolves left in the wild in modern times - though it is variable just how many you want there to be. In some ways, that makes it way easier for garou to keep track of them, not to mention literally being able to track them via their own senses, records, and spirits (or even human technology... ew). Garou may come around a specific wolf packs fairly often. A pre-change lupus might even have exposure to more organized sept life. They can no doubt sense that garou are somehow related to them and aren't human; garou certainly smell differently, they can interact with wolves in different forms to make them more comfortable, and (should) know the wolf body language and vocalizations. Not to mention animal ken and beast speech. I'm playing a lupus character in a game right now who grew up in a group of protected kinfolk wolves that were within the territory of a sept. It's entirely reasonable for a pre-change lupus to wander closer and closer to the sept, drawn by curiosity and even other garou, and may even grown comfortable there. Once there, a pre-change lupus would start picking up patterns and various aspects of more "human" interaction. Hell, dogs can learn human body language and tone - a lupus would probably pick up quite a bit, even if they didn't have the means to piece it together and understand the meaning behind the patterns they observe.

            But seriously though, beast speech. Garou can outright talk to pre-change lupus. Not the same as more advanced communication, and the lupus won't learn nearly as much as they will post-change, but still.

            Lupus might be 14 or so human years of learning behind, but it could be as few as 10. An early change lupus will have a decent amount of time to learn, and their minds are ready for it. Abstract concepts and cultural knowledge will take them longer, but they don't really need that much to get by. So much of what we learn is unnecessary. It's all about priorities. And, a lupus will mostly likely never be able to completely blend in outside of the sept, and probably will not want (or need) to be the one handling subtle interactions with humans. But remember, that's already a bitch and a half for garou at all, because of the curse. Werewolves stick out most of the time whether they want to or not, and not in a good way. People will be suspicious of them. People will be afraid of them. People will read them as aggressive and intimidating for no reason. And most humans will react to the curse by just avoiding them completely if possible. A weird, poorly socialized (for human standards) lupus garou in the city is probably not going to have to deal with many human interactions unless they specifically need to. It just won't be that easy for them, and therefore probably won't be that important. Let the homid handle the yakety yak.

            Right now I'm playing my fostern-rank lupus philadox as pretty much competent as far as the english language and being able to not act particularly weird around humans. His vocabulary isn't awesome, and he doesn't get most cultural references. A lot of homid/human humor is lost on him. The smells and sounds of the city are still awful, but he's been there long enough that he's desensitized to it quite a bit. He enjoys human food, and having thumbs. He doesn't get style on his own, but he can emulate it by observing people and ads and magazines etc.... because he's smart. Really smart. No academics, no formal education, missing a lot of context, but very sharp, and he watches and listens (and often smells things/people, often inappropriately). His eye for body language (esp. with Heightened Senses) mean he's really good at telling when people are lying, or afraid, or aggressive, etc, which give him a lot of context in conversations. He's a philadox, so judgment and negotiation and arbitration are part of his purpose, and he has the mind for it. Learning the various cultural meanings behind things is a matter of exposure, but discerning details, complex patterns, and emulating behavior don't require full understanding - and he absorbs as much cultural information as he can along the way. TV and internet make this even easier, if you aren't opposed to using it. He can learn a ton about various human cultures with a couple of hours of that a day. Seriously, the way mass communications have revolutionized learning and cultural exposure is immense, and there's no reason garou can't incorporate it into their lupus teaching regimens, even if they do so very selectively.

            To give some last thoughts: Lupus are part human, arguably just as much as homid; they lack experience, but it is an essential part of who they are that can't be denied any more than a homid can deny they are wolf (yeah, shut up Red Talons). While human civilization and technology has grown more complex, in some ways modern culture is more homogeneous and approachable. Humanity has way more shared experiences, language, and culture than ever before, so gaining a working knowledge of human culture might be easier now, because you can go to way more places and the same knowledge set will be useful. Garou have the means and motivation to track kinfolk wolf populations very closely and interact with lupus even before their first change. They have systems and practices (and even technology) for teaching lupus that have been refined and improved over millenia. Lupus minds are primed for learning not just through highly enhanced intelligence but through spiritual design and guidance. Their senses and instincts can actually help them navigate human social environments, as humans are still animals. Most garou don't live in cities, most septs are not in areas of high human population, and if defending Gaia is your purpose... well, a majority of the earth's surface is not yet inhabited by humans. Plus you know, the umbra. Lupus are difficult to play well, but they are not as foreign and hard to play as many players I know make them out to be. They seem intimidating to many because wolves are so different. But Lupus are not wolves, they are garou. Playing a lupus well shouldn't necessarily be harder than playing a homid well; homid players often downplay things like primal urge, rage, the curse, the difficulties of embracing their wolf aspects, as well as connecting with nature. Hell, most animals and spirits are going to be way more suspicious of you if they recognize you as homid rather than lupus. And lets not forget gnosis - the disparity in starting gnosis by breed is pretty damn big, and that isn't just mechanical! Coming into their full heritage as garou should be baffling for many years for pretty much any character.

                Minor nitpick: You mean sapient, not sentient. Sapience is "thinking" (aka why we call ourselves Homo sapiens), where sentience is an "awareness" and the ability to recognize oneself as a unique individual . Almost all social mammals (and probably a lot more) are provably sentient. Sapience is a very vague concept that's harder to nail down - and then prove exists - just like intelligence; but in general use the level of complex and abstract reasoning a pre-change Lupus is capable of is going to be above average for a wolf, but far below any changed Garou.

                        Erm, no. That's a specialized application regarding animal rights (the idea that sentient organisms like other animals suffer emotionally from pain, unlike say plants, and thus should be treated humanely). The philosophic concept of sentience refers to the ability to feel anything subjectively; rather than just reacting to a stimulus. Sentient and non-sentient organisms all respond to stimuli. Pain is one of those responses. Things like dread, joy, and anxiety are signs of sentience. The thing about those emotions is that they require an awareness of the context of the stimulus inspiring them.
