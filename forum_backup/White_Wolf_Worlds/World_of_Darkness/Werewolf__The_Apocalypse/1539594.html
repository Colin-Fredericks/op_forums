    W5 Shattered Nation - Preview

    07-31-2024, 01:15 PM
    Renegade released a preview of the upcoming W5 supplement "Shattered Nation" that has a section from the Introduction. The text presents the pitch of the book and what it is about:



    The text just reminds me why I have never been a fan of "The Garou Nation has fallen" and especially not in the wishy-washy way it was presented in the W5 corebook. This description is just such a mess in terms of worldbuilding.
    • The idea of something entering the realm of myth within one generation is so strange - especially while living people are around who still remember it.
    • It says "Older Garou have proven that they don't have what it takes...", okay... but they're still around. They can still take action. If we're going with the idea that the Fall happened in living memory, then things certainly can still be turned around by these people. I get the idea of condemning the failings of distant previous generations but old people who are still alive and able to take action...? They will still play a role in the local setting. And it's not like they're frail old humans in a retirement-home. We're talking about Garou here.
    • The Garou used to be able to work together - but also in the age of modern communication, the Garou found out (and why modern communication... no Garou ever talked to another Garou beyond the local level before then...?!) that everybody's doing things differently locally...? Then why did they all call themselves Garou, for example? This feels like the worst way to explain what the challenge of reforming/rebuilding the Garou Nation is. Also, this implies the pitch of a rebuilt Garou Nation should be to bulldoze local traditions...? Because why else would this be described as a challenge? Just such a bizarre paragraph...
    • If everything's local and nothing's universal... then wouldn't it make more sense in that framework for Tribes to be tied to specific people and/or regions...? Using this framework wouldn't you just recreate the old Tribes again, Egyptian Silent Striders, Russian Silver Fangs, Greek Black Furies etc.
    • Hopefully this book has more of a global perspective and gives examples of Garou-traditions from all around the world. Because it's hard to take this local focus seriously when all the adventures that have been released thus far were located in North America.

    This certainly has lowered my expectations for the supplement. It's like somebody just thoughtlessly threw all the typical WOD5E and W5 tropes together: street-level-only, lore is bad (nobody knows anything for some reason), old Garou are bad...

                      Tribes are definitely one of those things in the W5 corebook that could've needed an extra-dose of "re-imagining". And the idea of Tribes only coming into existence after the Fall because they're essentially divergent ideas of what to do makes more sense than what you get from the corebook. It also fits into the goal of making the Tribes more universal. So it's strange that the text also emphasizes the differences between local settings. On pg 232 of the W5 corebook in the Spirit Veracity sidebar, it says: "So, for example, a Ghost Council Garou with a Native American background may well perceive Horned Serpent in a different way than fellow Ghost Council Garou living amid Scandinavian, Iranian, or North African human cultures. Through the Garou lens, all of them are true, all of them are “the” Horned Serpent, even if all of them are different." That's why it's strange that this text is presenting it as a challenge. Because if you follow the logic of the W5 corebook, two Garou from two different locales should realize that they may have different names for these things but deep down they're both doing the same thing and have the same traditions. But what this text from Shattered Nation suggests is that you wouldn't have two Garou who while having different names for things belong to the Ghost Council Tribe and have the Horned Serpent as their Tribe-Patron-Spirit. What this proposes instead is that the two Garou talk to each other and they don't just have different names for stuff, they are of two different Tribes with different beliefs and practices and they have two different Tribe-Patron-Spirits.

                      Then again, tying things to the specifics of a locale is certainly one way you can enforce the street-level-only design-goal of WOD5E... It just would have been nice if they could've fulfilled their design-goals without contradicting themselves like this.

                      The same goes for the question of why the Garou-Nation is considered fallen... I'm sure one of the central ideas of this book is to play out a campaign that is attached to the "Your PC is King Albrecht's newest BFF"-Loresheet. But if King Albrecht is still around, if the old Garou who were at that disastrous gathering are also still around, if there are still a bunch of Garou all over the world living in Septs and having Moots... then in what way has the Garou Nation fallen apart? All the ingredients that used to make it tick are still around. It's not like King Albrecht died over a hundred years ago and nobody knows where the Silver Crown is.

                      Also, this could be another scenario where the Fenrir are just coming off as more sensible. If this is the gathering where the Fenrir had their hissy-fit, complained about the other Garou, got really angry and then left... Wouldn't the Fenrir be proven correct, if after all of that King Albrecht just gave up and and all the other Garou just went back to their caerns to mope till the world's end...? King Albrecht and the other supposedly "good Garou" would really come off like a bunch of losers if confronted by the angry Fenrir urging them to action, they just react with a shrug saying "Shit happens. I guess we've failed.".

                              Matt Stormcrow (he's a WOD-fan-content-creator primarily on TikTok, it seems... don't know his work) talked to Kevin Schluter (he's from Renegade and a Producer for their RPG-books) about Shattered Nation. The video can be found here. While they're talking, they're flipping through the book. I didn't take the time to pause the video and look too closely at the pages. I will read the whole thing anyway in a couple of weeks whenever the preview-pdf comes out.

                              But some takeaways from what I glimpsed:
                              • Pack-Types are done in the way that I hoped! They're an analogue to Coterie-Types.
                              • Sept-positions are back. It looked like it's pretty much everything from the previous editions plus some new additions like "Sheepwolf" (the Garou in charge of interacting with humans).
                              • There's a whole chapter dedicated to explaining the relationship between Garou and humans in a world without Kinfolk.
                              • Septs vary in how they relate to other Septs. I guess, that's part of that whole local focus that was already part of the excerpt I posted above. Hmm, that's the part that doesn't quite sit right with me. Because if the setting really leans into Garou being super-factionalized (where the divisions run so deep that they express themselves on a Sept-level) then there's no reason for the Cult of Fenris and the Stargazers to stand out/be unplayable and for the Cult in particular to be this demonized extraordinary antagonist.
                              • There were quite a few pages with just fluff in-character texts. If the writing's good, this could be good. W5 could use more flavor.
                              • There's a section about age-groups in Garou-society... Hmm... Hopefully it doesn't just end up being a "Old Garou are bad. Period."-explainer.
                              • Just as an aside, I wonder if this book will be more about the new lead-developer and the Renegade-team announcing that this is what they want W5 to be like in contrast to what is presented in the W5 corebook.

                                                  And here's another preview that makes me feel less hopeful again... The first two pages of the "Among Wolves"-chapter:
                                                  • The first couple of paragraphs in Layers just feel like a waste of space. The hierarchy of "Pack - Sept - Tribe - (in the past) Garou-Nation" had already been covered by the corebook. And as it points out, you'd think the smallest social unit would be the individual if you were to go by present-day modern viewpoints - so why isn't that the case in Garou-society? And what does that mean exactly? That's what needs to be covered. You don't need yet another superficial look at Garou society like the one mentioned here. And sure maybe the book will go into more detail on this later on. But you're still looking at a couple dozen words on this page here that were absolutely unnecessary and didn't add anything of worth.
                                                  • The Garou-Nation-skepticism still irks me. It always reads like somebody's ill-informed opinion that history before 1800 is just a bunch of hearsay and fairytales. Along with weird statements like "at a time when humans had barely managed to get from one continent to another". Okay, that was a long, LONG time ago and humans had been getting better at it ever since and for continents that were better positioned (like Europe, Africa and Asia) there have been strong connections for thousands of years.
                                                  • Also, who cares when "humans" were barely able to do this or that... We're not talking about humans here, we're talking about Garou/werewolves. This is a setting with supernatural elements! This is a setting where Spirits are real! Good fantastical settings work best when supernatural elements are employed in imaginative and creative ways. "How could there have been a global Garou-Nation when humans were barely able to travel between continents?" feels like the opposite of that. It feels more like somebody's argument for why they reject the setting/premise of this game. I don't think you win any points by allowing arguments like this and then just cover it up by saying "Who even knows what's true...". This just undermines the premise of the game.
