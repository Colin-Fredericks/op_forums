    Justice and punishment

    06-15-2017, 02:00 PM
    Say that a more pious and law abiding group of werewolvea lived in a city of about 100k mortals, and with only very rare reported wyrm or vampire activity.
    A young bone gnawer transforms for the first time, feels threatened by a gang in the city and ends up committing quintuple murderhby-Crinos on their asses.
    He is later introduced into the werewolf council and is accepted within the bone gnawer tribe. However, the murders have made the mortals ask allot of unsettlung questions, not to mention they were agents of the local Glass Walkers, the most influential tribe in these parts.

    What would happen to the newly transformed? If you want to be boring you could eityer go "kill him" or "forgive him" and be done with it. But isnt there a more complex scale of punishments? Or, couldnt the glass walkers at least demand some form of retribution?

                                  In the end most werewolves are going to accept that a new werewolf (even a 'gnawer) is more valuable than a three or four humans. It's unfortunate the newly changed werewolf killed them, but Rage is a fact of life, and a young pup can't really be held responsible for not being able to control himself. If he had been an experienced werewolf and knew what he was and killed some people that might be a different story. But I doubt the caern would agree to any particularly harsh punishment of a young werewolf who had no idea what he was doing. Certainly nothing like executing the poor kid.

                                  The Glasswalkers might ask that the new Bonegnawer do something useful for them, volunteer or otherwise offer some kind of reparation (nothing too dangerous) and the rest of the caern would go along with it. Ultimately I think the young werewolf would basically be given a stern warning and to let the incident that occurred serve as a powerful lesson for the future.
