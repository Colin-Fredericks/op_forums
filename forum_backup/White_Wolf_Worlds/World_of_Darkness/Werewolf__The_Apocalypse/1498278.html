    Banes and Fomori: the questioning.

    11-29-2022, 04:32 AM
    With the recent changes in the new edition stopping the games ive been in, ive had time to think. Thus, gremlin that i am, my mind drift to one of the more demented part of WOD lore: the fomori.

    I admit ive always been interested in the fomori as characters more than the garou, their personal and varied horrorshow was a change to spiritually enforced familial bonds of the garou.

    Ive made fomori characters myself, and aided friends who grew an interest; coming here on occasion for advice. In that time ive learned a few things:

    1. fomori player characters best adapted from the Freak Legions supplement. The guides in the main books are aimed at npc fomori creation and are noticeably more constricting in available options; also lacks the "bane and power" system that gives role-playability which is needed since fomori lack touchstones.

    2. Fomori characters dont work on normal games, at least not long ones. They only fit well in evil games (playing as the wyrm), or oddly in crossovers. Fomori work well in VTM, their various issues and needs parallel many vampires and can cooperate well in a rpg.

    3. Fomori are evil and malevolent, but not in the same way. Sure, there are some that are moral degenerates that sew pain and suffering; having been evil in the beginning or down the line of possession. However, even more are just at the mercy of their own mutations; hell, some fomori arnt even a threat, just a pathetic pile of weeping flesh. Fomori have more variety than dog breeds.

    I still have parts i question. If anyone has knowledge of these via document or precedent; please advise.
    Questions:

    1. How lore accurate is fomori cooperation with other supernaturals? I mentioned, the games ive experienced mechanics-wise, but in-game that was justified by the PCs being friends before eachs transformation, and "rolling with it". I question institutionalized cooperation; like Pentex and the Sabbat rooting out garou, or a coterie teaming with fomori mercs an elder kept on retainer due to "family trust issues".

    2. Can fomori be embraced, or ghouled? I cant find a reference to either of these. I know lore-wise both are creatures of the wyrm, so its not hard to believe both could gain more (or worse) than the base effect of blood or vitae consumption. Fomori are ... technically human, so ghoulification or embrace are theoretically possible, but i need a second opinion. Can embrace/ghoulism happen to a fomori? Why/why not? What possible differences would both sides experience if tried?

    3. What are the deeper effects of the spiritual connection between fomori and banes? I know the process ftmp:
    -bane infests/is implanted in the a void created in a human soul by horrible life experiences or being a terrible person.
    -bane feeds off of an associated emotion or experiences, influencing the hosts emotions and actions too increase the amount of prior mentioned foods source.
    -bane infests host over a long period of time without interruption of exorcism or plain life improvement.
    -bane fuses (for the most part) with the host soul and flesh, becoming one entity, a fomor.
    This is the main process, but can it go deeper? Some fomori are so connected to their bane that they can have long conversations. Some fomori can even have a kinship with banes, conversing with them (though garou can too). Maeljin are described as being "once human", but now spirit beings.
    Could it be that a fomori that lives long enough or becomes powerful enough without dying or devolving, will eventually evolve into a full bane?

    4. What are the characteristics elemental banes and fomori? There isnt much indepth info on banes, a few get spotlight in the book as used in specific fomori. Most banes can have their nature alluded too or obvious in some cases (rage banes personality is likely self-explanatory). Freakfeet fomori are created via sludge banes so there IS precedent for elemental fomor.
    What about balefire/furmas?:
    -Balefire is spiritually corrupt fire.
    -manifestes as green lavalamp-like fire whose emitted light is radioactive.
    -is rules by the maeljin, lord Kerne.
    -is believed to be the primordial wyrms blood.
    -is associated with willpower.
    Given this information, what would the personality of a balefire bane (furmling) be? We could assume by characterizing furmas, one would be somewhat disassociated with mortal emotions(elemental), aggressive or brazen(fire), focused on curruption or ecological devastation(balefires mutative effects) rather than more selfish singular goals (dominanting nature of lord kerne).
    What kind of fomori would a furmling create? What do think? What of other bane types? Any idea would be much appreciated.

          These are my answers, but keep in mind when I use fomori in my own chronicles I often diverge from their typical depiction in WtA. While I think fomori are a great concept because it is an easy way to justify any number of supernatural creatures that were once human or animal (or even plant) and are now a monster, I find most of the WtA canonical fomori very lackluster. They don't impress me or inspire me. I get a lot more mileage of the concept by using the concept to include more creatures from myth and legend or other horror properties in the game. I also tend to not use them generically, but create various factions based around certain concepts.

          Obviously monstrous or inhuman fomori often works, but I think the game tends to overplay those. I also like a lot of fomori to not appear inhuman so they can infiltrate society just as well as non-fomori.

          1. Fomori tend to not get mentioned much in other game lines anymore, but I feel that is a result of the Revised era wanting each game line to be separate and stop presenting an integrated setting. But as you stated we know PENTEX fomori cooperate with Sabbat vampires and Black Spiral Dancers. Fomori are mentioned in the Mage Book of Madness sourcebook. And in Rage Across Russia, Baba Yaga was building an "army" of various supernatural creatures. So there are individual or groups of fomori that ally themselves with others or cooperate with others.

          2. I think fomori can be blood bonded like anyone. Although the Bane possessing it would not be, and if the blood bond interfered with its plans, that might be excuse to break it or the bane may decide to just leave. Actually becoming a ghoul would usually be superfluous, but sometimes that point of Potence might provide something extra. In terms of embrace, I think this is mostly a point of taste. Do you really want to multiple splat templates on top of one another? I personally would not, and would rule that the act of dying drives out the bane and thus terminates the status of fomori before the corpse turns into a vampire. Depending on how long the person has been a fomori or the type of fomori, the newly embraced vampire that comes out of it may not be to your expectation (perhaps already a wight, lots of Flaws like Monstrous, low virtues, etc.) If you do want to stack splats, just accept your game is going to be a lot more gonzo in terms of what is out there. But different people have different tastes.

          3. Fomori and banes are not the same, and one does not turn into the other. The Maeljin Incarna were once human because the Wyrm can rise individuals to Incarna status under certain conditions. At least two Garou have become Incarna because they danced the entire Spiral (Hakaken and Number Two, the ruler of Malfeas). The Urge Wyrms can do the same when they want. They might choose to raise a fomori to that level or an ordinary human being as I understand the lore. Though in my own chronicles, I have a special breed of fomori associated with the Urge Wyrms that are divided into "cults" devoted to a specific Urge Wyrm, and the Urge Wyrm typically selects from one of those to become the next Maeljin. That is my own invention however.

          One thing I do for certain fomori though is allow them to progress from one instar to another. So as some fomori age and grow in experience or master more occult lore, they can get more powerful. Their stats increase, they get additional powers or their existing power advances. As long as it makes sense for the concept.

          4. These kind of questions are going to answer themselves as long as you give some thought to them. Spirits have purposes. I tend to run Furmlings much like Fire Vampires in Call of Cthulhu. They like to set things on fire because that is their nature. In terms of the fomori you can create from them, you have lots of inspirations to choose from. However, I would be careful because you can easily get over the top and end up with something more appropriate for a superhero game rather than a gothic horror setting. You don't want the Human Torch. But a pyrokinetic like Charlie in Stephen King's Firestarter, or Elizabeth Sherman in Hellboy would work. I'd give them the ability to create fire, to control flame, and probably effective immunity from fire. I don't care if there are not existing fomori powers in the book. I feel free to use whatever Charms, Gifts, Disciplines, Numina, Sorcery Path, or whatever is needed so they can do what they like. I am very free form as long as it accomplishes what it needs.

          In terms of other fomori, I use actual myths and legends for inspiration, horror media in general, and urban legends as long as it fits my conception of the World of Darkness. So I steal a lot and do my best to rename things. So I have Lovecraftian ghouls in my chronicles. I have the equivalents of the White Court vampires and Red Court vampires from the Dresden Files, although neither groups uses the name "vampire" to refer to themselves. I have underground dwelling derro from the Shaver Mysteries. I have various monsters from Greek Myth like harpies and sirens as well as various monstrous animals. I have fungal "zombies" inspired by the cordyceps fungal parasites that take over ants (I later learned the same concept had also been used in the computer game The Last of Us). I have fake Wendigo that are more like the actual legends than the Garou tribes that are created by banes of Narlthus. I have a secret society of Illuiminati-like "reptilians" that appear human and have tremendous influence in the mortal world rivalling vampires. Anything that I like which I think fits the World of Darkness and was once human but is now supernatural, I can usually justify creating a new fomori for it.

          I tend to not try to match existing banes to existing fomori, because most of the time the people who created them were not thinking like that. It is usually best to create your own banes to create the new kinds of fomori you want.

          My main issue is making sure to keep the overall fomori population low because it is so easy to go overboard and create way too many. As a result that encourages me to keep local Garou populations low, and to expand the chronicle setting to include more septs (though far away) in order to provide more interaction with other Garou.
