                  To come at it from a slightly different direction:

                  The WoD tends to give STs the option of having children notice the supernatural as a thematic part of the game. There's no game where children are mechanically automatically capable of piercing supernatural effects. This is purely for atmosphere. Is a normal child with an imaginary friend just a normal child doing a normal child thing, or are they interacting with a supernatural normally invisible to normal humans? Is that creepy child just having a goth/emo phase, or have they been haunted for as long as they can remember and accept ghosts are real and have to cope with it since nobody believes them? This is all stock trope and genre dressing in the source media the WoD was built from. The WoD is not a setting where the innocence and naivety of children is a source of hope or positivity... but rather a source of horror because it just makes children that much more targets of the darkness.

                  So... does being immune to Delirium help that? Not IMO. People at risk of Delirium are seeing a giant murder monster openly acting. Children acting "normally" would largely just act like they were under Delirium anyway: terror. A child that sits around calmly as a pack of Garou rip a squad of fomori guards to shreds is not making the WoD a more Gothic-Dark sort of setting. It's turning kids into already broken people.

                                      If we're going to get into development psychology and how children react, I think the biggest thing to change in how Delirium would work would be that younger children "fantasize" instead of rationalize. That is, younger children don't have a strong coherent mental model of the world for major new events to fit into. They can't rationalize as we picture the concept because new stimuli aren't challenging a strong set of presuppositions that require explaining the challenge, nor do they have the tools for that yet anyway. So they're going to make something up, that could range from odd by plausible to wildly fantastic but wildly inaccurate to what actually happened.

                                              I'm particularly prone to this interpretation because it increases the impact of any effect that removes the Delirium or otherwise forces the person to remember, or of just being immune to it, and how this may be good for the player, but is a curse for the character.
                                              The books kind of jump around on this, too. It might be conflating it with the Curse when it does.
