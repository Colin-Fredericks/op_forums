                Fair? Or too harsh?
                It's fair, I think.

                Chapter 1 doesn't add a lot. It's stuff like it proposes a good first question as ST for Session Zero is "Do you have questions about WtA?". It's really that obvious and surface-level. Like this point in the Session Zero section: "What do you hope to experience from the pack dynamic? (As the Storyteller, you can elaborate on the question to help your troupe: Should the pack have a leader? If yes, what kind of responsibilities do they have? Should everyone have a unique role, or would you prefer a more fluid dynamic?)" The book really lets itself off the hook with this stuff by simply saying "Let the players tell you how they think this should be handled." instead of delving into Auspices and Tribes and the interplay between them. And then if players want things to be different, sure. But simply expecting players to handle it on their own feels like a cop-out to avoid delving into the WtA-setting. And that then leaves this chapter with advice you pretty much already got from the W5 corebook.

                Hmm, really could be that Insect Spirits from Shadowrun were the inspiration here. I was wondering why it had to be insects. They got this explanation in that chapter that these insect-Spirits called Vaegesti, are Weaver Spirits corrupted by the Wyrm. Why not pick something more Wyrm-y to begin with?

                And yeah, it's easy to picture Chapter 3 within a videogame. And then it doesn't help that the last adventure is unable to tie it all together in a good way.

                Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
                W5 treats tribe the same as VtM treats a clan, it is a personal struggle over a communal one. Even Forsaken has a community based on a tribe.

                The garou of W5 truly seem isolated, not just socially but emotionally. Since you can never have another werewolf as your Touchstone and only one wolf, it seems the setting encourages this as well. Even your pack patron is banned from serving as a support network, likely because Touchstones are supposed to be links to 'normalcy.'

                It does explain the world of W5 a lot better than the core, though. It isn't that the werewolves are apathetic, it is that they just don't see the need to complicate matters with other garou. Or that they feel the unity of the Nation was a sham.
                The whole "Lack of numbers is one of the main-problems for Garou."-bit also helps, I think. That way it's not necessary to castigate Garou society at every turn (for example, there's still value to things like the Litany) and being an Elder doesn't come with some nebulous inherent moral deficiency (instead it's a result of breaking under the pressure of a worsening situation). With this take can center it all on "There are a lot of problems and not a lot of Garou.". At least, it's better than the way it's portrayed in the corebook.​

                                        The emphasis is on "broad" in W5, though. Black Furies are a prime example of this. You have an individual Garou and if they truly care about justice they're able to make a pact with Gorgon which then makes them a Black Fury. But questions of what exactly justice is and matters like what is righteous to do in the name of justice and what not... All of that is entirely individualized. You ask two Black Furies about what justice is and you get two different answers. That's what I mean.

                                        W5 Tribes are way broader than WtA Tribes, not just in concept but also in prioritizing individuals over the community. Tribe has much less communal value in W5 than in previous editions. Being of the same Tribe is of little value in W5 when it comes to companionship and shared identity. The things people of the same Tribe share in W5 are so broad that the individual identity matters more than being a member of any Tribe.

                                        In this book's third adventure Ghost Council PCs are just having an easier time convincing the Horned Serpent that Ridhaan (who's also a Ghost Council member) is wrong. Although I wonder how this would land with players when they hear about the relationship between Ridhaan and the Horned Serpent when one of the PCs is also a Ghost Council Garou. Because that moment would land very differently if you don't ascribe to the notion I outlined above.
