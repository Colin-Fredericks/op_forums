                                                            Justin's comments seem to be leaning back into Forsaken: where werewolves bite suspected new werewolves as part of bringing them into the fold. In Forsaken this is considered the source of the "biting spreads lycanthropy" legends by humans only getting part of the picture.

                                                            Forsaken just... actually fully explores this enough for it to make sense. In Forsaken the point isn't to try to ID new werewolves (it's not particularly hard to do so) but because Uratha have a significant tracking bonus if they've tasted a target's blood before. Biting pre-change or newly changed werewolves lets them stalk and pick how to approach the situation more easily. This doesn't need to be violent either. An Uratha could sneak into a probable new-werewolf's room at night and bite them carefully before sneaking back out. They only need a taste, not to try to rip a limb off. But over thousands of years of this practice, you can understand how the human myths have noticed a pattern without necessarily figuring out what's really going on.

                                                            Forsaken 2e really tried to fine tune high Harmony (aka out of balance towards The Flesh) as where most newly changed werewolves are going to be. If they fight to reject their wolf and spirit sides, it's only going to push them higher up the Harmony ladder, and the effects of that are very similar to a lot of pop culture werewolf stuff. Harmony 8+ is a recipe for a lot of movie-werewolf tropes to come in because it means extremely difficult access to purposeful shifting, any sort of control over access to the spirit world, your clothing shreds off of you instead of melding with you automatically, and you Rage-shift a lot. Such werewolves are in a very delicate state, and getting a lick of their blood to try to find them at the safest times to approach and to try to find out about/take into account other factors makes a bunch of sense.

                                                            The biting to try to provoke a First Change as the only reason to try to bite doesn't really make sense.
