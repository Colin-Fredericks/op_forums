    WIR Pre-made Werewolf Character Templates

    10-07-2023, 11:22 AM
    Following up the previous WIR Pre-made Vampire Character Templates thread, I will now go back and look at the pregenerated characters from the original second edition Werewolf tribebooks.

    Starting with 1994 with Black Furies, written by Phil Brucato (with some additional advice and input from Judith A. McGlaughlin) and artwork by Ron Spencer. Brucato became best known as the long running Mage developer, and Spencer would go on to be one the signature artists for Werewolf. It's interesting to look at his art throughout the book. His style is one he clearly refined and improved upon over the next decade, but you can clearly see what would become his distinctive style in the full page chapter opening splash pages. The template character art is a little rougher around the edges, and I think he may be trying to do something similar to Josh Timbrook's art from the Vampire clanbooks, but I can't say for certain.

    (I actually am lucky enough to own an original copy of one of Spenser's pieces, the full page chapter opener he did for Mage's Dead Magic 2's Norse chapter. The reprinted work you see in the various White Wolf books really do not do justice to the incredible amount of detail work he puts into these things, including the inking which gives the original piece an almost three dimensional quality to it.)

    And something I'd sort of forgotten about before rereading these was that White Wolf got super heavy into the use of quotes, sometimes from films or books, but often from music. Sometimes these work. Other times they are such low hanging fruit or borderline out of place thanks to questionable context (or lack thereof). Thankfully, White Wolf really started to dial this back later in the decade.

    Street Rat (Homid Ragabash)

    So, starting out with the chorus line from "Bad Reputation" by Joan Jett & The Blackhearts. There's that low hanging fruit I mentioned. Anyway, the character is an orphan and apparently a hunchback. She ran away from the orphanage at age 12 because the Priest was ... abusive. (That's as much as a I feel like saying on the subject.) She fell in with a gang of dangerous metal heads, but eventually had her First Change and was found by a member of her tribe. That tribe mate is now her much suffering Mentor who tries to get her to behave to some minor degree. I do like that sort of detail into the personal relationships behind Backgrounds. Roleplaying hints can be summed up as you have a lot of trust issues - especially with anything involving religion - and expect to be betrayed by everyone, but are afraid of being abandoned and alone, so you cover with a massive amount of bravado and bad attitude. I think that's interesting and you could get a lot of roleplaying mileage out of that. Equipment is a switchblade, 9mm handgun, leather jacket, flash powder, piano wire, and "assorted tricks".
    Getting to the character sheet stats, things are a little off. Physicals are secondary, with a 2/4/2 split. Socials are tertiary, but we have them at 3/1/1. So at least one dot is missing. I guess I can go with an Appearance of 1 for a hunchback gutter punk who sleeps on the streets and probably doesn't put much thought into personal hygiene. Although I can't help but wonder if the hunchback thing is an afterthought to somehow justify that low Appearance rating. I'm guessing that the Manipulation is supposed to be a 2. Mentals are primary, with 3/2/5. Personally, I might've gone for 4/2/4, but whatever. Wits has a specialization of Paranoid, which makes sense.
    Talents are primary, with what appears to be six extra points spent here, for a total of 16 dots. Intimidation and Streetwise are both at 4, which totally fits this character. Brawl is 3, Alertness and Dodge are 2, and Subterfuge is 1. Skills are secondary, with a 4 in Melee (and a Knife specialization), a 2 in Firearms and Stealth, and 1 in Drive. Knowledges are tertiary with one extra dot, for Enigma and Occult at 2 and Law and Ritual at 1. I feel that dot in Law might've been better spent on something like Investigation, Linguistics, or Medicine, which feel like they'd be more useful when living on the streets.
    Backgrounds are 1 dot in Mentor and 2 dots in Allies, which in the character write up may have been meant to be Kinfolk. Then we have a Rage of 4, a Gnosis of 3, and a Willpower of 5. This appears to throw off the freebie point count, with 3 for the rage, 4 (two each) for the Gnosis, and 2 for WP. With the 8 for abilities, that makes 17 total. I think back in the early days, there was a tendency to just dump points into "power stats" like Rage and Gnosis, without giving any thought to how this might impact the character's personality or behavior. (Revised edition was a lot more careful about this.) I don't think this somewhat high level of primal fury is totally out of place with this character, but I think 3 might've been better, at least IMO. The Gnosis on the other hand, seems a little arbitrary. Nothing about this character indicates much of any sense of spirituality or self-reflection. (Given that Hunchback is one of the Metis deformities listed in the 2nd ed core book, I can't help but wonder if this character started out as a Metis and was later changed to a Homid. I'll also mention that the Hunchback Metis deformity also includes a penalty toward Dexterity and Social roles, but the write up doesn't mention that anywhere.)
    That artwork is ... interesting. It's maybe one of the roughest and ugliest things I've seen from Spenser. I again mention that it looks like he may be trying to do something similar to Timbrook. I think it works OK for the character though. It's a very rough looking female gutter punk of the early 90s.
    Overall, this character is OK. It isn't exactly my first thought for a Black Fury, which is often a good thing. However, I don't think there's really much here that says Black Fury. The write up flat out says that its only a matter of happenstance that she isn't a Bone Gnawer, and that's sort of more what she feels like. And I say that as one of the last people who'd ever really argue that "concept X" can only fit in "tribe Y". But given how the tribe books only have five templates, one for each auspice, I feel like maybe they should strive to be a little more archetypical. (Honestly, I wish they'd gone with 10 templates like the Vampire books had, so you could do two of each Auspice, for one that's more archetypical and one more boundary breaking. Plus you can have more than a single Lupus and Metis.) Overall, I think it's a perfectly fine character. Just not the best character for this particular place, maybe.

    Anyway, that's part one. Next will be part two, with the Theurge and Philodox characters.

          A street rat Black Fury is a perfectly acceptable concept, but as you say, it'd be better if there was something more distinctly Black Fury about her. It doesn't need to be her pre-Change origins, but something that came out as a result of her tutelage as a cub. Runaway street girls are particularly at risk of sexual abuse, and some comment about her providing protection to other female street kids would have moored her firmly in the Black Fury tradition (as well as explain her Allies listed in the sheet, although the background implies they are kinfolk). A "Big Sister" type person who looks after her "little sisters".

          It also would have been better to integrate the Ragabash aspect into her background. What exactly does she do that is particularly Ragabash? She has a good selection of Gifts, and it would be nice to know how she would make use of them. Maybe she's acts as a scout and look out, and going back to what I said earlier, maybe her street kid allies help her with being look outs as well.

          Someone with a hunchback also isn't a bad concept. But if you're going with that, then the character needs to do something with it. It's just an odd comment to make, and then never refer to it again. Just as a reason why other kids made fun of her, well there's any other number of physical or mental or emotional reasons for that. She could have been a stutterer, had a lazy eye, was extremely covered in freckles, been extremely shy, or any other number of things that might have isolated her and made her a target. I also find it odd to mention she's a hunchback and then give her a Dexterity of 4, as I simply don't associate spinal disorders with being very dextrous. Maybe it's a slight hunchback? But if so, again why mention it at all?

          I agree with the comment on increasing Rage and Gnosis solely as power states, and neglecting to think what impact that should have on the character. But that was a common problem all throughout 1e/2e and to a degree even in Revised.

          Are you sure Ron Spencer is the artist on the templates? Although he is the only artist listed, I clearly see some artwork done by Dan Smith (recognizable by his signature of SMIF) in that first tribebook. So if WW failed to credit him, they might have failed to credit Joshua Timbrook too.

              I love templates as a concept, since I was a wee 14 year old anime nerd finding WtA.

              However, I only saw the 1st ed tribebooks later in life so I got no strong attachment to any of them.

              I can, however, say this; A lot of the 1st ed. templates are trying to 'find' themselves still. The lore isn't as developed and ideas bounce around randomly. Also, the Get of Fenris ahroun template made me genuinely burst out laughing when I saw them.

              If I am being honest, only the theurge, galliard and ahroun really read 'Fury' to me. Black Furies are a tribe that needs to balance itself between their Rage, their caring nature and their mysticism. While the Fury ways of mysticism are unique to them, angry!Fury can end up sounding like Fenrir and caring!Fury like a Coggie. To balance this out, I've often made Furies into a tribe that is either radical women defender or a frightening witches throwing curses.

                              So earlier today I got to thinking, in response to people’s dissatisfaction to the sample character Black Fury Ragabash and it got me thinking, what would I describe as the archetypal Black Fury ragabash? I’m also curious what other people’s answer to that would be

                              Heres my idea: The Living Taboo (Warborn, Ragabash, Black Fury)

                              Born from the union of a Black Fury Garou mother and a male Garou, the product of this union is the Living Taboo, the only masc. Garou who Flying-Horse accepts into their tribe. Once it was practically unheard of for the warborn to survive their birth without being slain in hopes of absolving the taint of their birth, but Flying-Horse is the avenger of wrongs done against the primeval order and forswears the slayings - their daughters must see the culmination of their violations and must learn from it.

                              The Living Taboo lives at the heart of the contradictions of the Black Fury tribe. The only masculine members allowed in a tribe governed by matriarchs, the Living Taboo is a testament to Furies who violate the ancient laws. Yet the Taboo must live as well. They are the only men allowed in the tribe, being absolved of the crimes of Man by the greater violation of their birth. The Living Taboo is uniquely positioned to be an outsider and to question the way of their tribe. Not necessarily as a masculine perspective, being sterile and raised within Black Fury society may well preclude this, but as a living testament to the greater scope of the Furies’ duties. They are dedicated to not simply avenging the subjugation of the Mother by the Patriarch but to all other violations of the primordial laws - the defilement of sacred places, the turning of blood-kin against blood-kin, incestuous unions, etc. If the Furies are to be the avenger of the most primordial transgressions than the Living Taboo serves as a reminder to them that they must not allow themselves to become myopic in their visions.

                              (Note 1: As readers can probably tell, this concept is linked with my perspective on the Black Furies as a tribe that their task is the avenging of violations against primordial laws, not just the Patriarch’s Usurpation but all other violations. As inspiration you might look to something like the arguments of Clytemnestra or the Furies from the Orestaia)

                              (Note 2: Another inspiration for this was the greater emphasis placed in first edition, in my opinion, of the nascent schism between the minority of masculine Furies and the rest of the tribe, embodied in the character of Spartacus - and of course his name is Spartacus, oh White Wolf… - and his insurrections against the leadership of the tribe)

                                      And finally part 2.

                                      Vagabond (Homid Theurge)

                                      So this one starts with a quote from Kate Bush's "Hounds of Love", which isn't a bad song. So this character is an outsider, "with some wild passion no one could explain", who ran away from home at 15 to live by herself in the woods. And somehow didn't die of starvation or exposure. (I honestly can't help but wonder if Brucato was familiar with the story of Chris McCandless, a young man from suburban Virginia who decided to venture out into the Alaskan wilderness alone only to die there. His journal he left behind became the basis for a 1993 magazine article that was later expanded into a book, Into the Wild, which later became a film. It's a fascinatingly tragic story if anyone is interested.) Anyway, our character was found by a wolf pack whose alpha submitted to her. Later a Black Fury appeared and guided her through her first change. But instead of joining a Sept or Pack, she remains with the Kinfolk wolf pack that found her.
                                      The spirits speak to her, but she can't understand them (yet). She's described as unstable, having no social skills, and her powers of concentration needing work. But (!) she's also described as a a consummate scout, good in a scrap, and her years in the Wyld giving her insight into Gaia's mysteries. Keep that in mind when I get to the stats later. Though human born, she's "more wolf than wolf", and prefers to be left alone ("A true lone wolf.") She might join a pack at some point.
                                      Equipment is a survival knife and the clothes on her back.

                                      The image is of a wolf. That's it. It's a pretty nice image. That's about all I can say about it.

                                      Attributes have Physicals as primary, with an extra dot for a 3/3/5 spread, and a specialization of "tough" for Stamina. Socials are tertiary, with a spread of 1/1/4, and a specialization of "wild" for Appearance. Mentals are secondary, with 4/1/3, and a specialization of "feral" for Perception.
                                      Boy, that's a lot of 1s. I feel like if someone were writing such a character today, they'd specify that she was autistic or some other form of neuro-divergence.
                                      Abilities are a bit of a mess to be honest. We have 10 talent dots, 11 skill dots, and five knowledge dots. So, presuming talents are secondary with an extra dot from freebies, skills are missing at least two dots, and really missing four dots because two of those dots are raising their skills to 4 and have been bought with freebies as well. Talents are three dots each in Alertness, Athletics, and Primal Urge, and a single dot in Empathy. For skills, we have 4s in Stealth (Woodlands) and Survival (No Gear), 2 in Animal Ken, and 1 in Melee. Knowledges are 3 in Enigmas, and 1 each in Medicine and Occult.
                                      I feel like the Melee was just an afterthought here. And given that the No Gear specialization in Survival would be nullified by using the survival knife, it makes me think the character would be better without it. Especially if you used it to raise Animal Ken to 3 instead. Also, make Talents primary, so you can put a few dots in Brawl and Dodge to make good on the previous claim that she's "pretty good in a scrap".
                                      Backgrounds put all three dots in Kinfolk, which makes sense given the wolf pack. I'm not sure why a character who has no social aptitude and shuns social interaction would have Persuasion as a gift, but when the only other option is Smell of Man, which feels even less fitting, I guess you go with what's available. Rage is 4, so I guess it is again just being used as a power stat. Gnosis is 2. Remember what I said earlier about years in the Wyld giving her an insight into Gaia's mysteries? Well, her Gnosis is lower than that of the angry gutter rat street punk. With the alterations to abilities I mentioned, you'd have some points to boost Gnosis to at least three.

                                      So, much like the Street Rat, I find myself thinking that this is a mostly good character (ability issues aside), but I'm not really feeling that it cries out Black Fury or Theurge. Honestly, if Werewolf had a version of Mage's Orphans (like the Ghost Wolves in Forsaken), this would feel like she should be one of those. And I'm not exactly sure how this really evokes Seeker of the Ways. If it was a Galliard, I could easily see it being the idea of Lover of the Ways as being completely absorbed with the Wyld, wolf-dom, and nature. But as a Theurge, it feels a little off.

                                      Anyway, that's the Black Fury Theurge. Next will be the Philodox.

                                          I'm in broad agreement with your assessment. Again, the core concept is fine, but needs tweaking to really make it feel like a Theurge or Black Fury. Some slight adjustments to character sheet would greatly improve it. Increase Gnosis at expense of Rage, and change Sense Wyrm for Spirit Speech. Emphasize the connection she feels with the wild. Reveal had she strong, primal dreams before she ran away, and that somehow gave her the instincts she needed to survive in the wilderness before her First Change. Or perhaps spend some Freebies in Past Life/Ancestors.

                                          The misfit outsider concepts fits the 1 in Charisma and Manipulation, but I don't think the 1 in Intelligence works. That's below average and makes her kind of a dunce, not just a social misfit. With her strong physicals, the concept becomes a slow witted beefcake of a woman, which is not what the text implies at all.

                                          I'd probably make her Talents Primary and Skills Secondary instead of vice versa as you suggested. It would give her some extra dots to put into Dodge and Brawl. I think the dot in Melee and very high Stealth and Survival was because the character had too many points here and needed to be put somewhere, and the concept restricted what made sense.

                                          Overall, it's a solid concept, but needs some work to elevate it as a Black Fury Theurge instead of generic Garou. It's certainly a concept a first time player can take and justify her involvement in a new chronicle with her friends.

                                          One thing I do like with both templates is that they lack Pure Breed. The true heritage of these characters are unknown. And to a degree, their involvement with the Black Furies is accidental. They might have been found by any tribe and indoctrinated in their ways instead of the ways of the Black Fury. Quarrels over Lost Cubs between tribes was a prominent theme in the early sourcebooks that was later forgotten in favor of Garou being from known lineages and growing up as a Garou.
