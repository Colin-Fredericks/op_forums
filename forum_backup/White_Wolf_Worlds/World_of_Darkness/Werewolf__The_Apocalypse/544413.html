    Flavor Text Ideas for Donating Gnosis during Caern Moot

    06-07-2015, 07:16 PM
    I'm looking at ideas for flavor text and examples of how Garou donate Gnosis, like to a Caern spirit during a moot.

    In the real world, temple sacrifices and such were done by sacrificing animals. Occasionally, this kind of blood sacrifice finds itself in Werewolf, usually by a Get of Fenris spilling his own blood to some spirit.

    Mechanically of course, the Gnosis just disappears from a character sheet. However, I'm interested in how people have done it or seen it happen in games. I assume most people have simply hand waved it (reduce your Gnosis by 1, and let's move on). However, I think at least some STs or players have done more. How do people see this happening? Just the Garou "concentrating" or "meditating" or "praying" and the Gnosis leaves them (essentially this means the Garou does nothing, at least physically)? Or does the Gnosis transfer because the Garou does something - make an animal sacrifice, burn incense, make some kind of formal proclamation, or perform an ecstatic dance that leaves physically and mentally exhausted?

    I'll be running a Caern Moot for the first time in game (as opposed to downtime), and I like to describe it in terms of real life - not game mechanics - as much as I can. While I think every Caern will be different based on its Totem, I think the vast majority of Caerns would likely have some kind of animal sacrifice. Of course, the game makes no mention of this. I'm wondering how people have seen it happen in their games.

      I don't have any in-game anecdotes for this, but I'm perusing Werewolf Player's Guide 2nd Ed (pp.93-96) to review the particular sections of a moot. Relevant excepts from this book are summed up below:

      The Opening Howl, led by the Master of the Howl (an office typically held by a Galliard), starts the moot. After the sept has finished howling, the Master of the Howl announces the purpose of the moot.

      The second part of the moot is The Inner Sky. The Caller of the Wyld leads the sept in this portion of the moot. Umbral spirits are the source of the caern's continued strength; as such, the Inner Sky is vital to the caern's health. The Caller of the Wyld must contact the spirits and treat them with appropriate honor and deference.

      The third part of the moot is called Cracking the Bone. This is when sept business is discussed, grievances are aired, and all temporal business is conducted at this time. Most tribes allow all Garou who wish to speak to do so. Even in this openness there is a proper procedure, however. The concept of rank is so ingrained in most Garou that lower ranking members inevitably defer to higher ranking individuals. A Garou who speaks out of turn, defying the sept hierarchy, invariably loses Honor. Still, some of the more tolerant septs, usually led by the Bone Gnawers or Children of Gaia, allow their younger members to speak out of turn without harsh penalty. The Garou system of justice is simpler than that used in the human world, as it has more in common with lupine ways. Judgment and the punishment that follows is swift, blunt and without appeal. Once a decision is made, for good or ill, the matter is usually closed.

      The fourth part of the moot is known as Stories and Songs and is led by the Talesinger. Tales of Garou heroism, old and new, are spun by the Talesinger. The nature of these stories depends on the tribe of the sept.

      Lastly is The Revel. This is the part of the moot where the Garou donate their Gnosis to the caern. Revels involve mock battles and other displays of strength and prowess erupt spontaneously throughout the sept, as the Garou prepare for the run. These demonstrations serve to take the Garou past the point of no return, a truly liberating release on every level. Once the sept reaches this degree of excitement, the Wyrm Foe thunders out of the caern proper, leading the entire sept on an exhausting run to clear the area around the caern of all enemies. During the run itself, many Garou eventually transform into their deadly Crinos form, as they fully embrace their Rage.

      Although any Garou in the throes of the Revel is a gloriously fearsome sight, the Get of Fenris are renowned for their incredible savagery during the run. The defense of caerns by the Get and other rural septs give rise to many small-town stories of "full moon fever." Similarly, the urban prowls of Bone Gnawer and Glass Walker Revels are often labeled gang warfare or particularly vicious killing sprees by the unsuspecting human populace.

      The Garou passion released during the Revel is effectively raw Gnosis that pours back into the caern itself. Such recharging is vital in maintaining the caern's power, just as sustaining the caern's connection to the Umbra is. Every Garou who participates in a Revel must spend at least one point of Gnosis toward recharging the caern. An active caern must receive five points of Gnosis per
      power level each lunar month to remain fully replenished. Thus, to replenish a caern with a power level of four, the sept must spend at least 20 Gnosis. These points need not be spent all at once (although they usually are during a Revel), but can be given to the caern at any time during the month. A caern that doesn't receive the requisite amount of Gnosis lapses into inactive status. Although its potential spiritual energy still exists, the caern must be reconnected to the Umbra and its spirits awakened before Garou may tap its power.

          I am running a historical chronicle in Germany. The tribes are near a major city. The sept includes mostly Silver Fangs and Get of Fenris, but Glass Walkers and sometimes Bone Gnawers can also be found there.               As varied as the spirits that inhabit the world.

              In our games, we've run the donation in all kinds of ways, including some unscrupulous and cunning sorts finding ways to trick others into sacrificing more than they might otherwise intend. Some thoughts from ways I've run it or seen it run:

              The type of Caern it is, and the Caern's Totem(s) and local Spirits should definitely be kept in mind when a Garou is wanting to donate Gnosis. You wouldn't necessary peacefully meditate in the glen of a Caern of Rage to return your own essence to the land - in some situations providing in inappropriate sacrifice or method could even be disrespectful. Sacrifice in the form of harm or slaying would be anathema to Caerns of peace, tranquility, or the like, and would at the very least simply not count toward the Gnosis. Theoretically Caerns that receive a great deal donations in in forms that are antithetical to the Caern's purpose and spirit would poison it, if somehow left uncorrected - although it would have to be a really terrible Sept and Keeper of the Land to let things get so far.

              Shrines of Adoration were often used in our games, and gifts imparted to those sites also trickled to the Caern, so any positive spiritual behaviour appropriate for the Caern would maintain the spiritual ecology of the place. When Moot time came, the donation of Gnosis during the revel, the expenditure was done over the course of the sweat and body heat of exertion, the depth of feeling in the sense of community, the closeness reinforced to their purpose, exuded in their very breath. I often imagined it like a mist or fog - not physically, but metaphorically - all of that vital life from the Sept affirming its bonds to each other, to the land, to Gaia, creating this spiritual miasma where nothing was left untouched, all breath was shared, and in that way, so too was that energy exchanged.

              And then, of course, there was often plenty of blood-letting. Blood was usually the ol' stand by. In arid regions especially, blood and saliva often made up the token gesture of the donations, returning some moisture to the land, if they had nothing else appropriate to offer as a physical, tangible thing.

                                      It always gets more complicated than I intended.

                                      Catfish - Caern of Harmony (Wisdom); takes place in a city with lots of rivers/lakes that bring unity to its various parts
                                      Fog - Caern of Mystery (Wisdom); takes place on a mountaintop known for spooky legends
                                      Woodpecker - Caern of Strength (Glory); representing the persistence of the woodpecker making holes in the tree all day; in one of the great primeval forests
                                      Boar - Caern of Courage (Glory); happens at an old battlefield reknown for its slaughter
                                      Swan - Caern of Dignity (Honor); representing dignity of patrician families in a city; a Glass Walker urban sept
                                      Goose - Caern of Streetwise (Wisdom); representing street smarts on the docks of a port city; a Bonegnawer urban sept
                                      Honeybee - Caern of Kingship (Honor); very ancient (pre-medieval) familial seat of former royal family, now nobles of the land
                                      Mountain - Caern of Healing (Wisdom); represents the mountain spirit Rubezahl in the Krkonose mountains which is the source of the River Elbe. Rubezahl is the lord of the mountain and master of storms and rains that fall there
                                      Lion - Caern of Ancestors (wisdom); caern is ancestral home of ancient Norman nobles who were in service to William the Conqueror

                                      The Totem aren't always "natural" for their type, but made sense. For example, Lion is in the Normandy caern because lion is on the court of arms of Normandy and natural as the home for someone who would found a royal dynasty, but the caern itself represents heritage and therefore ancestors.

                                                  Boar - Caern of Courage (Glory);

                                                  Break out the war trophies. Challenges of endurance, exhilaration, and balls-to-the-wall feats of daring.                                                       I think Yakecen is conflating Caern Totems with Caern Types. The two are not the same and I'm not sure they're related in any way, at least rules-wise, although some totems are thematically more appropriate for particular caern types (I doubt you'd see Boar as the caern totem for a caern of Calm, for example). It looks to me like Yakecen is inventing new totems to go with his new caern types, which I heartily approve of.

                                                      As for the Catfish Totem, Yakecen, it's an unofficial/unpublished totem so if you're "unfamiliar" with its traits and bans, it's because they don't exist yet.                                                           I find the list of Caern types in the core books and the few supplements I have limiting, so I very often make up my own.

                                                          I come up with a theme for the caern, and then decide on the kind of spirit that fits two things - 1 what kind of spirit fits the caern type and 2) what kind of spirit makes sense for the region. My preference is for the spirit to actually reflect the area - what kind of wildlife does it have, what kind of geography, what is the area well known for. Sometimes I go for the thematic more than actual (there are no lions where the Lion totem is, but Lions are part of the Coat of Arms of the local area and families in it).

                                                          It's entirely possible for the same spirit to be a Totem of very different caern types provided the spirit still seems appropriate for it.
