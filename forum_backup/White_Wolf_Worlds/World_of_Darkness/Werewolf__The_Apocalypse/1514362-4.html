    Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
    I have not followed the developments, can someone explain the following to me:
    I see a couple of RC values and the form changes mention a Rage Check, I assume it is the same. How does a Rage Check cost work now?
    First off, the funny thing about that is that "Checks" as a mechanic have come up precisely zero times in H5, so it's kinda weird to see Rage-Checks being plastered all over that char-sheet.

    But as for what Checks are, we go back to V5:
    Checks: Unlike rolls, checks normally use a single die. The player makes a check by rolling one die, attempting to achieve a target number of 6 or higher.
    Vampire primarily uses checks to determine Hunger gain (see Hunger, feeding, and Rouse Checks, p. 211).
    Characters may not use Willpower to re-roll checks. Automatic wins and taking half never apply to checks.​
    So any time you see a Rage-Check mentioned, it means that you roll a die and there's a fifty/fifty chance Rage gets an extra dot... or loses one maybe... It's complicated...

    This is the tricky part: In Vampire, the equation's quite easy - Hunger 5 bad, Hunger 0 good (which meant that the fifty/fifty-outcomes were either Hunger rises by 1 or nothing happens). With Rage, you have Rage 0=bad (because then you're "losing the wolf") and Rage 5=bad (because you're too angry). When the book comes out, we'll see how they handle that.

    Also, in V5 higher Blood Potency influenced Checks by letting you roll multiple dice and pick the highest for Checks. But what would be the equivalent for that in W5...?

    Once the book comes out, we can judge how well this idea of Rage as Hunger works.

    Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
    Is Rage at a maximum of 5 now?
    Yes.

    Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
    What does the Harano track measure? What does the Hanglosk track measure?
    Harano is extreme despair and Hauglosk is extreme urgency. But we don't know yet how exactly they interact with Rage or the game in general.

                                Additionally, due to the rushed development, W5 might be dealing with similar issues like H5. With H5 you had moments of oversights in rules-questions like when it proposed that you should use Desperation-Dice to bolster your Edge-rolls but never addressed how that interacted with the Creed-Fields. Or the entirety of the Danger-Track which is essentially a blank slate as a mechanic with the book just giving you examples of how an ST might use it. So one of the first things an ST for an H5 game has to do is decide how the Danger-Track actually works. And even with that, the book offers three different examples of how to apply the Danger-Track-rating to calculate the Difficulty of a roll. Meanwhile, the section in the rules on Difficulty never mentioned the Danger-Track at all.

                                So, it's entirely possible that once we get the full book, the systems in W5 could feel just as half-baked as the ones in H5.

                                And a case in point for that: The Harano- and Hauglosk-Tracks. That's why even with the Touchstones-preview I wasn't sure it would be two tracks. Because even if we don't know how exactly it works, having two separate tracks probably means that mechanically you could get into a situation where a particular Garou is equally close to be suffering from Harano or Hauglosk at the same time. So, you have a Garou with four dots in both tracks who is one dice-roll away from either suffering from extreme despair or extreme urgency. Talk about a roleplaying challenge!

                                Of course, this could be solved by connecting the tracks and say "Whenever you raise one, automatically lower the other." but then this would logically lead to yet another problem: That of telling the player that they should do something Harano-inducing to lower the Hauglosk-track or do something Hauglosk-inducing to lower the Harano-track.

                                                Originally posted by Psicose View Post

                                                I don't know, WOD games are supposed to be about personal horror, so coming with angst would be a plus I guess. By the way, that was a joke.
                                                No, Vampire is supposed to be about personal horror. The other games had different taglines following "A Storytelling Game of...".

                                                Werewolf: Savage Horror
                                                Mage: Reality On The Brink
                                                Wraith: Passion and Horror
                                                Changeling: Modern Fantasy

                                                There is, in fact, an essay about this in the Revised Storyteller's Handbook. The cliff's notes of this is that, while you can play around with those other themes, W:tA is fundamentally built around how anyone can die horribly at any time. The essay even explicitly notes that crossover characters in W:tA are basically fucking around until they find out. Typically, by being turned into an unrecognizable corpse. The horror of Werewolf is that there's a closed casket funeral waiting for you at the end of your run, and you have to fight like hell to keep that from happening sooner rather than later.

                                                (The combat in the what-of-Undertale-was-Berserk dark adventure PC game Fear and Hunger comes to mind, where you're perennially aiming to cut off the swordarm or what have you of dangerous enemies before they cut off your limbs... and the end goal for both sides is to unbalance and behead your foe.)

                                                A W5 that actually realized that wouldn't be trying to strangle the game by being a street-level Vampire knockoff with palette-swapped protagonists, it would be trying to tackle Violence, front and center. A WoD5 that actually realized the multiple themes across the games, too, wouldn't be trying to flatten everything, but maybe provide a take on the different themes relative to the game itself, so that we can stop having quite the same record-scratching disconnect between, say, Mage and everything else with the former starts talking about, to give just one out-there example, orbital prisons holding the cast of Little Witch Academia captive by immortal alchemists who are still in charge of determining the trajectory of modern scientific advancement from their otherworldly redoubts.
