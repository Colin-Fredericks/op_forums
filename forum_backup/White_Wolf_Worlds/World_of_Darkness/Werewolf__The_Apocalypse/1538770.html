    Failures of the fera

    07-17-2024, 02:02 PM
    It's a common trope in the game that garou fucked it up really hard with the fera during the war of rage. The world would have been different if that didn't happen, garou must made amends and so on and so on.

    Howether after thinking about it and reading the breed books. Ir has come to my attention that the other fera are pretty much, in their awfullness, massively responsible in the whole mess. Not in the war of rage per se (except the nagah and ananasi) but in the long aftermath.

    So bear with me as I go to the unspoken wall of shape for the other shapeshifter.

    By that I mean specifically their specific failure to accomplish the task given to them, either by flaws or misplaced ideology
    ​​​​​​
    For a start, just a common failure of everyone
    ​​​​​​The war of rage happened during human prehistory. When exactly is unknown, bit at least 10000 years in the past.

    That's a loooooong time to have lost. And yet the fera still act as if they just lost in term of numbers and uneffectiveness.

    Now in non specific order

    Bastet:
    Supposed to seek secrets (at least that's what they think, it's said they have not been given a direct task, an ironic gaillure for secret seekers to not have found their own purpose).
    Hyper individualistic and yet divided by a tribalism that makes garou inter tribal issues looks like neighbor's disputes (not helped by the fact their tribal lines are inborn rather than chose) and makes any chance of cohesive action impossible.
    Seekers of secrets for aeons yet does not put them to use to help Gaia in general, everyone seeks secrets and it seems to be it.

    Corax:
    The least awful at their job with the ananasi. And yet for messengers, one wonder what they've been doing for all these millenias. Garou and fera aren't anymore connected even by messages after all these times. Same for feras in general or garou with garou. Seriously, what were they doing?

    Ratkin:
    8 billions humans. Do I need to develop? Dudes, your sole goal in life is to maintain populations, especially the human population, at a certain limit. Their history is one of systematic failure. Even their prized plagues are happening a few times a millenia and with a generally weak effect.
    Also they're batshit inside and it's massively their own fault.

    Ananasi:
    Typically are doing their job. Their main weakness is not truly a fault of their own, but their utter dépendance to their creator.

    Nagah:
    Their obsession with the secrecy of their role is damning them at every step. Plus they are awful at their job, seeing the amount of shapeshifter that betrayed Gaia.
    Their methods of judgment is also biased, they basically apply everyone's rules to the letter, so the litany for the garou, the law of ananas a for the ananasi, etc, which means they specifically base themselves of laws that even the shifters concerned doubt and interpret. The garou have two auspices who argue about the litany every morning, and many consider their laws to be out of touch. By using them as guideline, the nagah are trapping the others in patterns that might stop them for actually achieve their purpose

    Mokole:
    I can understand that it's hard to be trusting when you gain the collective memory of past betrayal, but the point of the mongole is to remember so that others might not repeat the failures of the past. Yet by deliberately isolating themselves, they garantee that dire outcome. Furthermore, their longing for a new time of wondermaking is basically désertion of their role.

    Gurahl:
    Same as mokole, while their own grudge is more understandable as with their hibernation they are closer to the massacres of the war of age, they have basically abandoned the world to be without it's needed healers.

    ​​​​​
    ​​​​​Kitsune:
    Hard to argue since their purpose is deliberately not yet revealed by luna (Bastets? Get to work for once!), their main flaw is their extreme focalisation on asia, weakening their potential usefulness once their purpose is revealed.

    Nuwisha:
    Nearly the worse offenders. Their obsession with practical pranks makes them blind to the suffering caused if their target "don't get it".
    So yes clever boys, you cursed yourself to be limited at 100 members on earth at time I'm until the garou learn to call you with humility.
    But what are you gonna do if they don't?

    Rokea:
    The cream of the cream of pure stupidity. They have the most simple job of the world:survive. And every tool at their disposal for that. Yet it seems they do everything on lazy mode. They're hostile to other fera although they can help with rokea's survival by eliminating treats. And worst of all, their dogmatic obsession with eliminating the inlanders garantees that they have no knowledge of what transpires out of the sea, which led to the carnage with a specific atomic testing.


    And I originally didn't wanted to talk about the lost breed, as they're not really awful, the grondr and the apis were just in the way, but the camazotz existed until a few centuries ago.

    So Camazotz:
    Failed their job even harder than the Corax, plus the extermination is partly on their own hands/wings. For aeon they were trapped on the cycle of Bat going from gaian half to wyrm half, allowing their fallen brethren to take prominence during the darker period of the cycle.
    And more than 10 000 years should have been enough to deal with it. For ears if Gaia, they forgot to even try to talk to others about the issue. Hell, according to apocalyptic records, the garou succeeded in healing bat. So the camazotz have no excuses.

      I think your comments speak to a truth of the setting. This is how I handle it in my chronicles.

      1) There was no such thing as a "War of Rage". Like the Hundred Years War, it is a concept that historians created to better explain the event or era in retrospect. At the time those events occurred, nobody thought of them as a single struggle. That's why the tribes and Fera all have different ideas about who started it and why. They are actually talking about different events centuries and thousands of miles apart which were later combined into one story. Indeed, I don't think Stone Age peoples could even conceive of "war" as a distinct concept. Fighting and killing one another over territory and resources is simply what people did back then, the Garou and Fera were not exceptions.

      2) While the Garou clearly kicked a lot of ass in their own territory (the natural range of the wolf), the idea that they somehow invaded areas where wolves did not exist to kill other Changing Breeds there does not make much sense to me. Killing a bunch of Gurahl in Europe and northern Asia I can buy. But killing the Ratkin in southeast Asia? No, but I could see the local Bastet doing that.

      3) The concept of the War of Rage was probably invented by Garou Galliards centuries after its end in order to glorify the accomplishments of the Garou, but once invented the concept spread elsewhere. The Garou and the Changing Breeds as a whole retain a lot of archaic cultural features, and traditionalists still come from a Shame culture. Guilt cultures develop a lot longer on. It was the Children of Gaia who probably first took the concept of the War of Rage and turned it into something the Garou shouldn't have done. Then as the cultural collapse of the Changing Breeds accelerated in the twentieth century and modern notions of guilt culture spread, it's turned into this albatross that hangs around the Garou and an excuse for the Changing Breeds for their own failures. People like to blame other people.

      4) None of the Changing Breeds exist for a specific reason like "fighting", "spying", "sending messages", and so on. Those are just myths the Changing Breeds like to talk about themselves in order to provide meaning and purpose to their own people. Those myths express just enough truth about their kind that it makes sense on a superficial level, but they fall apart once you think about it.

      That is what I determined actually happened in my chronicles' history as an ST. That doesn't mean I present it to the players as the truth. NPCs have their own opinions, but mostly repeat the standard lore. But some dissent, and some even get close to the truth. PCs can believe whatever they want.

      I sometimes try to sneak in arguments as a PC in certain games along these lines, but not too much because some players and STs get really confused, angered, or defensive if other PCs depart too much from the books.

          I strongly agree with all of this! I don't really see the War of Rage as a singular event but rather a series of skirmishes and massacres spanning over a long period of time, with no clear beginning or end. In WtE, we don't list a second War of Rage, either.


          Age of Tragedy: The War of Rage
          Many conflicts and disputes plagued the history of the Gaians from the very beginning, and some have been dubbed as part of the greater war. The fact remains that it was a long-brewing problem that only kept escalating. Galliards ken the earliest skirmishes began as the first human civilizations rose to power. A time when killi ruled and guided mankind openly, some were seen as gods by their kin. And with those cultures and their territories, new tensions arose. The war is better understood as a collection of smaller disputes that escalated to much larger bloodshed. The Garou felt betrayed and disrespected by other shapeshifters, accusing them of failing their duties to Gaia or outright corruption.

          In the end, through failed diplomacy and hubris, the Garou went on a massacre that lasted generations.          ​ Believing in their own superiority, they began systematically hunting down other breeds. Some killi were driven to extinction by the conflict, lost forever to time and legend. Some still struggled to recover their populations, while a few began hunting each other. Some escaped the conflict by hiding in the darkest corners of the Earth, on faraway lands, or even in the Umbral depths. How it ended depends on which killi you speak to, but the open wounds of this conflict persist to this day.

                                                  Reproduction is a strange thing in WW
                                                  But suprisingly streamlined

                                                  "Genetics isn't an exact science, but the mating of werewolves and Kinfolk produces roughly 90% Kinfolk and 10% Garou offspring. The mating of two Kinfolk changes the numbers to 50% Kinfolk and 1% Garou, with the remainder of children being plain folks. Kin who mate with normal mortals usually bear non-Kin children, though certain factions among the Children of Gaia have a highly secretive rite that can alter this outcome. The Background Pure Breed on the part of either Kinfolk or Garou improves these statistics somewhat." - Kinfolk: Unsung Heroes, pp 13

                                                  "Pure Breed [possessed by kinfolk] also figures quietly into the chances for conceiving a garou child. For every level of this Background you purchase, the chance of producing a full Garou offspring with a werewolf partner increases 5%. (Note: The Garou's Pure Breed, if any, does NOT figure into the odds.)" - Kinfolk: Unsung Heroes, pp 51

                                                  "In cases of Kin/Kin pairings, the chance for werewolf offspring increases by 1% for every dot of Pure Breed." - Kinfolk: Unsung Heroes, pp 51

                                                  "...if two Shapeshifters of Different Breeds conceive a child, the child has a 10% chance to be a shapeshifter of the father's kind, a 10% chance to be a shapeshifter of the mother's kind, and an 80% chance to be a normal kin to either. If the mother is lupus, feline or whatever, however, any children will be normal Kin to her Changing Breed or shapeshifters like herself - a lupus cannot give birth to a Khan or to tiger cubs." - Werewolf Players Guide (2nd Edition), pp 190

                                                  "In no cases can a shapeshifter be of two separate Changing Breeds; a Garou with Bastet blood is simply Kin to the Bastet and nothing more. There isn't room for two shapeshifter souls in one body." - Werewolf Players Guide (2nd Edition), pp 190
