        The thing I've been thinking about with legitimate resistance is and what that looks is the novel Ministry For The Future by Kim Stanley Robinson. It's a very optimistic near-future hard sci-fi take on tackling climate-change. And the one part of that book I was thinking about in particular are the Children of Kali.

        From a blog by Jamie Wheal called "Children of Kali and the Monkey Wrench Gang":

        Both of these books came out within 12 months of each other and were widely regarded as profound commentaries on our current environmental and political crises––while still offering a measured dose of hope for the road ahead.

        But I couldn't help but notice that both of these books from fingers-on-the-pulse thinkers, included eco-sabotage as core plot points.

        [...]

        For Ministry for the Future (the title speaks to an imagined branch of the UN situated in Switzerland, dedicated to averting the climate catastrophe) there's all sorts of policy talk and nerdy solutions like a blockchain carbon coin that eventually supplants fiat currencies and helps us abandon petro-economics.

        But...the real needle mover, the intervention that actually changes behavior in this story is a ruthlessly effective eco-terrorist group dubbed the Children of Kali.

        Radicalized by a mass casualty heat event in India (eerily like the one they're having this year), the Children of Kali knock so many Lear jets out of the sky that the Davos crowd grows too scared to use them anymore, and they sink so many container ships that companies switch to giant sailing barges.

        The plot twist by the end is that the Children of Kali and the Ministry for the Future are actually in cahoots, and that the eco-terrorists function as a sort of black-ops arm of the UN Ministry. They can circumvent politics and diplomacy, can call bullshit on cynical stalling tactics, and make those most responsible for the death of the planet feel their own mortality for a change.​

        Are we past the point of reason?

        Just by themselves or taken together, those two books showcasing violent protest should mark a sea change in sentiment. We should note this moment.

        Because the subtext, the story between the lines, is that we're now firmly past the point where reasonable, progressive, civic interventions are going to be remotely adequate to get us out of the fix we're in. Even for the best and brightest thinkers among us.​
        And I think if you make the setting the Age of Apocalypse, W5 naturally gets to that same line in the sand or question of "Are we past the point of reason?". This is why Hauglosk and the Cult of Fenris are so ill-suited for this setup. Desperate times call for desperate measures and if you're ramping up the despair with something like an Age of Apocalypse, the list of justifiable acts of resistance becomes equally more radical.

        Also, Ministry for the Future makes clear that violence alone isn't good enough but here's the thing: In WtA, you won't be playing the guys who will solve things on that front. You're playing the scary, violent ecoterrorists who fight against the ones who actively stand in the way of those kinds of solutions.

                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                                    I mean, the book tackles it head on and has a very reasonable position.

                                                    "The Garou have resorted to radical ruthless measure for thousands of years and they have doomed the world. The only solution is to kill all the radicals because even if their heart is in the right place, they are IDIOTS."

                                                    Which is a nice twist on the usual, "Desperate times call for desperate measures."

                                                    It says the ruthless aren't just wrong, they're dumb because their radical ideas won't work.
                                                    So first off, note that the Garou have spent the last several thousand years infiltrating human society, maintaining the veil and generally not engaging in wild, reckless, radical actions. In older editions there were various camps which argued that the fact that werewolves chose to stop engaging in those sorts of actions (like ending the Impergium) is what has allowed the world to progress to its current point where Gaia is now dying.

                                                    Then, going back to the main focus of the thread, the problem that W5 presents the setting as in the middle of the Apocalypse. Gaia is in her death throes (if not possibly already dead), and the gameline presents it as an immediate save Gaia now-or-never crisis. And if the werewolves can't save Gaia right now, then that's it, the world dies and so does everyone and everything they care about. The primary issue is the sense of desperation and immediacy - in otherwords, with the way the Apocalypse is presented.

                                                    In prior editions, where the Apocalypse was on the horizon but not an immediate problem, there wasn't really an issue with characters pursuing longer-term strategies, like Monkeywrenching, to hit companies in their pocketbooks and slowly bleed them dry. But that doesn't feel like it's a reasonable course of action in W5 anymore. Werewolves don't have a timeframe of decades or possibly even centuries to try and save Gaia like they did before. The Apocalypse is happening right this moment, Gaia is going to die in the very, very near future, if again, she isn't already dead and everything is already for naught.

                                                    As such, it seems like the only reasonable course of action is one that's focused on being very direct, and very violent, and long-term, less forceful strategies feel like they'll be a waste of time (by the time those strategies start to pay off Gaia will already be long dead). Remember that with the entire world at immediate risk of dying, werewolves aren't just trying to change the course of one company, or one city, or one country, but need to change the course for every country, and they only have a couple of decades, at best, to do it. It seems like the game presents a setting in which radical and immediate action is the only possible solution.
