    Confusion on Gifts

    04-08-2022, 06:00 PM
    What does it actually take to learn Gifts? As I understand the process, the Fera can learn Gifts up to level 3 from each other. So why hasn't every other shifter race learned how to use that one Apis Gift that dramatically increases the odds of breeding true? Even if the spirits involved refused to teach the Garou, why aren't they throwing themselves at the other shifters like Gurhal to teach them their secrets? Also, is learning a Gift as easy as walking up to a spirit and asking it to teach you how to something that falls under its purview, or is their more required? If you heard from the Bone Gnawer Metis down the street that you just need to ask any old Elemental and they can teach you the Create Element Gift, do you now have everything you need to learn the Gift?

    Also, how limited are the Garou to the Gifts they know about? If you have the XP and you summon the Umbral Wind to a powerful enough Caern, could you pitch it the idea of teaching you some Gift that involves reducing the Gauntlet level even if know one has ever heard of that Gift?

      1) Learning Gifts from other shifters is a much more involved and arduous process than learning from spirits. It's not asking for a few hours of their time, it's asking for a few weeks or months of their time, and depending on the Gift can be dangerous to even attempt (not necessarily a lot of rules to this, but the books tend to use Silver Claws as an example of how the process can be dangerous and backfire as the student can harm themselves easily until they fully master the Gift). And of course, very few of the shifters are interested in sharing their specialty Gifts; esp. to the notoriously irresponsible Garou. The Apis had no reason to teach the Garou their Gifts freely before the Wars of Rage, and their ancestor spirits aren't about to now. You can't just force another shifter to teach you if they don't want to.

      2) You have to get the spirit to say yes too. The bar for that though is generally lower. Spirits that either reside or are summoned to more powerful Caerns are better treated, and thus more amenable to teaching; thus the rule that Garou default to needing to be at a Caern of equal or higher level thank the Rank of the Gift to learn it. Under those conditions getting the spirit to say yes is a formality more than a significant hurdle. If you want to learn a Gift without a proper Caern, you need to work harder to earn the spirit's approval for trying a non-traditional method. Sometimes this is inherent: lots of Gifts only taught by extremely powerful spirits are done as quest rewards in the first place. Other times it's a matter of negotiating favors and chiminage until the spirit is satisfied. Again, not a lot of mechanics to this, but it's the stated narrative hurdles.

      3) The books don't do a great job of detailing how well known Gifts are or aren't outside of specific circumstances. It's not really 100% said one way or the other if you can ask for a general idea of a Gift, or if you have to request a specific Gift that you're characters knows about already. That said, it's safe to assume that the core book Gifts are known at least to the members of a given splat and Rank. Outside of that they probably get more rare and hard to know about. Consider though, that if you need to ask a certain type of spirit for a specific Gift, you need to know who to ask. Summoning is arduous enough that spamming different spirits until you find one that can teach an idea of a Gift is impractical.

                      In addition to Heavy Arms analysis I would like to point out, that Axis Mundi's passage on Gifts and Gift "teaching" makes an effort to clarify, that Gifts are gifts and spirits more often bestow the power to use a Gift to a Garou (or shifter in general, I guess) then actually teach them in a way that we understand as teaching in a mentor/student-context.

                      That said, the spirits require something in return, which might be physical things, deeds performed or symbolic endeavours - as well as possibly a specific mindset. An example in the book goes, as far as I recall it, like this:
                      Garou approaches spirit. Spirit asks for a specific flower from a mountain top as payment for Gift. Garou climbs steep mountain and sits down exhausted next to flower - admiring the sunset. Spirit appears and tells Garou that he needed to be remembered of Gaias beauty and what to fight for, the flower is irrelevant. Spirit disappears and Garou has power to use Gift. The End.

                      The book also offers some alternative approaches, if STs or players don't like to make side stories about gaining Gifts - since they might take momentum from the main story.

                      As a general statement, things only have value if they aren't given freely. If any spirit of type X would freely give away Gift XYZ, wouldn't that mean that Garou wouldn't be as inclined to treat specific spirit Y of type X respectfully? They could always seek out another spirit of the same type to get a Gift for free. But with each spirit asking for something in return for a Gift to be bestowed, the Gift has a virtual (or literal) cost for Garou and therefore more value. It isn't something that was handed to him but something he earned, in so way. And there is a different level or respect towards the Gift and the giver of said Gift.

                      Also, in another topic we recently discussed that NPCs have relatively few Gifts. 'Kinfolk: Unsung Heros' made a point that Kinfolk at the most learn one Gift when even high ranking Garou have accumulated only 10 Gifts in their lifetime. We also discussed, that W20 publications have more Gifts on average when NPC stats are concerned.
                      Generally Gifts have the best XP-to-game-impact-ratio. So, why wouldn't Garou invest a lot of effort into Gifts?

                      NPCs often have Gifts evenly divided on Breed/Auspice/Tribe, but that is only a rule at character creation and wouldn't be needed, strictly speaking, on higher ranks - although focusing on one's Auspice/Breed/Tribe in favour over the other two might be grounds for Renown loss, iirc.

                      And while the character, as opposed to the players, don't have any idea what experience points are and that Gifts need those to be learned, it is unclear how within the game universe it is rationalised that Garou don't make more effort to acquire Gifts. One possible rationale could be, that they use their XP on stuff they pick up in their daily lives often, and therefore don't have that many XP to learn Gifts. I mean, if you are stalking the woods all day, you should have Stealth increased, and since it is capped at Survival in the wilderness, that too. And gone are XP you would otherwise use for Gifts.

                      ----

                      In our LARP we only have PCs and obviously they also realise that Powers gained through GIfts are a great trade-off for XP compared to raising abilities and attributes. Therefore we limit the number of Gifts a character can learn (I think MET did that too, but I'm not sure). Since we use a web tool for character management this approach is more feasible then on paper:
                      1. We count the total XP a character has (worth of all the stats and un-spend points)
                      2. Then we calculate a benchmark value for all the Gifts a character has. It is 10 + rank of the Gift * 5. So 15 for Cliath rank Gifts, 20 for Fostern Rank Gifts and so on. It is thrice the amount for Gifts not learned from one's own Auspice/Tribe/Breed.
                      3. The Benchmark value is compared to the total XP a character has. If it is greater than the the total amount, spirits aren't likely to bestow further Gifts on the Garou but argue, that he should work with what he already has for a while before asking for more. Greed is not a good thing after all. Is the benchmark lower or about the same value than the total XP, then the spirits are willing to teach Gifts.
                      So, we allow about one Cliath Gift per 15XP.
                      Also, we allow only two Gifts per game and following Downtime to be learned. For, we don't want a player to learn a large number of Gifts with unspend XP right after gaining a new Rank.
                      By this, we maintain a level of continuity and not have significant jumps in the power of a given character...

                                  At the end of the day, the answer is that while rules on learning Gifts may be broken in every conceivable way (you can learn from something that isn't a spirit, you can learn without access to a Caern, you can learn from lists you don't have access to, you can learn entirely new ones and you can even learn above your Rank), each and every of those ramps up the difficulty of the process enough to be a plot point instead of a reliable mechanic.

                                  Even learning a Gift that you completely qualifies for from another Garou of your own Tribe and Auspice takes a lot longer and far more effort, without even being guaranteed to succeed. Spirits impart gifts outside the rules when they feel like it. One way or the other, while the ST can use the possibility to give away unusual gifts to characters, the process is sufficiently unreliable as to justify you not seeing it around.
