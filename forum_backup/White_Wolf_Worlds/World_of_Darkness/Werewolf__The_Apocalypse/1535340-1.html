    A few points I would like to make in regards to this:
    Clay doesn't mock the Litany when eating the Fomori at the start of the game, you do. Clay gets poisoned because he is strictly adhering to the Litany without thought. Cuz y'know, Elder.
    When did you play the game? Because I noticed that as well on my second playthrough. That has changed. Because the first time I played it there were options and I picked the one where I reprimanded Clay for disobeying the Litany during that opening. And that moment wasn't there during the second playthrough. That opening-bit was much longer the first time I played it.

    W5 does mention patron spirits of Caerns on page 194. It doesn't specifically mention they need to Tribal-related, because they don't, but there's no reason you couldn't have them be an aspect of a larger Incarnae. Likewise Pack Patrons can be done through the Rite of Patronage just 10 pages before. Considering the Patronage bonuses are all 1-die and a Ban, they are perhaps the easiest thing in the world to Homebrew if you'd want to.
    Well, it's way more complicated than that. One, Favors and Bans are inconsistent. There are three different types of Favors in the W5 corebook (version one is just a list of Skills, version 2 is skills under specific circumstances and version 3 is just a specific circumstance) and the Bans have some wild swings in their "If you do this..."-scenarios with some Tribe-Bans being way more punishing than others.

    Additionally, the referenced Rite only refers to Tribe-Patron-Spirits.

    And so overall, it is a bit of a challenge to houserule this if you want to stay consistent with the W5 corebook. Then again, the actual issue is that the W5 corebook itself isn't consistent. In the past, I mentioned in another thread that for the sake of homebrewing it would be sensible to give up on the inconsistent semantics-game from the W5 corebook and instead just opt to replace Tribe-Favors with a suitable free Skill-Specialization. You still get the bonus-die but without the hang-ups of the inconsistencies you find in the W5 corebook.

    Most of these are just Chronicle Tenants and a lot of them are just example ones from W5, or related to it's stated themes. These are definitely covered and supported by the book.
    That's a very generous reading in my opinion. For those who don't have the W5 corebook, here are the proposed Chronicle Tenets in the W5 corebook (pg 200):

    Environmentalist
    • Never cause harm to Gaia
    • Do not hoard resources
    • Never put short-term gains ahead of long-term goals
    Apocalypse Radicalism
    • Do not cling to the old ways
    • Never kill an outsider
    • Vengeance must be served​
    Traditionalist
    • Uphold the Litany
    • Heed your elders
    • Only ritual challenge merits killing of other Garou​

    And of course that has to be seen in the context of a book that has lines like "For all of the self-glorifying tales that old Garou tell around the fires, younger werewolves note that the Garou Nation didn’t collapse because the Litany was so goddamn perfect." (pg 47) or "The Garou are built to act, but they’re probably too late. Worse, the resources they have aren’t what they need.​" (pg 202). The latter quote is made worse by W5 still being a game about Garou doing Garou-stuff. The "Garou have to reinvent/reform themselves"-part isn't supported one bit but instead it's just called out as a thing that has to happen. Instead, the W5 corebook assumes W5 players will end up managing a caern at some point (pg 191). And that the game really is all about "small victories". That's what you get from the W5 corebook.

    Also, please don't be too hard on corporations... That's another piece of advice you get from the W5 corebook (pg 26).

    Your starting pack being assholes Is The Point yeah. They're old bastards lamenting that they lost and all their old glories are gone to the past and taking it out on you by proxy. There's a reason your new pack reacts to them by going "hey fuck this guy". Though I had a pretty interesting mentor/student relationship with Black Tarn as a Theurge.
    Yeah, I get that that's the point but I don't like it. Seems a bit counter-intuitive to me to start introductions by telling players/readers that Garou can be real assholes sometimes. I played an Ahroun for the first playthrough so I didn't try to rescue Black Tarn.

    I personally can't agree on the new pack being unlikable. Elton, Podge, Melodie, and Nim were all incredibly charming to me. Like yeah they argue and get heated about it which has been a core Garou thing since I believe the inception of Garou as a Thing. But it's not like there's debilitating infighting, just ideological disagreements. Everyone still works together and obviously likes each other.
    Well, that's personal taste, I guess... I didn't like any of them. Usually with these sorts of setups where you have multiple companions and part of the story-structure is to pick them up, make them part of your "squad" and do "Loyalty-missions", characters emerge that I find more likable than others but that weren't the situation here. I didn't like any of them. None of them seemed interesting.
