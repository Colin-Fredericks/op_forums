                I think the game would be better with a clear vision of what makes something a Tribe vs. similar concepts such as Camps, Houses, and Lodges. This isn't a matter of setting building or anything, but a matter of game design. If you don't have a good idea of what X is, how do you make a good X? The Tribes started as rather shallow stereotypical archetypes, that were fleshed out in various directions to give them more depth. However those attempts lacked any guidance based on what makes something a Tribe, so they're all over the place in application.

                I'm also really not sure what you mean with the countries example. All countries are based on ideas and culture. An American can move to Germany and become a Germany citizen, without stopping to consider themselves American culturally. Citizenship and cultural identity are different things that may or may not overlap within a given individual's beliefs.

                Not every American or German holds the belief that every citizen of their country is a "real" member of that country just because they have legal status.

                                No tribe is actually "needed". It all depends on what you think each tribe contributes to the game you run. That is based on both personal preference and how that tribe is depicted in your individual chronicles. I've seen tribes like the Bone Gnawers in certain chronicles completely suck and be uninteresting, and others by different STs where they are awesome and contribute a lot to the game.

                                I do think there are some problems with the Stargazers, but that has little to do with how the tribe was originally presented in the corebook versus how they will developed in the subsequent tribebooks.

                                Originally as written, the Stargazers were not written as exclusively Asian. The description was:
                                1) A minority of Garou opposed to the Impergium (pre-Concord) choose to withdraw into seclusion
                                2) They sought a life of contemplation and enlightenment, and there withdrew to remote locations (1st edition also mentioned "or journeying to the Far East" which is the only Asian connection)
                                3) They gained their name because they could be seen at night alone staring into the night sky.
                                4) They seek the truth and inner peace.
                                5) They are a tribe of philosophers, mystics, and spiritualists.

                                That's it. Nothing that says they are predominantly in Asia. Or that they know some strange form of kung fu or stick fighting. Instead, they are just monks and ascetics seeking internal wisdom and enlightenment (which is a feature pretty universal in all complex societies).

                                The expectation seemed to be that in any remote location around the world, you might see a Stargazer. In first and early second edition material, you could find a Stargazer in New York City, or in Swiss Alps. They might have some kind of tie to the Far East, or they might not. Certainly the stereotype inspired by some kind of Buddhist monk was an early and popular trope, but it wasn't the only one.

                                It's just that the tribe was developed way too strongly in that way until it got to the point where if you weren't Tibetan, you weren't really a Stargazer but some kind of weirdo. In truth, the Stargazers everywhere should be the weirdos, and the Far East should have plenty of other Garou available to them (Silver Fangs, Shadow Lords, Glass Walkers, Bone Gnawers, Children of Gaia, and even some others).

                                This is something I can easily correct for in my chronicles.

                                To me, a more serious problem is the weird status of the Chimera totem. It is supposed to be a totem of enigmatic mystery, but its connections to dreams (and therefore Fae/Changelings) and the Greek myth of the Chimera monster sit very uneasily with the tribe's purpose. While it is certainly true that the term Chimera is used to describe impossible things, that has no basis in the actual legends of Chimera. Furthermore, despite Bellerophon riding Pegasus to slay Chimera in myth, nothing seems to ever be made of this in discussion between Stargazers and Black Furies. It certainly makes no sense for an "Asian tribe" to have as its totem a creature from Greek mythology!

                                The tribe would be better served in my opinion, if it had a different totem. Perhaps Sphinx (because of its famous riddles), or if you were trying to go with an animal/creature not bound to a specific culture, then perhaps something like Butterfly (because of its changing nature and the mysteries of death and rebirth), Elephant (prodigious in memory and wisdom), or even Crane. Or if you want a more solitary animal, perhaps something like Praying Mantis which lives mostly by itself and has lots of mythological connotations and legends about it.

                                            Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
                                            As for their Totem, I believe Chimera is meant in the sense of "an illusion of fabrication of the mind; especially: an unrealizable dream" (from Merriam-Webster), rather than the Greek mythological beast.
                                            I agree that is what they are going for in terms of what the totem can do and what tribe is about, but of course the only creature they could use for that name is the Greek mythological monster which is an extremely poor fit.

                                            The more I think about it now, Mantis would probably be the most relevant totem.

                                            1) Mantises are solitary creatures which is what Stargazers are.

                                            2) Mantises are known for their excellent camouflage so they can hide from both their prey and predators. Some Mantises appear like sticks; others like flowers; some like ants; and even more simply by crypsis coloration. That fits with the whole illusion theme.

                                            3) Mantises are known for their hunting skills to the extent that have inspired entire martial arts traditions named after them.

                                            4) Mantises have real world folklore that associates them with wisdom - from the fact they look like the pray, serve as a pyschopomp, or have various supernatural powers. They also transform since they molt and "abandon" one form to another as they go through their instars. While not as dramatic as though insects which have a pupa stage, it can still serve as a totem of spiritual change and development.

                                            5) Mantises are fairly ubiquitous covering India, the Middle East, North Africa, and southern Europe.

                                            The one major flaw is that they aren't particularly known for being in remote places (like mountains) and are mainly tropical and sub-tropical, not temperate or cold where wolves generally are. But overall, I think it's a good fit. I may end up keeping Stargazers in terms of descriptions and mechanics, but simply state Mantis is the totem in my game. That may help clear up any confusion that arises because of Chimera (I've never had anyone complain about Chimera as totem, but it's always bothered me).
