    Looking for some chronicle ideas.

    07-02-2026, 05:42 PM
    I recently offered to run a WoD game for my group, expecting they would pick VtM or MtA, but they ended up picking Werewolf. Which is awesome, but unlike some of the other lines, I don't really have any solid ideas for a chronicle prepped. Anyone got any general ideas you feel like tossing out? Pretty much everything, including the location of game, is all up in the air right now.

      A chronicle in the British Islands where the prophecy of the Once And Future King is about to come through, but it's nothing as expected. This isn't just a major point of pride in England, it is the return of an old supernatural entity that will take sides and, if revealed, move the tides of humans in the islands.

      For this game, the real "King Arthur" is a Celt from the 5th century with little actual relation to modern British. Red-haired, mixed old Irish with Celtic Breton, no modern concept of race or nationality. Taking from the Romans, he was taught in his time that Scandinavians were the most brutish barbarians, blonde hair was weird and dangerous, brown Mediterranean and Middle Eastern skin was the sign of the most advanced civilizations, and black people came from fabled Nubia, a mysterious land of nobles that learned from the Egyptians and palaces of gold.

      More importantly, he's supernatural and was openly so. My suggestion would be a Gurahl (they're among the groups that claim Arthur to be among their numbers, and they Hibernate) that slept decades before the extinction of the bear in Britain. He was not regular, as he was openly a king among humans of a small, short-lived but powerful kingdom. Or maybe she was a Queen, who knows.

      The werewolves will struggle among the many parties that want to claim alliance with the recently awaken, powerful leader that still has in their possession Gifts, Fetishes, and possibly other things that could justify their stint at glory having become such a legend. The tribes are in open dispute over them, and the minions of the Wyrm will take the opportunity to corrupt or destroy.

          Good one, monteparnas! I’m going to be try my hand at a set-up that’s a good deal more cliche, but only so that your winning notion looks even more cool for having smoked the opposition.

          …

          Once upon the time there was a cabin in the wood and everybody died.

          Everybody did not stay dead, but this sort of thing is a risk one runs when holidaying in horror movie territory (if any part of the World of Darkness isn’t horror movie territory then the owners are being sensible enough to hide it very, very well): this being the sort of problem Gaia put Garou on her Earth to handle with extreme prejudice, they duly did so, but the problem (and this is a problem Garou have trouble dealing with) is that all this left no real traces in the historical record.

          This became a problem because Silver Lake is so fabulously pretty and healthful that people decided they wanted to send their kids there every summer, especially since there was outstanding reason to avoid doing so because nobody knew of the … ‘dinner party’ … that played out at that long-forgotten cabin and the hairy, howling nemesis that arrived not long after.

          This being a Summer Camp in Horror Movie Territory, the Garou are genre savvy enough to expect a whole franchise worth of problems, but the local Sept are wishy-washy liberal types and work on the assumption that murder will cause more problems than it solves (Especially since Camp Silver Lake seems commendably dedicated to introducing campers to the Wonders of Nature).

          They’re also fairly elderly at this point (having acquired rights to the very minor local caern late in their career as adventurers) and could use reinforcements: enter the PCs, recruited through the web of connections veteran adventures tend to accumulate and introduced to this situation just in time to ready themselves for an oncoming summer with a whole season’s worth of teen drama, teen romance and gruesomely bloody horror.

          If they do well, they’ll make memories that last a lifetime: if they do really, really well those memories will only be a nightmare for the villains.

          Welcome to Camp Silver Lake, where the scenery is to die for!

              Anyway, I’m assuming that there’s at least one occult grimoire lurking around the ruins of the old cabin; that there’s one or more kids about to make First Change in the camp; that there’s at least one Bane buried so deep by now-absent Uktena that the older pack haven’t been able to dig up but that juicy, juicy teen minds can tap into through the adolescent gift for disaster; that Camp Silver Lake will be busy producing at least one Slasher of note unless our Garou heroes are really on top of things; and that at least one other pack has an eye to moving in and making this THEIR territory to boot.

              This is a somewhat sketchy set-up, but I suspect that if you brought together every ‘Summer Camp Mayhem’ scenario with a side of ‘Cabin in the Woods’ horror set-up, you could get a chronicle out of the dark comedy involved when howling, prowling horror movie heroes have to play sheep-dog rather than Big Bad Wolf.

                      Ancient Egypt, Mesopotamia ca. 1600-1500 BCE

                      As part of the Osirian League, the group needs to travel to Assyria and uncover a weakness of Set in the land of his embrace, to aid Shu-Horus in his final confrontation with the Antedeluvian. Caught up in the waves of the second Baali War, they learn of the attack on Knossos where they witness Set casting devastating sorcery against other vampires with the help of an artifact. After trailing Set's retinue with the artifact on their way back to Egypt, they discover its location in Saqqara, steal and bring it to Shu-Horus.

                      Pretty vampire-centric, but I always thought the League would make a nice proto-Ahadi that could be open to a few more tribes and Fera. The main attraction of course would be the journey through history, scenery, and WOD canon events. There is a lot of lore from other splats that can be integrated, so you can expand the plot to whatever resonates with the group. Plenty of opportunities for combat, too, if they should favor that.

                          Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                          I’m going to be try my hand at a set-up that’s a good deal more cliche
                          Clichés are usually better sources for ideas, I'm just too stubborn to use them because of how my Neurodivergence plays out.

                          I overly complicate everything I touch. Still hope those strange plots can help someone. Another coming:

                          The local Caern was wiped out. One day Wyrm servants came and, this being a very small and forgettable place with little contact to the rest of the Nation, the couple Garou defenders died in the scuffle.

                          It didn't fell, though. The local Kinfolk are loyal, and some are supernatural beings of their own, or allied to like-minded ones, forming an effort that, while desperate, was enough against the weakened foes.

                          Our PCs are the very first Garou to step in here in about a decade or two. The Nation has too much problems elsewhere and don't know how bad things are here, as the locals held the line no problem until now. The Nation doesn't know they're not regular Kinfolk, maybe not even that the local Garou were wiped out.

                          Here the PCs are the whole of the sept, and the support infrastructure in the Caern is all done by things the Nation would disapprove strongly or violently. They're resourceful enough to get a lot running, but need actual Werewolves. What is important:

                          1. Currently the Caern survive through an effort by anything but a single Shapechanger. At least one of those can use some spirit connection, a Spirit Mage, a Nunnehi Changeling or just one with knowledge of Paths that go to the Umbra, even a Koldun or some other vampire sorcerer can do the trick.

                          2. Yes, they may be Wyrm-Tainted. Even better if at least one important one is. Or all of them.

                          3. Kinfolk know their place. They may not like to be forbidden from access to the heart of the Caern they saved, but they follow the rules. The option is to trigger something, anything that'll bring the violent attention of the Nation.

                          4. Everything kinda work. They can get spirits or supernatural effects to accomplish the role of Garou functions in the Caern. Spirits that do or recognize formal Rank challenges, means to signal the rites of a Moot. It's not ideal, but do the job until the PCs can step in. It's possible, and my personal choice, that even the PCs are thoroughly initiated and educated by them.

                          5. If the Nation finds out what's going on, there will be conflict. The best a good diplomatic effort can help to accomplish is to avoid actual violence if the PCs cut contact with the Kinfolk or allied supernatural and accept intervention in a form that doesn't understand or care about local circumstances.

                          6. The size and variety of the supernatural lot is up to you. I'd use a single Vampire sorcerer, but anything goes depending on what you want.

                          7. Overall the PCs are free to decide what they want, but choices have heavy consequences. If the Nation step in they get more resources, but strain relationship with local Kinfolk at best. The current scheme isn't ideal, but brings more variety to their arsenal and tighter connections and intel on the area.

                          8. You can play crossover politics or avoid it all. The creatures present are here as Kinfolk first and already took care to cut avenues for their societies to find out and intervene. You can still bring them because things aren't always easy, but the scenario is meant to run pure Werewolf with the critters as standalone set pieces. They may even have cut all ties to their kind.

                          9. The Wyrm in the end is an enemy to them all. Some splats have a hard time recognizing it, no one wants the end of the world, though. Kinfolk are aware of this, and they can end up in any splat naturally.

                          9a. Kinfolk can be Embraced, Awakened or turn into Mummies, and won't forget about Gaia in the process.

                          9b. At least in theory they can stay as Wraiths or be Imbued, and likewise will still know what they learned all their lives.

                          9c. Changelings can't be Kinfolk, but are born among them sometimes, and will receive their education.

                          9d. Demons possessing Kinfolk will receive the same through the host's memories. While there are some who'll just feed the Wyrm for power or laughs, others will fight it with gusto, either for personal interest or sense of duty or love for Creation.

                          10. The Wyrm IS growing in the area, the PCs ARE needed, and no one else will do when the time comes. The locals don't have a pack's power and resources. The Nation won't have all the setup and intel needed in time (as without local support that's a helluva lot of time). If the PCs don't take up the challenge seriously, this Caern WILL fall in the end.

                              AnubisXy If you wanted a slightly more Gonzo chronicle, you could take Werewolf 5th Edition as a Bad Future and then set your players the challenge of ‘Setting right what once went wrong’ (Presumably after representing a Vision of that Future by playing a 5th Edition session).

                              Alternatively, you could do something more fun - perhaps a globe trotting chronicle placing your players in the role of a Silver Pack at some Crisis Point in Garou history and showing how some of the Mightiest Heroes in the Nation came together to make sure said Historic Crisis was more ‘Worlds lived’ than ‘Worlds died’.

                              Bonus points if this Historic Crisis involves averting Y2K (With the heroes racing to prevent the World of Darkness from turning into THE MATRIX).
