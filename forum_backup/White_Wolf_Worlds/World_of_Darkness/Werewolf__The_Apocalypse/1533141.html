    Making player characters out of “villainous” factions

    03-25-2024, 07:44 PM
    So the other day I made my very first World of Darkness character. I was fascinated by the powers of the Tzimisce clan, so I went and made one in V20, not as a depraved flesh-warping artist of torment and oh god why is it still alive, but as a cheerfully unhinged back-alley doctor with a generally friendly disposition even as he digs bullets out of your brain and gives you extra eyes because your forehead looked a little too empty.

    So yeah, I took one of the “designated antagonist” clans and made a character who isn’t just “not immediately repulsive” but actually fun.

    So I was just wondering: how might you guys go about doing that for the Red Talons? Make somebody who actually works as a member of a group that isn’t entirely composed of other Talons, and isn’t a totally insufferable and violent misanthrope.

    The Teller of Tales, and the Weaver of Dreams

          Read the Revised tribebook, it's great.

          Red Talons aren't really that hard to make work in a group, at least outside an urban setting. Just make one who's low rage and high willpower/gnosis and have their pragmatism outweigh their anger. People often play Red Talons as dumb or simple, but that's a mistake. They're clever; they successfully built up a black ops division, the Winter Packs, with only one Garou outside the tribe finding out. During the Middle Ages, their use of the Night-Fear displayed a pretty sophisticated understanding of psychological warfare, even if it didn't end up working out for them. So why would one work with other Garou and not Kill All Humans? Because it benefits the tribe, Griffon's brood, or Gaia to restrain themselves. Or maybe they just know they won't get their way and don't feel like trying to argue about it at every opportunity, even if it rankles.

          As far as making them sympathetic? That's simple. Emphasize that their hatred of humans is due to material concerns, namely that they're being wiped out. There is also psychological trauma; many Red Talons has had experiences at the hands of humans that would inflict PTSD simply because that's common for wolves. Then after their first change they hang around a bunch of other people who suffered the same traumas and the spirits of extinct and endangered animals. And you can sure bet that when they learn how humans wiped out most wolves, there's a little voice in the back of their head saying, "Oh Gaia, we're next." Like Damian May says, humans are using their families as rugs. 30% of the wolf population dies a year from humans and it wasn't too long ago wolves were extirpated in much of Europe and North America. They still are in many places. You know how you felt towards the aliens in Independence Day, how you didn't think twice about killing them off and wanted the bastards dead? That's how the Red Talons feel about humans. They do not care about humanity's internal divisions because when you are on the receiving end of genocidal violence all that looks pretty irrelevant. Even the humans who say they're against killing wolves tend not to do much about it.

          There's room for other character concepts. You can have a Talon meet a shapeshifted mage and come to the conclusion that there are ways to make humans no longer human anymore. You can have one decide that no matter how un-wolf-like it feels, if divisions among the Garou can harm them, turnabout is fair play and they should aggravate divisions among the humans. You can have one decide that the real problem is that any species could do what humans have done--so while they have to utterly break humanity's power, the species itself doesn't have to die. You can have one who thinks extinction before a species' time is inherently abhorrent (a view Griffon shares, by the way) and so hates the thought of entirely wiping out the human species. I've explored those concepts in my own play. But I wouldn't drop the misanthropy. As far as the Talons are concerned, they are at war with humanity. They may have different ideas on how to fight that war or how to win it than the rest of the tribe, but they're still at war.

          The Whelps often get brought up in these conversations, but they've always rubbed me the wrong way--the more in-depth look we get at them in Revised makes them seem naive by placing a large chunk of the responsibility for humanity's actions on an external force (the Wyrm/vampires). That's the sort of thing which will make most humans and homid Garou feel good, but it's wrong. There's no Wyrm or vampires in real life and we did the same shit.

                                  The Red Talons are one of my favorite tribes, and imo one of the most misunderstood and tragic. I feel when others try to cast them as villains, that they merely haven't read much about them as a tribe, much like I feel others see Kalaril (younger brother) as a villainous tribe. I firmly believe WtA wouldn't even be WtA without them. Their most powerful gifts are all oriented towards healing Gaia in many ways (and not just feeding the land) and towards reading prophecy. The prophecy of apocalypse originated from this tribe, even. I feel like people's brains shut down when they hear about a tribe that hunts humans, ignoring that there are rites across most of the tribes that touch on this exact theme, from bone gnawer to coggie.

                                  Desperation is the primary motivator of this tribe's actions. Seeing generation after generation of their families wiped out by humans both directly and indirectly, not hatred. They're no more villainous than an injured bear or a cornered mountain lion. They are alien in their thinking and mannerisms to be certain, because they aren't human. That's the start and end of it. Many of my favorite characters I've played are Red Talons.

                                  There are many ways to go about it, but having one driven by prophecy to cooperate would be completely canon. One that learned through war how to collaborate with humans would work too (like the war of Chicago, hunting vampires.) Perhaps one is following the directive of a personal patron to bring a prophecy to others, or perhaps is cooperating with a pack of homids with the specific purpose of teaching them how to be wolves.
