    Mage progeny and effects

    02-20-2024, 05:57 AM
    How would each of the shape changing tribes react to mage progeny? I hope to approach this from a multiple perspective angle, and using the older rules concerning "Kinfolk".

    I mean this question for ALL changers as well; bears and birds included, etc.

    How would tribes or individual members react:
    -a child awaken in the tribe.
    -an adult awakens.
    -a child/adult is revealed to be mage "in the closet".
    -if a kin awakened while abroad.

    how easily could a mage rely on their family? How easily could a group rely on a mage relative?

    What kind of relationships could form from this? Not just the negative or obvious results (i.e. immediate hostility), but the uncomfortable and complicated connections life gives time to time.

    how would werebears reconcile with an Hermetic?
    how would werelions reacts to a lost kin now a Gabrielite?
    what if a werespider discovered their spouse was an Etherite? Or vice-versa?

    Food for thought,
    I only wish to hear your input to this quandary.

    P.S.
    a slight tangent, but how would a mage handle delirium? Just curious and found no mention in books.

                      I'm going to have to answer in installments, because it's a lot and starting here I'm going to leave out the Red Talons, because there are only wolves, there's no way this could happen to them.

                      COG: At first I imagined that they would be the ones who would be most accepting, but when I looked at the tribebook, they seem to be quite fearful of mages as a general rule, I already think that they would not interact with a technological or notoriously violent tradition I'm in doubt about the others homever. Now starting from the pre-assumption that the magician awakens within their tribe, whether in childhood or already grown up, I don't think they would reject him, but they wouldn't accept him either, I think they would try to make him not be a mage, for lack of a better term, which eventually wouldn't work and he would become it anyway. Now assuming it's in a closed group where he would actually be more accepted, I think it would work as long as he has a philosophy similar to theirs, which is quite possible.

                      Shadow Lords: This one is kind of obvious, they would be seen as a resource to be exploited, it doesn't mean they wouldn't care, after all they have their blood, but they would be cautious about passing on information, I mean, I believe that a shadow lord would be able to assume a mage's betrayal.

                      Silent Striders: They are on point every case is different. However I think some of them would enlist the help of a mage in an attempt to study and possibly undo their curse.

                      Silver Fangs: I can't imagine this tribe wanting to mix with mages, I mean, they are quite elitist, but I can imagine one case or another isolated for personal gain or driven by desperation. If it came out of their kinfolk, I think they would isolate him at best, or kill him at worst. If someone had a mage child, I think they would choose to hide him or leave the tribe to protect him.

                      Fianna: They will kill him, if someone cared enough they would try to hide him from the rest of the Fianna, probably away from the tribe's eyes, perhaps the mage himself would make this decision in fear of the reaction/retribution. Depending on Caern, they could even integrate him into the team to help in some way, but still keeping him away from outside members.

                      GoF: Not very different from the Fianna in general, but some Caerns would try to use him as a weapon like Wotan and Mjolnir's thunder and making it clear that he would not be seen as a human being in thoses cases.

                      Stargazers: In that case I think they would see it with curiosity and caution, now if he grew up in the tribe, I see them wanting to guide them along a "good path" I think depending on the approach this mage choses later he would even be seen as a valuable resource to the tribe, perhaps even helping to establish new Caerns or protect those that already exist, of course other options would be for him to decide to leave the tribe believing he has another purpose or that the right thing would be to try not to use magic, except in extreme cases. A friendly reaction, in short.

                              I know that some books, possibly in their in-game perspective, give the mandate to never let a mage near a caern, but why? There is that idea that mages will suck dry caerns, but does that make sense? Nodes have value to mages and caerns could be regarded as nodes of a specific ressonance. Sure, Mage not allied with the garou could try to employ Prime to alter the caern to suit their needs better, which might "suck the caern dry" for garou and spirits of the middle umbra. If mages wanted to get rid of garou in their area, they might come to the conclusion, that the garou will move on, once their caern is destroyed, therefore making it a strategic target. Also, the Mage might with Prime have ways to use the supposedly existing spiritual energies as quintessence, therefore becoming more powerful when in the vicinity of a caern. Which is bad for an enemy mage. But all this wouldn't necessarily hold true for a mage who is also kin and allied with the garou.

                              Garou usually meet for moots in the vicinity of the caern, not at the caern heart but within the bawn. And, going with the PGttG, which is the only non-in-game description of typical kin activities I know, the kin would be present for parts of the moot. Not necessarily for Inner Sky and Breaking the Bone, but for the Stories and Songs and to welcome back the garou after the Revel. This is an important part of knitting the community together and it wouldn't make sense to exclude the mage, especially if one wants/needs to bind him closer to the group (emotionally), as well as keeping an eye on him. Also, if the mage does use this powers to help the sept, why not let him profit from easier access to more powerful spirits and lower gauntlet. Also, garou (and supposedly kin with the merit in question) have an easier time meditating for Gnosis renewal when at a caern, so extracting some of the spiritual energies of a caern is not unheard of for garou, either.

                              On the other hand, what would be gained if the kin had a sanctum somewhere in the protectorate and would try to communicate with the spirits of his ancestors by summoning them to his sanctum, possibly away from the caern?

                              I get that this "don't let them near the caern" mentality was introduced into Werewolf-lore as a general approach to mages. But it isn't explained in-game, iirc, apart from the vague dangers that the mages might harm the caern. So, this practice might be a remanent of time gone by, when mages were the protectors and eventually ruling caste of the human settlements, protecting them during the Impergium and in the ages after that. Does it hold up today? Maybe, maybe not. It comes down to how careful you want to be with the "Don't cause a caern to be violated" tenet of the Litany.

                              But there are different approaches. I vaguely remember a story: The Hengeyokai have a similar rule about dragon nests, still there is a story where they decided to have a meeting with all local powers, including eastern vampires, mages, fae and so on, to debate if outsiders (get of fenris in that case) should be granted access to the dragon nest. So they even decided to invite their enemies to a diplomatic gathering, much to the frustration of the get of Fenris.

                              So, I guess, there is a bit of different approaches on what exactly handle a mage at a caern and in general how fragile or resilient a caern is. Sure, the mage can't be allowed to exploit the caern, but neither can any garou. But to bar the mage kin from the caern doesn't make too much sense, since he already might know where it is (if he grew up in the sept before he awakened), and might have means to access it anyway, using spirit/correspondence, even from afar.

                                      Whenever we go through the Tenets of the Litany and explain them to cubs (which happens often in an ongoing LARP game), there is the question, what is allowed and what not, in regards to the "violating the caern" tenet. The most often asked question is a scenario where either the majority of the sept leave for an attack, leaving the caern potentially vulnerable. The second most often asked scenario is if the sept is attacked, how far can the Warder or Sept Leader let the enemy come in to effectively mount an ambush or get into another tactically better position to start the counterattack. Often, it seems, the questions can't really be answered and also often it seems like 'success makes right'. If the enemy that was allowed in to be dealt with in the best possible way, didn't have a chance to cause harm to the caern, then all is fine - since risking alone doesn't seem to violate the tenet. If these enemies managed to cause harm to the caern, however, than the decision was bad and a violation to the Litany.
                                      But the Litany is a collection of stories from which the tenets are derived, so there might be stories a Galliard might tell you, when garou were punished even for putting the caern in a potential danger. Also, there might be plenty of stories where the garou happily died in an uneven battle, instead of offering the caern as bait, and couldn't sustain the sept and caern any more a decade later and it died anyway.

                                      For a mage, or even any other supernatural kinfolk, the question would be the same and equally hard to answer - I think.

                                      A kinfolk who comes to the sept from the outside, totally awoken, possibly as entourage to a pack that plans to settle in the sept, things are different then with the son of the sept leader, who grew up in the sept. He basically lived in the bawn or "on the caern" his whole life. Being there every moot since the toddler age and often even in between, when the sept leader had business at the caern. Such a person isn't sniffed out by "Scent of the true form" every time and suddenly someone realises 'he smells like the namebreaker I once met' (if anyone in the sept even met one). If such a child awakens, which most often is a process, the sept might not realise right away and might set another set of boundaries in the process.

                                      I mean, sure, the Ritemaster or Warder/Guardian would probably never let even the sept leader's son mage to the heart of the caern, but into the bawn? Seems a bit insincere to prohibit that.
                                      As was pointed out in the 1st answer to the original post, that we could answer the topic only in the most general ways.

                                      Also, sorcerers with mana manipulation should be restricted, if found out what they are and potentially can do.
                                      That said, the Ritemaster also should make sure that nobody else gets access to the caern's heart that shouldn't. Like performing a Rite without proper skill (not having a good dice pool) is something that could harm the caern, and is therefore something that the garou should not allow, as a community. But even accomplished garou can roll a botch at the worst of times....

                                      So, I would think, the best approach to the whole "caern to be violated" tenet is to act reasonable - honour and wisdom should dictate the course of action.
                                      And with that in mind, the garou wouldn't allow a mage to experiment with the caern heart, surely, but might him to visit the moots or the shrines of the fallen heroes, which are his ancestors...

                                      Simply because there is a difference between "That mage guy that came with that new pack" and "The guy I grew up with, who is undergoing some change, much like I did when I had my first change not so long ago."
