        This post is not really about Griffin, but one of the camps of the Red Talons. It incorporates some of the idea previously mentioned to better develop the camp.

        Lodge of the Predator Kings

        As written in the original tribebook, it was the Lodge – not the tribe as a whole – who were dedicated to the return of the Imperigum and the complete eradication of humanity. However, this distinction was lost in the corebooks who described the entire tribe seeking genocide. Future supplements then described an “anti-extermination faction”. The tribebook then broke this down into three camps representing two extremes and a “moderate” faction which hated humanity, but did not push extermination.

        The “extermination” camp does not have much to interest me except a rather peculiar name. What is with the name? The Talons aren't known for being poetical, but rather straight forward. Why are they a “lodge” (a very human concept if you think about it) and who are the “predator kings”? Are the kings the Red Talon members of the lodge, or something else?

        A lodge is merely a small building. A man made structure would hold no appeal to the Red Talons. However, lodge is often the name for a local order of a secret or fraternal society. This must be the reason for the camps' name. So what kind of secret sor fraternal society is the Lodge of the Predator Kings? We know the purpose includes the extermination of mankind. But is there something more?

        The first thing that springs to my mind is that the Lodge sounds very similar to native American societies like the Ghost Dance. The Ghost Dancers were formed when then Indians were obviously losing to the white man, and their way of life and sovereignty were coming to an end. The secret society believed they had a way in which they could reverse their defeats and restore the world as they once knew it. This sounds exactly like the purpose of the Lodge of the Predator Kings. Such societies are not rare – they often occur in cultures under threat. The Righteous and Harmonious Fists, popularly known as the Boxers, in China was similar. The South African Xhosa began killing their cattle, under the influence of a prophetess named Nongqawuse, because they believed it would drive out the British. All believed they could summon s upernatural powers that would eliminate their enemies.

        Such thinking is common among apocalyptic religious sects. An apocalypse is actually a “a vision of heavenly secrets that can make sense of earthly realities." It often involves the end of the world, but at its heart is an explanation of why the faithful are persecuted right now, and promises that the oppressors and other evil doers will be eventually be destroyed. The ties to the general themes of Werewolf and the position of the Red Talons should be obvious.

        The Lodge therefore is an apoclyptic sect. Lodge members believe they have received divine visions related to the apocalypse, and they are acting upon these visions in order to prepare for the Apocalypse. As this camp is exclusive to the Red Talons, I believe these visions must be associated with Griffin.

        These are not Garou with a realistic plan to bring about the end of humanity. They are idealists clutching at straws in order to fight off depair. In many ways, membership could be a way to fight off Harano. The one advantage they have is that in the World of Darkness, supernatural powers are very real so they aren't entirely delusional.

        The Ghost Dancers believed they were reunited with the spirits of the dead, and could bring these spirits of the dead to fight to the living world and fight on their behalf. Therefore, I believe the Red Talons of the Lodge are in commune with the spirits of the lost “predator kings” - the saberetooth tiger, dire wolf, terror birds, cave lion, bear dogs (Amphicyonidae), and other carnivores that became extinct during the Pleistocene or early Holocene (all most likely driven to extinction by mankind, but this is not certain).

        These spirits of lost animals all seem to be part of the brood of Griffin so there is an existing connection between them and the Red Talons. The Lodge is in communion with them, hoping to rally the last ebbing powers of the spirits – and their anger against humanity – to destroy mankind and recreate the world back to what it was before. The best way to reach such spirits is to travel to the Near Realm of Pangaea, and it is likely the Lodge has a “home” there where the leaders of the Lodge go.

        Therefore, the Lodge of the Predator Kings is primarily a religious movement, not a warrior society. Its positions possibly violate several taboos (as did the Ghost Dancers) among the Red Talons which explains why it is a minority movement. Many Red Talons might respect and admire the Lodge and its members for reminding the other Garou they remain proud, but probably think it is just too extreme and not actually helpful.

        The Lodge probably spends most of their time talking to the spirits, training members, and preparing for the great day when the Predator Kings return and lead the Tribe to victory during the Apocalypse. Their “war” is not to be fought now. What direct action they take is probably limited, seem more as a training exercise instead of a battle, and intended more to keep morale up than an operation intended to lead to victory. They will however target specific violators of Gaia for destruction, perhaps as a result of local interaction with humans or as signs given to them by the spirits.

        They are still the most bloodthirsty camp of the Red Talons. Unlike the Warders, they still go out of their way to kill humans. They do believe one day humanity will be extinct, but that will be the result of the apocalypse and the new world they inherit will be cleansed of mankind and the lost predator kings will return. However, the camp now has other aspects which should deepen roleplay and give its members more of a three dimensional feel even if they remain villains in a chronicle.

        I think this makes the camp and tribe much more playable while retaining its main characteristics.

                    Griffin as a Predator and Totem of War

                    This post is not about a new perspective on Griffin based on myth and legend. But examination of why Griffin is a totem of war, and what this tells us. Griffin is a war totem that rejects homids and the ways of mankind. It it s a “war” spirit for lupus. From an animal perspective, “war” would mean hunting. I think we need to keep this in mind when we hear people (whether it be homid Garou or the human writers of the game books) describe the Red Talons as seeking “war” with mankind.

                    Do you mean war as mankind understands the term? Or how an animal would understand it?

                    I think the Red Talons should approach their war with mankind in the same manner that wolves function as predators in a Darwinian sense. The role of predators is to winnow out the weak and sick so that their prey becomes strong.

                    A good example of this is an eskimo folktale contained in the novel Never Cry Wolf. It is a folktale about the caribou and wolf, and it ends as “this is why the caribou and the wolf are one; for the caribou feeds the wolf, but it is the wolf who keeps the caribou strong."

                    This is the sacred relationship between predator and prey that is honored in the minor Rite of Prayer for the Prey. In werewolf, Griffin has the role of the king or leader of the predator spirits. So this should be an important element in his worship and relation to the Garou. It should inform how the Red Talons act.

                    This gives us a different perspective of how the Red Talons might imagine their war with mankind. At least, this is probably how Red Talons viewed it until the past few centuries.

                    Of course the big problem is that man is not supposed to be prey. Nor do I think the Red Talons really reject that. The Litany forbids eating the flesh of man. And it is clear that Man is a very different animal than the everyone else. At the same time, the Red Talons realize man is mortal and is not god. So while man is not prey, it is still through the lens of hunting that the Red Talons conceive of their war.

                    They see that Humanity became corrupt because predators did not play the role with man that they played for all other species. Predation kept sickness, weakness, and other forms of corruption out of the other animals. They didn't do it for man though, and that is why mankind acts how he does. He is filled with the weakness, sickness, corruption, etc. that the Garou should eliminated from him.

                    Now, in reality predators don't just go after the weak and sick. They kill anything they can at the time so they can eat. However, more often than not, what they kill is not the strongest and healthiest, but the least so. Over time, the weak and sick are winnowed and the prey become stronger.

                    While wolves are not aware enough to know this, the Red Talons will be especially – especially if we believe this symbiotic relationship is embodied in the spirit world.

                    Therefore, the important element/strategy in the Red Talons' war with mankind is to perform this predation function. Don't go after the “healthy” humans who display the characteristics that a healthy species shows. Go after the “sick” humans who show sign of corruption. Of course, this “sickness” includes “spiritual sickness”. The evidence for such sickeness would be the humans responsible for creating the pollution they hate, the development of resorts in previous pristine wilderness, and the other kind of plots STs will routinely use for their antagonists.

                    Even if the Red Talons consider all humans to be diseased to some extent, their proper role is to not target the “healthiest” humans, but the most diseased. Of course, the most diseased humans live in the scabs far away from Red Talon protectorates so true opportunities for their hunt is limited. But this attitude will be the framework how they handle any human settlements in or near their protectorates, and may be the basis of some Red Talon diplomacy with septs closer to human civilization.

                    Certain human populations might be totally safe from Red Talon predation this way. In the books, Red Talons seem to get along the Wendigo. Their kinfolk and humans who look like them, or at least in areas part of the Wendigo protectorate, might be excluded from their general war. Perhaps this is an important function of the “Whelps Compromise” - go to urban septs and identify these targets that maybe the Urrah aren't getting rid of. They may leave the rest of humanity alone, but they do bring back some good intel to keep good ties with the rest of the tribe.

                    So while in the books, the Red Talons really don't discriminate who they call among humans, it might be better if there was a method to which they adhered, especially when they are proactive about waging the war instead of reactive to human incursions in their territories. If we introduce the idea that the culling of the herd is not to be indiscriminate, then it shows the Red Talons are thinking and using their brains. It also makes them more like hunters or warriors than psychopaths. It might even be interesting to see the Red Talons decide to NOT kill a human because it isn't the right kind of human to kill, it could even shock the PCs. At least it introduces a means to better include Red Talons into a chronicle.

                    Ultimately, the Red Talons believe that Man will go extinct in the apocalypse so their current policy may not advocate that now (of course, this does not mean there aren't some Red Talons who feel this way, but they still need to figure out a plan on how to do this and convince the other Red Talons they can win. The rest of the tribe may be sympathetic, but still be “realist” and look forward to the day that will come). But that doesn't mean they won't kill humans. They do, and sometimes this will be done proactively. I'm just trying to put it in context. What the Talons mean by “war”, and what the homids THINK the Talons mean may be different things.

                    In any event, that the Red Talons have a “deeper meaning” to their war on humans and that the Impergium had a spiritual significance and important function that “benefited” Man would be something that could provoke debate or at least a good roleplayed scene that PCs might not expect when talking to a Red Talon.

                        Deep History of the Tribe

                        I've reached the point where I really don't have new ideas to add, but I still have things I'd like to explore. One is that for the vast majority of the history of the tribe, humanity isn't actually all that threatening. Even after the Impergium there is a good one to two thousand years before any significant amount of wilderness is lost to cultivation. So we have about 6000-8000 of the tribe where their modern focus has no reason at all. Red Talons may not like humans, but I don't think there'd be anything like their hatred nowadays.

                        So what do people think the Red Talons do in this era? Remember that for most of this time, there is no Gauntlet so the Realm and Penumbra are one. And that physical creatures like unicorns, griffins, and others are actually present.

                        Another question is how do the Red Talons interact with the Fae in this era? Most of this time is before the Sundering. Fae are not animals, but they are definitely not humans. Do they get along well with them and other chimerical creatures? Might they more frequently assume homid form in order to interact with them?

                        I'm interested in this as I see the Red Talons being one of the tribes with very strong ties with their Ancestors. And for most of their Ancestors, this is the time they lived in - a time when humans were not a threat.

                                While there is some debate about precisely when dogs were domesticated, the time I use is around 15,000 years ago. I think all the data best supports that. I also use the date of 12,000 years ago (the last Glacial Maximum when the Pleistocene became the Holocene) as the time when the Garou and other Changing Breeds happened. That's never been established as canon and know others prefer a different date, but it makes sense to me. Based on DNA analysis, the domestication of dogs appear to happen only once. So there weren't multiple domestication of wolves into dogs. Even the dogs of the American Indians came from the original dogs of Eurasia. Of course, in the World of Darkness, maybe that's because the Red Talons prevented any more from occurring.

                                The timeline of when domestication occurred is the following.

                                Around 12,000 BP (Before Present) - goats, sheep, pigs

                                10,000 BP - cattle, cats

                                8,000 BP - chicken

                                7,000 BP - donkey

                                6,000 BP - honeybee

                                5,000 BP - horse

                                During this time period, the human population went from around 1 million worldwide to 5 million. Very small numbers. I hold the Impergium lasting from around 8000-5000 BP. After 5000 BP, the human population growth begins to accelerate. Some people may want to push the Impergium back a bit. Most domestication occurred before agriculture took hold. Herding was established long before agriculture. Certainly the most important food animals were before the Impergium.

                                I don't think at this time period the Red Talons were much focused on humanity. They were probably the most negative Garou tribe towards Man, but there is not much Glory, Honor, or Wisdom savaging more or less helpless humans. The Garou of this period have to be fighting the elder evils of this time period - Wyrm-corrupted forces of the Wyld like giants and dragons or more alien horrors. The Garou also made up a much larger percentage of humanity than they are today, and there probably was no Veil. So a much higher percentage of humans "belonged" to one of the existing tribes, and they wouldn't like it if the Red Talons preyed on them actively.

                                So while the Red Talons may be hostile towards Man, I don't think it should be their defining trait in this time period. Man would be an irritant, not a threat. I'm looking for an alternate "hook" for the tribe during this time period. There's not much to do on the animal side of things, which is why I'm bringing up things like the Fae or other mythic beings the Red Talons would interact with or focus on as antagonists.

                                                    The most definite answer I can find in the books I have access to is that the Concord happened 9000 years ago which means the Impergium happened 12,000-9,000 BP. I've explained why I don't use that time period (if the Impergium ended in 7000 BC, then it ended before agriculture was introduced in Europe and China), but certainly it's supported by canon (given how nebulous when every other event happens, I think the 7000 BC figure is not firm, but just a guideline to show this happened in the early Neolithic).
                                                    First edition's Mnesis breakdown actually gave 12 kya as the time of the Impergium. The Revised Player's Guide to Garou mentioned that it lasted for more than three millennia, and the Revised ST Handbook mentions that it officially ended as an official kind of practice before humans began building cities (presumably ones that were on record and lasted for some time, since that section starts with talk of ancient cities growing out of river valleys worldwide and Garou wiping nascent cultures from history). So, that's where I got the years from. Also notable, some mini-Impergiums might have been practiced later on, much as with flare-ups of conflicts between Garou and Fera.

                                                    But the War of Rage can't be happening then unless you hold it happened while the Impergium is going on. Usually, the history we're given says it happened after the Impergium, but this is contradicted in other places when people says that it might have happened before and during the Impergium. Things are very, very loose here.

                                                    So while you're free to use that timeline in your own games and be happy that it's not necessarily contradicted by canon, others can hold it at a different time and also be happy that they can find things in canon to support them. I don't think either of us are wrong or right - the books can be used to support either of our timelines. It's just personal preference.
