    I liked the Bastet for one big reason - there are many people who do not live in lands that have wolves. Their shapechanging legends include other predators, and most of them tend to be the big cats.

    So if someone really wanted to play this game of savage horror, and if they really wanted to play a character who was African, Indian, or born south of the United States, the thing the makes the best sense is for them to play a wereleopard, weretiger, or werejaguar. Not to somehow figure out how they got this wolfish blood in them and then make them Uktena. It made the game more inclusive. They had that Uktena multiracial option if they did want to play a werewolf. But I can imagine a lot of would be players wanting something more appropriate for that heritage.

    Many of the other breeds seem more fantasy inspired than taken from real folklore, so they didn't have any of that benefit. Often, the backstory worked against how they were actually developed. The Gurahl were supposed to be almost dead except for a few ultra-rare lineages mainly among the animal breed. The Tribebook though mainly showed them from a homid dominated perspective, even the supposed ursine character examples in the back seemed to be homid! The attempt to make them more "playable" subverted how they actually fit into the setting.

    This is coming mostly from an ST perspective, as for the most part I've never been interested in playing any of them as a PC.

            You know, most RPG markets were pretty much covered by this. South America and Australia even has the benefit of importing all european cultural tropes and mythology. North Africa even had ver y similar myths with Jackals who ahppen to be cousins to wolves. And while your concept is rather inclusive and modern, it does pay to remember that when the WoD was designed the concepts of inclucsivity and multiculturality were in their earliest days. Saying that WW's research was bad and American-centric in the early days is an understatement.

            Sadly, this disqualifies the Bastet book, even if the cat shifters are a deeply intriguing concept. They just gave them too little space and never properly invested into making them different. The authors never got beyond the fact they really thought (household) cats were cool, and making all the cats in the world cool, sneaky, sexy loners. The Bastet as a concept deserve a whole hardcover of their own, and with time they have gained depth (especially the Khan, Bagheera, Simba and Swara), but they really could use facelift with better cultural research and tighter conceptual tasking for them as a Breed.

                    Unfortunately my faves don't lend themselves to mixed games: Nuwisha and Nagah.

                    Nuwisha: I love the concept of the Umbra and the trickster ethos. A Nuwisha game, which would necessarily be a solo game, or maybe a game involving a Corax, mage, or changeling pooka (who could conceivably"hang" with a Nuwisha's umbral-ness) just seems like it would make for a solid, deep treatment of umbral adventuring and daring-do.

                    Nagah: I love spy fiction and spy drama and spy everything. A Nagah game is basically what happens when you take that genre and mix in a changing breed. You could even modulate from a more action-oriented setting like La Femme Nikita (the original film, or even 90's USA series) to a more paced, paranoia-laden setting reminiscent of Tinker, Tailor, Solider, Spy.
