    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

    Last Howl is notably so far the only proof that Hauglosk can be reduced in-universe but, apparently, can be at the ST's discretion.
    There's a talisman in the book as well that does that (of course with the caveat that said talisman is super-rare; Gaia's Tear pg 188).

    But Harano/Hauglosk is poorly handled in W5. Mechanically, as far as PCs are concerned it's pretty much a doom-spiral. The trackers for Harano/Hauglosk are only increasing and if you dig into the corebook you can occasionally find edge-cases in the rules that allow a PC to reduce their Harano/Hauglosk. But narratively, it's weirdly very forgiving. Just take the descriptions from pg 51 to 53. Those sections suggest PCs can snap Garou-Elders out of Harano or Hauglosk. And yet, mechanically it's just this death-march to hitting a game-over screen with Garou-PCs. The descriptions from 51 to 53 suggest a dynamism that just isn't there in the mechanics of the game.

    There would be value in not making Harano 5 and Hauglosk 5 automatic fail-states for PCs because not only would you get gameplay out of PCs dealing with another PC hitting Harano or Hauglosk 5 but this would also introduce mechanics for the very thing that gets mentioned on pg 51 to 53. Of course, we instead get into contradictory claims from the W5 corebook here. Those pages suggest gameplay around snapping Garou out of Harano and Hauglosk but mechanically, any PC who hits Harano 5 or Hauglosk is doomed forever according to the mechanics.

    I believe Harano/Hauglosk needs to be reworked both as a mechanic and conceptually. I did it once during the one time I ran W5 for the sake of trying out what's in the book but if it were up to me I would never run W5 with Harano/Hauglosk in the way they are presented in the W5 corebook, both conceptually and in terms of mechanics.

                        The sheer number of them that would be equally at home with what H5's Driven get up to is also an issue. Most of them aren't even explicitly supernatural, and thanks to others changes to the setting, the few that focus on the supernatural side of the WoD are left to the same outsider and low-information position It only further hammers home the implication that the Garou are all better losing the wolf and then using their limited knowledge of the supernatural world to help human activists be more successful by knowing what they're missing when things just don't seem to go right despite their best plans and efforts. The Black Furies overlap a ton with the Martial Creed, the Bone Gnawers with the Underground Creed, and the Children of Gaia with the mystic side of the Inquisitive Creed.

                        Something H5 is actually better at, is how having a mixed group is beneficial. H5 puts more focus on the "what do they do?" when talking about Cells (aka the group the PCs all belong to), while the combination of Creed and Drive informs what role and benefit a hunter brings to the equation.

                        By putting such concepts in the Tribe write ups, they become divorced from the base group unit of the pack. Instead of talking more about how to establish what individual packs do, to then see how different Tribe and Auspice combinations can combine towards a unified goal or ideal, the entries focused on the Tribes makes things sound disconnected where members of the same pack will go off and do their own things rather than work together even if they're seeking the same end result.

                                                    Conceptually we know what Auspice and Tribe are supposed to do/represent in the game. Well, partly, I guess... It's a bit confusing actually. In the previews, it said "Tribe is what you do and Auspice is how you do it." whereas in the released book on pg 59 it's "Auspice is what you do and Tribe is why you do it.". In both versions using only verbs to answer those categories was a bit weird. Never understood that obsession with only using verbs...

                                                    Anyway, the Auspice-part can be written off because in the end Auspice has been rendered largely irrelevant by the game, both in terms of mechanics and roleplaying. Since much of the context and depth of Garou-society has been stripped away, there isn't a lot of substance to roleplaying your Auspice and mechanically Auspice only matters when it comes to picking Gifts.

                                                    With the Tribes (whether in answer to "What you do?" or "Why you do it?"), I think the biggest issue is that too little reimagining happened here. First, there's the issue that even with two NPC-Tribes, that still leaves 11 Tribes to pick from. In a broad categorization like "What you do?" that's way too much and so the result is that there's quite a bit of overlap between the Tribes. Second, aside from the Black Furies and the Native American Tribes the Tribes haven't seen any kind of re-imagining. Instead, the only move made here was to strip context and lore/worldbuilding away from the Tribes. Sometimes it's to erase ties to specific human ethnicities or regions and other times it's just bits of lore/worldbuilding that would make the Tribes as concepts more specific. Not only was the process not very thorough but the work wasn't put in to actually reimagine the Tribes under the goal of "What you do?"/"Why you do it?". Kinfolk and Pure Breed have been removed and the idea of becoming a Garou due to blood-relations has been de-emphasized and yet the W5 writers left the Silver Fangs as a Tribe of "inherited aristocracy" instead of reimagining what it means to be the leadership-Tribe in the new vision of W5. Silent Striders with the completely ambiguous to the point of irrelevancy curse have a similar problem. Since the SIlent Strider's ties to the Setites and Egypt has been removed, why not completely reimagine the Tribe as one of wandering, scouting and exploring...? The fact that despite erasing lore/worldbuilding the concepts of the Tribes are still somewhat beholden to their previous versions puts needless limits on how to make the Tribes answer the question of "What you do?"/"Why you do it?". Naturally, this isn't how the Tribes were conceptualized in previous editions, so it will create problems when you try to take these concepts and flatten them in an effort to fit the new conception of the Tribes. And so you get Tribal concepts instead that feel stripped down and bland instead of providing inspiration to players for what they want to play. Additionally, they may have strived to remove lore/worldbuilding but people know from V5 and the Clans there that there should be a bit of lore/worldbuilding attached to those Tribes. And at that point, you just then get people who immediately will go back to the previous editions and when a Tribe like Bone Gnawers makes someone curious, they're not working with any new conception that W5 was going for but will instead go back to the Bone Gnawers lore of previous editions as the worldbuilding of the W5 version isn't strong enough to stand on its own.
