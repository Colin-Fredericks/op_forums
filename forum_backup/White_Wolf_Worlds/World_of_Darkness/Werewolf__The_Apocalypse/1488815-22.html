            Originally posted by Manfr View Post
            Lots of answers! I will try to reply everyone tomorrow on the train before going into office, but I think I've managed to get the right numbers to answer Asmodai!


            So, I checked my library: I own, as far as I know, all tue published material for V20 and V5.

            V20 has published 1 Corebook (528 pages), 12 normal supplements (for a total of 1671 pages) and the mammoth BJD (559 pages).

            V5 has published in print (there are currently 3 short PDF-only supplements I have not counted in the page total) 1 corebook (428 pages) and 13 supplements (for a total of 2496 pages).

            Average size of a V20 supplement, excluding BJD in the count, is 139 pages, 172 if we sum also BJD.
            Average size of a V5 supplement is 192 pages.

            So, so far, V5 has published in 4 years more or less the same number of V20 books, which are generally a little longer.

            What is very different, and may cause the feel of "lighter supplements", is the kind of books that are out: most of the V20 releases are " Books with rules " , whereas most V5 releases are adventures and city /plot books . The rules-tickest book in the line is probably "Second Inquisition".

            I do find this publishing model coherent with the idea one edition is geared towards legacy /expert players, and another one to entry-level players.
            The key difference is something else.

            Cults of the Blood Gods (278) + Forbidden Religions (94)
            Chicago by Night (357) + Folios (177)
            Trails of Ash and Bone (196)
            --> 5 books, 1102 Pages or 44% of the total edition

            All of these books were pretty well accepted because they were in depth looks into the new setting allowing a lot of material to breathe and even selling some people on the setting changes. Note that none of these are really rulebooks - they are a city book with extras, a lorebook with some powers and a faction and a set of adventures. Also none of these books deal with the crucial staples of the setting as presented before V5

            On the other hand, you have:
            Corebook (431)
            Camarilla (201)
            Anarchs (201)
            Sabbat (134)
            Second Inquisition (140)
            -> 5 books, 926 pages , 37% of the total edition

            All 5 of these books comprise the backbone of V5 - they are the core rules, the core setting, the core factions. They delineate what you can and cannot do. You argue that these are not "Books with Rules", but you're wrong for the simple reason that the only "Book with Rules" is the Corebook. Like the old White Wolf books (core nonwhitstanding), all of these are essentially mostly fluff books with some player facing material. In so far they're not that different from the majority of releases of V20. Sure, they have a couple of adventures/city books, but they are in general a minority of the edition.

            And all of these books have caused problems with the player base. From the strange decisions that are not explained to the tightening of options When old WW books or V20 books addressed the hunters, they provided a full system to play them as playable characters and the tools to use them in a vampire game as antagonists or protagonists. When they did the Sabbat they did the same. Anarachs and Camarilla actually provided some options and lore that made sense, while doing their best to provide little of actual substance and a lot of material people took offense to. After all, it's not often a RPG publisher is called out in a diplomatic row between three countries including the USA, and then proceeds to scrub their books and fire everyone involved and dismantle the company that made them.

            Now they are making small books that are deeply proscriptive in how they can be used, they are not meant to share with the players and are generally not as engaging as the material Onyx Path made. For instance I hated the whole Hecata thing and I'm still not sold on it, but Blood Goods makes sense. I don't see that happening with any of the further Renegade only, Achilli helmes releases. If you add the downright bizzare game that H5 is you're left wondering how much insight they actually have into what they are making and how useful it is to the players they claim to be catering to.
