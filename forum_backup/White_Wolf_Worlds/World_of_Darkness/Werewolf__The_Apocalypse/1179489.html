    How difficult do you make First Teams?

    01-26-2018, 08:24 AM
    So, my thoughts on this matter were, if you were to make somewhat realistic first teams prepared for garou, They'd slaughter mid rank packs more than half the time.

    If you'd take a former military normie, even ignoring that armour is absolute garbage according to the rules, you've got someone who'd realistically beat most garou.
    First, the normie would be hidden, and if the garou fail their perception check, that's an easy aimed shot.
    Second, the normie would very likely be well positioned/fortified as they're usually defending. The Garou would likely waste a turn getting to the individual as they move a distance, get over obstacles, or avoid traps
    Then, we've got between 5-7 dice, before aiming, to shoot a silver bullet from a military weapon that does between 7 and 16 base damage, the individual can split dice 2/3 times or spray.

    Now, this sounds very dangerous to me, multiply the threat with each individual working together tactically. You've also got possession, which could monumentally increase one's ability to fire silver and avoid claws, and you've got a couple of below average, but still trained, fools that probably only have about four dice to shoot because they were sent to die.
    The team has various devices at their desposal, from poison gas, to ultrasonic emiters, to claymores, and likely has silvered bayonets at the end of weapons to account for the "jam weapon" gift


    I see first teams beating fostern packs of equal size 7 times out of 10 (and first teams usually have the numbers). But I know a guy who insists they're mooks and if they use silver it'll be from a sub machinegun at worst, I leave that to regular security. So I was wondering how everyone else does them.

    I personally don't know why anyone'd bother with a balefire thrower as the rules describe it, beyond the incredible cool factor and collateral pentex so enjoys. It's cumbersome,expensive, less effective than silver and the ammunition's a joke.

                      I think First Teams should be dangerous (they know that werewolves exist and therefore know their weaknesses), but how well they'd do against any actual pack depends on the circumstances. So while there should always be a threat that combat could go south and werewolves die, I think in most cases against PCs we'd see the PCs make decisions that will stack the deck towards them and likely victories with acceptable costs. But the threat should always be there.

                      These kinds of combats should almost never be one on one even odds. Both sides should try to get operational advantage and strategic surprise.

                      So First Teams when they are on "routine security" will be at a disadvantage since Garou will decide on when and precise places to attack. The teams will be reacting to events instead of controlling them. This is probably the most typical scenario when dealing with PCs, since it provides them the most agency.

                      At the same time, First Teams when they go on the offensive will get all those advantages. Based on good intel, they can identify and therefore isolate individual Garou or small teams. They can choose when to attack and how. When that happens, they should have a lot of advantages since they control events and are prepared while Garou are reacting. In these cases, First Teams should be able to easily kill one or two Garou. And with enough numbers or hardware and a good plan, even wipe out small septs.

                      I think the first scenario (teams eliminate lone or isolated Garou) to happen on an ongoing basis and is an ongoing threat and whatever frequency works out for the ST. The second scenario where small septs can be wiped out should be reserved for major plot points in a chronicle.

                      I don't think they should be mooks. They should be a clear threat to the Garou and even PCs. But how well they do in any one situation should depend on a variety of factors.

                          Related: specific weapons and tactics mean little, because things like using powerful weapons and/or your brain is something werewolves can do too. You make the assumption that the First Team is apparently smarter and better equipped than their Gaian foes. If you want to do that, fine, but there is no reason the scenario has to be constructed that way. I understand you like guns and thinking strategically, but Garou have their own weapons that hurt just as much if not more, and they have strategies and tactics of their own. Hell, they have an entire dimension open to them that First Teams cannot access. If you downplay the usefulness of the umbra in your own game, as you seem to, that will naturally remove a significant part of the Garou's toolkit. However, as-portrayed in setting, the hybrid nature of the Garou as half-spirit is a *massive* advantage. The Theurge in my players' pack can see both sides of the Gauntlet simultaneously, and attack across the barrier between worlds with impunity; even the lowest ranking Garou can peek to ensure they are within striking distance of a target.

                          If you want to set up situations where very informed, insanely well armed, already-in-perfect-position First Teams blow your PC's heads off, go for it dude. But generally werewolves are portrayed as having the homefield advantage, and their senses are better than any human's. For those reasons, such a perfect ambush heavily favoring a First Team-analogue is unlikely to occur at my table (and that isn't because I run them like brain-dead mooks, I just don't understand why they should have all the odds stacked in their favor to artificially inflate the difficulty of the combat encounter).

                              It should be noted that First Teams are not regular Pentex Subsidiary security. You shouldn't be seeing First Teams in many places except very secure locations or in operations where you're expecting to see professional or terrifying threats (Garou, Dies Ultimae etc). As a result what you get are First Teams as statted in the BotW20, they've got their silver knife and their clip of silver ammo, anti delirium ballistic goggle/glasses or are Fomori they may even have Pus Armour and similar tools They're well kitted, well trained and they know something of their enemy.

                              But... They will not always, or even often, be attacking from Ambush or with complete surprise so Initiative Rolls will still play the order of the day. A lot of the contact between Garou and First Teams will be happening in tight urban conditions, engagement distances of only a few dozen feet or so, thus werewolf movement rates will cut down on the firearm advantage. Anti material Rifles are delightfully scary in theory and utterly shocking in interior urban combat, even ASRs tend to suffer which is why SMGs tend to be Armed Police weapon of choice as they handle better in tight confines.

                              Gifts should not be overlooked (especially Jam Tech and Jam weapons) and the Umbra should really not be overlooked (on either side, where there could be First teams there are Banes). Rage and Stepping Sideways? If a pack mate is Opening/Leading The Way everyone is showing up at the same time and only the person who made the Gnosis roll isn't spending Rage that turn. Delirium is something that just isn't going to effect a First Team.

                              Also the one real problem First Teams will have is a Garou Raging Back. Silver is great and everything but you've got to be alive after the Frenzy that follows the initial "kill". Also First Teams while 'common' compared to Garou Packs are not exactly common in the World and are expensive, including replacing the fomori with limit lifespans thanks to their rather potent power boosts.

                              First Teams are certainly a threat to a Garou Pack of pretty much any rank spread and should be handled as such. But they wont be casually eliminating packs with half a brain either, it'll be a fight.

                                  When it comes to war, a scenario where you can win but they'll take out most of your number is the worst kind of victory. You always want to win with minimal losses. There've been a lot of times in history where one army has really fortified a hill, and expects to defend it, and the other side just waited for the enemy to get off the hill, because the fight'd be an outright loss or a terrible victory. Hell, the battle of britain was largely won because the RAF could pick and choose which engagements to allocate resources too and what to ignore. Pentex might be evil, but they're also a business, and whilst they might vary in price from st to team, losing a first team to a stupid battle they couldn't win is a loss of an investment.

                                  Thus; Bait, Trap Kill from the Employee indoctrination book, is a really good strategy. The garou need to stop some polutant, so you've got land they need to take, and can fully prepare a trap/defences/plan. The garou then need to anticipate this trap/work out how to beat it, decide if the risk is worth it, or die miserably. When the garou are defending, the garou can prepare the terrain if they're even aware of a possible attack, and the FT can work out if they've got a decent attack plan or need to wait it out. Or, either side can see if going for a different target will force their opponent to move locations. Ultimately, you want to catch your opponent unawares, or make them think they'll do better than they will. Silver VS claws makes for a very quick fight, though I suspect should the FT have some kind of heavy unit to engage/distract the garou for more than a turn, they should win. Garou have more mobility, but pentex has such a resource advantage that a first team could pop up behind regular security personel and wipe out garou as they tear into poor chuck.

                                          First Teams (IMHO) cannot get even returns from combat with Garou. Even the heavy combat fomori are not an even match one-for-one. For the humans (or mostly-humans) it's really no contest... But... That's not really the point. Even if they lose a hundred of their people for every Garou they bag, that's a win. The First Teams' single greatest advantage is their ability to win through simple attrition. EVERY Garou that falls is irreplaceable. Every hundred First Team troops that fall have another hundred to replace them. Pentex is simply that vast. They're NOT mooks... They're the consequence of the Garou turning the humans into enemies. For all of their power, the Garou are simply not equipped to deal with the human birth rate, and the best they can hope for from Humanity in the modern age is for them to remain largely ignorant to the Garou's very existence. The First Teams are a dark reminder of the fate that lies in store for the Garou Nation should the Veil ever be irreparably torn: An enemy armed to the teeth with the most advanced technology that humankind can craft, silver, and a human's intellect -- in almost unfathomable numbers.

                                          ​How tough should they be? However tough it takes to make the pack really work for their victory... and if the players get arrogant or too confident of that victory (like thinking of the First Teams as nothing more than "mooks") -- just tougher than that.
