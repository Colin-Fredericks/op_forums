    RANK 2 PHILODOX GIFTS

    Philodox have amazing level 2 Gifts with a strong emphasis on leading the Garou.

    Call to Duty: I'm glad there are non-Theurge spirit controlling Gifts, and this one suits the half moons well. I am not 100% sure whether the spirit arrives immediately ("summoned" in the magical sense) or arrives after travel ("summoned" in the linguistic sense). I'm assuming they arrive immediately.

    If you know the name of a spirit who has healing, tracking or fighting abilities you now sort of have those abilities. It also COSTS NO GNOSIS! This must be an oversight right? This feels like a very magical power, conjuring a spirit that could be a long distance away and forcing it to perform a task for you. There are also no restrictions on what kind of spirit you may summon it seems.

    I worry that this one might be in a similar camp to Jam Weapon - it's so good it could create problems and wind up getting nerfed or banned at a table. What are people's experiences with it? Naturally the Gift has inbuilt defenses against being abused in a proper in-universe sense, but IC abuse and OOC abuse are very different. Summoning a war spirit to help you fight regularly is a kind of "abuse" in the OOC sense. Forcing a spirit which has an important job to come to you and help you despite it saying it needs to guard a Caern etc. is IC abuse. The latter is easier to regulate.

    Overall I'll call this green, even before considering the "SOS" effect which summons all allied spirits in the vicinity. This is a potent effect which could do a lot of good in some dangerous situations. Just remember that flushing every Gaian spirit out of hiding in a dangerous area could spell disaster for the local ecology. 2 Gnosis is pricey but works well enough. And even Homids should have Gnosis 2 by this point. Discuss with your ST how they imagine the gift being used.

    Command the Gathering: The Garou Nation needing this power means that their rating is looking a bit more dubious, but the Gift itself is good.

    A funny use of this Gift is to filibuster; there is no max duration. If you can keep speaking for hours, you can force people to listen and refuse to yield the floor, perhaps while waiting for some crucial evidence to arrive. Stalling is a viable strategy here, even with enemies. Now people can spend 2 WP to interrupt you, or to leave, that is true, but there's also the possibility of you simply reapplying the power.

    RP wise a "it's time for my dramatic speech" power is great, especially for the lawyer/judge/leader Auspice.

    An underrated aspect of this Gift is that in forcing people to fall silent, stop what they're doing and listen you can interrupt Wyrmish rites, Mage rituals, Garou rites and so on... in theory. The ST might argue that people can automatically spend 2 WP to opt out of the silencing effect immediately, but even then a -2 WP debuff to all your foes as you try to interrupt their ritual is pretty good!

    Given that a Philodox will have Persuasion, and thus can convince practically any regular mortal of anything with some effort and a few rolls (in theory, YMMV, social stuff varies massively by table), the ability to shut people up and force them to listen is pretty great. I like this one; you can live without it but it's worth taking.

    King of the Beasts: Permanent control over a single animal could be good; having a powerful predator or megaherbivore (thinking lion, elephant, moose etc.) guarding your wild base is pretty awesome. This is a good power for an NPC, helps explain why there's a hippo who guards the river Caern ultra-religiously without ever bothering any of the Kinfolk or Garou.

    In urban environments you'd want to use this on smarter animals like rats, stray cats and dogs and so on, to fetch, carry, deliver, go on watch and so on. It's neat, and combines well with other animal-based Gifts like Scent of Man and Beast Speech. Note that this Gift lets you command an animal, it doesn't let you understand them!

    Strength of Purpose: Infinite Willpower. End of post.

    I should elaborate. Once per scene you can roll Stamina + Rituals, and every 2 successes grants you a point of WP.

    Keep in mind that Crinos form has more Stamina. You could get quite a lot of WP out of this Gift if you use it at every opportunity... which runs into the spam issue. I would defend using it on car journeys and so on as the Gift simply doing what it does - you are a Garou who thanks to spirit magic can replenish your mental fortitude rapidly! That's great! What feels like abuse is simply use; it's just a very strong Gift that restores a crucial resource.

    If you were gonna nerf this: make it once per session, diff 7 stamina + rituals, and you regain 1 WP per success. Once per scene is the issue.

    ==================================================

    Four great powers, all of which can be used creatively to get memorable outcomes. Strength of Purpose and Call to Duty are both so good that you might see them wind up nerfed, but even with adjustments they should be great.

        RANK 2 GALLIARD GIFTS

        Galliard get 5 level 2 Gifts for some reason! I was expecting 4 in each Auspice, can't lie.

        Call of the Wyrm: A Manipulation + Performance check which forces creatures of the Wyrm to rush your position is a huge gamechanger. Creatures of the Wyrm presumably includes BSDs, corrupted Fera and so on as well as Fomori, Banes, Vampires with Humanity below 7, Malphean Nephandi...

        The given uses are flushing out foes, pulling them into traps and ambushes, but even in a flat battle the ability to force all your enemies to move to a specific location could be lifesaving, as you draw them away from something critical.

        Naturally this Gift has potential for causing huge problems, but even if you can't handle the Wyrm creatures you draw out you can flee; few things are faster than a Lupus Garou sprinting! Some things are, of course, but you have a head start. Remember that just as calling all the local good spirits can leave good things unguarded, calling all the local evil spirits can leave evil things unguarded, and also leave victims of their attacks and attempted corruption free to flee, rally or fight back.

        It feels weird that the free will of intelligent and highly driven Wyrmish creatures is neglected in this power; even with a WP of 10 you can lose the contested roll and just become possessed by a need to rush a position, which is... cool in one way, but could feel silly in another. A meticulous plotter of a Fomori or Mockery Breed who holds great sway in Pentex having no choice but to run out of the lobby and into traffic to be sniped by a Galliard's packmates whose guns are on a nearby rooftop feels... silly? Awesome? Unearned? Brilliant?

        Regardless of some rough edges and risk of a situational nerf (truly powerful and self-possessed wyrm creatures can spend 2 WP to ignore the call etc. but are helpless to stop their minions), this is a solid Green.

        Command the Gathering: See the Philodox entry.

        Distractions: This is a big upgrade of the Taunt combat maneuver, and I love it. It has a 1:1 impact instead of 2:1, and it lasts for 3 turns. Being able to effectively taunt enemies is great, and will help you significantly debuff enemies in many cases. Make sure to check if your Pure Breed applies to using Distractions against other Garou, including BSDs. I wouldn't suggest allowing Persuasion to apply however; this is ultimately a combat check using social abilities, not a social check.

        This Gift is a rare example of a situation where the Manipulation debuffs of the various forms actually matter in fact! Though of course neither Spirits nor Garou are included in that Manipulation debuff.

        It's worth noting that nothing in this Gift requires you to use it in combat. Use it in a race, a challenge, against someone trying to intimidate your packmate, to undermine escape attempts or ritual efforts. Hell, RAW, this Gift absolutely obliterates M20 Mages. It also will interfere exceptionally well with Stepping Sideways, activating dangerous Gifts and so on. Brilliant power to grab.

        The examples of things you can do to distract is a little odd though; barks and yips. What are you supposed to do in Homid form? Bark like a dog? I mean that's a fun limitation of the Gift, don't get me wrong, but it would be nice if it was more clear!

        Dreamspeak: Reaching out to any sleeping PC or NPC you know and altering the course of their dreams is... amazing. It has no cost too!

        You can try to inception a Pentex rep into dissolving the firm he is inheriting. You can send messages to allies. You can play with your sleeping child a continent away. You can give your love a kiss. You can disguise yourself as a portent of some kind. You can visit the dreams of a Garou blessed with visions, and deliberately interfere with those visions. You can threaten or cajole your enemies, turning dreams to nightmares. You can apologize to a Garou who might otherwise kill you on sight. You can communicate with someone in a coma. The uses, both mission-critical and personal, are pretty amazing.

        Make sure you aren't on your last Gnosis point, in case you get kicked out of course.

        There is also great crossover potential with Changelings, especially if you are Fianna and/or fae blooded. In theory you could also help a Mage (say a Kinfolk) with a Seeking, though personally I rule that attempts to help someone undergoing a Seeking always fail.

        Howls in the Night: Situational, but neat. It depends whether you're going to see any returns on stopping your enemies from sleeping. Will missing out on some WP regeneration and getting a bit fatigued effect NPCs? In a huge simulationist approach 100%, it could sway a battle or effect ongoing skirmishes. But will the PCs see returns on it? That depends on the ST.

        I like it enough that it has to be Amber, especially since taking revenge on Vampires by keeping all of the low Humanity ones awake at night is funny.

        ========================================

        The Greens here feel like must-haves to me, but I could picture someone who is less interested in RP not picking up Dreamspeak. Howls and Command are a little more down to your preference.

                RANK 2 AHROUN GIFTS

                Ahroun get 4 level 2 Gifts, they're a mixed bag, but that doesn't matter. The only level 2 Gift that actually matters is here.

                Sense Silver: See the Metis entry. Nice to see the Ahroun get some powers which are combat-adjacent without directly influencing combat; sensing silver bullets, klaives and so on could shift a conflict, or help the Garou arm themselves.

                Shield of Rage: For a cost of 1WP, you can make all spirit enemies have -2 to their damage dice pool against you. Assuming a difficulty of 6, which is pretty universal outside of some weird edge cases (whomst among us hasn't been buffed by Dragon's Ire) this will mean the damage inflicted to you by spirits is reduced by 1.7. It's worth noting that this Gift is very handy against weak spirits; you can wade through an army of Sludge Elementals without any concerns of them actually hurting you; their 3 dice of Agg damage becomes 1 die, which is pitiful against an Ahroun with a Soak of 8 or so.

                Unfortunately the buff is to yourself only, which is a shame. If it applied to the whole pack it would be much more useful. Weirdly it incentivizes spirits to attack everyone but you, which is the last thing you want to do!

                In a typical 'dangerous confrontation', say with a Spirit with a Rage of 10, going from 4.2 Agg per attack to 3.7 Agg per attack is neat enough to get some use out of the Gift.

                I almost wish the Gift added your Rage to soak pools against Spirit attacks now that I think about it... the name would then make more sense.

                A marginal use of this Gift is in challenges; challenging another Garou to withstand a certain number of blows from a powerful spirit. Still, you'd want to find a spirit who is able to inflict bashing or lethal damage, and in WTA they seem to all do agg by default.

                Spirit of the Fray: You probably knew this was coming.

                In a serious confrontation between two dangerous enemies, whoever goes first will often win. Two Ahroun stand there, each planning to knock the other over and then eviscerate them with 3 bonus actions. Who wins? Whoever goes first. Confronting a powerful Mage. Confronting a powerful Fae. Confronting a powerful Spirit. In the WoD combat can be, in some cases, exceptionally fast and deadly. One on one fights with an Ahroun are skewed even more in this direction. You need to kill your foe ASAP because the moment they have any time to do anything they will drop a nuke on you, whether it's an absurdly high damage attack, teleporting you to another country, forcing you into a Frenzy, freezing you in place...

                Turn economy simply means that +10 to Initiative is game-changing. Even with a Dexterity and Wits of 1 (at which point you can't really take bonus actions... why have you done this...) your modifier of 12 will beat most opponents.

                The bonus +10 for spending a point of Gnosis is interesting. If you're rolling Ini once and then keeping that number for the whole fight (some STs do this) then the Gnosis expenditure is pretty great. If you are re-rolling Ini every round, as you should be, then it's less powerful. However if things are at their culmination, and you just need to get in one more attack, push that boulder a little further, complete one last action, and getting there before someone else is utterly critical? Consider spending that point of Gnosis. And of course if you have a way to spend Gnosis and Rage in the same turn (which there is a Gift for down the line) then you can do this more freely.

                True Fear: As an intimidation power, I much prefer this one. The mechanics are fixed enough that you won't see it getting worked around; in my experience if a "you can't do anything" power is too good, it winds up either being very un-fun and annoying the table as a whole (dnd5e is riddled with these) or it winds up being nerfed and worked around and undermined.

                True Fear hits a sweet spot for my taste; you overwhelm a target with terror, they can't attack you, but they defend themselves and otherwise act normally (with immense terror impacting their RP of course). Strength + Intimidation is a great thing to roll, and it makes me laugh that my sweetheart Ahroun has Intimidation 0 and will probably need to learn how to 'act mean' when he gets this Gift. Difficulty being the target's Willpower means that badass NPCs are harder to hit but not impossible, and of course you can use Crinos.

                My only question is whether this power should interact with Fox Frenzy or not. "If you get 5+ successes against a target who is capable of Fox Frenzy or similar, it will trigger" etc. I mean I could easily imagine this Gift putting a Vampire into Rotschrek for instance!

                Strong in all the right ways, a great way to avoid killing someone, complements Delirium and helps you to banish annoying security guards who are "just here to feed their families".

                "Simply looming ominously" is a good thing to note - you can use this in Homid, and with some degree of subtlety, even if the reaction of your foe will be anything but subtle. Having a great intimidation option which doesn't rely on the Manipulation attribute is wonderful for a meathead PC as well.

                ===============================================

                To my taste, having one or two amazing Gifts is better than a variety of above average ones, as it leaves you with more XP to spend elsewhere; Ahroun continue on that trend!

                    RANK 2 BLACK FURY GIFTS

                    Level 2 Black Fury Gifts are a bit odd, but there are some strong ones.

                    Curse of Aeolus: This one is odd. The fog 'obscures vision' but halves Perception pools... so can you see or not? As far as I can tell, RAW, this Gift doesn't blind the target, so they don't suffer the mechanical effects of having been blinded. Some guidance would be great, but we don't even get any hints. Halving a Per+Alertness pool of 10 down to 5 is great and all, but in a fight I don't generally care how well you can perceive me, I care whether you can perceive me.

                    If your ST infers a +1 difficulty, great, but there's nothing like that in the power as far as I can tell.

                    Losing 1 die from Willpower rolls is neat and all... but how often are people rolling Willpower? Even if people are rolling WP to abort to defensive actions, how often is that happening? And how much will 1 die impact it? It doesn't even make it easier to hit your enemies with most powers; most powers use WP as a target difficulty, not as a resisted roll.

                    Ultimately this feels like a far worse version of Shroud, the level 1 Elder Brother Gift. I'm going to make this Red, but if your ST counts enemies inflicted with this power as "partially blinded" with difficulty modifiers applying as a result, then its rating would be higher. The power also gives no indication of how large the fog is or how long it lasts. Basically the power is horribly underwritten.

                    My fix? Just make it Shroud (fixed area, blinds everyone in it but you can see through it, can be defeated by sensory gifts which allow you to see with scent etc.) with a few changes. It uses fog instead of darkness, it's level 2 instead of level 1, the difficulty is based on humidity instead of darkness, lose the pointless WP thing, and so on. Because right now I'm not sure what it does, for how long, or how large the area it effects is. It could cover a whole beach in fog and help you and your party sneak into a base. It could cover a small brawl. I have no clue. Basically this is red out of spite, as much as anything.

                    EDIT: It turns out this covers a city block, as per the final version of the PDF. It's still bad, but not AS bad. With that edit in mind Curse of Aeolus would be amber.

                    Form Mastery: See the Metis entry. Cool to see more Wyld themed 'empowering the natural powers of the Garou' Gifts.

                    Kali's Tongue: I wish they pulled through with the flavour for this power. The legend is almost irrelevant; it gives us the name, and it gives us the idea of stopping a foe from regenerating, but the actual execution of the Gift itself has nothing to do with the legend really. If the user had to bite their foe and touch them with their tongue for the Gift to work that would be cool!

                    Regardless of missing some points for flavour, this Gift is pretty amazing! Many dangerous foes such as certain Fomori, BSDs, Fera, some Spirits and so on will heal and regenerate. A relatively easy check to block healing is great! I'm mindful of the fact that Regen is only 1 point per turn, and it only effects Bashing/Lethal - typically speaking your foes won't be regenerating Aggravated damage. So I'm in two minds of where to place this; against an enemy who can regenerate or heal Aggravated damage, in a prolonged and strenuous fight where attrition is the best way forward, this would be Green. In most situations Amber feels right.

                    A weird and niche application of this power which is bound to get you in trouble would be to use it on someone who is suffering in Erebus; they'll die quite swiftly unless a Crone fishes them out. Say you did it to settle a feud between Septs or families or whatever. The problem is that you'll likely be thrown into the silver lake for your crimes, but still, fun idea. Alternatively you can get into a fist fight with a Metis or Lupus, in homid form, and disable their regeneration to catch them off guard. Also well worth having to use against Vampires, who can heal themselves quite rapidly with blood, and against certain Mage threats who may have regeneration or do some huge heals now and then.

                    Kneel: This one feels weird to me.

                    For starters, the flavour and reasoning behind this makes me cringe a little - I feel like when you have "Amazonian" type characters, whether in a book or a game or any medium, it winds up feeling very obvious and a little embarrassing when part of their thing is forcing men to kneel. It's a bit Girlboss. It's a bit recidivist. It's a bit lame. The takeaway from women being subjugated by men should be "subjugation is wrong" not "subjugation would be very awesome and cool if we were the ones doing it". I don't buy into the hate that the Black Furies get, this idea that they're 'misandrist', it feels like a misreading of the Tribe (a Tribe which does have problems!), but this power really feeds into that bad vibe. Ultimately the only Tribe that makes sense for this in my head is the Silver Fangs. Theirs is the only Tribal culture in which I can imagine someone supposedly wise forcing someone to kneel magically is considered to have okay vibes, given they believe in Divine Right.

                    Anyways, mechanically it's also a bit weird. You roll Manipulation+Intimidation against a difficulty of their Willpower, all good so far, works for one turn per success, and they can spend Gnosis/Quint/Blood/etc. to ignore the power.

                    See the problem here? Either it's amazing, in which case you've neutered a threat, or it doesn't work at all because they spent a bit of a resource. It isn't even a question of the target showing off how willful they are or how much of a badass they are; that would be better represented by spending or rolling Willpower, or taking a point of Bashing damage to ignore the impulse to kneel, or something. The fact that Rank isn't factored into this Gift either is weird. Many such Gifts give protections to Rank, but for some reason this Gift comes off as inherently insubordinate, which might be part of the point; bringing a more entitled man to his knees or whatever. I almost want to give this Gift a Red out of spite, but it's useful enough against mortals / Fomori who lack Gnosis / Homid Garou etc. that it can grab an Amber.

                    My fix? Flip the Gift. Change the name and mechanics to be about resisting such commands. The difficulty of powers which control or command you are increased by 1, and the difficulty is increased to 10 if the command relates to kneeling, bowing, begging and so on. Now I can see brave sisterhood and resistance towards patriarchal bullshit; in fighting back against such commands, not in giving them.

                    Pulse of the Prey: See the Ragabash section.

                    =============================================

                    I'm starting to worry that this deep dive will see more and more disappointing Gifts in the Tribal sections, as these may have seen the least playtesting or been given the least thought, especially at higher levels; we'll have to see!

                                            RANK 2 BONE GNAWER GIFTS​

                                            The Bone Gnawers get some nice utility and a slightly strange combat Gift here.

                                            Between the Cracks: I don't have too much to say here; being able to find abandoned and unused urban spaces is great, and this Gift facilitates Garou/Garou and Garou/Wyrm battles playing out on subway platforms, in abandoned alleyways and so on without Veil risk.

                                            The Willpower cost is entirely needless, and in fact given that the power is a little niche I wonder why a roll is even necessary. A Rank 2 Bone Gnawer is a character I trust to pass a difficulty 5 Wits+Streetwise.

                                            In terms of using this power offensively, you can surprise opponents by going WAY over the top with an ambush. Generally speaking while BSDs can be very mad, they know that Garou need to respect the Veil and will be careful about things like planting dangerous traps, preparing Crinos ambushes and so on, if the location could be stumbled upon by innocent bystanders or potential witnesses. A BSD who was expecting to chase you into some obviously discreet location instead gets caught out in a place which looks unsecured but the Gift has promised will likely remain abandoned.

                                            I don't have much more to say about this Gift really - it does its job and it has its uses, but there isn't that much to overthink. I could question the utility of more successes making the area you find EVEN MORE secluded and hard to locate - that isn't necessarily desirable. Knowing an alleyway in a busy part of the city will happen to stay empty tonight is really handy; finding my way to an abandoned sub-basement which hasn't been touched for decades isn't as helpful, especially since I could have taken one look at that location and gone "yes, this is well hidden". In that case the utility would be in the power drawing you to that location, which does have its uses of course. In all likelihood the ST will let intent drive the power somewhat.

                                            Blissful Ignorance: See the Ragabash entry. I'm glad that Ragabash Gifts pop up in this list, as it serves as a reminder to new STs and players that this Tribe isn't just a bunch of Ragabash.

                                            Cornered Rat's Ferocity: Spend a Rage, roll Rage (difficulty 8) and get +1 die to Brawl pools for the remainder of a fight.

                                            Analyzing this on its own, it's underwhelming. You pay a resource you only have 1-5 points of, most likely. Rolling against a difficulty of 8 means you can expect to botch, fail or get 1-2 successes with regularity. Taking this on its own it would be Amber.

                                            The Gift also, unfortunately, sends you into an automatic Berserk Frenzy, and ending that frenzy ends the Gift's effect. Frenzying has niche benefits, you ignore wounds and you don't need good Wits to do bonus actions, you automatically go Crinos or Hispo, you're harder to control with a bunch of Gifts. You also avoid any risk of entering Thrall, which could be relevant if your Rage is very high. Strangely the Gift also doesn't require you to be "cornered" per se - it can be used in any fight.

                                            Personally I would significantly strengthen the Gift (adding your Rage rating to your Brawl pools etc.) but make it conditional upon a failed escape attempt in which the ST considers you to be 'cornered', literally or figuratively. Frenzying is flavourful and makes sense for the Gift, but the Gift needs to be worth it. Another way of doing it would be making the difficulty of the Rage check simply be... the difficulty Rage checks usually are. That is, base it on the moon, adjust by Auspice if necessary, adjust by Crinos if necessary.

                                            Given the high difficulty and all the weirdness of this Gift I consider it one to avoid. The best possible scenario is rolling 5 successes and getting +5 brawl in a fight (0.243% chance of this happening btw), but the odds are very slim and being out of control isn't worth it. Given how much I love getting bonus dice to combat rolls, it has to be VERY bad for me to not want to take it.

                                            Guise of the Hound: Given that this has no cost, and the roll is pretty achievable (though it wasn't play-tested it seems; Manipulation is heavily debuffed in Lupus, and I don't think the writers kept that in mind at all), you'd be fine just giving this a duration of a day, or even making it a passive effect you can repress.

                                            Making it truly permanent, and something you can't repress, would actually give another reason to look down on the Bone Gnawers - having permanently tainted one of their forms out of fear of humans, at least in the view of more Traditional Tribes.

                                            Anyways, apart from people still feeling your Rage, you can be a big shaggy lovable dog with this power. Combine with Beast Speech to speak to dogs in the park about their owners, combine with King of Beasts to lead a pack of feral dogs, hell you can even attack people in this form (with Agg damage if you aren't Lupus, and most BGs aren't) without risking the Veil, though of course it will still cause a big stir!

                                            If you want to reach even more inaccessible places in this form, wear a yellow vest intended for guide dogs. Now you can't legally be kept out of most locations, Rage and The Curse notwithstanding.

                                            The RP potential and utility of this Gift are excellent, I just wish it were level 1 as it would help to give the Tribe an immediate reinforcement to its identity as the 'dogs' of the nation.

                                            Odious Aroma: Interestingly, if you smell amazing, it's possible that this Gift will still work as intended, you'll simply overwhelm others with the intensity of your scent.

                                            Anyways, this is pretty great; it's a solid short range debuff which requires no roll, and -2 dice to everything is pretty great; running away, using Gifts, rolling Rage to remain active, attacking, dodging... in crossover a Mage would be completely debilitated by this, unless the ST kindly ruled that your Arete is one of those special exceptions; for instance this Gift is unlikely to effect Soak pools.

                                            Regardless of precise interpretation this one is great, and your packmates not being effected is very handy. Solid Green, only to be avoided or used carefully if you are very frail or focus on ranged attacks etc. If you're a high Rage PC keep in mind that you should trigger this before combat if at all possible, since it will stop you from taking bonus actions, though of course using this power could give away your location before a fight starts (unless you have Scent of Running Water!).

                                            =================================

                                            Odious Aroma is worth taking for any melee character, but of course it comes with its own distinct flavour which makes it feel very unsuitable to many Garou melee experts; at the end of the day you'll find a different combat power which is a good use of your Gnosis, ideally in-list.

                                            Guise of the Hound is also amazing on anyone, but you'll run into a similar issue - Glass Walkers and perhaps Children of Gaia might appreciate the ability to look like dogs, as it helps navigate human locations peacefully, and there's always scope for members of other Tribes to want it, but it will come with some insults if you're surrounded by typical Garou.

                                                RANK 2 CHILDREN OF GAIA GIFTS

                                                Children of Gaia continue to have a lot of Gifts themed around peace, as you'd expect, with some shining better than others.

                                                Calm: This one is tricky. A Gnosis cost in a Gift which might be meant to be used in Combat is bad, of course, but often such Gifts have a powerful effect. Here you roll Cha+Emp difficulty of the target's WP, and for each success you remove a point of Rage.

                                                If you have an ally who has been overloaded with Rage by a spiritual location, a Gift, some infuriating situation, a charm or power etc. then quickly removing some Rage is handy. It's very niche, but handy. The thing is that they could easily burn off that Rage themselves by using it for extra actions and shifting; even in the worst case scenario of a maximum Rage score of 1, they could burn it at a rate of 1 point per turn.

                                                In a fight, if you can rob an enemy Garou of all or most of their Rage you will have severely handicapped them. However dangerous enemies will often have decent WP pools, and you can't depend on them all having ratings of 4-6 or so. You also have to weigh it against the utility of simply destroying them with 1-4 claw attacks.

                                                If you are in a life or death fight and you want to end it by showing that you are far more powerful, and they can't possibly win, starting by robbing them of all their Rage is a good way to go down that path.

                                                Finally you can end non-Rage related Frenzies if you get 3+ successes. Which is nice and all, but as a Garou I'm more interested in Rage-based Frenzies; I'm guessing this Gift can't handle those because it would step on the toes of other Gifts, but it's annoying all the same.

                                                My CoG has a Charisma + Empathy of 6, loves ending fights without killing, and even then I'm looking at this Gift and wondering if it's worth it for 6XP. Perhaps eventually, but it's way down the list.

                                                Grandmother's Touch: Mother's Touch, but you can target yourself. Instantly incredible. My boy has a Gnosis of 5, a Rage of 5, and a healing pool of 5 dice; his Intelligence is stuck at 1 because it's key to his concept that he be relatively unintelligent. Even with that limitation the Gift will be the first thing I grab. 5 dice, difficulty 5, healing 1 agg per success, and you can do it up to 5 times? Completely changes things, even if you're in a party with a Theurge like he is. Might be selfish, but I'm actually kinda glad it's limited to the CoG as well.

                                                A teacher isn't specified, but you can infer that the teacher is the same as Mother's Touch.

                                                Luna's Armour: Expect some ranting when we get to Troll Skin and Steel Fur.

                                                Lack of balance aside, this is a great Gift, and makes the Tribes that get it (CoG, Shadow Lords, Silver fangs) all the better.

                                                - only difficulty 6
                                                - Stamina + Survival is a great pool to roll, especially in Crinos
                                                - +1 dice to soak per success
                                                - the bonus dice soak silver
                                                - no reason it can't stack with armour
                                                - cool flavour (though it risks the veil)

                                                The main downside is that spending 1 Gnosis means you can't attack the same turn. At the same time this Gift is a potent boon and you can throw it out the moment there's any kind of pause in a battle, or when the fight is approaching, or before entering the fight's location, or before ambushing (though the light could give you away!), or when you need to take a moment to regenerate, or while kiting... you get the idea. Getting a dice pool of 10 on this Gift, in Crinos, is perfectly achievable and will serve you in good stead in any fight.

                                                Para Bellum: When your enemy shoots first you can spend a point of Rage, and as a result you'll have +1 Str and +1 Dex when you attack that specific enemy, or an enemy who has harmed one of your packmates (presumably not including you). This one has a lot of caveats which makes it a bit niche; if you ambush, if you pre-empt an attack you see coming, if the person who fires first is a minor threat, if you need to dodge or play defensive, this won't do much.

                                                Overall +1 die is +0.7 successes to most combat checks, and that will pay dividends in the long run. It not costing Gnosis is also good. However it costing Rage is actually annoying; is this Gift worth missing out on a whole extra attack, which could finish off a powerful enemy or take out a foe outright?

                                                Personally if I had written this power I'd make it entirely passive with no cost; a boon which kicks in whenever you are in a fight your side didn't start. Still, it's not awful, just not one I'd pick up.

                                                Unicorn's Arsenal: Much like Luna's Armour and Razor Claws, this is a prep power. Find out from your ST whether you can do more than one of these at once, or whether you need a turn dedicated to each of them, ideally before purchasing more than one.

                                                The cost of a Gnosis is a little annoying, but the resulting debuff is decent; any character suffering a wound inflicted by your claws or fangs has -2 dice from all attack rolls. Notably this means Brawl, Firearms, Melee etc. checks and not the Damage roll that follows.

                                                You might struggle to land an attack, you might not deal damage, they might heal or even regenerate it in rare cases, you might just kill them outright... this power will only stick around if the target takes damage from you and then has subsequent turns. Generally speaking your aim should be to focus down a dangerous target ASAP, and the problem here is that the power will only matter if the foe keeps going after you land a strike. Bad luck exists, dangerous foes exist, but it's still a slightly niche situation. At the same time, the best case scenario could save you a lot of pain; -2 dice to 3 claw attacks could save your life.

                                                I'd call this an Amber; how useful you find it will depend on what calls you have on your Gnosis and whether you have other preparation based Gifts or not.

                                                ==============================================

                                                Despite a few average or mixed Gifts, the CoG still stay very strong here IMO; two amazing Gifts for 12XP is better than 5 above average Gifts for 30XP after all. And it's important not to neglect your Attributes + Abilities, as they are what your Gifts often depend upon.

                                                    RANK 2 FIANNA GIFTS​

                                                    Fianna Gifts are a bit of an odd mix at this level, but the flavour remains pretty good. Notably the alphabetical order gets messed up here. I don't know why, and I don't know if it is interesting to anyone other than me, but it's a thing.

                                                    Glib Tongue: Spend a Gnosis, roll a Wits + Expression against a difficulty set by their Wits + Alertness, and they'll agree with any lie no matter how absurd...

                                                    For one turn per success.

                                                    This Gift heavily relies on your ST to have longer turns in social scenarios, and even then, this Gift is quite odd. Often a power will have a chart for durations, especially in C20 and M20; one turn, three turns, a scene, a day etc. instead even if you ace this, it will last as little as 15 seconds with 5 successes, or perhaps as long as 5 minutes.

                                                    Chat with your ST about the flow of time at your table.

                                                    Assuming you're able to get it to last a sensible and useful period of time, convincing someone that you're the ruler of a country, a government official, authorized to be present, that you in fact own the sacred Klaive you just picked up, that you're the one true King of the Garou... it's all very helpful. Until it stops working. This Gift is, unfortunately, not as good as simply being a really good liar. Yes what you can convince people of, even with Persuasion, is limited to the realm of sanity... but lies that are limited to the realm of sanity are better and more useful, and tend to produce more reliable results. They just rely on more thought.

                                                    If your ST allows the following lies, they could be quite effective:

                                                    Stabbing yourself in the abdomen will kill our whole pack
                                                    You need to surrender
                                                    We'll win this fight no matter what
                                                    What I'm holding is an antidote to a deadly poison you drank earlier
                                                    We're your allies, this fight was a misunderstanding
                                                    That bird is our totem, killing it will weaken us

                                                    I can't see any reason they wouldn't, but the ST could argue that how the target chooses to respond to the lie is down to their personality or tactics.

                                                    Based on the assumption that time will be kind to you here, and work out reasonably, this is amber. In all honesty they should have said "one minute per success" or given a table.

                                                    It's also worth noting that the power is poorly written; the target "hears what you want them to" and "agrees wholeheartedly" - what does that mean? It's possible that the intent of this power is that you can say "we should all jump off that cliff" and make your target go "yes, absolutely, great idea" - which is them agreeing with what you said. The text of the power doesn't explicitly guarantee that the target will jump off a cliff, and in all honesty it would be a little silly if it did.

                                                    I almost want to give this one a red out of spite, but we'll go with amber. I feel like there are themes in quality with some of these Tribal gifts, like they were all copy+pasted from the same handful of Revised books or something.

                                                    Flame Dance: This is great; you can attack once per round and save the rest of your Rage for dodging EXACTLY as often as you need to, never wasting a single point of Rage or a single action. The ability to enhance a dodge by +3 dice is neat, and worth using if you ever have a 7 agg plasma bolt flying at your eye or a 10 Rage spirit crashing down upon you with an attack.

                                                    Form Mastery: A Metis Gift? In my perfect Fianna list? See the Metis entry.

                                                    Howl of the Banshee*: I know that the Gift is a terrifying scream, but I just hate the idea of another PC using this, me failing, and being forced to flee in terror. It feels like a very un-fun interaction.

                                                    Anyways, this Gift is another fear effect; this one hits everyone who hears the Banshee's scream. Given that a Garou's howl can reach for miles... this could cause a "mass hallucination" or incite city-wide panic. The cost and roll work fine, having two difficulties for the WP roll to resist is fine, and the Gift is STRONG don't get me wrong, I just don't like the design or setting implications. Green on strength, red on design. And keep in mind that all enemies, even ones you can't see yet, will be hit... yeah.

                                                    My best 'fix' for this would be for the Gift to be bigged up as a very dangerous nuclear option, the Fostern who learns it is taught by the spirit that it is very dangerous for a banshee's yell to be in a wolf's howl, that if they aren't careful they could destroy things they cannot see, inflict death and terror etc. and even have an NPC say that they should think about their volume, who is present, will they tolerate the yell - but honestly your powers shouldn't have their balance and general implications for setting integrity determined by the sanity of everyone who has them!

                                                    *This power's Green rating is dependent on it being kept as is, which I don't think is a particularly sane choice given that a 10 mile radius mass panic is... an interesting level 2 power. The fact that it can ruin the fun of other PCs also means you may see it being discouraged or banned, at which point it's functionally red. I consider this to be a worse version of what Jam Weapon has going on.

                                                    Howl of the Unseen: A really solid utility power which allows communication across the veil, and usage of sound-based powers too. You can haunt someone, spy for your allies on spirits and feed back to them, follow an ally and silently guide them from the umbra, or talk to someone about what the real world looks like while they're on the other side.

                                                    The applications are very broad, and it's a great piece of utility; I just wonder if I'd feel delighted to spend 6xp on it? It feels like a quality of life improvement more than anything.

                                                    I really enjoy that it doesn't have a cost, and that it's a plain old roll, which is handy. This is a solid amber, and definitely a great investment for Theurges. If you're very into spirit stuff or playing around the Gauntlet with a split party frequently comes up, this could be a green.

                                                    ====================================

                                                    If Howl is kept RAW, it's a great take for anyone with a high Charisma + Intimidation and Gnosis to spare. Form Mastery is great as ever, and you might have an easier time ingratiating yourself with the Fianna and their wolf spirits than with those tied to Metis, if you find such a distinction matters to spirits. Flame Dance is an excellent addition to any character with more than 2 or 3 points of Rage to spend, and worth taking on any fighter (or survivor) really.
