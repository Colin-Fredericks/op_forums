    IMO Open Seal would be niche if it opened a niche sort of thing. Say it only opened doors; that would be niche, but even then you can think of so many really great uses.

    Anything that's locked or closed covers a huge number of potential solutions both directly relevant to the plot (breaking into an uncrackable safe, freeing the ancient Strider trapped inside a magical sarcophagus) and indirectly relevant, the solutions you come up with on the fly (breaking into a car when you desperately need one, opening a water lock to flood a drydock and drown a Pentex operation). You don't need access to the lock or opening mechanism after all; you don't need to understand anything at all. Touch a closed segment of a dam, use the gift, open sesame. Touch the stone exterior of a secret passage which can only be opened from the inside? Open sesame! You get the picture.

    If you can spend WP on the Gnosis roll it also helps a lot - I don't know if the books give a clear answer on that, to the best of my knowledge it's just "ask your ST", so YMMV.

        I think you're taking the Gift beyond it's intention. It can open, "any sort of closed or locked physical device." The idea that you can use it to force a water lock in a drydock open is stretching the Gift's wording a lot, as is the idea of causing dams to open their gates. The Gift doesn't say it's capable of a lot of the secondary steps you're skimming over. You're making Open Seal stronger than the Control Machine Gifts.

        Just because you can unlock a door that can normally only be unlocked from the other side doesn't mean you automatically overcome any physicals that would stop you from actually opening the door now that the lock isn't stopping you. If you need Strength 15 to push a door open, it doesn't matter if you can unlock it magically if you can't bring Str 15 to bare.

        Also, look at your examples. "Uncrackable safe," "magical sacrcophaus," "large scale water infrastructure," "secret doors that the power doesn't actually let you detect," and... you're trying to make the case that this isn't a niche Gift? The only non-niche example is breaking into a car, and mundane skills are going to be vastly superior to the Gift unless the car is parked in the middle of the woods.

        Part of the whole problem with the Gift's design is that it isn't complimentary to characters that would want magically lock-picking the most. As I pointed out, concepts that are going to be good at breaking locks get the least out of this. Tossing a tone into Gnosis is useful, sure, but it doesn't really make you a great thief/etc

        I get we seem to have a difference in taste here, since you seem to value big story beat moments where Gifts can generate some big memorable beat that people will talk about for years, where I seem more concerned with the more practical utility of the Gifts so all the times they don't help is a bigger detractor to me. But it really doesn't feel like you're listening to the points I'm trying to make here.

            "Any sort of closed or locked physical device" can be opened with the Gift, just as a Child of Gaia can stop "any Weaver-born weapon" with Jam Weapon. If a storyteller or player balks at Jam Weapon working on a nuke (with good timing and daredevil positioning to get within howling range) or Open Seal allowing you to get a water lock open (potentially with a physical push, depending on interpretation, or indeed whether the water does the pushing for you) then a sense of scale, size and "well SURELY the Gift can't do something THAT big" is being applied to ancient spirit magic. Open Seal does have a Nearly stuck in front of Any, which is a bit illusive. What kind of locked or closed device can't be opened by Open Seal? All we know is that it's nearly any.

            This is an important point because part of what's neat about Gifts is how global, piercing and absolute they can be. Most of them don't care about "this is a level one power, thus it is weak/limited". The absoluteness feels like an intentional aspect of Gifts baked into them; you get a lot of "the tunnel runner ALWAYS knows they're in danger 3 seconds before danger arrives", a lot of immunities, a lot of "can open any", a lot of "can stop any". I think that's good and makes Gifts feel unique and cool.

            The examples I gave are intentionally niche, but varied. When you can do a million and one niche things with a Gift, it stops being niche. For instance Jam Weapon can stop a HIT Mark V's plasma pistol from going off (very niche) and it can also stop a Sidhe Prince's bejeweled blade from cutting you (quite unlikely to come up)... and it can stop a goon's pistol from working, or a mugger's knife from stabbing (the "break into a car" examples). You can illustrate effectiveness with a variety of examples.

            As for needing complementary skills, with any Gift you do need mundane skill to back it up. For instance, take Razor Claws. Excellent gift. It's far less useful if you don't have good physical stats and brawl. Breaking into a car with Open Seal is best if you can then hotwire the car swiftly. Cracking open a safe with Open Seal is best if you can escape without getting caught once you get the hard drive inside.

            When it comes to Control Machine, I think it's worth keeping in mind that all we're doing here is making things unlock and open. With the Control Machine Gifts you could, for instance, close a water lock. Or make a ladder extend up to meet you. Or get a revolving door to freeze behind you so your pursuers pile in and get stuck. Another Gift having a little bit of overlap isn't a problem. It doing something is not a guarantee that no other Gift can do the same thing in a different way.

            To be clear regarding my taste, day to day utility is key and so is the ceiling of potential RP. I personally rate Open Seal as Green both because it comes up frequently enough to be useful, and because if you go out of your way to find a use for it you can do really potent stuff. And to really get deep into the mechanics, keep in mind how swift this Gift is compared to mundanely picking a lock. Picking a lock can be slow. Crouch down, pull out your tools, work your way through a number of tumblers. We're talking a matter of minutes, not seconds. Even if you're a master lockpicker, waving your hand and hoping the door will spring open on a difficulty 7 1-5 dice roll is pretty amazing. That's not considering situations where you can't physically reach the lock itself, or where the lock is protected by some sort of magic or anti-tampering.

            It's a powerful tool both for the amateur and the expert when it comes to thieving. For the amateur it's a cheat to outdo what you could naturally achieve, for the expert it's a means of going faster and circumventing protections which are designed to delay and frustrate expert thieves. Then consider that a person who opens a door immediately is less suspicious than someone who spends a minute crouched in front of it. Open Seal makes it look, at a glance, like you had a keycard or a key, if you time it with a bit of miming or use of a prop. That's before thinking about how often you may find yourself working without your usual tools. You get the idea.

            Finally, if you think I'm not listening to your points I genuinely am; I just feel differently. You'll notice that I agreed with you about rolling Gnosis, difficulty Gauntlet, spending Gnosis, being an unfortunate combo for a typical starting Homid, and also making the Gift harder to use. I also agree that the standout story defining moments are relatively niche, it's just that those exist in addition to the regular utility of going "oh you think this passage/container is sealed? think again", which is bound to see use more than once per mission.

                RANK 1 THEURGE GIFTS

                Theurge time! This should be good.

                Mother's Touch: the main healing Gift of Werewolves is incredible. Right out of the gate a healing PC in WTA, who will be a Theurge or a Child of Gaia, can heal Aggravated damage, Battle Scars (if caught in time) and can heal the same target repeatedly. All for one point of Gnosis per go. Having a pack member with this Gift is a huge lifesaver. On top of the above it also counters certain Gifts and Powers. Systems for disease and poisoning also make use of health levels. It's simply an incredible tool to have in your arsenal both as a fighter and in any other context.

                A memorable use of the Gift for me was restoring the health of a woman with the Permanent Wound flaw in a cross-splat game, to show kindness and mercy to her. Because of the nature of her flaw she regained her exceptionally brittle bones the next day, but for one day she was able to run around with the wind in her hair, do as she pleased, bump into strangers, go where she liked.

                It's also restored more Aggravated damage than I can count!

                A difficulty of 5 as the default is amazing, and very few Garou PCs will have a Rage above 5. This Gift only suffers when you're trying to heal a powerful Elder with Rage 8+. A really clear Green.

                Sense Wyrm: As in the Metis section.

                Spirit Snare: This costs Gnosis, is hard to use, only effects spirits (which is fine), reduces their Willpower (fine) but only for combat actions (getting nicher), doesn't stack (removing a big potential application) and has a low range. The only reason this is Amber rather than Red is that slapping a -2 Willpower, which is equivalent to Dexterity and Stamina, onto a powerful spirit enemy who you are going to be battling for a huge confrontation is pretty neat*. If you're thinking of cool combos, unfortunately it's -2 WP for combat actions. If you have a Gift where the target's WP sets the difficulty it won't apply. I find this one boring and that's a shame, as if it were better it would be a really cool power.

                If I were buffing it I'd make it range of sight, or being able to be heard, reduce the activation difficulty, allow stacking (it costs 1 Gnosis per use after all) OR remove the Gnosis cost, and make it a reduction in Willpower which applies to all uses. RAW a spirit who is trying to run away from you won't be impacted by a snare. A snare. That thing that traps enemies. Unless your ST interprets running away in combat to be a combat action.

                *Reducing their attack and soak pool by 2 means 0.7 fewer successes on average, which means you are dodging their attacks more often and dealing damage more often

                It is a bad Gift, but it's better than Prey Mind and Sense Prey, let's put it like that. If you're going hunting for a dangerous spirit, or a common spirit enemy has a WP of 1-2, this might be worth 3 EXP.

                Spirit Speech: a must-have. Opens so many doors for a party, which we know I love

                Some spirits can understand and speak human languages, but not all, and a universal translator for spirit happenings is incredible. Take this ASAP. If you lack a Theurge/Elder Brother, convince a Theurge your Pack needs to speak to spirits and ask for one of you to be tested by a trustworthy spirit. It's easily worth 5 EXP. I'd pay twice that.

                I could talk about potential applications of this Gift, but it's too obvious and does too many things. I also can't think of any clever tricks you can pull off using this Gift, besides pretending not to understand a spirit when you can, or mistranslating what a spirit says, neither of which directly utilize the Gift.

                Umbral Tether: getting lost in the Umbra is a little bit niche, but I like this one. An invisible tether only the Theurge can sense which guides them back to their point of origin in the Umbra is really great. No matter what illusions or trickery stand in your way, you can glance behind you and see the way back. It's a really good safety harness for Umbral adventures, and if you can acquire Gnosis from spirits it will last as long as you need it to.

                An aggressive use of this a Theurge could try is entering the Umbra while pursued by enemies, intentionally going to the most tricky and mindfucking place they can think of, and then once their enemies have fallen into the trap they can make their way home freely.

                =========================================

                Theurges are in a similar spot to Ragabash with three Green Gifts at level 1, so no complaints here. I wouldn't change anything about Umbral Tether. Spirit Snare needs a lot of adjustments, and with those it would be a solid Amber instead of barely scraping by.

                        RANK 1 PHILODOX GIFTS

                        Philodox Gifts start off pretty spectacularly.

                        Fangs of Judgement: Today I learnt that this Gift applies to your natural weaponry as a whole, not just your fangs. I like the flavour of this one, but I want more from it. Give us flavour that those who take the +2 damage dice can feel Gaia's judgement, or that they have fallen from balance. I also wish that "fallen from their original purpose" was the only restriction, rather than having had to fall into the Wyrm's service from your original purpose. Making it more universal would have some fun crossover applications, but still, that's a minor thing.

                        +2 damage dice to BSDs, Fomori (though presumably not if the Fomor was born into the Wyrm's service?), corrupted spirits, Skin Dancers (unless they were BSD kinfolk?), traitorous Kinfolk, Malphean Nephandi etc. is pretty neat. It's not amazing, but it's neat. In all honesty making this a permanent buff that doesn't have an activation at all would be more cool; the moment you learn this Gift your fangs and claws become instruments of Gaia's judgement, forever. That said a cost of 1 WP and a duration of a day is pretty good. Flavourful and useful, if niche, happy to stick it in the middle pack.

                        Persuasion: As in the Homid section; this makes Philodox a great Auspice for Metis and Lupus who want a more social focus.

                        Resist Pain: A huge buff to a standard mechanic; you can spend 1 WP to ignore wound penalties for a round (or one action, depending on your corebook: at my table we say one round). This Gift lets you spend 1 WP to ignore wound penalties for the whole scene. These penalties include significant discomfort which impacts RP, up to -5 dice, your speed being reduced to half, or to crawling only, and generally make you a liability in combat. Having access to this Gift makes you a much better fighter in many roles. This Gift should never de-prioritize getting yourself healed ASAP. It's a highly effective measure to use until you can access healing.

                        Scent of the True Form: Instantly recognizing Kinfolk and Garou is amazing already, and will serve you well in any game. Knowing that the Pentex rep is in fact Kinfolk or a BSD is helpful. Knowing for certain that the scrappy kid who lashed out at you is in fact Kinfolk / a pre First Change Garou (depending on how your ST runs it) is amazing. Now it's worth noting that it doesn't specify Garou Kinfolk; we can assume this is the case, but it's still interesting. Would the scent of a Fera Kinfolk be mistaken for Garou Kinfolk? Hell, how many non-Garou Kinfolk live their whole life thinking they're Garou Kinfolk? The only way Garou can find Kinfolk is with this Gift, through tracing ancestry, and through noting Delirium immunity. 2/3 of those things could take place without any blood tie to the wolves... I'm sidetracked, but a plot about a respected and beloved Kinfolk being revealed to be related by blood to another Breed would be really interesting. Where do their obligations lie? Are they suddenly less trustworthy to certain people? What does blood matter compared to service and loyalty?

                        Anyways, this Gift is spectacular even before you get to recognizing Fera, Mages, Mummies (if they ever escape the pages of that unused book on your shelf), Changelings, Demons and Vampires. Having to develop a Pokedex of scents as you play is also hilarious and fun. This Gift is already Green to my taste, but if you're in a cross-splat game it becomes Green with several exclamation marks. I'm now imagining a Get of Fenris Philodox who has become expert in sniffing out Changelings, then determining which ones are Sidhe...

                        Another thing to keep in mind with this Gift is how helpful it is in overcoming fakery. A Fomor might be able to perfectly imitate the voice and appearance of a Packmate, but you know instantly that they are not a Garou, and with some luck you know they're a Fomor. Whether Obfuscate overrides Scent of the True Form gets into Crossover Rulings territory, but the potential to overcome Obfuscate is also pretty neat. Even if you can't see past the disguise, you'll know that this "Kinfolk" is in fact a Vampire.

                        Truth of Gaia: An immensely helpful problem solver which is useful in both open use "I am going to ask you some questions with the ear of Falcon; you would be wise to speak truthfully my friend" and covert use "hey there, I'm with the press, I just wanted to ask you a few questions..."

                        Separating True from False, and True/False from Neither, is incredibly helpful in solving mysteries, rooting out treachery, casing a joint, interrogating captured foes, and the list goes on and on. My only recommendation would be not to become over-reliant on this Gift. Even if it's impossible for others to sense that you're using it, turning it on for every conversation is boring and arguably a fast track to the Paranoia Derangement. In all honesty I'm shocked there is no Gnosis cost; you'd think they would have wanted to make people pick their moment with this one!

                        ===========================================

                        As we can see Philodox start off very strong; I'd gladly pick up all five of these over time.​

                            RANK 1 GALLIARD GIFTS

                            Galliards get a slightly odd starting kit.

                            Beast Speech: I like this one a lot, but its utility will depend a lot on the Storyteller. Powers which let you communicate with things you normally wouldn't be able to communicate with, such as Wraiths and animals can put an unprepared ST on the spot and are a great window into whether they are facilitating a story being told or more "adversarial". Spirit Speech is useful even if a typical spirit acts like a dick at a given table; understanding and speaking with spirits is key to many Garou activities regardless of the disposition of the spirit. Talking to animals is still important to Garou, given their role as protectors of nature, but how the RP actually shapes up is key to this Gift.

                            When using this Gift, make sure to go through the section on The Curse in the book and confirm whether or not animals are bothered by your Rage, and to what extent. RAW suggests that animals are less upset by your Rage than humans are, but I've also seen people imply that animals are utterly terrified of Garou. Of course this is before you get into the fact that some animals will just flee from a large biped regardless of any supernatural energies.

                            Combined with Smell of Man, and with a good ST, this Gift could have really remarkable results. It's a solid Amber, but could be Red if you have an ST who is committed to making unexpected approaches crash and burn. In a way this applies to all Gifts, but I feel like powers which permit communication with unusual NPCs are particularly prone to this.

                            Call of the Wyld: This will be hit and miss depending on whether your ST lets it do anything. Making your howl be heard over a much larger area is handy, extra emotional power is handy, an extra die or point of Willpower here or there is handy, but it's so table and situation dependent it's hard to say anything definitive about the Gift.

                            One thing that's handy is that if a Gift doesn't specifically bar being amplified, this will spread a supernatural howl or sound based Gift over a much larger area, which is handy. Howls in the Night is a good example, as is Song of the Siren. Given that a wolf can already be heard about 10 miles away however, this isn't enough to make the Gift Green. It's good, but you could absolutely do your job without it and rarely miss it.

                            Heightened Senses: As in the Lupus section. This doesn't impact the rating, but I find it slightly confusing that this is on the Galliard list; the Philodox are the detectives.

                            Mindspeak: Very nearly Green, but not for the reason you think.

                            Covert communications are handy. The cost is exorbitant, which sucks, but being able to have a private conversation with others has great RP potential. You can speak, conspire, flirt, haggle, all without anyone else getting involved. Having to spend 1+ WP is bad of course, and super technically RAW you are not included for free as a participant. Of course that's a bad reading, but worth noting. All in all the intended effect of this power is good, allowing you to perform any social checks or perhaps even Gifts you need to perform within a semi-private environment. How NPCs and PCs react to this will vary, but it's a handy tool to have in your arsenal, especially if you're gagged or otherwise prevented from communicating.

                            The reason this is almost Green is because a somewhat reliable -2 dice penalty you can inflict on anyone within line of sight is pretty great. Drawing a significant foe into a waking dream which includes only the two of you means that you are at -2, they are at -2, and the rest of your pack are running with a normal dice pool. Its hidden utility is basically in debuffing a very dangerous opponent; after all they cannot make a check to escape, the only check is the one you make each turn. All dice pools decreasing by two means weaker attacks, less dodging, less chance to escape by Stepping Sideways, less luck running away... however this assumes that keeping Mindspeak going is a "free action" for the user each round. It seems intuitive that the Manipulation + Expression roll is a sort of automatic check to try and maintain the effect against unwilling participants; if it counts as an actual action for your turn this combo becomes extremely niche and isn't worth it in most cases.

                            It's worth noting that how this waking dream works is left up for us to figure out. Can you use Gifts such as Distractions via this waking dream with a full dice pool? We don't know. The book doesn't tell us. In ideal circumstances this could possibly be Green, but it's safest to leave it as a solid Amber. Talk through the Gift's powers and limitations before purchasing with your ST and see how they feel about it.

                            Perfect Recall: Any one detail. Detail is a precise wording. What's annoying is that this means that RAW you can recall the phone number of that suspicious element you tailed 10 years ago; you can't remember his known aliases, place of education, modus operandi and so on. Those would be 1 Gnosis each.

                            This Gift desperately needs a buff, but working with the Gift as is... I'd call it Red. Needing to remember something IC is a little bit niche. It comes up, but not that often. The ability to jab holes into memory wipes and amnesia is good, but again very niche. Niche is fine, but niche with a conscious activation which costs Gnosis in 100% of cases...

                            My edit would be to make the Gift free to use, no cost, but have a Gnosis cost for breaking through mind wipes and amnesia to grab a detail you question yourself on. For instance a Garou whose history was wiped from their mind by a Vampire could ask themselves "What Tribe was I initiated into" using the Gift, when prompted by another Garou, and remember that they were a Glass Walker etc.

                            I don't want to discourage anyone from playing a Galliard with this! They're really cool. Their starting list suffers compared to the other Auspices, but they're still a cool Auspice with a unique role. Their best Gifts just come later on. I'd suggest going for Beast Speech or Call of the Wyld at char-gen. Beast Speech, Call of the Wyld and Mindspeak are all worthwhile purchases for almost anyone, and Heightened Senses is handy if you want to be a super detective or the pack's best tracker.

                            The absence of Persuasion is noteworthy. Arguably the Galliards need it more than the Philodox. Overall a non-Homid Galliard will suffer slightly for lacking Persuasion, unless they are Fianna or Glass Walkers. If you are a Metis/Lupus Galliard outside those Tribes, I'd strongly suggest picking up Persuasion out of list, or asking your ST to add it to the Galliard list.

                                RANK 1 AHROUN GIFTS

                                A note on quality of starting Gift lists: You don't need a huge list of amazing Gifts to choose from at Rank 1. You won't be buying every single Gift available to you, even at 3XP each. Having a single amazing Gift in your list is great; Ahroun exemplify this in a way.

                                Falling Touch: I enjoy this one a lot. A difficulty of Stamina + Athletics can be very punishing against a Crinos form BSD. But against Spirits? A WP above 10 is possible, but against the vast majority of Spirits, Fomori and other foes it's going to be amazing. The mechanics of a prone enemy against a pack of Garou are not very fun for the enemy.

                                It's important to consider using a Sweep attack against many foes; a Crinos form BSD who will never fall to Falling Touch may fall very easily to a sweep attack. There are also enemies you could never trip over normally but are very vulnerable to this Gift (such as a giant spirit or a tentacled Fomor).

                                It's a good idea to make this part of another attack (including a sweep attack if you want to ensure they fall over), but if you're low on Rage you could devote your whole action to just lightly touching the enemy. Make sure they're grappled once prone to keep them there. How the ST runs "standing up" will also matter here; at my table it's a difficulty 3 check which you can split your dicepool for of course.

                                Used against the right foes this will turn a tricky fight into this: https://media.tenor.com/R24f2JXzuhgAAAAC/jojo-kick.gif

                                Being able to spend WP instead of Rage is great as well, though if you have this Gift you will almost certainly have high Rage. A Ragabash Stargazer will enjoy the Willpower option!

                                Consider using this outside of combat as well; knock someone over as you brush past them to create a distraction. Knock someone off a boat. Win a race. Activate it when someone tries to punch or shove you for a "I never touched him!". There are a great many situations where knocking someone over without any obvious effort is handy.

                                Inspiration: This is influential if you have a large number of allies, Gnosis to spend, and the fight is dragging on long enough that people have run out of WP to spend. In a prolonged series of encounters this could also help allies to spend 1-2 WP each scene without exhausting themselves. A "ghost" point of WP for every one of your allies is handy, and a good power to grab RP-wise if your Ahroun is meant to be an inspiring vanguard in battle.

                                For me personally, my Ahroun is a Child of Gaia. I'd rather keep his Gnosis for healing others. If your Ahroun is in a Tribe/Breed which lacks a good thing to spend Gnosis on then Inspiration becomes more tempting.

                                It's noteworthy that this does not have to be used in a fight. You'd assume so, but it can be used in any context. Say the himbo Ahroun of a Pack uses this Gift to keep a pack topped up with Willpower as they hack into a mainframe... it lets him feel useful!

                                Pack Tactics: If you have a Leadership of 5 and a pack of 5 this adds up to +1 die each. This isn't bad by any means, but most of the RAW pack tactics described in W20 aren't that amazing and worthwhile regardless. Generally speaking you're better off having one PC do a sweep and everyone else then follow this up with attacks, "manually", instead of doing a Pack Tactic dedicated to that combo. The best Pack Tactics seem to be in prior editions, such as the Forward Pass (a Crinos throws a Homid/Lupus to propel them towards an enemy).

                                If you have good Pack Tactics this is an Amber. If you have access to a short list of ??? ones which just describe things you can do manually, without much explanation of how the tactic is actually executed in play, then it could be Red. Playing it safe and making this one Amber, but it's a weird one. Needing a good Leadership pool is also annoying, but incentivizes purchasing the ability.

                                For me the ability to have +2 dice to my giant Metis yeeting a wolf at someone, and +3 dice to the yeeted wolf's tackle of the enemy they collide with, would make this worth it... but only once I've got his Leadership up to 5!

                                Razor Claws: This is excellent. The -1 difficulty and damage buff are great. I prefer claws to biting; putting any part of a Fomor or Bane inside your mouth is asking for trouble in my book. The downside is that Claws are +1 difficulty compared to Bites, and Hispo get bonus damage to Bites. Well Razor Claws help to make clawing better as well as safer; +3 damage total, difficulty 5 base. This then stacks later with Silver Claws for an amazing combo when fighting BSDs, corrupted and rebellious Garou, violent Fera etc.

                                Having to take a full turn sharpening your claws almost makes this one Amber. I love it, I use it often, it's great for ambushes and when battle is approaching you, when you have a heads up, when you're the instigator, when you're about to charge into a Pit. But the preparatory aspect and having to spend a whole turn on it is a real downside. Keep it in mind and always think about how you can prepare for fights and ensure you get a 3 second heads up.

                                This might also be helpful for cutting through things with your claws outside of combat, but non-combat applications are relatively limited.

                                Spur Claws: 10/10 for flavour, Bee Ahroun is neat.

                                It isn't clear whether both sets of claws transform with 1 rage point, and if that gives you two spur claw attacks before you run out of claws. I am assuming currently that you grow two sets of spur claws, and a successful claw attack empties one of your paws instead of both.

                                This Gift is best against a single powerful tanky enemy; if you're struggling to deal damage, instead you can inflict a +2 difficulty which will significantly hamper a dangerous foe. We aren't told whether this stacks. If you can get another set of claws into them in a bonus action from Rage... that could be +4 difficulty. Ask your ST. I also like to imagine that leaving a set of silver claws (from the Gift of that name) embedded into a Werewolf's body would make the difficulty spike +3 instead of +2, but speak to your ST.

                                If you have spare EXP to purchase this consider it a specialized tool in your arsenal; in many fights you're better off just blitzing with regular claw attacks.

                                Outside of battle this is a wonderful way to hamper fleeing enemies. They either take +2 diff to everything, or waste time stopping to remove the claws.

                                ================================================== ========

                                It's worth noting that Ahroun get nothing that helps with melee fighting at Rank 1! Klaive/Fetish Weapon users don't get much or anything from this list. But to use Ahroun as an example of what I described at the top of this post; say you hate Spur Claws, Pack Tactics and Inspiration. Can't see the point of them.

                                You don't really need them. Between Razor Claws, Falling Touch, and 2-4 Gifts from your Breed and Tribe you're set. If you have a list without much you want to buy, put more EXP into a different list, or into Attributes or Abilities. A list of powerful Gifts is good, but draws in a lot of EXP. Work with what you've got and buy what works for you.

                                    RANK 1 BLACK FURY GIFTS

                                    We're told that the Black Furies have Gifts that tie them to the Wyld, and that theirs are some of the most effective war-Gifts. Let's get into them!

                                    Breath of the Wyld: +1 die to mental rolls for the rest of the scene is handy, and will make your character a handy support to have around for any Perception/Intelligence/Wits based problem. Arguably this alone wins you a spot in the Glass Walkers' good books, and you're handy to have around for a tricky hunt. No cost means you can apply this to the whole party; the only risk of overuse is the potential for it being boring.

                                    The +1 difficulty to Rage rolls is a mixed blessing; this is handy when the moon is gibbous/full, or when it's half-full for a Philodox, and can offset the -1 Rage difficulty "penalty" of Crinos. It's also handy for keeping Garou/Fera/Fomori/possibly Vampires calm... but only slightly.

                                    The RP potential of this gift is handy; if you feel like your character would feel too defeated to stand up for what's right, feeling a rush of the raw power of the Wyld is a great impetus to start turning things around. Paired with the right words and actions this could make for very memorable moments. Rolling Gnosis is a little bit eh, but it's an important stat which is worth investing in. All in all a solid Amber and well worth taking.

                                    Man's Skin: The RP potential of this one is an obvious draw, as is the potential for disguising oneself to avoid drawing attention.

                                    I'm also charmed by a Seahorse being a potential teacher!

                                    No cost, pretty workable roll and difficulty, lasts for a scene, grants a handy yet fixed disguise; I'm down. Handy if your Black Fury needs to enter a place only men typically go (men's room, certain bars, competing in certain events).

                                    Weirdly this Gift reminds me of a quote by Sylvia Plath:

                                    Yes, my consuming desire to mingle with road crews, sailors and soldiers, bar room regulars — to be part of a scene, anonymous, listening, recording — all is spoiled by the fact that I am a girl, a female always in danger of assault and battery. My consuming interest in men and their lives is often misconstrued as a desire to seduce them, or as an invitation to intimacy. Yet, God, I want to talk to everybody I can as deeply as I can. I want to be able to sleep in an open field, to travel west, to walk freely at night…
                                    I'm desperately tempted to mark this one down as Green, but it's an Amber that I particularly like instead. I also like that the Gift establishes how it will work for male users.

                                    Heightened Senses: See the Lupus section; makes sense to have it given how wild/wyld the Furies are meant to be.

                                    Sense Wyrm: See the Metis section; I had no idea Black Furies got this one! Makes sense with them being the favoured daughters of the Wyld. It saves them from having an all-amber starting list. Any good Pack needs to have this, even if you need someone to purchase it out of list.

                                    Wyld Resurgence: the description of this power is epic! It sounds like a really potent ability, and there are prismatic lights and everything! There are level 5 gifts with more boring, grounded descriptions. It's almost too much.

                                    The mechanics are bad, unfortunately. You must spend a turn concentrating. You must spend 1 Gnosis. By default it heals 3 bashing or 2 lethal. If you spend 1 WP it heals 1 Agg, but not Agg caused by Silver of course.

                                    If you can wait a full round and do nothing but concentrate, then you have a way of getting rid of lethal and bashing damage; regeneration. Short of being stuck in your breed form this isn't a helpful Gift. Restoring Agg at 1 Gnosis and 1 WP a point is... having the option to do it is a bonus. But it's so expensive, time-consuming and far worse than simply being healed with Mother's Touch (or Grandmother's Touch, for those lucky Rank 2 Children of Gaia).

                                    I can see a few handy situational applications: you're a Kinfolk (with Gnosis) and thus lack regen. You have 4+ bashing or 3+ lethal on your sheet and have enough wiggle room to pause for 1 turn, but no more. You are poisoned or diseased, supernaturally, and are racking up Bashing/Lethal your regular Regen can't handle. You have access to a lot of Gnosis, you have high WP, and you need to remove some Agg.

                                    On top of being very niche, this Gift also is very obvious in a way which risks the Veil. I'll call it Amber because it can have its uses, but it's low down. I would never bother learning this out-of-list, and a Black Fury could easily skip it. If you lack a healer this Gift is a bit stronger.

                                    =======================================

                                    I enjoy this starting list; most are middle of the pack, not incredible, not awful, which means it's pretty well balanced. Lacking an astounding top tier and unique level 1 Gift is a shame; Sense Wyrm is incredible, but shared with many others.

                                        RANK 1 BONE GNAWER GIFTS

                                        The Bone Gnawers have Gifts that help them to survive.

                                        Cooking: Useful to have in real life, genuinely, and I like that it's on the list, but this one is almost Red. Much as with Sense Prey my feedback is "you are a Garou, you can find dinner". This has the advantage of not requiring you to hunt animals, having a handy roll, fixed difficulty, easy ingredients, but honestly would be better balanced as a level 1 Mystic Rite which is generally learnt by Bone Gnawers.

                                        The RP potential of turning a pot full of newspaper, mud and empty food cartons into nutritious food is good. The Gift also allows you to theoretically feed an unlimited number of animals/people, Veil permitting. If you care about the environment and poverty, and you have a game that is heavily focused on social issues and urban decay over fighting the manifestations of such decay, this Gift could see frequent valuable use.

                                        A stealth aspect of this Gift is that it permits you to dispose of things you might otherwise struggle to dispose of. Incriminating documents can be burnt, yes, but eating them is hilarious. We are told you can fill it with "whatever you can find", so now we may start to wonder about disposing of plastic. Famously a nightmare for the environment. A Gift that mystically transmutes shopping bags into a bland mush that can be used as fertilizer, pig feed, or safely burnt without any nasty emissions... we're deep into the kind of territory where an ST's opinion matters a great deal, but arguably the Gift is a sort of environmentalism hack, which is nice.

                                        All that said, a disposal unit is ultimately quite handy, especially if it only takes 1 minute to use; no time is given.

                                        Desperate Strength: This Gift is awful when you get into the fine print. It doesn't add to your Strength for Feats of Strength, 1 point per point of Bashing taken. That would be good! Sometimes you need to lift something super heavy in Homid. Sometimes you can't lift something in Crinos, and will gladly take some regenerate-able bashing damage to do so. But it doesn't do that.

                                        It adds dice to the Feat of Strength roll. Which is a WP roll, difficulty 9. More dice on that check is good and all, but it's a difficulty 9 check. You could take 6 bashing to fuel it and get 0 successes out of it.

                                        There's also the fact that if you botch a Feat of Strength roll, which is a real risk at difficulty 9, you can wind up taking unsoakable lethal damage. You know, after taking a bunch of unsoakable bashing damage...

                                        And then you consider that the maximum benefit from a Feat of Strength roll is +5 to your Strength. Even if everything works perfectly for you, there is a hard cap on the mechanic it feeds into. Red.

                                        My fix would be simple; the damage you take grants you an extra point of Strength (1:1 ratio) without having to roll Willpower at all, unless you want to.

                                        Resist Toxin: Total immunity to natural toxins (from arsenic to alcohol) and +3 dice to resist Wyrmish toxins. This Gift will come up frequently, and saves you from potentially deadly threats, along with a huge variety of debuffs which range from annoying to debilitating. A great many Wyrmish gifts, Spirit Charms and Fomori Powers will trigger this Gift.

                                        If you want to use it aggressively, have a water bottle laced with a deadly poison, drink freely from it, and offer it to your target. Stuff in that vein. Make yourself sick with poison to seem helpless, then turn the Gift back on to become a major threat. Win any drinking contest you like. Pour alcohol very freely at events to ply Pentex Reps as they get inebriated while you stay mostly sober at your own will.

                                        The ability to turn it off at will is great for roleplaying of course. Expect accusations of cheating in drinking contests with other Garou... though of course only Homids can partake in those.

                                        Scent of Sweet Honey: Being able to trigger this within an hour, regardless of distance, is great. Even if you have nothing to gain, it's fun to ruin the day of a dick who crossed you. Just be mindful that if they're a Garou, and there's even the tiniest chance you'll ever meet again, it's a bad idea.

                                        This Gift also has impacts which aren't mechanically explored. For instance you make the target smell of sweet honey; that's quite easy to track, say, compared to a scentless opponent or a conceptual spirit that lacks scent. Many people will freak out and behave erratically when covered in stinging insects. Some people are allergic. Some people are phobic. The -1 die penalty and obvious RP implications are already great, but the Gift can do a lot more.

                                        Tell someone they're cursed for a misdeed and then slam them with this later. Teach kids a lesson when they mess with nature or pick on the homeless. Make a mysterious foe stick out like a sore thumb. Prevent a Pentex rep from being presentable for their big presentation. Make a frog's day. Mess with a horse you're betting against. Use it on yourself to make an avant-garde and suitably disrespectful fashion statement for a moot. The limitless applications of the Gift are great.

                                        And then, if we think of the most boring possible use... is 1 Gnosis worth it to subtract 1 die from an enemy's dice pools for a fight? If they're a "boss" enemy, fighting solo, then potentially yes. If you lack a good use for your Gnosis and you aren't a powerful combatant, simply debuffing them could pull a lot of weight over time. Even if it prevents a few Agg from being dealt or dodged over the course of the fight it will be worthwhile I'd wager.

                                        There is also the potential that this Gift could do even more; if you release a huge container of insects nearby... who knows. Perhaps the penalty could end up larger. The -1 is a pretty good floor to build off of, but there's no reason the scent couldn't attract thousands of flying insects and completely blind/deafen the target.

                                        Trash is Treasure: Even in the realm of ordinary objects this is pretty good. Utilizing broken items as though they worked, potentially for a full day with WP, is a great power to have in your arsenal.

                                        If you're crossover-minded it's interesting to wonder whether this will allow broken Wonders, Treasures (and indeed Fetishes!) to function. A strict RAW approach from "full functionality and usefulness" could argue that such objects would be temporarily restored, but I don't believe this is intentional. If you bust up such an object the Magick/Art/Spirit escapes.

                                        An offensive application is to carry around busted up items; they are easier to acquire, ironically easier to maintain, and your enemies can't use them. "It's obviously broken" will also get your "replica" or defunctioned weapon past some kinds of security, but I don't believe it would fly with any serious security staff. You can do tricksy and fun stuff with this, and engineer fun situations where you become critical. You're the only one who can work the plane, the only one with a working gun, the only one who can get the damn lift to work. The RP potential of slipping into the broken cracks of a city's underbelly and living in the places others have written off as "in need of maintenance" is wonderful.

                                        Given the standard use is a roll with no cost, you can also do this to show off, which is fun. And at the upper end of absurdity you can restore a destroyed military helicopter or abandoned World War tanks for a full day, if you've got ammunition and the right fuel.

                                        Glass Walkers should try to learn this Gift since it will allow them to work with a busted computer, which counters attempts to destroy such tech.

                                        ======================================

                                        Bone Gnawer Gifts start off dripping with flavour, and all 5 Gifts are unique. I'd gladly pick up 4/5 of these on any BG character, eventually. Scent and Trash I'd gladly pick up for 5 EXP, given the chance, and given the right concept (a witchy Black Fury who punishes wrongdoers for Scent, a jury-rigging Glass Walker for Trash). Resist Toxin is worth the purchase on any Garou I'd argue. Eventually it'll save you from something bad.

                                            RANK 1 CHILDREN OF GAIA GIFTS

                                            "Surprisingly good warriors", healers, negotiators, peacemakers. CoG Gifts have a lot of ground to cover, and I reckon they deliver. They're my favourite Tribe, so be aware. Still, I think I can be objective about their starting offerings.

                                            Brother's Scent: This Gift feels a bit like the Obfuscate Discipline. You aren't invisible, you just go overlooked. Psychic invisibility. The example of wandering around naked, soaked in blood and holding a Klaive at a gala is fun. I also enjoy the lore behind it; drawing upon the brotherhood of all living things establishes some truth to the ideals of the Tribe.

                                            The roll is simple enough, it costs Gnosis which is a little annoying, and it automatically works against the vast majority of NPCs. Where it falls down is with security. If the ST decides someone would reasonably be watching out for people who are out of place, you'll be spotted, short of having an insanely good dice pool.

                                            It's easy to overlook but this power does work on supernatural foes. Just be mindful that their supernatural abilities may help them to pierce the illusion more easily.

                                            Jam Weapon: This is a potent Gift to use both offensively and defensively.

                                            Invest heavily in Brawl. Focus on your claws and fangs. Klaive duelists, staff users, Fetish weapon experts, gun users, enemies from many other splats, Pentex First Teams, some Fomori, plenty of enemies and potential opponents in challenges will wield weapons. Just make sure you aren't breaking any rules by invoking this power. Some might think it dishonourable to use a Gift of peace to win a fight, but the Gift isn't explicitly about peace; it's about making weapons not work.

                                            Defensively you can stop everything from a mugger's knife to a pre-detonation nuke. You simply need to have the weapon within the range of your "voice" - which can be quite far indeed. The Gnosis cost is well worth paying when you have a good target, be it a crowd of mortals with silver bullets loaded into their automatics, or an enraged Silver Fang with a Grand Klaive.

                                            Rolling WP against a difficulty of the highest WP of an armed individual in earshot favours you in most cases. Clarify with your ST whether your allies' WP's count if they are armed, or whether those who are not resisting the Gift are ignored.

                                            Various traps will also fail with this power, which is handy when you're wandering through the home of a paranoid werewolf hunter. Striding across a minefield safely would also be a good application, so long as the mines are within the reach of your voice when you shout.

                                            Finally, and most earnestly, weapons failing is a great opportunity to get up on your soapbox and tell people to give peace a chance. They're more likely to listen if you're a ripped giant who is infamous for being as master of vivisecting enemies with his claws, and if their gun/klaive/rocket launcher just stopped working.

                                            Mercy: Spend 1 Gnosis, you can only inflict Bashing. Niche, but definitely worth the investment.

                                            Great to use in Frenzy, as the Gift's flavour suggests. Amazing RP potential if used in a mock battle or hunt; my Ahroun has ragdolled a defeated ally with their entire head inside his mouth only for them to wake up with a few bruises, which feels neat and mystical.

                                            Don't use this Gift in a life or death battle unless you have a strong upper hand and a plan for what to do with your spared foe. Executions feel worse than killing in battle. Switching from Agg to Bashing is a nasty debuff, unless your foe soaks Aggravated. Even then, a foe is more likely to be able to Regenerate/Heal bashing. If you're looking to fight a Frenzying Garou, wayward Ronin, mind controlled ally etc. then this is a good Gift to trigger.

                                            Mother's Touch: See the Theurge entry. This healing ability is the envy of the whole World of Darkness, perhaps with the exception of Changelings, who still have to invest a decent amount of EXP into Spring; this only costs 3 EXP, and you're done. Ideally you should have a Theurge who has this, and a Child of Gaia who also has this, so they can heal one another.

                                            Resist Pain: See the Philodox entry. Having access to this Gift outside of being a Philodox is incredible; the role it complements most, of course, is the Ahroun's.

                                            ====================================

                                            Despite 2/5 Gifts being found elsewhere, the list ends up feeling unique and getting across the Gaian flavour. All five have their place and are well worth learning, which is unusual.

                                                RANK 1 FIANNA GIFTS

                                                Fianna Gifts have fae ties and also link to their vigour. Given that 3/5 are copied, this should be a quick one!

                                                Faerie Light: Given a surprising lack of Gnosis cost, this Gift is a pretty handy tool for lighting your way, tricking foes, luring parties off the road, and creating all kinds of distractions.

                                                Appearing anywhere within line of sight is handy for messing with people; from a high vantage point and with good eyes that's far indeed! There's also nothing stopping you from creating multiple lights for bonus confusion, or having them head in directions which mislead enemies. Generally speaking you should only start messing around with this gift to deceive if doing so is worth the risk of losing an ambush.

                                                Hare's Leap: See the Lupus entry; as I said there this is shockingly strong once you investigate the rules for jumping.

                                                Persuasion: See the Homid entry. It's a genuine shame that the Fianna get this as a Tribe while the Galliards don't get this as an Auspice; a Theurge of the Fianna could wind up a better 'face' than a Galliard of another Tribe. It's great to see here, but does add up to a problem with the Tribe's identity; they're seen as being Galliard: The Tribe at times.

                                                Resist Toxin: See the Bone Gnawer entry. I can't for the life of me figure out why this one is on the Fianna list. Because of the 'vigour' theme? Because the snakes (some of whom are venomous) were cast out of Ireland? Still, it's a strong addition.

                                                Two Tongues: This is a handy way to maintain the veil and trick enemies. It also 100% feels like it should be a level 1 Galliard Gift. Two Tongues fits better there than Heightened Senses, at least.

                                                The potential applications are pretty readily apparent, but here's a few neat spins on it. Speak one conversation into the phone ("Pretty sure they're Fomori, get here ASAP") and another to those standing nearby ("Listen I'm in a meeting, can you call me back later?"). Arrange one meeting location and time with one mark, and another with the other. Call emergency services and sound like you're ordering pizza or just carrying on a conversation with whoever is nearby. Insult one person while everyone else hears complements, then gaslight them when they fly off the handle. Provoke a Frenzy while looking like the innocent party.

                                                As with all Gifts that allow you to do tricky manipulative things, keep in mind that Truth of Gaia exists. Pick your deceptions carefully when it comes to your fellow Garou.

                                                Given the cost is 1 WP, there's no roll, and people only roll to see past it if they have a reason to be suspicious (say, for instance, you say your favourite colour is blue and someone yells "YOU BASTARD, WHERE ARE YOU KEEPING HER?!" in response to the threat only they heard), this one is green. Overuse it and it drops to Amber. Combined with Persuasion and good Charisma/Manipulation along with Social Abilities, and indeed Pure Breed, this Gift can make you a pretty incredible face without too much difficulty, and allows infinite creative applications.

                                                And on top of that, a double convo is just such a good RP prompt, especially in text RP.

                                                =====================================

                                                Fianna are off to a great start, though honestly they shouldn't have Persuasion. As I say above, they wind up feeling like a Tribe defined by an Auspice; the Bone Gnawers feel distinct from Ragabash, despite new players understandably conflating the two after their first skim of the book. When I look at the Fianna sometimes, as much as I like them, it feels like they're defined by a role that is too close to one Auspice in particular. This means Fianna Galliards feel kind of "obvious", and non-Galliard Fianna are dipping too easily into the best face gifts without having the appropriate Auspice. I can imagine being a Galliard of another Tribe and finding myself wound up by a non-Galliard Fianna stealing my thunder with Persuasion and Two Tongues.
