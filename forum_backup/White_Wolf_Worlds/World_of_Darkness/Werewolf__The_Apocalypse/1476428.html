    1st edition combat rules

    03-15-2022, 09:16 AM
    I am a huge fan of first and second editions. Much more horror and mistery and less fantasy.
    I was checking out again the various rules for combat, since I remember about the narrative combat described in wta1.
    The narrative combat seems to be extremely easy and totally ok (and deadly), but the advanced one is a mess. Maybe I'm missing something.

    ''Damage: The amount of wounds which have been inflicted on the target is determined by adding the number of successes obtained to the damage score of the weapon. The resulting number is called the damage total and indicates how many health levels the target loses. Soak: The target is allowed to make a roll in order to see how many wound levels might be avoided. Not every injury actually causes as much damage as it is capable of. The victim rolls Stamina+ 2, with the difficulty based on the general deadliness of the weapon + 2, or in the case of guns, the caliber of ammunition that is being used; this number is listed separately for each weapon as the Impact Factor. In the case of Garou, silver and biochemical weapons may not be soaked except with the gift of Luna's Armor (see Gifts). ''

    Does this include strength? Because this would be a carnage, even if soaking with +2 with difficulty based on the weapon damage.

    So, I decided to check out Vampire 1st ed and....the combat system is totally different:

    . Resolution Stage: The two matched opponents each roll Dexterity + Brawl (or Melee, if using weapons) in a resisted roll. The difficulty is dependent on what type of maneuver or weapon is being using, as described on the table below (...)
    The combatant with the most successes manages to make contact, and thus do damage. Each success more than the opponent's total gives the combatant an additional damage die. The damage is calculated by rolling the number ofdice indicated on the chart above (stremgth+weaponbonus) plus the number of additional damage dice figured from the last roll due to your extra successes. Difficulty is the opponent's Stamina + 3. Each success on the damage roll indicates that the target loses a Health Level. The other combatant is unable to inflict any damage this tum. Vampires can make a "soak" roll using Stamina + Fortitude to see if they can resist the damage inflicted. The difficulty for this roll is the number of damage successes the attacker achieved + 5 (max of ten of course). Each success reduces the loss of Health Levels by one. A failure means that the character takes full damage. Each botch on this roll indicates that one of the wound levels received is an aggravated wound, and is thus much more difficult to heal. Most often this indicates some sort of maiming, such as the loss of an arm or just a finger - it takes time to regrow that sort of thing.

    Anyone knows if there is some kind of system that is ok for both games in some errata or other books from first ed?
    Or everything was different on purpose? How is Under a Blood Red Moon to work?

                      Originally posted by Helur View Post
                      Yea, I agree. Multiple actions are plague, especially in combat. Everything slow don too much during moments that are suppose to be quick and brutal. What do you suggest to fix them? Automatic damage for rage?
                      I allow for each point Range spend a bonus of half the permanent Rage to attack rolls. The maximum of Rage spend per turn is also half the permanent rate (or dex, whichever is smaller).

                      I also experiment with multiple actions. The rules, when no supernatural effect is involved (like Celerity or Rage), also exist in 2nd edition, or revised edition and changed a lot, too.

                      I use them like this:
                      1. Declare all intended actions (ST may veto some combinations of actions, like multiple Gifts for example).
                      2. Determine dice pools for those actions. Select the largest dice pool. Bonus dice from Rage spending are factored in, if one action is an attack of some sort.
                      3. Remove one die for each action, even actions that do not need rolls, like activating a gift or moving.
                      4. Assign the remaining dice to the actions, as the player sees fit. Actions that don't need rolls, don't need dice assigned, but each action that needs a roll, needs at least one die assigned, but not more than the original dice pool was.
                      5. Roll for each action, in the intended order.

                      So, Rage spending can lead to a devestating attack, since successes from the attack carry over to damage.
                      But it also allows a bonus when using the "mundane" multiple action rules. It would even allow for multiple attacks, just not at "full capacity", so to speak.

                      Also, my approach factors in gift usage and other actions that don't need rolls. Normally these aren't mentioned in the RAW, when reading multiple action rules.
