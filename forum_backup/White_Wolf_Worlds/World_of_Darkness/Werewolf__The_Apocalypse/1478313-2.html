    I don't have any huge interest in WtA to be honest, but the turn the recent discourse took grabbed at me and I want to second some being baffled at the idea that the deeply flawed protagonist has gone anywhere. Peaky Blinders alone had a just finished pretty triumphant six season run that is getting a full out follow up movie, just to add onto Heavy Arms' list (and also because, y'know, I like excuses to mention that show. It's good stuff). Raised by Wolves is a tale up and down the stretch of it of often outright intentionally horrifying people/entities as the protagonists if you want a genre material example. Dexter of all things just came back for a season to redo the way it ended. Snowfall and Mayans are rolling along as from the perspective of the criminals crime dramas. Snowpiercer goes pretty grey pretty often. Even Foundation spends as much time with the Emperors as its outright protagonists as any other character (which, if you were into the novels, is honestly kind of exasperating for what you might have been expecting the show to do instead, but the example stands all the same). The list goes on.

    Deeply flawed protagonist stories are everywhere. Media is drowning in them. They're still heavily popular and acclaimed. Throw a rock and hit one.

    Honestly if anything we are still pretty much in that early 00s era overplay. It never went away. The only real difference is other stories are also being told with some amount of prominence.

                                                    Originally posted by Mercurial View Post
                                                    They had that high profile quality where their fall would have been shocking, while also having an aura where their value was questionable at best. And it would have fit with the dissolution of the Garou nation.
                                                    Kinda taking this in reverse order:

                                                    Again, I'd caution against assuming too much about what Garou society is going to look like in W5 until we get far more concrete previews or the book itself. Even if the Garou Nation completely dissolves, it doesn't mean the end results are a society where the Silver Fangs don't make sense. The BNS metaplot was a division of the Garou Nation into two factions, with the Silver Fangs heading one, and the Shadow Lords heading the other. Dissolving doesn't mean we're going to have a W5 setting where a leadership oriented Tribe no longer has a purpose because there's no larger social structures.

                                                    Also, I'd say the very fact that the value of the Silver Fangs being questionable is their most compelling reason to continue to exist. If the WoD is supposed to explore left-political criticisms off political issues, we want a vehicle to examine the role of the elite; esp. elite that claim the right to executive power. Even as we've moved away from absolute monarchies (though those still exist too), we still have autocrats, and we have constitutional chief executives that are still firmly controlled by the new elites. Even if some try to cultivate narratives to the contrary, presidents and prime ministers are people that had prestige and power before that. Since we need elites to do commentary on elites, if you cut the Silver Fangs, you run the risk of just diluting the Shadow Lords into being Silver Fangs but with different fur colors.

                                                    Of course, this is me, I'm completely amenable to the argument that the Silver Fangs and Shadow Lords are redundant and we only needing one Tribe of rulers with factions around those currently in power vs those currently out of power. But that's reboot talk.

                                                    Originally posted by Sergeant Brother View Post
                                                    Though, I really don’t see why a Tribue has to fall.
                                                    We haven't actually seen an argument from the creators yet around this. We should let them try to explain their thinking at some point. Even if you're completely right that the fact that it's not readily apparent is troubling and there are clearly other options.
