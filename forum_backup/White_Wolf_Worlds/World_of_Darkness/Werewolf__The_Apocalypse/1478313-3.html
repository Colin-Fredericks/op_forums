        I can understand why Onyx Path isn't doing the open dev thing as much as they used to. It makes the process even slower, the noise (bad feedback) to signal (good feedback) ratio is horrible, the open dev era led to lots and lots of unfounded speculation on the forums (that makes threads like this one look tame) as people poured over every word to divine the final product instead of voicing useful concerns, for the WoD specifically Onyx Path also has to consider Paradox's own feelings on open dev and how much control Paradox wants over the books, and you get really nasty things like Changeling: the Lost 2e where the first lead developer left the project and the second developer took the game in an entirely different direction which led to a lot of division between people that wanted the first version and all that drama that only happened because people saw so much during the open dev period.

        It's also not just Onyx Path. A lot of developers that used to do open dev have stopped because it was a fad people tried, and for the most part didn't really improve things as much as was hoped.

                        Originally posted by Damian May View Post

                        We're 4 years in and we haven't even seen a release of whose working on it yet.....
                        Well a second release. We got a first release before they switched which company was handling it and the first team announced was disbanded (though with no declarative statement that none of them would be involved or something like that).

                        The fact that W5 is in a second completely restarted development cycle is its own big problem, but not really guiding to the point at hand.

                        Originally posted by heinrich View Post
                        There were several ideas I didn't like in the game test at the WoD Convention in Berlin - and I'm glad they didn't make it to the final product.
                        There's a huge difference between playtesting (something both Paradox and Onyx Path continue to do) and open development (which neither does as policy, and while Onyx Path leaves it up to individual developers it's fallen completely out of favor outside of a few blog posts here and there.

                        Keep in mind that large scale playtesting like Paizo, WotC, and nWW did are huge time consuming and costly endeavors to do right. Most companies, including Onyx Path, don't have the resources to do a global playtest. The sheer amount of data collection, sorting, and analysis for dealing with thousands of playtest reports is months of full time work for a small team of people.

                        On the other hand, I feel, freelance authors as well as the decision makers at nWW or OPP work from a kind of confirmation bubble that possibly leads to content that caters to some (felt) requirements (and possibly theories about long term brand development) but not necessarily to the majority of the fan base...
                        Knowing what you do about open dev could you actually create a functional guide with a good accuracy rate at figuring out which books got open dev and which didn't for people that don't know where to look it up?

                        And again, it's not just nWW or Onyx Path here. During the early 2010s, lots of smaller companies were doing open dev because it seemed like a good idea for a number of reasons. By 2020... almost nobody does them any more. Whatever the trades off are, lots of RPG developers have decided that open dev takes up more energy than it's worth.

                        Is what you're talking about a risk? Sure. But it's also how RPGs were done for ~30 years before enough fans were online for open dev to even be real thing..The entire cWoD original run was closed dev. Meanwhile, D&D 4e's failings are regularly pointed to it's quasi-open dev process that resulted in a "designed by committee" process that resulted in a fantastic system that completely lacked the enticing flavors of 3.X, PF1e, or 5e.

                        There is no perfect solution here. But when the majority of the industry jettisons a design approach... there's probably a good reason for it.

                                                            For me, the Get were simply the guys you picked if you wanted to play Thor as a werewolf. This idea that you absolutely positively had to be a Nazi as a Get is, to say the least, news to me.
                                                            "...had to be a Nazi as a Get..." is a strawman.

                                                            Thor is actually a great pop culture example of the problem! Look at Thor in his first MCU solo movie and the first Avengers movie (though there's plenty in the later movies too, but Loki's redemption arc muddies things): watch how Thor bends over backwards to try to defend Loki. Loki is his brother (if adopted). Loki is Asgardian. Loki's cleverness is "strength" in Thor's eyes even if not Thor's way of being strong. And because of all of that Thor never stops giving Loki another chance even while Loki is still unapologetic for being a mass murderer and only really cares about the damage he's caused when it gets Frigga killed.

                                                            Thor is, if you stop and think about it, complicit in a huge amount of harm that Loki caused because Thor just chalks it up to Loki being Loki and it not really mattering because Loki's toying with lesser species (so are Odin and plenty of others of course). You can play Thor-Get all you want, but you're playing the guy that stands up for Nazi-Loki because he's in your Tribe and you can't bring yourself to cast him out no matter how much twisting of your dad's teachings he does.

                                                            That's the problem the Get have always had, and had even after the Swords were wiped out: you can't play a Fenrir without being the Thor that ends up enabling a Loki. The Thor/Get way of exalting the strong is inseparable from the Loki/Swords ideals of oppressing the weak. That's why MCU Thor exalts in seeing Captain America being worthy of wielding Mjolnir: Steve's idea of strength is better than the one Thor inherited from Odin. That's why he gives up ruling the remaining Asgardians to Valkyrie. Thor grows up and learns that being the strongest doesn't actually make you the best; it just makes you strong.

                                                            All werewolves have a subtext of social darwinism, except maybe the COG, but that subtext was about behavior, not race. If you behaved with strength, honor, and courage, you were worthy, if you didn't, you weren't worthy. Your skin color meant jack. The Get seemed to buy into that even more than most werewolves, and that was about it.
                                                            This is ignoring something that's been in every edition of WtA: The Fenrir don't like how the spirits judge strength, honor, and courage. There has always been a conflict between their ideals, and the fact that "weaker" Garou like the Bone Gnawers and Black Furies somehow still manage to attain Renown and Rank.

                                                            "Strength" is the sticking point because who's strong and who's weak, and thus who's worthy of power and who has power they don't deserve is a completely arbitrary cultural norm the Get have constructed. That's why they're fertile soil for bigots. The Valkyrie Camp of Fenrir have always been on the verge of defecting to the Black Furies because they're sick and tired of mainstream Get "strength" being biased towards masculine associated ideals of strength: a female Get has to be twice as strong than a mediocre male Get to be acknowledged as strong "We don't care what's between your legs, we just care about strength! Pay no attention to the fact that all our tests of strength at biased towards people with high levels of testosterone." "We don't care about the color of your skin, we just care about strength! Pay no attention to the fact that all our tests of strength are biased towards Garou with strong breeding with the right kind of people."

                                                            Institutional discrimination is a thing, and the Get are extremely guilt of it. Claiming you only care about personal merit doesn't work in a world that starts people on unequal ground.
