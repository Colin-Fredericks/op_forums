    Hey guys,

    My players have reached the point of going to rank 2. I'm looking for fun ideas for the challenges for they being recognized.

    Some background:

    Tha Pack:

    Ragabash Lupus Silver Fang.

    Theurge Metis Child of Gaia.

    Philodox Homid Silent Strider

    Galliard Homid Shadow Lord

    Ahroun Metis Glass Walker (Cyber Dog).



    The Master of Challenge in the Sept is a Fenrir Ragabash that hate their guts, but as he has a thin hold on the position (being a ragabash and not a philodox), he can't send them to anything that is cleary a suicide.

              Mind you, we're coming at this as a "typical" situation, and in a "typical" situation, each member would challenge someone outside of their Pack, and preferably of their Auspice to prove themselves worthy of Rank Fostern.
              However... how an ST wants to handle a whole pack ranking up together (more like a Pack Rank than personal Rank), takes this out of the core books and into the ST's hands (which is sounds like you're asking us to help you with.)

              So, what does "Rank 2" mean?

              Firstly, let's look at my own perspective on Ranks and what it takes to achieve them:

              Rank 0 (Cubs) are preteens. They're learning the basics, not getting their hands dirty. They're on probation, "Probies" waiting for their opportunity to become members of the club.

              Rank 1 (Cliath) are teenagers. They've proven they're capable of some duties, but not without supervision and having an eye kept on them. Sure, they have jobs, but they still live with their parents and aren't seen as "grown up" yet.
              Proving yourself worthy to be Cliath is usually pretty minimal in the grander scheme of things, but enough to show that you've learned what it means to be Garou and to work with others who may become your Pack.

              Rank 2 (Fostern) are grown-ups in their 20s. They've proven they can hold a job, manage themselves, and are making their way in the world. Now's when they're starting to be given real responsibilities, and are being held accountable for the mistakes they've made with more than slaps on the wrists.
              Proving yourself worthy to be Fostern usually means that you have shown you understand what it means to be of your Auspice, but if you're going to test what it means of a Pack, consider a test that will include a little bit of everything.. some espionage (Ragabash), some tooth-and-claw command (Ahroun), some sort of tough moral decision (Philodox), Some greater understanding and revelation of the Spirits (Theurge), and the morale and storytelling of the Galliard when the mission is complete.

              Rank 3 (Adren) is where folks aim for (if they're still alive) before things become their toughest. You're given quite a lot of responsibility in the Sept. You'd better have figured out your place by now, or you're going to be given a hell of a lot of grief. Now you're helping to educate and raise those beneath you. You're in charge of others. You are responsible for important tasks of the Sept. You've proven yourself of your Auspice and have made quite a name for yourself.
              This is where individual tasks start to make more sense, otherwise just "upping the difficulty" seems a bit over-done. These challenges can take weeks to accomplish and should really start to test the Garou's ability to make good decisions. Afterall, they will start to become the leaders, educators, and important individuals of the Nation, if not the Sept. They will be taking on responsibilities of the Sept that could mean the life, or death of the Caern. Maybe have each one see the Eldest of their Auspice and challenge. Sit with each player and give them a private game, or have them go in groups of 2 so that they feel a real sense of importance to the missions. Again, they are challenging for the right to be recognized as Adrens, of learned individuals in their prime.

              Rank 4 (Athro) is the position of great importance. You're not quite an Elder, but you're damned close. Not many Garou make it this far, and generally (not PCs, apparently) most Athro are powerful, but keep to the Sept to protect and guide the underlings to becoming great Garou warriors too. The Gifts of this level also typically show just what the scope of their powers are here, but in general, you're getting some pretty cool stuff by the time you reach this level, which means that the Spirits have recognized your power, status, and station in the grand order of things. You are a great and powerful Garou. You'll be the head of a Sept, more than likely, in charge of a Caern, a representative to the Nation. You will likely spend more time at the Sept than out on the field, but when you do go out, you'll be leading Packs in battle.
              Rank Challenges to become Athro are damned hard. They can take months to accomplish, often trying to do great and heroic things to show that you can step up beyond the every-man that is expected and become greater. You may spend a great deal of time going through Umbral Realms, recovering lost relics. A whole pack becoming Athro at once is unlikely, but I suppose not outside of the realm of possibility--but it's likely not the same group that started as Claiths (most Garou don't make it to this Rank, let alone deserve it.)

              Rank 5 (Elder) is really the highest of station any Garou can really hope to achieve, and this is often because they've out-lived most others through their cunning, skill, and talents. They've proven themselves over the years to be the most useful, hearty, smart, clever, wise, and lucky Garou. They've accomplished so many great things and have proven themselves to be greatest among their contemporaries and great in the eyes of their ancestors.
              Becoming an Elder means challenging a council of Elders come up with some great test to prove yourself worthy of this greatest of station that any Garou can be awarded by their peers.
              This task could be a personal mission that takes months or years to accomplish, could be a lone-wolf mission deep into the Umbra, or could require a Garou to prove that they are able and willing to lead and command a group of other Garou on a very important task (or series of tasks) that could take a long time to accomplish. I don't see a pack all doing this together by canon, but in a TT game, an ST has their own game's identity and if you'd like to do this all together with whatever make-up the Pack has, then sobeit--it's your game.

              So... what do I think you should do for this pack who want to all become Fostern together? A Fenrir Master of the Challenge is going to focus brute force, might-making-right, but he would still administer the task with honor. This Pack may be required to acquire something by force, whether it be from the Wyrm, or the Spirits. Incorporate force, theft, trickery, bravery, a few tough decisions, a sacrifice that will be missed (not just Gnosis, but perhaps a relic, a Battle Scar, a scary favor to be returned, etc.), have it deal with the spirits and utilize the Bard's talents for keeping his Pack going, and let his moment shine when it comes time to tell the tale of the Pack before the Sept. Again, Rank 2 Challenges should be them showing that they understand their places in the Nation, so let the Auspices have their moments to shine... Ahroun are the Generals of the Garou, so even if he thinks he's there to be the brute force, let him start to think about what it means to lead into battle with his brains. The Galliard is not just the tale-teller, but the inspiration and heart of the Pack, so let there be times where her words must inspire and raise the Pack up with words and song. The Philodox must learn to adapt the real world to the Litany and understand that the Laws of Man are not the Laws of the Garou. Make a tough call for her to make... what is right, what is wrong by the laws of the Garou. The Theurges are the most attuned to the Spirits--give them the opportunity to find secrets among the Spirts, to attune to them and connect with them in ways the other Auspices just can't seem to grasp, and to understand the ways of the Spirts which may be quite alien to the Garou. And the Ragabash... tricksters, sure, but the Questioners of the Ways. Stealthy, sneaky, and manipulative, the Ragabash are also the ones who must seek the flaws in the enemies and allies plans and expose them. Give the Pack a plan to follow, and let the Ragabash find the flaws you've created, and when they've discovered them, let them KNOW that they've done so as a reward for having done their job!

              I cannot stress this enough: Reward your Pack when they do what they're supposed to be doing. Not every decision they make needs to be twisted back on them with an "a-hah! I gotcha!" from the ST. Especially at this point of the game, reward the players for following their roles, Don't muddy the waters, but rather enhance those dynamics that make the Auspices so fundamental, so necessary. You have a full-Auspice pack here, so let each one shine. Let each player enjoy that role and get the most out of it. Later you can twist the world around and start to pull the pieces away from them, but for now let them bask in their Auspices and roles of the Pack.
