    Hauglosk and hunters

    09-04-2023, 03:03 AM
    This is just something that came to my mind, but the concept of Hauglosk is that you get lost in an objective to the point of going to the last consequences, at least that's what I understood. I don't know much about Hunter, but if I'm not mistaken, they didn't receive powers from an entity, which to this day I don't know what it is, which allowed them to use their abilities? Wouldn't it make sense for them to have something like Hauglosk? They feel an impulse to act on their main virtue to the point where they act like mere soldiers. This would also give this entity extra weight, making it more sinister.

      In the old Hunter the Reckoning, once your Imbued character's Virtue went past 7 that character started to accrue derangements related to being a Hunter. So it's kinda there.

      H5 is a bit of a mess when it comes to defining its special type of Hunter. Technically you have Vigilante hunter-orgs (which I always call the "You're playing H5 wrong"-hunter-org) which is all about hunter-cells that are too focused on the Hunt and have a whole "the ends justify the means"-thing going on. They are fine with collateral damage and using whatever unethical means necessary to get the job done. The mechanics of the game itself are just a mess. Drive is supposed to be the big thing that is the difference between a Hunter and a "hunter" (from a hunter-org, for example) but in the game Drive doesn't actually play a big role. It tells you what you need to do to get out of Despair - and that's it. Drive doesn't matter in any other kind of context.

      For W5 specifically, there you got the idea that hunters are associated with the Weaver, so you could bring in some Weaver-stuff (like Drones, for example) from the previous editions to depict that.

              To expand a bit:

              The identity of the being responsible for the Imbued running around is actually answered in the books, but in a ST book with a lot of spoiler warnings around it. It's not really something that adds anything to playing the game, it barely adds anything to running the game besides knowing that your own personal idea is going to be so off base that it isn't going to fit with some of the later supplements. It's not a mystery the PCs are going to solve, or even really get a chance to interact with in a meaningful way. The doubt and conflict of not having an answer is part of the themes of the game.

              As noted above, HtR 1e basically had these ideas covered. If your Virtues go too high up, it starts messing with your head. Though going beyond the derangements you rack up, there's also becoming on Extremist: a state that requires having 10 dots in a Virtue and falling down one of the three paths towards obtaining one of the 5th level Edges While Extremists aren't inherently unplayable like hitting Hauglosk 5 is... it's generally still marking the end of that character's journey as a PC. Hitting a 10 in a Virtue is like getting Hauglosk 4: you're close to the edge, but you can stay like that if you try hard, while becoming an Extremist is like hitting Hauglosk 5 with some time to enjoy your new toys before your character becomes unplayable (or more likely just straight up dead).

              The two Broken Creeds also map onto the Harano/Hauglosk divide fairly well. The Hermits are excellent information gatherers, but they're broken because of sensory overload that's so painful it drives them to seclusion and inaction, while the Waywards are broken by the fact that they can't turn off (literally, a normally activated power all Imbued have is constantly on for them). On thing unmentioned about the Waywards that makes them hated and feared: they have access to some of the few Edges that work on other Hunters. Waywards can force other Imbued to fight even if everyone else would think it would be best to assess more before engaging.

              In many ways, the two Broken Creeds map on to the two unplayable Tribes in W5: The Hermits, like the Stargazers, are distant and do things in strange ways they can't really explain well to others, but usually try to help if they get involved, while the Waywards are like the Cult of Fenris without any historical baggage with the other Creeds.

                              I don't think Harano/Hauglosk as a mechanic is that great. First, unlike previous morality-systems in any WOD- or CofD-game (with H5 being the exception), the system is less a sliding scale and instead an on/off-switch. Before... take your pick, Humanity in V5, Integrity in HtV, Harmony in WtF... when you get to an extreme place (very low or very high) on these trackers, there would be a mechanic to go along with that which would influence how the PC behaved. With Harano/Hauglosk, the game doesn't care if your PC has Hauglosk 1 or Hauglosk 4, for example. You can roleplay the difference, sure, but the game itself doesn't care. The only thing the game cares about is the moment you hit Harano/Hauglosk 5 because then the character is taken away from the player. The second thing is how the mechanic actually works is weird. There are separate triggers for Harano and Hauglosk but the dice-pool you're rolling for both is adding up your Harano-points and your Hauglosk-points. That means the best protection against Harano and Hauglosk is having 2 to 3 points in both. This is similarly strange to how Touchstones allow you to transfer a point between Harano and Hauglosk. The mechanic itself is clear but what it's supposed to represent narratively is just weird.

                              The reason H5 has no morality-mechanic whatsoever is also a deliberate choice by the developers. Justin Achilli had talked about that before. He didn't like how Humanity in V5 worked because of how low Humanity would influence your roleplaying with its mechanics, that mechanics like these would restrict player-choices which is bad (and we kinda see that again in how Harano/Hauglosk is an on/off-switch in W5 instead of the trackers from other WOD-games). Additionally, the lack of moral mechanics in H5 was supposed to indicate how other supernatural splats are influenced by their urges like the Beast in VtM or Rage in WtA while since you're playing a human in H5, you're not supposed to be influenced by anything and the player-choices are all your own. And that's why there are no morality-mechanics in H5. At least, that's how Justin Achilli explained it.

                              So, ultimately, I don't think there's any need to look at Hauglosk at all. Both the old HtR and HtV have functional systems that can express a Hunter's descent. Well, and H5... I personally don't like the system but the developers wanted it to be like that, so there's that.
