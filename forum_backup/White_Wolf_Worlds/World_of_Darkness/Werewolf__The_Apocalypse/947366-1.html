            Just my additional thoughts:

            1. Umbra revised is the only book that mentions that it is bright for a werewolf during daytime in the umbra. All other books (mainly the core books of the series) state that it was dark, since garou can't see the sun. How Helios-related shifters would perceive the situation is not describes. I'd go with Umbra revised, all the time.

            2. Peeking isn't limited to a turn, if successful. At least I have no indication that it was not lasting as long as the garou concentrates. But it is dangerous since the garou can't perceive the penumbra any more, that is spirits, beings that are there and inanimate ephermal objects and landscape. Unless the garou takes damage, spirits might be able to surround the garou and call for backup, as loud as they want. I guess, some other instances will end peeking to, falling from a cliff for example, or being knocked prone or other forced alterations to movement. So, the garou wouldn't realise someone is collaring him, but once the leash is pulled he stumbles and chokes and that he would probably realise, even if it doesn't add to actual damage.
            As far as affecting the material world from the umbra goes: the earliest gift allowing for that is from Book of Auspices and it is a level two gift. Unlike discipline levels, a garou needs to have accomplished something to be allowed to learn level two gifts, but level two is fairly early. Although the gift has some limitations as to how high the gauntlet in the area where the attack occurs may be, it is a decent assassination gift.
            The most recognised gift to do such an attack allows for claw usage and is level 4 for one specific tribe and a couple of other werebeasts. This is really uncommon, I'd say. Especially if compared to the level two gifts which can only be used with a knife or handmade weapon (although, I guess if you can hand-craft a nuclear device it counts; also fetish-weapons may be handmade, like a Fang Dagger). The 'Spirit Knife' is available to all shamans, which is one in five werewolves approximately and knife damage plus crinos strength is still a fairly high amount of lethal damage.

                                Originally posted by Crusher-of-Cities View Post
                                I would generally rule that with a Gauntlet of 0 the two worlds are co-located, but I wouldn't consider anyone to have 'crossed' into the other once the gauntlet rises again. But as you said, I don't think that's directly clarified anywhere.
                                The more interesting question for me is, what happens when a person actively leaves an area where both are co-located. Meaning a human is at a spot in the world where the gauntlet is zero, i.e. in a forest clearing. He then sees something "strange" (as in a spirit), he would normally not see and decides to follow that being. His intent is to leave this place on the penumbra side of the Gauntlet. If intent is an contributing factor in (pen-)umbral travel, then the human might just be on the other side of the Gauntlet when he leaves the scene for a place where there actually is a Gauntlet..

                                Stuff like this may very well be the source of some fairy tales about strange worlds, humans might get trapped in. (Aside from similar places where the access to the Dreaming is possible, instead of the middle Umbra).


                                The process of becoming a spirit is called "Disconnecting" and it is iirc only detailed for garou. If they don't enter the Umbra, the difficulty for stepping sideways increases by 1 per year. If the increase reaches 10 they are considered a spirit. There are actual Silent Strider NPCs in the books who turned spirits in that way and are therefore the closest the Striders have to Ancestors. Also, a week (I think) of sustenance is equal to one temp Gnosis, so Garou can survive quite a bit in the umbra.

                                I think it is never detailed for beings other then garou (and by extension several fera). My guess is, that such a person could eat parts of the umbral landscape to get sustenance, as well as spirits. I mean, if such a person could kill a animal spirit gaffling, it could be eaten. If they are in a penumbral-equivalent of a grocery store, then there might be something resembling food there. But starving would be the most likely scenario. That is, unless the person makes it into a realm, where per definition other rules might apply.
