                                            Prehistoric Explanation, sometime in 7,000BCE the Vietnamese started to generate agriculture and pottery almost identical to that of Mesopotamia. This is one possible scenario that explains outsiders in Vietnam.

                                            Roman Explanation, 336 BCE - 323BCE, Alexander the Great marched and conquered from Europe to India, in his wake he left diplomats and soldiers in each area he conquered. If the CoG camp followed in their wake and decided to keep going after his death it would be easy to understand how they passed through Indian cultures until they finally came to the ocean on the east side of Vietnam and decided to stop.

                                            Silk Road Explanation, in the 1st Century BCE Romans began to create trade routes beyond the territory left by Alexander the Great, one of the stops for travelrs along the Silk Road was in Vietnam on the way to China.

                                                            Not so much an alternate tribe as a sub-group, but something I've been thinking about for a while due to the utter waste of lore I sometimes see in the Fenrir:

                                                            Úlfhéðnar (northern Germanic), Wargs (western Germanic), Outlaws/Rebels/Vargr (to the Get of Fenris)

                                                            "Odin could make his enemies in battle blind, or deaf, or terror-struck, and their weapons so blunt that they could no more but than a willow wand; on the other hand, his men rushed forwards without armor, were as mad as dogs or wolves, bit their shields, and were strong as bears or wild bulls, and killed people at a blow, but neither fire nor iron told upon themselves. - Heimskringla

                                                            The Fenrir are some of the deadliest creatures to walk the earth, and they count as one of their greatest enemies Woden, a shamanic god-king they say walks with the Wyrm. They howl paeans to Luna, she of the changing face, a goddess incarnate in the sky. Somehow, these mighty beasts were unable to stop their Kinfolk from honoring Wyrm-folk as their gods.

                                                            This is because the Fenrir lie. They are weak.

                                                            Not in battle, no. Not in lore or song or history. But still, they are weak. They are outsiders amongst those they call Kin, they are the true vargr.

                                                            When the ancestors of the Germanic peoples left central Asia for Europe, they took with them veneration of their ancestral gods, those the Fenrir call evil. For all their vaunted strength, the Fenrir could do little but call their gods monsters; they could never convert the folk to their ways, nor force the folk to their blasphemous subversions. Woden was a god, not a Wyrmthrall; the moon was Mani's face, not some Latinate goddess...all they agreed upon was that the earth was their Mother, and wolves were, indeed, holy. "Mere humans" defied them, for thousands of years, and always among them were the faithful, the true, Woden's wolves.

                                                            Is it the way of a strong warrior to hide in the shadows like a Leech, proclaiming their dubious dominance? Is it holy to spit on the body of your people to follow some alien creed of moon-goddesses and evil ancestors?

                                                            The Úlfhéðnar, the Wargs, say no.

                                                            They say it is the Fenrir who are weak. They say it is the Fenrir who are unholy. It is the Fenrir who spit upon the old ways. The Fenrir call them outlaws and rebels, but they know themselves as the true people of the north, holding fast to gods that were old when the Fenrir were still cubs. The Fenrir claim dominion among werewolves over the Germanic peoples (those who aren't Bone Gnawers, or Children of Gaia, or Glass Walkers, or Shadow Lords, or Silver Fangs), but they are little more than dogs, skulking in the darkness outside the people they claim, propagating a foreign creed because they were not strong enough to be true to their heritage.

                                                            The Outlaws remain true to this day. A rebel camp within the Get of Fenris, a conspiracy of fanatic heathens, they are the werewolves who still feel the truth of their ancestral culture, rather than denigrating it, hiding from it and calling it "weak." They organise in their own packs, when possible, and hold moots far from the eyes of the psychopaths who proudly proclaim their descent from a monster destined to help destroy a world.

                                                            Ironically, their greatest allies in the World of Darkness are rarely other werewolves; among their kin they count all others who follow the ancestral ways, whether human or wizard or even draugr. The Garou (a name which, somewhat ironically to them, comes from the Frankish word for "werewolf"), are rarely willing to openly defy the Fenrir by succoring Wargs, but in multitribal septs without the presence of the Get, they do well. Unfortunately, most septs close to their Kinfolk contain Fenrir, so they range far and wide in the modern day, not unlike their human relations.

                                                            Common totems of Úlfhéðnar packs include Bear, Bull, Grandfather Thunder and Stag.
