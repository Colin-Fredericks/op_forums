    Originally posted by Manfr View Post

    The Sept of the Water's Birth, though, is told to be born explicitly out of Garou activists who are there for "the purpose of trying to keep people from dying in the sea; its members radicalized by the easy acceptance of death in the human society around them". The two named characters of the Sept turned into Garou respectively because they witnessed the atrocities of the sea lanes and because they survived them.
    And it doesn't work. This takes a real-world human activist mission and just adds "What if some of them are Garou?". And the thinking stops right then and there. That is such a lame premise! The way it's written the fact they're Garou adds nothing to this. Okay, the Garou have supernatural powers... So what? It still all amounts to them just doing the same things these human activists already do. The WtA-premise is irrelevant.

    Originally posted by Manfr View Post
    Also, if one of the frequent complaints against W5 werewolves is that they are not heroes enough, getting involved against human trafficking only if there is some hint at a Pentex front ... well, they would be quite touchy heroes, to put it mildly, when confronted by one of the biggest tragedies of these recent times.
    1. All previous W5 work includes Fomori as bad guys when it isn't the Cult of Fenris/BSD or Garou who have fallen to Hauglosk. I'm just following the guidelines set down by W5.
    2. Going after whatever human evil even if there's no confirmed presence of Bane Spirits or Fomori... That's called the Cult of Fenris agenda in W5. The Cult of Fenris gets chastised by W5 for "seeing the Wyrm in everything". W5 precisely doesn't want you to do what you're describing.
    3. Because there's a reason you put Fomori in there, why you have Pentex and it's about the war against the Wyrm. You put some distance between that and the real world. What you're proposing instead is to ignore the animism, the Wyrm and all that. Instead, you want to put a real-world-issue in front of the players, make the opposition mundane humans with no connection to the supernatural and then have werewolf-PCs use their violent powers against them. Additionally, at that point, this would just be a game about terrorism (using violent means to produce political change) involving real-world-issues. You would need an even longer "Considerate Play"-section than the one in the W5 corebook if you want the game to be about that.
    4. And again, this also amounts to just throwing the WtA-setting into the trash-bin. Because in this conception spirits, Garou, the Wyrm... none of that matters. You're just doing a game about a real-world-issue and for some reason the PCs are werewolves. You don't need any WtA-concepts for this.

                    Let's get the whole quote because we're talking about some tone-deaf, poorly thought-out writing here:
                    In the years since the sept was established, all of its members have been arrested at least once. It had lost a ship to the Libyan coast guard and an entire Garou pack to an assault by the Cult of Fenris. They believed that the Sept of the Water’s Birth had to be stopped because they were allowing potentially Wyrm-tainted people into Cult territory. The fact that the people saved by the Sept are not more Wyrm-afflicted than any other random selection of people doesn’t seem to matter to the Cult.
                    What this section is referencing is the fear of anti-immigration-activists of good refugees and bad refugess (who are criminals and so on). And so you can't just be tolerant of people moving in and should be paranoid and fearful instead. It's a tone-deaf, badly written section because right there at the end, the text says the anti-immigration-sentiment is right and yes there ARE good and bad refugees but you should be tolerant nonetheless. And for that, the writer makes the truly bad choice to use "Wyrm-afflicted" as an WtA-analogue. Why is it bad, you may wonder? Have you ever heard in the history of WtA of a Garou who argued that among this certain number of people this certain amount of "Wyrm-taint" is normal and therefore everything's okay? Of course not! If there is a Fomori, you don't hear a Garou say "Well, among this neighborhood of 2000 people, one Fomori is normal. No need to fret.".

                    So on one hand you have the Cult of Fenris go after Wyrm-afflicted humans which is something every Garou does. And on the other hand, the notion that the Cult of Fenris cares about immigration-policy and is invested in stopping illegal immigration is the same kind of silliness as just making a Sept all about human activism. This has nothing to do with them being Garou at that point.

This book really didn't help by dragging in a bunch of legacy content and executing it badly, and imo it'd be better off if it just stopped trying and just became more of its own thing apart from old editions.
