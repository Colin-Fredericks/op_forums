                    Originally posted by Manfr View Post

                    Well, every artist using a model or depicting a real-life landscape, from the Classic Age onwards, would be "tracing", then: I don't think there's a lot of artists whose work only come out of their imagination, without prior reference.
                    The issue isn't that any artist in question shouldn't be allowed to do this. But the process we're talking about here is taking photos that somebody else took, put online and then tracing them for your own art-project. With stock-photos the process is, of course, a lot easier, but if you're taking somebody else's creative work in the form of photography and it's heavily referenced, the artist should at least mention the reference or once we get past stock-photos have to ask for permission first and/or recompense the original model/photograph whose photo was used. Especially once we start talking about art in a product that's being sold. The GW-preview-debacle happened because this wasn't taken seriously. Some guy's face was traced and the art-work was put into the book. But it turns out... there should be a little more to this process because otherwise, you end up creating bad PR (to say the least).
