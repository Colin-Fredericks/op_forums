                                                    This would be my argument: In the Book of Chantries, it talks about nodes and caerns. It states that their functions are similar, but different. It also says that werewolves are able to create their caerns nearly anywhere (at least to Mages' eyes). There doesn't need to be an existing node. The ley lines come to them after the caern is created.

                                                    That seems to indicate that to a Mage that location is not a "place of power." Yet to the Garou, it is a "place of power" that allowed them to create a caern there. So the two groups seem to be using a different definition of "place of power." A place which had no node and which mages were not interested in at all (and could never turn into a node themselves), now becomes a place where they could obtain a node.

                                                    It seems to me that the reverse should also be true: mages might see a place as a "place of power" (it's a node) where the Garou don't see anything at all (no potential for a caern).

                                                    And I think Mage metaphysics supports that. Mages are interested in Quintessence. That's what they get from Nodes. That's why they are interested in caerns - they can draw Quintessence from it. But mages can get Quitessence from lots of things. As an example, Tass is quintessence in solid form, and mages can get it from supernatural creature's blood (and perhaps other body parts). They can get tass from a Garou because Garou have Gnosis. But they can also get it from vampires which are completely different Garou. To a mage, the blood of a Garou and vampire have similar properties. But vampire's blood does not mean anything to a Garou. They can't get Gnosis from it. Mages can also get Tass from a changeling's Glamour or a wraith's corpus. Again, these do nothing for Garou. So there are things that a Mage can derive quintessence from that are immaterial to Garou. Certain Nodes should be the same way.

                                                    I believe Mages also consider Haunts to be Nodes, and Haunts function similarly to caerns in the sense they are a place where the Gauntlet (the Shroud) is thin and two dimensions (the physical realm and the Shadowlands) meet. Yet I don't think that makes caerns and haunts the same thing. It makes sense that mages can harvest Quintessence from Haunts. It makes a lot less sense that Garou would be interested in such a place. It's possible that they might, but the ST would need to develop things more for that specific place to present a convincing argument.

                                                        Book of Chantries is a 1e book with a lot of aspects that are contradictory to other things in Mage (such as later books allowing mages to make Nodes without any stipulation about ley lines because not all mages include ley lines in their paradigm, making the whole idea that mages are impressed at how Garou can put a Caern anywhere retconened out)..
