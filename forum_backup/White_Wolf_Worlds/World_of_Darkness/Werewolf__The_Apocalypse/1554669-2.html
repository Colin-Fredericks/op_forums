                        OK... for the sake of us with religious educations from childhood that include being trained in Biblical Hebrew and being decently informed on critical scholarship of the Torah/Hebrew Bible/Old Testament... please avoid "the scriptures tell us that..." because they most likely don't.

                        Also, the answers are really simple:

                        1) Genesis contains two different creation stories (the cosmic creation story of the days of creation, and the hands on creation story of Eden) that were merged together kinda messily as Judaism moved from a henoistic religion, with multiple deities within a larger pantheon even if they weren't all honored, and into monotheism. The gaps are because the actions of two different divine beings are being smooshed into a single narrative. Ancient cultures are fully of the hands on creation myths where the first humans are made and then things just skip to there being lots of humans. It's not a sign of some other lineage out there, it's just a common early mythological narrative device.

                        2) The original sin narrative is a far later invention that only adds to the mess that was created in point 1. Adam and Eve also eat the fruit of the knowledge of good and the knowledge of evil before they got around to making any more humans (and they had a third son, Seth). By the original narrative this is supposed to mean all humans have this knowledge in them. Because they're the original humans however you want to square their boys finding wives. And remember that whenever the old books are talking about redemption, they're not talking about spiritual salvation from sin. The word is being used in the same sense as redeeming a coupon. The Lord is redeeming their people from being servants of other deities after they got taken to other lands. The Lord is getting their property back from the deities currently in possession of it, so they can go back to being loyal followers the Lord's land and so on. Sin exists because humans know of good and evil, the Eden story is just a myth about how that knowledge came to be. It's not an implication of some other spiritually separate group of humans; and even if those existed they'd be childishly ignorant like Adam and Eve before eating the fruit.

                        There's no deep mystery here, there's no need for complicated explanations. Just a need to remember that scripture is a collection of stories from across time with multiple authors and editors.

                            The idea of some particular people not suffering Delirium in the same degree comes from the notion that the Impergium happened at a point in time after the dispersion of humanity, and only where wolves lived, as there were no Garou away from wolves. Keeping in mind that the Bunnyip weren't originally Thylacines, they became them later.

                            With this in mind, the Impergium would have happened later than 40k years ago, if we assume South American population coming from Polynesia, or 15k years ago if we assume it coming from the north. Both possibilities still put the Impergium thousands of years before written records, and way before the creation of humanity according to literal readings of scripture (something between the end of the 4th millennium to the end of the 6th millennium depending on source, so at most 8k years ago).

                            And Werewolf consistently works with this time frame, even if not directly stated in the legends. The Impergium was at least 9k years ago, probably longer, and lasted for thousands of years.
