            Strangely, we are not given much detail on Silver Fang tribal moots. The second edition corebook simply says the tribe holds their moots in isolated, idyllic locations, and that they are very ceremonious with dancing and singing, and litanies to the spirits. The Fangs where white robes on these occasions.

            I cannot find any specific mention of tribal moots in the first edition corebook at all. I suspect a major reason is the space taken up by the various Royal Houses and lodges which other books do not need to deal with. However, it does mention that the Sun and Moon lodges organize annual Summer and Winter Solstice celebrations, and that when the lodges meet, it is to maintain the Litany, laws, and the Concord. These typically happen within a house, but it is mentioned that multi-house gatherings are multihouse moots.

            So while it is not stated explicitly, it seems clear to me that Silver Fang tribal moots are organized by the Sun and Moon lodges. That they always hold a Winter Solstice and Summer Solstice moot (where presumably they perform the seasonal rites), and that other tribal moots are called as needed in order to oversee the proper interpretation and following of the Litany. Because of the extreme depth of ceremony involved, I think these would require a lot of organization beforehand in order to make sure all the protocols are properly followed. (Much like the Glass Walker conventions, I imagine there is a reason for much of these ceremonies - the tribe needs to perform them because of chiminage promised to the spirits in earlier days).

            Strangely while the first edition tribebook is one of the few (like Red Talons) to not go into tribal moots, the second edition tribebook is one of the few of that edition which does. It states that Silver Fangs sometimes attend the moots of other tribes in order to learn what is going on in those tribes, and also to give legitimacy to those moots which address concerns that affect other tribes. It is also stated they aren't always welcomed, but that long tradition gives them legitimacy to do so - but that it is not always wise to do so. Individual Silver Fangs likely need to judge the political situation correctly lest they dangerously offend the other tribe.

            The second edition book also says the Silver Fangs hold moots where they invite all their subjects - I assume this is merely concolations that the tribe organizes, and not tribal moots. The Summer and Winter Solstice celebrations are mentioned in this section as well which reinforces the idea that these are actually the normal tribal moots of the year.

            The first edition Storyteller's Handbook section on seasonal rites gives us something completely different - the Rite of Long Remembering always held on August 2. That rite commemorates the supposed date when the primordial first Wolf entered the World Navel to save Gaia, and in doing so died and brought back to life. The rite itself is composed of a religious drama where the Silver Fang act out the various roles, and is then followed by a grandiose history of the tribe. This seems to be a rather big deal, so it is disappointing the tribebooks don't mention it. (it is mentioned that a silver fang which does not attend loses 3 temporary Honor; if the ritemaster botches it, he loses a permanent Honor renown!). While neither a solstice celebration or a judicial moot, this very much seems like a tribal moot, but a very specific one - it is held to just do this rite. As primarily a religious ceremony, I imagine responsibility is given to the Moon Lodge. Of course, when this was written ('94), there was no Moon Lodge as the first tribebook had not yet been written. And it is said that "the King of the sept" always takes the role of he First Wolf in the the dramatic play. Since this is before the idea that the Silver Fangs only have so many royal houses, "King of the sept" should be interpreted as the eldest/highest ranked Silver Fang at the sept or this specific gathering.

            On the basis of that, we could imagine a variety of other Silver Fang ceremonies throughout the year that could potentially eat up a Silver Fang PC's time. If so, I'd make these more like typical weekly church attendance - something that only eats up a few hours and could be handwaved (and not cost renown if not attended). It would be a good way to distinguish Silver Fang culture from the other tribes, but at little cost in time. Maybe a few sentences of flavor text.

                    Down to the last two tribes - Uktena and Wendigo. I am going to take a slight break before I do, but hope to get them done next week.

                    This has actually been a good exercise for me as I have short notes for all the tribes and how to distinguish their different tribal gatherings, while keeping my STing to a minimum.                                                         The corebook description of the Uktena tribal moots are that they are very strange with emphasis placed on spirit summoning and mysticism. They involve elaborate dancing, chants, and incantations. It's also said they read passage from ancient books, which is very strange as the traditional Indian peoples the Uktena sprang from were not literate. The Mesoamericans had written language, but they were so far south that the native North American wolf population range did not include them. Later some of the tribes developed their own written languages after seeing Europeans with it, but not until the 19th century. Some peoples used petroglyphs or pictographs they'd mark on bark or other medium, but those aren't books. So whatever books they have, they aren't from their own culture. However, we also know that the Uktena are supposed to be spooky, occultists who raid other supernatural factions for secrets, so we can assume these are Necronomicon style books in the WoD setting, and thus a relatively recent development of their moots. This also means that their moots aren't necessarily about their own culture, but learning about occult practices in general. I take it that the singing and dancing are forms of religious liturgy.

                                                        The Uktena tribebook actually has one of the more descriptive sections on tribal moots. We're told they have moots and councils. Councils end up being tribe moots where only the elders speak, but all Uktena can attend. More on them later.

                                                        We're told that Uktena moots resemble powwows. Since I feel very few readers would actually know what powwows involve or how they are organized, I'm simply taking this to mean "it looks very American Indian." But we're given a lot of detail. Music is important; sharing food is important; there is lots of gift giving which creates a lot of obligations and thus loss of Honor if you can't reciprocate; and there are lots of storytelling sessions. There are also lots of Garou rites being done, especially tribal rites like the sweat lodge.

                                                        It's said that any person at a moot can speak about anything to the assembled, but since no one has a duty to listen to them, they can be shouted down by the crowd, or people just begin to leave until you are talking to no one. So it is really a case of people better having something important to say, and having the social weight that others want to listen. It's a nice touch.

                                                        Towards the end of the moot is the "good part" - the secrets of the tribe. This is where they call upon Uktena Ancestor spirits from the tribal homeland to attend and share in the festivities. This is when I assume the sacred occult stuff is done including reading from forbidden books. After this, but before the Revel closes the moot, the Uktena begin their howls that commemorate the dead and praise any new Uktena heroes. The last howl made is always the Death Song of the Croatan.

                                                        Uktena tribal moots are closed to all except the Wendigo. And while the Wendigo are welcome, the Uktena still include from their most secret practices. I'll interpret that they are told to leave before the end when the secrets of the tribe and shared and the howls begin.

                                                        There appears to be both Councils and Great Councils. The difference is that while only the elders speak in both, all Uktena can attend Councils while ONLY the elders attend a Great Council. Based on what we're told later, my interpretation is that the Great Councils are always hold at same time, but that Councils are held when something of unexpected importance happens to the tribe and thereby are like emergency meetings when waiting for the Great Councils would be inappropriate.

                                                        The Great Councils, or the Great Moots, are heldfour times a year at the equinoxes and solstices. Once again, we get continued confirmation that during these times of the year, Garou generally meet as part of their tribes and not as part of the septs. Each Uktena protectorate (not sept, although some individual septs no doubt are their own proctorates) sends a delegate - that's the elder - and debate the most important tribal issues. Interestingly, these Councils aren't really held to come to an agreement. They are mainly there to share information and allow all sides to speak on an issue so everyone knows where they stand. The Uktena really believe each individual sept needs to decide for itself how to respond. Though no doubt those septs which do agree on issues coordinate with each other voluntarily. This marks the Uktena very different from a lot of the other tribes who have more centralized leadership.

                                                        We're not told where the Great Councils meet, but I suspect it is rotating with Great Councils being held at various Uktena caerns in order to spread it around and not favor any particular Uktena sept. These locations are probably established in advance so the host septs can prepare in advance. This fits in with the decentralized structure of the tribe.

                                                        If we know that Great Councils are held four times a year, than when do the normal tribal moots are held. We're not given much detail. We're told that Councils often take place during or just before a moot, and that they often happen at same time. So likely Great Councils are held during normal tribal moots, and emergency Councils likely piggy back on them too. So tribal moots likely happen fairly frequently as there must be times when tribal moots are held when Councils are not. This risks getting into territory that our consensus wants to avoid - having monthly tribal meetings. So if you don't want to go that route, my idea is that the Uktena have scheduled tribal moots during the solstices and equinoxes. The entire tribe holds local tribal moots at those times. However, local Uktena in different regions may decide there is a need for local tribal moots at other times. These hold tribal moots, but the rest of the tribe does not. Then if an emergency council is held, it may piggy back on one of these local tribal moots, or just meet independently.

                                                        So as an ST, I'd schedule for four, but allow for more if circumstances warrant.

                                                        The first edition Storyteller's Handbook isn't much help. The Uktena seasonal rite is the Rite of the Wyrm Eaten which is a harvest festival held on America's or Canada's Thanksgiving Day where they eat a ritual meal which also serves as an omen of how well the Pure Lands are doing. The foot eaten at the meal becomes "corrupt" as much as the Pure Land is Wyrm Tainted at that time - so slaughtered pigs have maggots in them, and vegetables go bad. While this is now held in mockery of the Thanksgiving holiday, I'd prefer the rite to simply be a normal harvest festival rite that was held locally centuries past, but which modern Uktena now hold on Thanksgiving to both be less conspicuous among humans, and intentionally as a rebuke. I don't see this as being part of a tribal moot, so likely it is just a local tradition among the Uktena. In areas where Uktena control a sept, it is done there. If not, local Uktena meet up just to share a meal and share the sacred rite.

                                                        Overall, there is a lot of detail here, but still some work to put it together for practical use by an ST.
