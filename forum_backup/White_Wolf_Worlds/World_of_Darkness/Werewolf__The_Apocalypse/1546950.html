    Good alternate names for the Garou to use for the Revenant Families

    12-13-2024, 10:59 PM
    I like to use non-canonical terms when referring to beings in the World of Darkness (at least outside that splat) in order to introduce some mystery back into the chronicle. If I use the actual terminology, players usually know the powers and abilities because of their OOC knowledge. If I use some other term, it becomes unknown and the players can only speculate. The unknown creates uncertainty and therefore fear.

    So in my Werewolf chronicles, I use the term "vampire" to mean any number of beings who feed on the life force of the living. This includes the Leeches of course, but it also refers to a variety of fomori as well as some wraiths/spectres or other spirits. I call ghouls "thralls", but that term is also used by anyone enslaved by vampires which includes people just blood bond to them (but are not ghouls) or enslaved by Dominate or Presence. I use the term "revenant" to refer to the Risen because that is the more accurate term in folklore (and I was happy to see this is what they were actually called in the W20 corebook!). Likewise I never use wraith or spectre, just ghosts. If I talk about fairies and changelings, I never use the CtD kith terms, but actual fairy names from folklore.

    Of course that means I need an alternate term for the actual Revenant Families in Vampire: the Masquerade. That's fine because "revenant" really doesn't make sense for them. There is nothing obvious to replace it though. So I decided to crowd source that here.

    Now we know the Revenants are creations of vampires and that they use Disciplines, but I don't think that's necessarily clear to the Garou. They could easily be interpreted as some kind of Fomori Family or other monster. They should know the connection of some Revenant Families to the Tzimisce from the long war between the Shadow Lords and Garou, but again there's nothing preventing vampires from enslaving Fomori Families. And in canon, the contemporary Revenant families have limited interaction with the Sabbat and none with the Camarilla. At the same time, the Bratovitch are probably known to the Garou since that family does still hunt the occasional Lupine.

    In my own chronicles, I use the original idea in Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand that the Revenant families were created by Verbena blood magic experimenting with Kindred vitae, and I don't use the later idea of V20/V5 that Revenants can be created "naturally" simply by ghouls reproducing with each other. So no new Revenant families in my chronicles.

    Vampire sourcebooks never describe how they detect as the Wyrm, but I default to the assumption that they work identical as vampires: it is based on their Humanity, so Revenants with Humanity under 7 or on Paths detect as the Wyrm.

    So I think the Garou would know is that:
    1) They are exceptionally long lived, but are mortal and do eventually die.
    2) They have various supernatural powers that mimic some well known vampire and fomori powers.
    3) They pass along their Wyrm taint down the family line.
    4) Several families were once enslaved by the Tzimisce vampires who battled the Shadow Lords, and some families still have some connections to vampires but much less than in the past.
    5) Unlike vampires, they do NOT need to feed on the blood of humans. Nor do they need to drink the blood of vampires.
    6) Their Wyrm taint seems to manifest in bizarre, inhuman and cruel psychology rather than physical deformities.
    7) These families seem to have originated in Central/Eastern Europe in the early to mid Iron Age.

    This being the case, what would the Garou think about these creatures and what are some suggestions of what to call them?

    Right now I am tying to the Greek legend of Tantalus who stole the ambrosia and nectar of the Olympian gods (which made them immortal), revealed the secrets of the gods to mortals, and who murdered his own son and tried to feed him to the gods in order to test their omniscience. I think this covers a lot of the same ground as the Revenant families. Their curse, which is passed down the familial line, comes from an ancestor who grossly violated the natural order after stealing some sort of supernatural power or engaging in forbidden black magic. Greece is not far from the geographic origins of the Revenant families, so it is quite possible the ancient Greeks could have preserved some elements of their existence in their myths. So I am leaning to calling these families the Houses of Tantalus, and individual "fomori" or fomori type as Tantalids.

    But I am interested in alternate takes or ideas as long as it comes from a Garou perspective based on the limited information they would know, as opposed to a mastery of out of character game sourcebooks. What are your ideas?

      I can think of several different approaches.

      1) You can build on the fact that Garou can't tell different kinds of vampiric servants apart. They can just use the term "Thrall Families" or "Thrall Dynasties". It underscores the Nation's ignorance though it's pretty likely the players will figure out what the term stands for.

      2) You can make Garou not just uninformed, but outright confused. They can mistake these families for Dhampyr, and make some faulty folkloric assumptions (like Dhampyr being dirty, boneless, or natural slayers of vampires). Werewolves can also bundle Revenants together with Thin-Blooded or see them as some sort of living blood-aligned sorcerers.

      3) You can reach into Greek Mythology for an example of a thoroughly cursed family. My Google-fu suggests the House of Atreus. Their members included Tantalus, Pelops' sons, Menelaus and Agamemnon, Iphigenia, Orestes and Electra (and if you're feeling cheeky, House Atreides from Dune). Generations upon generations of degeneracy, blasphemy and misery. Garou can call them Atreians.

      4) Those Addams Family f***ers over there.

So I think it is reasonable for outsiders - like the Garou - could interpret this as meaning these families somehow rebelled against their vampire masters and some succeeded in liberating themselves, although some have tangential ties to their former overlords.

              Also, as originally written the Revenant families had various and estranged connections to the Sabbat. The Bratovitch were not active with the sect at all, and most Sabbat vampires avoided any contact with them. The Bratovitch, though their occasional Lupine hunting, were also the ones most likely to have contact with the Garou. The Obertus did not have an active role in the sect either, though they did provide research services and information to some Tzimisce. The Zantosa remain rich and provide hospitality to travelling Sabbat nomads and covens, but are no longer seen as useful. This is probably on purpose as they want to hide their protection of the Tzimisce antediluvian. Only the Grimaldi continue to provide real service to the Sabbat, but as the ones hiding among humanity and working to preserve the Masquerade, they are probably the least likely Revenants to become known to the Garou. Likewise any Revenants tied to the Tal'mahe'ra probably hide their connections to vampires really well. So it would be easy for Garou to not notice their current connection to vampires or determine they only have vestigal connections with them.

              The reason I am doing this is I want to build a "faction" for the Revenants as an "independent" (from the Garou perspective) Wyrm-oriented group. Developing different factions gives me tools I can use to diversify the enemies of the Garou, develop the setting, and make things more immersive. So I want them to have their own name, description, and lore about them that the Garou "know" and tell each other. Preferably in such a way that it is not immediately obvious to the players that I am talking about the Revenants. Retain the original lore about them, but develop them in a way that makes better sense for a Werewolf chronicle than in a Vampire one.

                  Bratovitch

                  Although we call this family Bratovitch, I assume the last names of these families use something else. Otherwise their enemies could easily identify them. They are probably the family most encountered by the Garou so their views would be heavily based on them. They are known to be rather depraved, vile, inhumane, and cruel, and they resort to torture and mutilation. Stories about them would be similar to the Texas Chainsaw Massacre, the Hills Have Eyes, and We Are What We Are and other exploitation muder films.

                  In Werewolf game terms, they’d be seen as a fomori family with powers like Animal Control, Mega-Attribute (Strength) or Unnatural Strength, and something like Bestial Mutation to account for Vicissitude or equivalent Gifts. They sometimes frenzy so maybe one level of Berserker.

                  They live by themselves in isolated communities on dilapidated estates that have 10-20 fomori (all in the family) on them. They are known for raising large wolf-hounds that assist them in their hunts. These are not typically ghouls though it is possible that they might secure payment in vitae for allowing Sabbat nomads to temporarily stay, and that might be used on hunts to temporarily create “fomori” animals.

                  They make their living in some combination of farming, ranching, and hunting. There is always some family patriarch that rules the household which again feeds into the fomori family idea.

                  They know how to hunt Lupines so they know to arm themselves with silver, have large wolfhounds who should be resistant to most forms of Garou control, and have some amount of Lupine lore. Like most fomori, they would not normally provide much challenge to the Garou. Despite their powers, they are just not in the same category. However, they “cheat” because they extensively use silver in their hunts and make good use of their Animal Control powers. They are adept at picking up Lupine spor and tracking them. And when they hunt, they probably use large numbers and do so with members of other families who arrive at the estate and hunt together. Individual packs should be outnumbered.

                  In terms of Garou going after them, their estates are likely well fortified with lots of traps, alarms, animal sentries, and other things to warn them in advance. Their family estates also likely attract lots of Banes who would provide good defense in the Penumbra. They would be unknowing of this, but their abhorrent behavior attracts a lot.

                  Their estates should be hard targets for the Garou who need to judge if attacking it is worth the risk. It is probably beyond most rank one septs, maybe even rank two septs.

                  I think the Bratovitches would make a good group that hunts stupid Garou who leave too much evidence of themselves. They don't restrict their hunts to local areas. When they pick up word of a likely Garou pack that violated the Veil, they do some scouting and research. Once they find the pack, they make plans and coordinate a large scale hunt.

                  Since in my experience PCs often act stupidly and violate the Veil with some level of impunity (many players like to do stupid things if they are "fun"), they'd be a good way to scare them into acting smart. It's probably unfair to sic a group of thirty or so Bratovtich hunters in off road pick up trucks, armed with silver bullets, long range rifles, and lots of large wolf hounds against a small pack of PC Garou without the PCs having any kind of fair warning. But an ST might set up a small pack of Garou (perhaps Anruth that visited the sept early in the chronicle) who would act stupid and do typical PC things, and they just have them disappear. All that is found is evidence of lots of off road vehicles, tracks of huge dogs and human hunters, and evidence of a firefight that turned into a massacre. Perhaps a traumatized pack totem that can eventually be summoned who did not directly witness the pack being wiped out, but was in constant communication with them the entire time they were being hunted down, with some of them being horrible tortured before being dispatched. Make it obvious that this NPC pack was found and killed because they did not take the Veil seriously, and many PCs will begin to act more smartly.

                  You could do the same with any number of groups (DNA, PENTEX First Teams, Technocracy), but I think the flavor text would be very different for each one. The Bratovitch would have much more of a party atmosphere with lots of empty alcohol bottles, more cruelty, and a more primitive vibe than the professional, high tech approach of these others.

                      In terms of names, I am now considering if it might be better to go down a Celtic inspired route simply because the Fianna are responsible for many of the terminology the Garou use, and they favor things from their Celtic heritage (like calling monstrously possessed humans and animals "fomori"). Nothing speaks clearly to me, but perhaps something like Bodach-kin or the Children of Bres. Though I'll still likely keep many details of the story behind Tantalus, just use different names and references.

                      = = = = = =
                      Zantosa

                      The Zantosa are the other obvious Revenant family to use in a Werewolf chronicle. They are extremely wealthy, influential, and behind much of the Latin American drug trade. They probably are behind, or directly control, one of the great drug cartels. This would mean they also have access to paramilitary forces and probably rule their estates without interference from many governments in Mexico and the Andean countries. I'll need to come up with some fictional cartel name because like the Bratovitch, I don't think the Zantosa Revenant Family actually uses that name among mortals.

                      Like the Bratovitch, the Zantosa have only vestigial ties to the Sabbat. They don't provide any services to the Sabbat except for their occasional hospitality to travelling Sabbat, and Sabbat vampires seem mostly peeved and tired of the Zantosa's decadent games.

                      Their hedonism and depravity also implies significant involvement in human trafficking and prostitution. Their preferred path if also the Path of Cathari. This puts them clearly in conflict with tribes like the Black Furies and Children of Gaia (among others) that are known to oppose these kinds of human crimes.

                      This also neatly ties them into the Defiler Wyrm, and that got me thinking that the Bratovitch could also be seen as being connected to the Beast War. By this, I don't mean the Revenants have any knowledge of Wyrm lore or direct ties to the Triatic Wyrm. It's just that this is how the Garou think. They have their own mythology and belief systems. So that got me thinking if there are obvious ties of specific Revenant families being connected to specific Triatic Wyrms, then the Garou might actually believe this is a thing. Certain lineages of these "fomori families" serve one of the Triatic Wyrms. I think that is a good hook for Werewolf.

                      = = = =
                      For different reasons, I imagine both the Grimaldi and Obertus avoid detection of the Garou. The Grimaldi are the most human of the Revenants and lack the obvious cruelty and inhumanity that may attract the attention of the Garou. They might come across individuals covering up the existence of vampires as the Sabbat's appointed Masquerade keepers, but the Garou would likely assume they are just more examples of individual servants of vampires, not an entire Revenant family. Likewise the Obertus do little more than research in a very few secluded, isolated communities. They should be mostly overlooked by the Garou.

                      So we don't have an "Eater of Souls" Revenant family. However, while there are no canonical families that can easily fill that role, the sourcebooks always empower STs to create new, unheard of Revenant families. In fact, we could easily decide on creating an entirely new Revenant family that isn't mentioned in the Vampire sourcebooks simply because they actually succeeded in breaking entirely free from the Tzimisce. Vampires have forgotten entirely about them. Such a family could exist, perpetuating their bloodline, retaining their vampiric Disciplines, and pursuing their own goals unfettered by any lingering ties or complications to their former vampire masters.

                      Instead, we can focus them solely on providing an interesting opponent for Garou in terms of the Werewolf setting and chronicles. I'll have to do some thinking of what would be good. However, I am thinking there may be something worthwhile in revisiting the Danislav family originally mentioned in the Transylvania By Night sourcebook. Simply because they were once Shadow Lord kinfolk, they would have an incredible amount of knowledge about the Garou, and they have an incentive to fight them (as the Garou would utterly want to wipe out their treason). I think it would make an interesting hook for a "fomori family" to have once been kinfolk who got involved in forbidden knowledge that lead them to betray the Garou. Of course, like the other named families, it would be best if the ST refrained from using any canonical names simply to keep things a mystery and see if (or how long it takes) players figure out who these fomori families are.

                                                      Thanks. Glad you liked it.

                                                      I kept "Revenant" because I couldn't find nothing better, although I first thought about changing their names to "wights", too. I decided upon "strigoi" because, in Romanian folklore, the word refers to an undead creature that rises from the grave, not necessarily a vampire; I've seen it described a troubled spirit or ghost, too. But, to be fair, "strigoi" it can also be applied to vampires (as it is common with legends and folklore, it varies a lot from region to region). In my canon, the name was originally given by the Shadow Lords, because vampire's blood is one of the main ingredients of their elixirs.

                                                      I use a few of the names and have created others myself.

                                                      In my last campaign - the one I run a very, very long time ago - the Karasmanoglu (an Anatolian Revenant family from the 17th century) were one of the main antagonists.
