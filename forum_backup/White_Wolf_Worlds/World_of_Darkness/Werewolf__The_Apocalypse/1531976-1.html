    Honestly as someone whose been fighting Nazis both on the street and online for a few decades now I just can't with W5s deeply centrist take on the entire thing ( Putting aside the loud pro-W5 voices who are incredibly quick to call Legacy fans Nazi sympathisers and get zero pushback for doing so in most WoD spaces online.).

    Fascists recruit by appealing to a perceived loss of power and suggesting simplistic solutions by generating scapegoats and othering to provide targets for venting frustrations. Today the vast majority of fascist recruitment lies in cults of celebrity linked to misogyny and other forms of bigotry it most certainly does not involve magical Clockwork Orange displays and psychological damage caused by violation of arbitrary rules of reality imposed from outside of the perception of fictional individuals.

    The idea that the CoF is in the wrong is deeply hurt by the depictions so far of every single non-Cult Garou doing less than nothing which gives the impression that taking any sort of direct action is being proscribed as equivalent to fanaticism.

    The CoF depictions need to decide what the Cult is....are they fanatics? Fanticism does not automatically lead to Fundamentalism and/or Fascism. And the same is true in all other directions. I think the writing for them suffers greatly because it has to work back from the directive that ' The Cult of Fenris' are Facist without doing anything to earn that organically so the entire thing feels kludged together with little meat on its bones.

    But what do i know.....

        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
        Changing my mind to address a few things:

        1. Fascism is certainly the bugaboo of W5 and certainly a problem in the real world. However, the fact is that it's not really a great fit for the Garou as a whole. Especially since it's fairly easy to draw a comparison between religious fundamentalism (which can go with fascism as we see in America and Franco's Spain) and the Garou Nation. The problem is that they cleaned up the Litany of some of its more ridiculous tenats like "Garou shall not mate with Garou" so there's less overtly objectionable and less ridiculous tenats to rebel against.

        2. This point reminds me of INGLORIOUS BASTERDS where one of the people I most respect in the world hated the fact that I refused to engage with the film's message that the protagonists were becoming every bit as awful as the Nazis they fought by becoming monsters to fight fascists. My response is, "Yeah, but they're killing Nazis so....I'm okay with it." Hauglosk seems like a very easy place to explore the, "He who gazes in the abyss too long finds it gazing right back."

        3. Yes, which is why this is a fan document asking the ST to decide whether it's curable or not. That is something that is left to the table to do so. It doesn't say it's NOT incurable, though. It doesn't give a system for it, though. Which is the same as V5 and Humanity yet absolutely regaining Humanity is a thing in the setting.

        4. Humans also suffer depression but Harano is a system that has been there since W1. It's just an extrapolation for reflecting a very Garou specific issue, IMHO. Yes, it might be supernatural PTSD but it's still a metaphor for RL PTSD. Just the same as Hauglosk is supernatural radicalization of ideology but still just a metaphor for RL radicalization.

        5. I don't think Hauglosk "misses the point" of WTA but simply adds a new dimension to the struggle of saying, "So, you think that saving Gaia at all costs is worth it? Well, what does that REALLY look like?"

        Which is to say that I'm not disagreeing with any of your criticisms, I'm just saying discussing them makes for a more enriching WTA experience.
        I think point 1 and 2 are a matter of taste and differing interpretations of the text of the game. So I just disagree but I don't think any points will shift the conversation.

        3. Yes and no. The book gives conflicting account. Pg 141 "Hauglosk is also a long-term ongoing state, and many Garou never come out of it." and on the same page: "If the tracker ever becomes filled, the werewolf has succumbed to hauglosk, becoming an unredeemable fanatic and a Storyteller character." Bold to highlight conflicting ideas.

        4. I think the difference here is Harano has always been poorly defined in the rules and didn't have to be directly dealt with by the players if they didn't want to. In addition, Harano was written in Legacy as structural failures leading to supernatural despair VS Hauglosk in W5, which is written as supernatural fanaticism/fascism creates structural problems. Which is why I don't think the comparison is quite one to one. And kind of the core of why it rubs me the wrong way.

        5. I think this has existed since the beginning and I think Hauglosk detracts. The reason is because it increases by either refreshing a resource or violating a chronicle tenant. One example in the book of chronicle tenants shows how this mechanic fails to support your thesis of adding a new dimension to play. Under the Apocalypse Radicalism example of chronicle tenants (pg 200) we see the following tenants:
        Do not cling to the old ways
        Never kill an outsider
        Vengeance must be served
        So RAW, if you gain information and another perspective where vengeance isn't the best option you become more fanatical? That seems like poor planning on the game's part. Or finding out some of the old or traditional ways are a good way of doing something and modernize other things makes you more willing to throw them out in the future? I'd argue it removes nuance by turning these deeper questions into an enforced mechanic.​

                Originally posted by Knightingale View Post

                I gotta be honest. Sometimes this aspect weirds me out about W5. We know these fascist movements thrive on conspiracy-theories... elders of zion, great reset, QAnon... somehow they always convince themselves it's them against these dark forces that secretly control the world. And then you have the Cult of Fenris here who are fanatical warriors and who are linked to fascism/Nazis - but they're fighting to prevent the actual end of the world. Not these imagined conspiracy-theories as in the real world but instead, you have a fascistic movement fighting to prevent the end of the world (which is an actual thing in the setting).

                Personally, what I think would be more fitting for the Cult of Fenris as fascistic movement would be something like if Crinos-born Garou were still a thing... they convincing themselves that Gaia had withdrawn her favor from the Garou because she's gainst Crinos-born Garou and so if the Fenrir were to kill every single Crinos-born on the planet, everything would be fine. You know, just this wild assumption/conspiracy-theory. I think that's more for the depiction of the Cult, I guess, but that's the vibe Hauglosk needs to invoke. The W5 corebook should've mentioned the Cult's "grand plan" to prevent the Apocalypse and it should've been an intentionally stupid one, just this list of wild ideas that supposedly "will fix everything". It wouldn't detract from the portrayal but make clear what's being invoked and why joining the Cult of Fenris isn't a solution.

                And then there's still the issue that I think this is a tough sell to players that their PCs are on the track to behaving like that as well.


                I don't disagree with most of your post and think the Cult of Fenris loses some obvious baddie points by the fact the Crinosborn no longer exist. However, one of the things that I brought up with the "Hauglosk is self-defeating" point is the fact that the Cult of Fenris' primary goal right now is fighting other Garou. Fans complaining about the Get of Fenris/Cult of Fenris being all determined to go save Gaia miss that they're going after their fellow warriors to save Gaia. It's the equivalent of saying that the person who needs to be taken down in order to get Congress to finally pass the Green New Deal is AOC or Biden.

                The Cult of Fenris aren't going after Pentex with a renewed fury, they're trying to take everyone else's caerns and magic items. Because ideological purity is more important than actually accomplishing their stated goal.

                I mean, we HAVE an example of a tribe who didn't think the Garou Nation was going to do anything good and left peacefully. It was the Stargazers.

                                            I tend to think of the Cult of Fenris being at fault partly through unwavering fanaticism, but also because a majority of its members cultivate a “VICTORY MEANS A GLORIOUS DEATH!” (As in, ‘If you’re planning to come back from your next battle, you’re doing something wrong not “Win at all costs!”) attitude.

                                            They’re not fighting to Save the World, they’re fighting for their own chance to die a Glorious Death and **** all optimists who dare claim there’s any possibility for an honourable life without aiming for a Glorious Death sooner, rather than later.

                                            (One presumes that while this is the most common public attitude, those members who form the solid core of the Cult are more interested in making sure their colleague’s Glorious Death is not wasted on some penny-ante operation).


                                            This is, to be clear, founded on my understanding that Hauglosk is fundamentally the Rage of Despair, not merely ungovernable fury.
