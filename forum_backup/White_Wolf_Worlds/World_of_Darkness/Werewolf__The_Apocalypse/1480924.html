    Bone gnawers

    05-13-2022, 12:27 AM
    I have spent almost a year reading the tribe books and the kith books and now I am coming to an end, having only one more to read. The last one I have read is the bone gnawer revised tribe book, and I really like this tribe! Probably, one of the the top 3 tribes which I like and one the bests tribe books written, in my opinion.

    The only thing that bored me to exhaustion was the USA patriotism of the text, there are other countries out there, emerged and built under the idea of freedom and opportunities. And, besides, the outcast have existed since the beginning of times, I see no reason why they are more numerous in USA.

    Anyway, I liked the book, it is plenty of cool character ideas, it is remarkable different in both, cultures and aesthetics, and it begins with an interesting short story. The totems and spirits are also useful, although perhaps the level 1 gifts are uninspired, being reduced to dirty combate style (explosives and stones) and ways of make some easy money. The premade characters are all playable and I have not found any racial issues or political troubles in the text, which seems to be a constant in werewolf.

    So, what do you like about the Bone gnawers and what would you change? Would you change the motto to "anything for a chessburger"? Do you like allying them with the ratkin? It is USA the land of the homeless?

      Originally posted by Justycar View Post
      The only thing that bored me to exhaustion was the USA patriotism of the text, there are other countries out there, emerged and built under the idea of freedom and opportunities. And, besides, the outcast have existed since the beginning of times, I see no reason why they are more numerous in USA.
      While the WoD found a worldwide fanbase, White Wolf was an American company primarily catering to an American fanbase, and its sourcebooks and other gaming material reflects this.

      Although I am an American, I have always been a world history buff and often found its depiction of history to be too centered on America. It's certainly OK to depict "this is how the tribe is in the USA" as long as you acknowledge the tribe is present in other areas and has according differences. As such, we can understand the Bone Gnawers in the favelas of Brazil, slums of India, and so one would be different in ways that make sense. I'd encourage people who live and ST in different areas to adjust as necessary so the tribe makes sense there. (Or indeed any other tribe).

      Originally posted by Justycar View Post
      So, what do you like about the Bone gnawers and what would you change? Would you change the motto to "anything for a chessburger"? Do you like allying them with the ratkin? It is USA the land of the homeless?
      I can honestly state that while I do like the tribe in many ways, I think fundamentally the tribe is depicted wrongly in official materials. The key to the tribe is that they are the "omega tribe". They are the "runts of the litter" of the Garou Nation. That does not necessarily mean "homeless bums". There's no reason it shouldn't include those. But it should not be definitive. So while many canonical depictions are appropriate, the developers and writers often got it wrong to my taste. What the "omega tribe" means is going to be different in many different human cultures.

      And I think the Bone Gnawers are often depicted wrong in the USA because the writers fundamentally did not understand the reality of homelessness in America. There are two types of homeless. The first are people who are down on the luck and need a temporary helping hand. Those kind of people - people who are capable of holding down a job and living on their own - are typically only homeless for about 6 months at most. Then they find a job, save up some money, and are able to move into an apartment again. Various charities and homeless shelters help them in the meantime. Of course, that time of difficulty can be adjusted on various economic factors. The second type of homeless are people who are either mentally ill or long term drug addicts. Their problems are fundamentally different than the first kind, and require very different solutions. Unfortunately, many advocates of the homeless pretend these second kind either do not exist or promote solutions completely inappropriate to them. There is an extreme problem of "pathological altruism" where the "solutions" do not actually address the real root causes. And the material on the Bone Gnawers effectively pretends this second group does not exist. This is very different than the underclass situation in other countries where true lack of economic opportunity exists for many people.

      So in my chronicles, I try to keep to the essentials of how the tribe should be (the omega tribe, the runts of the Garou Nation, socially disadvantageous, and a combination of outraged social justice combined with severe problems that undermine both the tribe and Garou Nation) and change a lot of the specifics to reflect my view of reality.

      My advice to everyone is to do the same. Figure out the essentials and adapt as necessary for your chronicle. We'll all have different interpretations. That's OK.

                  Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
                  Those people who are just temporary homeless are not represented much in the tribe. The assumption is that you are stuck on the streets or mooching off your friends.
                  I think the tribe isn't always presented coherently or consistently. It's all over the place. That's not necessarily a problem. Lots of inspiration can be good. I just don't think it's tied well together. From the corebook you get the impression that it is all hobos and street bums. But the tribebooks introduce a lot of camps which really expand the tribe. And while they don't match the corebook description, do fit the idea that the tribe is among the low income.

                  The Frankweilers don't seem like severely mentally ill people unable to exercise control over their own lives. The camp description talks about educating people, helping them improve lives, and even lawyers are members of it. They can still be relatively low socio-economic class, but we are very far from severely mentally ill people and drug street people.

                  Likewise, it's hard to imagine the Rat Finks being so dysfunctional. They're supposed to be running one of the best intelligence networks in the World of Darkness. And when talking about occupations that they (or more accurately, their kinfolk) are having include janitors, trash collectors, waiters, clerks, receptionists, etc. These are all fairly low status jobs and often don't earn much money. But these are not people who can't find places to live, or obviously unable to care for themselves.

                  The Hood are supposed to be criminals that steal from the rich and give to the poor. They don't seem to organized crimelords. They're more like street gangs engaged in local protection rackets and targeted theft who then provide help to the destitute. This doesn't sound like homeless people. They have enough cash to give it to others. They probably make pretty good money. Nothing that would make them obscenely rich, but wealthy and powerful enough that they can push out loan sharks and rival street gangs that would otherwise infest their domain.

                  The Deserters spend most of their time in the Umbra so discussion on their socioeconomic status is besides the point. But clearly umbral exploration takes a lot of skill.

                  Hillfolk live in isolated mountain homes often cut off from the outside area so they're not doing well. But they eke out a living on their own in rural poverty or low income, and aren't dependent on others.

                  Sure, you have the unnamed typical Bone Gnawer of the corebook, the Man Eaters, and the Rabble. But for the most part they aren't expanded upon. Maybe it's just my bad memory, but I don't remember much discussion that the Bone Gnawers themselves are mentally ill or drug addicts. Although those are acceptable character concepts that would fit in, and certainly some NPCs match that. The Man Eaters tribe can certainly be seen as an analogue to drug addicts living on the streets.

                  And I have to admit I have never yet seen a PC who is an actual homeless Bone Gnawer. They're poor, yes. But they're usually very competent, just doing dirty jobs and providing services to the community. And I almost never see NPCs in chronicles that show any tremendous dysfunction among the Bone Gnawers.

                  But we can get a lot of Merits, Flaws, and Tribal Disadvantages that seem to support this is what they are. Like no Resources ever. They're permanently without money of any kind. Where are the janitors, night school trained lawyers, waiters, garbagemen, and others who seem to earn a living? We have this strange dichotomy within the tribe. On one hand are really functional Bone Gnawers that don't seem well supported by the rules. Or homeless street bums that don't match many actual characters, but seem to be permanently homeless. Few characters in between that show much more social mobility.

                  Nor do we get much direction on what happens should a Bone Gnawer Garou or kinfolk becomes too successful. Do they just migrate out of the tribe and be accepted elsewhere? Who would accept these ex-BG "refugees"? Maybe Glass Walkers and Children of Gaia, but what about others? Does Rat abandon the Garou, or have some unmentioned tribal ban? If not, what's to stop some successful kinfolk or Garou expanding their career or business and acting as a real benefactor to the rest of the local tribe? Is there social pressure to prevent this - that such Garou or kinfolk are getting too big for their britches and need to be less ambitious? Or are such characters plucked up by one or more of the other tribes and the Gnawers can't do anything about it?

                  This is what I am talking about. The Bone Gnawers seem to be portrayed as perpetually poor and never able to move up. But that's not realistic, even if most of the tribe may not. And some of the camps seem to sit uneasily given other things we're told. If the Hood or Frankweilers can help some people (including kinfolk) get off the street and move up to hold a steady job, maybe even get some education and get a better job, then that could continue. It makes sense that it would happen, but the books don't see to address this possibility despite setting it up for us. What happens for those who continue to progress? One doesn't need to pretend that is what happens for everyone to address it. Instead we seem to have this weird situation of two levels of BG society (the classic homeless street bum of the corebook, and the elevated working poor of many camps) that are in stasis where nobody moves from their tier to the next, and certainly never progresses from that?

                  So it doesn't seem articulated well to me. I have lots of questions that the books don't answer. So I had to answer themselves as best I can while trying to remain true to the "spirit" of the tribe. Maybe you don't have the same problems with the material that I do, or see the tension, or interpret it from a different angle. Which is fine. Everyone has their own ideas and takeaways.

                          They could have gone down the route that some kind of totem ban was involved. Raven for example explicitly doesn't want its pack children to carry wealth but trust in him to provide. Something similar could have explained a lot. Another route was to create something specific in the tribal culture, such as the expectation that any Bone Gnawer or kinfolk family with money is expected to provide for the others at the sept or protectorate. Money quickly goes away when individuals or families can't keep the money they earn themselves, but have to share it with their clan relatives or communities. Maybe that's part of providing "loyal service" to tribe or sept so at least they can get some Renown while getting screwed over financially. Sharing among everyone is an excellent survival strategy when living in some peasant village for most of humanity's history. But it prevents wealth accumulation in a modern economy. That would make some good tension within the tribe, and explain why it remains poor. There can be negative aspects to too much egalitarianism, but if Rat values the Swarm above the individual rat, it would make sense that this is an important part of tribal culture.

Does the tribe perform the Rite of the Lone Wolf on them and kick them out? Do they become Ronin, or perhaps get accepted by one of the other tribes? How does the tribe treat the "traitors" of Rat who abandoned their responsibilities to their kind by becoming "greedy"? I personally like conflict like this in Werewolf because I feel too much of the game gets wrapped into the struggle with the Wyrm. That's welcome too, but has a tendency to suck the air out of all other kinds of interesting conflicts. It's like if the only acceptable conflict in Vampire is for Camarilla to fight Sabbat and vice versa (though Gaia knows Justin Achilli has tried his best to make that so).
