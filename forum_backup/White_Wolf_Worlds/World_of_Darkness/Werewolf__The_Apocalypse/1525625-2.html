            Verbs + Adverbs also give you more "actions" to build ideas off of. Three Ahroun adverbs with three Tribal verbs gets you nine total ideas instead of six. Nouns are probably easier, but adverbs does get into the "how" vs. "what" dynamic you pointed to. if Silver Fangs "lead" how does each Auspice express leading? If Ahrouns doing things "bluntly" you can get some interesting contrasts between the more aggressive Tribes like the Black Furies and Red Talons, vs. ones like the Children of Gaia (how does your Ahroun "heal blunty?" as a concept).

                I did a breakdown of the Tribe-verbs when the preview came out over a year ago:
                Glory Honor Wisdom
                Stealth Shadow Lords Bone Gnawers Glass Walkers
                Fight/Confront Black Furies Galestalkers Ghost Council
                Community (Get of Fenris) Silver Fangs Silent Striders
                Protect Fianna Red Talons Children Of Gaia
                Now the logic here is that there are two traits: The Renown and a specific way to express the Renown. The way I got the four categories on the left is:
                • Stealth are Tribes that have verbs about doing things secretly or using subterfuge (SL: scheme, BG: Eavesdrop, GW: Hack)
                • Fight/Confront are Tribes that look to engage enemies (BF: Overcome/Demolish, Gale: Ambush, GhoC: Subvert)
                • Community are Tribes that do something that benefits other Garou (SF: Inspire, SiSt: Deliver (ie being messengers))
                • Protect are Tribes that have verbs for defending (Fi: Protect, RT: Ward, CoG: Heal)


                Now as for Renowns, I think there are also similarities:
                • Glory Tribes have aspirational verbs about achieving some future goal (SL: Envision, BF: Avenge, Fi: Reclaim)
                • Honor Tribes are about contributing a service to Garou society (BG: Witness, GS: Track (ie tracking down enemies), SF: Motivate, RT: Purge)
                • Wisdom Tribes deal with the occult secrets of the world (including the Umbra) (GW: Research, GhoC: Unearth, SiSt: Scout, CoG: Puzzle Out)
                __________________________________________

                This could be a starting-point. A 4x3-grid with one verb expressing the Tribe's focus on a particular Renown-type and another verb expressing a particular category as outlined by the matrix. That way the Tribe-verbs would already be reduced from four to two. Of course, when I wrote that last year it was more meant as an analysis of the verbs listed in the preview than an actual model for how to deal with the Tribes. It's a beginning but there's still a lot to think about...

                As for the Auspices, I agree with HeavyArms that limiting this to just verbs doesn't make much sense. Honestly never understood the insistence on this having to be verbs. So I have two ideas:
                • Specific phrases describing activities associated with each Auspice. There's even precedence for that: The Coterie-Types in the V5 Players Guide were described with short phrases as well.
                • The other idea I have is that you go to Renown-related stuff but link it to blatantly stealing something from Forsaken: Five types of Renown. So it would go: Ragabash - Cunning, Theurge - Wisdom, Philodox - Honor, Galliard - Glory, Ahroun - Purity. And not to confuse things with the three Renowns in the game, here are some alternative names: Trickster, Sage, Mediator, Vanguard, Ideal. Not sure about the last two. Maybe someone has better ideas for those names. But the narrative reasoning is that acting like that is a Garou perfectly impersonating their Auspice and that's how you justify the Brutal-Outcome-bit.

                    Okay, I was traveling and playing our V5 campaign this weekend, so I'm behind on integrating all your feedback.

                    Here's the subchapter "Hauglosk Redefined". What do you think?

                    "In the rulebook hauglosk is defined as a “state of extreme righteousness” (p. 52), when a werewolf loses “all compassion and mercy to the fanaticism of their cause” (p. 140). It is interpreted as a long-term, if not permanent, state of violent extremism that all Garou can fall into. In these house rules, the state of hauglosk is redefined.

                    Instead of a spiritual state of fanaticism, hauglosk is understood as another way of resignation, of giving up on the cause of fighting for Gaia. Harano and hauglosk now represent two dysfunctional strategies of dealing with the hopelessness Garou feel when confronted with the Apocalypse. Whereas harano is all about internalizing their rage and despair, hauglosk is all about externalizing them by acting out.

                    In essence, harano is a spiritual state of depression and learned helplessness, when utter despair makes Garou give up on themselves and Gaia, stopping to fight altogether, withdrawing into themselves, and accepting their doom. In contrast, hauglosk is a spiritual state of nihilism and moral disengagement, when complete disillusionment and overwhelming rage make Garou abandon their fight for a greater cause or higher goal. After all, if Gaia is dead and the Apocalypse is inevitable, what’s the point of honoring the spirits, protecting and nurturing caerns, or upholding Garou traditions? Instead those in hauglosk unleash all their anger and hate on those who have hurt them, on those who they deem inferior, or those who just can easily be hurt. Some Garou in hauglosk recklessly follow their own selfish desires for power, dominance, and survival, without consideration for any collateral damage they might cause. Some just continue to fight and destroy, constantly roaming around and looking for glorious fights, not because they think it makes any difference but because it’s all they’ve ever known. Garou in hauglosk are convinced that they are doomed no matter what they do, so they lash out any way they can. For some Garou, falling into hauglosk ends up as the first step towards joining the Black Spiral Dancers."


                    EDIT: Given the redefinition, I now need a more fitting term for Fervor. I'm thinking Frustration, Outrage, or Resentment. Any good ideas?

                        I've also mulling over the Chronicle Tenets. I agree with Knightingale that Tribe bans are a bit of questionable design. They don't have any precedence in the setting and they're not particularly interesting, but require unnecessary book keeping.                         In effect, I'm thinking about removing Tribe bans, having two regular "Garou should never..." tenets and one pack patron ban.

Tribe Favors will be replaced by the additional successes on Brutal outcomes for Auspice and Tribe verbs. However, how Tribe Favors work in RAW will serve as a template for benefits from pack patrons.

                            Originally posted by Caldorian View Post
                            In effect, I'm thinking about removing Tribe bans, having two regular "Garou should never..." tenets and one pack patron ban.
                            Sure. Another benefit of this system is that it's easy to modulate. This moves the Tenets away from an indication of a general tone, mood and atmosphere and more towards a reflection on the actual setting. Like here, it's more about setting up Tenets about Garou. With V5, for example, having a Tenet like "Thou shalt not kill." in a struggle between Humanity and the Beast it might seem quite obvious what sort of conflict is set up. But with a more arcane concept like Garou the process is more guided as to help bring out the actual themes of the game. So it can't just be this freewheelin' thing anymore.

                            But this model also has room to be expanded for fans of old lore. There's no limit to the number of Tenets and what sort of questions are linked to them. They need to work for the people you're playing with - but they also need to work for the game you're playing. Chronicle Tenets is a concept that can be guided by by a game's thematic boundaries and themes but ultimately it's molded by the people at the table.

                            EDIT: Also, what do you think are appropriate benefits from a pack patron? Should I just import/adapt the old "totems," including making Patron a background?
                            Well, to be honest there are two ideas that I have in that regard: First, translate Coterie-Types to W5 as Pack-Types with a focus on Pack-Patron-Spirits. Second, translate expanded Coterie-creation-rules/Domain-management-rules to the Caern-management-system. Third, make it all more Blades In The Dark Crew-sheets-like. Justin Achilli mentioned that this was his inspiration for abandoning Pack-Types and introducing the caern-management-system instead, anyway. Who knows what happened behind the scenes but the actual system in the W5 corebook for caerns sucks.

                            But that's... quite a bit of work.

                            EDIT 2: Okay, thinking more about it, I'll remove Tribe Bans and Favors. Tribe Bans will become a ban from the pack patron (in the form of a Chronicle Tenet). Tribe Favors will be replaced by the additional successes on Brutal outcomes for Auspice and Tribe verbs. However, how Tribe Favors work in RAW will serve as a template for benefits from pack patrons.
                            You don't need to do that, I think. Tribe-Favors is another inconsistent writing-project in the book but the solution is simple, I think. There's already a system in the game that gives you a situational bonus-die: Skill-Specializations. So that's what you turn it into instead: Every PC can pick from three Skills associated with a Tribe and get one free Skill-Specializiation. If it's a skill with no dots, you get a free dot and the Specialization.

                            I mean, if you look at the wording of these Tribe-Favors, that's what they were essentially aiming for anyway. Personally, I'm a fan of a bonus-die only tied to a situation without being linked to a Skill as a Tribe-Favor but trying to figure that out for all 11 Tribes is a lot of work. Also, the irony is that giving a free Skill-Specialization offers more freedom for interpretation as well. If you want to deemphasize lore/worldbuilding the free Skill-Specialization makes way more sense.

                                                            Originally posted by Caldorian View Post

                                                            The only other thing that I think needs to be addressed is the caern-management-system. It only becomes an issue in a longer campaign, I imagine, but I don't think the system quite makes sense.

                                                            So the caerns have a rating of 1 to 5 and are defined like this on pg 192: "The Caern Value Trait represents the power of that connection. A caern with Caern Value 1 may be a small, local, or specialized caern, whereas a caern with Caern Value 5 is one of the most powerful, spiritually resonant places on Earth.​" That definition seems strange, though, because the way the Rite of Caern Building works is you do the Rite, pay the XP cost (which is "New Caern Level x 5") and raise the Caern Value and then you can pick one (!) new caern trait. But in that system a Value 5 caern (which according to the description would make it one of the most powerful caerns in the world) would at maximum have 5 caern traits and if you look at the list of traits most of them end the description with "This Trait can be taken multiple times.". That seems contradictory. Why would you make caern-traits this limited in choice but then present so many traits that are supposed to be taken multiple times?

                                                            There are multiple ways to resolve this but it needs to be considered in some way. Because I can't imagine any group getting to the point of investing narratively in going for a caern, then spend the XP for the two important Rites and the XP for raising the caern value to 1 - and then immediately being confronted by these limits.
