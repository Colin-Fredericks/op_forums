        The Silent Strider Tribebook (Revised) has some things to say about old Garou, losing the wolf, and the Rite of the Winter Wolf.

        Silent Striders don't kill their elders for being old. They don't kill them for losing the wolf. They don't kill them for being sick. They consider killing them if they're incapable of passing down any knowledge (suffering advanced dementia) or suffering constant physical pain that cannot be treated. Otherwise, they treasure their elders because once they die, their spirits are gone forever. No Ancestors background. No summonable ancestor spirits. No path to reach the Tribal Homeland. The Striders don't even know if they have immortal souls like other Garou.

        Second, Silent Striders has an example of a very old Strider elder who lost the wolf and lived like a Kinfolk for many years. At the age of like 104 he got in a fight, got his Rage back, and died in combat in Crinos form. Which demonstrates:

        1. Garou are Garou from birth to death and losing their Rage doesn't change a thing.
        2. Killing your elders because they're "useless" is exceptionally dumb.

                                Originally posted by Erinys View Post
                                I still wonder how horny, teenage cubs who haven't changed yet avoid inbreeding. I'd expect this to be a recurring problem, with frequent mules being born... but it doesn't come up in the books I have. I don't recall any Garou railing against teenage promiscuity.
                                There are lots of things one must simply determine on their own for the setting because it's not directly relevant to the actions of most RPG games, so they never get mentioned. It is also going to be something different STs and gaming groups will determine on their own based on their own group's ideas and needs. I think it's best the game does not down into that detail so everyone can come up with something they feel works best, and they don't have to worry about that they are violating canon.

                                Nevertheless, of course I have my own ideas. Garou culture, on the whole, is very traditional. They are not creatures of modernity and do not hold modern values. Yet their kinfolk in many parts of the world live in modern societies and their culture is being undermined by modern values.

                                I imagine most tribes promote cultural values for their kinfolk that would seem antiquated, quaint, backward, or even oppressive to much of the game's fanbase (and therefore, it is frequently ignored). They don't like promiscuity; want strong, stable marriages for life; and encourage kinfolk to get married as young as possible and raise many children. There are exceptions - the Children of Gaia, Bone Gnawers, and Glass Walkers all have different reasons for adopting more modern views. The CoG are often depicted as free-love hippies, the Glass Walkers deal in an urban environment where their kinfolk are dominated by modern culture, and the Bone Gnawers simply don't care. I imagine the Black Furies are divided - they reject much of modernity, but they dislike traditional cultures more patriarchal mores and want more egalitarian conditions if not outright female leadership (though perhaps in informal or secret conspiracies that can't be seen by outsiders).

                                As for the possibility for pre-Change Garou accidentally mating with each other, I imagine this is something monitored for by Garou who "oversee" these closed kinfolk communities. To a certain degree, this is policed by the Rite of Baptism by Fire, the Gift Scent of True form in previous editions, and close inspection by the Garou over their kinfolk charges looking for obvious signs that someone may be approaching their first change. Since most Garou are supposed to change sometime during adolescence, this likely stops being an issue when kinfolk reach the age of consent/maturity anyway.

                                I think how serious an effort is made depends a lot on the specifics of that kinfolk community. Isolated communities near caerns are probably very strict about such things. Kinfolk who are integrated with normal humans and under more of a modern cultural influence, this becomes progressively harder.

                                                        The game's setting described in the corebook assumes most Garou change around adolescence. They are either raised in knowing kinfolk families to have Garou values, or are runaways from their lost kinfolk/human parents. In either case, normal humans would ostracize them to various degrees because of the Curse. Their "job" for the most part is simply being Garou. After several years of tutelage as cubs, Garou begin their advance as rank in their late teens.

                                                        I will have to admit I have never seen a group of PCs fit this description (there have been individual exceptions within a group that mainly does not fit). Furthermore, most NPCs in games I've played in also do not fit this description. Even cubs - PCs or NPCs - are late teens at their youngest.

                                                        Instead I see many late changing Garou who already have careers - often successful ones, and they continue in those careers as Garou. I think there are several reasons for that.

                                                        First, the time scale of many chronicles don't really allow aging so any PCs are essentially "trapped" in that age forever. While I know many players who don't mind starting in their teens, are running preludes of that age, they perceive their character in at least young adult terms - mid twenties to early thirties. They don't want to play characters who couldn't even legally get into a bar - at least not for the entire game. And most chronicles take place "now" and don't allow for the passage of time. Second, players in general love to frontload accomplishments and achievements into their character background. And a character needs time to do that. You have to at least be in your early to mid-twenties to be combat veterans, professionals like lawyers, successful businessmen, etc. That can't be done if your PC starts at age 16. Third, people really see "career/job" as a defining characteristic - so their character concepts often start with that. They want certain skills, life events, and training that is unlikely to have happened to adolescents. Fourth, they want to interact with the human world and don't want to do so as scary, unemployed vagabonds who'd have the cops called on them constantly.

                                                        I don't see this in fantasy RPG settings, but this is how it goes in any setting that takes place in the modern real world or sci-fi equivalent. And most of the other WoD game lines support this because being turned into a vampire, awakening into a mage, or dying can happen at later dates. Changeling has a similar problem as Werewolf - every PC is at least a late Wilder or a Grump. I've never seen someone play a Childing, or really use those as NPCs.

                                                        So I think there is often a tension between the game's setting as written in the corebook, and how people actually run games. And Werewolf character templates usually reflect that. They don't reflect the setting as described in the corebook. They describe it by how people usually play it - which ignores a lot of supposedly key features of the setting.

                                                        When I ST games, I don't even try to police this with the players although I do encourage them to start as young as possible. Instead, I work on making the NPCs reflect the setting accurately, and usually run games in the recent past (anywhere from starting several decades ago to a century ago) so we can have the passage of time during the game and try to have the game take place over several years if not several decades.
