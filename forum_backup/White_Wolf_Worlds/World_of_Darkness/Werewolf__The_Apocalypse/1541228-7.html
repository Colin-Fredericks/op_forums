                Sure, you can do that. The whole "history is written by the victors" kind of thing. But I think you've already put way more thought into this than the Shattered Nation book has.

                The first Legendary Rite example is "Ross the She-Wolf Fights Union Bromide" and it's Ross fighting this Pentex-front. That's it. No nuance whatsoever. It's an event that happened, the Legendary Rite is just a point-by-point reproduction of that adventure and then later you get a Loresheet for Ross the She-Wolf as a good character PCs should associate with. And then when there's a sidebar, it's heavily implied that it's all coming from Dominique. And there's no Dominique/Little Sister sidebar for the "Tale of the Storm Eater" either, for example.

                I mean, you're right: Legendary Rites would be a good vehicle to make sense of how Garou look at their own history in W5 and how it could reflect on how so much historical knowledge was lost by the Garou over the centuries. Sadly, this book doesn't have any insight to share on that topic.

                When it comes to the real world, though... The lack of truthfulness shouldn't be overstated, however. The perspectives might be biased but history won't be rewritten in some grand fashion. That's conspiracy-theory-stuff like that "New Chronology"-conspiracy from Russia:

                The new chronology is a pseudohistorical theory proposed by Anatoly Fomenko who argues that events of antiquity generally attributed to the ancient civilizations of Rome, Greece and Egypt actually occurred during the Middle Ages, more than a thousand years later.

                The theory further proposes that world history prior to AD 1600 has been widely falsified to suit the interests of a number of different conspirators including the Vatican, the Holy Roman Empire, and the Russian House of Romanov, all working to obscure the "true" history of the world centered around a global empire called the "Russian Horde".
                That's how weird this would sound when you take actual Garou history and present a version that's just somebody making shit up. In a society with a shared identity and a belief that this identity is tied to a certain history, you can't just make up huge claims about that history contrary to common-held beliefs and knowledge.

                                I feel one of the biggest signs how disjointed Shattered Nation and W5 as a whole is, is the way the outsider tribes are treated.

                                BSDs are redeemable, all but one of the example plots are about that. Which would be fine if the text didn't also paint the tribe as much softer than in previous versions. Yet they still look like the typical BSDs. It also undercuts the whole 'you but evil' theme the tribe should have.

                                It isn't helped by the way the CoF are treated in comparison. Even though the CoF are on the same side as the PCs, the example plots and the text still paint them as the worst of the worst. And also unredeemable, even though a big part of fighting fascism is to deradicalize its followers. I know the CoF are the favourite scapegoat of W5, but how can you tell a story of why extreme methods are bad when the CoF aren't allowed to be anything more than cartoon villains? Given how BSDs are written, it also erases the very point of CoF as the 'uses worse methods than the actual bad guys' group because the CoF are ALWAYS bad and BSDs are MOSTLY bad.

                                The Stargazers then are treated as an afterthought, the text even stating they WOULD help the PCs if the PCs would only listen. The book deciding for the players how they would react is never a good thing. It isn't helped by the way W5 is supposed to be post-Nation, which would mean each tribe is doing their own thing anyway. Yet the book presents the post-Nation garou functioning mostly like the Nation garou, without offering an example how the Nation garou were different from the post-Nation garou.

                                And, yet again, tribeless garou are not even mentioned. As if one cannot exist as a tribeless garou in the setting, even though they should be very common in the setting thanks to the shattered nation.

                                My gallery.
