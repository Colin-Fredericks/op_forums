    W5 Complaint: The Handling of Pentex

    01-12-2024, 02:18 PM
    I'm fairly okay with the W5 update and my big issues are ones that a lot of people are somewhat more ambivalent about (I am a lore junkie and hate whenever the games attempt to divorce themselves from continuity rather embracing it with a vampire's fangs).

    For me, one issue I'd like to discuss I am not happy with is the handling of Pentex.

    I get what W5 was going for by attempting to address the real life ecological devastation and brutally of the world's various corporations with the strong encouragement to ("Just use Walmart and McDonalds where you can") but I feel like it's a missed opportunity not to have even a sidebar listing all the wonderful evil brands from WTA's long history.

    * Endron
    * Magadon
    * Black Dog Games
    * RED Network​
    * O'Tolley's
    * King Beer

    I feel like you're losing out on the brands that most of the audience is smart enough to get the parody of. It also enriches the setting by having these discount brands.

    Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

          Yeah, I remember that bit... Justin Achilli didn't like the Subsidiaries so they were all removed from W5. I think we'll see the new dynamic for how confronting "Pentex" works in the upcoming Scent Of Decay book.

          The two quotes from the W5 corebook that I think summarize it best are these (from pg 268):
          • "Pentex is also, for the vast majority of its operations, absolutely, unremarkably mundane in its affairs. Ninety-eight percent of what it does doesn’t involve supernatural creatures or malevolent spiritual entities, just toxic runoff and banal worker exploitation."
          • "Pentex Group does very little directly and by itself — it prefers simply to own things and collect the revenues those things generate. As such, it’s unlikely for a Garou pack to encounter Pentex itself.​"
          So most of the time Pentex is doing mundane stuff and it won't actually be Pentex itself but another mundane company doing mundane stuff supported by Pentex. That's how Pentex is portrayed in W5.

          The thing I never understood with this change in direction for Pentex is how you calibrate that portrayal in light of an extreme faction like the Cult of Fenris. If it isn't the parody-Wyrm-cult-y Endron or Magadon and instead work with completely mundane companies... I mean, is the idea here that you're actually supposed to play a scenario where Garou go into a completely mundane oil-refinery and start killing people...? And you find out that the oil-refinery isn't by chance so close to a caern because behind the scenes Pentex arranged this? But this still leaves the scenario with an abundance of real-world mundane stuff and very little WtA-specific animism and war against the Wyrm. And ignoring the innocent perspective of the company used by Pentex while destroying them is something I would associate more with the extremism of a Cult of Fenris. But if this version of Pentex is always beyond the reach of the PCs, they necessarily would end up just confronting mundane companies who unknowingly do the bidding of Pentex which is beholden to the Wyrm.

          But as I said, there's a scenario in the upcoming Scent Of Decay. This is the summary: "The Break Room: A corporate fulfillment center seeks productivity at a terrible human cost". Given what abilities Garou have, I wonder how this scenario is supposed to play out according to these writers from Renegade. Are you supposed to play judge, jury, executioner for bad corporate culture? Are you supposed to tear the place to shreds? Do you need to have a heart-to-heart discussion with a Bane Spirit and convince it to go to therapy? I guess we'll see...

              I don't think PENTEX has ever been used well in Werewolf sourcebooks because ultimately the writers could not decide what exactly it was supposed to be. Is it a source of supernatural evil in the world? If so, what are the supernatural horrors being unleased by these individual companies? Sometimes we would get this, but most often not. Is it just roman-a-clefs of real world companies and the ecological, human, or economic harm they do? If so, why involve the supernatural at all? A lot of subsidiaries are just like that. Is it just supposed to be a dark parody or social commentary of various companies? If so, what place does it really have in a game of "savage horror"? To me, these are all separate things and best handled separately.

              So the end result was too often just a poorly written version of evil comic book companies done in a Captain Planet style who did evil things just to do evil things. It didn't help that very often the writers obviously did not understand actual corporate America and often would write things wrong. For example, it's fairly clear that the original writers in 1e Book of the Wyrm understood that the "directors" meant members of the Board of Directors. They were there to look over the interests of the shareowners. They were not management of the company. Whatever agents of the Wyrm managing things in the individual companies had to be someone else. But in the 2e Book of the Wyrm, the writers didn't seem to understand that and seemed to think the directors were part of management and started assigning them all sorts of managerial responsibilities. I understand it's an easy mistake to make, and very young people would have no idea about these things and not have it negatively impact their game. But it did represent a major flaw in a gameline that otherwise was known for its quality.

              So for me, PENTEX and its various subsidiaries were never well presented. The only way I could use it was to ignore what was written, steal what names and concepts I could get from it, and create for more usable and interesting horrors from the raw material.

                          Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
                          As Knightingale said, what is the purpose of Pentex-plots now? Is it just trying to solve issues in the board room or as a consultant? Because the game has painted the typical actions against the company as CoF territory.
                          I mean, I don't think that's what it does at all. You have a bunch of werewolf hunting teams and evil monster possessed executives in the description. It also describes taking down a subsidiary as an institutional change.

                          At its core, Pentex is set up as a narrative antagonist to
                          force an institutional-level change and also to put an in-
                          dividual face on the threat and flay it from the executive’s
                          head. It’s almost always done through proxies, as well —
                          the Garou probably don’t go to a “Pentex headquarters”
                          and slaughter their way through the building. Rather,
                          the fronts act as minions and proxies, through a series of
                          blinds, layers of plausible deniability, and any number of
                          contractors, so Pentex operations themselves are never on
                          anything resembling a front line.

                          Destroying a Pentex Group operation is effectively
                          regime change. Sure, the front is a threat and needs to
                          go, but what happens in the aftermath? For the Garou to
                          call it even a small victory, how do they ensure that what
                          happens after eliminating the threat is any better than
                          the status quo of the operation’s continued functioning?
                          If the pack is just an agent of destruction rather than an
                          agent of change, they’ve effectively justified the human
                          world’s perception of them as blood-spattered monstros-
                          ities instead of anything resembling Gaia’s champions.
                          Simply acting destructively is what got the Garou where
                          they are today — and has created opportunities to fill
                          power vacuums and exploit resources Pg. 271

                          This isn't quite the radical change that I think some fans are perceiving it as. It's more that you can't beat the Umbrella Corporation by going into its factories and blowing them up. You're instead more likely need to do some Monkeywrencher antics and even then you're going to probably only take down one branch.

                                          I always enjoyed the Subsidiaries quite a bit. First, I liked the idea that most people had no idea what Pentex was. It's sort of like in the real world, few people on the street know YUM! Brands, but everyone knows the companies that part of YUM! Brands. Pentex was able to operate a bit in the shadows because it was always hiding behind its subsidiaries. Importantly, they also put a layer between the character's and the players, like gh0st-fox says. It's one thing to go on a shooting spree through the O'Tolley's. It's a completely different feel when your characters are shooting up a McDonalds. In light of real world problems and issues, the latter feels more in bad taste.

                                          I understand why Achilli wanted to remove them - the nature of parody and satire innately brings a level of humor into the game that he doesn't feel belongs in W5. Among the old WoD games, Werewolf was absolutely the one with the most humor involved, nearly all of it revolving around Pentex parodies and satire of real life companies. And to some degree, even if you try to play those sorts of parodies straight, they will always inject a tonal change into a game.

                                          But I don't think that was ever a bad thing though - the humor in W5 was a core part of the game's identity and did a lot to set Werewolf apart, not just from other WoD games, but from other roleplaying games in general. In contrast, W5 feels a lot more generic and lacks a strong identity.
