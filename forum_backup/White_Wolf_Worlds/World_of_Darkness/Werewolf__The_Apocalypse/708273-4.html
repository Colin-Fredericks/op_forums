        I'd reckon the best way to think about it, is that the Ananasi aren't a singular monolithic entity. Lots of Ananasi exist over the years, and some of them do the Weaver's bidding, and some of them do the Wyld's and some of them do the Wyrm's. Some of them serve no one, others serve all of them over time.

        Unfortunately when wordcount is so limited writing that every time gets prohibitive, so generalities get used, which will never be as specific as you'd (the general you, not you you) would like.

        Also, the time spans involved definitely muddy events. Not to mention trying to pinpoint a date such as 'Queen Ananasa is taken hostage by the Wyrm'. That way lies madness! (Though I have my own thoughts on the matter, those are purely fan ideas).

                        Question on "Time Streams":

                        First of all, I like the idea -- playing on Quantum Theory and all is cool. The thing that comes to mind is, will there be any explanation of how this differs from, say, Mirror Zones? Up to now, it's been very difficult to muck with time even with the biggest, baddest magic -- but not impossible. The hang-up has always been that if you goof with time too radically from what it's supposed to be, the path of least resistance is that you essentially get trapped in a Mirror Zone that represents the changes that you "made" to the timeline -- ignorant that you're not in the "real world" anymore, and ignorant that the existing timeline is chugging merrily on without you.

                        Or, more to the point: Will it be that Time Streams and Mirror Zones are, in fact, the same thing?

                                    They're basically alternate timelines that may or may not actually be equally real... they probably aren't, as I understand the book, but they might be, or PCs can start thinking they're real. Each one contains its own entire Umbra and planet Earth.

                                    I think another possibility mentioned was a world in which the Garou lost the Wars of Rage. Too lazy to go check my book. No timelines got actual descriptions, they're entirely for the ST to create.

                                    They also go along with Temporal Zones, which are like simulations of past eras, and maybe of possible futures. Visitors might think they're time-travelling, and may even emerge "back in the present" in a Mirror Zone in which they "changed history."

                                    *cues creepy music*

                                    Of course, an ST can make the Mirrors and time travel as real and valid as they want.

                                    The adventure Past Lives also has a funky pseudo-time-travel story, although the point is for the PCs to experience the past and learn lessons from great tragedies, not to have an opportunity to change them. Aaaand I think that was the first book to give us written Gifts for Apis, Grondr, Camaztoz, and White Howlers.
