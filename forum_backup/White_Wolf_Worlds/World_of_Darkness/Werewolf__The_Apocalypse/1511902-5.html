    RANK 2 GET OF FENRIS GIFTS

    Get of Fenris Gifts improve at this level - they start 'coming online' as a better combat Tribe at Rank 2.

    Fangs of the North: This is a preparation power, and it combines well with Razor Claws given enough time to prepare. This won't be all fights, but many of them. You inflict a -1 penalty onto those you have injured with your attacks (a penalty to everything, notably, including attempts to dodge, block, run away etc.), which is a small debuff but applies to everything. With less chance of dodging, landing attacks or harming you a dangerous enemy is a tiny bit more manageable. The power also blocks all regeneration and healing, which is great, but keep in mind that your claws deal Aggravated damage; very few enemies will be regenerating or healing that.

    The ability to send a foe away with a festering frigid wound which won't heal is neat, but generally speaking you'll be aiming to finish the job within a given scene.

    Given that neither the -1 die penalty and the no-healing rule will have a HUGE impact, and a Gnosis cost is relatively tricky to work around, this is a solid amber which is well worth taking if it's on your list. I couldn't imagine paying a premium for it though. If you will frequently be facing enemies who regenerate or heal Aggravated damage (which some STs might have a habit of doing) then this would be Green.

    Halt the Coward's Flight: No cost, a combination of two worthwhile stats (Charisma and Intimidation), and the target difficulty is WP. Given that you could try again on the next turn, even a difficulty of 9 or 10 is manageable. Being able to half the speed of a fleeing foe is pretty neat; half is not zero, so this foe could still pull some clever escape, but it will severely hamper retreats.

    It's worth clarifying with your ST whether "Exit (NPC)" is more of a stage direction, or more of a challenge; if their intent is simply "yes, they get away" then intervening with the intent of fully stopping it is only going to lead to frustration. I wouldn't consider this ideal Storytelling, especially if the ST isn't transparent; if a character is simply 'meant to get away' (something you want to do very sparingly) then the right thing is to be up front about it. Anyways, assuming the fleeing NPC is more of a workable challenge with stakes, being able to half their speed will greatly assist you in hunting them down for capture or killing.

    This power is useful on hunts, in large scale battles (when applied to a leader it will either split the force or create other difficulties as they all adjust pace and try to help their hampered ally), against Garou packs and Fomori packs, though notably not in races.

    Snarl of the Predator: No cost, a very good difficulty band (4-8), a sensible combo of Charisma + Intimidation, and the duration is 3 turns - it may take "a full turn" to execute, but it's a great power. Hitting a target with -2, -3, even -4 and beyond for 3 turns is great.

    It's worth noting that you can use this outside of combat, in any kind of confrontation really, so long as snarling at them like a wolf is acceptable and won't risk the Veil. Ideally you'll get this off before combat starts, in an attempt to scare them into not fighting. Given that Garou can take anywhere between 1 and 4, or even 5+ actions in a single round, a -3 dice penalty is huge as it applies many times in a single round, and this lasts three rounds!

    Besides remembering you can use this outside of combat, there aren't many ingenious uses. a Charisma or Intimidation spec which will proc for this Gift could help, and ideally you'll have a Merit or Gift which helps reduce the difficulty... at your table. Different STs will vary on what applies. Worth grabbing even for 10XP!

    Troll Skin: Very frustrating, and pretty clear evidence of copy+pasting without giving much thought to balance or standardizing.

    It's very similar to Luna's Armour, but it comes with a +1 difficulty, a small social downside, the flavour is much 'uglier' (though it suits a martial character well) and it doesn't work on silver.

    Rolling Primal-Urge is good in the sense that you're encouraged to upgrade a very useful ability.

    There isn't too much to say here; use it the way you use Luna's, but honestly I even wonder whether getting Luna's is a better idea; it's only a difference of 4XP, and if you're going to be fighting a lot of battles a difference of 1 difficulty and silver soak could be crucial.

    If you're going to adjust Troll Skin, I'd suggest reducing the difficulty to activate it by 1, and making it provide some other benefit to make it unique; for instance halving Bashing damage after soak, or reducing the difficulty to soak Bashing, or fire, or electricity, or something!

    EDIT: As per the final version of the PDF, Troll Skin is a difficulty 5 check; this is a boring upgrade, but it's good. As a result Troll Skin would be Green.

    ================================================== =====

    Not too much to say about these, besides Snarl being a much better way of handling "enemies are spooked!" than Gifts like Howl of the Banshee, and even True Terror - I like True Terror as it only prevents attacks, not paralyzes foes or forcing them to flee, but it's still not as well designed as powers that focus on debuffs.

        RANK 2 GLASS WALKER GIFTS​

        Glass Walker Gifts continue to be weird, in a good way.

        Cybersenses: Heat vision. Xray vision. Echolocation. Radar hearing. Picking up ultrasonic noises. Seeing UV light, such as those reflected by urine and plants. Scenting chemicals. Run a PCR with your taste. RAW this power lets you pack the effect of any diagnostic, investigative or perceptive tool into one of your five senses. Make your sense of touch become an accurate Seismometer. Make your sense of temperature upon touch entirely accurate. Measure altitude. Sense magnetism and become a compass.

        You should chat through the exact limitations of this power with your ST, for instance you might not be able to do senses which are higher order processes. For instance determining the age of something with a machine, or sensing the presence of a particular kind of viral DNA, are both multi-step processes with various reagents and so on; does this mean the sense is very slow, or that you can't do it? Ask! Similar story with trying to get a CT or MRI or Ultrasound using your senses, as those rely on emitting something, and also can involve a lot of processing of the information that returns in a computer to get a useful image.

        A solid Green, so long as you have any creativity and real-world scientific know-how. This also pairs VERY well with high Perception, high Science, high Technology, high Medicine and so on. The more cybernetic senses you know about, the more this can do!

        Just make sure you spend your Gnosis wisely here; if you go too wild it could become a sink for a precious resource, and of course nothing lasts longer than one scene...

        Hands Full of Thunder: Unlimited ammo; on a gun which can fire as fast as you can load it, this is a potent Gift indeed. Depending on your equipment this could be a QOL upgrade or a serious buff to damage potential. Make sure to have, or even make, a gun which is ideal for this Gift. I'm gonna call it amber, but with the right weapon this could be a green.

        It's unclear whether the bullets being fired are 'real' or ephemeral. If they're real then you're turning 1 point of Gnosis into a potentially unlimited amount of metal, which is interesting. My best guess is that the bullets fired with this Gift are like 'ghost bullets'. Grabbing an unloaded gun and using this Gift is also quite fun.

        Combining with a special gun, such as a Fetish or even a Wonder, would make this all the better. It's unclear whether the special properties of the bullets in the weapon would translate over; my best guess is no. So unfortunately you can't reuse a single Talen bullet 1000 times, as cool as that would be. The big problem with guns is Lethal damage, but against mortals and many other enemies the difference between Lethal and Aggravated is entirely moot regardless.

        Jam Technology: See the Homid entry. Good for Metis and Lupus members of the Tribe.

        Power Surge: Another hard to control Gift! You spend a Gnosis, roll Wits + Science, and it seems that luck determines how big an area you black out. The possibility you could overshoot is a fun idea, but it also means that this Gift arguably becomes less useful with a really good dice pool.

        Personally I would adjust this so that your difficulty is based on the area you're trying to shut down, and 1 success means you undershoot a bit, 2 successes means you overshoot a bit, and 3 successes you're bang on target. Imprecision is cool, but RAW the Gift is just bound to cause widespread chaos and can't form a reliable part of any plan.

        "Ah I might overshoot and shut off power to something we do need, too risky."

        There's also the fact that the Gift doesn't really qualify what is 'blacked out'. Just the electricity GRID, or all electrical currents? A power cut is handy, it'll interfere with a lot of security measures, but it would be good to know whether this Gift messes with laptops, earpieces, phones, robots, pacemakers and so on. I'll assume it only impacts the grid, but still.

        Steel Fur: I desperately wish this one were better. Immediately it has a leg up on Troll's Skin and Luna's Armour; it costs WP, not Gnosis, so you can use it and then do a bunch of Rage attacks (or dodges) on the same turn. I am also delighted by rolling Stamina + Science, I love that. What's unfortunate is that it shoots itself in the foot with a +1 difficulty to Dexterity, and being restricted to certain forms isn't great either (though it does make sense, and is hardly an issue on its own).

        If you want to fix this Gift, make it difficulty 6 and change the downside; instead its weakness is that any attack which bypasses your fur (targeted attacks of the inside of your mouth, your eyes, a spike being shot up your snout etc.) ignore the bonus armour. Imagine if Luna's Armour made you have +2 difficulty to visual Perception checks because the light was blinding you... it's spirit magic, why should the metal fur make you less flexible?

        As much as I like this one, it has enough problems stuck to it that it gets red. +1 difficulty to all your attacks and dodges is a cardinal sin for a Gift.

        ================================================

        Despite some bad design, I continue to really enjoy GW gifts. Cybersenses is a great standout, and I'd argue that it's worth grabbing on any Knowledge-focused PC, especially if they have a strong understanding of the various Sciences and the associated Knowledges.

            Troll Skin is already -1 difficulty compared to Luna's Armor. Which is a decent boost where every success counts. The social penalty is also pretty minor for Garou about to get in a fight. Something I've had success with to give it some extra oomph and keep it distinct from Luna's Armor (aka the soaking silver part): increase natural regeneration by 1HL/turn, or heal 1 extra HL whenever receiving healing. This means whatever damage you don't soak, damage you (or your Theurge) can clear from you that much faster; as well as offsetting the potential issues for high Rage combat characters trying to get aid from Mother's Touch.

            A small nitpick but I'm really not sure how Cybersense would let you turn your sense of taste into a PCR. PCR machines aren't sensory: they rapidly and artificially replicate an existing DNA sample, you need to run them through a separate apparatus to actually make use of that. While I agree with the overall take on the Gift, I think it's pretty clear anything that's not a sensor in some fashion is off the table. Giving yourself UV sensing is fine, but you still need some luminol/etc. to spot things that don't naturally give off UV radiation to go full CSI on something.

            I also think you're overlooking how powerful Hands Full of Thunder can be with a simple thing: silver bullets. One of the big problems for Garou carrying silver weapons, is that it negatively impacts their Gnosis. Having a lot of silver ammo is really rough on that front. Having a single clip of it last a whole fight is utterly brutal to foes weak to silver (like BSDs) with the small impact on your Gnosis as a klaive would have. The Gift also explicitly doesn't work with an gun with no pre-existing ammo. It doesn't create "ghost bullets." It clearly has to copy existing ones. So recovering all that silver bullet machine gun fire you can toss around could also be a fun payday for a kin cleaning up the mess.

                Troll Skin is difficulty 7 in my book, and Luna's Armour is difficulty 6 - are you looking at W20? Or maybe it's a PDF thing?

                As for the PCR example, it's worth keeping in mind that radar involves transmitting electromagnetic waves and then measuring what bounces back; so one of the given examples in the book is already more complicated than a purely sensory effect! PCR is a complicated process and it does involve replication, but Xrays work by exposing a sheet of material to the electromagnetic waves that make it through an object; there's a "higher order process" to most kinds of cybernetic sense! You could argue that if a machine can sense something, even if it involves a complicated process, you can make one of your senses do it with this Gift, even if the process involves stuff you could label as "not sensory". But like I say, discuss it with your ST and see what they're down with. "I can taste viruses b/c there are machines that can determine whether a sample has a viral gene in it" might work at some tables, but not others.

                As for replicating silver bullets, the Gift simply says it won't "run out of ammo, so long as it had bullets to begin with" - you could interpret that as it spewing out silver rounds until the cows come home, or you could interpret that as it producing regular statted shots for what the weapon normally does. I can see why someone would assume it would keep firing copies of the first thing fired, but given the potential for the Gift to fire 100s of copies of a single Talen bullet, that's gonna be a conversation to have at your table. Even if the silver example does work, it's worth noting that the only enemies you'll fight with a weakness to silver are Garou and Fera - common enough to be good, but not common enough to be a Green unless your campaign is heavily themed around fighting Garou or other Luna-touched werecreatures. Still, it's made me consider an angle to the Gift I hadn't, so I appreciate it!

                    I'm using my W20 pdf, yeah, which has Troll Skin at Diff 5. I distinctly remember this getting changed because we complained at the W20s devs during the Kickstarter feedback cycle for keeping the old diff of 7 despite giving you less than Luna's Armor did.

                    On PCR: the problem is that PCR isn't an active sensor like radar. All PCR does is take a sample and copy it a lot. Like my point about UV and luminol, PCR "senses" wouldn't let you sense anything, because PCR doesn't make DNA fragments "visible" through some process. It just makes more of those DNA fragments so techniques that do visualize DNA have more to work with. If you said your character could have "electrophoresis" sense where they could perfectly weigh different DNA fragment samples to mentally create comparative weights and ID matching samples... OK, I can see that. But then you're actually measuring something to sense. PCR doesn't measure anything. Letting Cybersenses work with machines that don't measure anything means they're not "senses." Running a PCR with your tongue is like firing off sound wave, but never measuring the returns to act as sonar/echolocation. You're creating data but not collecting it. And you're clearly moving beyond the spirit - if not letter - of the Gift if you let a character perform functions that aren't part of a measuring apparatus: the ability to use active pinging microwave sensor-sense shouldn't let you cook food by bombarding it with microwaves. Letting the Gift perform a PCR is like letting it cook food.

                    On Hands Full of Thunder: "Unlimited Talen bullets," is a bit overblown as something to worry about. You'd still need to activate each one, which is a Gnosis action, with all the complicates that would add to a gun-focused character. Talens aren't hard to make. A gun-focused Glass Walker can easily come in with a revolver full of Talen bullets, and probably not get a chance to use them all in one fight thanks to the whole constant activation issue, HFoT and "unlimited" Talens would only really matter for extremely long fights, or multiple fights back to back with little rest (it only takes 10 minutes to make a few Talen bullets).

                    Also, frankly, I think people underestimate lethal damage in WtA because Garou can regen it... but the regen rates for it aren't that bad, and soaking it is the same as soaking non-silver Agg. Even if your ST disallows the Gift from copying specialty ammo like silver, the damage output and range of guns with one of the biggest limits of ammunition taken off the table is a huge deal .

                            - Some of the errors in the Example Of Play section are removed
                            - Calm Heart is fixed so you roll for Frenzy at +2 difficulty... though that is ALL Rage rolls. Problem with this is that it makes Remaining Active and other important Rage rolls harder too; "+1 successes needed to Frenzy" would have been so much more elegant!

                                RANK 2 RED TALON GIFTS​

                                Red Talons get a standout here.

                                Beastmind: No cost, a pretty simple roll, and you can reduce a target to animal-like intelligence. As much as a lawyer in a trial having a "sudden mental breakdown" could risk the Veil, it's a very potent disabling tool which can be greatly lengthened against regular humans by spending WP. The only problem with this Gift is that it's stuck onto the Red Talons. I will not be elaborating on that, you already know what I mean.

                                This will have limited utility in a fight against a claw or fang user, but might be useful against sword wielders (debatable) and will definitely be useful against gun users. Most types of Mages will be left powerless by this Gift, in crossover land, and that's pretty handy. Though watch out for the animistic shaman who has a Seeking accidentally triggered by the bestial clarity of this Gift and proceeds to turn you all into fleeing deer /j

                                RAW there's no reason you can't use this on a Spirit, or any number of supernatural beings. In a complex fight filled with traps, this is great. When language and communication are key, this is great. Making the Alpha of a BSD pack (who you can pick out remember - Red Talons have a Gift that lets you identify the leader of a group) go feral and lose the ability to give orders could sway things in your favour, as could making a Pentex First Team lose all coordination and fight like apes. Basically this Gift lets you drag enemies down to the level of a brute. Brute vs Brute is a matchup that you, as a Garou, will win quite frequently... and there is nothing which forces you to fight like a brute against a victim of this Gift.

                                If you found this Gift problematic, a nerf which would suit the Tribe's vibe would be to make it so that the user also falls into the same bestial mindset if the Gift succeeds. I don't think it's necessary, but it's a neat idea that popped into my head.

                                Try using this against enemies leading rites, driving vehicles... if someone is using a tool, you can sabotage the operator. The ability to silence people, even in a suspicious manner which draws attention, is great. This Gift has so many infinite uses it's almost hard to come up with examples. The RP potential is also pretty neat; a "human" (Kinfolk, Homid Garou, allied supernatural) who wishes to know more about nature might learn a lot from the experience. Plus giving a trophy hunter an animalistic mind and hunting him like he's prey for a whole day might turn his life around.

                                Oh... and there's nothing stopping you from using this every turn until all your foes are running around like wild animals. Just saying.

                                Pulse of the Prey: See the Ragabash entry.

                                Howls in the Night: See the Galliard entry.

                                Shadows of the Impergium: If you're regularly encountering regular humans (remember that supernaturals are all Delerium immune!) with WP 9 and 10... something weird is going on. Delirium is a double edged sword, but I'm gonna say that overall it's beneficial to you in a lot of ways. Making foes panic, lose coordination, flee, attack wildly, forget you were ever there... and this Gift grants it (if weaker) to Hispo, which may be your preferred fighting form, and also eliminates the risk of humans who react in clever ways when you go Crinos. The ability to repress this also lets you avoid collateral damage, and means the Hispo form doesn't get permanently altered in a way which removes utility.

                                All in all this Gift has some handy utility, but it's more like a Quality of Life improvement than a good buff. Regular Delirium does the job fine against humans. Humans who have WP of 7+ and are not supernatural in some way (Bane, Kinfolk, psychic etc.) will be rare in most games. This might be handy if you're regularly being hunted by badass hunters and mercenaries and First Teams.

                                If you prefer Crinos form to Hispo in fights, and you aren't going to be facing high-WP mundane human enemies, this becomes red.

                                ================================================== ====

                                Strong entry from the Red Talons. Beastmind would be worth 10xp on pretty much any character... though Griffin is noteworthy for being scornful of Homids especially, and to a lesser extent Metis, so a non-Lupus learning this is unlikely.

                                    RANK 2 SHADOW LORD GIFTS​

                                    Clap of Thunder*: Spend a Gnosis, all characters within 20 feet / 6m (a small range, but not tiny) must succeed in a WP roll (diff 8 for "foes", 4 for "packmates", no difficulty listed for any other category of person, so you could assume 6 or 8 for bystanders, non-pack allies etc.). The target must succeed or be entirely stunned for one turn.

                                    So they can't flee, or defend themselves, they're just stunned. And yes, you, the Shadow Lord, have used Gnosis and thus your whole action for the round on this... but your whole pack can utterly savage whatever individual or group you've stunned.

                                    The fact you make a loud thunderclap does risk the Veil, and it does draw attention from other enemies, so there are potential balancing factors written into the power (accidentally perhaps), however the Gift is still far too strong in most standard TTRPG "encounters". I don't know why W20 is so afraid of enemies being debuffed in interesting ways, and has so many "can't act" powers.

                                    My fix for this would be that the target is only stunned for one round on a botch; otherwise they should suffer a difficulty spike to all their rolls for the round (+2 or 3 perhaps). RAW this one falls into "so good it's bad".

                                    (Despite this being too good, it's also funny to note that if a target has a WP of 7 there's only a 30% chance they'll be stunned, and as low as about a 20% chance with WP 10, meanwhile if you have a pack of 4 allies standing around you, there's still like a 5-10% chance of each of them being stunned, so you might stun a packmate and not stun the dangerous boss enemy you wanted to, which is funny)

                                    Cold Voice of Reason: This Gift is better designed, but it's insane to have two stunning Gifts in a single list, at the same level. You make a witty remark and a target can't attack you. Costs one Gnosis, you roll Man+Sub diff 8, and the target has no roll to resist, and no way of increasing the difficulty. However they can attack anyone else other than you, and they can make efforts to stop you escaping. You attacking them also breaks the spell. Buying yourself a bit of time where a character can't attack you is a neat little power, and I like it.

                                    You could use this after confronting a very powerful and angry spirit to stop it from attacking you; this fits very well into a Theurge's kit. Alternatively if you're a scout you could use this to stop yourself from being ganked by a single powerful enemy if caught. You could use this as a diplomat in negotiations to stop someone from striking you, so long as there's enough time for that person to then be restrained or stopped. The problem is that Garou work in packs; there will almost always be more than two people in a given scenario. Other Fomori. Other allies, around you, who can still be attacked. This Gift basically stops you, and you alone, from being attacked by one target. An option to protect your whole pack for a second point of Gnosis would be much appreciated in all honesty.

                                    All these ideas are nice of course, but I don't see why you'd buy this if you can get Clap of Thunder RAW!

                                    Howls in the Night: I don't know why they thought so many lists needed this Gift; costing a Vampire a night of sleep ain't that great! See the Galliard list.

                                    Luna's Armour: Amazing, as always, but I'm biased so I wish it was a CoG exclusive. See the CoG entry.

                                    Song of the Earth Mother: This one has flavour that feels a bit more Elder Brother than Shadow Lord, but I'm guessing the writers were thinking along the lines of eastern europe, being tied to the soil and the land itself... which is a bit more Tzimisce-flavoured in the WoD. Anyways, we'll overlook the Gift feeling like it's in the wrong list.

                                    Takes 10 minutes, costs 2 Gnosis, so the result had better be good! Perception + Occult, difficulty 7, okay... and the result is that you know about supernatural activity within 100 yards of your location, per success. Even with 5 successes, 500 yards isn't that much. In the wilderness. And that's what's weird about this power; it shines most in tight, dense, urban areas. Which is where its flavour makes the least sense; why should the spirits of the earth know about a guy in the penthouse of your apartment building who is practicing magic?

                                    Essentially this is a slow, expensive Sense Magic which effects a larger area, but not large enough to be useful in the umbra or wilderness. The saving grace is that you can sit down in the middle of a village, or a town square, or a city block or park, and find wyrm tainted locations, along with other locations touched by magic. The problem is that Sense Wyrm already does a key aspect of what this Gift does, though this one will get around there being places you can't access, or don't have time to check.

                                    Do I think this gift is useless? No. It's just very weird and rough around the edges. If the range granted per success were more narrative than strict measured ranges I might like it more, or perhaps if it covered a pretty big area by default and more successes just narrowed down the location further and gave a better impression. The flavour is also weird in that it suggests the place touched by magic should be close to the ground, and that isn't the gift's mechanic.

                                    =========================================

                                    Luna's is worth 10xp as always, and there are probably harder Gifts to learn given its teacher is quite common. Clap of Thunder would also be worth 10xp RAW, but should probably be adjusted. Given the teacher is a Stormcrow it will be hard to acquire out of list!

                                        RANK 2 SILENT STRIDER GIFTS​

                                        Axis Mundi: See the Lupus section. Silent Striders REALLY suffer from having too many gifts like this, for what it's worth. They already get a compass Gift at level 1, so WHAT is this doing here?! Replace it with a reasonably realistic Gift of the player's choice, perhaps tied to birds, or travel, or tracking. Pulse of the Prey would really help the Striders.

                                        Blissful Ignorance: See the Ragabash entry.

                                        Messenger's Fortitude: Allows you to run at full speed without any breaks or rests for three days, act for 10 minutes, then collapse to sleep for a full day. This is horribly weak for a level 2 Gift, and I just can't imagine a PC getting any worthwhile use out of it. It's a niche of a niche, especially in a setting with planes and moon bridges. Doing ANYTHING but running cancels the Gift. The ability to add others to the Gift for one Gnosis each is cool, but I would edit the Gift so that adding your whole Pack is only one Gnosis.

                                        Sadly the gift is just bad, even if you need to flee there is an uninteractive and unfun element to it; running and ONLY running is monotonous by its very nature, and it means you can't handle obstacles in any interesting way. Either you can run, in which case the Gift works, or you can't, in which case you wasted 6xp and a bunch of Gnosis.

                                        Owl's kids deserved better. Feels like they're under more than one curse with these Gifts.

                                        Speech of the World: See the Homid entry. Fits the Striders really well, but a Homid Ragabash Strider faces a real slap to the face.

                                        Tread Sebek's Back: Costs a Gnosis, has a roll, and lets you run across any liquid.

                                        In Erebus, RAW, you could run across the surface of the lakes of molten silver. You'd be taking 1 point of agg every turn, but regenerating it too! Though I imagine if you were supposed to be IN the silver, the Crones would forcibly dunk you in like a kid refusing to go for a swim.

                                        This gift is handy enough, and could help you if you need to cross a pool of radioactive waste, lava and so on. However Garou are pretty good at leaping, especially with Gifts like Hare's Leap and Great Leap, and even without them. They also have ways of flying. This Gift could be very useful, don't get me wrong, and is great if you need to run across a lake or sea, or avoid Rokea or so on, and lore-wise the tribe having this is helpful for the tribe, but given how rare the need to run across liquid is they could've made this a no-cost, or had the cost only apply if you want to grant it to your pack too.

                                        Still, it's handy, and the utility can't be denied. Perhaps less urgent to pick up if you have Hare's Leap, or a Totem/Fetish which permits flight or similar.

                                        ===============================================

                                        This list needs Axis Mundi removed. So that leaves two gaps; which two Gifts you add would be down to personal preference, but I'd be drawn to anything that helps them do their job whilst also being USEFUL IN AN RPG.

                                        Gifts being repeated across lists also did give me an idea; if a Gift appears in more than one of your three lists you get a small discount. If it appears in two of your lists, you get a 1xp discount, if it appears in all three, you get a 2xp discount. The Gift is even more natural for you to acquire. For instance a Metis Theurge from a Tribe who get Sense Wyrm, such as the Silent Striders and Elder Brothers would pay 1xp for Sense Wyrm if they acquired it in-game.

                                            RANK 2 SILVER FANG GIFTS​

                                            Empathy: Atrocious flavour text. Wolves are highly social creatures, and W20 seems to feed into the myth of the 'alpha male' as often as it denies it. This is likely an issue of copy+pasting without thought. In all honesty it would be just as accurate to say that men respect strength above all else, while wolves care about empathy. Both ourselves and canids care about a mixture of things. Power, tenderness, social dynamics, family, territory, food. Wolves have concepts of good and bad, in a sense, and they will choose a member of the pack over someone powerful.

                                            It's also worth noting that a key flaw of the Silver Fangs is that they believe in divine right, ruling through might, through power. This Gift could speak to a better side to the Tribe, or it could be a cynical tool for those who don't really care about others. I do like that in fact; a Gift isn't good or bad, it's simply a power to be used as the user wishes.

                                            Spend Gnosis, roll a low difficulty Int+Emp, more successes means you have a better understanding of the group's wishes and needs, what they expect. I would also like to point out that the Silver Fangs could make a habit of asking.

                                            Used against enemies, this could glean a lot of useful information without risking running into traps or defenses against mind reading and similar. Looking at a gang of bikers and knowing that their expectations of their leader include beautiful kine ready to be drained of blood provides a lot of context - though with one success this might be mistaken for wishing to be provided with food and lovers. You could use this on wolf packs, Garou packs, groups of other supernaturals, business meetings, and use it to play havoc. You can give them what their leader doesn't understand, can't provide, WON'T provide.

                                            Group 'vibe' checking is a pretty great ability overall, just remember that the Gift is about picking up expectations and general group emotions, it isn't a sort of group mind reading. Its precision is both a neat limitation and actually helps direct and focus the Gift, making it good. If not for the Gnosis cost this would be Green. It would be nice if the Gnosis cost were waived for Garou of lower ranks than the user, or for the Garou's pack.

                                            Hand Blade: If only Garou already had access to a weapon that deals Str+2 aggravated!

                                            Jokes aside, this is a very handy QOL upgrade for Garou who rely on the Melee skill; if you have this, then you can safely leave Brawl relatively weak... but you'll still need Brawl to escape from grapples! The RP potential of being able to turn both of your hands into swords, really hammering home the idea of a living weapon, is great. Hell, combine this with partial transformation and a permissive ST and you could do all kinds of cool things.

                                            A potential cinematic use is swinging your arm and transforming it mid swing, perhaps after the point where an object would intercede. If you're using weapon length rules this is also handy.

                                            It would be neat if the difficulty were lower, but 6 is perfectly fine to work with. If gifts that buff claws apply to this blade then it gets even better, but RAW gives us no clues there. I would argue that Razor Claws and Silver Claws should both work on this.

                                            Luna's Armour: See the Children of Gaia entry. Especially important given the importance of Klaives, silver weapons and wealth in Silver Fang culture.

                                            Sense Silver: See the Metis entry. Ditto Luna's armour.

                                            Unity of the Pack: This suffers from W20's Pack Tactics not being particularly good, or engaging, and the system being poorly written. Most of them are simply descriptions of things you can do using existing maneuvers.

                                            Even if you're using good, well written pack tactics and the rules for them are laid out well at your table, +1 die to everyone is... a marginal benefit. It's alright. Just not incredible. This Gift also feels like a very, very obvious retread of a level one Ahroun Gift, Pack Tactics. Whether you prefer this or Pack Tactics will depend on the size of your pack, and how much leadership you have or intend to get. All in all I feel this is too similar, and also not a great level two Gift. Despite it not being objectively terrible, it's a rare example of a red which has clear utility. The utility is just so marginal that it would only scrape by as an amber at level one.

                                            ============================================

                                            Hand Blade might be worth 10xp for any melee Garou, especially if you don't want to invest in Brawl. For 9xp you could buy Brawl from 0 to 3; if you have Melee of 4 or 5, and a Brawl of 0, buying this Gift out of list would be worth it. Given that all tribes have Klaive Master Ancestors (though some will have them be much rarer) you shouldn't have much issue, just a higher price. Were it not for Empathy having a Gnosis cost it would definitely be worth 10xp on any Garou who cares about supporting or destroying groups of people.
