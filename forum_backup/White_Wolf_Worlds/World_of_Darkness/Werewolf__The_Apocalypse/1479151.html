          The original way sorcery was handled in the WoD was basically a mortal's version of Thaumaturgy. Sorcerer Revised, and the STV project linked above, expanded on that idea, but keep the core idea of having Paths rated 1-5 that allow for more potent hedge magician arts, and more access to rituals. The big addition in Revised in terms of capability was the idea of very rare sorcerers that mastered a 6th dot in their paths, granting them powers that could even outdo some of the things "full" splats can do (though sorcery Numina Paths are not simple affairs to enact even if their specialization can be more effective than general power).

          KABA does have some Kinfolk specific Paths as well (as did its predecessor Kinfolk: Unsung Heroes).

          As NPCs, the limits on sorcery are basically what you want them to be. With enough time and study, a sorcerer could have power rivaling the dynamic magic of mages, and even surpassed them in a few ways. The big drawback is that sorcerers suck at "fast" effects and generally need to pre-prep things for anything really potent. As well their rituals tend to be more intensive.

          As PCs? Expect a power level slightly behind Thaumaturgy (esp. not being able to rely on one trait for powering Path powers, and one for rolling rituals, each Path is going to have its own roll). It's worth noting that Garou learning Paths is not impossible, but difficult enough that there's a 7-dot Merit to get access to it in some editions.

                          The 6th rank of Alchemy is it's own can of worms.

                          Sorcerer: Revised does say that you're, supposedly, cappable of transforming into any splat. It does mention werewolves, vampires and mages. However, the specifics of the ritual (bc it's a ritual) are left to the ST. The wording it's vague, and the book doesn't say what ingredients are needed, and offers no actual systems nor rules for the transformation. In no place says that the ritual should be unconditional, nor devoid of consequences.

                          So, it could very well be that the Sorcerer would need to do something similar to the Wyrm-rite, with similar consequences. At the very least, such level should allow someone to research that transformation independantly. The Sorcerer could discover a different way of achieving the transformation, even. But, ultimately, it's ST discretion.

                          This capability is an artifact of Revised, BUT no 20th anniversary book has truly retconned it. It's just not present, but neither was it present in most books of Revised. 20th anniversary edition doesn't have a new system for Sorcery, so that we could unequivocally say that such powers were deliberately ignored. 20th just rescues the most common "system" for paths, that it's similar to that of vampires, and that was present in most editions and books.

                              It depends.

                              If you're just trying to make a quick and dirty sorcerer NPC, the hunters hunted and paths of power are good enough.

                              Both the above use a nerfed version of "npc" sorcery. Non-sorcerer books like Halls of Arcanum, Hunters Hunted, and A Breed Apart have a much weaker version of sorcery than actual the sorcerer books(which has been true since 2nd edition). Paths of power doesn't update the mechanics either, it's just a complication of paths found in non-sorcerer books for WoD20(which aren't updates on Sorcerer revised, they're NPC powers for a out-of-splat characters) with some of the powers taken from Sorcerer revised, Sorcerer's companion in the STvault, and some new paths the writer thought up.

                              I wouldn't recommend any of the above if you ever have a player wants to be a sorcerer. In that case, I say just pick up Sorcerer revised and tweak things to reflect the power level of the game.

                                  Sorcery Revised or the STV book Paths to Power would be the best bet since they're most recent but while similar they take very different views on the concept and how the paths are implemented (Alchemy being a prime example of one of the complicated aspects of Revised.) Which suits you probably depends on the kind of Sorcerer you intended to build. Both feature the 'struggling awake' merit which covers what is essentially a wild talent you have a bit more control over at times (IIRC PtP even treats it as a wild talent mechanics-wise.) PtP also has a merit that allows certain rotes as a sort of magical gift (sort of like the Proximi from Awakened I think.) What you sound like seems like its leaning towards wild talent.

                                  If you gave M20 'Gods and Monsters' there's a short section on hedge magicians that give rough rules to approximate hedge magic/sorcerery that might give you ideas on how to simulate paths.

                                          Originally posted by Cain Loup-Noir View Post
                                          I'm looking to create a NPC Sorcerer but as i don't know how they use Sorcery or what kind of roll they make to use it and if they have a reserve of mana i cannot finish.
                                          Since this is only an NPC and you're not looking for rules for a PC, you can do the same thing that the books do when they show one of the other splats in their Antagonists chapter - give them whatever powers already in the corebook that can emulate that. This is why Lupines in Vampire are shown having Vampire disciplines, and why vampires in Werewolf are given Gifts. It's not that these creatures actually have those powers. It's just an easy way for the ST to run those creatures if they don't have access to those other books.

                                          If you don't want to do that and want the actual rules for Sorcerer, that's fine. The other posters have given you some options. But if you just want to be able to run your plot, then just give that NPC Sorcerer a combination of Thaumaturgy Paths and some appropriate Gifts (since you have both Vampire and Werewolf books) that give your NPC the powers you want him to have.

                                                  But consider that Garou generally don't get to speak directly with the true creators of the universe found within their mythology. They don't get to ask Gaia, the Weaver, the Wyld, or the Wyrm for proof that their legends are true. They don't generally get to speak to anything higher ranking than an Incarna, and even the answers given by them tend to be limited to rumour and hearsay for most members of the Changing Breeds.
