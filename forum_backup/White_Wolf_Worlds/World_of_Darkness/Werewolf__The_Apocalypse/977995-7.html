    Originally posted by Crusher-of-Cities View Post
    Heavy Arms touched on it slightly, but just a reminder... when the game was first written (by authors who probably didn't have degrees in the historical groups the tribes were mimicking, and 15 years before anyone could just look it all up on their phone) most of the Garou were the BAD GUYS. I can't stress that enough... a major plot point of the game was that the Garou were mostly to blame for everything being as bad as it was, but they're the only ones still around to fix it (also their fault).... and they weren't about to get their act together any time soon. Supernatural Rage left most Garou presented as super-charged racists, bigots and abusers... with the PCs as perhaps the sole beacons of hope along with a select few NPCs. The World of Darkness, and especially Garou, were a caricature of everything an angst-ridden Nine-Inch-Nails fan from the 90s would say was wrong with America (because the game was written from a pretty American perspective, as well).

    So, in a game world designed to highlight the frustration of beating your head against stubborn ignorance and unapologetic ego, the question of how a Tribe would react to anything would have been pretty clear: whatever the worse answer is. (And for the most part, this is still the answer I choose)

    Of course there have been rewrites, retcons, new authors, more research, and even new discoveries in the cultures and mythologies involved. If you know more about a culture than the authors (which you well could... they're RPG authors, not gods) then you might want to adjust your game setting to make more sense in that capacity. If you want a more progressive game where Garou are sensible, thinking people rather than the biggest obstacles to their own success... go for it.

    Just keep in mind that there have been very stark changes in a lot of elements over the course of 4 editions and 24 years. Any forum-goer's opinions of what makes sense for Garou was probably forged by the first book they picked up, not the most recent one they own. I probably own every WtA book ever written, but I can say for certain I haven't reread the fluff chapters end-to-end four times, nor has all the new stuff effectively overwritten the old in my mind.

    A lot of the game we play today is layers of band aids wrapped around nostalgia.
    Yeah, I have often thought that. I would go far far as to say if there is a question about whether or not the Garou tolerate something, the default answer should be a resounding "no" unless there is some strong reason to suspect that they would. An exception would be Children of Gaia or other groups written as explicitly tolerant. The Black Furies are not that. They are zealots consumed by the bigotry and hatred. They have a history of killing male children and occasionally still do, it is hard to imagine what kind of mindset that could lead to such actions. Did those Black Furies who murdered their male infants ask the babies if they actually identified as females before they murdered them? I suspect not.

    This is a bit of a tangent, but it reminds me of an essay I read about Werewolf years ago, I don't recall where. The gist of it was that W:tA isn't a game about playing heroes. It is a game about playing religious fanatics. It is a game about being the Nazis. Why the Nazis? Because you are playing violent fanatics who hate and kill anything that falls outside of their narrow worldview. It was exactly this violence and hate which created many of the Garou's problems and still plagues them. Furthermore, the Garou have a worldview that they accept without the slightest bit of doubt, one where they are fighting absolute evil personified and where any horrific action can be justified for this supposed greater good. Murder a group of innocent children? They were going to grow up to be polluters anyway. Your buddy rapes kinfolk and likes to torture humans for fun - well, such foibles can be forgiven because he is fighting the Wyrm after all. A group kills their own male children for reasons - well, they serve Gaia and fight the Wyrm so those reasons must be alright. We can see how this sort of attitude could be used to justify virtually any evil and indeed has for the Garou, including the War of Rage and the Impergium.

    But, people didn't understand Garou as a game about getting into the mindset of villains, they thought that it actually was about heroes and so the game has become a mishmash of heroism and villainy.

    Of course, this is a controversial take on W:tA, but I think in many ways this vision of the game appeals to me more than "Garou good, Wyrm bad, pass the wheelbarrow full of d10's"

    In any case, I don't think you have to go quite as far as my post to be alright with a fictional organization within a fictional setting believing something that you don't.

The Red Rage is an uncontrollable berserk rage where you are aware of your actions while the Black Rage is an uncontrollable berserk rage where you are unaware of anything. In every case though, the Black Rage is much more impressive than the Red Rage, as all of the governors of the brain are turned off, and everything amps up to '11'. It is also a beautiful thing, as you experience an unbelievable euphoria when you come back from the Black Rage.
