                                    The other side of the issue the ethnic ties, is that even if you do them well, you cause problems. A simple example is just the sheer number of ethnic groups that are out there. Giving each ethnic group that lives just where their are wild wolf populations now, would end up with dozens of Tribes to give them all equal respect and consideration when looking at how a Tribe should be relating them. Even with the best writing that avoids all potential offense, you end up with a game that's entirely too much to use.

                                    The better solution is to have the core character types not tied to ethnic groups, and then give space for individual groups to add those ties in on a more personal level that fits with the group's desires.

                                    One of the places I think W5 should be taking from Forsaken 2e is how Forsaken handles packs: A central group of werewolves that control some locale of importance to a community, with concentric circles of kin and trusted humans around them. What the pack is centered around sets a lot of tone for the game. It could be a criminal kingpin's luxurious home, a legitimate business with strong ties to a specific subgroup within a larger area, a cultural center or religious building for a local minority group, etc. Let the players take the lead on what sort of issues they want the game to center on in this sense.

                                                I'm not a huge fan of the "The Get Of Fenris have fallen."-narrative. What I would have liked more would've been a divide that is more about individual political beliefs than "This Tribe is bad now.". And with political beliefs I mean the question of how to deal with the fact that in this scenario Gaia is dying or dead and now the question is "What do you do?". And there are various political factions with different solutions and what your Tribe is has nothing to do with what a particular Garou thinks is the best thing to do. The social milieu or a Tribe may have some impact on what faction a particular Garou might favor but that's about it.

                                                As a way to bring it back to street-level (as W5 probably will try to do), I think it could be an interesting scenario when the premise is "The Garou-Nation realizes it's not doing so well in the whole 'Prevent-the-Apocalypse'-campaign and how do they respond? The Garou split into factions and start arguing with each other."

                                                That way you can emphasize that the Tribes aren't monoliths and get away from simplistic descriptions like "Every Red Talon wants the Second Impergium and every Get is a Nazi.".

                                                A Tribe falling or being lost should be a momentous event. I wonder how the lore is gonna justify this in a satisfying fashion. Did the recent games explain it? I mean, the Nazi-connection with the Get is terrible but how exactly is this supposed to play out in the WtA-setting? What does a good explanation for them falling look like? Because if the reason for them falling is them being Nazis they better be considered "fallen" in the setting since 1933 or so (or whenever the whole Tribe became pro-Nazi). Sadly, the whole ideology of course didn't disappear with the end of WWII but what would it say about the Garou-Nation if the Get are kept around despite their affiliation with the Nazis...? (Although the idea of the Garou Nation staging their own little "Operation Paperclip" would be a dark premise for the lore)

                                                On the other hand, I've been thinking the past few days - would anything really be good enough here? The association of Gets with Nazis, the association of the Younger and Elder Brother Tribes with Native Americans… The real pain, injustice and loss associated with these things… How could you ever do any of that justice by making people involved in that Werewolves? You’d need to put in a lot of work to justify Native-American-Werewolves and Nazi-Werewolves running around in your setting and present that in a respectable fashion. In 2022 you would never go with a “Tribe=ethnic community/political orientation”-framework anyway, I imagine. WtA 20th Anniversary works perfectly in that regard here because the core-premise is nostalgia. Even by turning it into a pseudo-new-edition and changing mechanics and rewriting lore the core-premise of nostalgia is there to justify why some warts created by 90s writing remain and readers can be made aware of this arrangement.

                                                But how do you do that with a new edition of WtA that still wants to keep one leg in the world of the 90s with the Tribes? WtF was wise in ditching the axes of Tribe and Breed and creating something different. What sort of troubles will this new W5 run into if it wants to be a “re-imagining” but isn’t brave enough to just ditch any notion of nostalgia for the very flawed writing in the 90s? My fear is that W5 will suffer from some sort of schizophrenia where it’s trying to talk to an old fan of WtA and someone who has never played WtA at the same time in order to justify this half-step of how W5 is set up.

                                                    I think you might be over complicating that a bit.

                                                    In a game about werewolves, and one were werewolves and humans have coexisted since prehistory, every human group has werewolves in it. It's easy to justify Native American werewolves, and Nazi werewolves, when werewolves are part of everything because they're part of humanity as a whole.

                                                    The hard part is what I noted in my last post: how the werewolves organize themselves. You don't need to justify why there are Native American werewolves, you need to justify why Native American werewolves have their own separate institutions (not noted as recently, the idea of geographic isolation with how WtA handles things is nonsensical) which is probably impossible at the Tribe level of the game as it currently stands (and that doesn't seem to have changed enough in the preview). You don't need to justify there being Nazi-friendly werewolves because there are Nazi-friendly humans. You need to justify why they have the level of institutional power they managed to achieve in werewolf society (as I pointed to earlier, and some of the WtA books have highlighted, just killing off the Swords isn't enough, because they're a symptom of the Get's philosophy taken to a specific extreme that will come back in some form if you don't address the root flaws in said philosophy).

                                                    I think one solution that might have served Paradox way better (even if it would have been nWW making it): set the game in the 90s. The 90s are not some far off detached era of history. A lot of what's happening in the world now can be traced to significant events then. A critical look back on the 90s from a 2020's lens lets you indulge in 90s aesthetics while updating your setting's play experience to be more in-line with current tastes. Defaulting to the idea that the WoD games will take place in a perpetual fictional version of the 90s lets you keep a lot of things that help keep the feel of the original games, but operate more like D&D or Exalted where each edition can update the setting to address shifting popular real world issues because each new edition is going to default back to 1990.
