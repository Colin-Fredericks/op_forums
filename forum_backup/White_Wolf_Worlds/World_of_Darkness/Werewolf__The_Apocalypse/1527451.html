    Let's chat legacy Fianna!

    12-26-2023, 12:16 AM
    I'm feeling a pretty solid grasp of many of the issues inherent in the old game, but one of the issues I'm still quite hazy on is the Fianna, and I'd love to hear some feedback and ideas about them. From my readings on them and catching whiffs of discourse regarding them, the following sticks out to me:
    • Stereotyping them as the tribe of drunks, depicting an incredibly unhealthy stereotype of Irish culture, and romanticizing the Americanized 'fighting Irish' caricature of the culture i.e., Spirit Brew, Hangover Cure, et al.
    • There are some rumblings that their tribe name may be problematic, but nothing definitive sticks out, and I'd like to learn more about this discourse and how important it is to Irish folk that the name change/stay the same.
    • The unhealthy language that pertains to the objectification of their kinfolk, e.g., Guardians of the Caerns pg. 94:
    Merryck's comments: It's not without a certain sense of irony that the Fianna, self-avowed masters and mistresses of passion and abandon among the werewolves, despise the M***s. They believe that physical deformity shows a malevolence in the spirit as well. But at the same time, they're really into having wild, raucus parties, orgies if you ask my opinion. The Fianna dismiss any potential risk with this kind of activity their answer to slowing those driving passions is "find a Kinfolk to slake your lust." How practical. Don't kid yourself; there are many M***s running around with red hair and green eyes, both in their tribe and outside it.
    I THINK the original intent was to depict them as the parallel to dwarves in D&D, but it only really manages to caricature unhealthy stereotypes. How do each of you approach these topics at your tables? If the Fianna were to get a redraft, how would you depict them? Are there more issues with the tribe I'm missing here? Also, how do you think they approach the relationship between Northern Ireland and the rest of the UK?

    I have some vague ideas on how to address this, but I am unsure if it's comprehensive enough or too heavy-handed:
    • Reorient them away from the fighting/drunkard way they're written to recast them as storytellers and artisans of the Western Concordat.
    • Drop the boozy powers entirely and replace them with something else, perhaps related to the way they recall past events, to play them up as storytellers.
    • Further the meta by maybe altering the way they're written away from something that enables STs and players to avoid some of the more rapey/breeding aspects.
    In short, I'd like this to be a thread to talk shop about the Fianna! What do you love, what do you hate, what causes headache or head-scratching at the tabletop, what is critical to hold onto in the meta, your favorite and least favorite NPCs, etc. Let's chat the red-furred Garou!

      The Fianna are one of my favorite tribes so I’ll try to provide some help to the points you bring up and your questions. (Disclaimer: I must admit that as a younger person I was also fascinated by an imagined Irish culture that I was descended from; I listened to the Pogues religiously, I re-read ‘Trinity’ until the cover was falling off, I read all I could find about the supposed lineage of my surname, etc; all of this while being the fifth generation of my family born in the US. I cannot deny that this influenced my affection for the Fianna)

      •The Fianna have definitely been given the treatment of an ethnic Irish stereotype in the US of rowdy drunks. If you go back on the forums you can find a thread from a now-gone (I believe) poster MyWifeIsScary which explored further exploring those ideas by making them the explicit lower-class/working-class footie hooligan tribe. Opinions varied. I’ll have more to touch on this below but this description, which was clearly stereotypical and one could argue based in internalized stereotypes applied by an anti-Irish/anti-Catholic Victorian cultural attitude in both the US & UK, further emphasized by the optional tribal weakness making it harder for the Fianna to resist temptation and indulgence across the board.

      •The tribal name of ‘Fianna’ is less an issue of being problematic than being potentially limiting imo. Unlike the tribal names for Older Brother or Younger Brother, “Fianna” does not have a cultural connotation that would make it taboo to invoke. Instead the issues surround the name are more pragmatic. For one it refers to an Irish legend of an ancient war-band; this in itself is not necessarily an issue, but it suggests a particularly Irish character which diverges from attempts to make the Fianna pan-Celtic/Gaelic. Now that being said, it’s not unheard of for the Fianna or their equivalents to be invoked by non-Irish Gaelic-identified cultural media. The song ‘the Men of Knoydart’ has a good Scottish example of this. That said, in W5, the tribe name was an issue because that edition was explicitly trying to divorce the Garou tribes from real-world cultures, but kept the name Fianna which is explicitly Gaelic, if not necessarily explicitly Irish. Some alternates proposed for a more pan-Celtic tribal epithet have included W5’s “Hart Warders” as well as the “Horned Singers” or the more general “Children of Stag”.

      •The relationship between their Fianna and their kin is something that I think is one of the most interesting parts of the tribe and the tensions of their stated beliefs, their actions, their values, and contemporary values are rich fodder for drama. That said, of course, some groups may not want to explore these themes and by no means should they be obliged to, but as a game that describes itself as being for ‘mature storytelling’ in a ‘gothic-punk world’ I feel that it would be more of a loss to remove such elements. That said, they ought to be handled with care and attention, as with any other theme for a mature game.

      That said, moving onto your further ideas and points.

      •Something like what you described has always been true, particularly as of the revised area. Of course the tribe in the past has maintained its Celtic connotations but the notion of the Fianna being the leading artisan and storytellers of the Garou Nation is well-established in canon; with some sources describing them as the originators of the Garou High Tongue and the first Klaives. That said, I do think further exploration of this theme could be beneficial. Explorations of the material culture and storytelling of the Nation in general with the Fianna as a lense for such discussion. Perhaps connect it to a further exploration of moots in general and the concept of the Silver Record and the Greater Litany (the oral tradition we are told surrounds the simplified tenets we get in the books).

      •I’ve got kind of a soft-spot for the booze related gifts but I wouldn’t complain if they were made a Homid gift instead (I believe the Bone Gnawers also get the drinking gift iirc).

      •I’ve mentioned in a few places before on this forum that I’ve come to imagine the Fianna and Fenrir as embodying a sort of yin-yang relation with each other as the tribes of Northern/Western Europe. The Fianna I see as being the tribe which explicitly upholds social bonds and the greater social life; they are the tribe most concerned with fertility, familial bonds, the story of the people as it relates to the life and history of the people. As the tribe most actively involved with their kinfolk and in tune with human customs as well as the relationships maintained with spiritual and Fey worlds. (With the Fenrir on the other hand being a more narrow, inwardly focused, death/war society who often live separately from their kin in order to uphold their obligations).

      •My view on the geographical question is that I’m perfectly content with saying that the Tribes of Stag and [War/Death Wolf] are not limited to particular regions on the European sub-continent although they may perhaps have particularly notable populations there alongside presence in other regions. Perhaps something like a slight crib from WtF to have both ideological/political Camps as well as geographical/cultural Lodges.

      •While I can understand why a table may want to downplay certain themes of kinfolk with the Fianna, i personally wouldn’t want to. While I think that accusations of exploitation writing (read this as something akin to the ‘exploitation flick’ genre of film) wrt the Fianna & the Troubles has some merit to it, I do also think that it draws attention to themes that should be relevant for the Fianna and Werewolf in general. I think high-lighting the connections homid Garou have to the circumstances of their human kin-groups and how it affects their decision making is something worthwhile to explore. Furthermore, sectarian warfare is something while plentiful basis in human history and storytelling, I think extending it to homid Garou makes sense and I think that the Fianna are perhaps the tribe that exemplifies it best.

      This post has gone on fairly long so I’ll leave it off here, though I do have more ideas on the subject, but I hope people find things in here that are interesting and I’ll try to answer any questions folks may have.

                          I don't feel that this name is problematic, as from my conversations with a couple of Irish folks and what I have learned about Fion MacCumhil and the roving bands known as Fiana, they seem to fill the exact niche of wandering heroes the Garou Pack is bound to be. For me the major reason why Fianna is the well known name is because it's the dominant strain of the tribe that colonised the world, has significant cache in Europe and are the ones that got into the New World, Australia, Africa and the far East through accompanying their kinfolk and taking up opportunities to carve out their places outside the traditional strongholds of the Tribe.

                          In practice, they would actually have quite a few localised factions based on Celtic ancestry throughout Iberia, France, Northern Italy and Western Germany who all felt the Fianna subtribe are selfaggrandazing assholes, but they all follow the same Totem and are part of the same nation even if they had conflicts at certain times.

                          The fighting drunkard stereotype had mostly been fixed by Revised calling out the fact that earlier portrayals could look like really bad stereotypes of pop culture Irish. I do feel that there is an element here that should be kept, though. To me, a defining characteristic of the Fianna is passion. They are probably the most passionate tribe, driven by a desire to experience everything life has to offer and protect that which the Mother has left them. This means that they should be defined by a certain joie de vivre. They should be enjoying food, drink, company and sex. They should love the world and its inhabitants, enjoying every moment they spend indulging and defending the bounty they are given. This does not mean that they should be defined by excess, but I do feel that this can be played up without going into the whole Dennis Leary's worst Irish Drunkard.

                          Another element to consider is that considering this joie de vivre of the Fianna they tend to adapt to the locals and their customs at a faster pace than others, creating offshoots trying to make a name for themselves beyond the identity of the settlers from the lands of the British Crown.                                   As for objectifying kinfolk, I think the simplest thing would be to say that they are in favor of free love and even casual sex, and that's why it's normal for them to have frequent relationships with kinfolk and even with those outside of kinfolk. lineage, I know this is weird and more like my headcanon, but I see them like that, they just aren't shy about relationships, it's fun have someone like that in the group (mostly).
                                  I have what, I think, is a pretty strong argument against this, and it's that the game offers no robust tabletop tools for navigating consent between the players and storytellers to gauge whether one is receptive to this or not. World of Darkness games on the whole aren't really built for it anyway, and it's important the horror is happening to characters and not players (traumatizing players is a good way to lose players IMO). While long-term tabletops with people who know each other well enough to navigate this can and will explore this of their own accord in a chronicle, I just don't think this is something that's a good idea to offer up in print as a turnkey 'yeah this tribe does this so you should too', especially since your average tabletop player is anything but equipped to navigate this kinda thing maturely without any safety tools to do so. It can be fun for mature players that make it a consensual experience, but I'm not sure it should be pushed as a social norm that's emblematic of the tribe. If it serves no narrative purpose, it's a lot more likely to generate groans and eye-rolls at the table, or turn people off from playing at all.
