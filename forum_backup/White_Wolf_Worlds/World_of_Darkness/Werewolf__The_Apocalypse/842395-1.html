                            Ok, so we can just head this off then:

                            1) Open Seal can effectively hotwire a car by letting you turn the ignition via bypassing the locks on it (electronic or not).

                                                        While this is mostly settled, my problem is this assertion that "modern" is some important distinction here. Immobilizers have existed in various forms for almost 100 years. While the current common immobilizers have become standard (even regulated as such) in lots of places, they're not universal. Unless your sole definition of "modern car," is, "got rid of steering wheel ignition keys, and has a transponder based immobilizer," it's inaccurate. Not every market has this as a legal requirement. Japan, for example, has been making cars without this specific technology to this day. Has Honda or Toyota not made a modern car in 15+ years because they consider this an optional bit of technology; all the other new technologies be damned?
