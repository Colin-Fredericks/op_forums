    W20 Jam Weapon and Fetishes

    09-07-2021, 10:59 PM
    Greetings,

    Does the gift Jam Weapon, disable weapon fetishes? I am particularly interested on Klaives and the Hunter’s Talons fetish.

    I am almost positive that Klaives are affected but unsure about the Hunter’s Talons as those are claw complements and not manufactured but provided by an Incarna.

    Jam Weapon (Level One) — The Child may stop
    any Weaver-born weapons from working within the range
    of his voice. A dove-spirit teaches this Gift.
    System: The Garou shouts an ancient word of power
    and grace and spends a Gnosis point. The player rolls
    Willpower against a difficulty of highest Willpower of
    any armed individual within earshot. For each success, all
    manufactured weapons will not function     for one turn. This
    includes guns, crossbows, flame-throwers, and even knives
    and swords, which refuse to cut.     Natural weapons (such as
    claws) and natural objects appropriated as weapons (such
    as rocks or naturally-fallen tree limbs) are unaffected.
    Emphasis mine.

    Thanks.

      Easy part first: Hunter's Talons clearly wouldn't fall under the Gift. Not only do they come from an Incarna, but Jam Weapon explicitly doesn't apply to natural items like branches or rocks in that are found in the environment. Hunter's Talons are made from bits of metal that "naturally" occur in Rorg's Realm.

      For a Klaive.... there's enough ambiguity in the phrasing here to go either way. W20 dropped the "moving parts" aspect of things to make it work against blades, but the Gift stresses Weaver tools and manufacturing. You could interpret that any tool is manufactured, and thus a Klaive would be affected. You could also interpret this not to apply to "artisanal" weapons that are custom crafted using traditional methods that don't rise to the Weaver-ish-ness of manufacturing; thus most Fetish weapons like Klaives wouldn't be impacted.

      I personally tend towards the second simply because it's a level 1 Gift and being able to shut down powerful magical items like that with no extra effort strikes me as a bigger impact than the Gift should have.

                          I think the "like" before "some sort" is meant to indicate that it isn't a gauntlet, but for claws, it just acts in a similar fashion. If a Fetish was made of a natural tree branch but acted like a baseball bat, it wouldn't be a baseball bat and shouldn't be treated as a manufactured baseball bat; it's a natural stick.

                          The art is... art. It's meant to be evocative and inspirational, not a way to judge the rules. Art is generally commissioned long before the text is finalized, and the artists rarely have the full context of the image (esp. smaller pieces like that one). I'd infer that an artist got a note to draw a werewolf with a metal gauntlet, and thus we see something befitting Crinos plate armor instead of something you'd expect to see made of the metallic chunks of asteroid collision debris. As such, I don't usually give weight to it unless there's a very compelling reason to.
