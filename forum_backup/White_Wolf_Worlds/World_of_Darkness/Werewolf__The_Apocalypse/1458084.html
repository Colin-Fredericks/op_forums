      I think the difference between needing just an action, an action and an activation roll, expenditure and an action, or expenditure, an action and a roll, or none of those is keyed to two factors:
      Practicability and Balancing.

      Not that every author who wrote a book and wanted to being in a new Gift or Rite also provided extensive game testing to those new rules to verify that they work.

      So, if the effect makes only sense if permanent or reflexly activated, then the Gift rules should written accordingly. Doesn't mean that a roll or expenditure of points isn't a factor. Also, the authors opinion on what should be an ability that needs activation or not changed and so you and someone else might feel differently about the effects you have in mind and how to fit them into the mood of the game. "Aura of Confidence" for example once needed an activation roll, indicating a Shadowlord needed to actually bolster herself and activate the Gift to appear to be without weakness. In W20 the Gift was made to be a permanent effect. One can have different opinions on the implications of that change for the greater narrative, as propably every Shadowlord has learned that Gift by the time they are Fostern.

      Also, the Storyteller's Handbook Revised, iirc, states, that the Rank a Gift is bestowed on a garou by a spirit is totally arbitrary, within the game world and only relevant as a balancing mechanism for characters. Which might explain why a Cliath garou in a Pack with a certain totem might be able to use Gifts that are far beyond her rank.

      As for the spending of points. I get he impression, that Gnosis is spend if the effect is generally something that is easily described as magically. Rage is usually spend on effects that are aggressive in nature or deal with action, swiftness, and similar themes. Willpower is often spend when the effect is akin to overcoming one's own limits or focus of though and similar themes.

      If you would name the effects you have in mind, we might give our opinions on rolls and point expenditure.

          It isn't about knowing when to put such things, actually they are part of what you want to have in the end.

          When to call for a roll:
          - If the effect scales, then a roll should be made to determine its potency, probably with normal difficulty;
          - If the effect should be resisted, then it calls for a Contested Roll;
          - If it should have different chances of working depending on circumstances, like the complexity of a target, then t calls for a roll with varying difficulty;
          - If you deem the effect too volatile, you should call for a roll with difficulty 8 or 9 to check for Botch;
          - Otherwise, only call for a roll if you want the effect to be unreliable.

          You make it have a cost when you want to limit the frequency it can be used. Each choice impacts a different resource economy, so its more a matter of knowing how frequently each resource is used and recovered.

          RAGE is meant to fluctuate a lot during combat, easy to get and many options to spend. Use Rage when you want for an action-oriented Gift that the players could have a reason for spamming in combat, but you don't want them to, making for more complex and varied decisions;

          GNOSIS is circumstantial to recover and affect other innate mystic abilities. Use Gnosis when you want the Gift to be used sparingly and to have a tighter control for the ST on when the resource can be refilled, so the players know sometimes they may use it more freely, others they need to ration;

          WILLPOWER is slow to recover, but steady. Its pace of recovery is mostly on the Players hands but never too hard. Use Willpower when you want the Gift to be used sparingly, but never be truly out of their reach, so it is an interesting option and always available, but not be used lightly.

          After you take your decisions, playtest it. Think about each aspect added as a dial to control balance, so the initial value isn't as important as the possibility of changing it as you see the results. The ultimate balance checker, though, is the Gift Rank, so no need to get too carried away.

              I disagree.
              The main source of Rage regain is the Moon and that is once per day (or night, well whenever the moon rises and sets) - during combat this is basically useless.

              The other sources are humiliation, but during combat few character use their turns for taunting or something like that. Sure, you could encompass a lot of things that happen during the combat as humiliating to the character, but I'm not sure if it is meant to be that encompassing. Also, it overlaps with the next entry, so results from bad rolls shouldn't be humiliating unless they are a botch. In my opinion the humiliating source is meant for social interactions.

              Botches allow for regaining of a Rage, but they shouldn't happen that often during combat - well, combat is dice roll intensive, so they happen there more frequently than during other activities but still should be rare with revised/W20 rules.

              The last source of Rage is anticipation of combat, meaning exactly before the combat starts. But this is only relevant, if the character had spend Rage in activities before. If it is their first fight since seeing the moon they are probably at full Rage anyway.

              A lot of players I know mistakenly grant Rage if a packmate or the character herself is wounded, but RAW this is a trigger for a frenzy check, not a rage gain. Gifts like Primal Anger or Silver Claws and some others provide Rage regaining, but they are not at most garou's disposal.

                      Additional consideration on Gifts:

                      Rage is (though as I frequently point out very weakly enforced) not something PCs can draw on outside moments of stress. If a Gift uses Rage, it's a Gift you can't use casually, or to buff yourself on relatively calm actions.

                      Rage and Gnosis can't be used at the same time. If a Gift requires you to spend or roll Gnosis (or activate a Fetish/Talen), you can't spend any Rage that turn. This is mostly important for combat Gifts, but it's an important factor to consider. This also makes Willpower based Gifts a bit more valuable because they don't interfere with Rage or Gnosis actions.
