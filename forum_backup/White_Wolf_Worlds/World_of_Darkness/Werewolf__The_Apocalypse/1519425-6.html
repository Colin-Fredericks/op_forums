        Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post


        Well holy macaroni, p.38 of W5 has some INTERESTING takes.
        So I've looked a bit into this... It's a complex topic, like here's an abstract from an article in an academic journal called "The Historical Trauma Response Among Natives and Its Relationship with Substance Abuse: A Lakota Illustration":

        Historical trauma (HT) is cumulative emotional and psychological wounding over the lifespan and across generations, emanating from massive group trauma experiences; the historical trauma response (HTR) is the constellation of features in reaction to this trauma. The HTR often includes depression, self-destructive behavior, suicidal thoughts and gestures, anxiety, low self-esteem, anger, and difficulty recognizing and expressing emotions. It may include substance abuse, often an attempt to avoid painful feelings through self-medication. Historical unresolved grief is the associated affect that accompanies HTR; this grief may be considered fixated, impaired, delayed, and/or disenfranchised. This article will explain HT theory and the HTR, delineate the features of the HTR and its grounding in the literature, offer specific Native examples of HT and HTR, and will suggest ways to incorporate HT theory in treatment, research and evaluation. The article will conclude with implications for all massively traumatized populations.
        So "historical trauma" is a thing but I haven't seen anybody tying it to DNA. I guess you could use it as an excuse that it's an in-character-perspective (and from a Silver Fang no less). But this is a real and serious subject, so just dedicating one page seems ill-advised - and on top of that trying to make a case of linking that to Garou... I can see with some of the theories in this book of how Kin are created that you could link those things. But yeah... This thing almost reads like it is making the case that the reason for why Native American Garou exist is "historical trauma"...

                                                    Originally posted by Nyremne View Post
                                                    soooooo historical trauma is "passed foward through DNA" but all exemples given are about culture and language lost due to social conditioning.

                                                    I have many way to describe that kind of stupidity, but the least of them would probably push the mods to ban me.
                                                    So, inherited trauma is thought to be something that occurs through epigenetics at the DNA level, although social and psychological factors may also play a role. The BBC has a decent overview here: https://www.bbc.com/future/article/2...is-epigenetics

                                                    Think of your genes like a bunch of switches. Real-life events can flip switches on and off to help you better adjust to your environment now, rather than requiring generations of adaptation. (This is overly simplistic, but it's just an analogy.)

                                                    The basic theory is that major stressors and environmental harms can flip these switches and then when we have kids, they're born with some of the same switches already pre-flipped.

                                                    HTR (historical trauma response) is an older, closely related theory, that assumes the effect is entirely psychosocial, rather than biological. Other theories include that it might be biological changes occurring as a result of stress in the womb. The counterargument is that things like a consistent increase in risk of brain haemorrhages or cancer can't be explained by behaviour alone.

                                                    The problem with the writing here is that it doesn't explain or make clear the connection between the things it details and the DNA shifts. It also assumes a general fluency in these issues. Even just an extra line to explain how inherited trauma works would have helped. The problem may also be that it's written IC.
                                                    ​​​​​
                                                    Personally, I would have added an OOC text box to add a bit more context than you'd get from a character writing to one of their peers. But then again, Netchurch never did that, so maybe the problem is bringing in complex IRL science and mixing it meekly with the urban fantasy.
