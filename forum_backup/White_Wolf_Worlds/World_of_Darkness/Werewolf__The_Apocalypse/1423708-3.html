    I also think, that for a lot of the templates in the TBs the normal background of the game doesn't make so much sense.

    When Black Fox writes, that garou are "cubs" for several years, where they get training, I wonder if there is a reference for that. Because, I vaguely remember some garou being thrown into their "Rite of Passage" pretty fast, but I'm not sure if these were from the books or from the LARP, back when I joined the game 20 years ago (man, I'm old).

    Pre-W20, when one could be certain if a person was a garou or a kinfolk, even pre-change, there was certainly potential for a two class upbringing of children within the kinfolk community. One out of eight (factoring in Pure Breed and talens to help with breeding true) would be a chosen warrior and certainly raised another way than their kinfolk siblings/cousins/peers. With the Curse already present, they also would need special consideration regarding their education. Public school would be difficult, but home schooling isn't an option every where. In Germany for example, school is mandatory and exceptions are rare. The number of garou wouldn't be so high as to get permits for private schools (which also may be suspicious once hunter figure that out). A boarding school might be an option, but all septs in Germany would hardly fill one school, unless kinfolk was also permitted, and even then the different tribes might object sending their kids there.

    Post-W20, one can't be sure if a kid is garou or not, and that leads to whole new dynamic. Does one educate all kids in a way that prepares them to be garou, or none? Starting from what age would one try to see if there are indicators of the Curse when interacting with humans? How accurate can this be?

    "Warriors of the Apocalypse" has backgrounds to some characters from the WtA-based TCG. I always liked the story of "Anna something", who was raised as a garou and part-took in garou festivities even pre-change. When during a Rite emotions ran high, she had her First Change and entered a frenzy. A (supposed) loss of Renown for which her father disowned her on the spot, and she challenged him for that. Having been trained by her father and being basically inexperienced she shouldn't have had a chance, but she had mastered a combat move from another teacher her father didn't expect and she bested and killed him. Claiming his Klaive she also proclaimed Cliath rank for herself, and the sept agreed.
    I didn't re-read the story, it's the way I remember it. This is a very bad-ass story, but it wouldn't make sense under W20 rules, for a kid wouldn't be trained like a garou.

    In our LARP palyers are all older than 20 and even those who join for the first time play as cubs when they start. So, we ignore the whole "first change in adolescence" and the danger of becoming a Lunatic for the most part. Still, we try to make players aware, that their characters probably aren't the most successful entrepreneurs or have any career where interaction with people is required, due to the Curse.

        Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
        The game's setting described in the corebook assumes most Garou change around adolescence. They are either raised in knowing kinfolk families to have Garou values, or are runaways from their lost kinfolk/human parents. In either case, normal humans would ostracize them to various degrees because of the Curse. Their "job" for the most part is simply being Garou. After several years of tutelage as cubs, Garou begin their advance as rank in their late teens.

        I will have to admit I have never seen a group of PCs fit this description (there have been individual exceptions within a group that mainly does not fit). Furthermore, most NPCs in games I've played in also do not fit this description. Even cubs - PCs or NPCs - are late teens at their youngest.

        Instead I see many late changing Garou who already have careers - often successful ones, and they continue in those careers as Garou. I think there are several reasons for that.

        First, the time scale of many chronicles don't really allow aging so any PCs are essentially "trapped" in that age forever. While I know many players who don't mind starting in their teens, are running preludes of that age, they perceive their character in at least young adult terms - mid twenties to early thirties. They don't want to play characters who couldn't even legally get into a bar - at least not for the entire game. And most chronicles take place "now" and don't allow for the passage of time. Second, players in general love to frontload accomplishments and achievements into their character background. And a character needs time to do that. You have to at least be in your early to mid-twenties to be combat veterans, professionals like lawyers, successful businessmen, etc. That can't be done if your PC starts at age 16. Third, people really see "career/job" as a defining characteristic - so their character concepts often start with that. They want certain skills, life events, and training that is unlikely to have happened to adolescents. Fourth, they want to interact with the human world and don't want to do so as scary, unemployed vagabonds who'd have the cops called on them constantly.

        I don't see this in fantasy RPG settings, but this is how it goes in any setting that takes place in the modern real world or sci-fi equivalent. And most of the other WoD game lines support this because being turned into a vampire, awakening into a mage, or dying can happen at later dates. Changeling has a similar problem as Werewolf - every PC is at least a late Wilder or a Grump. I've never seen someone play a Childing, or really use those as NPCs.

        So I think there is often a tension between the game's setting as written in the corebook, and how people actually run games. And Werewolf character templates usually reflect that. They don't reflect the setting as described in the corebook. They describe it by how people usually play it - which ignores a lot of supposedly key features of the setting.

        When I ST games, I don't even try to police this with the players although I do encourage them to start as young as possible. Instead, I work on making the NPCs reflect the setting accurately, and usually run games in the recent past (anywhere from starting several decades ago to a century ago) so we can have the passage of time during the game and try to have the game take place over several years if not several decades.
        That's what I've been talking about. Playing "by the rules" is nearly impossible. Especially that I don't agree with you that training lasts for several years. I think it's more a year or so. Especially if the cub was raised by Kinfolks, they wouldn't mind the sept talking them over after the First Change. With cubs raised by ordinary mortals that would be a bigger problem. Funny thing, most ready-to-play characters doesn't have Kinfolk Background and don't mention them in their bios. As for templates are I remember only one who seemed to be about 12 years old, a Wendigo Ragabash from 2nd edition Tribebook.

        As for players I've been experimenting with various methods of creating the characters. The one I use now divides the responsibilities of creating them between my players who describes background and me rulling the dots. It goes like that: players decide their characters DoB, fills some things like medical and school chart, police rap sheet (if there are any) and, with that as a base I fill the character sheet like that:

        Attributes 7/5/3 (Garou are homo sapiens superior; they have a headstart if we consider the attributes; the only exception is Appereance cause I know players who won't accept characters with Appereance below 3).
        Abilities 11/7/4 (they are ordinary teenagers, the rest they will get after the training; I allow them to decide). Restricted abilities are Streetwise, Drive, Firearms, Melee, Bureaucracy and Medicine, unless the character's background justifies that. Max 2 dots on every trait, unless the charater is planned as a professional in one area.
        Backgrounds varies (generally my decision, especially Pure Breed, Ancestors and Fetish, I also use backgrounds system from 1st Edition, with dots restrictions based on Tribes).
        Freebies 18 (distributed according to bios).

        Auspice is based on DoB (plus potential planetary Merits&Flaws), they are members of the tribe which got them first.

        That way the players "get" (they don't have access to their character sheets) an untrained base but with a lot of Backgrounds and secondary abilities (usually). That makes their lives harder but, on the other hand, more realistic. As for advancing in age and experience I wrote about that in other thread.

        You could also notice that there are didn't mention Metis (the same "rules", but as they usually live near the sept their skills will rather be useful in fighting the Wyrm, more like John Connor's in Terminator) and lupus (I rarely allow playing lupus but if I do I use standard lupus rules). My players accepted that and we have fun playing (there were no complaints, on the other hand no one forces them to play with me). I run the Preludes of course, to faciliate filling the dots.

        I also don't have a problem with character's aging. My stories rarely have a clear start and finish so there are only two ways to end: death or player's decision that he/she has enough and puts the character away.

        I also plan to start a LARP. The character creation will be basically the same, except for those who wants to play more advanced characters. In that case they will get 3 sets of question, one set for Race, Auspice and Tribe they want to play, one question for each rank (the higher rank, the tougher questions, not always from W:tA sourcebooks). That way I will decide their ranks and add some freebies accordingly.
