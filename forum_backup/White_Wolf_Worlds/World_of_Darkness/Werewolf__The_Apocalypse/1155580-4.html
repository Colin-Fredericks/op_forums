    Sorry for the thread necromancy, but I must thank Black Fox for this thorough review of Rage Across NY.

    This was one of the first W:tA sourcebooks I've ever bought and, boy, oh boy! What a disappointment it was!

    I was expecting a book full of intriguing plot hooks, inspirational stories, badass foes and, at the very least, an interesting depiction of NYC in the World of Darkness. And I got exactly the opposite of that.

    As you pointed out, everything is painted in very broad strokes. I also noticed that there's no synergy between the different sections of the book (history mentions the Native Americans, Colonial US and so on, but none of those elements are integrated in the book's grand picture), and most NPCs are completely bland and uninteresting.

    What seems to be the best part of the book - inter-tribal conflict - was useless to me, because, as a game master, I don't think I'm good at running such games. Personally, I don't like such stories in Werewolf, and pretty much prefer the Epic and Dark / Urban Fantasy mood and atmosphere from Revised than the grimdark vibes of early Werewolf books.

    There's also the fact that the author wasn't any good and his writing was quite dull, boring, and uninspired. That didn't help, either.

    And don't get me started on that bloody Seventh Generation. I remember being appalled at that time to see such a serious subject (sex trafficking of children and women, and pedophilia) being treated in such a frivolous and thoughtless manner. Although I just love the idea of Wyrm cults (much more than, say, Pentex) I never felt inclined to use such crap as the 7th Generation in my games. Quite the opposite.

    The only good part of the book, for me, was the description of caerns and protectorates, something that I used extensively when I ran a NY campaign a long time ago; however, I added a fourth caern, around Kaaterskill Falls in the Castkill Mountains (a level 3 Leadership caern shared by Silver Fangs and Fianna with a solid alliance with the Catskill's fae).

    Oh, and one last thing: I liked your suggestions of dividing the 7th Generation in three distinct cults. In fact, I have a plague cult in my campaign, the Brotherhood of Fly, a cult that worships the Fly totem, which they call Druj Nasu (in real life, a zoroastrian demon, but a Wyrm totem in my canon), a neomalthusian apocalyptic cult whose aim is culling what they see as the overpopulation of Earth by spreading plagues and disease.

    Anyway, thanks again for your thread.

                            RANY is definitely not my favorite, and I think it really shows it wasnt made by new yorkers, or at least ones who had any high degree of awareness of social issues facing the city in the 90s. I appreciate many of the NPCs it introduces, but I don't use much of the book beyond it. It does become a kind of 'generic everycity' and tbh thats kind of what I wish it was, a fictional metropolis that exists solely within the WoD in a nonfictional location. History is out there, and I think such an undertaking would be pretty fun.

                            I remember bringing this up in a Q&A ages ago when Revised was being promoted and demo'd at different game shops, and being told by the promoter that a big reason locations and social matters were often really fuzzy in WoD and didn't always coincide completely with the real world is that it was a deliberate design choice, acknowledging that there exist people in the real world who may get it in their heads that they might want to recreate some of the stuff they read in the books, and that disassociating key locations and plots from real life people or places was largely a matter of safety.

                            With that in mind, I wish they'd have just taken it all the way and going with something entirely fictional they could go whole ham on and not have to hold back, because what we ended up getting was a book with some bad stereotypes and very little 'new york' in there that isnt a movie or comic trope. Not to bash on those, but that wasn't what was being sold.

                            Author of Hearthbound and Project Director for Werewolf: the Essentials

                                        So I just read through Rage across New York and share the concerns indicated in this thread about the 7th generation.

                                        However there were two things that I think could be added to the area that I didn't see mentioned.

                                        1. Fort Drum is a sizable military base in the North Country Protectorate. With a major combat unit, 10th Mountain Division, stationed there a lot of different plot threads could be generated.

                                        Have the Get of Fenris gotten some kinfolk embedded on the base? They could be ensuring that military grade ammo, weapons or equipment is lost, "broken" or declared surplus and makes its way to the local Garou.

                                        Has Pentex used the base as an excuse to have a military contractor subsidiary set up a facility on base or nearby? Lots of sketchy things could result.

                                        Maybe one of the various government agencies that is becoming aware of the Garou has pulled some strings and helicopter training is being used to try to find isolated locations that could be sites of Garou activity.

                                        2. I didn't notice a single mention of any of the other Fera in the book. While I understand that some Werewolf players don't care about the Fera a few mentions could go a long way to helping storytellers that want to use them. NYC is big enough that some forgotten kinfolk of any of the Fera might have resulted in an unexpected birth, there is the persistent urban myth about alligators in the sewer system of NYC a Mokole born among that population could lead to some interesting sessions.

                                        Ananasi almost certainly exist in some numbers in NYC.

                                        Did any of the Ajaba who fled black tooth's massacre make it to New York? It has been ~40 years since that happened, a couple Ajaba and some kinfolk could now be a small clan of them somewhere.

                                        Do either of the Baset tribes of North America pass through the area?

                                        Corax must be at least occasional visitors to the area. The Caern of the Hand of Gaia is supposed to be a major link in the wold moon bridge network, given how much Corax love to travel, they should use it occasionally for that.

                                        Two tribes of Gurahl could easily have a few members in the area, the Forest Walkers and the River Keepers. Even a mention of two of areas Garou elders tell their young to avoid because they don't want to piss off a werebear could be interesting.

                                        All the interpersonal drama between various Garou in the area seem ripe for a Nuwisha trick or two. It could be listed as a plot hook or way the storyteller could kick one of the festering problems into high gear.

                                        Ratkin must love New York City.

                                        The US is supposed to have the most Rokea betweeners of anywhere, some of them must be in this area.

                                        As shown it really wouldn't take a large page count to briefly cover most of the Fera in broad strokes in the region and give at least something for those interested.


                                        Since I am trying to sort out the current situation in southern Ontario for a new campaign, and the situation in at least part of New York State is certainly relevant. So if there are people interested in collaborating on a doc covering the whole region I would be interested.
