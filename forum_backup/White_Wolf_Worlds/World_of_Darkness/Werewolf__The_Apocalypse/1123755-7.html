        I just came back to the forum from a long absence and to be honest, haven't really read the whole 8 page thread on Pure Breed.

        However, I kind of hate Pure Breed, though I do like the "Great things are expected from you" aspect of the background. Also Pure Breed never made much sense for tribes like the Children of Gaia who accepted outcasts and didn't enforce arranged marriages.

        This is how I house ruled it in my game:

        In an effort to make social status in the game less about eugenics/appearance and more about being descended from heroes, we will be removing Pure Breed from the game and changing how Notable Heritage Merit works. Notable Heritage will not be tied to a specific tribe, but to family lines created by the player. The Fortunes of Family Lines can rise and fall and Garou with no notable family history can found new lines of heroes.

        Your PC could come from a line of Black Furies but if they join the Children of Gaia, they do no lose their Notable Heritage. Your Get of Fenris could look like a Red Talon or really whatever wolf makes sense for their background without having to spend points on it.

        There will now be two levels of Notable Heritage, a 3 point and a 5 point merit. Each point ranking of the merit still adds to rank challenges and social rolls involving Garou and Kinfolk (Even Ronin and Black Spiral Dancers).

        Notable Heritage (3 Points) - Your character descends from the heroes of the Garou Nation. Other characters tell you how you look just like your father/mother/great aunt and expect great things from you. Stories are told of how your great grandmother, Bays at the Moon, bested the wretched Black Spiral Dance, Back of My Hand, in combat. Please list at least one notable ancestor of at least Adren/Athro in Rank in your Character History and add public information about your ancestor to the Silver Record.

        Notable Heritage (5 Points) - Your character descends from the leaders of the Garou Nation. Other characters tell you how you look just like your father/mother and expect great things from you. Sept Alphas and Tribal Elders can be found up your family tree. Please list at least one notable ancestor of at least Elder/Legend in Rank in your Character History and add public information about your ancestor to the Silver Record.


        Tribal Restrictions

        Silver Fangs - All Silver Fang Characters must have Notable Heritage at the five point level. Falcon only accepts those Garou that are descended from the leaders of the Garou Nation.

        Bone Gnawers and Glass Walkers - Notable Heritage is restricted for all Bone Gnawer and Glass Walker Characters. Both Urban Tribes are focused on what your character accomplished rather than what your ancestors did.
        The side effect of this change meant there was a LOT less Notable Heritage in our game. Pure Breed is suppose to be rare, but it seemed every PC in a non Pure Breed Restricted Tribe had some. With this house rule, it was more work on the player to add a Notable ancestor to their background and thus made the Merit more rare. I even dropped it from some of my NPCs cause I couldn't think of a suitable ancestor for them.

        For those who did take Notable Heritage, it added an extra dimension to their character. Instead of Pure Breed being dots on a sheet, the other PCs treated the Notable Heritage characters differently because they KNEW the story behind their ancestors.

            Originally posted by wonderandawe View Post
            The side effect of this change meant there was a LOT less Notable Heritage in our game. Pure Breed is suppose to be rare, but it seemed every PC in a non Pure Breed Restricted Tribe had some.
            I've rarely seen Pure Breed used in table top. Even the players who pick SF tribe for their PC seems reluctant to give up precious build points on Pure Breed. Once they get to the more supernatural Backgrounds, most PCs prefer Totem (by far), Rites (especially for certain auspices), or even Ancestors or Fetish. They want to use their Background points and Freebies on other things. But everyone's game is different.

            The only place I see Pure Breed picked by many players is in LARP and Online roleplay because most social interaction there is with other PCs and not the ST plot or NPCs. That's no different from players making sure they have a tag showing Glamorous x5 pinned to their shirt, or picking an attractive model for their online model of their character.

            PCs, of course, are always special and will do whatever. I've long given up trying to police them during character creation unless they do something spectacularly off basis. When it comes to setting limitations (such as Pure Breed is rare), I concentrate on the NPCs and the setting as a whole. Not the PCs. Usually once I know their general concept and character creation choices, I will drive them to things so I can better have them make sense in the chronicle. (OK, you will be playing a Pure Breed 4 SF, which of these three SF NPC families would you like your character to belong to. Hmm, you are playing a high Pure Breed Black Fury, let's figure out together the best way we incorporate that into the setting, and by the way since none of the other Black Furies have anywhere near your PB, it'll become an important element in play.)

            Originally posted by wonderandawe View Post
            With this house rule, it was more work on the player to add a Notable ancestor to their background and thus made the Merit more rare. I even dropped it from some of my NPCs cause I couldn't think of a suitable ancestor for them.
            The problem I see with your house rule is that given the small number of Garou and kinfolk in the world (especially once you get down to single tribes as individual populations), their oral traditions, and ancestral memory, I would think almost any Garou would eventually be able to find one or more notable ancestors very quickly. One in every 12.5 people in Asia (and one in 200 worldwide) can claim descent from Genghis Khan. There's around ten other men just as successful as he was. Then you have lots of other people who could be called notable that just weren't as successful genetically. And of course Garou don't just judge the male ancestor as being notable, but also the female one. Even if you take people outside the tribe and begin breeding with them, and thus that half of their family has zero notable Garou ancestors, the other half could be extremely notable.

            There is also the issue of the Ancestors ability versus the Pure Breed. I've seen PCs with almost no Pure Breed but lots of Ancestors. And those ancestors (especially distant ones) they contact could be quite notable. It's just that the PC doesn't appear to be like them at all.

            Physical similarity between siblings, cousins, and parents is something often remarked on. Is there any father who isn't told that their newborn baby looks like him; or finding various things they can match back to others (she has Grandmom's curly hair, or eyes like Great Aunt Sally)? Or if you see photos of past family generations and notice that only a certain grandson or great grandson still has similarities to the original family patriarch, then that is something the family as a whole mentions. Or in big extended family get togethers how you pierce who is who ("You can tell he's one of the Hartwells because he has thin lips"). Other times it may not be something physical, but another trait ("he has the typical Johnson sense of humor" or "she has the fiery temper of her mother's side of the family")

            So personally, I like the idea that there is a physical aspect or other trait that is associated with "Pure Breed" that others recognize. It's not just that someone is descended from some notable Garou (who isn't if you go back enough in time?). It's the idea that right here, right now, it seems that someone from that Golden Age of Heroes is with us.

            I think what hampers some people is the idea that there is ONE ideal way of looking. If you are told Silver Fangs are supposed to have blond hair, blue eyes, and pale skin, that is very limiting. So I've never done it that way. Certain lineages may be known for that, but others do not. Silver Fangs from House Wise Heart or House Blood Red Crest don't look like Nordics, but people from their native region. I also have members of the more European based Crescent Moon hail from the Uralic or Siberian peoples. Their notable features may come in the form of bushy eyebrows, keen eyesight, oddly shaped birthmarks, or whatever.

            Pure Breed isn't something obviously physical because it's not noticed by humans or even kinfolk. It's a factor from the Garou's spirit side as a result of their totem. What the Garou sense is something instinctive that would be hard to put into words because human language cannot refer to it. (Just had the idea that it might be possible to brainstorm with your players new nonsense words that refer to Pure Breed traits to make up for that. "Oh, look you can tell he's very lurr. I wonder if he's part of that renown Fianna lineage?" And then you define what "lurr" is, like someone who has the noble aspect of a buck deer trotting in the autumn leaves, or someone with the eyes of a predator looking for an escaped prey). So physical appearance is one aspect, and often defaults simply to those kind of traits that typically announce someone is healthy genetically. It's just that there are also other things outside of that which only Garou could sense.


            Originally posted by wonderandawe View Post
            For those who did take Notable Heritage, it added an extra dimension to their character. Instead of Pure Breed being dots on a sheet, the other PCs treated the Notable Heritage characters differently because they KNEW the story behind their ancestors.
            I think in general whenever PCs or ST actually do more work creating flavor text for the dots on their character sheet, the other players become more engaged. Many players put a lot of work into their individual character's backstory, but I find most players don't want to put any effort in detailing those aspects if their character sheet immediately outside of them. Allies and Contacts are supposed to have a specific number of "major" Allies and Contacts that should be detailed, but players don't want to put the effort into a brief one to two sentence synopsis. Likewise with kinfolk, I don't see players who want to identify any outside the immediate family of their character. If a player picks a Fetish, it is a generic utilitarian fetish - despite the fact it should have a very well known history and lore behind it.

            Most players simply want to handwave those details. But whenever one puts effort into detailing something, it almost always adds greatly to the game because those individual touches become an important part of that chronicle's lore. Far more important than detailed work the ST does because it is always directly relevant to the PCs in a way the ST's NPCs, items, and events may not be.

            I generally ask my players if they have Backgrounds that involve NPCs, that they put some effort into detailing them. So if they have Allies, Contacts, and Kinfolk, who are they? If they have Pure Breed, provide some details on that lineage. If they have Ancestors, who are some of their ancestors they contact? I even ask them to put down what spirit taught them each of their Gifts. Stuff like that fills out the game world nicely as you noted.

                            In a table top game this is fine since the ST populates the entire rest of the world and can make it look like it is in canon (or their version of it).

                            In online games and big LARPs, being laissez faire on character creation will quickly get you a chronicle of homid Red Talons, at least five White Howlers, a Croatan, Bone Gnawers who are really lost Silver Fang princesses, a redeemed Black Spiral Dancer, and twenty lupus Stargazers as your standard sept. Many PCs want their character to be special, but most players hate playing in a game where everyone is a unique snowflake (regardless of whether their character is). That's why most of those games put various restrictions in place or caps on certain concepts in an attempt to have their chronicle vaguely resemble people's expectations (how it is described in canon).

                            In practice this has middling results because STs have to balance keeping a lid on ridiculous things and keeping an active player base.

                            But it is a different dynamic STing a small table top game with a closed playing group and an open online or LARP chronicle with lots of players, many of whom can be brand new. So the ST style needs to be very different to accommodate those differences.

                            But I agree it was particularly weird to read in actual White Wolf published material that "you are doing it wrong!". It would have been far better to just ignore the weird stuff players did, and simply publish a very good setting book showing how it is supposed to be done. Instead, they just kept introducing more and more weird stuff and then complained when playing groups actually used those concepts.

                                                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                        Then you tell them they should form a Lodge.
                                                        Nope, you tell them to play another tribe. Crafting a white fly is the most abused and trite narrative trick to craft a "different" character, because it's so easy that everyone can think about it. Make a Get of Fenris and have him not acting like a GoF, here's a cool character for you! It's such a cliché that from a literary standpoint it marks the author as "bad" just for using it, and I discourage my players every time they try to create this kind of character. If you want to play a pacifist then you play a Child of Gaia, not a Fenris.
                                                        Of course, some storyteller may feel perfectly fine with white flies, especially when they raise the banner of some particular cause they believe in (like equalitarists Silver Fangs). But it's your campaign, so if it doesn't bother you or your players I see no issue in playing it however you like. In a tabletop game isn't even that harmful, since it's 4 players against a world of stereotypes.

                                                        Thing is, though, that this kind of "versus" character is horribly harmful in a multiplayer online game. Partly because they break the game lore (potentially ruining the setting to other players), but also because they are the kind of character that lives on the shoulder of the others. When your toon's personality is to be against the status quo then he doesn't have a personality, he's just leeching some from the action of others by disrupting their gameplay.
                                                        This may lead to some fun when controlled: the single thief in a city can offer a lot. But when half of the city is made by thieves they become harmful to the game world.
