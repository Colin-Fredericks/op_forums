    W20: How to assert oneself as a comparatively "weak" character in a sept?

    04-19-2021, 02:33 AM
    Hello everybody,

    I come to you again asking for a bit of advice.

    My playgroup plays WTA now for approximately half a year. More or less one session per week (exceptions happen of course due to personal matters).

    I play my second character now after my first one died due to very unfortunate circumstances and bad rolls. Shit happens, lets move on.

    Now I play the packs theurge, and one of only three Theurges left in the Sept after an attempt for a coup. One of whom is an Elder, the other one Keeper of the Land.

    The one thing that I noticed during our half year-playtime is that the "fighters" always seem to reign surpreme or have more leeway in many situations. As much in combat as in social situation. For the later they just threaten to challenge your character to combat if they dont get their way or do the "stare down". Garou Society can be very martial and without compromise, as I understand it. I absolutely accept that from a player perspective.

    Since my character is not exactly a great combatant (for a Garou), he had to back out of many arguments. As a Metis he cant expect to reliably be tendet to afterwards if "mistakes" happen. Fortunetly those conflicts never happened in the pack, they are all super dandy with each other, but with other sept members of the same societal Rank or one above.

    So I come from a VTM-focused Background where my characters always had an easier time to outmaneuver potential rivals through non-combative means. In our WTA Game I can't think of many other means to be honest. Probably because I am not that knowlegable about WTA as I am about VTM. I considered leveraging his position as one of the few Theurges in the Sept. Asking Spirits for help with other sept-members seems petty to him from an in-character perspective. And too dangerous. He had a very bad experience with an air elemental that summoned a Typhoon, which heavily backfired and almost killed the pack.

    I considered for him to ask for other challenges than combat. As I understand it challenges must be appropriate for the capabilities of challenger and challenged.

    Of course I reflected on if my character was unintentionally looking for trouble. Keeping your head low as a Metis always seems to be a good option but arguments happen with a fuse as short as that of many Garou. My characters included.

    So long story short. Do you have some advice for me to better portray my character in these situations? Reading advice for sourcebooks is also very welcome, though I try to limit myself to W20 books. Since the situation is strictly in-character I did not want to talk with my ST about this yet.

    I hope this topic is not discussed to death on these forums. During my internet seach I only found threads from STs asking how to "handle" "overpowered" player characters which does not fit for this situation.

      Garou have a martial culture, and combat skills and physical prowess are always going to be prized. In most tribes (though not all), the higher rage auspices will tend to be the leaders. But there's always an important role played by other character types because the Garou aren't just fighters. They need people to contribute at all levels of society. Spiritualism, art, law, history, etc. are all vital.

      I don't know all the situations you are facing so it is hard to comment. If it is stuff that affects combat, fighting, defense, etc., then I'm not surprised the challenges are oriented towards physical combat. But a good Master of Challenge should be able to identify the nature of the dispute and determine a relevant challenge. And there should be a lot of disputes where it is more important to win a riddle contest, recite old lore, create a new epic poem, craft a useful fetish, or something else that should play to the strength of your character and others who have other skills than combat.

      It's also important to know who is doing the challenging. Typically the person being challenged determines the nature of the challenge. So it should never be the case of a bully going to up characters and taking away their stuff because he knows in advance he'll win any dispute.

      In terms of packs and septs, there should be ways known in advance of how to settle certain disputes, heavily based on pack and sept culture, which in turn should be based heavily on pack and caern totems, pack quests, and other culture. A pack under a totem of war will likely decide pack leadership challenges and other disputes in a martial fashion. But a pack under Unicorn or Chimera or Stag should have very different challenges to settle those same questions, because the totem (and likely the pack quest) makes other demands. Sept culture is likely heavily based on the caern totem and the type of caern. A caern of sacrifice with a caern totem of Fenris will likely require a lot of bloodshed, physical pain, combat, and the like. A caern of Gnosis with a caern totem of Owl would be very different. They would employ different approaches to the exact same challenges.

      The issue is whether the challenge is appropriate to the issue being decided. If it's not, the setting probably isn't being properly presented.

      So is the ST showing this? Or is the ST showing a much more constrained, and perhaps cookie cutter approach, to these issues? If you think the ST isn't giving enough thought to these other aspects of Garou culture or avenues of advancement, speak to the ST. And maybe your PC will speak about these things at the next sept moot. Talk with NPCs who are in a similar position than you - Theurges, Ragabash, and the less martially inclined members of the other auspices. Build a political coalition to make changes in the sept, if the ST will allow that.

      Another thing is to look at the Renown chart to see what it is that Garou value. There are lots of other things that earn Renown than just combat. Does the ST look at that as being equally valid? Is your character contributing to the sept in ways that generate Renown? Vampire is very individually based - what is in it for me? Werewolf is much more community based - how are you helping the society you are in?

      Another issue is the concept of chiminage. By doing favors for others, you accrue debt just like boons in vampire. You just need to be able to perform something other people need so they will approach you for it. Once you are useful, NPCs should be less likely wanting to anger you because you can cut off the supply of goodies.

      I do not know the specifics of your situation, but if I was a Theurge belonging to a sept and wanted to get recognition within it, become a leader by gaining followers, and becoming a respected voice in the sept, here are some things I might do:

      1) Crafts Talens for the benefit of the sept leadership. The Sept Elders likely need talens like Bane Arrows, Gaia's Breath, Nightshade, Wyrm Scales, and others on an ongoing basis in order to protect the sept and fulfill sept needs.
      2) Likewise other packs might need them as well, which can be exchanged in terms of chiminage. This might include use of other mystic rites or rites in general, like the rite of talisman dedication. There are lots of useful services a Theurge should be able to provide.
      3) Make friends with spirits. You should have Spirit Speech and lots of other useful Gifts and rites. Treat spirits like real NPCs. Get to know the names they are known by, find out what things they need done, what they like and don't like. You can build Spirit Networks. You can gain favor of spirits. And you can tell spirits who are the Garou they should like or not like based on their reputation. If you have a good reputation with spirits, they should want to be nice to you and do you favors for any help you provided them. And the people you don't like will become the people they don't like. That will impact other Garou's ability to learn Gifts, create Talens or Fetishes, or get help in the Umbra.
      4) Become a subject matter expert. Learn the lore of the setting, whether that be actual history of the setting, or simply a lot of things that often gets handwaved in game but represents real knowledge NPCs respect and want. Once you are recognized as that expert, people have to go to you if they want to use that. They need to offer chiminage in exchange, even if that ends up only being respect, tokens of gratitude, and the like.
      5) Look for opportunities to resolve conflicts through non-violence. There are advantages of doing so for preserving the Veil, protecting kinfolk, learning lore that can help you defeat certain Wyrm creatures. That builds the case of resolving internal Garou disputes through non-violent means like riddle contests, storytelling, and other means of problem solving.
      6) Look for things the sept needs, but doesn't have whether that be knowledge of certain rites, locations of Wyrm minions, protection of the local kinfolk community, or whatever.

      A lot of these things depend on a good ST. But even if your ST isn't currently thinking of these things, the ST can still be open to changing things once he understands how they can add to the game.

      In short, find allies, build coalitions, make yourself useful, and leverage your strengths. A metis theurge might never become sept leader, but can still gain a lot of informal authority or even formal leadership in the sept.

      Just keep in mind some of your problems might because the ST is purposefully doing things to undermine you because the NPCs would do that to a metis.

      I would start with having an OOC conversation with the ST and let her know that you are looking for ways for your Theurge to have more impact and gain followers in the sept, and you wanted to share some ideas. See what she says. Then build on that OOC conversation until you know how to proceed IC.

          To expand on the above with something from the books:

          There are three primary types of challenges in Garou society; combat, facedowns, and gamecraft. Those that challenge Ragabash and Theurges best be ready for a challenge of wits and cunning over force because gamecraft heavily favors mental focused characters (and the Enigmas Knowledge for a traditional riddle-contest, but maybe other things if as Black Fox noted the game is less traditional).

          With it being traditional for the challenged to set the terms instead of the challenger, that alone level the playing field a lot. A lot of Septs and Tribes favor gamecraft challenges because it's the only one that doesn't risk violence (facedowns can result in Frenzies since Rage might be rolled instead of normal skills), and even if fairly hardcore strength focused groups it shouldn't lose face to have a gamecraft challenge over an intellectual dispute as a bare minimum. In a direct challenge, as opposed to a challenge petitioned to the Sept via the Master of the Challenge, Garou don't, at all, feel the challenged needs to be nice to the challenger; esp. if the challenge is bullying or petty. Ahrouns trying to force combat challenges are just brutes, not warriors, and need to prove the strength of their minds after all.

          The other thing to remember is that you don't need to challenge to get your way. Politics are part of Garou life, and getting the higher Ranked Garou of the Sept on your side is a much more potent tool. A lot of this if covered above.

                      Throwing punches between packs at the drop of the hat is... kinda extreme even by Garou standards. Even more so if the Sept is at a Caern of Healing. While some tussling, snarling, and shoving around is to be expected, if it's regularly getting to the point of Frenzies (remember that Frenzies are a Renown loss, which many Cliath's can't afford) means the higher Ranked Garou aren't doing their jobs of keeping the youngsters in line.

                      And while you are correct that during the challenge both parties stand alone, having friends in high places can help in a few ways:

                      1) Stepping in and setting the terms of the challenge (esp. Philodox and the Master of the Challenge) if the nature of the challenge would render it "unjust," which sufficiently high Ranked Garou are within their rights to do and give characters they like an edge.

                      2) Picking a winner anyway. If an Elder walks up to some bickering Cliaths and demands to know what they're squabbling about, the Cliaths better answer, and the Elder can order one side to concede to the other; anyone on the losing side has to now challenge an Elder to get their way. An Elder tried of violence breaking out between packs because the Cliaths are too on edge can add on all sorts of things, like demanding the losing party perform the Rite of Contrition within a month or face punishments for disobeying orders. Etc. One of the reasons a lot of Garou don't like the Silver Fangs, is that they're extremely talented at using this for a sort of nepotism to control power.

                      3) Less extreme, the higher Ranks can still step in after the lower Ranks settle things, and for them to focus on cleaning up the messes they made to discourage this sort of conflict. Packs that don't keep their more hot tempered members in check get stuck with all the crappy jobs around the Sept, leaving the good jobs (esp. the Renown earning jobs) to packs that make sure to impress the Sept office holders.
