    Rank 4 Children of Gaia Gifts

    Beast Life: Again?! Still unrated, see Lupus. For Children of Gaia this fits into a peaceful master of nature vibe, great, but the Gift needs work.

    Serenity: Spend a Gnosis, roll Charisma + Expression (difficulty of target's Willpower), and you block the target from spending Rage, frenzying, and make sure they fail all Rage rolls for the duration. What's annoying is that this Gift overlaps a lot with Calm The Savage Beast, which Children of Gaia already get access to. This costs Gnosis, while Calm costs Willpower. The difficulties vary as well. Overall while Calm can stop a frenzy, and doesn't block it from recurring, this Gift can prevent a frenzy, and stop it from happening for a while. It's a VERY small improvement on Calm, and it should have been written as an upgraded power which improves Calm the Savage Beast.

    My rewrite would be that Serenity has Calm the Savage Beast as a prerequisite, and simply improves upon the prior mechanics, allowing you to spend Gnosis on a success to block frenzy for the whole scene.

    One of the saving graces of this Gift is that it blocks a Remain Active test, which makes it Amber. An even more potent application of the Gift, however, is against Spirits. "Fail All Rage Rolls" is a BUSTED effect against any spirit. That's an automatic fail to all damage rolls. That's an automatic fail to any strength-like roll. A Spirit hit with Serenity becomes, RAW, as weak as a kitten. If this effect works at your table against spirits, after all the power doesn't specify, it would be Green, and potentially overpowered given that it could, strictly RAW, make a hugely powerful spirit become powerless. That said, a Spirit which goes beyond normal limits of WP/Rage would typically be very, VERY hard to use this Gift on, due to a Willpower of 10+.

    Strike the Air: Spending a Willpower and succeeding on a Wits + Athletics test (difficulty of the opponent's Willpower) makes you unhittable, so long as you don't attack, and no one attacks for you. This Gift makes you 100% unkillable against a single foe, and you can use it repeatedly to make yourself resistant to a whole pack of enemies.

    If the enemy is smart enough to figure out that you are not just dodging really well, but rather that you are impossible to hit, then they will likely adjust their plan accordingly. But there is basically nothing they can do to stop you from leaping around fulfilling your objective. The really nasty thing about this Gift is that a Child of Gaia could make themselves unhittable for a Garou, and then attack all of the Garou's packmates or Kinfolk, while they are unable to harm the Child due to this Gift. Nothing in the Gift blocks you from attacking things OTHER than the target of the Gift, mind. You could destroy objects, allies, steal things they need and so on.

    The sheer absoluteness of this one is very handy, and it's a pretty great Gift all in all. This hits the spot for where high level Gifts ought to be. Using this in a Challenge is also very likely to give you a distinct edge, or at the very least force a (dishonourable?) stalemate in a duel.

    Uncaught Since the Primal Morn: The flavour of this one is great, and the lore it hints at is fun. With this Gift you can, in theory, outrun any pursuer, making yourself impossible to catch. The problem is the roll; you roll Stamina + Athletics, against the highest Stamina + Athletics of any of your pursuers. If you drop this when your enemies are still in Homid this should be very doable. If your enemies are in Crinos this power becomes very hard to use. It also lasts for one scene per success, which is pretty generous.

    Needing to run is already a bit niche. Needing to run, alone, is more niche. This power is cool and all, but unless you're on your own it means abandoning your Pack, unless you have a homid or two clinging onto your Crinos for dear life.

    Theoretically you could use this Gift, along with a Mage or Changeling ally, in order to travel at a speed faster than light (you just need to be chased by something moving at the speed of light, which is achievable with some Sphere/Unleashing nonsense) and break through some unbreakable barrier or kill some powerful foe, but the problem with that is that you'll likely be reduced to slurry, or perhaps down to your composite atoms. Very unlikely to matter, but funny to picture.

    Given that this power is hard to use against something genuinely dangerous (a powerful spirit, a Crinos-form pursuer) and you run alone, I'd ultimately say that this one isn't good enough. This one is a niche of a niche, and I don't really put much value in being able to escape alive when your whole pack is torn apart; you should be investing XP to avoid these situations, not to escape them when you've lost. Even if an encounter is meant to be very hard and the chance of losing is very high, fleeing in Lupus is a great basekit. If you can't figure out a fun way to escape, that is likely a skill issue.

                Rank 4 Fianna Gifts

                Balor's Gaze: The most interesting thing about this Gift is its name. Balor was a Fomorian of immense power who once defeated the Tuatha De Danann in a great war, took a Tuathan bride, and whose grandson killed him. He had one eye, and anyone he gazed upon died. What ancient pacts with the Fae make this Gift possible? Is it simply named for Balor due to the human legend, or due to Garou links with the true Fae war? Does this Gift reek of the Fomorian Dream to Changelings?

                Anyways, I'm pretty bored of paralysis powers at this point, so a power which inflicts lasting agony is a good step in the right direction. This power costs 1 Rage and 1 Gnosis (you can spend both at once thanks to the rules of W20, though you can't spend any more Rage or Gnosis for the rest of the turn of course) and requires a difficulty 8 Perception + Occult test.

                FOR THE REST OF THE SCENE if you glare at someone, they suffer intense agony unless they beat your successes in a Difficulty 8 Willpower test. If they don't EXCEED your successes, they are struck with a -5 die penalty from agony for the scene, and if they're already Crippled they become Incapacitated. It's unclear whether the other detriments of being Crippled (movement impacts) apply or not.

                It's worth noting that this lasts for the scene and while healing and regeneration normally undo wound penalties by removing the wounds, this is wound-agnostic. Targets who ignore wound penalties (via the Rank 1 Gift) or who benefit from pain (BSDs, Fomori and some rare Vampires and other supernaturals etc. do this) may be bad targets for this...

                All in all, this is what I'm expecting at Rank 4. This Gift can be set up once, either right before a battle or right as it starts, and you can continue to debuff enemies for a whole scene with it simply by glaring at them. The cost is a bit intense at 1R 1G, and taking a turn to set it up isn't great, but across a fight it could inflict a lot of harm on foes. It's also non-lethal and a great way to torture foes reversibly.

                That said, unfortunately, the fact that any being in the WoD can, RAW, ignore wound penalties by spending a point of WP makes this... a little sad. Spirits can't, sure, as they can't spend WP. But everyone else will spend WP to ignore the penalty when it counts. In a longer fight this is a great way to burn a lot of enemy WP, and the power isn't bad, it's worth grabbing if you're a Fianna, but it's ultimately sitting where most Gifts should be; it's good, it helps, but it isn't a game-changer.

                Phantasm: This Gift lets you make an immobile and fully immersive illusion, including olfactory elements, sound, touch and so on. Since it impacts smell, presumably taste also applies. The cost is high, 1 Gnosis per 3m radius, but the fact that you can make a fully believable tableau is great. You could leave behind a phantasmal corpse of yourself in your Breed Form, you could create fake evidence against someone to make them slip up and admit their crimes, you could frame someone, you could hide a trap beneath false ground.

                All in all I'd call this Gift perfectly balanced; it's Green if you are a genius, and Amber otherwise. Generally speaking people won't be breaking through this illusion since they only roll their Perception + Alertness to see through it if they are given reason to do so. Being "given reason" might mean, for example, noticing that there are no flies around an old body, or that a body doesn't seem to be getting any older, or seeing tracks leading away from a corpse which match the paws/boots of the corpse. So if you take time to come up with something convincing, it's unlikely anyone will even roll to see through it.

                If immobile simply means the illusion is static (no roaring fire, no buzzing flies) then that's fine. If immobile means the illusion can't be moved around (the fake corpse is stuck on the floor) then any deeper examination will reveal it to be fake, even if the illusion isn't beaten. It would help if there were some suggestion that the Gift is psychoactive and convinces people under its spell that what they're seeing is real, but as it stands it seems to be a fully sensory illusion.

                =======================================

                Only two Gifts at Rank 4 - bit sad for the Fianna! Personally I'd give them another Gift at Rank 4 which relates to plants, or something from the Galliard list.

                    Rank 4 Get of Fenris Gifts

                    Body Shift: Still bad, see Homid list. If this were better it would really complement the heavy war focus of the Get.

                    Heart of the Mountain: Spend a Rage, Spend a WP, for the rest of the scene you can't fail any task involving Stamina. You'll never be broken by torturers, you'll never die from drowning, you'll never tire of marching, you'll never fall asleep. This is a pretty great Gift! It also guarantees that you will always soak 1 point of damage. From what I can see this overrides a source of damage being unsoakable, so if you were taking 1 point of unsoakable damage every turn, this Gift would nullify the damage. If you were taking 3 points of unsoakable damage, you'd take 2.

                    All in all this is a pretty great Gift. It's niche, and it comes down to ST judgement on a lot of things, but it makes you outright immune to a lot of non-damaging threats and it helps to somewhat reduce the damage you take, especially if the damage normally can't be soaked. All in all, given that you don't have to roll to activate it and it costs Rage rather than Gnosis, I'd call this a Green. Basically the amount of stuff this lets you fully or mostly sidestep (elite pentex knockout gas, having to run over puddles of molten silver etc.) is good enough that it's worth Green. This is a power which lets you overcome stuff designed to take out Garou, which is pretty handy.

                    Hero's Stand: Roll WP. Each success grants +1 to all physical pools, which is pretty great. No cost. You're stuck in one place, unable to move from that spot. You cannot be surprised, all attacks count as frontal (so no benefits to trying to backstab etc.) and with that bonus to your dice pools you'll fight well.

                    That said... an intelligent foe may well keep their distance and just pelt you with ranged attacks. Nothing stopping you from returning fire with Gifts or with ranged weapons, but still, the 'stuck in one place' restriction is big.

                    This gets a solid Amber. It's worth grabbing, it's handy, it has some handy benefits, but you could definitely live without it. The ideal situation is rolling insanely well and getting +3 to all your physical dice pools - +3 to hit, to dodge, to soak, to damage, ain't bad at all. Though at Rank 4 that isn't a game-changer on its own.

                    Scream of Gaia*: See Lupus list. Lots of repeats at this Rank!

                        Rank 4 Glass Walker Gifts

                        Attunement: See Bone Gnawer list. Suits the GWs perfectly.

                        Doppleganger: A perfect disguise to match any human, wolf or Garou is pretty great! The cost and the roll are fine, and a duration of 1 day per success is perfect. Since traits aren't copied, the best way to watch out for a doppleganger is with codes, a secret language, testing Gifts/Knowledges/Skills etc.

                        Using this to make yourself look like an Elder, a packmate, an enemy, a helpless ally such as a vulnerable kinfolk, letting yourself get kidnapped and then going Crinos once you're back at base... the possible uses are endless.

                        Signal Rider: Costs 1 Gnosis and 1 WP, and only requires a roll if you use a computerized method of transfer such as a video call. The range is 8km per dot of Gnosis you have, so 40km with Gnosis 5, or 80km with a Gnosis of 10!

                        You instantly arrive at your destination; so you can leap out of the pocket of a pentex rep if you have their mobile number. I don't think I need to get into why this is great. A GW pack can leap across a city near-effortlessly with a network of telephones, only restricted by running out of Gnosis.

                        Tech Speak: Often useless, and clunky when it's useful. If you have access to a piece of technology, there is a good chance you have access to a phone or computer. But, you might be out of signal, sure. "Often useless" means that often, this Gift has absolutely no use at all. You just use your mobile phone. But you don't have it, or it doesn't have signal or battery.

                        So you need this Gift. Now you have to spend Gnosis (which you could use to teleport with Signal Rider, immediately above this Gift!) and make a potentially very difficult Charisma + Science test. Difficulty 4-9 depending on distance of the receiver of the message. If there are communications devices near the person you want to contact, they print/show/shout the message. More successes means a longer message. This could be really handy, don't get me wrong, but again, at this rank you can teleport. You are also a rank 4 Garou. You can bash down a wall or go Lupus and run away.

                        Yes, yes, subtlety are important. Being able to send a text without touching your phone is great! But this feels like it could even be a Rank 1-2 Gift, not Rank 4. Again, at this Rank we're teleporting using phones, so sending a text using a door alarm feels a bit... lacking. Amber purely because having this Gift will almost inevitably be useful at SOME point, but I don't have to like it. The fact that 1 success sends only a SINGLE word is also horrible and feels like a real 'fuck you' to someone who wasted 12xp and a point of Gnosis to get there!

                                    Rank 4 Red Talon Gifts

                                    First we have to deal with Gorge. I like this one enough that I'm annoyed the Red Talons of all Tribes get it - I wish it were a Lupus Gift! Since it's more like three different Gifts, best to rank all three separately.

                                    Gorge (Rage): Being able to hold 13 points of temporary Rage instead of 10 feels like a side note. Holding 10 points of Rage is already a bad idea, holding 13 isn't WORSE per se, but it's still a bad idea. If the Gift clarified that the bonus capacity didn't impact Frenzy tests, it'd be handy... ish? For example if an Ahroun could carry 8 Rage at the start of a session instead of 5, without increasing their Rage pool for the purposes of Frenzy tests, that'd be nice? The problem is that Rage is cheap, Rage is easily regained, and there are many Gifts which grant Rage.

                                    Gorge (Gnosis)*: Having capacity for more Gnosis is pretty good, since there are times where it's easy to fill up (spiritual hunt, access to a Caern) and times where it's almost impossible. This Gift is as good as your best use for Gnosis is. It also counters some of the difficulty of carrying Silver; if your normal Gnosis is 5, and you're carrying a silver object, you'll have 7 points of Gnosis to spend instead of 4.

                                    This could be Amber or Green depending on your best uses for Gnosis. If you can heal Aggravated damage with every point of Gnosis you have, it's amazing. If your best use for Gnosis is a meh Gift, it's meh.

                                    Gorge (Willpower): Willpower is important, and being able to carry +3 points is great. For example say you have a WP of 7, being able to carry 10 temporary points instead of 7 is amazing. This has niche interactions with powers which target your temporary Willpower instead of your permanent Willpower (such as Dominate powers and some other supernatural abilities).

                                    This power lets you pay 12xp to gain +3 capacity without getting the benefits of +3 dots. For 12xp you could increase your actual Willpower by 1 dot, or more if it's very low. But the important thing is that increasing WP is hard to justify and most STs won't let you have a WP of 9-10, meanwhile this Gift is easily justified, at least if it's in-tribe for you. All in all I'd call this Green, so long as you aren't spending xp which would be better spent on actual Willpower!

                                    Keep in mind that spending WP is incredible and can get a lot of great outcomes. It also lets you ignore wound penalties and overcome your instincts and terrible impulses, as well as interacting with a lot of supernatural abilities (e.g. stunned if you don't spend WP).

                                    Howl of Death: This Gift has no cost, and can target anyone you can see and who can hear you. The damage is lethal, which is a bit weak for Werewolf, but doesn't really matter against Spirits, who treat all damage types the same. It also only matters against Garou if you care about regeneration, which won't always matter. This is a pretty neat ranged attacking action which could make a group of Red Talons absolutely terrifying (by WTA standards) as you get absolutely shredded from a ridge a mile away by a howling pack.

                                    Worth noting that this Gift does LEVELS of damage rather than dice. It's soakable, and it's Lethal, sure, but this is a neat power. Is it dodgeable? I'd assume so, but most targets won't try to dodge the first attack since it won't seem like an attack.

                                    All in all I'd call this Green since it has great advantages over some other ranged options. For a Red Talon Galliard this could become their go-to attacking power. Naturally this won't be a good attacking option unless you have a dice pool to back it up!

                                    Quicksand: Spend a Gnosis, make a check, turn an area to quicksand. You and your pack move through it normally. Anyone else is slowed, takes +1 difficulty to combat tests and can't make combat maneuvers which require overland movement (like a charge, presumably) at all. This power is great for tying down a group of enemies, reducing their damage output (less likely to hit) and increasing the damage they take (they either can't dodge at all or attempt it at +1 difficulty).

                                    This power is pretty handy; the Gnosis cost and it being Rank 4 means it's bound to be Amber, but it's worth grabbing. The possible clever uses of this power are also pretty broad, but would rely on the ST's take on it. For example, it seems to me that using this below a pillar of a building would make that pillar structurally unsafe, but how that plays out would be down to the ST.

                                                    Rank 4 Shadow Lords Gifts

                                                    Open Wounds: Spend a Gnosis, Strength + Medicine difficulty 7. On your next attack, and note that it says attack, not melee attack, not unarmed attack, you cause the target to bleed continuously. They take 1 level of unsoakable Lethal damage each turn, for a number of turns equal to successes. A Crinos form Garou preparing to ambush a target could very easily activate this and roll a great many successes; even with a Strength of 1 and a Medicine of 1, this Gift could still be very handy if you took to Crinos before rolling.

                                                    But let's say you get 10 successes. How good is 10 turns of an enemy taking 1 point of lethal damage each turn? Well, that really depends! If you're hitting them with a sniper round from 10 miles away and then leaving the scene, this might help to 100% guarantee that they will die. Let's say that you're fighting a powerful ancient vampire; 1 point of lethal every turn is actually quite bad for them! Let's say you're punching someone, and then watching smugly as they bleed out from the sudden gush of blood that follows their lost tooth.

                                                    This power has a marginal benefit in combat (DOT against a foe, especially a foe who lacks regeneration, or whose regeneration is already 'busy' or disabled) which would make it Amber on its own. The more clever uses, like killing someone with an attack which really should never have hurt them at all (beyond 1 bashing), is great. All in all I'd call this a solid Green, I couldn't imagine hitting rank 4 on a Shadow Lord and not taking this!

                                                    Durance*: When using a Talen, spend a Gnosis, and keep the Talen instead of its spirit escaping. This is a really great ability which would let you use a rare or irreplaceable Talen in perpetuity. This is very hard to rate since it's as good or as weak as your best Talen option. Unfortunately it doesn't undrink potions or unexplode grenades, but for Talens which aren't inherently wasted in their using this is great.

                                                    The problem is that many, MANY Talens are consumables, or ammunition, or would reasonably be broken or lost in their usage. So this is good to get endless use out of a really good Talen which sticks around, like an Easy Money Credit Card for instance, which essentially would give you unlimited cash (though never spending more than your credit limit in a single scene).

                                                    Easiest to simply say that this is Amber, with the * potential to be Green. If you have the right Talens which could be great, but that's so table-dependent that it feels a bit silly to get into a list here.

                                                    Strength of the Dominator: Wits + Intimidation, difficulty of the target's Willpower. For one turn per success they will lose one Rage, and that Rage will go to the Shadow Lord. No cost, and it can only effect a single target once per scene. Noteworthy that you could use this Gift on a whole pack of enemies from hiding in the run-up to a fight, and then blitz them with an insane number of attacks each turn, however this would be dependent on your usual maximum Rage. An Ahroun with a Rage of 7 might well benefit greatly from this Rage income each turn, making 4 bonus attacks each round... but then again, at Rank 4 you have a lot of Gift options to generate Rage.

                                                    The unique aspect of stealing Rage is great, but this sets up a slow siphon. It's handy, hell it could be absolutely incredible in some situations, but most of the time it's just a neat option to have. Worth noting that a pack of Shadow Lords could completely rob a Garou of all their Rage with this. Also important to keep in mind that the potential targets are limited. A tiny number of Fomori, all Garou, most Fera, some Kami, and that's it. If you're constantly fighting BSDs and Fera, great, if you aren't this is less handy.

                                                    There is a side effect of being able to siphon Rage out of a rampaging cub who might not know how to burn it, and you could use this on a Frenzying Garou to rob them of their Rage and slow down their rampage (fewer bonus actions)... but at Rank 4 you probably have other, better options for this, including Direct the Storm, which lets you outright choose what a Frenzying Garou does.

                                                        Blah, just inspiring me to go on tangents regarding the issues with the rest of the game these powers have to interact with. One thing I've noticed with powers like Durance (it's not actually the first time WtA has published something for this purpose) is that it just encourages players to make custom Talens that don't get consumed by being used. WtA's item crafting rules have always run mostly on "feels right" than any sort of mechanical rigor. I don't think it's bad that the rules for this are fairly loose, but they wouldn't sacrifice anything valuable if they had more functional guidelines. Something as simple as "Talens with reusable physical vessels sacrifice a bit of potency for the ability to rebind spirits into them, while Talens that destroy the physical vessel get some extra oomph for it," would reduce the variability of the value by taable of a Gift like Durance significantly. The practical value to any given character would still be hit or miss (largely based on personal crafting ability or access to skilled Garou or Kin artisans), but it's at least a lot easier to see why characters would consider Durance if they aren't focused on making Talens on their own and thus want to avoid running out of useful ones.

                                                            Rank 4 Silent Strider Gifts

                                                            Attunement: See the Bone Gnawer list. Silent Striders may choose cities or wilderness when they learn the Gift, both of which are great. Ideally you'd learn both, though the RAW doesn't explicitly permit this. Presumably the 'wilderness' version is still taught by a rat-spirit, but I would be open to making it practically any Naturae or animal-spirit which is comfortable adapting to lots of different kinds of wilderness.

                                                            Black Mark: After hitting with claws, but before inflicting damage, you can spend a point of Gnosis. For every level of damage you wind up inflicting the target becomes haunted for a day. So if you hit someone for 6 Aggravated damage, they'll be haunted for 6 days.

                                                            Here's the thing. If you're clawing at someone, you should be aiming to kill them. You achieve that by spending Rage to hit a bunch of extra attacks in this one turn. On the off-chance someone is just about to escape and you have time to land a single attack... what does this even do?

                                                            The Pentex rep escapes with a nasty black wound which draws in spectres for 48 hours. What does that MEAN for him? No NPC is going to be murdered by ghosts offscreen, and it's very unlikely that them being harried by ghosts is going to play into the events of the chronicle. If your ST is very Wraith-savvy this might help you to track down the target, guided by a savvy detective Wraith who has heard tell of this Quick fella who has been drawing in lots of vengeful ghosts. But you're a Rank 4 Garou. It's likely that you can track down targets with a variety of Gifts.

                                                            The existence of Mark of the Death-Wolf means that this Gift is somewhat redundant, all things considered. If you are fighting, fight to kill. If you aren't fighting, curse people with the Mark. This is a strange hybrid power which only helps in strange situations, and feels like it would be a trap most of the time, where you throw away a chance to kill a target (spending Rage for bonus actions) to instead mess with them.

                                                            Dam the Heartflood*: Inherently niched, but that's somewhat okay; you wouldn't buy this unless you were in the right Chronicle, and a Rank 4 Silent Strider with a sensible ST will be running into Vampires and Ghouls. This power, when it works, totally blocks all magics related to blood. This presumably doesn't just block Blood Sorcery (Thaumaturgy, Dur-An-Ki, Setite Sorcery, Koldunism) and should include Disciplines, which from a WTA perspective feel like "blood magic", but ask your ST. There is a bonus effect which blocks all blood expenditure. The duration is 1 turn per success on a Manipulation + Occult, difficulty of the target's Willpower, and it costs 1 Gnosis.

                                                            Is it worth using this? Well if you're up against a powerful Vampire, Ghoul or Ananasi... maybe? RAW there is nothing protecting an Antedeluvian from this power, so there's that! When this power is relevant it could be useful, but it should be saved for hitting targets you can't simply kill in one round, and at Rank 4 you should be able to kill most Vampires in one round. Honestly this power should be able to hit a whole COTERIE of Vampires at Rank 4, at which point it would have a great use at the start of a pack-vs-coterie fight, but still. I'll call this Amber, assuming Vampires and blood-related magics are relevant to your story.

                                                            Speed Beyond Thought: Boring, irrelevant, niche and overly restricted.

                                                            This Gift lets you run at 10x speed for 8 hours, concentrating on doing nothing but running. When you finish running you have to eat immediately or Frenzy. To top it all off it costs Gnosis and there is a roll required, which feels entirely pointless. You'll probably pass, but still, you could fail; it's difficulty 7. At Rank 4, as a Silent Strider, you probably have better transport options, including GROUP transport options. If this let you bring along your whole pack it might be okay. It would still be pretty meh.

                                                            You'll only get use out of this because you bought it and the ST wrote in a situation where it could shine. Pass.
