    RANK 1 GET OF FENRIS GIFTS

    Get of Fenris get off to an alright start, but lack unique Gifts - again we have a Tribe which is similar-ish to an Auspice, but unlike the Fianna there isn't a problem of role/identity being stolen. A Get of Fenris Philodox is unusually skilled as a warrior for their Auspice, but they don't steal anything critical to the Ahroun's role, or get anything that totally clowns on the Ahroun regardless of Auspice.

    Lightning Reflexes: I originally rated this Gift a Red as it interferes with rules basically no-one uses. That said, let's get into the details of what it does, assuming the ST is playing the initiative system 100% RAW.

    Normally you have to roll Willpower (difficulty 6) or spend a point of Willpower to abort an action. Everyone goes from lowest initiative to highest, declaring their actions. If you're last in the fight you might declare an action of "claw that guy!", only to find that before it gets to you someone is trying to claw you instead, and whoops, looks like if they hit you'll be taking 13 dice of Aggravated. If you would rather dodge or block, you need to roll or spend Willpower to abort.

    This Gift, which you can activate as a reflex, lets you spend a point of Willpower and for the rest of the day you can abort an action to switch to defense for free, no roll, no expenditure.

    How good is this? Well, if you aren't going to use any Rage actions this could be very good. Say you're a Rage 1 Get Ragabash; you don't have the luxury of going "I'm going to use my main action to defend, and all of my Rage actions will be attacks". This Gift lets you take the risk of going "I claw on my turn" and then if no one attacks you you're going to claw, if someone attacks you then you get to dodge instead. Given that everyone faster than you gets to see what you're going to do (usually) this could help a lot.

    But a difficulty 6 Willpower is pretty easy to pass. So this Gift is at its best for a low Willpower character and a low Rage character. I can't identify with either of those especially, but that's personal preference!

    In an ideal situation of playing a Willpower 7+ Ahroun, for example, the Gift's advantage is that instead of going "I'm going to dodge for my main action and attack 3 times with my bonus Rage actions" you can instead go "claw for main action, 3 more attacks for Rage actions". If no one happens to attack you for the whole round then you have the advantage of a bonus attack! And a 1 Willpower cost for the whole day is... good. So even if you obsolete this Gift mostly with great Willpower and Rage, I'd say that it's still useful enough to be worth an Amber. If you aren't using the rules to declare actions and rules to abort your action naturally this becomes unusable since it would need reworking.

    Master of Fire: See the Homid entry. Handy for a character leaping headfirst into danger, and for a Get who prefers to bring incendiary weapons with them.

    Razor Claws: See the Ahroun entry. Does nothing for Get who want to focus on melee, but an amazing Gift if you have any intention of focusing on claw attacks.

    Resist Pain: See the Philodox entry. Given the martial focus of the Tribe this will complement any Get member.

    Visage of Fenris: Against non-Garou, and Garou of your Rank and below, this is budget Persuasion. -1 difficulty to Social checks is pretty neat. Becoming larger and more fearsome could potentially have Veil implications, or help NPCs to determine what you are; the flavour hardly sounds subtle, shall we say, and I would reckon that in 99% of interactions with Mages and Fera a Garou who already seems to be on the more violent side growing larger and bristling with intimidating energies would make things worse, not better; but it's spirit magic. Let's be nice.

    Using this Gift against Garou of higher Rank is very difficult. If it weren't for Visage working on humans and Kinfolk, and your peers in Rank, this would be a Red.

    The impact on initiative would be a Red. Useless. Clearly not playtested. Negligible. My fix would be that the impact on Initiative is instead equal to the Get's Intimidation rating.

    ===========================================

    Despite having two Greens, the Get have a pretty mixed bag. A Homid gets a far better social Gift in Persuasion, making Visage useless, and also gets Master of Fire. An Ahroun already gets Razor Claws. A Philodox already gets Resist Pain. They're good to have on the list, for the Ragabash, Theurge and Galliards, but it's still not ideal to lack unique Gifts and unique identity.

    Ultimately it really sucks that their two unique starting Gifts aren't very good.

        RANK 1 GLASS WALKER GIFTS

        Glass Walkers get Weaver-related Gifts and have a focus on technology as well as human society.

        Control Simple Machine: This one is pretty neat. A Willpower point and a manageable roll aren't too bad, and the control lasting for a scene is really good. Being able to command ladders to extend, doors to bolt, chairs to unfold and so on emphasizes that the Tribe's relationship with the cutting edge of technology is an ancient one. I'm shocked that "push a button" is not listed in this Gift. You can man a keyboard remotely with this Gift. You can press buttons and pull levers of much more complex machines.

        Unfortunately we aren't given an indicator of what kind of Strength we're working with, what the range is and so on, so it's hard to explore the most shocking and powerful applications. When you command a pulley to pull, how powerful is it? What is the most complex machine that falls under 'simple machine'? How far is your reach? Can you command something to take a more complex action, like a pulley lifting and lowering in sequence?

        Anyways mastering the world of dials, switches and so on is pretty neat. Turning off light switches alone can be pretty influential.

        Diagnostics: No special tricks with this one, it's pretty straightforward. Always know how to fix a broken object, can spend Gnosis, time and successes needed are halved.

        Always succeeding at attempts to figure out why a machine isn't working is pretty amazing; you'll know it's the humidity or the ambient temperature or the shoe someone threw into the mechanism. This is the sort of gift that would be Green to actually have, but it's Amber in practice in a TTRPG. If you want to get the most out of this, have some kind of venture where diagnosing the complaints of machines is critical to pleasing customers, and perhaps use this Gift to justify building up Resources and influence within your field.

        Persuasion: See the Homid entry. Simply an amazing Gift, and I do get why it's here. They're the Tribe who pride themselves on immersing themselves in city life and human societies, guiding humanity since the beginning. But many other Tribes can claim such a heritage. This Gift is handy for those rare Lupus Glass Walkers, and for Metis of course. I kind of wish we didn't see so many repeats, but it is what it is. In all honesty this slot should have gone to Apecraft's Blessings; it's also a repeat, but one that makes a lot of sense, and would help establish that all Breeds within the Tribe are incredible at working with human technology. At least that's what I do at my table.

        Plug and Play: One of my favourite Gifts. Wrap an N64 controller around a fork lift's steering wheel. Break into a security system with your Tamagotchi or Gameboy. Turn on an MRI scanner with your smart watch. If you have Apecraft's Blessings and a good Intelligence + Computers, this Gift turns all high tech situations into a personal playground. The duration of a day and the cost of 1 WP are also pretty helpful; in a way it would make more sense to use Gnosis.

        An underrated aspect of this Gift is that you do not need to know a single thing about computers to use it. The most tech illiterate Lupus imaginable can control things with a joystick or button if they like. The mileage you'll get out of the Gift is severely diminished, that should go without saying, but a saboteur or thief who does the simplest things you can think of with machines they've connected to using this Gift is a fun concept.

        Trick Shot: Given that a great many PCs will have 0-1 Glory when they start out, this is an odd one. If you're a firearms focused character and you manage to get a few dots of Glory this is a neat one. The ban on shots that directly damage foes is quite annoying, and there's only so far you'll get on dropping chandeliers and scaffolding on enemies. Still, with a high Glory and support from Apecraft's this could pull off some utterly insane tricks, which is neat.

        If you want to use this aggressively, try going somewhere with a lot of suspended objects, volatile substances and so on. A building site or laboratory for instance. The potential for a memorable kill is pretty neat if nothing else.

        ===========================================

        If you're 100% dedicated to gunplay, getting Trick Shot out of list might be worth it once you have very high Glory.

        If you have any interest in computers or machines, getting Plug and Play out of list would be a great addition. Many thieves and tinkerers could learn a lot from the Gift, and I could easily see a Ragabash and Glass Walker doing a "trade" of Trash is Treasure and Plug and Play.

                        RANK 1 RED TALON GIFTS

                        Red Talon gifts are supposed to help them get back at humanity for their sins. A lot are tied to nature and to it triumphing over humans.

                        Beast Speech: See Galliard entry. Glad the Red Talons get this as it would be pretty weird if they didn't! Being able to converse with predators, prey, other canids etc. is pretty neat. When you're playing a character who was not only born a wolf, but also rejects human society, being able to RP with wolves and nature more directly is pretty key. Of course all Garou can communicate with wolves in Lupus form but that's based on body language and howling, not full on conversations playing out human-style.

                        Eye of the Hunter: This provides useful information regarding the pecking order of a group, and a marginal benefit to focusing down the weakest member of that group. Being able to identify the Alpha of a Pack at a glance is pretty handy on its own, and this Gift will come up in most fights against groups which is handy. The flavour tying into predator animal tactics of picking off a weak member of a group is pretty nice.

                        Hidden Killer: Handy Veil-preserving Gift which helps to explain the Tribe's activities. Removing all evidence you were present at a fight scene is handy, but of course it only applies to your own trail, and the wounds you inflicted. As it stands this is pretty niche, and doesn't have any super smart unique applications either. It's handy enough to be worth grabbing for when you pull off a lone kill.

                        Scent of Running Water: See the Ragabash entry. Lots of unique ways to use this to trick enemies in that writeup.

                        Wolf at the Door: This effect is flexible in the ways you can apply it, with good flavour. It has no cost, the roll will be quite easy against most NPCs but very hard against major antagonists. Turning an enemy into a shut-in is quite handy. Draining their Willpower is quite handy. Impacting their Social and Mental pools is neat. It's still niche in the sense that you need to be dealing with an NPC who is a recurring issue but you can't or shouldn't kill, which doesn't describe most Garou enemies. You can use it to teach someone a lesson, punishing humans for mistreating the wilderness, which has fun RP potential but won't solve many mission-critical problems.

                        Being able to target a big crowd, if you have a high Rage, is nice. There are real-life situations where getting a group of 1-5 people to stay in their homes and refuse to come out would be handy, and perhaps you'll make a splash by making some Pentex executive miss an important meeting, or getting a party of cryptozoologists to stay away from the Sept's hunting grounds during the Moot, or making a land developer cower in his home instead of furthering his plans to despoil the wilderness, but ultimately these people can try again later, or work via phone from home, or stew over their hatred of the wilderness and how much it terrifies them and later emerge even more dedicated to destroying it. Unfortunately the Gift doesn't seem to trigger instantly; you can't make someone lose 3 dice from their mental/social pools instantly, it triggers if they can make themselves leave their home.

                        =========================================

                        Red Talons have a pretty consistent set of Gifts; none of them are incredible, none of them are bad. Eye of the Hunter is potentially worth picking up regardless of Tribe, if you have the opportunity and think it would complement your skillset. Hidden Killer is also pretty great for any Garou who makes their living as an assassin.

                            RANK 1 SHADOW LORD GIFTS

                            Shadow Lord Gifts have a lot of themes; politics, dominance, storms, secrets, shadows.

                            Aura of Confidence: Attempts to read your Aura or find weaknesses just convey a sense that you are superior. This is handy in the sense that it protects you from aura readers (Vampires and Mages, near exclusively) determining you're a Werewolf, knowing your alive/dead status, whether you're Ghouled or not etc. but the problem is that it makes you stand out.

                            Being resistant to weaknesses being detected protects you from a lot of Gifts, and also from some uses of the Entropy Sphere. A cool Gift for crossover. In a Garou-focused game this one is likely going to come up once or twice a chronicle. Amber, but could easily be Red depending on the content of a game; I like the flavour however.

                            Fatal Flaw: Great for sizing someone up before a fight. You get to know a weakness, which could be very critical with interesting enemies like spirits and Vampires, and +1 damage die for the whole fight is neat. Naturally the previous Gift guards against this. The difficulty could be quite high depending on the enemy as it's Wits + Subterfuge, but it could also be very low. Keep in mind that a Spirit's "Wits+Subterfuge" will always be equal to their Gnosis.

                            The RP potential of this includes finding a flaw in a sparring partner, or revealing a weakness of a foe in conversation without intending to fight them. Finding out the person you're looking at is terrified of fire, or sunlight, gives you a pretty good clue as to what they might be. Knowing when to keep such info close to your chest and when to reveal it is key. You could also find and fix weaknesses in your allies.

                            Given that the idealized role of the Shadow Lords in the Garou Nation is the "beta" to the Silver Fangs, recognizing when someone is weak and having the opportunity to either exploit it or compensate for it is great flavour for them. All-around great Gift design.

                            Seizing the Edge: Could potentially be niche, but draws on checks will come up semi-regularly if you are sneaking around, lying to people, intimidating people and so on. Winning those draws makes you a little bit sneakier, a little bit more subtle, a little bit stronger, when you really need it.

                            Shadow Weaving: Budget Lasombra powers, activate! This costs 1 Gnosis, the range is good, the buff to stealth and intimidation is pretty small, but the potential to send messages, terrify bystanders, reveal hidden things, protect the Veil, hide objects and groups etc. is pretty neat.

                            Whisper Catching: An amazing Gift for Moots, investigations, and generally enjoying roleplay. The potential to learn critical information is great, as is the potential for other PCs to whisper things to you.

                            Keep in mind that canny Garou and Kinfolk may know there's every chance this Gift is in use, if there are several Shadow Lords present. Also keep in mind that people sometimes whisper things because they're sensitive, but at times it's simply because they don't want cannier ears to call out some nonsense. "I heard it whispered" is about as good a source as "I read it in a comments section".

                            ========================================

                            I was almost tempted to bump Fatal Flaw and Seizing the Edge up to Green in all honesty, but Amber feels most accurate for them. Whisper Catching is worth learning for any political character, but I reckon an ST would be quite slow to relinquish that one!

                                Rank 1 Silent Strider Gifts

                                Overall I'm not a fan of the Silent Strider Gifts, but that's across the whole board of 1-5, not just level 1. They start off alright; many of their Gifts relate to traveling and speed, along with ghostly stuff.

                                Heaven's Guidance: Easily overlooked, but having a magical sense of direction and being able to trace back your path is pretty amazing. The Gift's main impact is not having to make a bunch of rolls which could otherwise get you into trouble.

                                As with Umbral Tether, the most aggressive use of this is to make your way into a very tumultuous and hard to navigate place while pursued by enemies, then easily find your own way out once they are mired. A solid Amber, assuming your ST isn't handwaving navigation; generally speaking they should be thinking about practical stuff like this!

                                Sense Wyrm: Elevates any Tribe who has it. See the Metis entry.

                                Silence: +2 to stealth is handy. But it's only to Dexterity + Stealth, notably. Silently sneaking up on enemies would be effected, but checks using attributes other than Dex won't be.

                                For a Ragabash this makes you even better at sneaking than others of your Auspice. For other Auspices it helps you to add stealth to your repertoire, which complements basically all playstyles in my experience; whether you're sneaking around with a klaive, a camera, a stolen egg, you name it, stealth is just good.

                                Speed of Thought: The Striders are meant to run alone, at least conceptually, but if you're playing one you're likely in a mixed pack. Outpacing your allies has its uses, don't get me wrong; you can run ahead to send a message, run back to scout then catch up etc.

                                The cost of 1 Gnosis a pop is just annoying though. The Gift is handy, don't get me wrong, staying out of reach of foes, being nearly impossible to catch, winning races and having the edge in hunts, it's all good. A Rage cost, or WP cost, would just be more workable.

                                Visions of Duat: This gives you eyes on a world most Garou can't see. Unfortunately it's a world which has much less relevance to the Garou than the Middle Umbra but it's still good! Understanding that a place is being harmed by vengeful ghosts, rather than Banes, can save a lot of time and effort. The Veil risk of your eyes visibly changing is a little cumbersome, but it has cool flavour. Generally speaking this is a great way to pick up clues other Garou will miss.

                                If Wraiths and the undead are critical to a plot, or your enemies include necromantic mages or vampires, this Gift could be Green.

                                =============================================

                                If you find yourself getting lost a lot, picking up Heaven's Guidance might be handy. If you're able to get your hands on Visions of Duat, it helps to complement both Elder Brother and Theurge concepts. Silence is a good addition to any Garou, though keep in mind that for 5 EXP you can just get Stealth 1 and Stealth 2; this Gift is a good investment once you already have Stealth 2-3.

                                    RANK 1 SILVER FANG GIFTS

                                    Silver Fang gifts are meant to help them to lead, though many relate to silver, the moon and personal battle prowess.

                                    Eye of the Falcon: Pretty great for a sniper, though unfortunately the Silver Fangs are among the Tribes less likely to produce a sniper! Still, -1 difficulty to long range attacks, visual perception rolls, and a really great long range vision are great. If you have a Gift you can use on anyone you can see this combos well.

                                    This complements a scouting role, which is handy for a Ragabash especially.

                                    Falcon's Grasp: +3 dice to grappling is pretty handy, especially since one Garou grappling an enemy while everyone else claws them is so effective. Not much else to say really!

                                    Inspiration: See the Ahroun entry.

                                    Lambent Flame: +1 difficulty to incoming attacks increases your survivability considerably. The Veil risk of emitting light means you should save this for proper Garou on Garou battles, fighting in the Umbra and remote locations etc. The cost being WP is excellent, it lasts all scene, and it grants light to your allies. An amazing Gift.

                                    Sense Wyrm: Green, as always! See the Metis writeup.

                                    ==============================================

                                    The Silver Fangs start off relatively strong; if you do a huge favour to Luna, Lambent Flame is a better reward than most.

                                        RANK 1 STARGAZER GIFTS

                                        Stargazer Gifts have an emphasis on visions, wisdom, balance and non-lethal methods.

                                        Balance: The effects are permanent and don't have an activation cost. I much prefer Gifts with niche benefits working like this! You can always keep your balance and have -3 difficulty to climbing checks. You could combo this with other Gifts or Merits, but honestly it's good enough on its own.

                                        Channeling: This Gift makes you better at everything. Everything. Being able to spend 3 Rage for +3 dice is incredible. You're better at Rites, at crafting, at telling a great tale at a moot.

                                        The other use of this Gift is disposing of excessive Rage. If you're gaining Rage from an emotional or supernatural source, being able to 'destroy' it at a rate of 3 points per turn is pretty amazing!

                                        Utilizing this in combat is less optimal, as your Rage will almost always be best spent on bonus actions. Any Ahroun or Metis, and especially a Metis Ahroun, would benefit enormously from this Gift. An easy Green.

                                        Falling Touch: See the Ahroun section. Great martial arts flavour. Fits with the balance concept, and non-lethal means of fighting.

                                        Iron Resolve: Much like Channeling, this makes you better at everything. Crafting a Fetish. Binding a spirit. Making an impossible jump. Combine the two on a critical check you need to do amazingly on and you'll see great results. Iron Resolve is another Gift that is well worth getting at 5xp, though you'll likely have to jump through a lot of hoops.

                                        Sense Wyrm: Still Green, see the Metis entry.

                                        ================================================== =====

                                        Stargazers give the Children of Gaia competition for starting lists. I reckon this Tribe have the best set of level one Gifts, in fact. Anyone would benefit from learning Channeling and Iron Resolve, whether from spirits or from Stargazers. Spending 10xp on both Gifts is well worth it, if you can find a way to obtain them.

                                                    RANK 1 ELDER BROTHER GIFTS

                                                    Uktena (I prefer Elder Brother) Gifts are often blessings from animals, or let them deal with Occult stuff. These magical Gifts aren't all just Theurge powers 'copied over', so they have their own unique mystical identity; I like that a pack can have an Elder Brother Ahroun and a Get of Fenris Theurge who both have a solid role and identity. Still, them getting Sense Wyrm and Spirit Speech as in-tribe Gifts at level 1 does mean that playing an Uktena of any Auspice other than Theurge almost feels like playing with two Auspices.

                                                    We're told that some of these Gifts were only recently unearthed, which is intriguing.

                                                    Sense Magic: Picking up on Gifts, Fetishes, Disciplines, Sorcery, Magick, Cantrips and so on is incredible. The radius is generous and gets even bigger with more successes, and this Gift can reveal endless secrets. It almost feels like cheating.

                                                    If this one is giving you grief, I'd suggest adding a Gnosis cost to gain the ability to use the Gift for a whole Scene, or charging one point of Willpower per use.

                                                    While already amazing in a standard game, this becomes 100x better in a Crossover game. If you're a mystically inclined Garou of any Tribe, and you ever get the chance to learn this, go for it.

                                                    Sense Wyrm: See the Metis entry. Amazing as always. Critical to the Elder Brother's role of finding, understanding and sealing away the Wyrm. Great for locating those dark places which require your Tribe's touch.

                                                    Shroud: Creating an obscuring effect which doesn't impact you is incredible. There's a reason that Darkness + Devil's Sight is such a potent and frequently seen combination in DND5e.

                                                    If you have allies who are skilled at fighting in the dark, and/or have Gifts which grant them special senses, this Gift becomes even better. Use it when it disadvantages your enemies the most in fights, and ensure your allies are well prepared to work around this Gift. Generally speaking you will be looking at attacking enemies who cannot dodge or parry, sometimes at -2 difficulty, while they attack back at +2 difficulty.

                                                    Outside of combat this Gift can help to minimize Veil breaches, hide fights, block lines of sight, handle security, terrify humans and animals alike, delay someone, cause chaos in traffic, disrupt a search party and so on.

                                                    Shadow Lords will be jealous of you.

                                                    Spirit of the Lizard: This Gift is good, though the potential to be dislodged by taking strenuous actions is a little worrying. Generally speaking being able to move at walking speed across any surface, even a ceiling, opens up a great many approaches to problems which could be hard to navigate otherwise. Try to do things with your mouth when possible.

                                                    Note that the surface you're walking on doesn't matter it seems; you can climb glass as easily as granite. Possibly this Gift could help you to "climb" on a tricky floor, such as slippery ice, or to work your way up a giant enemy.

                                                    And honestly the image of a giant bipedal wolf beast mission impossible'ing across a ceiling is just too good to pass up.

                                                    Spirit Speech: See the Theurge entry. Key to the Tribe's identity, and they're lucky to be the only Tribe who get it on their list. If you're not a Theurge, playing down your possession of this Gift might be wise; a Ragabash trickster or himbo Ahroun who surprises a spirit hours into accompanying them and their Brood with "that isn't a very nice thing to say" could be a memorable moment.

                                                    ==================================================

                                                    Much like the Children of Gaia and Stargazers the Elder Brothers start with an incredible Gift list.

                                                            Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post
                                                            Great point with aristocrats hunting! I considered bows and thrown weapons, but keep in mind that the gift specifies Long Range Attack Rolls - we are given rules in the ranged section which specify that using a gun at its suggested range is difficulty 6, up to 2x that range is difficulty 8 (is this what the Gift means by Long Range?) and point blank is difficulty 4.

                                                            Offsetting a penalty makes the most sense for this Gift's effect, as long range attacks are where sharp eyes matter most. So unless you are strong enough to throw a silver dagger 50+ meters (far from impossible given that Garou are Garou, and the thrown weapon could have a Gift or some kind of magic behind it) it won't be applying to thrown weapons.
                                                            The section on throwing weapons (before general combat maneuvers) makes it clear that thrown weapons still have close and long ranges, but instead of being determined by the weapon, the ST is just supposed to figure it out based on the aerodynamics and Strength of the character (and allows a greater range of difficulty modifiers). While W20 forgot to actually say how to figure out the ranges for thrown weapons in any way, they're still capable of long ranged attacks like firearms and bows.

                                                            The RAW don't really care what the actual distance is, just that it's beyond the base range of the weapon (yes, I agree it's meant to reduce the penalty for up to double range from 8 to 7). A light pistol with a range of 20 gets the benefit of this Gift shooting from 21m to 40m. Thrown is no different. As well, while I don't think W20 addressed this, the WoD games do generally treat throwing assist mechanisms like slings as spear-throwers as still operating as thrown weapons mechanically, vastly increasing their ranges beyond 50m. There's at least a few legendary figure worthy of Silver Fang emulate from early history that used slings to great effect.
