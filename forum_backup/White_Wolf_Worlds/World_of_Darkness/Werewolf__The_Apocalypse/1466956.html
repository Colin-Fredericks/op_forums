    Soaking AGG in Breed; Metis

    11-06-2021, 01:08 PM
    Hi team,

    I'd like to get some clarification on the matter. The book states that soaking agg is not possible in breed form so would a Metis born Garou not be able to soak AGG in Crinos?

    A metis is born in his Crinos form, a form halfway between Homid and Lupus. An adult werewolf in Crinos is a killing machine, a massive, nine-foot tall monster plodding to battle on two stocky legs. The very sight of one conjures up images of an age long gone, when massive shapeshifters stalked the Earth and herded their flocks of human breeding stock.- W20 44

    Humans can’t soak aggravated damage. Werewolves can soak aggravated damage with Stamina in any form except their breed form, with the exception of damage from silver.- W20 255

    Werewolves heal at an incredible pace. A Garou regenerates her worst bashing or lethal health level every turn. Homid- and lupus-breed Garou can regenerate a health level each day while in their natural forms if they are in critical condition, but doing so doesn’t let them do much more than sleep. If they’re conscious and moving around in their breed form, they heal as humans do. Metis are blessed with full regeneration in every form. - W20 256

    Is this to be taken as verbatim or can Metis soak AGG in Crinos but cannot soak silver in all forms?
    It just seems very off to me that a Crinos is able to dish out agg so easily with its natural weaponry but cant soak it just cause the said Garou was born in that form.
    Thanks!

                          Well, my simplified version that I think captures everything just fine:

                          All Breeds soak bashing, lethal, and aggravated damage from most sources regardless of form. Homids and lupus can soak silver in their breed forms, but have reduced regeneration in their breed forms.

                          Boom. Done. It's 95% the same as what the books say, without the 5% that throws everything off.

                          The "fire does Agg" thing stems way back though, and even Vampire took on the idea that Agg is more than just supernatural damage, but also extremely grievous damage from mundane sources. Though flamethrowers aren't generally that good given how fire works in the WoD rules, and how easily most splats can just blow you up for trying to use one in a fight (combat flamethrowers aren't really a thing because every time people tried the systems prove too prone to killing the user and any allies standing near them; military flamethrowers are generally engineering tools).

                                          Originally posted by heinrich View Post

                                          V20 is exactly the same, it has zero to do with OPP being new. Almost the entire text of V20 is simply copy/paste, even the changes are made by just as little editing as possible.

                                          As an example, the rules for resisting/breaking Blood Bonds are unusable as RAW because they changed from the number of doses increasing diff to increasing successes needed, but in the line where they'll tell you the limit on this increase they re-use old text with un-matching rules.

                                          Werewolf may be a little worse, but in general the cause is just that the X20 core books were this kind of undertaking until then. They're gigantic books, whose main purpose is to cram as much mechanical content from an entire game line as possible, while receiving little adjust and a focus on improving the horrendous layout and content organization from previous cores. This's a lot of work outside the text compiling material, and makes for a confusing mess to tune the whole text no one in the team really wrote.

                                          From Mage onward what actually changes is the amount of original work on each book, and even then I'm not sure if it can be considered such a seamless job. But it does change the standard approach of the work to a more hands on that makes editing easier in general. Also, the fact that from Mage onward you have actually less crunch to delve into. Mage is far less rule-intensive outside the core in any edition, and other lines are simply smaller.

                                                  Outside the Original Trio, other lines were in general badly needing actual new editions, and at least C20 gave me that in spades, Wr20 I just started reading, so I don't know for sure, but I got the impression that it was the same in this regard.

                                                  As someone who still had the old material fresh in mind for VtM and WtA, I admit I didn't read W20 so thoroughly, while V20 had the difference of being something I play far more frequently. But my own account only V20 was just and good an anniversary compilation, and W20 seemed to follow suit, or at least try. Both compile a lot of previous material, give too little updates to it as this wasn't their purpose, and have the good and bad of doing so.

                                                  The inexperience of OPP at the time may have played a role in W20's final quality, as I said I didn't read it thoroughly enough to know, but the goal is a clear thing and the same as V20. Of course, that talking about core, since it was already evident that it wouldn't be just the core, indeed.

                                                  But by M20 it was clear that treating X20 as a new edition was on the table and they tried. A lot of unique aspects of the game made Mage the best line of the three to try it out anyway. Different from the others, whose powers and Z-splats required a lot of individual mechanical attention, Mage is light in its specific rules, it's mostly the Magic system, so supplements never added as much to crunch, for example.

                                                  You may say the result is a mixed bag. M20 have a lot of good decisions and new problems because of author's vision. But it did well enough to show that treating the X20 line as a new edition from core was indeed the way to go. And that's what Changeling and Wraith are, those lines desperately needed an actual new edition, and at least C20 I can say that deliver from core. And I never once using it (it is my current main chronicle) felt it let me down, even if I not always agree to some lore directions (in the amount to be expected at a new edition of any TTRPG).
