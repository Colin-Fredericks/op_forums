    Originally posted by Gryffon15 View Post
    Legally speaking, unless someone can stop them, any garou is perfectly within their rights to demand it.
    This is actually one of the issues I wanted to address in this thread, so thanks for bringing it up.

    However, I haven't read anything that actually says the Garou have anything like the right of prima nocta/first night (which does not seem to have actually existed, but makes for good fiction). If there is explicit mention, please let me know.

    I do think Garou are able to claim mating with kinfolk even if that kinfolk doesn't want to. Arranged marriages are a very real thing, both in history and in some cultures even today. So that the Garou could do this fits right in.

    However, I don't think this means any Garou can claim any kinfolk. For example, I don't think the Silver Fangs could actually attempt to mate with some other tribe's kinfolk. That would likely produce a war unless that tribe agreed to do so. The nature of Silver Fang "kingship" is an interesting topic in itself. But to make it short, I don't think it's anything like a divine rights of king. It's more like a first among equals with certain sacral king responsibilities. Of course, the Silver Fangs absolutely would like it to be a divine right absolute monarchy. But it's just not.

    I'm fine with Garou raiding other sept's for mates, not because it would be legal, but because that is exactly what people of rival city-states/petty kingdoms would do in the heroic legendry of ancient cultures. Of course, those are precisely the kind of things that cause septs go to war. See the Trojan War or Deirdre of the Sorrows. So I do think it is regulated in practice in order to precisely avoid war. Many cultures have an "abducting the bride" tradition, but if you look closely at it, generally it is a kind of playacting with willing participants.

    So I think this is a complex issue, and I'm not sure where I want to fall. I do think Garou claim unwilling mates. But I think there is a select group that a Garou can safely claim those within it - something that has sept approval. But if you go outside that group, you either risk breaking the law or risking war with another sept. Defining what that group is where Garou can claim mates is at the heart of the matter.

    But because I am so uncertain, I want to hear dissenting views because there is a very good chance I'll change my mind.

                                As I have said before, I do think Garou can claim Kinfolk as mates though the kinfolk be unwilling (to varying degrees though we need to remember kinfolk are not defenseless and can cause trouble). But this does not mean a Garou can just claim anyone.

                                A Garou's “claim” of a kinfolk is also a claim made against another Garou. This is not terra incognita where anyone can plant a flag. It is either an individual Garou like a family member or appointed guardian, or at the general level of the sept itself (likely adjudicated by a hearing of the Sept Elders or their designate like a trusted Philodox). If those Garou don't approve of the match, or have concerns, that claim is going to be disputed. In other words, kinfolk should always have another Garou, in some capacity, to watch out over their interests. (I am not saying those interests are always protected, or not considered or overruled when convenient – but there is something there).

                                That could lead to a Challenge, and the Challenger doesn't get to decide the nature of that contest.

                                So I think for the most part, Garou claim mates only when they can pacify or have the approval of that kinfolk's “guardian”. The kinfolk might still be unwilling, but that would be no different than examples of arranged marriages among humans. We may view that as wrong, but it is not unique to the Garou. There are exceptions.
                                • If they don't have approval, but the kinfolk wants to be the mate of the other Garou, they might be able to get away with it in some tribes/septs, though not in others. There would be some kind of precedent or example that could be followed.
                                • If the Garou doesn't get approval and claims an unwilling kinfolk anyway, it might be accepted in certain circumstances, or it might result in punishment even leading to ostracism or worse.
                                Then there is the issue of kinfolk as “war booty” claimed during a raid between septs. Let's be clear here – this is war pure and simple. Whether or not such a “claim” is recognized depends on the outcome of the war. If your sept is defeated in the war, your claim is rejected, and that enemy sept might take its revenge on you during the war or as a price to restore peace. Only if your sept wins or there is some kind of negotiated settelment, might you keep your prize.

                                In these cases, unless the sept itself has decided on war, I don't think it will support some lone Garou or a pack disrupting the peace for selfish reasons. They may force that Garou to return whatever booty was stolen. Though there could be cases where the sept decides its hand is forced and must go to war regardless. You can make sure there will be some sort of retaliation though for forcing them into an unwanted war.

                                “War booty” is a very different thing than claiming kinfolk within your sept or an allied sept.

                                If it happens, I think it usually happens during the course of a war, as opposed to something that instigates one. And it is probably something more likely to have occurred in the past than nowadays. I could see this happening a lot for thousands of years from the Stone Age to the Iron Age. But how realistic is it in modern day America or Europe when abduction of kinfolk might trigger police investigation? I think this scenario must happen much less than what it once did. I can imagine scenarios where this still occurs, but I think it is a much less common one than in previous eras.

                                So I think outright abduction of an unwilling kinfolk as a mate is more likely to be done when the kinfolk is somehow lost or not claimed, but somehow now identified as such. (A good example would be finding a Lost Cub, and the Garou feel that Lost Cub's siblings or cousin could also be kinfolk which they confirm with Scent of True Form.) Such a kinfolk would not have a Garou defender, and the sept is more likely to approve of something that “returns” a kinfolk back to the Garou. Of course, this is also the scenario that might blow up in the Garous' faces since such a kinfolk is the least “conditioned” to accept this and most likely to cause trouble.

                                Sorry for rambling, but this is how I think things through so I can figure out how I want to handle it in my own chronicles.

                                            Yeah, this is definitely one of those areas where I feel that the dials of 'Realism' and 'Narrative' are important to keep in mind.

                                            If the World of Darkness is being used to create passive narrative experiences such as books, comics, shows, movies, etc. then the creator is fully empowered to maintain their vision of setting realism or to propose some sort of alternate vision and create freely. So long as the audience is given information going in about what sort of content is being produced, they can choose whether or not they're interested in absorbing the story.

                                            When you're actually playing however, any concept of 'Realism' is far less important then 'Player Enjoyment/Interest'. Even if a game is disturbing and horrific, as long as everyone is enjoying themselves and interested (and no one gets hurt), then they're doing it right. Every group will have its own needs and every person will have their own needs. As someone with experience playing with an ST who prioritized their vision of 'Setting Realism' over player enjoyment, I can state that it gets old fast and it makes things worse for everyone involved.

                                            That's why I'm a huge advocate of things like Session 0, the X Card, and the Golden Rule. Screw what the books say, do what your group wants to do to enjoy themselves while playing a game and creating a narrative experience that is safe for everyone.

                                            Most folks probably already know this and agree with that but y'know, sometimes it warrants saying on the off chance someone out there needs to read it.

I'm not a lawyer. Don't use anything I say as legal advice. I'm not a mod, don't take my word here as meaning anything concrete aside from my opinion. Anyway, long disclaimer over.)

                                                    I haven't seen any comments good or bad on my "categories" that distinguish different zones of control, so I'm moving forward. (Though people are still free to comment, disagree, or add anything they want).

                                                    I'll start with the easiest - the kinfolk are immediate family. Maybe immediate only means the nuclear family. Maybe it is extended in the sense its immediate generations - grandparents, cousins, and such. Maybe it includes immediate neighbors. Still fairly compact. It might include in-laws if their mate's family is not already "controlled". If so, Garou just gets the mate, not the in-laws. Let me know where you think the boundary should lie.

                                                    So this is what I see:

                                                    Garou can control people's mates - arrange marriages and the like. Typically this will either be something like "marry my packmate" or "if you marry this other Garou or kinfolk, it is a good political alliance for me within the sept/tribe." Something that reflects the Garou as being "head of the family". It might also include vetos if the Garou disapproves of the marriage for some reason. Since these are likely close blood relations, this won't include that Garou's ability to mate with them (except for perhaps Silver Fangs which can sometimes be verys squicky).

                                                    Garou can call upon extensive financial obligations IF the Kinfolk are capable of it. I think it's limited to kinfolk being able to live and survive in a routine manner. A Kinfolk only available through the Kinfolk background can only give so much. But if you have a wealthier Kinfolk who owns his own business, has a high net worth, investment income, etc. then a PC who wants to use it better have it recorded as Allies or Resources. But in terms of general handling, not mechanics, I think the Garou should be able to "draw the water from the well" though NOT cause the well to dry except in exceptional circumstances. Even if it is limited to just the "wealthy" kinfolk, and how much is rich will have different opinions, I think the Garou can be very tyrannical here. I'm thinking something like 10% take "for the cause" on a regular basis.

                                                    I also think things like directing careers, education, etc. because these are the kinds of things we see parents influence or dictate to their children.

                                                    And of course normal Kinfolk Background stuff - sending people off to do errands and such, acting as the Garou's hands among the Flock because the Garou has the Curse to deal with, or can't be everywhere.

                                                    So the model here is "strict parent in a traditional society". Again, this is just the base guideline - tribes and septs will modify this.

                                                    I think the greatest control can be exercised here because the assumption is "control" is always balanced by the close personal connections the Garou has with those people. Garou wants the best thing for their parents, siblings, children, cousins, best friends, neighbors, etc. So that should prevent the worst abuses.

                                                    Of course, I still think there is some kind of supervision here. Bad treatment will reach the sept leaders who will intervene if needed, even if it is only loss of renown.

                                                    I also think we need to think of rights that kinfolk have since that will be something that can override control at this level.

                                                    So what are people's thoughts? Does it need to be more strict, less strict? Do we need additional examples of control because we want examples of things that may crop up in game?
