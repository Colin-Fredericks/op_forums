    Ananasi (Mechanical Deep Dive)

    03-18-2025, 03:05 PM
    Werespiders! The Ananasi are the children of Queen Ananasa, daughter to the Weaver, 'little sister' to the Wyld and Wyrm. Queen A is not a servant of Gaia, as it were, and she considers each member of the Triat to have fallen from grace and into insanity. Because of this it is important to deal with one common misconception immediately.

    Ananasi do not serve the Weaver. Their ties to the Weaver are undeniable, their similarities to her spirits are obvious, and their detached logical assessment of reality is often Weaver-like. While Garou struggle to Step Sideways where the Weaver's works are strongest, Ananasi can cross over more easily in such spaces, and in turn the werespiders struggle to cross the Gauntlet where the Weaver's power runs thin.

    So, what is their relationship with their Queen's mother? They fear her, they hide from her, and they pity her. While Ananasi don't have the whole picture, they're arguably closer to having it than the other Changing Breeds. A typical Ananasi may well understand that the Weaver and Wyrm are both insane, that their insanity results from the Weaver trying to trap the Wyrm. They know exactly who created them and why, and they have a direct connection to QA, who dictates their rise and fall in Renown.

    Given that the Ananasi were not created by Gaia, they are not tasked with a primary purpose. Garou are warriors, Gurahl healers, Ratkin control the human population, and so on. The werespiders are tasked with restoring the Triat to its former state, creating a new power known as the Triumvirate. Because this is a metaphysical task beyond reckoning they fulfil many roles. Each werespider specializes in a different member of the Triat, and has a specific job relating to that mighty spirit.

    Blood

    Ananasi don't have Rage, instead they have a Blood pool which they refill by feeding on humans and animals. Note that this is more physiological than the processes of a Vampire. A Vampire does not feed on humans because they are the most nourishing organism in nature, they feed on humans because their blood, to Kindred, holds the most nourishment. Ananasi, meanwhile, simply benefit from blood by quantity.

    By default an Ananasi can hold 10 blood points. Draining a human dry (taking more than 5 points) will kill them, naturally, but a typical human holds 10 blood points if you need a refill. It's also worth noting that Ananasi can feed off of cold-blooded animals and even insects. Werespiders don't need blood, but blood is the source of much of their power.

    Spending a point of blood allows an Ananasi to:

    - enter a form of their choice without a roll
    - take one extra action
    - heal a Wound (5 Blood for 1 Aggravated)
    - create Strength 9 webs, enough to ensnare a Crinos form Garou or cover an industrial doorway

    A werespider cannot spend Blood and Gnosis on the same turn, and they can't spend Blood for multiple purposes in a single turn. So, for example, an Ananasi can spend blood to heal, or to take extra actions, or to transform, but not a combination within a single turn. Ananasi can also heal themselves of serious diseases using blood, sometimes in huge quantities requiring many victims. Remember, importantly, that you don't regenerate. If you don't spend blood to heal, you're stuck healing like a (resilient) human. Finding a means of healing without blood, like spiritual allies, or a Gift, or a Fetish or Rite, is ideal.

    Forms

    Ananasi forms are rather alien to Garou. They may take Lilian (giant bipedal warrior), Pithus (enormous spider) and Crawlerling (swarm of spiders) forms. A Glabro-like form (Araneid) can be learnt using a Gift.

    Araneid: +1/+3/+1, claws inflict lethal
    Lilian: +2/+3/+2
    Pithus: +4/+1/+3
    Crawlerling: 0/+5/0

    Delirium is inflicted by Lilian, Pithus, and by humans seeing you enter or leave Crawlerling form.

    I would suggest using Lilian for a typical fight, given the importance of Dexterity, and Pithus for battles where you require your webs or where you need significant Strength (lots of grappling, lots of heavy hazards) or Stamina (high damage unavoidable hazards, potent attacks, any situation where you can't afford to heal).

    Ananasi have Homid and Arachnid Breeds. Their Metis are stolen away into the Umbra and are not playable typically. In a startling display of bad game design, Homid Ananasi have a Willpower of 3 and a Gnosis of 1 while Arachnid Ananasi start with a Willpower of 4 and Gnosis of 5. There are no drawbacks at all to playing an Arachnid Ananasi, since they learn human skills during their initial training in the Umbra, allowing them to start with human-specific Skills purchased without Freebie points. Ananasi don't bother with Breed Gifts, so the player who chooses a Homid Ananasi is sacrificing a lot of power (9 freebie points worth of Gnosis and Willpower) for the flavour they prefer.

    I would strongly suggest treating Homid and Arachnid Ananasi as mechanically equivalent at your table. The only mechanical difference, if you do this, will be which form they can reflexively return to without cost, both of which have upsides and downsides. Willpower 4 Gnosis 5 works well enough for an Ananasi-only game, but might not work as well in a mixed-Fera game.

    Crawling Sideways

    Ananasi treat the most intensely Weaver-aligned environments as difficulty 2 for stepping sideways, which they can only do in their Crawlerling form typically. Pristine wilderness is difficulty 9, conversely. Any area which is Wyrm-tainted adds +2 to the difficulty unless you are of the Hatar, the Ananasi whose work is closest to the Wyrm.

    Totem

    By default Ananasi get Queen Ananasa as a 'free' Totem. She grants +3 Occult and +2 Enigmas, but only while you're communing with her. Not bad at all for a free Totem, but in a mixed-Fera (Ahadi, Beast Courts etc.) game you will inevitably replace this with something better.

    General Ananasi Gifts

    Green Gifts are often must-haves, or have applications which are incredibly game-changing, story-impacting or overall powerful. Amber Gifts are solid, their utility is often determined by how likely you are to need the given effect or whether it's important to your character concept. Red Gifts are, sadly, too weak to typically be a good investment. A Red Gift might have an insane cost for its effect, or have too hard a roll to reliably pass, or be entirely redundant, and so on.

    If a Gift's rating or function is changed by the unique circumstances of the werespiders I will make a note of it. For previously rated Gifts, please see the Garou Gifts Deep Dive: https://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/...g-overthinking

    Balance, Cling (Spirit of the Lizard), Resist Pain, Resist Toxin
    (No pre-existing Level 2 Gifts)
    Jump (Great Leap), Spider's Grace (Catfeet)
    (No pre-existing Level 4 Gifts)
    Survivor

    It's worth noting that Jump, while still excellent, is far better for Garou. For a Garou it can stack with Hare's Leap, and with the insane movement speeds of the Lupus form. An Ananasi in Pithus or Lilian form will be far quicker than you'd like it to be, but it is never going to outrun a wolf, so the insane power of Hare's Leap and Great Leap is lost here. Cling is bumped up from Amber to Green since the various Ananasi forms have plenty of limbs, meaning that remaining stuck to a wall or ceiling or equivalent is far easier. Just keep in mind that 'strenuous' activity risks knocking you off, so rolls might be called for.

    Many Eyes (1): 360 degree vision for a scene. The only tell for this is that if you look at the user of this Gift through the corner of your eye they will look as if they have many eyes.

    Given that this costs a point of Blood, and it only lasts for a scene... this is okay, but it's a little niche. The key benefit of having eyes in the back of your head, as it were, is that you can see things happening when you least expect it. This lets you see things when you expect it. You could catch a glimpse of something behind your back, sure, and you can always pull a "fine, I'll look away while you type in your password"... so it isn't useless, especially given the jobs the Ananasi often find themselves doing.

    Stolen Moments (1): Touch someone, spend Gnosis, roll Gnosis (difficulty of the target's Willpower), on a success you steal the last 15 minutes of the target's memories.

    This Gift, more than anything, feels like a way to explain how the Ananasi do what they do. They're non-Gaia shapeshifters who exist everywhere on the planet, practically speaking, but are largely out of the line of sight of the other breeds. I would argue that the Crawlerling form already does this quite well, but this Gift helps too. Do note that rolling Gnosis, even 5 dice, against a difficulty of the target's Willpower isn't ideal. Against serious threats this Gift may fail or botch quite frequently.

    Hand Fangs (2): Aggravated damage on your hands, available in Homid and Araneid, with the bonus of being able to inject venom. Given that Ananasi venom is variable in power and we aren't even given a suggestion for how strong it should be (very weird choice by W20), this is hard to rate.

    Having Aggravated damage 'claws' in Homid is good however. The cost being a single Blood point works well.

    Man-Spider Form (2): Much like Glabro, this is the 'plausible deniability' form as it were. It doesn't really, TRULY pass for human... but what else could it have been? The problem is that the Garou have a variety of forms which are all horrible Veil-wise (giant wolf monster, giant wolf, a wolf, what the hell is a regular wolf doing here), making Glabro pretty handy. Araneid would be useful in a situation where you can't rely on Homid and you can't rely on Crawlerling... but that's a niche situation to be in. It isn't like with Garou where a Homid slipping from Homid to Glabro gains regeneration; Ananasi don't regenerate. If the Araneid form passed for human, reliably, this Gift would be much better.

    You can enter this form with a normal transformation roll, or by spending blood. It's a neat form in the sense that you can fight in it and enjoy +3 Dexterity without the giant size of Lilian (which is 1.5x your normal height!). All in all I'd say that this Gift is worth 6xp, and you really ought to take it when you have a chance, but it isn't top priority either.

    Replenishment of the Flesh (2): This Gift allows you to spend 1 Gnosis to recover 1 point of damage. Bashing and Lethal only. Yes, you need to supplement your healing, cover for the fact that you aren't much of a healer, but this isn't the way to do it. The damage you'd really want to use this on is Aggravated, and this Gift doesn't cover it.

    Spines (2): This Gift is known as Gift of the Porcupine for Garou, and it is an excellent Level 4 Gift. This is an outstanding and somewhat baffling Level 2 Gift. See the Porcupine writeup for full details, but giving this much lethality to a Rank 2 character is arguably a very silly move! If you're struggling with this Gift I'd suggest bumping it up to Level 3.

    Blood Pump (3): Spend 1 Gnosis. For the rest of the scene you can spend blood at any rate so long as you have blood to spend. For example you might heal 6 Lethal by spending 6 Blood, or heal 2 Aggravated by spending 10 Blood.

    You could also spend 10 Blood to take 10 extra actions... if you had 10 blood, and Wits 10, and Dexterity 10. It's very easy to forget that extra actions in W20 are limited by your Dexterity and Wits. With a Dexterity and Wits of 5 each you could take 5 extra actions for 5 blood with this Gift active, which is pretty great. It's also worth noting that you can mix and match different types of blood expenditure with this Gift.

    So, in a given turn an Ananasi could turn to Pithus (1 blood), take 3 bonus actions (3 blood), heal 2 lethal (2 blood), web up a fallen foe (1 blood) and activate a blood-based Gift. Note that you still cannot ever spend Blood in the same turn as you spend Gnosis, including the turn you activate this Gift. An incredible buff so long as you keep drinking blood.

    Entropic Bite (4): Spend 2 Gnosis upon biting someone. Roll Gnosis (difficulty of 3 + target's Stamina, max 9), and the target suffers a level of unsoakable Aggravated damage for one turn per success. If you kill a human suffering from this poison they'll grant 15 blood instead of 10.

    The Gnosis cost is very unfortunate, since it blocks extra actions and so on. The difficulty is also going to make this very unreliable. Even if you use this to gain as much blood as possible, the problem is the Gnosis cost. 2 Gnosis to get 5 extra blood is a terrible deal!

    Hydraulic Strength (4): Spend Blood to boost Strength at a 1:1 exchange. This bonus is limited by your Rank, making it very strange to put this Gift at Level 4. This Gift would make much more sense at Level 2, but whatever. +4 Strength for the scene is a great bonus, and makes Lilian a much more dangerous form given its high Dexterity, now coupled with far greater Strength. With Blood Pump you can hit this +4 Strength cap immediately, which is pretty great.

    Carapace (5): Spend 1 Gnosis, get +4 Soak, and Regenerate like a Garou. This lasts for one scene. Honestly even if this only gave Regeneration it would still be pretty solid, that's just how good the Garou basekit is. Still, an Ananasi with this Gift will have up to 11-12 soak before armour and other oddities, which is a great start.

    Just imagined Fetish Armour, like full plate or chainmail, for Pithus for the first time. Terrifying but hilarious. Perhaps something more chitinous or insect-like would suit the Ananasi better.

      Tenere

      Dedicated to the Weaver, the Tenere are basically what you most expect of the Ananasi. Detached and pattern-focused, the Tenere learn Gifts that help with subtlety, but there are major oversights. Various Weaver-related Gifts are missing from this list. The theming is somewhat loose, and very few of the Gifts here will actually help you with managing the affairs of Queen Ananasa when it comes to replacing her mother.

      Groom (1): Become immune to all "Appearance roll penalties", and you'll never suffer any downsides from being covered in blood, having just been through a storm, your clothes being torn, and so on. Basically you always look your best.

      It's very funny that a strict RAW reading of this Gift means that you keep your full Appearance when in your various forms, since Groom protects you from "all" penalties to Appearance. Clearly not the intended use of the Gift but still interesting.

      While Appearance is criminally underused, and this Gift isn't useless, paying 3xp for what will be a flavour tax in most sessions, and indeed a flavour tax which makes you more distinctive and odd, is a waste of time.

      Patience of Ananasa (1): +1 automatic success to any Stealth roll where remaining absolutely still helps. The Gift basically makes it so that when you want to stay still, you stay still, you are no longer someone who shivers or twitches, perhaps even your reflexes are entirely voluntary now, which is pretty cool! I wish this had more mechanical impacts, like helping with Subterfuge checks, but it's good all the same.

      Beneath Notice (2): Spend 1 Gnosis to make an object, or small group of objects, blend in. Anyone searching for the objects takes a penalty equal to your Obedience, which is going to be a 1-10 dice penalty. Potentially very minor, potentially huge. That said if anyone isn't searching for the object just the base effect will hopefully do the job. This would be useful for spying, thieving and so on. Make a spy cam blend in, make your tent blend in, make the car you've left nearby blend in. 'Object' isn't given a size, only groups of objects, which must be small.

      Worth noting there isn't a maximum duration either. So assuming that you can make a giant tent blend in, this is actually very good... if your Obedience is decent. You won't start to see huge returns until you have very high Obedience unfortunately.

      Breath of Ananasa (2): Giving yourself an air bubble is annoyingly niche at Level 2, but the fact that there is no Gnosis cost makes the Gift potentially worthwhile. If you're going to be out in the weirdest parts of the Umbra, or exploring underwater locales, or space etc. then this Gift is much better. The duration is pretty neat and will handle almost any situation since you can refresh the Gift for free.

      Mother's Look (3): See Eye of the Cobra. Understanding the Tapestry grants bonus dice here, and Sex Appeal will reduce your difficulty on the roll in some cases.

      Reshape Object (3): Still terrible. See the relevant post in the Garou thread. Somehow this Gift being on the Ananasi list makes it even funnier, like you need to be a truly loyal servant of Ananasa to turn a log into a shield with a touch, fear our power!

      Understanding the Tapestry (4): +3 Enigmas at Rank 4 isn't that great. The flavour of the Gift suggests much more than being good at puzzles, even in a game where puzzles can be fundamental to life. Sure, this will help you out in the Umbra sometimes, and with some spirits, but you don't have any Gifts to help you navigate the Umbra or talk to spirits. If you have some really powerful Gift or Fetish which requires Enigmas rolls this might be better, but it just isn't good.

      Web Sheet (4): Spend 2 blood and cover an area the size of a football field (120 x 53 yards) in your webbing. As always this webbing is Strength 9 and has 4 health levels.

      This is... a very underwritten Gift, like many of the unique spider Gifts. What happens to people in that area? Do you have to be in Pithus, as you'd typically expect? How do you cover such a huge area - is it a giant spray or do the webs seem to materialize?

      I'd much prefer this Gift covered a more intimate area with more solid mechanics. This could completely sway a battle by trapping a huge enemy army, or do next to nothing, depending on interpretation. It's also a very blunt and obvious Gift for such subtle masterminds.

      Thieving Touch of Spiders (5): See Thieving Talons of the Magpie. Sadly still terrible.

      Time to bite the bullet

      I like the Ananasi, they're among my favourites, but there are things they absolutely need to be able to do which aren't reflected mechanically. The Bastet get a version of Sense Wyrm which can, for +2 difficulty, pick up on the whole Triat. Ratkin get Sense Weaver.

      Ananasi don't even get Sense Wyrm.

      Ananasi get a shortish General list, a short Aspect list, and a short Faction list. They get no Breed Gifts. No Apecraft's, no Persuasion, no Spirit Speech. It feels like the writers either assumed the Storyteller would fill in the gaps, or forgot to cover the basics. And say the Storyteller is left to do it. Accusations of unfairness will be inevitable. All Metis Garou, and many Tribes, get Sense Wyrm. So sure, perhaps Sense Wyrm should be easily available to the Ananasi. But what about Weaver and Wyld? Presumably they're very important to your job as well. Then there's Apecraft's; you can't have the perfectionistic Ananasi being inferior to clumsy Garou, but if you give them that Gift in the General list then the whole species gets too powerful, arguably. Same with Persuasion. Any fix a Storyteller comes up with will be guided by player expectations and won't be as well balanced as a dispassionate list made long in advance by a sensible game designer.

      If these weaknesses were addressed quite deliberately, if W20 explained that Ananasi can't talk to spirits since it helps them to avoid ever forming attachments with other spirits, that it's a deliberate design on QA's part, that would make sense... but it would still arguably be very, very silly. Ananasi don't really get any spirits which help them to deal with spirits, despite being able to Crawl Sideways. They also don't get anything that helps them navigate the webs of the Umbra, which is very strange.

      So what we're left with is some impressive unique Gifts, but none of the basics are covered. Ananasi feel unfinished, frankly, and a lot of gaps are left to be filled with Fetishes and Rites, which isn't a good approach. The Gifts available to Ananasi in Revised are more extensive, and worth carefully referencing.

      Oh, and no Cocoon. That alone is a giant shame.

          Hatar

          Dedicated to the Wyrm as it once was, a pure and clean destroyer which permits new life and new patterns, Hatar learn Gifts which draw upon entropy and allow them to better manage creatures which serve the current corrupted Wyrm. The writers seem to have gotten very stuck on an angle of interacting with Vampires, however. You'll see.

          Blood of Pain (1): Anyone who drinks your blood becomes poisoned. Every turn they have to roll Stamina or become paralyzed for one turn.

          Who is going to drink your blood? And if they do, why would they fail a difficulty 6 Stamina roll? And even if they do, they're only paralyzed for a single turn. This Gift combines a weird, niche effect with very weak mechanics.

          Wyrmling Kinship (1): -2 difficulty to all social rolls with servants and creatures of the Wyrm. They tend to see you, narratively speaking, as a friend or someone to ignore. This Gift helps to explain how a Hatar passes as a 'ghoul' at Elysium, or a minor cultist in a Pentex front, or a mysterious ally to a Black Spiral Dancer. Generally speaking this Gift gives you a mechanical license to get involved with the bad guys and sell yourself as their ally, servant, helpless subject and so on.

          Even if you just use this Gift to help with social rolls against Banes (if only you had Spirit Speech!) it would be pretty great.

          It's noteworthy that while this Gift can only be taught by Queen Ananasa, in theory a traitor to her cause could teach this Gift to outsiders who could pass it on. Alternatively a legendary Ragabash or clever Bastet could, in theory, steal this Gift for their people. This is a Gift which could really change the game for enemies of Pentex, and sadly it's tucked away here!

          Blood of Illusion (2): More 'you drink my blood, you get messed up' stuff. This time it's hallucinations. Bad. Keep your Vampire 'friends' in line.

          Call of the Wyrm (2): See the Rank 2 Galliard post. Very good, but very obvious for the Hatar. This is the most over the top power to use on the servants of the Wyrm imaginable; I'd prefer if the Ananasi got a subtle version which draws Wyrm-tainted people, animals and spirits towards a location gradually, with plausible deniability, like a carefully baited trap.

          Corrupt (3): Roll Gnosis, targeting an object. Every success reduces the object's soak by 1.

          This is somewhat annoying as a Gift since, to the best of my ability to skim the book, W20 doesn't have rules for damaging objects. Whoops.

          If you use M20's Durability and Structure rules, where Durability is like Soak and Structure is like Health, a vault might have a Durability of 8 and a Structure of 10. So, with this Gift, you could reduce the bank vault's soak from 8 down to 0 by rolling a cumulative 8 successes. Neat. Then you still need to roll a bunch of damage to get inside, but the lack of any soak means you can actually get in, and it helps narratively speaking too.

          Pulse of the Invisible (3): See the Rank 3 Theurge list. Note that Ananasi treat the Gauntlet very differently; it's very likely that you will find yourself able to see through the Gauntlet very easily in high gauntlet areas, and struggling to see through it in places where Garou find it easy.

          Ill Winds (4): Spend 3 Blood, roll Intelligence + Occult (difficulty 5), every success reduces the local Gauntlet's strength by 1, for 1 hour per success. So, for example, with 5 successes you could reduce the Gauntlet from 8 to 3 for 5 hours. Keep in mind that this will make it much harder for you and other Ananasi to get through the Gauntlet unless you have a Fetish or an ally who can help you through.

          While this Gift is sold as helping Banes to materialize, it can be used for any number of purposes. You could set things up in advance to help a Garou pack win a battle you want Pentex to lose, or you could weaken the Gauntlet in a place where Banes will inevitably harm humans who are helpful to the Wyrm's corrupt works, allowing the mad Wyrm to undo itself.

          Still Blood (4): Anyone you splash with your blood (no mechanics given) loses 3 points of Strength, which return at a rate of 1 point per hour. This is a neat trick, but we're not given an example of how this splashing would happen. From you getting hurt? Hard to say. Assuming you just splash it onto a foe deliberately with a Dexterity + Athletics, this is... sorta weak? -3 Strength will paralyze even a strong human, great, and that's a handy trick, but at Rank 4 your serious problems relate to powerful enemies. Being able to just take a NPC out of a scenario is handy, especially when it comes to physically frail foes, so this isn't useless... It's just quite underwritten.

          Since we don't have concrete mechanics for how you get this Gift going, this will remain unrated. If I had to rate it, it'd probably be Red.

          Burning Blood (5): Contact with your blood now inflicts (Gnosis) dice of damage. Great. The Vampires feeding on Rank 5 Ananasi better watch out!

          Naturally you could splash your blood on an enemy, but we don't have mechanics for that. If combined with a Gift which allowed you to do so, like Blood Webs (see the Ahadi Gifts) this might be better.

                      Kumoti

                      The Kumoti are devoted to the Wyld, making them dynamic creatives who bring about change through subtle adjustments. Keep in mind that like all Ananasi, the Kumoti are meant to represent their aspect of the Triat as it ought to be, not as it is; the Wyld in its current state does not fit with Queen Ananasa's designs, and the Kumoti help to encourage new life and growth through understanding the tapestry, not simply bringing about wanton chaos.

                      Unfortunately their Gifts don't do much to help with this.

                      Inspire (Breath of the Wyld), Mother's Touch
                      Insight of the Great Mother (Sight from Beyond)
                      Sense Motion (Scent of Sight)
                      Mindblock, Nature of the Beast (Feral Lobotomy)
                      Assimilation

                      Note that Sense Motion is slightly different from Scent of Sight in its flavour; rather than seeing the world through scent, you see the world through motion detection. The Gift is also Level 3 for Ananasi, instead of Level 2. Nature of the Beast works as Feral Lobotomy, but fades at the end of the scene, making it more precise but also arguably weaker. Getting it a level earlier isn't an improvement given that Beast Mind is just far better. I would also question the utility of Sight from Beyond for Ananasi since they commune with Queen Ananasa directly on demand; if the Storyteller wants you to have a vision or umbral message there is already something far better which would be a high-Level Gift for most Breeds but is part of the Ananasi starter kit, much like Regeneration is basic for Garou but a Level 5 Gift for others.

                      Arachnophobia (2): You roll to inflict someone with arachnophobia, making them irrationally (rationally?) afraid of spiders. This costs Gnosis, and the effects are wholly mundane with no explicit mechanics tied to them. You just give this character the relevant phobia, for a point of Gnosis, and the roll (Manipulation + Intimidation, difficulty of the target's Willpower) might even fail!

                      Terrible. This Gift is left to the storyteller to figure out, but what we're given is very weak.

                      Alter Lilian (3)*: Cosmetically alter your Lilian form. This would be okay at Level 1, but it's the most powerful unique Gift the Kumoti get, and it's dressup. Making your Lilian form look scorpion-like at will is a pretty niche ability. Yes, this is a sort of 'disguise' in a sense, you could change the appearance of your Lilian form regularly to avoid getting a rep, but you have a memory-removing Gift as standard and you also can change how your Lilian looks by eating loads of arachnids. If you want to go all scorpiony, just start eating a scorpion heavy diet, don't waste 9/15xp on this Gift.

                      If you read "rearranging it to suit her whim" very, VERY generously, in a way unlikely to be intentional, it might let you speak normally, be more beautiful and so on. If you take the wording of the Gift literally and take that literal wording to its conclusion, a beautiful spider-person who an entire cult can see as a god-figure... well the problem is that you still inflict Delirium. If, if your ST lets you mess around with the Lilian form very freely, gaining benefits like an eye in your back or a poison gland in the tip of your finger (for mixing drinks!), this might be alright.

                      The Kumoti have very few unique Gifts, and nothing that actually helps them with their highly esoteric work, except for Insight of the Great Mother, which is pretty redundant. Sadly they can't even sense where the Wyld is being stifled by the Weaver or Wyrm, and they don't have anything that endears them to Wyld spirits.

                      It's also time to point out that many Ananasi Gifts just have terrible names. Breath of the Wyld worked fine. Making it "Inspire" just makes it entirely generic. Nature of the Beast just feels like a poor reverse translate of Feral Lobotomy. Sight From Beyond is also a perfectly fine name for the Gift, there's nothing wolfy or un-spidery about it. It's like Queen Ananasa is insecure and needs to brand every Gift to suit herself... which is very thematic and in-character for her, it's just a shame that they spoilt the Gift list for it!

                          Myrmidon

                          This Faction are the warriors of their Aspect; a Hatar Myrmidon might selectively battle servants of the Wyrm who are lynchpins of its corruption, and whose replacements may take a more fitting 'destroyer' role. Naturally Ananasi lack the Regeneration most Changing Breeds possess, so even their most ardent warriors will still make good use of spider tactics, ambush, traps and so on.

                          Illusion of Size (Icy Chill of Despair), Open Seal
                          True Fear
                          Weak Arm
                          Drying Bite (Whelp Body)

                          There's a bad habit in these lists of finding multiple Gifts which do the same thing and giving them to one Fera. So, for example, Weak Arm and Whelp Body do vaguely the same thing. Instead of picking whichever is better, or more suitable, or making a good Gift which covers a similar concept but is unique to the werespiders, instead the Ananasi get their list clogged with both! I'm just glad that they had enough restraint to keep Open Seal's name the same instead of changing it to something like Open Spider, since you don't want to name-drop a mammal in the spiders and bugs chapter.

                          Hydraulic Strength (2): See above. Myrmidons get access to this at Level 2 rather than Level 4, making it much cheaper, but very importantly, also making it arrive much earlier.

                          Scorpion Tail (3): This costs 3 blood points and creates a very mediocre weapon. Strength +2 Aggravated (difficulty 7) isn't a great attack. The key upside of this Gift is that the attack is entirely independent of your limbs and your mouth, and that you get to inject your Ananasi venom (totally undefined) and poison from any poison Gifts (the Myrmidon list has none).

                          If the Hatar blood-based Gifts could be injected with this it wouldn't be bad. Still, the Ananasi list doesn't have many Gifts that work well with this attack, and spending 3 Blood to access an attack which is worse than your claws and teeth doesn't feel great at all.

                          Blood Hunt (4): This is Pulse of the Prey, but acquired two ranks later and with a significant disadvantage. The Ananasi follow all the same rules as with Pulse, but with the following restriction; the Gift requires you to have encountered the target in the past day, or to have fed upon their blood at some point in the past. Honestly just rewriting the Gift so that you can always find anyone you've fed on in the past would be better.

                          The takeaway is that this is Amber. If you can learn Pulse of the Prey as an "out-of-breed" Gift, do so.

                          Image of the Great Mother (5): This Gift requires you to spend 2 Gnosis and then pass a Charisma + Rituals (difficulty 9).

                          Immediately this is akin to the worst Gifts in W20, but sure. If you succeed you enter a very powerful form which gives you Strength +5, Stamina +6, Perception +2. You inflict Delirium on everyone, not just humans (though people who would normally be immune can roll Willpower, and shapeshifters who fall prey to Delirium suffer Reduced Delirium).

                          That is a very powerful Gift, and it will combine well with Carapace. With a base Stamina of 5, and this Gift plus Carapace, your soak will be 15 base, before other bonuses. Great! But the problem is that this Gift requires a difficulty 9 roll to activate. Even ignoring that, spending 2 Gnosis isn't cheap, and the Gift has an added drawback; it lasts for one scene and after that you're forced to stay in Crawlerling for a full week.

                          This Gift, to me, is a great example of why the Ananasi's Gift list needs a complete overhaul. Which is very sad given how interesting they can be.

                                  IMHO "Crinos shouldn't last indefinitely!" is a skill issue on the part of all involved. If you have frictionless, riskless, uncomplicated battles where there is no need for the other forms, ever, then yeah. Nerf Crinos.

                                      Viskr

                                      This Faction are judges and mystics, akin to Theurges and Philodox. If only they got Spirit Speech or Sense Wyrm or Sense Magic or anything to help them detect lies. Annoyingly a lot of Weaver-like Gifts find themselves here rather than in the Tenere list.

                                      Shroud
                                      Mindspeak
                                      Cocoon
                                      Attunement

                                      Curse of the Great Web (1): Touch a target and roll Wits + Occult (difficulty 6). The Gauntlet is treated as 3 points thicker for them this scene.

                                      No cost, and the effect is somewhat neat. In a typical urban environment a Garou might be at Difficulty 7 to step sideways, while this Gift makes it Difficulty 10! Not amazing, but solid. A major use will be vs spirits, to stop them from manifesting or from retreating through the Gauntlet.

                                      Minor Unweaving (2): Spend 1 Gnosis, roll Dexterity + Occult (difficulty 6), and cancel out ANYTHING which is Level 2 or lower. Level 1-2 Disciplines, Cantrips, Rotes, Gifts, and sufficiently weak Charms. This is an incredibly powerful ability which will completely change the way you interact with other supernaturals. Keep in mind that Level 1-2 doesn't necessarily mean weak. Protean 2 is deadly by Vampire standards, many Level 1-2 Gifts are incredible, and if you broaden things up to include Changelings you'll see even more ridiculous powers. If you're playing crossover this Gift is even better, but it shines perfectly with only WTA as well.

                                      Calcify (3): Roll Gnosis, difficulty 7, and each success increases the soak of an object or area. The area you fortify can't be bigger than 1 acre, and it lasts for 1 day per dot of Obedience.

                                      If this works on armour, and I can't see why it wouldn't, this is pretty incredible. Use it on Fetishes, on vehicles, on crucial objects which form part of your plan, on restraints, on a cage, and so on. There's also no reason you can't use this on your own webs! The lack of a cost and the generous duration mean that this Gift, while focused on fortifying objects, has limitless applications.

                                      Brethren Call (4): This is an SOS power, or a means of summoning your closest allies. Spend 1 Blood, roll Willpower, difficulty 7 for spiders and 8 for Ananasi. For every success you get 50 spiders, and success with the difficulty 8 version calls upon the 2 closest Ananasi. The Ananasi you summon aren't compelled to come, but they sense your call immediately and can react as they wish.

                                      Perfectly reasonable ability, could have been front-loaded to help with the Viskr's role, but good all the same.

                                      Shattering (5): Similar to Minor Unweaving, but costs 3 Gnosis and works on anything up to Level 5. That means only Level 6+ Abilities, the domain of Elder Vampires, Legendary Shifters, Incarnae Spirits and so on, will resist.

                                      Game-changing, role-defining, suitably strong for a Level 5 Gift. If this were a Garou Gift I'd expect it to be Level 6; I don't mind this being Level 5 for the Ananasi since it's so key to their identity to mess with the tapestry. Just wish they had more tools to do so on the way here, like basic sensory abilities!

                                          Wyrsta

                                          The Wyrsta are the Questioners, perhaps akin to Ragabash, spending their days and nights questioning their Aspect's nature, its place in the Tapestry, and pondering viable replacements. They also handle testing and assessing other Ananasi.

                                          Beastmind
                                          Visceral Agony
                                          (No pre-existing Level 3 Gifts)
                                          Wither Limb
                                          Summon Net-Spider

                                          Getting Beastmind a whole Rank early is pretty great. Reducing someone to an animalistic intelligence feels more like a Wyld Gift, in keeping with the Kumoti, but whatever; the logic of these lists is lost on me. Visceral Agony also arrives a Rank early!

                                          Alter Mood (1): Enhance or dampen a mood massively. Happiness becomes elation, despair becomes sadness. So long as an emotion exists, even a very weak one, you can massively amplify it.

                                          This costs 1 Gnosis, but there is no roll, and the only way to resist this Gift would be immunity to emotional powers, or having something which blocks incoming Gifts or provides a chance at 'counterspelling', which is very rare. Extremely solid for 3xp. Example uses would be making a frightened person utterly terrified, help with seduction or feeding, or reducing the consequences of conflicts.

                                          Blinding Spit (2): Spend 1 blood, roll Dexterity + Melee (difficulty 8), on a success you blind the target for a number of turns equal to your Cunning. Given that you're spitting you'd think it was Athletics, or Brawl, or perhaps even Dexterity + Stamina if you're feeling spicy, but whatever. This Gift would be best against a target who you can get a difficulty reduction against.

                                          Given you get some great Dexterity bonuses with your various forms this is pretty neat. I won't say it's incredibly powerful, it has its uses, and it will help out now and then. Just remember that difficulty 8 is worse than most people think, and a significant step up from difficulty 7.

                                          Tick Body (3): Bonus Blood; pretty amazing! This Gift grants you a Blood pool of 15, 20 or 25 depending on your Rank. Given that Blood is healing, bonus actions and so on, it's very strange to me that this is a faction-specific Gift, or that it isn't a Myrmidon Gift, but it's great all the same.

                                          Blades of the Mantis (4): Another Myrmidon-like Gift! Blades of the Mantis is a very impressive weapon transformation. Two of your limbs become mantis-like swords which you can launch Brawl attacks with. The weapons are long, inflict Strength + 4 Aggravated, and you make your attacks at difficulty 5. This is enough of an upgrade from biting that I would personally call this Green, even at Rank 4. The utility of keeping your foes at arm's length and safely decapitating them rather than dragging them into your face, near your vital organs, is excellent. No reason you can't slice up someone who is webbed up two feet away for instance.

                                          Razor Webs (5): If you choose to make razor webbing, using the normal web mechanics, then anything touching it takes one automatic unsoakable level of Aggravated each turn, unless they stay perfectly still. If you use this Gift on an enemy they may well kill themselves trying to escape, or suffer serious injuries in the process. The possibility of using this Gift to block doorways, make traps and so on is also great.

                                          Given that this buffs a core mechanic of your Breed and deals unsoakable automatic damage, this gets Green, despite the high bar for Level 5 Gifts.
