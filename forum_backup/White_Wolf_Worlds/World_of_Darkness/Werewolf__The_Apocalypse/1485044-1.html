                            Here's the update, we spend the whole evening playing the session, being reasonnable with the length. Okay, we *may* have been reminded of the external world by the apparition of Helios in the sky, but that's not important.

                            So, the judgement was not very much in Sargon's favor, as you have expected. Howether, the glass walker elder convinced, at first, the others to allow a form of redemption.

                            He "pleaded madness" so to speak, affirming that after too much time with vampires, Sargons was subtly wyrm tainted.

                            Of course, none of the other elders swallowed it, but they were not against finding a way to not kill Sargon without angering the rest of the sept (that's of course an info the players and the sept didn't have).

                            They agreed on sending him, with an escort, to the realm of Cerberus, to be purged in it laked of molten silver.

                            Howether, the child of gaia elder (you know, the one who hates kindred and asked for the attack) wasn't happy with otherwordly torture, she wanted the vampires to pay.

                            So she agreed to sending him to Cerberus at one condition: Sargon must meet and murder his contacts amongs the kindred, to prove that he understand his fault, that his real loyalty goes to the garous and to prove that he desire redemption.

                            So, happy (but torturous) ending right?

                            Nope, Sargon heard the demand, and after a minute of thinking, straight out refused.

                            He stated that he will stand by his principles, notably that he would not harm someone who accepted to become a contact, and for some, something like friends, despite the danger of working with lupines. And that if anything caused the death of the two warriors, it was "the puerile vengeance of a deranged elder who cares more about settling scores with the undead rather than doing her job of fighting the wyrm" and that i was because of incompetent clown hiding behind their rank like her that garous dies in useless battles and that Gaia is dying".

                            He then proceeded to tell everyone present their home truths, things he knew thanks to his contacts, including the Bastet and Corax players.

                            There was shock, outrage, dramatic fainting of a tightass kinfolk (he's a recurring joke of the campaign), many frenzy checks, many failed frenzy checks, and after calming everyone, including usingthe "elder-slap-of-taking-the-younglings-out-of-frenzy" technique, the three elders all agreed that Sargon should be executed. (The spirit of the cairn refused to give it's verdict, or talk to anyone, Sargon revealed too much secretly broken oaths from the caern members).

                            You might have facepalmed yourselves reading this story, after all, Sargon had a paved way toward forgiveness and did the worse thing possible. (And as another player stated "you could have accepted and run away while pretending to go to your targets") but it was epic for the table, it was daring, a satisfying conclusion to the researchs made by the aforementioned PCs on the sins of the sept members, and it made us fall into a dozen of minutes of endless laughting while roleplaying the drama following the revelations.

                            Oh, and he nearly erased another PC from existence. At one point, he called the child of gaia, well, something I cannot repeat on a forum for fear of the banhammer, but it was so not expected that one the player bursted in laughters, spitting a whole handful of coffee on his character sheet. Which was miraculously saved by a strange technique of drenching it into water and drying it like an old photograph.

                            Yes, we sometime think he's a legit magician.

                            Back to the story. Sargon was condemned to execution the next day as the elder didn't want more garou bloodshed the same day the two warriors died (they died around 3am and the judgement happened in the beginning of the afternoon). So he was chained with silver in a caban on the ground of the caern until the next midnight.

                            The corax PC, who spied the whole thing in crow form, warned the other PCs, a bastet and two ananasis.

                            And they began to plot to save him before the execution.

                            And they did so by using a tactic I've never seen in a dozen of WOD campaign.

                            They called the police.

                            And they told mostly the truth, they have a friend who entered a "cult" in the woods and after failling to prove himself by murdering people the cult deemed "controlled by the dark forces" and that they will execute him in the night.

                            Of course, this being the world of darkness, the police would normally not investigate this, if only because of the leading officer was a kinfolk of the caern who uses his position to hide the actions of the garou, but said officer was "mysteriously absent" that day.

                            Don't worry, the kinfolk is alright, although he might need psychotherapy after being attacked, bound in web, kidnapped and abandonned in the forest by a mass of hundreds of spiders.

                            And the bastet finished to convince the leading officer in function using the eternal power of apparence 5 + "crime agaisnt decency" t-shirt.

                            So Sargon and the sept were surprised by two dozens of armed policemen. The elders had to explain themselves to a famous character *probably* used by every people running games in modern day france: cliché policeman with a south of france accent.

                            No seriously, if you ever saw any kind of french comical movie with policemen, you know the character.

                            Cue another laughting context, Sargon, understanding what's happening, play the role of a traumatised victim, and he's taken under legal protection. Of course, not trusting the ability of mere mortals to protect him from a bunch of garous, the group organised their extraction.

                            And this is how it ended

                                                            The repercussions of this should be massive. Sargon is now an escaped fugitive, and the sept has every reason to believe that Sargon's allies (whether they know it is these other Fera, or they imagine it was arranged by his vampire friends) purposefully endangered the Veil to arrange his escape.

                                                            The Punishment Rites in general are only able to be used if the guilty Garou is present. But at this point, he's already been been proven guilty and sentenced. Having fled, I definitely believe when he is caught he'd be sentenced to the rite of Gaia's Vengeful Teeth. All the other Garou septs in the local area are going to be informed. There does not necessarily need to be some kind of manhunt to find him. Just all Garou have probably been informed of his crimes and given his description. If he's discovered, many septs will attempt to take him into custody or kill him themselves, while others may just spread the word about where and when he was seen. There may even be a concolation called at some point, and what the area's septs should do about the fugitive would be an agenda item. Sargon might even attract the attention of packs looking for glory by eliminating the fugitive, or Shadow Lords in the Judges of Doom camp.

                                                            There's also the question about Renown loss, and whether a sept can cause it without the Garou being present. This matters because if the ST decides the sept can, there's probably enough temporary Renown loss to translate into permanent Renown loss that could affect Sargon's rank. He would have lost a lot of Wisdom Renown and a ton of Honor Renown due to all of his violations.

                                                            This also brings up a question about Garou fleeing justice in general. If one can escape the typical penalties of Punishment Rites and Renown loss by just fleeing, and nothing bad happens unless the Garou of the sept (or another) can catch up to him, it creates a lot of bad precedent. There's a big difference between some scofflaw that avoids minor judgments, and someone sentenced to a major Punishment Rite who flees. Can the Garou really do nothing in the meantime before he's caught (if ever)?

                                                            There are actually options.

                                                            First. it is not necessary for the person who is the object of the Rite of the Silver Forge to be present. The rite participants do not have to be in the presence of the target - all that is required is that the know the target's Garou name and be within 100 miles of the target.

                                                            In other words, Sargon fleeing the Rite does not mean the Rite cannot be performed. The sept can carry it out anyway - presumably that very night once they escape the comical French police.

                                                            Since it is very unlikely that the rite is performed "unjustly", it should work. Therefore the next time Sargon steps sideways, he'll be sent to Erebus instead.

                                                            Of course, if Sargon is ever released by Charyss in Erebus, he still has to answer for his new crimes in attempting to escape justice ad endangering the Veil, which will mean another (very quick) trial and sentencing, most likely the aforementioned rite of Gaia's Vengeful Teeth.

                                                            Second, what about a hypothetical situation where the Rite of the Silver Forge was not the rite to be used, but one which requires the participant to be there? While the Rite of Silver Forge is an option if the Garou retains some hope of redemption, perhaps the sept does not want to use it. There is the ultra-rare Rite of the Shattered Soul, a rite only found in the first edition Werewolf Players Guide. It's even worse than Gaia's Vengeful Teeth, and the way it is written, it is not needed for the Garou to be there when enacted (though it's mentioned the rite participant through the dead's Garou body on the flames to be consumed - so this is something often done after the usual death penalty or killing during combat). Of course, being a first edition product, the rite as described is missing a lot of information that would be provided for the updated Punishment Rites. Any ST would need to make certain adjustments. It call spirits to inform everyone else in the Tellurian that the Garou is disgraced and no longer part of the Garou or Gaia, as he is an agent of the Wyrm. One of the things I do for my chronicles is say this Rite is held in reserve for lawbreakers in order to discourage nonsense just like this. Now there are some aspects of this Rite that many STs may not like (it states that the punished Garou will be reborn as a Bane), and it's probably why it was left out of future sourcebooks. But even so, I imagine there should be some rite similar to it that can be used against fugitives to discourage people from attempting to flee, or to encourage fugitives to return to justice. Such a rite would probably spread the world to whomever the spirits can, as well as inflict automatic Renown loss. No matter the specifics of such a rite the ST feels comfortable with, some form of that rite would benefit the setting precisely because of situations like this. It wouldn't need to be done in all cases, perhaps only reserved for major crimes. But it would be something I think some STs might like to have in their chronicles.
