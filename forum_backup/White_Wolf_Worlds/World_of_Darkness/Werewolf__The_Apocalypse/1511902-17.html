    Homid Gifts (Changing Ways)

    Weaver's Eyes (2): Spend a Gnosis. For one scene you get to hear everyone declare their actions before you choose yours, regardless of your position in initiative. This is inferior to actually having better initiative of course (Spirit of the Fray is far, far better than this), but if you're playing the game exactly as intended the strategic benefits you get from this Gift could be very handy.

    For example, the guy who is going to go first may well be planning to cover the floor of the whole lab with a caustic slime. Knowing this, you might bake crossing the room in an unusual way into your plan for your turn rather than having to change plans when moving. For a highly strategic game where the rules are followed carefully this could be pretty great! The problem is the Gnosis cost, combined with the fact that it needs to be activated at all. This could be entirely passive and still work.

    Rally the Troops (3): This Gift lets you rally bystanders (but not supernatural bystanders like Garou or supernaturally powerful Kinfolk) to help you and also grants bonus dice to a task the group could help you with. There is no activation cost and the Charisma + Leadership is only difficulty 6. If the group is mostly Kinfolk you also find the task easier, at -1 difficulty. Important NPCs do get to opt out of your rallying call if they have good reason to.

    This is pretty neat; it would arguably be fine at Rank 2, but it somewhat overcomes the problem of The Curse when it comes to interacting with large groups of humans, so long as your goal is short term intense group action. It's important to note that what you rally the crowd to do doesn't have to be violent, the examples given include protest, rescue and heckling. You could use this Gift to inspire the bull-pen to trade like they've never traded before, which would suit a banking Ragabash Glass Walker perfectly. Assuming an urban setting this is handy enough to be Green.

    Web of Knowledge (4): This one is a little hard to parse. It basically lets you pick up info from the ether. I won't do a deep dive into the mechanics, the main thing is that it lets you do a bunch of types of rolls as a simple check without having to take time to research, examine and so on. It's a cool concept, the Gnosis cost makes sense since it feels pretty magical, and it lets you do that BBC Sherlock "I figured it out using magic, I mean, genius" trick where you make giant leaps in logic and then the plot agrees with you. Which is bad TV, but great in a game. The max difficulty of any check you can make with this Gift is 7, since anything of higher difficulty is too obscure.

    Metis Gifts (Changing Ways)

    Stoat's Guise (2): As always I love Metis Gifts which pick up on the weirder capabilities of animals who would be so easily overlooked by Garou. Metis being beloved by the 'oddballs' of nature is a lovely theme to me. Here we have a Gift based on species who change fur types depending on the season. With a Manipulation + Subterfuge (difficulty 6) you can alter features of your body to suit your needs. Plus or minus 20% on size, skin tone, hair type, eye colour, fur length, hiding scars, adjusting markings, you name it. Assuming a decent dice pool you can pull off a dazzling array of disguises with this Gift, though of course your scent is going to stay the same. Incredible for any Garou, but especially for a Metis Ragabash who will find themselves changing their looks and suppressing their scent.

    Notably this Gift can never totally hide a Metis deformity, which actually makes albinism have a sleeper downside! My Metis is an absolute giant, a unit, and as well as being very obviously blind his hair is a very stark white. Even if he made himself 20% smaller, changed his skull shape, shortened his hair and gave himself a few scars, at absolute most he'd wind up looking like himself slouched over with a haircut. If you have a very distinctive Metis deformity chances are the disguise potential of this Gift will remain limited, but it's still a solid Green.

    Deny the Birthright (3)*: This Gift lets a Metis ignore their Metis deformity for a whole scene, though at great risk to their Honour if they are part of the Garou Nation. The Gift requires a point of Willpower and Gnosis each, and lasts for one scene, though it can be extended at a rate of one Scene per point of Gnosis spent. While using this Gift the Metis can't regain Gnosis.

    Being found to possess this Gift costs you a permanent dot of Honour, and being caught using it costs you a temporary point of both Honour and Wisdom. The Garou Nation and Litany see a Metis' deformities as being a punishment from Gaia for the sin of their birth after all. If you aren't a member of the Garou Nation, or you are part of an ultra-progressive Sept of Children of Gaia or Glass Walkers, it's possible that these Renown penalties won't apply to you, much as in many Septs an Elder won't lose Renown for refusing to end their life if they develop a disability which requires care. The value of this Gift is inherently variable, and is determined by the deformity (blindness vs albinism) and by politics. It's balanced and well written, but impossible to rate.

    Rage of the Underdog (3): This is a Gift which depends on you suffering your wound penalties instead of ignoring them, which we know I dislike. The ideal scenario is that you're at -2 from wounds, your target has no wounds at all, you spend a point of Rage when you hit, you deal +2 dice of damage, and if you put them to -2 on wound penalties or beyond you get back that point of Rage. A situational bonus to your damage pool if you are damaged and your opponent isn't is... okay. A handy passive. The Rage cost is annoying however.

    Ultimately you are better off ignoring your wounds entirely. Get Resist Pain, or high Willpower, or a method of Willpower restoration. Or invest in dodging. Or healing.

    Tenacious Fury (4): This Gift lets you spend Rage on your Remain Active roll to guarantee successes, but it almost certainly guarantees that you will Frenzy, since you can't spend Willpower to resist Frenzy if you use this Gift, and frenzying requires 3 successes rather than 4 if you utilize it.

    A potential use of this Gift is to spend a number of Rage points to ensure that you will fully heal yourself back to normal when you do the test, say for example you are an Ahroun and you spend 5 Rage to get 5 successes, plus whatever you happen to roll on the test. The problem with this is that Thrall of the Wyrm is almost certain! But, life or death situations... all in all it might be best to spend a single point of Rage on this to help ensure you get a good bit of healing, or perhaps 2 or 3 points. This Gift could do without its downsides, but the ability to help save your life is pretty neat.

    Lupus Gifts (Changing Ways)

    Go for the Jugular (2): I've seen this Gift cited as the reason Changing Ways is BUSTED and BROKEN and should NEVER BE ALLOWED. My hot take is that this Gift isn't so bad. It should be +2 difficulty, not +3, absolutely, but Rage is best spent on bonus actions, or to avoid being stunned if necessary. Spending it to make a soak roll be difficulty 9 instead of difficulty 6 doesn't increase the maximum amount of damage you do, it just reduces the average amount the target soaks. Let's be as generous as possible and say our target has a soak pool of 8 (being a BSD or perhaps a Spirit), which sees their average soak fall from 3.5 to 1.8. That's not a giant damage spike, it just reduces the amount soaked by ~2. +2 damage is great, don't get me wrong, but there are more busted things in W20 than +2 damage. There are Fetish weapons which double your damage output, for instance.

    If you think this Gift is too busted, experiment with +2 difficulty to soak instead of +3. I personally like that this Gift allows a Lupus to successfully chomp a Stamina 8 Fortitude 8 Ventrue Methuselah. No, the Gift won't cause that Vampire to instantly implode, but it will reduce their soak by a good bit, from an average of 6.5 to 2.7, which might make the difference between some damage and no damage. Do keep in mind that you still need to successfully hit, and spend the Rage on every hit you want to use the Gift with, and roll good damage for the Gift to have been worth it.

    Shattering Howl (3): Wow. Gaia's true enemy. Shoddily made structures. This Gift lets you destroy stuff, stuff made by man's hands, how iconic, groundbreaking in fact. We've seen this Gift before a dozen times, this one costs Gnosis and the list of things it destroys is weirdly limited, don't take it.

    Hibernation (4): A pretty complex and wordy Gift which lets you slip into hibernation for prolonged periods. Successes can result in a week, month, year or decade of hibernation, depending on expenditure. This Gift might be plot-relevant, but if it is, the party can't benefit from it unless they're all Rank 4 Lupus Garou. Which is silly. This falls into the classic "should have been a Rite" situation. Don't take this. If the ST needs you and the pack to be hibernating for 100 years, that will happen.

        Ragabash Gifts (Changing Ways)

        Disguise Fetish (1): A cost-free ability to make a Fetish look like a mundane object. Roll Manipulation + Subterfuge, difficulty 6, which makes a Talen or Fetish look ordinary. Any special ability to find Fetishes resists the successes you get. The result lasts for one scene.

        This is a handy way for a Ragabash to get away with having a Klaive. If I were STing and there were a Rank 1 Ragabash with this Gift who started with a 4-5 point Fetish, I'd be much more likely to go "yeah, this works!" especially if the story of the acquisition was interesting and well thought out. Many Gifts in this vein cost Gnosis, so this is refreshingly cheap to use.

        Petal Float (2)*: Roll a Dexterity + Athletics while falling, difficulty 6, every success reduces your fall distance by 10 feet. Pretty neat, though honestly a Gift which just makes you immune to fall damage by spending a Rage/Gnosis or equivalent could work fine.

        Say you were falling 100 feet, terminal velocity in W20's rules, you take 10 dice of lethal damage. So, every success with this Gift reduces your number of dice of damage by 1. For a Lupus Ragabash this Gift could combine very well with Hare's Leap. If you want to use this Gift aggressively, tackle a target off of a cliff. That said, at Rank 3 you get Catfeet which makes you fully immune to falls; for most Garou it would be better to get Catfeet. Personally I would refund the cost of this Gift, but the overlap does make this potentially Red.

        Play Dead (2): Stamina + Subterfuge, difficulty 5, no cost. In the course of one action you take a form resembling a corpse. If used outside of battle this makes you seem like you've been dead for a few days, if used in a battle this makes you seem like a fresh corpse. The effect lasts for one turn per success.

        Using the Gift in response to a fake 'killing blow' costs a point of Gnosis, which is the fresh corpse option. Either way you are stiff, you can return to breed form in the same way a Garou would upon dying, you lack a pulse and your body matches what you'd expect for a corpse in every way. Interestingly you could probably use this to pretend to be a Vampire, though timing would be important, and it's unclear whether the Gift ends if you move or 'break' the illusion by acting alive.

        Handy gift, neat effect, definitely worth taking if you like the concept and you have 6xp spare. Unfortunately many enemies of the Garou love to infest, desecrate, burn, devour etc. the corpses of their foes, so this could be a bit niche, but well worth using against many foes. Better to 'die' to Pentex so you can later rescue your kidnapped packmates rather than be captured with them, or killed at the scene of the battle.

        Terrain Shift (3): This Gift lets you create a means to travel through the wilderness (a small bridge or tunnel, thick thorns opening to let you through) or close a path behind him. It costs a point of Gnosis and requires no roll, which should be the case for more Gifts. If you want to open a path for yourself and then close it behind you, that costs 2 Gnosis.

        This is solid enough, and there's nothing stopping you from using this Gift for ambushes, guerilla tactics, escapes from wilderness crime scenes, accessing a glade or Caern or similar through a path that should be impossible, and so on.

        Misdirection (4): This Gift permits a Ragabash to essentially distract a target in a way which lets them get away with daylight robbery or murder. The cost is a single Willpower point, and the Gift utilizes the Fast-Talk mechanics from W20 Corebook. More Gifts should key off of core mechanics like this; Hare's Leap is a good example of a Gift which works with a basic mechanic instead of introducing a new one.

        The problem is that victims of fast-talking can simply spend a point of Willpower to overpower your nonsense. The core mechanic this Gift keys off is a bit... unsteady, and this Gift doesn't fix that. Since the basic mechanic is a little wonky, it's hard to rate this. Assuming that this Gift works as the flavour intends, and you can basically dazzle a single target while doing insanely provocative stuff like searching through their wallet or replacing their pills with poison... it's neat. But for a Level 4 Gift? Not good enough. Assuming you follow the RAW, where an enemy can spend a WP to go "okay, no, ignoring this" as per W20 Core, this Gift is sadly Red.

                        Theurge Gifts (Changing Ways)

                        Hear the Silence (1): This Gift allows you to hear when someone has a secret weighing on them, and the intensity of its importance from their POV. An example of the Gift in action would be using it when a Kinfolk has died. You might notice that another Kinfolk is carrying a very large secret - it would be easy to assume that the secret is that they killed their own kin, but it may be that there was some unknown tie between them, or they have stolen some inheritance, or they suspect who the killer is (rightly or wrongly) and isn't telling anyone.

                        The Gift requires activation and costs a point of Gnosis, but has no roll requirement. This makes it useful and interesting; you can drop this Gift during a classic "gather everyone in the drawing room" scene in a murder mystery. Since the Gift gives you no clues as to what the secret is, but context will likely lead you in the right direction, and simply interrogating the target is a valid (though potentially difficult) option, this is pretty good. If it didn't give you a sense of how important or intense the secret is to the person this Gift wouldn't be that great.

                        Simply because this costs 3xp, has no roll, and you can trigger this in a moment where you discover there is a doppleganger hiding in your pack and likely get led to the target, I'll call it a solid Green. Having this option can solve problems you have no other reasonable way of solving.

                        Eyes of the Lynx (2): ... it's very weird to give Theurges cost-free xray vision in a supplementary book. Rolling Perception + Alertness difficulty 6 lets you see through solid matter for the whole scene, but only through a number of yards equal to your successes. So with 2 successes you'd be able to see a body buried under 6 feet (2 yards) of earth.

                        This is exceptionally good but it absolutely needs a Gnosis cost. Or even Willpower. The Gift is simply too good as a power you can spam; you can see through walls and ceilings and floors, down into graves, through tombs, you can see enemies hiding behind cover, you can see enemies waiting to ambush you up in the trees etc. all without cost. Very strong, but in the 'so good it might be bad' category.

                        Blood Life (2): This Gift lets you spend a point of Gnosis upon tasting blood (mostly from bite attacks) to get a vision of mystical importance relating to the target. It must be the target's own blood, so according to this Gift if you taste the blood of a vampire you'll get a faint memory from one of their many victims. The vision must be of importance and give you insight, and it could grant 1-5 bonus dice to the next check you make relating to the target (if the ST thinks it makes sense).

                        So, for example, you might taste the blood of your mysterious Ronin ally and see a mighty wolf wearing a silvered crown with many cubs suckling from her. She growls at the runt every time he tries to feed or play, and the pitiful creature turns away from its family. If the Theurge then took a small risk and said "... you know there aren't any Silver Fangs in our Sept, as it happens" while trying to convince the Ronin to come and meet some allies it might add a bonus 1-5 dice to the relevant check. The tricky thing is that you need to ask to taste someone's blood for this whole scenario to play out, or bite them. So this will primarily be used against enemies... though the Gnosis cost means that you can't take Rage actions in the same turn.

                        The Gnosis cost in combination with the other restrictions makes this Gift too niche unfortunately. But the value of a powerful vision relating to a plot-critical enemy could be hugely influential, which saves this from being Red.

                        Watchful Eyes (3): Spending a point of Gnosis reduces the difficulty of all checks to impress, intimidate or persuade by (7 - Gauntlet). So, for example, in a typical village you might reduce your difficulties by 1 due to a Gauntlet of 6. The Gift works by attracting local spirits to judge proceedings, and they will typically act as a backup squad to the Theurge, accentuating what they say and do.

                        If used in the Umbra this reduces the difficulty of such tests to 3 instead. Which... RAW, would turn a difficulty 10 test down to difficulty 3, regardless of the context. This Gift is just a bad idea and poorly written.

                        For starters, the flavour is weird. The judging attention of the world of spirits influencing social tests is a great idea! The interaction with the Gauntlet is a great idea. But the execution is simply bad. My version would be that the Gift is a passive effect where if your Gnosis exceeds the local Gauntlet you gain a certain benefit, and if you want to gain that benefit when the Gauntlet exceeds your Gnosis you spend a point of Gnosis.

                        I'm marking this Green because, strictly RAW, it's a very good Gift. Difficulty 3 social tests are great, this Gift would make you a social powerhouse in a Moot. But Theurges aren't meant to be social powerhouses. Perhaps if the Gift granted you a social benefit if you have a spirit backing you up? So if you're bringing the message of an Incarna you might get +5 to the relevant checks, and a weaker bonus with weaker spirits. Anyways. I wouldn't permit this Gift, not without significant reworking.

                        Spirit Blossom (4): Spend 2 Gnosis, plant some part of your body (which can be fingernails, which is a little lame), and let it grow a spirit plant. Within a week the plant appears, and it's one of your choice. Once per day you can try to take Gnosis from the plant, rolling Gnosis, and getting points of Gnosis equal to your successes. If the plant is destroyed or ripped from its roots you suffer unsoakable Lethal equal to your permanent Gnosis.

                        This... I don't like this. Conceptually, for starters. Gaia is dying. Getting Gnosis hard. Yes, a Gift which is only for Rank 4+ Theurges is a good start for such a Gift, and the investment concept works, the risk works, but ultimately this Gift is just functionally unlimited Gnosis for one PC so long as no one destroys this plant. It's one of those "it's so great, or it's useless" things which is too big a swing from one extreme to the other.

                        The simplest way to deal with this Gift is just to not allow it.

                            Philodox Gifts (Changing Ways)

                            Sense Trauma (1): I would add a note that one success simply gives a sense of whether the target has a Derangement or not, and more successes gives more information. But the RAW Gift works perfectly well; you make a Perception + Empathy (difficulty 6) and success lets you smell things like trauma, split personality, magical insanity and so on. It also lets you identify people who are suffering from the effects of Delirium, precisely what type, which could be useful if you encounter humans who were recently exposed to Garou or Fera and have forgotten, but are still suffering from the after-effects of Delirium.

                            Amazing Roleplay potential with allies and enemies. Determining that the "Ronin" you are chasing is in a Fugue state might lead to you uncovering that they match the description of a missing Garou from elsewhere in the region, one who disappeared when their pack was killed. Now you have a picture of someone who denied his memories and went into a fugue state because he watched his whole pack die, which might help you to help him. Great for the Auspice's job. I would also add this to the Children of Gaia list at my own table. Though I am biased.

                            Firm Stance (2): This Gift is socially defensive, which is rare, and much-needed. When your Garou has a social test made against them, they can spend Willpower (this seems to be an OOC-driven decision based on the flavour of the Gift, not an IC one, so the ST shouldn't be saying "but you don't know anything is up, why are you activating the Gift") to roll their Honour (difficulty 6), and every success reduces the successes on the social test against you. The Gift fails against enemies with higher Honour, including BSDs and Fera notably!

                            Pretty handy, especially when it comes to overcoming lies and bullying. There will be Garou of higher Rank than you whose Honour is lower than yours due to different Auspice requirements.

                            Building a Legend (2): When you gain a permanent dot of Renown, regain your Willpower. Pointless, especially in an Auspice which gets infinite Willpower regeneration. Reworking this Gift so that when you gain a dot of Renown you temporarily get set to 10 points of temporary Willpower would work.

                            Flow Like Water (3): A very valid move when using Rage actions is to devote the main action on your turn to dodging and all of your Rage actions to attacking. This Gift makes that combination far, far better, and also keeps you alive by incentivizing it. Spending a Willpower when dividing your attention between attacking and defending (either with Rage or split dice pools) reduces the difficulty of both by 1 for the round.

                            So, for example, if you were to dodge one attack and then make two attacks, all of them would be at -1 difficulty. -1 difficulty really adds up over time, it means you're inflicting more damage and taking less damage, and I'm a big fan. Solid green, and it's nice to see more combat-refining Gifts on lists other than Ahroun.

                            Rebirth (4): Pointless and bad. You waste a bunch of health bringing an animal back to life... for one scene. NPC power, or perhaps better as a Rite. Perhaps this Gift could allow an animal Kinfolk to mate one last time and keep their species / lineage going, or you could restore a recently dead shark/elephant/tiger to fight for you for the scene, but you're taking a lot of damage to pull this off. The corpse must also be free of Wyrm taint and it can't be too old (Gnosis months).

                            The whole thing needs reworking, either as a better Philodox Gift or as a Rite favoured by the Red Talons. That said, I'm not a fan of the 'necromancy' flavour of this Gift, even if the text implies this is all natural and cool, it will look like necromancy to the Garou Nation and would likely be rejected as such. In my book the Garou Nation will only put up with restoring things to life if those things are Garou, or Kinfolk at a push. See the whole Gurahl thing.

                                Spirit Blossom is in a weird place for me because, from a perspective of just W20, you're completely right. From the perspective of WtA's long history of trying to do powers along these lines... it's the most nerfed version yet (seriously, if you think Spirit Blossom is bad for the themes of the game, look up The Rite of Spirit Brew from 2e).

                                The balance of "Gnosis should be kinda hard to recover for themes," and, "Gnosis needs to be accessible so you can enjoy all the stuff that uses it," is a tough one. And one that really deserves to have more dials for different play styles. M20, for example, made the Quintessence economy of Nodes a dial based on the style of game the ST wants to run. The fact that WtA has consistently opted to make Gnosis the most "management" heavy advantage by making it relatively easy to top up in downtime, but hard to regain in the middle of important events, only makes the desire to get something better with the Gnosis economy that much stronger.

                                Though there's a very simple fix for this that isn't just dumping the whole Gift: Have the presence of your Spirit Blossom make the meditation-for-Gnosis roll better. More frequent rolls (default is 1/week), reduced difficulty (esp. if you make it more personal like Auspice moon based increases to that), more Gnosis per success, etc. As you've said, link stuff back to existing systems if you can.

                                    Galliard Gifts (Changing Ways)

                                    Canine Call (1): With this Gift, a howl will be heard only by Garou and canines (dogs and wolves primarily). This works with the various Garou howls, and with Gifts, but doesn't work with Rites. The Gift costs one Gnosis to activate.

                                    Great quality of life upgrade and a very important lore detail to make the Garou Nation feel like it's feasible. City Galliards will definitely benefit from this Gift.

                                    Battle Cry (2): Roll Charisma + Expression (difficulty 6), and allied Garou who hear the call gain a bonus to initiative rolls (remember that initiative is rerolled every turn) equal to the successes.

                                    Adding a bonus to the initiative of every Garou in your pack, even a small bonus, could completely change the outcome of a fight. Adding a bonus to the initiative of many Garou could change the outcome of a battle or war. And if you're a competent Galliard chances are you'll be granting a decent bonus. With Pure Breed, a good Charisma and Expression, the right specialty and so on chances are you'll be providing a very good bonus consistently.

                                    Ritual Tuning (2): Do a performance to set the stage for a Rite. This Gift costs one Gnosis and requires no roll; you automatically add your dots in Performance (usually 1-5) to the dice pool for the Rite. This could massively improve the chances of success.

                                    If you're having difficulties with this Gift, cap the bonus dice so that they can't exceed the Rituals rating of the Garou performing the Rite. This would stop a dice pool of 2 (inept Theurge Cliath) spiking up to 7 thanks to a rock concert from a Performance 5 Galliard. There is a mention of Performance specialties which is garbled nonsense and hard to understand; it's unclear what benefit the specialty applies to the result of the Gift. An additional +1 die? 10s counting as two successes on the Rite? Very unclear. The Gift is strong even if you can't decipher this weird line.

                                    Social Butterfly (3): A pretty simple yet very wordy Gift where you can set yourself up to later have Contacts. Charisma + Etiquette/Streetwise, difficulty is variable, your successes give dots in Contacts which are temporary and more general 'generic' contacts (I can ask around) as opposed to having a solid high quality contact (I'll call this judge I know). I like that the whole pack benefits also; far, FAR more Gifts should benefit the whole party.

                                    Words Like Wind (4): This Gift lets you force something (a statement, short performance etc.) to go viral, being spread from person to person by word of mouth and also by technology, whatever means it has of spreading. If you make a social roll as part of what you're making go viral, that applies to everyone. Your lie with 10 successes is hitting every ear with 10 successes, typically, even if the people spreading it normally wouldn't sound convincing.

                                    People with Willpower that beats your successes on the test ignore your social roll, but they'll spread the message anyways.

                                    Also, you get Reputation (+3 dice on social rolls) in a 10 mile radius while the Gift is active. All this for a point of Gnosis! It's actually pretty good. With this Gift you can really mess around with information and identity and reputation in ways which wouldn't be possible without magic. You could massively convince people to go green, you could turn a whole town against the company bringing in the new mining venture, you could spread certainty that someone is a killer through a whole jury. This Gift lets you solve the kinds of problems a Rank 4 Galliard may actually be struggling with.

                                        Ahroun Gifts (Changing Ways)

                                        Rhythm of War (1)*: Immunity to being knocked over, and immunity to being stunned by damage. These are both activated by spending a point of Rage, and maintained by attacking every turn. If you don't attack on a given turn you lose the benefit.

                                        If - after the soak roll - a character suffers health-level damage that equals or exceeds her Stamina rating, she’s stunned until the end of the following turn. The stunned character can’t do anything except perhaps stumble around in a daze, and attack rolls against her during that time reduce their difficulty by two.
                                        You can overcome this kind of stunning by spending a point of Rage, so if you got stunned by damage once in the whole fight, this Gift could break even. It would need to happen multiple times to save you Rage. And the problem is that if you know you're in a fight, chances are you're in a Form which grants bonus Stamina. Not necessarily, but probably. And with that form, you'd need to be taking a lot of post-soak damage all at once to get stunned. With a Stamina of 3, and being in Crinos, you'd need to take 6 damage to get stunned. And if that happened you'd spend a point of Rage to ignore the stunning. Even with regeneration and healing, that's unlikely.

                                        Even with this only costing 3xp, it's still... just alright. With the cost and the attack-every-turn aspect, it needs to do more than occasionally stop you from being stunned. It also stops knockdown, which is inflicted by many Gifts and Charms, as well as many battle mechanics. Getting up from being knocked down costs an action, so you'd need to split your dice pool to do it. So this is handy... but the setup and possibility the Gift will stop make it not as strong as you'd hope. If you are constantly running into enemies who knock you prone, this could be Amber or even Green.

                                        Pack Shield (2): This Gift allows you to tank more effectively by putting out a 'taunt' mechanic which forces enemies to attack you in some cases, or suffer penalties if they attack others. Note that this doesn't just protect your pack, it protects ANYONE else that they might attack.

                                        Roll Charisma + Intimidation, difficulty equal to the lowest Willpower of all your enemies. Enemies who try to attack anyone but you suffer a dice penalty equal to your successes, so with 5 successes they'd suffer a -5 die penalty to attacking bystanders, allies and so on. They can attack you as normal, but you may well be very hard to hit. Enemies whose Willpower is lower than your successes (e.g. Willpower 4 vs 5 successes) can only attack you and no one else.

                                        Now, is this Gift very easy to abuse, RAW? Absolutely. But WTA's Storytelling section very swiftly points out that:

                                        There’s only one true rule in Werewolf: There are no rules - just guidelines to make the game more fun for everyone.


                                        So, if you use this Gift then run away, that's clearly not in the spirit of the Gift's. This Gift draws fire by you being a top priority threat. It supernaturally enforces that idea, and you don't have to be killing fifty enemies with a single swing of your claws, but if you behave like a total non-threat then the Gift isn't gonna work, is it. Could the Gift be better written? Possibly, but it might also be a waste of ink.

                                        Now, what are some fun uses? Well, make yourself very hard to hit (with Gifts, Dexterity or Wits, Athletics, the right specs) and make yourself very hard to damage (good Stamina, enter Crinos, hell you could wear Fetish Armour or use a Gift for bonus Stamina or soak). Being forced to attack the Child of Gaia with Luna's Armour, whose soak pool is 50% bigger than your damage pool, might make you less keen to fight.

                                        Story-wise, this Gift enforces a "pick on someone your own size" situation. Some bigots are messing with a Metis cub, or tormenting Kinfolk, or causing trouble for your ward? Well, now their only valid option is to attack you. Two people who hate eachother, but like you, are about to fight? Make it clear that if they start fighting, you will be intervening, and they'll be forced to get through you to get to eachother. A frenzying packmate is clawing at someone you need alive? Grab them and activate this Gift; now they can either attack you, or attack the person they're furious at with a huge penalty. Great Gift, fun mechanics, just make sure you don't bite off more than you can chew. If the enemy are spreading their damage, that might be for the best. Only bother drawing fire onto yourself if it actually helps!

                                        Loyal Defender (2): +1 difficulty to any attacks you make against your packmates, permanently. If mind control or Frenzy causes you to damage one of your packmates, you may roll Willpower with a difficulty equal to the successes on whatever made you attack your packmate, for example 5 successes on a Dominate effect would be difficulty 5. If you succeed the effect stops and you gain a point of Rage.

                                        The primary effect is... alright. +1 difficulty to attacks against your pack. But this includes willing ones, such as attacks (tackles, punches) against possessed packmates. The main impact is that you get this Willpower roll when you successfully damage an ally. This Gift freeing you from Frenzy, Thrall or supernatural mind control can be arranged by a packmate deliberately getting in your way. All in all, a pretty solid Gift!

                                        Home Turf (3): With this Gift you can establish an area of 10 x Rage yards which is your pack's 'turf'. This requires a Charisma (not Strength?) + Intimidation, difficulty of (8 - number of packmates present). If you succeed, attempts to intimidate you and your pack lose dice equal to your Rage (-5 minimum), and your pack's own intimidation attempts gain bonus dice equal to your Rage as well.

                                        The usefulness of this Gift will vary, but the mechanics are pretty neat. If your ST runs the kind of game where scaring challengers off solves problems consistently, this might even be Green.

                                        Black Mamba Strike (4)*: RAW, moving more than a foot or two takes up an action. So, if you run W20 strictly by the RAW, which the Gifts assume you will of course, this Gift grants you quite a big effect across the whole battle. With this Gift you can move up to your running speed for 'free' each turn, without using one of your actions. It costs a point of Rage, but across a whole fight it'll save you a decent amount of Rage.

                                        If at your table characters get something of a 'free move', this Gift becomes defunct. Unfortunately most of the obvious ways to repurpose this Gift, if you do that, don't really work. Double your free movement? The main thing is moving at all, doubling it won't often be useful. Say your run distance is 35 feet, or perhaps 37 because you're on all fours in Crinos, doubling that to 70 or 74 feet isn't nearly as important as going from 1-2 feet to 35. It's a difference of kind vs difference of degree sorta thing.

                                        Assuming you play the game RAW and moving requires an action or splitting dice pool, this is great. One Rage frees you from the mechanics for the whole scene. While this is great, and would be worth getting at any Rank, this Gift could easily be Level 1.

                                        You might be wondering about Tackles, Leaping Rake and so on. Yes, there are combat actions which move you. But they're quite specific; with this Gift you can do your full run and then attack however you like (shoot from best position, use your Klaive, use a specific attacking Gift, and so on). This Gift is also very useful in non-combat action scenes. Escapes, chases, deadly puzzles, challenges and so on.

                                        Green, assuming the game is run RAW. If it isn't, this Gift needs reworking entirely.

                                            WYRMISH GIFTS

                                            These Gifts, from Book of the Wyrm, need important context. If a Gift is inherently unsuitable for a non-corrupted Garou, as many of these Gifts are, it will be rated with a $ sign.

                                            Some of the Gifts are feasible for ordinary Garou however, as neither the Gift nor the teacher is inherently corrupt. Ratings will assume that you can learn and use the Gift. So, a powerful attack which is a must-have will be a Green, regardless of whether the attack involves a puppy in the Umbra dying.

                                            I will also make note of when BSDs lose access to a certain Gift. They could have learnt the Gift before falling, of course, but these differences are worth noting all the same. Furthermore, my reading of this chapter is that while a Glass Walker will learn Power Surge in a Gaia-approved way, another Homid can only learn Power Surge at the in-list cost (3xp, rather than 5xp) if they learn it in the corrupt fashion that a Wyrmish Garou would. This is because the Breed and Auspice Gifts are suggested to be part a constellation of corrupted blessings which are learnt in polluted and mercenary ways. Ultimately ask your ST. If you and your table think that this list adds Power Surge to the in-Breed list for all Homids, and not just Homids messing around with Wyrmish stuff, go wild!

                                            Homid Gifts

                                            Wyrmish Homids do not get access to Calm the Savage Beast via their Breed list.

                                            $ Aura of Poison (1): Focus for a turn and spend a point of Gnosis. No one will attack you unless you attack first. Creatures with Rage can ignore this, so for example a Garou will attack you normally, but an Ananasi won't! Naturally all conventional spirits have Rage, so this is of little use in the Umbra, but homid BSDs simply being able to sidestep combat with Vampires and many Fomori, for instance, is very impressive.

                                            Arguably this falls into "so good it's bad" territory as it is ripe for abuse. Imagine if Bone Gnawers got a Gift with this effect, and with a less Wyrmish flavour! But I would point out that being unable to do violence is different from being powerless. A Vampire under the effect of this Gift can still Dominate or Presence you, a Mage can send you away or trap you or try to purify you, Kinfolk can shout for Garou allies and try to block you inside a certain location. If you attack in response to any of these then you're giving up the benefits of the Gift. Assuming that the ST does not passively let abuse of this Gift happen (abuse as in abuse of the story!) it should be fine.

                                            Power Surge (2): See the Glass Walker Rank 2 list.

                                            Call the Rust (3): See the Bone Gnawer Rank 3 list.

                                            $ Feast of Man-Flesh (4): This is a passive buff to cannibalism. So long as you eat at least a pound of a human's flesh you can gain facts the person knew, or abilities they possessed. You can get a maximum number of benefits equal to your Gnosis. So a Gnosis 3 Garou could learn 3 things a dead human knew, or get 3 dots of abilities from the person, but not in excess of what they had. So a Garou with Etiquette 0 could eat the flesh of a socialite and get Etiquette 3, if that socialite had Etiquette of 3 or higher.

                                            I really like the flavour of this one as an 'evil power' and I would gladly give it to a Bone Gnawer or Younger Brother character if they were going down a 'cannibal Werewolf' route. Naturally since this breaks the Litany it's $. We also get a handy list of who is 'human' per this power and who isn't.

                                            Metis Gifts

                                            Wyrmish Metis do not get access to Totem Gift via their Breed list. Given that Metis are key to the Dancers I was hoping for more Gifts, to be honest, and some very cool ones! Perhaps a Gift where you weaponize your deformity or spread it like a plague.

                                            $ Bleed (1): Produce big quantities of fake blood. You can use this to play dead or frame someone, or pretend you have terrible injuries. The blood is fake, a spiritual ichor. This Gift's teacher is the only thing that makes it $. The Gnosis cost makes this bad, so don't bother trying to find a Gaian equivalent for this. Yeah, you're lying in a giant pool of blood, but this won't fool Garou senses.

                                            $ Nobody's Bastard (3): Become unrecognizable. With this Gift you're just 'a stranger' to people, and that's how they remember you, not as 'you'. This costs Gnosis and requires a Manipulation + Stealth (difficulty 7) but there is no way to resist and no way to overcome it, save for things like Auspex or special Gifts, or anything granting immunity to illusions. Very neat, great concept, great execution. The teacher is what makes this corrupt; given that this Gift is pretty good it might be worth trying to find a purified alternative. A spirit of abandonment teaches it currently, but presumably a spirit of anonymity could achieve the same.

                                            Visceral Agony (3): See the Rank 3 Black Fury list. A great addition to any Metis PC if your ST will let you.

                                            Lupus Gifts

                                            Wyrmish Lupus get all the typical Lupus Gifts, plus a few more.

                                            $ Ways of the Urban Wolf (1): Taught by Wyrmish Elementals, this Gift grants a -2 reduction to the difficulties of Perception, Stealth and Survival rolls relating to hunting intelligent prey (humans, Vampires, Garou) in urban environments. If a regular Lupus could learn this from an ordinary elemental, or a city father, or perhaps from Lupus Ancestors, that would be a great tool for Gaia. Solid Green.

                                            Thousand Teeth (3): We have a Gift Gaian Garou can explicitly learn! Taught either by Scrags or Shark-spirits, this Gift makes you develop loads of bonus teeth. Spend a Rage, roll Stamina + Primal-Urge (difficulty 5), get +3 damage to bite attacks, lasting for one bite per success. Great preparation power for bite-lovers, which suits the Lupus well.

                                            $ Instincts Unbound (5): Spend Rage and Gnosis, roll Wits + Primal-Urge against a difficulty of the target's Willpower. For one day per success they give into every instinct they have. If they want to eat, they eat, if they want to kill, they kill, they become raw unfiltered id, with no respect for law or consequences or the litany. It's interesting to consider whether a Metis born as a result of this Gift (two packmates follow their instincts with no care for the consequences) would lead to punishment of the parents. Still, this is a NPC power if ever there was one, though it's not fundamentally unsuited to a PC either.

                                            If a target wants to resist they can reduce the duration by 12 hours for every point of Willpower they spend. Which makes this an alright way to shed a target's Willpower before you try to brainwash or indoctrinate them anyways. An impactful Gift, but perfectly average for Rank 5.

                                            ================================================== ============================

                                            The important takeaways for a standard game are that Lupus get a bonus biting Gift and that Metis might get Visceral Agony.

Infinite combinations exist and make this an incredible Gift. If a Gaian alternative could be taught by a Spirit of Patience, or perhaps an Avatar of Gaia, it would be a great weapon for the Garou Nation.
