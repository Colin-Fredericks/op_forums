        The Black Furies originated as, mainly, a third wave feminist critique on second wave feminism. This seems.. painfully obvious if you understand the broad concepts of what each wave of feminism is about, and how historically they've related to each other.

        The leadership of the BFs was coded very strongly as reflecting the feminism of the "60s." Women suffered a major setback in equality following WWII as they were expected to give up on careers that were more traditionally masculine that they made significant in-roads in due to the war, leading to reduced power in domestic relationship dynamics. The second wave feminist activism was heavily oriented around reclaiming "motherhood" as something women controlled and defined instead of something men defined for them (also leading to the origin of radical feminism as the desire for extreme societal reordering to dismantle the patriarchy). The rise of easy access to hormonal birth control, the free love movement, and the push for legalized abortions eventually coming to a head in Roe v. Wade, all focused the dominant feminism of the day on having a functional uterus.

        The young (aka PC) BFs were coded after the earlier third wave feminists largely before third wave was even a recognized thing (mostly the west cost feminist punks of the late 80s/early 90s). Third wave grew out of the discontent that during the late 70s/80s, the second wave feminist leaders basically acted like they'd won the moral argument and could focus on slow incrementalist change; while the anti-porn vs. sex-positive clashes left a whole generation of feminists without any clear movement to join that reflected current issues. Since third wave was so dominated by being highly critical of how second wave had been very successful for middle/upper class white cis-women, and did very little for all other women, it lacked significantly in cohesion (leading to fourth wave feminism's unification around intersectionality) as it was more opposition than movement with distinct goals.

        So, where's the claim from? It's from that being how the BFs were constructed as a Tribe (esp. their Camps) even if the books never calls any of this out by name.

                    Why?

                    The idea that Garou "can't" keep up with human social trends isn't in the books (as opposed to don't want to), and Tribes like the BFs, GWs, and CoGs, are notable for their focus on keeping current as issues they care about change fairly rapidly.

                    Garou aren't vampires where being born 75 years ago means you're still a baby in the eyes of most vampires. Anyone playing a starting level BF in a game that's set in 2020 is playing a character that grew up with third and fourth wave feminism (including third wave's increased advocacy for solidarity with the LGBTQ movement, and fourth wave's increased focus on intersectionality) being the normal of what feminism looks like "on the ground" even if their elders are more old school (just like feminists in the real world). Most of the Adren and Athros of the Tribe are also going to have grown up with third wave feminism either as it started or solidly part of their lives as second wave feminism would have been at least two generations back for them.

                    It makes no in-universe sense that transwomen aren't part of the Tribe by 2020. We have the Millennial and Zoomer generations as the default PCs that have had enough time to get the Rank to try to initiate transwomen into the Tribe regardless of what their seniors think (keeping in mind that most second wave BFs are long dead now as old age isn't really a common thing for Garou and they're Boomers). Pegasus has no reason to completely reject transwomen (even if you want to say that Pegasus demands some level of transition or whatever rather than blanket acceptance).

                    What makes sense, in-universe and out, is for the biggest philosophic struggle within the BFs to be about healing men instead of punishing them as the younger BFs are going to come in with attitudes more in-line with the CoGs regarding restorative justice and deprogramming toxic masculinity instead of treating men as bad simply because the patriarchy benefits them socioeconomically. That's where a lot of the struggle is, because that's where a lot of the lines between third and fourth wave get drawn.

                                The Garou are religious zealots as a culture, but their belief system does not hinge on Gaia being infallible like the Abrahamic creator.

                                The idea that transness only happens because of a "mistake" or a "failing" is a conceit of human cultural bias; and is also fairly modern. Nature does not give a single shit about humans that think that the "natural" order is that you have some creatures born male, and others born female. Some things in nature follow that, and most do something different on a vast spectrum of variations (and that's just looking at sexual reproductive life!). All evidence points to humans as having an inherent minority of non-conformists (not just regarding gender), and that for most of the time humanity has existed, humanity chose to create cultural norms that elevated socially productive non-conformity; not denounce it as a flaw. It's easy to make the case that this acceptable non-conformity is one of humanity's greatest strengths as it gives space in human cultures for radical thinking and thus greater social adaptability.

                                Gaia (and whoever else) created the Fera out of mixing the spiritual natures of humanity and animals. The human mind is always a keep thing that's considered a positive that the human side brings to the table: complex cultural thinking beyond what other animals do without the rigidity of eusocial behaviors. Gaia wanted the Garou to have humanity's ability to transmit complex cultural behaviors, and the ability to reconcile non-conformity.

                                Or there's a simple answer: If trans Garou are a mistake, so are the Ragabash.

I mean, looking at trans people in the military (A messy topic and a lot of it goes against my argument, but I try to be objective) it seems to be fine (most people who join the military want to be there) but there's still some heavy biases both for and against that (I'll note that one group have suggested a high estimate that USA about 21% of trans people have served in the military: That's 1 in 5. The trans people I've played with were decisively not these people... but also, are such people likely to seek out the Black Furies for membership rather than another tribe? it seems the Get of Fenrir would hold more appeal now that I think about it)

                                        Then there's the last issue: You, the reader, might see the Black Furies as the Champions of Gender, but your character probably isn't. The Black Furies aren't an easy-going tribe: I'd rank them as No.4 in the 'Which tribe has the most enemies' ranking, and that's impressive because they're one of the smaller tribes. If you live off the reservation, When you go through the first change, and you are found, you aren't given an unbiased list, If you don't have the kind of pure breed that'd attract the demand of a tribe, the first thing your finders will do is try recruit you and sell you on their tribe alone, and failing that the other locals will try to recruit you, and failing that they'll point you to people they like. The tribes that like the Black furies tend to be the ones that'd accept trans members.

There's plenty of reasons for them to fight on behalf of the Trans community, but for them allowing trans homids is only a moral victory, it isn't a practical victory. The practical logistics and value of the move would be... debatable; You aren't getting many more members and the ones who do seek you out amongst all the tribes tend not to be the macho-murder-machine women you're looking for. You encourage in-fighting in the tribe, you have to go through a whole load of bullshit sorting through spiritual contracts, The male-sex characters who join up will likely be confused with the metis who have long been associated as the only male members of the tribe.
