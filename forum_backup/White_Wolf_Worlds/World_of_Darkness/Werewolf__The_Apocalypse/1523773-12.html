    Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
    I like this template in a lot of ways with one big exception. It is very hard to see this character as an actual lupus - meaning a wolf. Its description of its life as an animal points more towards being a dog. There is no problem with Bone Gnawers having lupus members. But why would a wolf pack be living in a city? Does it make sense that some kinds would actually mess with a wolf pack? Or that the wolf mother could not have seriously injured or scared this group of kids/teens who messed with her cubs? "Street punks" won't normally screw around with an actual wolf pack or den. None of this seems like it is talking about wild wolves (who somehow found themselves in a city for some reason). It naturally reads like they are talking about dogs.

    I remember there being a LOT of confusion about the nature of the Bone Gnawers in the nineties. A LOT of players had the impression that Bone Gnawers were basically dogs, not wolves. It doesn't help that page 29 of the tribebook actually shows a picture of a dog. (Even in the 1e corebook there are images of Bone Gnawers that seem much more like dogs than wolves, though this might simply be because of the lower quality of art in that sourcebook.) Even terminology like "the Barking Chain", which this template references, creates the impression of dogs as opposed to wolves. Wolves don't really bark. Everyone says that wolves howl, but dogs bark. I understand the term may be poetic, but combined with other things, it creates a lot of confusion.

    I don't see the lupus of the Bone Gnawer being urban based. They are probably one of the tribes that are most wolf-starved. If you are going to assign a camp to lupus, the Hillfolk are more natural. The Bone Gnawer as described are VERY homid based, so there actually is not a lot for the lupus breed to go by. The tribebook just ignores the issue rather than engage it. It's major weakness that is not sufficiently addressed even in the Revised version.
    In my country many times wolves were seen on the city streets, eating trash, or in the smaller settlements, attacking herd, dogs and cats. We also have Kamyk (Stone in english), which was found on the side of the road after being hit by a car. He lost one of his front paws and still was able to escape from the animal shelter. For a few months, despite being hunted by the people from the shelter, he was living on the city streets, as he was without the pack and being unable to hunt. Many people wished him good luck and fed him (it was a holiday resort and he became kind of a tourist attraction). After a few months unfortunately he was caught and brought back to the shelter. There he has an enormous, 0.5 ha. big hunting territory, with better defences than the previous one. And still, the first thing he tried to do was trying to escape. So wolf living in the city for me it's totally acceptable. In my city, population about 700 k, it's normal to see boars, foxes, or deers on the streets. Sometimes even moose and, since few days, a lynx. And still, it's not Alaska but middle Europe.

There are still plenty of areas in Europe where wolves still dwell in the mountains and forests. Even in Los Angeles there are occasional reports of coyotes, mountain lions, and bears because those animals still live in the nearby mountains.         I think that is a different scenario than what I am talking about. In the US the wolf is only extant in Alaska, the northern Rockies, and the northern Great Lakes area plus a few other isolated places. So if there are wolf packs scavenging in most major US cities (or say the British Isles, much of France, Australia, and other countries where they've been extirpated), they simply cannot wander in from the nearby wilderness. A pack of wild wolves in the city limits of New York City, Chicago, Washington, Dallas, London, Paris, etc. would not be a normal occurrence. It would be very unusual. Especially if they are now hunting exclusively in the urban environment and not the rare wolf wandering in and out from the wilderness. So if the Bone Gnawers - and perhaps other tribes that dwell in the city - have a stock of urban based lupus kinfolk that regularly scavenge there, it takes a lot more to explain. It changes the world. At the very least the ST needs to describe to the players how the setting departs from reality because players won't be expecting that based on their understanding of the city. The ST needs to do some world building and think through the scope and ramifications.

                                    Hillfolk Hitchhiker (Metis Galliard)

                                    Because, of course, the example character of the Breed born from spiritual incest is from a culture frequently stereotyped for inbreeding. In hindsight, I'm rather grateful that Ethan Skemp was actually from said culture so that most of that sort of thing went away under his tenure as developer.

                                    And, again, one whose name includes which Camp their from. At least this one has a little more than just "Auspice + Camp" like our first one.

                                    So, the character is the offspring of a pair of migrant workers. And yes, these turn out to be their real parents and not just Kinfolk who took them in. I'm not exactly sure how a pair of Garou live as migrant workers, let alone while having a Metis cub with them. But they also spent Falls and Winters with Kinfolk in the city. (This part actually makes a bit of sense, as during the boom period of WW2, a lot of Appalachian people moved to nearby cities for work. Baltimore, for example, has an entire area that has long been referred to as "Billytown".) Eventually, they - the Kinfolk apparently, as the writing isn't all that clear - revealed they were werewolves and taught them how to change form. Apparently they took all of this in without even blinking. I wish I could be that blasé about things.

                                    So, the Prelude is entirely about being Hillfolk. And now for the Concept section, which is about the Auspice and Hitchhiker parts. So they love mountain people music, and learned to play fiddle, harp, and harmonica, and also like to howl in the high mountain places. That's nice. I mean, I like the idea. But I'm left wondering why? I mean, besides the painfully obvious answer of "because they're Galliard and that's what Galliards do." They now roam the roads, listening to the music of everyone they encounter and sharing their own music.

                                    They're very friendly and good natured. Their Metis deformity is Loose Teeth. Equipment is coveralls (I think they mean overalls), white T-shirt, harmonica, roadmaps, three cans of cheap beer (that's pretty random), backpack, and hiking boots.

                                    For Attributes, Physicals are tertiary, with 1/2/3. ::Sigh:: Again with the 1s.
                                    Socials are primary, with 4/4/2. I'm quite frankly shocked that Appearance isn't 1. And I'm sure that no one will be shocked to learn that the Ahroun character does have a 1 in Appearance. So out of these five templates, the only one with an Appearance above 1 is the Metis character. That's just sad.
                                    Mental is secondary, with 1/4/3. Again, another 1. And I'm not sure how such a character gets buy with such a low level of Perception. And then there's the Intelligence, which is the highest of the five. I get the idea of such a character being very intelligent, especially when it comes to remembering music, folklore, and the like, but I'm not sure this level is necessary.
                                    Honestly, I feel all of these need to be balanced out a little better. Socials are fine, but I think Physicals should be straight 2s across the board and Mental should be either 2/3/3 or 3/2/3.

                                    For Abilities, Talents are primary, with 3 each in Alertness, Athletics, and Empathy, 2 in Dodge, and 1 each in Brawl and Primal Urge.
                                    Skills are secondary, with 3 each in Performance and Survival, and 1 each in Animal Ken, Drive, and Stealth.
                                    Knowledge is tertiary, with 1 each in Enigmas, Investigation, Law, Linguistics, and Politics.
                                    Overall, these are fine. However, I'm not entirely sure why the character has Law. (Politics I can accept as a Grapes of Wrath/Tom Joad reference.) I think that dot might make more sense in Linguistics. I might also move one of those Athletic dots to Expression or possibly Dodge.

                                    Backgrounds are 5 dots in Allies. I guess I can see that. Although you'd think this might be spread among Allies and Contacts.

                                    Rage is 5. Gnosis is 4. Willpower is 6.

                                    That point of Rage, point of Gnosis, and two points of WP are the only five freebie points spent. There are ten more that haven't been used. It's been a while since I've seen missing freebies at this level.
                                    I'd probably spend at least six of them on Abilities (one each in Performance, Expression or Subterfuge, and Firearms or Melee).

                                    Overall, this isn't a terrible character. The screw up with the freebies is almost inexcusable, and I can't understand the obsession with the 1 ratings in Attributes. But the general concept is OK. However, I feel this would've been a lot better as a Homid rather than a Metis. A character who grew up in the Appalachian mountains with a love of music and culture, who now wanders around collecting the music and folklore of other poor and working class groups as a Songcatcher. (If you're unfamiliar with the work of Sharyn McCrumb, her novels set among the Appalachians and drawing on local folklore and music are very good.)

                                            A hitchhiker concept is a good one, but there are all sorts of issues with how it is written.

                                            First, the metis background just doesn't work. Wouldn't she be in crinos form growing up? Early WtA often had metis newborns change to a homid or lupus form, but that was always mentioned. Not addressing it really goes against the metis concept. Second, there is no indication her parents ever did the things we expect them to do as Garou. There doesn't seem to be mysterious disappearances as they go off and fight the Wyrm. Nothing that indicates her parents may essentially be a two person anruth pack going from caern to caern. Or are we to assume they are essentially AWOL from the Garou Nation? If they are, then that should be addressed. Furthermore, since they are Garou why does the template character need to learn about the Garou and how to change from him kin in the city? Wouldn't her Garou parents be doing that? The one advantage metis characters have over other breeds is that they grew up in Garou culture and know things much better than homids and lupus. Why remove that from this character?

                                            This seems like something written for a homid and then quickly rewritten to make it metis without the writer bothering to think things through. Homid breed would probably make more sense, but metis can work - it just needs to be properly explained and addressed.

                                            Second, I agree that her harmonica, three points of Performance, and listening to music everywhere she travels seems tacked on to make the concept a Galliard. A better approach might have been her collecting stories and rumors from the different areas she visits. She can compile news, stories, and folklore, figuring out stuff concerning the Wyrm while doing so. That would give her hitchhiking some background and be of value. These early Galliard templates all seem to fall back to Galliard = musician. There needs to be more variety for this auspice. If you do need to go down the musical route once again, put something in that better establishes it for the character like how her parents and her constantly used music to occupy their time when they travelled.

                                            Third, Allies 5 doesn't work for me. There's no indication in the description that she is building strong relationships with people who are now strongly inclined to help her out. Being "friendly" just isn't enough. This would work better either as Contacts or an extended Kinfolk network. Kinfolk 5 would net her an impressive 50 Kinfolk who could provide a lot of support for a hitchhiking Garou. Especially in terms of people willing to pick her up and drive her to the next stop (some could be truckers who could be on a long haul), as a Garou with Rage 5 should be affecting 63% of people with the Curse. Not many people would want to pick up an unknown hitchhiker that would cause them to fear they are picking up some dangerous person. Unless you justify it by using Kinfolk, the hitchhiking concept would work better for a Ragabash. But like with many concepts, the Curse just gets ignored so this will be a common complaint. If the writers even bothered to address the Curse and mention how it affected the concept and addressed it, I think these templates would have done a lot to help STs and PCs integrate that concept into their chronicles in a useful way.

                                            The Metis deformity is weird to me. It's one of the example deformities provided in the first Players Guide, but it only comes into play if you use bite as a weapon. There's nothing really symbolic or impactful in terms of the character concept which is unusual. Unless the writer intended it to be a commentary on poor dental health for a hillbilly. I do like the idea of a metis deformity that is not readily apparent. There should be more of those. Unfortunately, the 2e corebook and 1e Players Guide do not have many examples so we're limited here at finding a replacement that would make sense for this tribebook. Maybe silver sensitivity instead. But there is nothing inherently wrong with Loose Teeth. It just seems like another afterthought though.

                                            As you mentioned, this is the only concept with an Appearance greater than 1. Though given that she's supposed to be an incredibly friendly hitchhiker who makes lots of friends, an even higher Appearance could be justified. Nothing wrong with just a 2 though. But in line with the other templates, it's still part of the pattern that Bone Gnawers must not be attractive.

                                            While I want to like this template, I think there's too many problems with it. The basic concept is fine and has lots of potential, but I would alter her background and much of her character sheet. In addition, there is a lot of white space left on the character description page. This could have been used to flesh out the character concept more and address these issues. And what kind of migrant work her parents did would be good to know.

                                                            Frankweiler Knight (Homid Ahroun)

                                                            And, once again, the Camp + Vague Descriptor naming convention.

                                                            So, the Frankweilers are a Camp who tend to lurk about and make their territories in various urban museums, libraries, churches, theaters, and other places of culture. If you've ever wondered, the name comes from a book by E L Konigsburg called From the Mixed-Up Files of Mrs. Basil E. Frankweiler. Someone must have been a fan of the book as a kid to have used it here. I'm not sure why though, as the titular Mrs. Frankweiler is one of NYC's wealthy elite, while the two kids who live in the Metropolitan Museum of Art are named Kincaid. Also, the book came out in 1967, so I'm wondering what this Camp would've been called before that.

                                                            Anyway ...

                                                            He was born backstage at a theater during a production of Hamlet, and grew up in and around that world. At least until Child Protective Services took him away and put him in a foster home. Apparently he was bullied to some degree, and at age 13 freaked out, underwent the First Change, and killed several of the other kids in the home. This may be the darkest First Change out of the ones we've looked at so far.
                                                            He was found by local Bone Gnawers, and now lives on the run (I presume wanted for multiple murders), spending his time hiding in various theaters and museums. He likes to fool around with antique armor and weapons and speaks in a mix of Shakespearian English and modern American. I honestly can help but imagine this character coming across a bit like Thor in the Marvel Comics (back in the old pre-MCU days).

                                                            Equipment is antique silken clothing stolen from a museum, a book of Shakespeare plays, a broadsword, and a big black cloak.

                                                            The image is of a man, maybe early 20s, dressed like he's costumed for a Shakespearian play. He looks fairly handsome (this will be important in a moment), and may be meant to be African American.

                                                            For Attributes, Physicals are primary, with a 4/5/1 spread. And again with the 1 dot attributes. Honestly, when I see a character with a 1 in Stamina, I expect them to have some sort of health issue.
                                                            Socials are secondary, with 5/2/1. Good grief. Remember what I said about the image? This guy looks like a fairly handsome young actor.
                                                            And finally Mental is tertiary, with ... 2/1/3. Oh FFS.
                                                            I think this may be the only time I've seen a character with three attributes at 1. This is just ridiculous. A wannabe Shakespearian actor turned knight, who has the stamina of an asthmatic octogenarian, the appearance of Quasimodo, and the brains of Forrest Gump.

                                                            For Abilities, Talents are secondary, with 2 each in Alertness, Dodge, and Empathy, and 1 each in Intimidation, Primal Urge, and Streetwise.
                                                            Skills are primary, with 3 each in Etiquette, Melee, Performance, and Stealth, and 1 in Leadership.
                                                            Knowledge is tertiary, with 3 in Linguistics, and 1 each in Enigmas and Medicine.
                                                            Some of these abilities are fine. I'm sort of surprised he doesn't have anything in Athletics or Expression. Streetwise also feels kind of low for a guy who effectively squats in whatever urban cultural center he can find.

                                                            Backgrounds are 2 in Allies and 3 in Contacts. These seem fine, but I wish I had some sort of information on who or what these represent.
                                                            I will also note that, aside from the Theurge having the Rites background, Allies and Contacts are the only backgrounds any of these characters have. I realize that the tribe is barred from having Past Life, Pure Breed, and Resources, but no one has Kinfolk, Fetish, Mentor, or Totem?

                                                            Rage is 7, Gnosis is 3 and Willpower is 6. These eight freebies are the only ones spent. There are seven unspent points here.

                                                            Conceptually, this isn't a bad character. The write up is fairly decent and he seems like a potentially interesting person to play. However, the stats are mostly a broken mess.
                                                            The Attributes need to be balanced out to get rid of the 1s. I think 3/4/3, 4/2/2, and 2/2/2 would work fine.
                                                            I really don't think he needs the extra Gnosis, and the eleven freebie points could've gone a long way toward boosting his some of his abilities. A Mentor might also be useful.

                                                            And so that's the Bone Gnawers. Overall, these are some fairly decent ideas, but they tend to fall down a little (sometimes a lot) in execution. I've no idea what the deal is with the low Appearance traits, beyond the suggestion that someone decided they were the Werewolf equivalent to Nosferatu.
                                                            The weird limiting of Backgrounds to only three of them is also a mystery.

                                                            Next will be Children of Gaia.
