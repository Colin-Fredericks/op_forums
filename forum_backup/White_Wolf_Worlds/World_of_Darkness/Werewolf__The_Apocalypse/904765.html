    Ajaba in the New World: El Chupacabra

    06-01-2016, 03:18 AM
    From the 15th to 19th centuries, untold numbers of African Slaves where captured, and forced across the Atlantic by European and American powers. For 400 years, men, women and Children where ripped away from their homes, and forced into slavery in strange lands across the ocean, never to see their homes again. This brutality was eventually ended, but the resulting social problems and prejudices exist to this day, an echo of one of the most cruel ways of making money the world has ever seen.

    However, in the World of Darkness, it wasn't just mortal humans who where enslaved. A significant number of Ajaba Kinfolk where also abducted, relatives to the werehyenas who dwelled in the Savannas and the dunes of the Sahara. While the Ajaba eventually took notice and attempted to take steps to stagger the slave trade, they where unable to stem the tides entirely and Kinfolk where occasionally forced westwards until the abolition of slavery at the end of the American Civil War.

    Upon arriving in the New World, the slaves where subjected to utter barbarism. Forced labor, scourging until the backs of the slaves where scarred with keloids, the forced separation of families, and innumerable other cruelties. Most of the Kinfolk abducted where unaware of their true nature, as those who where in touch with their Shifter relatives tended to be more capable of avoiding capture. Some where aware they where Kinfolk, and the resulting distance from their homelands threw most of them into a great depression similar to Harano.

    Over time, however, the Kinfolk population steadily grew as the slaves reproduced. And as the years went by, the occasional Homid Ajaba was born, a slave would be lashed with the whip one too many times, undergo his first change, and eviscerate his master, setting dozens of slaves free all at once before escaping into the wilderness. Freedmen who became aware of their nature worked towards abolition, and more then a few found their way into the Underground railroad.

    Around the time of the Civil War, there where around a dozen Homid Ajaba packs throughout the Deep South, Mexico, South America, and the Caribbean, with a few other Ronin Ajaba here and there who tended to have no idea what they where or what was happening. Even if they ran across Garou or other Changing Breeds, most of them had never even heard of an Ajaba before. Only the most obscure lore carried by Theurges spoke of these unusual creatures.

    As a result of a lack of education and a lack of ties to their bestial side (Being entirely Homid), a great many of these Ajaba became struggled to survive, feeding on farm animals before retreating into the wilds once more. The western shifters gave them the name "Chupacabra", as a result of this unusual behavior, and more then a few thought them to be some strange Wyrm Creature. Only a handful of Garou knew the truth of what the Ajaba where, but with the Storm Eater and the Civil War on the horizon, most packs had more pressing issues then 'a strange creature eating the occasional goat'. Eventually, the Ajaba began referring to themselves as Chupacabra, knowing no other name for what they where.

    When the Union was restored, and the Civil War Ended, the scattered Ajaba packs helped clean up the Umbral landscape. The Garou steadily became aware that they had encountered another Changing Breed, and as a result of the Chupacabra having no knowledge of the War of Rage, they actually got along rather well compared to how the Garou usually got on with their cousins. As the 1900s pressed on, a great many Ajaba rallied their fellow former-slaves to try and take on the problems with the Reconstruction. Sharecroppers who treated their farmers poorly would be found in the swamps, torn to shreds by a great beast. Members of the Ku Klux Klan where also frequently the targets of Chupacabra, as the orgnization not only disgusted them, they also frequently targeted kinfolk. (It was eventually discovered that the Grand Wizard of the Klan at the time was a Formor, resulting in a pack of Chupacabra and a pack of Garou slaughtering their way through an entire Klan meeting to destroy the monster)

    As the Civil Rights era pressed on, more then a few Chupacabra claimed that the leadership of the movement was kin to them, with different packs claiming relation to Martin Luther King and Rosa Parks. These claims where never substantiated, but it did give the new generation of Chupacabra a sense of civic pride, and while their spiritual heritage had been stolen from them, it gave them something new to be proud of.

    The Chupacabra where mostly untouched by Black Tooth's endless storm, though a few Simba did attempt to accost packs in the Americas, they found it impossible to root them out. And with the Ahadi becoming the dominant force in Africa, attempts by the Ajaba to finally develop a rapport with their long lost kin began. Kisasi herself is said to of visited a Chupacabra Caern in New Orleans in an attempt to open up a dialogue, which seems to of been productive so far, with a few even traveling east to try to reclaim some of their lost heritage.

    There are perhaps 200 Ajaba throughout the New World at the moment, they have never been numerous as the Slave Trade tended to make establishing lasting Kinfolk lines difficult. Though a few Freedmen Kinfolk managed to marry other Kin, and raised families that endure to the modern day, resulting in the occasional Ajaba undergoing the first change. The numbers have steadily risen in the decades since the 1970s, but they have never been, and likely never will be, anywhere near as numerous as their Garou neighbors.


    Chupacabra Culture

    90 percent of all Chupacabra are black, with a small percentage being white men who happened to have a distant African ancestor (These white Chupacabra are generally assumed to be the result of a dalliance between Master and Slave, and its generally not discussed in polite company). Most Chupacabra, around 60 percent, live in the Southern US, with the rest spread throughout Mexico, Cuba, Haiti, and Sao Palo Brazil (Mostly among the Confederados). As a result, Chupacabra culture is as varied as any other Shifter beed, though the majority of Chupacabra have adopted Voodoo and other Afro-American folk traditions into their spiritual paradigms, with gifts and rites linked to Loa and Orishas.

    Chupacabra Mechanical Differences

    As a result of the lack of Hyenas or even Hyena-like creatures in the New World, all Chupacabra are Homid, with a very small percentage being Metis. Metis Chupacabra generally suffer no discrimination as a result of their breed, as until recent decades, most Chupacabra had no idea what would occur if a pair of Werehyenas where to mate. Chupacabra suffer no restrictions to which abilities they may purchase, and in this modern time where they have begun communicating with the Ahadi, suffer no restrictions to their gift lists. (For games set in the Wyld West, the Chupacabra are limited to General Garou and Homid gifts to represent their spiritual disconnection from their homelands)

    Rites of the Chupacabra tend to invoke varying Loa, Orishas, or Catholic Saints, depending on the specifics of their culture. While Garou rituals and gifts have been passed down for thousands of years and change only gradually, the Chupacabra had to discover their own ways of doing things, and variations from Hyena to Hyena are the rule rather then the exception. A few unique rituals and gifts of the Chupacabra are listed below.


    Walking With the Baron (Level 3 Death Rite)

    An unusual ritual, the rite involves taking a scarecrow, and putting a top hat atop its head, a pair of dark glasses on its face, putting a cigar in its mouth, and setting a glass of whiskey in its hand. Upon doing so, a servant of Baron Samedi possesses the scarecrow, and leads the Chupacabra across the gauntlet into the Dark Umbra. The Scarecrow then hands off a cigar to the Werehyena, and then hops away into the shadows of the Underworld.

    When the Hyena wishes to return to the realm of the living, he simply lights the cigar, inhales as he closes his eyes, makes the roll to step sideways, and when he opens them once more, he finds himself back in the Skinlands. If he fails the roll, he may try again until the cigar is finished, at which point if he hasn't succeeded yet, he might be stuck there for a while.

    St. Mary's Touch (Rank 2 gift)

    The scourge of the whip, long hours, and brutal punishments frequently resulted in lasting deformities and disfigurements to Slaves. Those with this gift, however, had a way of letting them recover from such trials. The Ajaba lays his hand on a human suffering from a deformity (It could be anything from a cleft palate to keloid scars on the back) and prays to St. Mary for the victim to find peace. This gift immediately heals the deformity, and any and all side effects from it, including chronic pain, spasms, and eating difficulties. Though the Chupacabra don't understand why, this gift fails to work on Metis.

    Simbi's Scorn (Level 5 Mystic Rite)

    This rite calls on the fury of Simbi, the Loa of Storms, magic and foul weather to smite the land, sending cleansing rainstorms to clear the land of filth and to smite those who've made a mess of things. The Ritualist carries two heavy ewers of water at arms length while dancing a jig in a circle, praying to the Loa to send a storm. The ritual functions as the Garou Rite "Rite of Opened Skies" (Page 205 WTA20th edition) with the added benefit of Simbi's fury striking those tainted by the Wyrm. Wyrm creatures who do not immediately flee the area suffer 3 points of lethal damage per turn as lightning and searing rain strikes their flesh.

    Legbas Lock (Level 3 rite of accord)

    Papa Legba is the Loa of Crossroads, and he has dominion over the Gauntlet. The ritualist smokes a pipe and dons a straw hat as he meanders through the area, puffing out tobacco smoke all the while. Wherever the smoke trails, the Gauntlet gets thicker and sticky with tar, raising the Gauntlet by the ritualist's successes, and causing anyone who fails to step sideways to get 'stuck' in the gauntlet as if they botched the roll to step sideways. This ritual is sometimes known as the Rite of the Tar Baby further north, or the "BP Special" in Louisiana.

                          Originally posted by Autochthon View Post

                          The Atlantic slave trade wasn't really that relevant to werewolves because of the lack of direct impact on werewolves. The original Bastet book did actually touch on this as a rarity that a true-born Bastet (generally a Simba) would pop up in the Americas, but the extant Bastet population would usually take care of it.

                          So, the things that kind of bug me in your presentation:

                          1) The idea that "a lot" of kinfolk would get taken seems overstated. While yes, ~11 million people made it to the Americas via the slave trade, that is also over ~300 years. The number of kin needed to be in that total when you take into account the time period and the number of different territories they were taken to, would have to be huge; bordering on something like the Simba purposefully getting Ajaba kin captured and "deported" rather than the Ajaba successfully keeping as many kin as possible safe.

                          To try to put some numbers to this. Lets estimate that 1% of the slaves that arrived in the Americas were Ajaba kin (I'm being generous here, since kin are supposed to be fairly rare, and the African Tribes most raided for slaves were in some of the least hyena populated parts of the continent which seems like it would correlate to less Ajaba kin among them), and that 20% of the slaves ended up in territories that would be part of the USA by the time of the US Civil War (it's hard to be specific about that number due to the changing of hands of territories and local slave trades between those territories, but it's a decent educated approximation). That equates to ~75 Ajaba kin coming to what would be the USA a year, spread out over the entire territory (since even in the earlier abolition states, some of them would be freed people there), and for the majority of that time they had no freedom to choose partners, making maintaining kin-families nearly impossible. Kin-kin pairings managing to just create a few more kin would be a statistical anomaly over being a regular event, and with no way to find other kin, inbreeding among those families would be the only way to eventually generate a population of Ajaba.

                          Basically, the math comes down to the only way you get to a stable population of Ajaba in the Americans is by (a) Ajaba kin being sold into slavery at rates there's no way the Ajaba would tolerate, or (b) sibling incest as the only way to create true-born producing kin family lines since they lacked the Gifts or Rituals to find other kin to expand their breeding pool to this goal. B is not only adding all sorts of negative connotations to this, it also requires a further buy-in that those kin would care enough about their Ajaba blood to force their kids to marry each other.

                          200 active shifters is a ton for what is, essentially, an accident of history. There are entire Garou Tribes with strong purposeful breeding programs brought with them from Europe that probably don't have 200 active members in the Americas; with the caveat of how the books present the number of Garou around being wildly out of step with how other things are presented.

                          2) The culture of group presented makes very little sense. It is heavily US-centric, despite the majority of slaves ending up in South America and the Caribbean. If any stable population of Ajaba was going to form, it would be on Hispaniola or in Brazil; where the issues of race relations are exceedingly different (The idea that 60% of them are in the US is mind-boggling). In most of the countries where there the highest number of slaves ended up, there was much less cultural taboo around mixed ethnic breeding. In Brazil for example, "mixed" is pretty much the default, and most people wouldn't consider it strange to have siblings of multiple skin tones because that happens. There is far less pressure to be "white" or "black" because most people are both in ancestry.

                          Dovetailing off of this, the "Voodoo Shifters" aspect is extremely forced and shows very little knowledge of non-US history regarding African diaspora cultures. At a minimum this needs a lot of work looking into the differences of how Spanish, French, and Portuguese African diaspora cultures evolved in the Americas, and when it is appropriate for this to result in a connection to a specific cultural practice.

                          3) How are these Ajaba managing well... anything of import? They have no way to gain Renown and Rank. They have no way to learn Gifts. They have no Rituals instructors. They're basically all Kits. Until the Endless Storm drove some of the Ajaba to the Americas (which did happen in canon) to hide among people of African descent there, being able to fight any supernatural threat is non-sensical; let alone something like holding an actual Caern. Without these tools how are they doing things like joining in the civil rights struggles without getting slaughtered by mobs for it? They have no skills to hide their nature from even normal humans; Rage alone would give all the excuse needed for them to be targeted as clearly "troublemakers."

                          As a side note, making one of the KKK's Grand Wizards a fomor is repeating a widely acknowledged mistake in past WoD books: human evils need to stay human evils. Making the Klan a front for the Wyrm excuses far too much purely human evil to be worth it. Mowing down humans associated with hate groups because they're controlled by and breeding grounds for supernatural evils, destroys the deeper thematic points of tangling with dark topics like racism in a game.

                          4) Why have the Bastet of the Americans done zero about this in the past 300 years? Whether they'd fall in with Black Tooth or not in modern times, why have they completely ignored this situation? Or the other non-Garou Fera for that matter. Just like they have minimal skill in avoiding human notice, they'd be even worse in hiding from the Bastet or Corax, and the Mokolé probably have somethings to say about it once they find out.

Even if Heavy Arm's reasons for disliking this iteration of the Ajaba were strong (which they aren't), most creative obstacles facing the werehyenas could still be overcome with a bit of effort.

You say this could not happen but there are extraordinary ways that recognition from the spirits could be given. It might take something big, something dramatic, something Chronicle worthy, but it is hardly impossible. There are plenty of creative ways these Ajaba could succeed despite their status as feral ex-slaves. Some of their kinfolk might have been more involved in shifter society, perhaps the oldest among them recall the names of a few spirits or even possess Gnosis or ritual knowledge. Or maybe nearby shifters did nothing, because there are barely any Pumonca left following the Second War of Rage. Either way this is something to explore, not reject out of hand. This isn't even hard.
