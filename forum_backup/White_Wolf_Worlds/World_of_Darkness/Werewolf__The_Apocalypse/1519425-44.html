        Originally posted by Ermenegildo View Post
        Knightingale

        1) W5 has definitely more settings content in the book than the one you can find in the three basic books of D&D. Did they remove the Litany/Umbra/Spirit/Rite/Auspicies/Forms?
        Hmm... One might wonder, though: What exactly is “setting content”? Is it the mere info-dump of what has happened when in the past of the setting? Of course not, it’s a game after all! It’s all about what you can do with it as a player or Storyteller.

        So, in that sense, I have to say: You’re quite wrong. D&D 5E has more setting-content than W5. Excessively so even. And yes, I’m just talking about the three main-books. After all, the quantity of options of all those classes ranging from level 1 to level 20, the variety of monsters offered that present challenges to the aforementioned variety of adventurer-PCs of various levels and a system supporting tactical fights and map-based dungeon-delving.

        W5 is rather lackluster in terms of setting content in comparison

        2) There is only one W5 book, you can not compare it to the whole D&D books lineup, it is not fair nor sensible. Please note the D&D 5ed settings book is few and usually criticized by some of the fans because they assume that the player already knows something about the settings due to a decades-long legacy. For example, you may search for some reviews of Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide which was particularly infamous for this.
        I can’t compare it to the whole D&D 5E lineup…? I was already constricting myself to only talk about WotC-content and even that already frustrates your imagination…?! There’s third-party-content as well, mind you, of course and what even is the point of your complaint here? Where’s the unfairness? And W5 is getting more books… The first one just has been announced and Renegade has already released additional supplementary material. So “only one W5 book” is a temporary thing and already untrue at this point anyway. And since W5 is about to be a line of multiple books, of course I will mention it within the context of D&D 5E’s multiple books. After all, the whole comparison to D&D started with you. You were the one who invited us to delve into this comparison. You were the one responsible for choosing this to be the two game-lines that are getting compared. No reason to shy away from it now when the comparison is helpful in elucidating W5’s flaws.

        3) I think that both justifications for the Silent Strider's wandering are plot hooks, you like the tightest one which makes all Silent Strider cursed and introduces the vampire's elements, I like the loose one which allows for multiple interpretations of the wandering and for exploring different idea in different games. Could you please elucidate to me why the vampiric curse is the only explanation for the Silent Strider's wandering that is compatible with the Rage, the existence of the Garou Nation, and the Impergium?
        The thing is that the old version of the vampiric curse wasn’t just about wandering. It also cut off the Silent Striders from their Homeland in the Umbra and also cut their connection to their ancestor-spirits. And while the Silent Striders acclimated to the curse by being nomadic and fulfilling roles like messengers and diplomats, that loss of their homeland is mark of shame for the Tribe. And that then colors how other Garou treat them and how, for example, Silent Striders treat vampires wherever they encounter them.

        There’s conflict here, drama and complication. That’s intriguing. What this delivers is a hook. Something that inspires curiosity and interest. And your mention of the “only explanation” only invites the obvious notion: At least, you got an explanation in the past. While the past explanation may introduce constraints, the “no explanation”-approach to making Silent Striders being forced to wander only shows off a lack of effort and/or imagination by the W5-writers. If the writers want to pay so little attention to a concept like the Silent Striders having to wander, they should have just not mentioned it to begin with. Just turning it into mandatory homework for STs is not an improvement.

        By the way, maybe is a cultural or linguistic thing, but I am quite confused by your posts. From the content and the context, it is quite clear that you are expressing your personal opinion and your tastes, yet the way you are expressing it makes it look to me like you are proclaiming some undeniable truth.
        Well, I’m not running for President, if that’s what you’re worried about .

        4) Each tribe could be defined by a small set of attributes, a sentence of a list of 3 to 5 words. Once the core is fixed you can explore different implementations of the core idea. Removing the vampire curse from the Silent Strider does not change them into the Black Fouries or the Star Gazer because the curse by itself is not the core point of the tribe.

        Do you think that any chronicle with a Silent Strider PC or NPC must involve their vampiric curse in an explicit way? Not its generic consequence (the wandering and the lack of contact with the ancestors) but the explicit fact that a powerful vampire cursed the whole tribe, making it relevant that the curse comes from an Egyptian vampire with godlike power and not from other supernatural sources.
        See, you ask the most important question right there: “Removing the vampire curse from the Silent Strider does not change them into the Black Fouries or the Star Gazer because the curse by itself is not the core point of the tribe.” Good point… So why mention the wander-bit at all then…? See, that’s the thing you’re overlooking: Why mention it at all…? W5 is supposed to be a “re-imagination”, so… where’s the re-imagination then…? Forcing Silent Striders to wander but only this time around you don’t explain it at all is the complete opposite of that. The W5-writers just copy-pasted a bit from previous editions and cut out the explanation. That’s it. Zero imagination and effort happening.

        5) I came out with those questions in less than 20 min, I'm not a long-term player of WtA nor a super expert in world-building. I'm sure that 80% of the W5 readers without prejudice toward the game could come up with a sensible story to fill the gap in the book in less than 3 hours.
        Oh, and so you think the game-developers’ time is too previous to offer aid in that process? I guess we need to give PbtA-game-developers a call and tell them that putting those questions on the Playbook-sheets is a waste of time. I wonder why PbtA-games have kept doing that despite you insisting it’s a waste of time…
