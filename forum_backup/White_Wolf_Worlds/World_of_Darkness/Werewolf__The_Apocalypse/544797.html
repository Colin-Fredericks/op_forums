    Heretical Thoughts on Rat

    06-08-2015, 01:09 AM
    This is a long post, but I think it's worth it.

    I like taking the assumptions of the setting seriously and then find things that the authors did not intend. Rat as a Totem for the Garou is one of those. Rats are animals native to southeastern Asia. Rats did not spread into other lands until humanity developed enough to have agriculture, cities, and trade. For example, rats are known to not have reached Europe until the time of the Roman Empire. Brown Rats (also known as the Norwegian Rat) didn't reach Europe until the Middle Ages, and Ratkin are mostly tied to that species, not the black rat which arrived most earlier. I don't think most authors realized this. They assumed because Rats are universal around the world now, they must have always been so. They didn't realize how much the spread of rats across the world depended on humanity. Incidentally, Southeast Asia is also a region of the world where wolves did not exist.

    This has lots of interesting implications. Implications I don't believe any published material took seriously although it has occassionally been mentioned that rats originate in southeast Asia.

    First, it is extremely hard to imagine how the Bonegnawers could have become a Tribe until rats and wolves existed in the same place. I don't see how Garou could have selected Rat as a Totem when they had not ever run across his children in the Realm. This most likely points to India as being the original homeland of the Bonegnawers, and that the tribe could not have come into being very, very late in terms of history. At the very least, the Bonegnawers could not have existed for either the Impergium or the War of Rage. It also means that the Bonegnawers likely did not exist throughout much of the world until rats spread to those areas. Can you imagine some Bonegnawer explaing to a Fianna what his Totem is when no Fianna would ever have seen a single rat? Bonegnawers had to arrive in Europe quite late – not until well into historical times. No Fianna or Get of Fenris ever saw one in their native homelands until the Roman era or the Dark Ages. Most tribes probably had thousands of years of history where Bonegnawers did not even exist.

    Second, it implies some interesting things about the Ratkin and the War of Rage. To put it bluntly, the Garou are highly unlikely to have killed many of them other than some odd traveller who was very far from home. While it is entirely conceivable some random Garou used Moon Bridges to show up in southeast Asia and slaughter some Ratkin, it is extremely doubtful they stuck around long enough to really exterminate them. Ipso facto, someone else must have done so. We have lots of candidates – the most likely being the Bastet since southeast Asia is home to tigers (Khan) and leopards (Bagheera). However, bears (Gurahl), crocodiles (Mokole), snakes (Nagah), and just maybe foxes (Kitsune) were also in the same habitat. Isn't it more likely some of them slaughtered the Ratkin? The Garou no doubt slaughtered lots of the other Fera giving the habitat range of the wolf, but blaming the death of Ratkin is probably a smear. This fits into my idea posted lots of times earlier that the concept of a “War of Rage” was created by historians well after the events in question.

    Third, the idea that Gaia gave them the role of controlling human population by eating their grain and spreading disease is just bizarre. For thousands of years of human history, rats did no such thing. Not only did it take long for humans to develop agriculture, but it took even longer for it to spread to southeast Asia so obviously the Ratkin's purpose was not to devour grain to induce famine and keep human population low. Furthermore, without large cities generating trash and sewage rats could live on, they wouldn't be very good at spreading disease. It's like saying that if Prarie Dog-People existed, their purpose is to limit human population, and if you don't see how Prarie Dog-People could do that, just wait several additional thousand years and it'll all make sense then. Whatever the original “purpose” of the Ratkin – and I think any “purpose” of the Changing Breeds are posthoc justifications of them asking themselves, “why are we here” - it couldn't have been to control human population. If they had a purpose at all, it's likely related to their small size and perchance to hide in trees and burrows. They are the spies of Gaia who were meant to go unseen and explore where others could not go (this would also explain why rats – and hence Ratkin – eventually cover most of the globe). This sounds like an overlap with the Bastet's purpose of learning secrets (since cats are supposed to be curious). This goes back into who ended up killing the Ratkin. If the local Bastet felt these small creatures were intruding on their domain, I can see them wiping out from spite. Given the connotations of what cats do to rodents, it makes perfect sense.

    Fourth, it is strange that Ratkin are considered to be a force of the Wyld. The only thing truly Wyld about them is 1) their fecundity, and 2) their ability to have established a wild animal habitat in the midst of human cities. If the Ratkin see themselves as a force to cull the human race by famine and disease, this clearly is not a function of the Wyld. It marks them as a minion of the destroyer – the Wyrm, even if they think they serve the old “Balance Wyrm”. I'm going to assume that the Ratkin originally were a force of the Wyld – their small size compensated by large birth litters and ability to flourish elsewhere than their original habitat to aid them in the mission to act as spies and go where the other changing breeds could not. However, the vast majority of Ratkin are now fallen, become corrupt, and are now effectively minions of the Wyrm. However, they don't know they are, and neither do the other Fera. Ratkin are in effect what the Children of Gaia are always afraid the Garou will become – so bound up in anger, despair, and hate that they fall to the Wyrm. Likely, the Ratkin are in that intermediate stage between the point of their fall and before they consciously chose to align themselves with the Wyrm. This is probably the same stage the White Howlers spent decades or even centuries before the tribe died and was reborn as the Black Spiral Dancers. It's just possible they can be redeemed, but too bad no one knows it. Everyone is so paralyzed by the equivalent of Garou liberal guilt and Changing Breed multiculturalism they can't admit what seems obvious to me – the Ratkin are traitors.

    Lastly, this implies some interesting things about Rat as a Totem. The books tell us that Bat fell to the Wyrm, but that it was effectively “insane” for many years – devoted to both Gaia and the Wyrm schizophrenically until it finally fell. Rat is likely the same way. Since the Bonegnawers are the Children of Rat this has an important impact on them. We should see evidence of the Bonegnawers falling to the Wyrm. And we do see this. Such sentiments are found throughout the game line from many tribes who constantly complain about the Bonegnawers shirking their duty. I don't think the tribe overall has fallen as the Ratkin have, but they are approaching a major crisis within the tribe. Right now, the Wyrm taint is merely causing them to give up on their duties as Garou. It has not caused them to begin actively serving the Wyrm. However, we already know of the mechanism the Wyrm intends to use – the Maneaters. The Maneaters were large enough to be a f ull blown “camp” within the tribe, and we know this “camp” exists in many places and septs. While this is now downplayed in official material, we can imagine that the Maneaters is the eventual destiny of the Bonegnawers if they fall to the Wyrm. The Wyrm just needs time – perhaps even only a few more decades – for the seed to grow. It even explains why the Maneaters persist as a camp, its members truly believe Rat wants them to do this since Rat is in the process of falling just like Bat did.

    Those are my thoughts at least. Like many of my posts, I'm putting this on here not to argue I am right and others are wrong if they disagree with me. But to generate ideas some of you may find useful in your own games. But I am interested in hearing what you think.

              I can't help but think this is a bit too... scientific.

              While the Rattus genus originated in Asia and didn't spread to Europe until much later in history, rodents aren't exactly strangers to other places. Rodentia goes back to Laurasia (which split into North America, north Africa, Europe, and Asia), allowing rodents to spread to every modern continent besides Antarctica. A pre-historic Fianna might have never seen a Rattus rattus but they had plenty of rodents in their ecosystems. Early humans weren't exactly very picky about "small rodents that eat grain and carry disease," to consider the ones with fur on their tails significantly different from the ones without.

              And it isn't like most Fianna ever ran across a griffin, unicorn, pegasus, or chimera running around in the wild... but those are all Tribal Totems.

              While the theory has a lot of interesting ideas, it feels way too reliant on the ancient Garou and spirits caring about extremely modern concepts like "true rats" needing to be from one particular lineage of rodents, rather than lumping dozens of lineages of rodents together and calling them all rats. The idea that the Bonegnawers couldn't exist in pre-history because 2-3 species of rats hadn't become so widespread yet; doesn't seem to really hold up when every inhabited continent has animals called rats on them that got there without human intervention.

              I think there's more solid territory in exploring two things:

              1) Why are some of the Fera heavily associated with a small number of species while others are associated with vastly large taxonomical groups? Even without being scientific about it, it is remarkably odd that some are incredibly strict (Nuwisha are coyotes only) and others cover immensely diverse groups (Rokea are any sharks).

              2) How exactly did the mundane animal associate Totems get where they are. How did Rat get to the top of its brood so fast compared to Mouse? How did Stag beat out Elk, or any other deer? What happened to put Owl in over Scarab?

              These seem to be much bigger holes in how the setting is described, and raise far more questions that could be interesting to answer, than getting picky about the taxonomy of the small furry things eating seeds and grains in ancient Sumer.
