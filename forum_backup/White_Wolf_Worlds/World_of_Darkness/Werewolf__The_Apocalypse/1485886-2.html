    Yeah, now we're talking. With actual knowledge about street life and a talent for acting you can go places.

    Using the Scent of Sweet Honey in front of Gnawer witnesses early on would help you out a lot. If you use it in a creative way, so that your story will be told for you, you've opened an idiosyncrasy credit you can work with, for example when you use that Gift to lure insects to a corpse to mess with the forensics of human law enforcement. Gnawers will tell your story and have a laugh and you're golden as far as first impressions go.

    Your way to introduce yourself wouldn't fly far with how we play it. You see, as much as all Gets are considered almost an Ahroun honoris causa, most Gnawers are versed in being Bashy. So planning and fantasizing about how you could work around Truth of Gaia yourself makes you sensitive for verbal acrobatics. And messing with the wording of your introduction is something we know from Stargazers and Ronin where I come from. So your Nuwisha would have to work around how exactly the players of your area choose to play the Howl of introduction. It's not impossible, but not trivial either.
    With enough clever Intel you might be able to just state a Camp, if you're really that good. That would work around not stating 'I am a Gnawer' because as far as anyone knows 'I am a Frankweiler' is everything you need to say to become one if we're being picky to trick the Gift. But as I said, that means you'd need to witness the Garou introduce themselves to each other to work out a strategy beforehand. (And no that's not impossible when the coyote can turn invisible anyway). And yes, pulling that off in a way that fits how your troupe plays introductions would bring you 'pretty much in'.

    I think that if suspicion is raised, it won't go immediately in the direction of you not being a wolf, it is much more likely that the initial suspicion would be that you're simply a Ronin who lied about his past trying to scam Gnawers to learn survival Rites and Gifts.
    Depending on how useful and funny you have been to the Gnawers you might fake being that Ronin and maybe even a simple 'please don't ask, I mean you guys only the best' could work towards staying even a little longer.
    In fact, I think faking a Ronin who refuses to talk about the Name and Tribe he doesn't have anymore might be possible to pull of, if you just really made them like you beforehand.

                        I think that one could argue that the breed books weren't written with every detail of every other book in mind. They tell their own story with their own theme, and with the Nuwisha book you can even get a different feel from the illustrations.

                        It's like with making Umbra crossovers work: sometimes you have to decide whether you're telling a Mage story or a Werewolf story to decide how a certain aspect "really" unfolds.

                        In a Nuwisha-centered story one could argue that of course those ultimate Tricksters got a deal worked out with the 'truthful' spirits ages ago and even the most honorbound entities of pure honesty somehow managed to at least stay silent out of respect for the Nuwishas role.

                        The same question can be equally well answered the other way around when the focus of your story lies elsewhere. Then those pesky coyotes (who have no honor but humor instead) are just one of the many reasons why those honesty-bound spirits are adamant in their ways in the first place.

                        If you exaggerate in both directions you'll see that the answer neither can be that no Nuwisha ever manages to come near a Caern with a Totem of Respect, nor can it be that there is an ancient deal for a free pass for all Nuwisha ever, because, like Asmodai said, in the history of the world there would have come up at least one 'awesome clusterfuck' because leaving such a back door open can't go well indefinitely.

                        That's why I like to brainstorm possible complications and have a look on how the arsenals and tool boxes of the different sides interact with each other to get a feeling on how this game of hide-and-seek could play out.

                        I am a fan of the Idea of some kind of standing deal, but I would argue that this deal wouldn't be worded in a way that lets the Trickster off the hook completely.
                        As a powerful Totem Spirit of honor and honesty I might be able to sense the nature of every visitor to my Caern and I might be able to accept that Gaia gave that Werecoyote the job of tricking my allies/servants into learning a lesson and I might see how me seeing right through the charade would hinder Gaia's plan for that Trickster so I might take him to the side and tell him that his secret is safe with me.
                        But I might also tell him that Gaia made it his job to stay sharp and be smart about it and that Gaia didn't give me my watchful Spirit eyes just to look away.

                                            So yeah, a pro could pull it of to just show up at a moot, but the 'noone would suspect anything' part is a gamble I'd be very paranoid about.

                                                Originally posted by Ilsaena View Post
                                                While I agree with every point you make, I take it you have never Larped a WoD System?
                                                And you're the first one to even mention a LARP here. I'm not sure if any Sept would even have 40+ members.

                                                LARPs have a distinct dynamic that changes everything for a lot of reasons. For example, while all those kinds of people exist as NPCs too, in a TT game you can just avoid them, because you're not physically constrained as a player to be regularly in line of sight of other people. In LARPs everyone is a PC, you have to consider their behavior as such (for example, probably 95% knows the Nuwisha, and 80% or more read their own Tribe Books), and so many factors change drastically.

                                                Things that work fine for one mode of play are plain stupid in the other, which is why sometimes key aspects of the setting must change or be reconsidered under a whole new light. This isn't to say that your considerations don't hold some water, but that assuming that the LARP dynamics should inform the general question is silly and not really helpful for others in a vacuum.

                                                We would need a whole new discussion (here or elsewhere) to talk about the infiltration trope in LARPs altogether. Which is a valid topic, just not what was being discussed.
