    Tips on running a pentex office raid?

    10-04-2021, 09:27 PM
    I want to introduce the players to Argus Industries by having the wyrmfoe send them on a raid on an industrial plant of some kind. A self contained 4 hour session mission. Any tips on how to make it fun/interesting so that it's more than just killing fomori guards/sneaking past security cameras?

      What is the goal of the raid?

      If it's destruction, then it may involve multiple steps to put the facility out of commission rather than just temporarily disable it.

      If it's espionage, there may be room for hacking or investigation to uncover the relevant information.

      Some kind of unconventional defence or obstacle may help, something that can't just be punched out of the way, at least not at first. Example, a psychic fomori that serves as a surveillance/alarm system that also can penalize targets with a 'psychic shriek.' Before they can overcome it, they have to reach it, either evading its surveillance or overpowering its defences.

              Start with a common industrial plant and expand from that. Twist machines, surveillance, cafeteria, building, personnel, but keeping in mind that they're a plant first and foremost. Their main mission isn't to fight players, but to do whatever this place does.

              Add secret spaces. They may be extra basements, locked safes, spatial anomalies or exist only in the Umbra. The Core-Wyrm-Feature that makes the plant tick is to be hidden in one of those, and another one protects the Chief Administration. Think a door that seems to be an elevator when used by them, but actually opens to the gates of Hell and the elevator is just a magic trick. You need at least those two, but you can add more to your hearts' content.

              Layer it. Probably not everyone in there is already a fomor and some areas are open to the public, others are at least open to investors and public official of all sorts. Wyrm taint and mutations should go from non-existent > very subtle > Subtle > Weak > Strong > Complete. Take care when positioning them as visitors and features shouldn't "leak" to an area more than one level from their intended positioning during normal working days.

              Keep a list of Key Features and the reason why the players should take them out. The #1 Key is the Core-Wyrm-Feature, it may be a machine, a tainted well, whatever the Pentex either created or explored there to make the Plant "special". If left alone it only takes some days for Pentex rebuild, restaff and resume operation. The #2 Key is the Chief Administration. Whatever those guys are they are dangerous people, and left alone they'll start a new plan somewhere else soon and plot their vengeance. You can add other Key Features, like labs, ambitious personnel, cunning monsters or stacks of knowledge that can rise from the ashes of the building if not taken out.

              Bring reinforcements. As soon as the PCs are detected reinforcements will be called from a Pentex paramilitary station. Industrial plants may be designed to withstand nuclear bombardment, but not to house soldiers. As hellish as the place may be and as powerful as you make its denizens, a platoon of battle-hardened wyrm-tainted hunters will come prepared to deal with a Garou Pack. Open fight with them should not be an option.

              Finally go back to ground one and remember it is an industrial plant first. There are humans around and with more time police and firefighters will be called to keep the facade and force the Garou to lie down or risk breaking the Veil. If the PCs are still there, this is a win-win situation for the Wyrm, every decision is bad.

                  Some of the ideas here are quite good.

                  It's hard to give specifics, but I think in general what you want are some encounters that 1) can't be resolved by simple combat or use of Gifts, and 2) lead to interesting interactions or dilemmas. Those will break up the monotony of show up, kill someone, move on, kill again, continue, etc. These kind of things are different than just "building a better dungeon". They will make the facility actually interesting by making it a story.

                  For example, maybe there is someone working at the place that intends to turn evidence to the government or media and is compiling evidence to turn it over. But the PCs won't know that yet. They'll need to discover that. So what happens if they meet the person and kill him before they learn about it? Or because of their raid, they destroy the evidence he's been compiling. Or do they take the time to learn about things and are able to help him? What specifically will happens depends on how you stage it and what the PCs decide to do. Just try to prepare it so that you can quickly change things in reaction to the PCs.

                  Another idea is that Argus is preparing to destroy the facility when the PCs arrive. Maybe the facility has outlived its usefulness, and they are sabotaging it so they can collect insurance (and possibly spread taint). In that case, the raid may actually serve their own purposes. The PCs need to figure out what is really going on, and improv a change in plans. Maybe they need to prevent the destruction of the facility and take out the sabotage team posing as eco-terrorists (maybe the Green Knights?).

                  Maybe there are lost kinfolk working that shift at the facility. Somehow the PCs will learn about that after they arrive, and the PCs now need to decide what to do. Do they not change plans? Figure out a way to remove the lost kinfolk to safety? Think about how to eventually bring them back into the Garou Nation? Even better - make the kinfolk some important person or people managing the facility, so it isn't a mook. It's someone they would normally want dead. Maybe sweeten the pot by making that person even more special like a kinfolk who has Gnosis. Maybe she's not necessarily a "bad guy". She loves her family. She contributes to charity. She never kicks puppies. She's just very ambitious and Argus has been good to her. Make her someone the PCs should be wanting to recruit. And therefore someone the sept back home would want the PCs to not kill or ruin, but recruit and may cause the PCs to lose some Renown if they just abandon her.

                  There could be a project just wrapping up there the night the PCs hit the place. Maybe it is a physical item that is intended to be shipped out. or just shipped out earlier that evening. Maybe a software program or other file completed and emailed out. An important meeting that is just ending and the people are going home (maybe they are currently at the airport, or staying overnight at a hotel before leaving the next day). So the PCs have an opportunity to learn about that and decide what to do (do they bother to read the whiteboard in one of the meeting rooms, or find other evidence somewhere in the facility). Do they leave the facility to try to intercept or stop whatever it is that's left? Do they realize there are higher priority targets and move to stop them first before continuing on with their lower priority plan? Do they never learn and miss out on the opportunity before learning about it in the news the next morning?

                  Lots of other ideas are out there that may occur to you because of your own creativity, existing chronicle backstory, or PC backgrounds.

                  Once the players have meaningful choices, it becomes more than just a dungeon crawl. Their raid now has a real purpose. You only need to fill up four hours so don't go overboard. But having one or two things that should cause the PCs to think and make a decision will turn it more into just mowing down nameless mooks just because. But you may want "back up" ideas if you intend for one thing to happen, but for whatever reason the PCs never run into it, and you need to suddenly add something new in that they do bite on. In that case, then dump the original idea as if it was never there.

                      Wonderful feedback, as always. This place is such a resource.

                      We didn't get to the Pentex raid last session, as the who game suddenly derailed in a wonderful and unexpected direction as a character's decision resulted in their ultimately having to choose to leave the pack. Lots of great rp, and as a result I have another week (or two, as we now need to introduce a new PC to the pack) before running the aforementioned raid.

                      I ended up deciding to run a small recycling plant that backs into a general scrap/towing yard surrounded by mostly abandoned buildings, a whole block, as it were, complicit with the wyrm's activities. The crux of the plant is that it uses a bane antenna to draw low level banes into large vats, where they're boiled down and refined into a serum that's then used as a foundational ingredient in other subsidiary's poisons and products. Most of the plant is thus low-level security, but there's a cordoned off part at the back with unusually high security whose cameras are linked into a First Team or elite, silver-armed squad.

                      I've also added a corrupted Weaver spider spirit that's watching over the Branch Director's office, a drattosi in the main scrapyard, and put drums of toxic chemicals that Clean Earth (the company) is midnight dumping across Detroit as a side gig. Those are watched by fomori that live in the abandoned rowhouses. Finally, there's an Obfuscate using ghoul on the premises who was assigned to uncover financial irregularities by a higher up, and who might trouble the PC's down the road. He's mentioned in communications in the director's office, and provides a lead into the kindred activities in the city.

                      So, a series of low-level encounters, with leads locked away in the director's safe and trapped inside his computer, along with the potential to disrupt the Wyrm's activities by destroying the antenna. I tend to adapt these things on the fly to the player's curiosity and how well they're doing, but it should prove a good taste into the mortal corruption side of their war against the Wyrm, as well as setting up large things to come.

                          Make it funny.
                          Pentex is an object of satire. Think of all the things wrong with the office/labour jobs, then turn them up to 11.

                          The players sneak in at 03:00 thinking such a late night attack will avoid unecessary casualties. Nope. half of them are still working overtime.

                          The players become subjects of suspicion for non-garou reasons- journalists, PIs, cops, Lawyers, Union members.

                          There's a dress code. The secrataries look like they're in a porn shoot. all the men have the same haircut and everyone has a lifeless look to them. When the players start killing, most staff members are elated, others grumble that postal episodes are too common.

                          If players are a bit too successful sneaking in you can actually have someone go postal to spice things up.

                          The guards are either useless or a lawsuit magnet.

                          lots of company propaganda. Odd slogans everywhere.

                                      I haven't seen anything I would consider bad advice in this thread, so much so that I'm going to bookmark it for future ference. That said, if you're running this using a VTT or any kind of visual representation, don't be afraid to make a lot of the areas samey, and feel free to lean into an almost absurd take on the idea of an endless cube farm. Otherwise, there's interesting symbolism to be explored by having worker's proximity to power (i.e., how close their workstation is to the executives' or managers' offices) track with how corrupted or drone-ish they are. Employees working on the first few floors might be relatively normal people, with more quirky or potentially dangerous employees situated near the corner offices (as if the corner offices where managers are located emanate corruption.) The employees on the top floor, or the last floor before the executive suite, might all be fomors, or complete husks because they've got one or more banes that have been hanging off them for years.

                                      If you have Glass Walkers hacking the office's network, you could potentially have them be able to find a corporate, or location-specific org chart, the interpretation of which could reveal significant and helpful information about the metaphysical hierarchy of the office. This could provide a benefit as follows (mind you, this is just one possibility): as the pack ascends or progresses, they start running into stiffer opposition--however, by looking at the org chart, they deduce that there are certain elements of middle management that are more than mere drones, and whose destruction would significantly lessen the opposition of hostility from the rest of the prole workforce, at least within a given area.
