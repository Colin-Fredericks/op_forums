      No, not everyone loves Lupus, but I digress.

      The Lupus affects the game in two major ways, aside being a character option: the great interest on the Garou Nation's part to keep them existing, and the Red Talon's Tribe, that is Lupus Only (and the main source of Lupus Garou).

The setting takes on a even more accentuated fall to the Apocalypse, but remains mostly intact.

          I think if you take the setting in broad strokes, you can keep it more or less completely intact without the Lupus breed. In modern nights, in setting, Lupus only account for 12-14% (depending on book) of the Garou population.

          What to do with the Talons comes to mind as an initial question. You could just write them out and pretend they never existed. You could have them be a Metis Tribe. Sort of an alternative to the Spirals that isn't completely corrupt but still hates Homids and "adopts" the unwanted (Metis) from other Tribes. Griffin could stay in place with little rewriting.

          You might want to boost starting Gnosis by 1, if you're going to eliminate Lupus. But that's more a mechanical idea than an in story one.

          All in all, I think it is completely doable without breaking the setting.

                              Well, there's a large chunk of lore centered on the phrase losing the wolf. On a personal level, it means that you've lost a measure of ferocity and wildness, mechanically defined by dropping to 0 temporary Rage and Willpower, and the individual in question can no longer shapeshift or regain Rage until they regain Willpower. Occasionally, more extreme needs for recovery crop up, such as with Vic Stryker, who lost the wolf for decades before becoming able to shapeshift again.

                              In a wider, more group-related sense, it means something way more serious, because the werebeast cannot exist without the beast. The Apis are pretty much gone for good because all wild aurochs are gone; recovering the changer would mean somehow recovering the aurochs or a spiritually-compatible equivalent in some fashion. The same would happen to the Garou if all wild wolves died.

                                          W20 ended a rather important lupus problem of not speaking a human language without Linguistics, too, just as it ended Linguistics and replaced it with Academics. Lupus still get one (human) language for free, so that's one major hurdle of old out of the way. It's less a matter of having no idea about what to do and playing someone who's at times uncomfortable and tends not to see human people in the same light they see themselves, albeit way farther off than just coming from another human culture. They'll be taken aback at how humans blithely move about without any concern for potential threats, feel the need to move around more often, and also, the concept of grocery stores will make them uneasy, but they'll ultimately be better able to adjust to life among normal humans than a certain anime character. Also, they won't punctuate their sentences quite so strangely as another, similar character.
