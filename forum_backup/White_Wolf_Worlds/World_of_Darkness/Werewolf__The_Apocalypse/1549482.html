    Bastet (Mechanical Deep Dive)

    03-21-2025, 12:54 PM
    Alright. Now this thread might actually be quite long!

    Werecats! The Bastet claim to be the youngest Changing Breed, since Cat is a very selective type and Gaia had to come to him and ask for help before he would create a Changing Breed with her. Bastet lore holds that Gaia created them to rule over the Gaian Shifters.

    Divided into species-based Tribes, the Bastet are about as diverse as the Garou, but far rarer due to the losses suffered during the War of Rage. Still, by Fera standards, they're not all that rare, especially in Africa, South America and Asia.

    Naturally this thread is not a substitute for W20 Changing Breeds; I won't be getting as much into the lore, geography, character and stuff, mostly the mechanics.

    Yava

    The Yava are supernatural weaknesses of certain Fera, the Bastet and the Ajaba. If you know all of the Yava a group suffers under then you know how to exploit their weaknesses to the utmost; supernatural abilities against a target whose Yava you know are at -2 difficulty, and using supernatural abilities against someone who knows your Yava gains a +2 difficulty.

    So, for example, an Ajaba trying to use a Gift such as Pulse of the Prey against a Simba would most likely suffer +2 difficulty. Meanwhile a Simba would very likely have a -2 difficulty to use the same Gift against an Ajaba, as most of them have been taught the Yava of the Ajaba. The Yava of the various Tribes will be covered below.

    Tribes

    As I review the Tribes I will be giving them a Red, Amber or Green rating for Yava, Forms, Stats and Gifts respectively. These ratings aren't meant to put you off playing them, but something to keep in mind when considering your character, especially if you enjoy some gentle character-optimization. If a Tribe has a Red rating for Yava it probably means there's an insta-kill in there, which might be worth avoiding with the relevant Merit. Stats-wise it's beneficial if you have the option of playing low-rage, and if the total number of your Rage + Willpower is good. For example, Rage 1 Willpower 5 would beat Rage 5 Willpower 1; they add up to the same value, but the former has the option of being Rage 1, while the latter must be Rage 5.

    Keep in mind that a Tribe with amazing Forms, stats and Gifts, and a generous set of Yava, could be contextually worse than another Tribe in your story, or make for a worse character. When I rate these aspects I'm very much boiling them down to the bare essentials.

    Bagheera
    Wereleopards are noted to be mystics, though they share this with many Tribes.

    Forms
    Their Crinos form gives +3/2/3 while their Chatro form gives +2/3/3

    Stats
    Rage 2, Willpower 4.

    Yava
    Their Yava are sleeping during the new moon (though they can be awoken by violence), being forced to follow a trail of salt if they see one, and that if their prey are blessed with a special sign of protection they will be forced to flee if their rage is not just.

    Gifts
    Bagheera get a few interesting unique Gifts, including a meditation stat boost, a time-slowing debuff and a mystical superform.

    Balam
    Werejaguars are Rage-filled and have relative expertise in uncovering lost and foreign Gifts and Rites, and have even been known to learn the powers of Fetishes for themselves.

    Forms
    Both Crinos and Chatro forms give +3/2/3

    Stats
    Rage 4, Willpower 3.
    Having a total of 7 is good, but you are forced into a high-Rage character here.

    Yava
    Demons will force you into a maddening rage, burning your heart (presumably after your death) will permanently destroy your soul, and a means of instantly killing you by taking the feathers shed by the Tribe's flight gift and burning them in your face.

    Gifts
    True Fear, Wings of the Pegasus, this list is pretty decent. Their Level 5 Gift, Feed the Gods, has the potential to instantly kill a target by force-pulling their heart out, but the odds of this actually happening are very slim.

    Bubasti
    Shifters who are kin to the Kyphur cats of Egypt, the Bubasti are considered beautiful and mysterious even by the standards of the Bastet. For the third time in a row we have a Tribe of magicians, but the Bubasti are said to be even EVEN more good at it. This includes copying foreign Gifts and learning 'cat magic' spells, though anyone of Rank 2+ who practices such sorcery has a light scent of the Wyrm about them.

    Forms
    Crinos gives +1/3/1, Chatro gives +2/3/1
    Mechanically these forms aren't amazing, but your Feline form blends in decently well; you look like a rare variety of cat, not a wild animal.

    Stats
    Rage 1, Willpower 5.

    Yava
    2/3 of these are related to the Tribe as a whole, making you relatively light on personal weaknesses, though the politics of your Yava will still bind you quite badly. If all of the Immortals of the Bubasti die, the whole Tribe dies. If there isn't a single Bubasti in the land of Khem then the Tribe becomes doomed. Finally, on a personal note, you will eat any food put before you.

    Gifts
    Bubasti Gifts include magical charity, perfect translation of written language, and an annoyingly weak Bane-banishing Gift with severe downsides. Their list is very cool, but it is a little lacking in mystic powerhouses... until you get to the Rank 2 Spirit Ward, normally unlocked at Rank 4, which is amazing.

    Ceilican
    Fae-touched and able to change their Pyrio (Auspice) as they go through life, Ceilican can constantly reinvent themselves, and have even changed species to evade death!

    Forms
    Crinos gives +1/3/1, Chatro gives +0/4/1
    +4 Dexterity is great, but the lack of a significant Strength and Stamina buff is a little worrying.

    Stats
    Rage 3, Willpower 3.

    Yava
    Ceilican suffer from pure iron as if it were silver, speaking their birth name backwards makes them uncomfortable, and once per year they must completely abandon their memories and identity and start over anew. The final Yava is very problematic but could be a great plot point in a game with a ST who is ready to do something interesting with it.

    Gifts
    These include musical instrument mastery, enchanting weapons, summoning Fae allies and turning into a housecat. All in all I would say that Faerie Kin alone makes this list Green, so long as the Storyteller is ready to roll with the Changeling angle, and if they aren't, you probably shouldn't be playing a Ceilican.

    Khan
    Powerful and filled with Rage, Khan are closer to Garou than other Bastet and are primarily focused on raw power.

    Forms
    Crinos gives +3/2/3, Chatro gives +4/2/3

    Stats
    Rage 5, Willpower 2.
    Having a total of 7 is good, and if you're playing a Khan, chances are you were always going to go Rage 5. Context makes this Green.

    Yava
    Fall asleep during a solar eclipse. If you are presented with the meat of a human child you will be forced to eat it. A direct challenge can't be ignored or you lose your Rage. I'm marking this as Red because the 'refuse a challenge' aspect is a real weakness which will come up almost every session; it's a great weakness to have, it's great storytelling material, but it's dangerous. Khan are powerful and extreme, and this weakness forces you to keep that in mind!

    Gifts
    Bonus soak, attack spirits through the gauntlet, walk with the weight of a petal, a very powerful dragon attack, and a neat shockwave. This is a very solid combat list.

          Pumonca
          Werecougars wander North America and are heavily disparate, more likely to meet other Fera than their own kind. Being the main North American Bastet also means this is probably the most realistic Bastet to play in a random game set in the USA.

          Forms
          Crinos gives +2/3/3, Chatro gives +3/3/2

          Stats
          Rage 4, Willpower 4.
          The first 8! This is undoubtedly amazing.

          Yava
          Leaving 'your land' for more than a moon cycle kills you, being immersed in poisons which harm the land kills you, every animal except the 'great cats' fears you and flees from you.

          So, what we have here is a guy who might be stuck in a tristate area (works fine for most games), at most leaving it for a week or two. If you get immersed in a tank of toxic sewage you die, no questions asked... and you're going to set off every dog, cat, cow, bird, wolf, fox and so on you encounter. I'd strongly recommend using the relevant Merit to ignore the second Yava in the list here, and perhaps to overcome all three for 5 Freebies.

          Gifts
          Pumonca can mimic any voice or sound they've heard in their life, like a Corax, and they also get Call Elemental.

          Qualmi
          Introspective and wise, the Qualmi are meant to remind other Shifters of their duties. Sounds like a really dangerous job, especially where Garou are concerned; people love being told how to do their job! They're lynxes, for what it's worth.

          Forms
          Crinos gives +1/2/2, Chatro gives +2/3/2
          Using Chatro makes this okay - it's slightly worse than average but not terrible.

          Stats
          Rage 2, Willpower 5.
          Total of 7, and the choice to leave Rage low if you prefer. At Rage 2 you'll only be bothering exceptionally weak-willed humans with your Curse.

          Yava
          Unraveling your riddles undoes your magic, pollution poisoning your local salmon will harm you, you're unable to tell people about your parents and if you see proof of who they were you become confused 'for days'.

          The riddles one is a little confusing, but a good Storyteller can do interesting things with it. Needing to ensure your local salmon are looked after sounds like a faff, but could be interesting. The parents one is unlikely to come up, but someone being able to confuse you 'for days' is a potentially severe weakness.

          Gifts
          Camouflage while naked (i.e. in any form besides Homid), +3 to Enigmas, a powerful Enigmas-based Gift where you terrify a group of people by confusing their sense of direction, potentially triggering a fox frenzy or total panic, and an xray vision power with a huge range.

          Simba
          My impression of the Simba isn't great, lore-wise, but their mechanics are pretty great. The Simba see themselves as the natural rulers of the Bastet and indeed of all Fera, a bit like Silver Fangs but for the whole of Gaia.

          Forms
          Crinos gives +3/2/3, Chatro gives +4/2/3

          Stats
          Rage 5, Willpower 2.
          Total of 7, very Rage-heavy, but if you're playing a Simba you were probably going to grab Rage 5.

          Yava
          A rope made of your mane will hold you, "stealing your roar" will put you in a very difficult position and force you not to harm the one who has it, and if you're male, you'll never let someone kill your wife (husband of the year), and you can't kill your wives either.

          Female Simba basically have 2 Yava instead of 3, which is quite funny. The rope made of your mane thing is great, very folklore-y. Stealing your roar is given no context, so presumably magic is required. The wives one is more the area of NPCs.

          Gifts
          +1 to Intimidation and Leadership, force others to kneel, Luna's Armour, Coup de Grace! This list is very good, with a mixture of leadership and combat focus.

          Swara
          Fast-moving werecheetahs, Swara love fixing problems but they are also particularly xenophobic, meaning that a Swara who bonds with outsiders will be an exception.

          Forms
          Crinos gives +2/3/2, Chatro gives +2/4/2.
          +4 Dexterity is very good, especially with +2 Strength and +2 Stamina. That is all.

          Stats
          Rage 2, Willpower 4.

          Yava
          Insulting your mother (figuratively as well as literally) drives you mad, soil from THE MOON drugs you if you drink it, and you fear Frenzy so you seek to avoid it at all costs.

          Being very wary of Frenzy is wise, but the Yava suggests that the Swara have especially good reason to fear Frenzy, as it lets the Wyrm in. The mother one is worse than you might imagine, someone calling you a "son-of-a-bitch" might provoke a Rage roll or force you to punch them in the face.

          Gifts
          Step Sideways at Rank 2. Beyond that... a bunch of mediocre stuff stolen from the Silent Striders. On the assumption that you don't just grab Step Sideways, which is meant to be a very rare Merit and is very expensive anyways, being able to Step Sideways at Rank 2 (likely most of the game's runtime) rather than Rank 4 is a huge improvement. I wish this list had more to it, but you know.

              Breeds

              Bastet are divided into Homid, Metis and Feline. They don't use the Garou Breed Gifts however, unlike the Ajaba they have their own Breed Gifts. Metis are still deformed but face no discrimination from the Bastet.

              Flavour-wise I'm very glad that Bastet get their own Breed Gifts, but they really suffer from it in a way. The Garou Breed lists are immense. These are very short. That said, as a Bastet you have a very good opportunity to steal Gifts from other Shapeshifters, so perhaps consider these lists a starting point.

              Homid

              Gnosis 1

              Urban Hunter (City Running), Sweet Hunter's Smile (Persuasion)
              Eavesdropper's Ear (Whisper Catching), Jam Technology
              Cowing the Bullet, Craft of the Maker (Reshape Object)
              Monkey's Uncle (Doppleganger), Tech Speak
              Madness

              Monkey's Uncle is slightly changed from Doppleganger, as a Bastet using Monkey's Uncle can disguise themselves as a human, Bastet or Great Cat, rather than just humans.

              Cat Claws (1): Get the partial transformation of Crinos claws in your human-like forms without spending Willpower, and the difficulty of the transformation is reduced to 6. What this means, essentially, is that you can get high-damage Aggravated claws in your Homid and Sokto (akin to Glabro) forms. This is actually pretty useful! It will also work well with any claw-buffing Gifts.

              This list suffers a little from lacking Apecraft's Blessings and stealing some of the worst Homid Gifts, like Cowing and Reshape.

              Metis

              Gnosis 3

              Create Element, Shed
              (No pre-existing Level 2 Gifts)
              Visceral Agony
              Moon's Gateway (Open Moon Bridge)
              Wrath of Nala (Divine Wind)

              Sense Primal Nature (1): This is Sense Wyrm on steroids. For a +1 difficulty modifier you get to sense each member of the Triat, distinguishing their energies from one another. So you might use this Gift and notice that a person is heavily tainted by both Weaver and Wyrm, which immediately gives you a lot of clues as to their true nature.

              I have a feeling that if the Garou had this Gift on their Metis, the World of Darkness would probably be in better shape!

              Blinding Moonbeam Gaze (2): Your eyes become like headlights on a car, which is very funny to me. Roll Gnosis, difficulty 7. For the scene you may turn the lights on and off at will. They illuminate darkness, which is handy, and also "distract" and "blind" people, but we are given no mechanics for this.

              Given that Bastet get night vision in their General list, this isn't great.

              Whisker Sight (2): Roll Perception + Primal-Urge (difficulity 7). Success grants you the power to see everything within arm's reach of your body, even if it's invisible or shrouded, out of sight, and so on. The utility of this is pretty great; you will notice a lot of stuff moving around a scene slowly with this turned on. "Everything within arm's reach" is a pretty big distance in Crinos and Chatro too! Great for combat, detective work, avoiding being ambushed and so on.

              Fist of Cahlash (3): Snarl at something, rolling Rage (difficulty 6 for objects, difficulty 8 for characters). Every success turns 10 pounds of matter to dust, or inflicts a level of Bashing damage. Examples used for object destruction are weakening a building or destroying toxic waste. The ability to clean up the world is pretty nice. You do risk Frenzying using this Gift however, if you have high Rage!

              This Gift gets Green because you can use it on anything you can see and which can "hear" you. The briefcase the pentex rep is running away with. The rungs of that ladder. The key in the Fomor's hand. The jeweled sceptre a dickish Simba says makes him the rightful ruler of all of Gaia's children. And so on. No protections for Fetishes and other special objects are mentioned!

              Feline

              Gnosis 5

              Heightened Senses, Pounce (Hare's Leap)
              Catfeet, Prehensile Tail (Monkey Tail)
              Chains of Mist, Underbelly (Fatal Flaw)
              Beast Life
              Revolt of the Land (Gaia's Vengeance)

              Sadly Feline Bastet get two unrated Gifts (see their relevant entries), a Red, and a bunch of Ambers. But the Greens are great... and they'd get them immediately with the Lupus list. Catfeet is confusingly included here, despite being a Level 1 Gift in the General list. Though if you take the RAW extremely seriously you could ask to buy it a second time and double up on the difficulty reduction

              Mine (1): Rub yourself against an object and spend 1 Gnosis. Anyone trying to steal the object from you has to pass a Willpower roll (difficulty 9) before they can do so.

              This is great and very flavourful! Use this on your Fetishes, your valuables, stuff you steal, you name it. The duration is permanent and the cost is very doable. Great for a conquerer and for a cat burglar, hell, great for a Player Character since even in a very grounded game they tend to be slightly more looty than an average NPC.

                      General Bastet Gifts

                      Worth noting that Bastet are prone to learning Gifts from eachother, and to stealing them from other Shifters. These Gifts are a flavour of the abilities most Bastet are able to most easily learn.

                      Catfeet, Diamond Claws (Razor Claws), Lick Wounds (Mother's Touch), Open Seal, Sense Magic, Sense the Truth (Truth of Gaia), Sense Unmaker's Hand (Sense Wyrm)
                      Cat Sight (Eyes of the Cat), Eerie Eyes (Staredown), First Slash (Spirit of the Fray), Pulse of the Prey, Sense Silver, Swipe (Taking the Forgotten)
                      Farsight (Scrying), Invisibility, Touch the Mind (Mental Speech)
                      Wolf's Terror (Silver Claws)

                      Banish Sickness (1): Spend 1 Gnosis, roll Manipulation + Medicine (difficulty depending on severity of the illness), and you need to lick your target. On a success you cure the target's disease, so long as it is curable.

                      This is the missing piece that makes a great healer. Typical Garou healers are great at keeping you alive, restoring health levels will even stop someone from dying of poison or acute illness, but curing the disease is often beyond them and requires a Talen or Ritual or Spirit. This skips all that. It's a great bargaining chip which can ingratiate you with various NPCs, Veil and local culture permitting.

                      Silent Stalking (1): Dexterity + Stealth difficulty 5, on a success you can move silently over ANY surface (fallen autumn leaves, creaking floorboards) for the scene. No cost, just a risk of the Gift being blocked off if you fail the test. More Gifts should have this stipulation about failure to avoid pointless rerolls.

                      A solid stealth buff which is well worth 3xp.

                      Night's Passage (2): So long as you are in shadows, even if the shadows are mostly illuminated by sudden bright light but still exist, you remain invisible. Dexterity + Subterfuge/Occult, difficulty 7, remain invisible until you are out of a shadow or attack someone, and the cost is 1 Gnosis.

                      This is a very solid invisibility power which would be usable in most rooms under most circumstances, and in many outdoor environments. Just remember that invisible is not undetectable. Scent of Running Water would complement this further, as would Blur of the Milky Eye (which effects all attempts to find you, not just visual, based on wording).

                      Shriek (2): Stamina + Intimidation (difficulty 7), on a success everyone within a 20ft range is deafened for 1 turn per success. Great ability, no cost, you can use it any time, deafening a crowd of enemies and then slipping into the shadows invisible would be a very good combination for instance. Those deafened also get a +1 difficulty modifier while the effect is ongoing!

                      If used strategically you could use this against a crowd of enemies before your allies step sideways or drop down from above, that sort of thing. The lack of selectiveness isn't ideal, but the absoluteness of the wording is great. I wouldn't say that protective earpieces would help against this Gift, and I believe it would work on spirits and creatures that hear but normally couldn't be deafened (hearing with hair all over their skin etc.).

                      Caper (3): Very powerful distraction power. This Gift requires you to move or perform in a way which draws the eyes of bystanders, for example a beautiful dance in homid or gracefully skulking around in your feline form. A difficulty 3 Manipulation + Expression guarantees a lot of successes, and so long as you have more successes than the WP of bystanders they're mystically forced to pay attention to you. Violence breaks the spell, and you have to keep performing, but your allies could get up to a bunch of useful things in the background.

                      Impala's Flight (3): Stamina + Athletics difficulty 6, for 2 turns per success you double your running speed. No cost, and you can use this as often as you like.

                      Given that your feline form may be very, very fast indeed (anywhere from 40-120km/h), the ability to double that speed might be useful. The Gift being free is neat, but I'd like it if you could make it last for a scene by spending a point of Rage or similar. All in all this is pretty good, especially in chase scenes and giant maps.

                      Clawstorm (4): Spend 1 Rage and 1 Willpower, make 4 melee-range attacks in your turn. This seems to mean that you do all of these attacks during YOUR turn, rather than the 'extra' attacks going at the end of the round as they typically would with Rage.

                      Naturally you don't get to spend Rage for bonus attacks if you use this Gift... but you can use Rage for bonus ACTIONS if you use this Gift. So, for example, you could spend 1R1W for 4 attacks on your turn, and 2R for +2 actions (dodges, running away, environmental checks) in the same round, assuming a permanent Rage of 5.

                      You're limited to (Homid Stamina) uses of this Gift each Scene, so for example with a Stamina of 4 you could use this Gift 4 times in a single battle. This is a great saving in terms of Rage and the turn economy of it is great, since you get your 4 attacks upfront; with spirit of the fray you could very easily kill many Ahroun with this. Because what tends to happen is that two Rage-heavy characters both do a single attack, both are fine, and it's the bonus attacks at the end of the round which decide who lives and who dies. With this Gift you can kill your foe before they have a chance to do a single Rage action, even if they're above you in initiative.

                      This is also a great boon to powerful Bastet with a Rage of 1-4, who normally wouldn't be doing so many bonus attacks anyways. It also saves on Rage vs how much you'd normally need to spend to do this many attacks.

                      Walking Between Worlds (4): Allows you to step sideways. Taking the Merit, or a Fetish, to do so sooner wouldn't be unwise. That said a huge part of why Bastet have such incredible Gifts is that they are missing the ability to enter and leave the Umbra at will. So in a typical game you'll be waiting until Rank 4 to get this... though I could see it causing problems for the flow of the game if you are the only person in the party who can't step sideways.

                      So this is Green if you need it, and irrelevant if you don't.

                      Perfect Passage (5): Become intangible to objects, but remain tangible to people. This is a bit of a complex intangibility power since it has caveats; if you don't see a knife or bullet coming it can hit you, so you need to be aware of something to let it pass through you harmlessly. But this Gift lets you move through non-living weapons, walls, armour and so on. You're also immune to things like poison gases, but you are still breathing.

                      You can pick up an object and it becomes tangible to you in this form. Your clothing and held objects are along for the ride, letting you do weird things like pull a chain through a wall.

                      1 Gnosis, Wits + Larceny (difficulty 6), lasts for 5 minutes per success. A practically legendary power. This is especially useful against foes who depend on Melee and Firearms, along with explosives and so-on.

                      Withering Stare (5): Spend 1 Gnosis, roll Rage (difficulty of the target's Willpower). Every success inflicts a level of Aggravated damage. We've seen a lot of iterations of this Gift, and it isn't that great for Rank 5, unless you need to kill a weakling with a glance, which is potentially very intimidating to bystanders.

                      The problem is that rolling Rage against a difficulty of 3, say with a weak enemy, is going to put you into a Frenzy or Thrall. This Gift can only EVER kill a target if you get 7+ successes after all! It doesn't feel like that's been thought out.

                      This is a GIANT general list. Sense Magic! Mother's Touch! Sense Wyrm as standard, and Metis can get an even better version to go with it, you could get either or both. Catfeet at Level 1, making it being Level 2 for Felines redundant. Razor Claws is a favourite, and Truth of Gaia is a standout. Essentially this list feels like it was put together by a Bastet player, which makes me smile. Who needs a good Tribal or Breed list with Gifts like these? Though having other good lists won't hurt at all.

                          Beast Courts

                          Bastet are welcome in the Beast Courts, and thus can gain access to the Court Gifts. These Gifts are covered here:

                          Garou Gifts: Ranking, Thinking, Overthinking - Onyx Path Forums
                          https://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/main-category/main-forum/the-classic-world-of-darkness/werewolf-the-apocalypse-aa/1511902-garou-gifts-ranking-thinking-overthinking?p=1549277#post1549277
                          I'm going to break down Gifts by level and category, bit by bit. If it's Wyrmish, Fera-only, obscure or lost it's going to feature much much later, if at all. This is intended to focus on the Gifts you will actually be getting in play, hence starting at level 1 and working up. I'll be considering utility (both inside and


                          Part the Wall at Rank 3 is a real upgrade for most Bastet, who typically get to Step Sideways at Rank 4. Lantern is a great buff to most Bastet, as it grants Willpower generation and various powerful social Gifts. Fists gaining Resist Pain is a neat benefit, and Stoking Fury's Furnace is always a good addition. Mirror is a brilliant addition to almost any Werecat since it grants Spirit Speech, Persuasion, Command Spirit and various other Umbra powers. Leaf is likely the worst; Bastet get many of these Gifts already. Since Bastet get healing by default, there isn't much point to Pillar. Courtier getting Persuasion, Flame Dance, Scent of the Oathbreaker and a few other benefits makes it quite worthwhile.

                          Bagheera Gifts

                          Treeclimber (Balance)
                          Lawgiver's Legacy (Command the Gathering)
                          Eye of the Cobra, Weak Arm

                          Treeclimber is rated Red because Catfeet, available at Level 1 in the General List for Bastet, makes it essentially redundant.

                          Humbaba's Escape (1): -2 difficulty to rolls to escape restraints and squeeze through small spaces. This is entirely passive, which I like, and of course for 3xp this is probably just about worth it, but if I'm getting a Gift to help with escapology I expect it to be like "you can fit through any gap bigger than a grape" not "-2 difficulty to a roll you'll almost never make".

                          Ojas Surge (2): Meditate for (6 - Rank) minutes, roll Gnosis (difficulty 7), and boost a variety of Attributes with the successes. The bonus can boost a stat above 5.

                          A highly niche use of this Gift is that it overcomes a problem WTA characters can face at very high levels. Bonus actions are limited by Dexterity and Wits. Say you have Rage 9, Dexterity 5 and Wits 5. You can only take 5 actions each turn, meaning that you can't spend 5 Rage to take 5 bonus actions for a total of 6. If, however, your Dexterity were boosted by your fighting form to 6 or 7, and your Wits were boosted by Ojas Surge to 6, you'd be able to manage it. A more likely problem to overcome would be if you had enough Rage to take 4 actions in a round (Rage 5) but you had a Wits below 4; this Gift could help alleviate that problem... if you can meditate for a few minutes prior to battle. Not impossible! But niche.

                          Beyond that it's handy to boost Stamina for Soak, Dexterity for dodge/hits/damage, Strength for feats of strength and damage, and various Attributes for various different Gift activations. The duration is a single scene, but this could be great for projects, crafting and so on, or preparing for a Rite. The lack of cost is also very neat. If you have time to prep there's never any reason not to use this.

                          The Paradox of Time (4): Spend 1 Gnosis, roll Manipulation + Enigmas (difficulty of target's Willpower), and for each success the target is disoriented by the non-linear flow of time for 2 turns. So, with 5 successes, you'd daze someone for 10 turns. The penalty is losing 3 dice from your rolls, which could do very little (11 dice to hit on a claw attack) or a lot (rolling 3 Gnosis to try and step sideways).

                          Mages with the Time Sphere ignore this Gift entirely, which is a neat bit of Flavour, but honestly doesn't make much sense to me. A Technocrat with Time 1 has no special relationship with the flow of Time when they aren't using a Procedure to observe and measure time somehow; being able to overcome this Gift with a simple Time 1 effect I can understand, but immunity doesn't really check out. You might as well give the immunity to anyone with Chronos or Temporis too.

                          How good is this? Well targeting WP kinda sucks at this level of play; anything worth destroying will probably have decent Willpower. Two turns per success is alright, but many Gifts last for a scene. -3 dice is pretty neat... but it depends on what kind of enemy you're facing. Arete gets utterly trashed by this, as does Gnosis, but Attribute + Ability rolls will probably be fine. Now if it halfed dice pools, now we'd be talking. Basically this is akin to giving someone a nasty painful wound, but without actually hurting them. Between the Gnosis cost, and the Willpower difficulty, and the duration measured in turns... and the simple fact that attacking will probably inflict a worse dice penalty... this isn't that great unfortunately.

                          Shiva's Might (5): Spend 2 Rage and 2 Gnosis to enter a "Juddho" form where you are a hyper-giant Crinos with many limbs. +6/3/6 for stats, and its flaming weapons deal Strength + 4 Aggravated (no difficulty to hit given, so presumably 6).

                          This Gift is... decent. Costly. But decent. Being a huge combat monster is neat. But the cost is annoying, and the downside, which I won't get into too much detail on, is very annoying. Basically you lose all your Rage and fall asleep, which could easily be deleted, the moment the Gift expires.

                              Balam Gifts

                              Hunter's Mists (Curse of Aeolus), Storm of Pests (Scent of Sweet Honey)
                              Pathfinder, Terrors (True Fear)
                              Ancestral Wings (Wings of the Pegasus), Wandering Forest (Trackless Waste)

                              Pathfinder arrives a rank early, and Ancestral Wings interacts with the Balam Yava; the feathers you drop from using this Gift can be used to kill you. That doesn't change the Gift's ranking, but worth keeping in mind if you don't take a Merit to overcome the Yava.

                              Vision Cloud (4): Fill an area of 50 feet with a cloud of gas that forces those that breathe it to have visions. These visions are generally helpful, showing the future, or revealing lost information.

                              Spend 1 Gnosis, roll Manipulation + Enigmas (difficulty 5), and on a success the Gift just... happens. To me, however, the key question is what happens if someone is trying to resist. Does holding your breath work? Presumably. How thick is the cloud? What happens if it's used in the middle of a fight? There are a lot of questions that'd need answering, and I actually kinda like the idea of a Balam stopping a fight by filling the area with vision mist that leaves everyone seeing the deaths they'll suffer if they fight one another... but that's highly ST-dependent.

                              Ultimately this Gift could be great, or awful, and it's so much in the hands of the ST that there isn't much to say. The roll is easy, but I would just get rid of it. The cost is fine. If the Gift works as intended it might help a little, or a lot.

                              Feed the Gods (5): Just read this!

                              The Balam can rip out the heart of an enemy from a distance, pull it to his hand, and consume it in a burst of fire.
                              Sounds really awesome and powerful. Unfortunately the Gift is just a way to stunt on incredibly weak foes. Roll Gnosis, difficulty 7, and spend 2 Gnosis. If your successes are double the target's Stamina (e.g. 6 successes for Stamina 3) then their heart flies out and you can catch and burn it. Oh and also, things like Fortitude subtract 2 successes per level.

                              This Gift needed playtesting. They were so scared of an instakill power they made it useless. But here's the thing; if you're gonna do that, don't write an instakill power! There are a lot of other ways you could write this power, for example spending more Gnosis for more successes, needing only to match the Stamina (not 2x Stamina) if the target is Mauled or worse, circumstantial difficulty reductions and so on. There could also be a benefit to the Gift successfully going off, like it strengthens the local Naturae or Gaian spirits or something like that, or your Totem grows in strength.

                              All in all a terrible power with a really, really over the top and decently cool description.

                              Bubasti Gifts

                              (No pre-existing Level 1 Gifts)
                              Dreamspeak, Spirit Barrier (Spirit Ward)
                              The Many Tongues of Ptah (Speech of the World)
                              Arms of Darkness (Coils of the Serpent)
                              Shadow Brethren (Shadow Pack)

                              Spirit Ward at Rank 2 is pretty amazing; it normally comes at Rank 4. Speech of the World is bumped up to Rank 3 from 2, annoyingly. Coils of the Serpent is also bumped up from Rank 2 to Rank 4, which still leaves the Gift as being very useful, but is pretty annoying. As a Bubasti you could very easily steal Coils of the Serpent if you ever met an Elder Brother, and it seems a relatively likely combo given the mystic inclinations. Easier said than done, don't get me wrong, but the point is that if you stole Coils of the Serpent from a Garou you might get it for 10xp at Rank 2. If you wait patiently to get it on your own list you might pay 12xp at Rank 4.

                              All in all the Bubasti list is highly focused on esoteric and academic roles, mystic stuff too, but the base Bastet is so good at this that I'd rather play a magic-obsessed powerhouse than a Bubasti, at least personally. It's more fun in my book to play a nerd with giant muscles, and if the Bubasti list doesn't offer anything unique to help with the role of mystic, people will default to another option. Scholar's Friend is pretty handy, but also replicated by a 1-2 point Fetish almost certainly.

                              Alms to the Poor (1): Spend 1 Gnosis to create a handful of food or a few coins. This could ingratiate yourself to locals... but if you are a Bastet and you can't get your hands on some food or petty cash, I question your skills. The ability to turn 1 Gnosis into $3 or so (the book says $2, but I'm accounting for inflation) repeatedly is interesting, and in theory you could generate infinite money if you had access to infinite Gnosis, but you're never going to have access to infinite Gnosis.

                              Scholar's Friend (1): Understand anything written down in any language, so long as it isn't written in a code of some kind, in which case you can read what it says, but you don't necessarily understand what it means.

                              For example reading some old French poetry will translate the meaning of the words. If there is a hidden message in the poem it won't be revealed, but you might spot it yourself with the right skills.

                              Banish Cahlash's Brood (3): Evoking the authority of the Wyrm itself, based on the flavour of the Gift, you banish Banes and other Wyrmish spirits.

                              Spend 1 Willpower, roll Manipulation + Enigmas, difficulty of either the spirit's Rage or Gnosis, whichever is higher. Every success removes 3 Essence. Which is nothing.

                              In exchange your fur turns darker, and your actions get more eratic, and if you ever botch the Gift you gain a Derangement. You also smell of the Wyrm for a week after using this Gift, meaning Garou NPCs might just kill you on sight.

                              Immediate thought is that this Gift should target whichever is lower out of Rage or Gnosis, allowing you to go after a spirit's weakness. Or perhaps you choose which to target, testing your knowledge of spirits; if you can infer from observing a spirit, or your own Occult knowledge, that a spirit has very weak Rage/Gnosis you might choose the better of the two options. Then, and this is important, the Gift needs to do something other than "remove 3 essence per success". If you want to do that you have an option, though it's much more aggressive; it's called attacking!

                              The Wyrmish spirit could lose 20% of its Essence (minimum 3) per success, for example. So say you fight a 50 Essence Wyrmish Elemental with a Gnosis of 3 and a Rage of 6. The cunning Bubasti works out that the spirit is relatively slow in its thoughts, and infers that it likely has weaker Gnosis. Manipulation + Enigmas, difficulty 3, the Bubasti gets 3 successes and the Bane loses 60% of its Essence, in this case, 30. There would be a restriction on the Gift saying you can't reuse it on the same spirit in a given month.

                              Anyways. RAW, this Gift is absolutely terrible.

This Gift exists alongside options like invisibility, ignore-me powers, hypnosis, teleportation, stepping sideways and so on... but a lot of supernatural creatures who will foil or see past those won't necessarily be fooled by a cute cat.

                                          Khan Gifts

                                          Rhino's Favour (1): This is a prominent, ugly, veil-breaking and mostly useless weapon. If your hands are out of use and you want to be able to gore with a charge, neat, but the difficulty 7 is annoying and Strength + 2 Aggravated isn't that great. Plus you need to spend Rage to access it. Gifts like this need a rider effect, like bonus dice to hit or lower difficulty when charging, to be worthwhile.

                                          Skin of Jade (1): Spend 1 Willpower, gain +2 soak. This is a great ability whether your soak is poor or excellent; going from a soak of 2 to 4 is great because you've doubled your soak, and going from a soak of 10 to 12 is great because you've significantly increased the reliability of you going "nuh-uh" to incoming gunfire. If you can combine this with Fetish Armour or Luna's Armour you'll get even more mileage out of it. We aren't told whether this soak works against Silver if you are in a vulnerable state, but presumably it doesn't.

                                          Heart of Fury (2): See the Rank 3 Ahroun list. This Gift is somewhat different for Khan since their Rage difficulty always defaults to 6, rather than shifting with the moon. Adding +1-3 difficulty to Rage rolls is a double-edged sword and is also very hard to pull off if you have a high Rage, which is the reason you'd want this Gift in the first place. Unfortunately the Gift still needs reworking even removed from the Garou context.

                                          Ricepaper Walk (2): Roll Gnosis, difficulty 6. For 3 turns per success you exert a weight equal to that of a scrap of paper on any surface. In theory this would let you walk on water, impossibly fragile glass, a leaf canopy and so on. If paper could sit on it, it works.

                                          This isn't a gamechanger given the Gnosis roll, which interferes with Rage, and the duration being measured in turns rather than a whole Scene. But a major downside of being a multi-tonne primordial hypercarnivore whose Chatro form makes Hispo look relatively small is that there are many, many times where your weight will lead to you breaking surfaces or causing lots of noise, setting off pressure alarms and so on. Used cleverly this Gift could be great.

                                          Asuras' Bane (3): See Banish Cahlash's Brood. This Gift will turn a weretiger's coat white instead of black, but otherwise it is identical. The Gift needs reworking and as it stands it is terrible.

                                          Paws of the Raging Spirit Tiger (3): I'm absolutely shocked that there isn't a Garou Gift that does this! Swiping sideways is a hilarious idea and very fitting to their work. It's especially interesting to give this Gift to the Khan, who won't be Stepping Sideways (unless they're in the Beast Courts, or using a Merit or Fetish) until they're Rank 4. This is what made me go "oh wait... can Bastet peek through the Gauntlet?" And to the best of my understanding they absolutely can! That's how you aim this Gift after all.

                                          So, let's assume a Gnosis of 3-5, you roll against the local Gauntlet, you see through, and now you spot a Bane. It won't be spotting you currently, chances are it isn't peering through the Gauntlet 24/7. Spend a Gnosis, for the next 6 turns you're able to murder it through the Gauntlet with your hands/paws. I'd suggest waiting a turn, for what it's worth, so that on the very first turn you attack you can also spend Rage for bonus attacks.

                                          It's worth noting that this attack isn't impeded by the Gauntlet at all, and there's no roll for the Gift's use, though of course you have to roll to attack (probably a very easy roll since your foe won't see the attack coming in most scenarios). What this means is that if you have a good way of peeking through the Gauntlet in cities, you'll be a peerless Bane-slayer, since their ability to fight back will be very limited. This is the sort of Gift which, if it had been shared between Breeds, might have led to a very different outcome in wars against the Wyrm.

                                          Dragonroar (4): This is a powerful dragonfire attack which costs 2 Gnosis. I like that it's aimed with Dexterity + Brawl, instead of randomly expecting you to have mastered Firearms.

                                          On a hit the attack deals Ferocity levels of Aggravated damage. At this point, as a Rank 4 Khan, you likely have a Ferocity of 8-10. 7+ levels of Aggravated, even if it's soakable, is insane.

                                          And then it keeps going. Next turn 8 levels becomes 4, 4 becomes 2, 2 becomes 1, then it dies out. The odds of survival aren't amazing, and there's really nothing stopping you from using this Gift repeatedly beyond the Gnosis cost. This would be great even at Level 5.

                                          As a worked example, imagine a Ferocity 8 Khan uses this on my Metis PC; his default soak is 8 dice (base 5, and he is in Crinos form when Veil permits) and he has a bonus health level from Huge Size. 8 levels vs 8 dice of soak comes to an average of 4.5 levels of Aggravated damage, though in theory it could be anywhere from 0 to 8. Next turn the damage would be 4 levels vs 8 dice of soak, so an average of 0.5 levels of Aggravated, but it could be anywhere from 0-4. The 2 levels is unlikely to do anything at all, but could do 0-2 Aggravated, and finally the fire will go out with almost certainly 0 damage, but possibly 1 final level.

                                          On average this Gift wouldn't kill my boy, but a single poor soak roll, or starting in Homid, could completely ruin him. A Ferocity of 10 is also entirely achievable and makes the Gift even more deadly.

                                          Heaven Thunder Hammer (5): This one is very strangely worded.

                                          The player spends three Rage and makes a Strength + Primal-Urge roll (difficulty 3) to hit every thing within 10 feet directly in front of the weretiger. The attack adds the Khan’s Ferocity to its damage dice.
                                          So you "hit" everything within 10 feet that's directly in front of you. You "add" your Ferocity to the damage dice. So does it deal Strength damage as a base? Or is the base damage your successes on the Strength + Primal-Urge roll.

                                          This is unrateable per the RAW, but let's imagine for a moment that your successes on the Strength + Primal-Urge roll, plus your Ferocity, makes up the damage dice. And that it's Aggravated damage, why not. It could easily be Bashing or Lethal, but it's a Level 5 Gift.

                                          With maxed out stats, you might expect 9-10 successes on the initial roll, and at Rank 5 your Ferocity may well be 10. So let's say it's ~20 dice of Aggravated. That, in a small directional AOE, for 3 Rage, ain't bad at all. If that is how it's meant to be read... then this would be a Green.
