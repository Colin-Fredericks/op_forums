    Gaming the System

    Alright, so lying about your accomplishments absolutely doesn't work. Telling great tales bulks out your Renown a bit, but that's a feature, not a bug. Timing your big wins? Well if you can organize that, sure, but you really shouldn't leave a pack of Fomori until your 'career schedule' lets you tackle it. Even if the risk of that being figured out is very, very slim it would look bad if anyone did a deep dive into your calendar, which feels like a very Glass Walker thing to analyze. Bragging ahead of time helps, but again, that's intended, and it does introduce the risk of another Pack interfering to take you down a peg or kill-steal.

    The big thing that comes to mind, setting wise, is simple privilege. Say you have the pooled Kinfolk, Resources, Armory, Mentor, Contacts, Allies and so on that make things just... easier. You see, the crux of Renown is your accomplishments, especially for Glory. Taking out a 'minor' Wyrm threat, whatever minor counts as for your Rank, is worth the same Renown whether it was a huge struggle against adversity, hunting night and day for leads on a hard-to-find foe, or whether the whole thing was handed to you, target-wise, on a silver platter. That's where Backgrounds and Merits come in.

    Glory

    Every single member of your pack gaining a permanent level of Glory every single month is achievable, with the right organization and forward planning. With a well-developed information network (ideally in both worlds), the kind a Garou with extensive Kinfolk and Contacts might have, you could find yourself with more options of different things to hunt. A healthy mix of Banes, Fomori, Leeches and so on makes it look less like a Renown farm and more like you are proactively keeping an area clean and under control. Such a 'proactive' style does come with its risks, you're poking your nose into conspiracies and plans which are likely beyond your ability to fully vet, but that's where Resources and other Backgrounds are a big deal.

    Extensive spying, bribery, leveraging of allies and contacts, kitting out your squad with body armour and various useful weapons (to be Dedicated as Talismans via the Rite ASAP of course) can turn a fraught and potentially deadly combat into a walk in the park. Of course Garou are living weapons themselves, but tear gas can achieve in one turn what a single PC might struggle to get done in several rounds, given how turn economy works out with large groups of enemies.

    What we're looking at here, basically, is the difference between PCs and NPCs, in the simplest terms. But it's also the difference between packs who were born with silver spoons in their mouths and packs thrown together with awkward leftovers. So, in the medium of storytelling, you could show how at a Moot you have a pack of down-on-their-luck Garou who, in objectively a very impressive way, managed to make a lot out of a little this month. They wasted a lot of time hunting for a very slippery enemy, and the thing they killed was only a minor threat, but the ingenuity of getting into that Fomor's den despite it being so intangible and leaving so few traces makes it a sign of their skill as hunters. Another pack has a laundry list of their successes, and at first glance it's like every enemy just fell into their lap.

    If you were to ask questions in the follow-up, you'd find that the various members of the pack are responsible for guarding various important things and are plugged into various mentor-mentee relationships with vital Garou in the Sept. So when there's an easy win to be had, they hear about it first. And, to their credit, they're handling these informal missions very well, and it's not that they're doing anything wrong per se, but it means that Garou who aren't plugged into these mentorships and receiving the right 'tips', the ones who don't have family in every important institution in the town or city or state, lag behind.

    The more frequently you get opportunities to prove yourself, the faster you can ascend. It's also an opportunity to mess up and lose Renown of course, but the more support you have the less likely that is to happen. It might be worth, when writing Rank 4 and 5 NPCs, to consider who got to the top through an easy path, a relatively steady career with victory after victory lining up neatly for them, and who suffered ups and downs with hardships and punishments and chastisement for messing up. It might reflect the themes of the chronicle or the personality of the character, along with their origins and circumstances.

    Wisdom

    For a Garou's career as a Theurge, nothing beats a Theurge mentor. If you're basically the personal assistant of the Master of the Rite then the busywork you're stuck with will, luckily for you, build up your Wisdom faster than expected. Learning Rites grants you Wisdom, riddle contests with Garou and spirits (more easily accessed at a friendly Caern) grant you Wisdom, taking part in purifications (a likely task for a junior Theurge assisting the right people via Mentor and similar) grants Wisdom.

    Essentially, Wisdom is most rapidly accumulated by being at the beck and call of the most important ritualists in a Sept. Hands-on experience with learning and performing Rites will do a lot to build up your Wisdom, and the faster you gain Wisdom the faster you'll gain Rank, which justifies you getting involved in more senior Theurge business.

    It's also important to note that a very hefty Wisdom bonus comes from a threat being defeated without a single Garou coming to 'serious' harm. This is where being in a pack such as one described in the Glory section becomes very relevant. With the right equipment, the right allies, the right planning, the right preparation and support, chances are you'll be striking your prey in situations where they don't get past the ambush round. The turn economy, for an enemy or even a pack of enemies, of a whole Pack of Garou suddenly appearing out of nowhere and all using their Rage for bonus attacks while you're still surprised, is very hard to survive. I think any good ST would be very careful with the 5 point Wisdom reward for 'ending a threat with no serious harm to a Garou', since it basically has the chance to, depending on the story, grant you a full Wisdom gain every single in-game Moot, so long as you have two encounters in the whole month!

    Still, taken as RAW, Wisdom can be very easy to gain if you're in with the right people, and handed the right targets, and could be very difficult to gain if you have to scrounge for favours to learn Rites or be given leads on potential hunts.

    If for some reason you need to top up your temporary Wisdom before a moot swings around, finding and solving problems in your Kinfolk community is a great option. Naturally this requires you to have a Kinfolk community you're part of! But assuming that you have access to such homesteads and communities, you may well have disputes to mediate, matches to suggest, arguments to defuse, and Garou-appropriate jobs to fulfil. Keeping your Kinfolk sweet may well be a good supplementary source of Renown, and is probably easier for Garou who were raised in the Tribe, rather than being 'fallow' until the time of their Change, such as with wolves and humans who were away from the 'fold'.

    Honour

    This is the hardest to quantify. Killing a Fomor, that's very substantial. But keeping your promises immediately brings in some ambiguity. How big a deal does it have to be? Ultimately the more immersed and integrated you are into the culture of the Sept, the more of a common face you are at the gathering places of the Garou in your Tribe or Caern, the more opportunities you will have to gain Honour. If you're the child of an Elder and you have your dinner with honoured Kinfolk and Garou family each night, chances are you will find yourself more often in situations where you need to make and uphold promises, where you overhear things which you are required by honour to inform your Elders of, and where there are disputes to mediate.

    With all that said, the single best way to gather Honour may well be to teach other Garou, which is where your Backgrounds become relatively key. If your Mentor has an important teaching role within the community, it will likely fall to you to help them teach others. If you have been raised in the Garou Nation, learning the responsibilities of Kinfolk and Garou from when you could talk, chances are there will be much you can teach ignorant new cubs, much you can teach your packmates, much you can instruct others of as you go up in Rank. A clever careerist who takes on a difficult mentee at the first opportunity not only looks like a model citizen, they also get ahold of a potential Renown farm as they have more opportunities to teach another, uphold the Litany, give advice and so on.

    Naturally this is assuming it works. If your student is disobedient and willful, and causes trouble, there's every chance it'll reflect poorly on you. So wise advice from a canny political animal would have you pick a compliant or desperate pupil who knows they're on thin ice. For example, someone from a more marginal family, or who lacks any of the connections that power players in the Sept tend to have. Perhaps someone who is a poor fit for their Auspice and knows they need a lot of help to get into the right 'shape' for their job.

    It's important to remember that while we, as players, can read the Renown table and get a great idea of what rewards Renown, PCs, especially Rank 1 PCs who are recently changed Lupus, or recently changed Homids who grew up outside the Nation, won't know IC what will get them Renown. A bit of meta-gaming is likely to happen there without anyone realizing, but NPCs certainly won't have a clue unless someone tells them. While everyone will eventually get clued in by watching the Rite of Accomplishment, which includes recitations of your Honourable deeds, a Garou with the right family and mentoring will likely get a pretty big head start!

        Challenges

        Getting enough Permanent Renown to rank up is one thing. Think of that like padding your CV. Short of legendary acts which make your promotion basically a given, all Garou who want to go up in Rank need to be ready to undergo Challenges to prove themselves.

        W20 actually does a good job of explaining the difference between the Ranks in this regard. It's possible to be an exceptional Cliath for your Auspice but not quite have what it takes to go up to Fostern, let alone Adren, since you're missing out on some key stuff. This is more and more the case as you go up the Ranks. If you want to make the setting feel cohesive and like it 'adds up', feel free to have Rank 2, Rank 3 and Rank 4 NPCs who have more than enough Renown to go up to the next Rank, but who simply haven't done so.

        They might be waiting for certain Elders to die (not in a mean way!) before they seek ascension, or they might have political reasons to stay where they are, or they've never felt a calling to reach the highest Ranks. There are roles taken on at the highest Ranks which involve much more stress, responsibility, and risk of failure. While there are Garou who are career politicians, political animals, I would wager that the default are those who are fully bought in and who would never seek to be promoted unless they genuinely thought they were bringing something to the table that the Sept needed, at least when you get beyond Rank 3. Adren might be the 'natural ceiling' for many Garou, Athro is much more involved.

        Fostern

        Acquiring the rank of Fostern means that the Garou has grown from a raw recruit into a seasoned veteran. These werewolves have some familiarity with Garou society, and they have learned the nature of the war they are part of. Suitable challenges for this Rank should be moderately difficult - they should test the challenger, not overwhelm her.

        • “Acquire” a specific item hidden by a Ragabash of appropriate Rank.
        • Receive a boon from a minor spirit.
        • Convince the challenged Garou through impassioned speech and debate to recognize your Rank.
        • Compose a song, poem, or tale honoring the challenged Garou.
        • Defeat an Ahroun of appropriate Rank in a formal duel.
        • Stand guard at the caern for seventy-two hours without falling asleep.
        My takeaway is that while reaching Rank 2 isn't exactly 'easy', it's also expected that you will do so when you're no longer a 'raw recruit'. Fostern are Garou who are familiar with the Nation and generally have gotten past the stage of asking questions constantly. The example Challenges are pretty pedestrian and are probably easier than whatever you did to get the Renown to be ready to rank up. This one is kind of a rubber stamp. If you are a Galliard and you're unable to come up with a decent piece of prose honouring the Elder you're asking for elevation, I'm a little worried.

        Adren

        Those that reach the Rank of Adren have taken on major responsibilities within their sept. They hold most of the positions of consequence, such as Warder, Master of the Rite, and Gatekeeper (among others). Challenges for this Rank should be designed to weed out those who can’t act quickly or think on their feet; these are, after all, the future leaders of the sept and the tribe. Very few Garou pass their Adren challenge the first time.

        • Borrow a fetish from the challenged using body language, gestures, and non-verbal cues to ask for it.
        • Finish a difficult scavenger hunt within a predefined time.
        • Create an original (and useful) fetish from scratch within a certain amount of time.
        • Make peace with an enemy or negotiate a truce between two feuding werewolves.
        • Find an obscure hero of the Garou and create a fitting tribute.
        • Withstand severe beatings or taunting from your sept mates without frenzying.
        Rank 3, Adren, means 'major responsibility'. This means that the nature of the Challenge ought to test for those who are ready to take on major responsibilities, and a wise Garou coming up with a Challenge should be sure to pick something which challenges the Garou appropriately. If the character, or player, goes "ah great, that's easy" it should be because the task is deceptively difficult, not because they've been asked to do something they know they're great at. Challenges with intricate wordings which contain potential tricks and loopholes might be wise.

        I would point out that the difficulties of the suggested challenges here vary wildly. Going into Lupus and using your tail and ears and pointing with a paw to try and get something is pretty easy, I imagine a Cub could pull that off. Making peace between two feuding Werewolves could be anything from difficult to impossible. Regardless, the Challenge really needs to be individualized at this point, and like the writeup says, it should be time-limited and focus on checking to see if the Garou is incapable of being clever or reacting to new problems. Some of the examples are pretty awful at that however.

        Given that reaching Rank 3 means taking on enough responsibility that you will have significant numbers of "underlings", i.e. basically every Cliath in the Tribe will be bound by the law to obey reasonable orders, I believe that the best Challenges for Adren would involve having to get some Cubs or Cliath to achieve a goal. Delegating under time pressure means you need to identify the skills of your underlings and work with those skills to the best of your ability. Ideally such challenges would also include a curve ball where things change unexpectedly and the Challenged Garou has to quickly instruct their team (perhaps played by the other players!) to take on a different approach.

        Athro

        Athro have risen very high in the hierarchy of the Garou. They are mighty warriors, renowned lorekeepers, wise leaders, notorious tricksters, and famous judges. Werewolves that want a place among this elite group need to demonstrate exceptional skill and intelligence, and those very rare Garou who manage to attain this Rank on the first try are held in awe even by their peers.

        • Uncover every aspect of the business plan of an organization of potential interest, and do it without getting caught.
        • Restore a lost treasure of the tribe or sept.
        • Convince a spirit to act in a fashion completely contrary to its nature without harming it.
        • Talk a local street gang (or similar group) into a month of community service.
        • Write a song praising a rival, and use it to end your differences with him.
        • Rescue a Garou captured by powerful enemies of some sort, such Black Spiral Dancers, Pentex teams, vampires, or magi.
        Unfortunately the distinction between Adren and Athro isn't as clear here. Reaching Rank 3 has a key discriminating component of needing to be ready to think on your feet and change your strategy in emergencies. We're told that Garou reaching this rank need to show 'exceptional skill and intelligence' but we aren't given much heft to this concept. Are all Athro and Elder Garou 'intelligent'? I'll let you be the judge.

        To my taste it's very important that tests for ascending to Athro are designed to show that Garou's readiness to handle great affairs which are important to the Sept, and to the wider region of allied Septs. The examples are just very difficult low-level tasks for the most case, which is to be distinguished from higher-level tasks which relate to whole Caerns, local Tribe networks and so on. So a Challenge for Athro might be 'get these two Garou/Kinfolk families to sit down to dinner together', 'bring a whole Shame of Ronin back to Gaia', or 'leave clues to your whereabouts which cannot be solved by the Cliath and Fostern within the time limit, but the Adren manage to overcome with some difficulty'.

        Elder

        The most difficult challenges await those who seek the Rank of Elder. Elders are at the heart of a sept or tribe, and they hold the fate of the Garou Nation in their hands. Their decisions guide the Garou. Their deeds are held up as examples for younger werewolves to follow. Their mistakes can cause severe harm to Gaia, the Garou, or the war against the Wyrm. These kinds of challenges should test every quality the werewolf has.

        • Visit a caern of each tribe without being noticed, recognized, or challenged, and bring back proof of each visit.
        • Locate a lost caern and convince a totem spirit to adopt it.
        • Redeem, cleanse, and rename a powerful Bane without using Gifts (other than Spirit Speech).
        • Find a Garou lost in Harano and reclaim him.
        • Destroy a powerful Wyrm artifact.
        • Lead a group against a major Wyrm creature or other enemy, and defeat it without losing any of your comrades.
        At this point Challenges should be holistic. While you have to embody your Auspice, you should be ready to show passing familiarity with the work of all five faces of the moon. An Elder Ahroun needs to be ready to sing the song of her coming victory, carefully weigh up which Packs of Garou shall follow her, entreat the right spirit to lead the hunt (acting through a Theurge if necessary) and come up with a cunning plan to complete the hunt with great tact. At least that's how I see it!

        Most of the examples listed are okay, so long as you assume there is enough context around it to make it a true challenge. The Elder concocting the challenge shouldn't just be watching for victory, but also how the would-be Elder reaches victory. To my taste this should be the only Rank where it's possible to complete the challenge you are set but to be found lacking in your execution and told to try again later. How a Garou responds to this is in itself a test; a mistake made by an Elder is far worse than a mistake made by any other Garou, and so it makes sense that a Garou must be ready to show patience when it is required of them.

        So, do we learn much about Renown and Rank from the process of Challenging? Well one thing is that bias will inevitably creep in, if no one is guarding closely against it. Even if a Garou doesn't mean to play favourites, and they really shouldn't, it's possible for someone to give a test they know someone will shine in, even if they can't be certain they'll succeed, because they are invested in their career, or perhaps their ascent would help them politically.

        By equal measure it's possible for a Challenge-giver to give a Challenge which is harder than it should be; the examples given do vary in difficulty quite greatly! This might be done to hamper the progress of a pawn of a political rival, or out of sheer dislike, or bigotry. That said, it's likely that any examples of this kind of discrimination, or favouritism, in Challenges would need to be minor enough that there is plausible deniability, in most cases. You can't look like you're being unfair, so you'd need to play your cards close to your chest.

        That said, it's important to note that a Garou can challenge any Garou who is of the appropriate Rank. A Rank 2 Bone Gnawer Metis isn't going to bring their Challenge to a Rank 4 Fianna Homid unless they're incredibly ballsy and looking to prove a point. Chances are they're going to bring their Challenge to a Rank 3 Bone Gnawer who they know is a 'good sort' and will challenge them appropriately. This massively undercuts the possibility of an unfair Challenge, but it does increase the likelihood of a Challenge which is too easy. As I understand it, it would be very easy for Garou to pick their mentor, or a relative, or someone who has good reason to play favourites. It's possible to say that this is illegal in the Sept, or that the Elder Council of a given Sept chooses who you should challenge, or that the individual you challenge is chosen randomly, or by the direction of a Rite.

        But if you're going to run it as W20 lays it out, the simplest thing to say is that Garou of various Auspices and Tribes and Breeds are keeping a close eye on proceedings. A Rank 4 Metis who achieves their Rank and begins helping other Metis to climb the ranks at an unreasonable speed won't stay at that Rank for long. A Silver Fang who is showing huge preferential treatment to their Tribe may wind up in trouble with their own kin, as they risk embarrassing the Tribe by bringing unworthy youngsters up to a greater Rank than they can handle. Ultimately, if the social element of the game is well written, it should be an unusual situation if someone feels they can show favour to those who Challenge them without some kind of special leverage or unique situation.

        With that in mind, a Sept where a powerful Garou has such a cult of personality going that they've gotten away with bringing some Garou up to Rank 3 and 4 who really should still be at Rank 2 could make for an interesting subplot in a game. Similarly showing a Sept where Challenges are handled centrally, and where this has resulted in Metis and certain Tribes being left at lower Ranks even more than in a typical Sept, could be a good plot hook for a Philodox looking to get into the weeds of how running a Sept works. The Challenging system looks like it should work, essentially, but there's enough room for it to go wrong that you can get some good stories and Sept concepts out of it.

If you're not doing one really long game and starting new Chronicles, you just play the game differently. They don't require continuity with each other.

                    twig, your article clearly provides ample food for thought and I appreciate your willingness to share your theories of Renown, as applied and policed by the Garou.

                    I also wanted to point out that your critiques of various Rites of Punishment seems to be purely grounded in the geopolitical & social situation of Modern Nights - where the Garou are never more than a dance away from the Apocalypse.

                    It seems only fair to point out that, back in the Dark Ages and comparable eras (When Garou and Gaia were doing better) Rites like The Hunt were probably created because there were far fewer chances to send a Garou to perish in honourable combat against Creatures of the Wyrm - if only because creatures of the Wyrm potent enough to fell a Garou would have almost certainly been targeted by local packs, leaving few other possibilities for an honourable demise that did not involve some risk of breaching the Veil.

                    It also seems reasonable to point out that The Rite of the Omega is evidence in favour of the notion that while ‘Right makes Might’ in Garou society (with a particular emphasis on individual might), there have always been means by which the over-mighty can be taken down a peg or two by collective action, methods fully-approved of by Gaia and the Spirit World, even.

                    Also, I find it difficult to agree that the Stone of Scorn is wholly pointless or disproportionate when our own reality obliges us to daily endure wealthy, powerful, prestigious individuals who really, truly and richly deserve it.

                    If nothing else, it bears considering that this Rite was clearly out together for the benefit of the community, not the condemned individual - being allowed to sit around and just talk smack about some malefactor for hours at a time with your friends, colleagues & family must be extremely therapeutic, even (or perhaps especially) in the Age of the Apocalypse, though one trusts it would be a fairly rare luxury by this point.

                    Anyway, doubtless you’ll be able to conjure up counterpoints, but it bears pointing out that the Garou National is built not on institutional, but on interpersonal politics for the excellent reason that (with perhaps no more than 11,000 Garou in the world as of AD 1992) there would be be a very real difficulty in constructing a system of impersonal justice when just about every single party in every single case brought before a sept’s elders would involve persons who have either known each other all their days as a Garou or have quite literally known each other all their days and will almost certainly have worked, fought and hunted together).

                    That’s a way of life arguably more alien to our lived experience than any Honour culture (Since there’s a very strong case to be made that ‘cancelling’ and similar institutions of the Digital Age are merely the latest manifestations of very ancient impulses).


                    Black Fox, I also wanted to push back a little against your disbelief in the notion that the Spirit World would pay much mind to the average Garou - given the rarity and raw power of even a fairly young, untested Garou (and their nature as a melding of spirit & flesh) it makes perfect sense that they’d draw the attention of local spirits the way Classical tragedies produced a Greek Chorus (and for almost exactly the same reasons, Garou being in many ways Heroes from a Mythic Age fighting on long after their age went up in flames).

Putting out a whole supplement on money and magic can prompt people to reconsider if they want to have this come up more in their games and how just because it is suddenly a topic worthy of a whole book.

                                                Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                                                I also wanted to push back a little against your disbelief in the notion that the Spirit World would pay much mind to the average Garou - given the rarity and raw power of even a fairly young, untested Garou (and their nature as a melding of spirit & flesh) it makes perfect sense that they’d draw the attention of local spirits the way Classical tragedies produced a Greek Chorus (and for almost exactly the same reasons, Garou being in many ways Heroes from a Mythic Age fighting on long after their age went up in flames).
                                                No, I heavily disagree. It doesn't work that from a mechanical perspective, and it would be bad from a narrative perspective.

                                                As I had previously said, it's OK if some spirits observe some things and that information is gossiped around. The occasional spirit is going to observe something at some point. However, spirits are neither omniscient nor omnipresent (either individually or as a collective), and that is what they would need to be in order to act as some kind of objective automatic renown removal and scandal note taker.

                                                We know several things from the setting. The first is that in order for spirits to peek from the Umbra into the Realm, they would either need to Peek (which means the spirits are not able to observe the Umbra around them) or need a Charm like Realm Sense (or perhaps Materialize). But not all spirits have Realm Sense, and those who do are limited to only that area of the realm to which that spirit is intrinsically linked. Furthermore, that Charm is not on all the time and requires a Gnosis roll to activate. Simply put, spirits are not constantly observing the Realm. And when they do observe the Realm, it's probably not for the purpose of observing Garou. Spirits in fact have other things to do than just observe Garou. Not even pack totems work that way. Even a powerful pack totem is just one spirit who can't shadow every single pack member in the Umbra if the pack members split up. Being able to instantly communicate with them or find them is not the same as knowing as what every single member is doing.

                                                Second, in the modern day setting we know the Umbra is severely depopulated compared to how it was in the oldest days. There are vast swaths of the Penumbra that are basically denuded of spirits, or just have enemy spirits. Only at caerns and domains like glens are there probably spirits who could potentially be looking in the Realm and observe what a Garou there is doing. It's not even clear if spirits would know all the things Garou have done in the Umbra itself, much less the Realm. Even in places like caerns where we can imagine there are lots of spirits present, and that some of them will be viewing the activities and discussions of the Garou, that one of the spirits will always be able to track every single Garou and know what is going on. Even in parties with dozens of people, it is possible in real life for two or more attendees to go off and have their own private discussion or activity unknown to the rest of the party goers.

                                                Third, there are Gifts, Rites, and Fetishes whose entire purpose is to conceal what goes on from spirits or banish them outright.

                                                From a narrative perspective, this idea is awful. An ST could never tell a story about betrayal, crimes, violation of the Litany, and so on because all Garou are constantly being snitched on. And things that are subjective suddenly become objective. It makes for a bad setting. Let's use Rage Across New York as an example.

                                                Is Mother Larissa in the Sept of the Green such a bad sept leader that she is constantly losing Renown as the spirits tell everyone how she doesn't run regular moots, isn't controlling man eating by Bone Juice (whose crimes of course would be constantly reported upon), making deals with the Sabbat, and otherwise acting like a Bone Gnawer? Or because she runs what is an essentially Bone Gnawer sept, is this OK and her tribe thinks she's doing a great job even if Garou like Zachary Ellison thinks differently? For that matter, how exactly can Ellison attempt to orchestrate a coup and seize control of the caern if the spirits are constantly snitching on him? How can Bone Juice be secretly organizing a group of man eaters? How come nobody knows that Arn Guth Stormbright and Bladetooth are the parents of Aeschylus/Pink Tom? Why isn't Jacob Morningkill being hit with massive Renown loss as he tyrannizes his tribe and unjustly exiles his heir?

                                                For that matter, how can any Garou possibly become a Black Spiral Dancer if the spirits are constantly observing Garou and can automatically warn everyone else? In fact, why aren't the Black Spiral Dancers and their plans constantly being monitored and exposed? Or the plots of any other Wyrm minions? In Rite of Passage, the Winter Wolf Sept should not just know that their cubs have been killed in New York City, but also know that Black Spiral Dancers did it and that the Garou at the Sept of the Green are innocent. That would invalidate the entire scenario.

                                                The setting and the game would lose a lot of mystery, intrigue, suspense, secrets, and other dramatic elements that make it fun and interesting.

                                                The potential for spirits observing what a Garou is doing is present. There's no problem for an ST to decide that something was, in fact, observed, or even that some particular spirit is indeed spying on one or more characters (either because it has individual motivation to do so, or sent by some Theurge, other Garou, or even by a non-Garou). But 1) that should be decided in advance, and 2) smart characters should be able to counter that spying if they so choose.

                                                The desire for "fairness" is understandable and necessary from a player perspective, but there's no reason to try to force it into an in character perspective by suddenly having the spirit world act in ways completely different from what we've previously been told.
