            Originally posted by etherial View Post
            There is a difference between a surgeon cutting your leg off with a saw and a running it through a meat grinder. It's not pain I object to. It's torture.
            The idea of pain = healing is as old as medicine itself. I am not sure if you are rejecting the idea of Erebus because it is too harsh in degree or because you think it should be spiritually unnecessary, but both seem to be misconceptions if we are reading the same books (not being smarmy, lots of my stuff is outdated).

            Werewolves are not squeamish about pain. Their rage drives them into situations where injury and agony are unavoidable. Physical suffering is a constant of life in the Garou Nation. Erebus is merely an extreme extension of what the Garou already know. Werewolves do not shy away from violence, they respond to it, react to it. If a Garou is in Erebus, there are not there to suffer mindlessly. The injuries they suffer force them to confront their own shortcomings. Spiritual corruption is real and deadly in WtA. Erebus is brutal and unpleasant, but it does serve a purpose. Hell, to use your example, perhaps running around in a meatgrinder may in fact be beneficial- a crazed, Wyld-tainted werewolf is getting their mutating in-flux body shredded over and over again. When all the warped flesh is carved off the Garou can regain a measure of sanity, and only when he regenerates his wounds without manifesting any mutations will he be free of the taint.

                Originally posted by wonderandawe View Post
                I don't think the beliefs and the like get cut off. I see Erebus as extreme therapy with visions of your actions and the effects of your actions. Being dipped in molten silver and having the mistakes of your life and the consequences of your actions flash before your eyes will change your viewpoint....eventually. I see it something like this:




                Everywhere your body touches silver, you burn. You tread trying to keep your head above the surface. Your claws attempt to find purchase on the shore, but the molten silver makes the rock slick. You loose your grip and slide beneath the surface.

                You are back in your old childhood room. You are in your small twin bed with your lover. Your fellow cub. All senses, taste, smell, touch are of her.

                You struggle to breathe. You open your mouth to scream. Silver pours down your throat and sears your lungs.

                You walk the sept with the blessing of Falcon upon you. Your mother wishes you to attend the trial. Guilt keeps you away at first. Fear of discovery forces you to the trial. Your lover stands in a circle of Elders, a metis pup in her arms. The Elders words blow past you meaningless. You stare at the girl, still a cub. Her own rite of passage delayed until the trail's end. You pray to Gaia she doesn't speak your name. She says nothing. She does not even look at you.

                There are moments of relief where you break surface. Air, not silver touches your burned skin. Then a wave crashes over you. You are drawn into the depths again.

                You stare down at the body of your lover. Dead, killed by the Black Spiral Dancers. The pup mourns his mother's death, but all you feel is relief. You hear the elders whisper about the orphan's cub's future. The old shame returns and for a moment you consider speaking. "This is my son, I will care for him." you consider saying. Then a Galliard comes and begs you to allow him to sing of your battle prowess at the moot. You turn away from the elders and the pup and follow the Galliard to Glory.

                You awaken and find yourself clinging to a rock. The silver waves lap at your feet. The tide rises and soon you will burn again.

                A Child of Gaia pack wishes to join your sept. You are down warriors and have no reason to turn them away. Except the metis, a pup no longer, is with them. You had not thought about the metis pup in years. As your homid and whole children make pleasantries, you stare at the metis. His misshapen body conceals any resemblance to you. To your surprise, he stares back defiantly. He knows who I am, you realize. Suddenly, the secrets you thought safe in your lover's death is not so safe anymore.

                You cough up silver and blood. You gasp for air. You find a pattern to this madness. You break surface when you are about to suffocate. You barely get enough air to survive before being pulled back down again.

                Arranging the metis' death was easy. Tragedy stalks the Garou at each step. You simply make a rare tactical error. Send the Child of Gaia pack into death. The Bawn will be cleansed of taint. When news of the pack's death came back to you, you thought you could rest easy. The simple mistake made in youth has been erased. Your position as Sept Alpha was safe. Yet when you sought your bed, whispers of death and decay haunted your dreams. Tempted you to more power.

                With your rare breath, you beg for mercy. You did what you did to protect your children. Sacrifice one misshapen pup for the rest. Yet more memories flashed. The hunger for power. The fall of your sept. The dance for even more power. Your children chanting the Rite of the Silver Forge.

                If it wasn't for him, you would not have been tempted. You would not have fallen.... Your crime was you should have killed him sooner.

                The silver is silent and without mercy. It chokes out your excuses and drags you back down again.


                ((It will be interesting to see what everyone thinks the Silver Fang's true Crimes are. ))
                This is almost exactly how my character's erebus trip went, nice! (Read it here, please note it was not the most uptightly played of games ^^'').

                So yes, this is good Revised era writing of the healing of Erebus.

                My gallery.

                            Originally posted by etherial

                            There's a subtle, but relevant, difference between torture and an ordeal. Torture it's meant to break you, to make you do things you don't want to. An ordeal it's meant to be endured, it's a test. If Erebus it's a place of torture, it isn't very good at it's job, as it's unable to change you if you won't regret your actions in the first place (proved by the BSD that spend millenia there). If it's a place of punish, then it's quite forgiving, you can get out if you're sorry (not all versions of Hell give you a second chance). If it's an ordeal...yes, it's a very fearsome ordeal, it tests you with horrible pain, and if you fail you get to suffer forever.

                            And what's more important, it works. Taint on a level that's beyond magical healing and sin goes away. If you're a walking wyrm fueled cancerous tumor in constant pain that wants to rape everything in sight, but have earned a moment of clarity and regret, you may want to go there to burn your misery away. Sure, it's scary, but it may beat the alternative.

                            Werewolf it's the stuff of might, of epicness, it makes sense for them to have a place where they can purify at the cost of immense suffering. If Erebus was a nice place of healing, going there and returning renewed wouldn't be so badass.
