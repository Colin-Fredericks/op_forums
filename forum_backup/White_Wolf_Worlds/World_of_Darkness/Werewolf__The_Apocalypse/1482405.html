    Thoughts about The Bloody Man

    05-30-2022, 05:35 PM
    This legend is fascinating, to think that Vampirism's born from the weaver and corrupted by the wyrm's blood
    It seems that the weaver power gave vampires their immortality and Disciplines. The Wyrm's blood seems to be the beast which devour the vampire soul and make him fall to savagery.

    So many questions about the lore could be asked, like if the weaver gift another being without wyrms interventions, could that create a uncorrupted vampirism? The ananasi's use blood pool and are direct creations of the weaver after all
    Could a fera be gifted that way and become full hybrid?
    What would be a gift from the wyld itself or the wyrm ?
    If a vampire get to golconda is he "free" from the wyrm influence and obtain true vampirism?

    What do you think?

                  The Bloody Man was someone's attempt at creating an in-setting legend of the Garou to account for vampires. But I don't think it's a very good one so I don't use it.

                  The Triat are supposed to be several degrees removed from the fiddly bits of creation. They are anthropomorphized entities of metaphysical principles. I would have preferred a legend/myth that attributed it to one of the Bane Incarnas (and perhaps certain patronizing or curses by the Celestines) and left any reference to the Triat to the speculations of Garou theologians and lorekeepers. So in the chronicles I ST I replaced this with a legend I like better for the Garou (and of course, certain Garou occult experts have their own ideas that don't align with the popular myth known to most Garou).

                  The advantage of the Garou perspective is that they are a third party with advanced understanding of the Umbra and its metaphysics. The disadvantage is that the Garou have their own biases and vastly incomplete knowledge on vampires. Ideally the Garou should have some kind of insight, but be wildly off because of their own ignorance.
