                                        Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                        In the end how we all feel bout W5's RPG books basically isn't going to matter to Paradox.                                         And if what you said is accurate, then they're even More short sighted. The reason to buy an established IP in the first place is so that you A.) have a product already tested to have potential for popularity(less risk of investing in a new product that flops) and B.) have an initial contingent of fans capable of causing audience takeoff(it's well know most people don't try to invest in something new unless they see other people invested in it, so having an existing fanbase increases a product's ability to hit market appeal numbers "quickly enough" increases geometrically proportional to the initial fanbase).

                                        Changing core aspects of the IP to that piss off existing fans destroys both those positives, gives you nearly the same risks as creating a new IP from scratch, and even creates a Negative feedback loop of bad advertising thanks to disgruntled fans. This means the company spent an initial investment of money on an IP only to need to spend More money on advertising and marketing than if they made a new IP from scratch. It's not unrecoverable by any means, but it Is a complete failure of management and waste of investment.

                                                    Overall I find that 5th edition's rather juvenile idea of personal horror is really just... how to say this... I feel like all the splats have become allegory for neurodivergence?
