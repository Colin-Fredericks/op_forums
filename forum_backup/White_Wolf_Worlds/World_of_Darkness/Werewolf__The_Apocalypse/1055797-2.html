                            God, I hadn't thought about Werewolf in forever. I'm getting nostalgic now. As far as these political/social changes, a lot of them worry me. It's not that I necessarily dislike all the ideas in theory, but more that the way they've been framed here simplifies things in a manner that doesn't really appeal to me. Granted, I haven't actually read the book yet, so I could be misinterpreting. From the sound of things, a lot of garou have been died, and traveling between caerns is a lot more difficult, so I'd imagine we'd see a more fractured political structure with a younger guard making decisions and regional decisions taking precedence over tribe-wide decisions. But for instance...

                            I like the idea of more inclusive Black Furies, but only if there's still conflict within that idea instead of just "Well, we're accepting men now". It strikes me as a good reflection of where feminism is today. With resources drawn thinner, there'd probably be more arguments about how the specific interests of women of color and other marginalized groups are prioritized in comparison to "universal" issues (that have a tendency to be more white people problems). And I can't doubt queerness would be a part of that discussion. Where do non-binary and trans people fit into the BF perspective on femininity? And if they're allowing men into the tribe, what are their criteria, and is their hierarchy? Feminism today is a very split and complicated philosophy, and I think that makes interesting fodder for the tribe.

                            Similarly the Get of Fenris. With a lot of discussions about "heritage not hate" and the increased prominence of nationalism, ethnic supremacy, and alpha male nostalgia, I could see a resurgence of traditionalism for the reformers in the tribe to deal with.

                            I'll admit I'm not a big fan of the "Walkers are team Weaver" bit or the "Camarilla/Sabbat" split (as Loki said). It doesn't seem practical to me to have two camps split across these clean delineations. A two party system makes sense for Vampire, but it's much less so for Werewolf. I'd look at the splintering among leftists as a better parallel: where you have everything from classical liberals to MLMs to tankies and varying degrees of respect and disgust between them depending on where their ideologies lie. Given the other changes they seem to be making to the setting, I think a regional rather than top-down approach to how politics works makes a lot more sense.

                                                I think part of the problem is that "minority" spaces aren't meant to be 100% exclusive. They're mean to be places where minorities don't have to feel like minorities.

                                                Allowing members of the majority in isn't a "violation" of that space as long as that member of the majority respects that space.

                                                For example, there's a Christian member of my synagogue. She's not converting, or anything like that. She's not their to appropriate Jewish culture in some weird fashion. She's there because she wanted to learn, and stayed part of the community after. She enjoys participating on the level she can without being disrespectful. She insist on Christian things being brought in, or accommodation made for her beliefs.

                                                The Black Furies having men in their ranks is acknowledging the same ability for allies to be in minority spaces by earning their status as allies. Male Black Furies are raised or taught how to fight for equality and respect how to be allies instead of white-knights.
