    Estimating garou population percentages

    12-11-2025, 10:26 AM
    As my old correspondent Black Fox has noted at various points, the classic ‘1 Garou for every 500,000 Humans’ ratio is rather flawed by the fact that it does not account for the fact that Garou tend to prefer places with fewer people and more quiet spaces (Amongst other things).

    Being recently reminded of this on a VAMPIRE: THE MASQUERADE thread, it occurred to me that for the sake of forming more accurate off-the-cuff estimates it would make sense to add a modifier to the 1: 500,000 ratio, revising the numbers up and down based on the population density of a given area (For example, if the human population density per square mile enters double figures subtract a percentage from estimates of the local Garou population, but a single-figure population density adds a percentage?).

    The problem is that I’m not very good at arithmetic and would greatly appreciate advice on the most sensible way to calculate this modifier.

    Please note that this modifier would be intended to make quick estimates a bit more accurate, not produce in-depth calculations of the werewolf population (Also, I make no mention of wolf populations simply because human population figures are always easier to find than those of wolves).

          The books have never really pushed the idea that there's a Garou:Human ratio like VtM did. WtA has always - as Saur Ops points to - gone for a more narrative metric.

          As well, the idea that Garou gravitate towards low population density isn't really true. The two largest Gaian Tribes (how many BSDs there are is an entirely different issue) are the ones most comfortable living in high population density areas, while the two smallest Tribes avoid said areas for entirely different reasons. The majority of the Tribes also consider specific ethnic clusters to be good for their Pure Breed through a high number of generations of drawing their Kin from those human groups. American Fianna are generally presented as more urban-friendly then their counterparts in Ireland, the UK, and mainland Europe, because most of their human Kin ended up settling in higher population density areas when arriving in the US in large numbers.

          Population density itself is also a tricky number to work with because how you measure it is going to skew your results heavily. If you do it by country, Ireland is twice as population dense as the US. But Dublin is between Boston and Miami in terms of population density on a finer scale. Denmark's population density varies drastically if you include Greenland or not (cutting Alaska from the US would has a less drastic but still noticeable change of a similar nature).

          Ultimately, to make this approximation reflective enough of WtA's setting, you'd need to decide on the average Garou preference regarding how evenly a population is distributed in a given area, not just it's density, because humans do not spread out like a gas in a confined space. It could be more helpful to consider metrics of the area's proportion of urban-to-rural populations rather than density itself because that's probably more useful. A high population density area, but that population is packed into ~10% of the Garou's "territory" would provide them plenty of areas to be away from humans when needed without losing access to humans when that's desired; especially compared to an area where the same population density is spread out more evenly.

                  Jumping off of what Heavy Arms was saying, that does seem to suggest that there’s some interesting tensions going on in where Garou live and their population numbers.

                  Garou are generally associated with wildernesses, particularly true wildernesses (ideologically loaded terms I know, but work with me), which in North America, compared with even 1,00 years ago, much less 200 or more years ago would be significantly larger. This often gets invoked to discuss the significance of wolf populations, which in North America has been largely reduced to the less developed areas of northern Canada, Alaska, and particularly rugged or intentionally preserved sections of the Western United States.

                  The idea that such places with significantly vast wilderness areas, especially if that includes an active wolf population, would see a relatively high concentration of Garou and Caerns of significant power.

                  But then you add the wrinkle that the overwhelming majority of contemporary Garou are said to be human born and Heavy Arm’s observation about the largest Gaian tribes (the Bone Gnawers and Children of Gaia iirc) and their tolerance or even penchant for urban environments. Plus the practical arithmetic that if most Garou are human-born, it would make sense if most were born in areas closer to where the most people live, urban areas and the surrounding suburban penumbras and extensions.

                  That would seem to suggest to me that for those rural areas, where presumably the most dedicated and active (and traditional) garou are, they either reproduce via maintaining relatively small but stable populations of kinfolk within some proximity to those wilderness septs or they semi-regularly send out bachelor/casanova wolves to areas of higher human population density in order to ‘sow wild oats’ and eventually reintegrate any successful products of that process back into the Nation.

                  An idea I wonder about related to this is what proportion of the BG/CoG membership should we imagine as immediate descendants of those more traditional and rural septs who for whatever reason reject or are rejected by their septs and thus end up joining the more cosmopolitan tribes and how many are garou born into those tribes and their surrounding base of kinfolk. One could probably ask a similar question about the kinfolk of those tribes, how many of them are either direct or indirect descendants of kinfolk leaving behind more traditional septs and how recently.

                  I think that could be an interesting dynamic to play with: emphasizing that these deeply rural septs are more likely to be traditional and high-prestiege (within the reward system of the Nation) and probably gather highly active and driven Garou true believers because of that but are also at risk of being somewhat brittle or fragile in a sense compared to the larger and more cosmopolitan septs in proximity to large concentrations of humanity but which might host significant numbers of “Easter-and-Christmas” garou who aren’t interested in dedicating themselves 24/7 to the Cause (which the Bone Gnawers have certainly been accused of in different places).

                  Anyway, this was a bit stream of consciousness, but I hope there are some thoughts in it others might find useful or interesting.

                      We have to start out understanding some bullet-points about Garou population:
                      • They don't depend on humans for sustenance or any other vital resource, so while humans may help or hinder their goals, there's no ecological pressure like there is with vampires;
                      • Garou can be born from any human or wolf, with Kinfolk being the main sources, but not the only ones;
                      • Kinfolk don't suffer any population pressure from normal humans, either, they may exist in any number among their respective populations, up to and including everyone if you want;
                      • At the end of the day this is a spiritual process. Deus Ex Machina may interfere to make them be born more or less frequently for whatever reasons;
                      • More important to the setting than their ratio to humans is their ratio to work. It may well be that Garou are being born in lower and lower numbers even as human population grows;
                      • Population fluctuates, and for Garou more than any other splat. With the highest friction among them all and numbers that replenish constantly, their numbers and members can change drastically from a year to the next.

                      At the end of the day I think the whole idea of giving hard numbers to the whole state/area is foolish.

                      Jot down the main Caerns in an area and main sources of problems, have an idea of how many Garou would be necessary at each main sect for it to deal comfortably with their issues, and give then less. How much less sets the tone for the setting, which is what really matter. Then just describe some of those main sects as a snapshot in time and that's that, don't bother with numbers outside them.
