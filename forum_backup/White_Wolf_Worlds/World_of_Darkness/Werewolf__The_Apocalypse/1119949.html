    Fera x Garou couples against the Litany?

    07-24-2017, 01:43 PM
    I don't expect certain fera species or breeds to have any romantic interest, or even be genetically compatible, with werewolves, but here's a plausible example. If a female Nuwisha gets a wild hair and seduces a male werewolf, has the werewolf broken the Litany? If she gets pregnant by him, would her cub have any chance of being metis, or would it be a normal Nuwisha, after the breed of the mother? If the cub is born metis, would it necessarily get the ragabash auspice, regardless of the moon phase of its birthday? Would its lupus/latrani form look like a coywolf rather than a pure coyote or wolf?

    Before you reply that this can't or wouldn't ever happen, remember the historical precedent of the Bunyip, who transitioned their animal kinfolk to thylacine wolves.

      It's not explicitly against the Litany, though that doesn't mean such a coupling wouldn't get punished as such. The Garou interpret the Litany all the time, and there's nothing stopping some portion of them considering the First Tenet to be about ensuring mating with humans and wolves. Thus this would be a violation of the spirit of the law even if not the letter of it. So it's up to whatever the local Philodox and Ragabash debate it as.

      Any child wouldn't be a Metis, it would be Nuwisha or Nuwisha kin, as per the rules to follow the mother's lineage. It could still be a good explanation for abnormal traits like a hybrid appearance in latrani form, or Merits/Flaws to represent some of that Garou blood.

      The Bunyip also managed to transition their animal kin via Rites, not just breeding.

                  Is it breaking the Litany? - Nope. The Litany says "Garou Shall Not Mate With Garou." A Nuwisha is not a Garou. HOWEVER, if the Nuwisha is posing as a Garou, her lover and the rest of his pack and sept might very well believe it to be a violation,m and punish him accordingly. strictly speaking though, it's not violation.
                  Would the child be metis? - Nope. Metis only come from certain changing breeds making children with their own kind. Nuwisha and Garou are different changing breeds, and Nuwisha do not produce metis anyway. Assuming the mother is homid, the offsping has a 10% chanve to be Garou, a 10% chance to be Nuwisha, and an 80% chance to be kinfolk to either or both. If the mother is Latrani it has a 10% chacne to be Nuwisha and a 90% chance to be a nuwisha kin coyote.
                  Would its lupus/latrani form look like a coywolf rather than a pure coyote or wolf? - A purely cosmetic question, the answer is "Eh, why not, go ahead." After all, that merit that the Nuwisha get that makes 'em Garou sized has to come from somewhere.

                      I do not recall any published material that discusses this so I think every ST will interpret it differently. One of the reasons Garou exist is to serve as a bridge between the animal world and man. This is why Garou are supposed to mate with kinfolk of either side and not amongst themselves. Metis are just a sign of Gaia's disfavor. Mating with another Changing Breed is not only the same kind of violation, but it also violates the idea that each of the Changing Breeds exists for its own purpose.

                      So in my Chronicles, this would be taboo too. This is how I would run it.

                      It violates the Litany.

                      Such a union would prove to be infertile. At best, miscarriage would happen early on of some deformed monstrosity. Breeding with kinfolk of other Fera would result in a higher chance of producing Garou than humans, but not as high as mating with kinfolk of the proper Changing Breed. Now when it comes to the Nuwisha, it may seem a little different because Coyotes are also canines that can interbreed with wolves. However, the Nuwisha do not have metis at all by which I mean offspring. In fact, it seems the Nuwisha simply do not breed with each other, or if they do they simply never have children - they are infertile and incapable of doing it. So if Garou breed with them, the infertility angle still holds.

                      Also, I would be very careful about being overly semantic when it comes to the Litany. The Litany is NOT a simple code of statements like the Traditions in Vampire. It is a vast epic poem and oral lore of law that requires a lifetime of study to master. The Litany of thirteen points shown in the books is ONLY a list of common precepts so that PCs have some guidance, not the actual law itself. And as the Litany itself is actually composed in the Garou Tongue, there can be lots of arguments of what the appropriate translation is into English or other human vernacular, or every connotation the word actually has. And each tribe and each sept has its own variations of it. So while I would understand why a tribe like the Bone Gnawers might not have a problem with Garou/Fera mating, I am also sure most other tribes - especially Fianna and Silver Fangs - would consider it to be a violation.

                      Also, I do not see the analogy between the Bunyip and their animal kinfolk/form and two of the Changing Breeds mating. In canon, there was some pretty wild and might magic being performed back then. There isn't in this scenario. (Personally, I have always found the idea of Bunyip as Garou to be nonsensical. I have no problem of them being a Changing Breed, but it has always strained credibility in my eyes that they were a tribe of Garou. If the Nuwisha aren't Garou, then why would marsupials be?)

One of my old PCs was a Philodox whose concept was someone wanting to become an expert on the Litany and traveled extensively to learn all of its variations and rules, so this is something I as a player studied so I could work it out with my ST.                                       Originally posted by The Cat Came Back View Post
                                      The tenets of the litany are what they are. They're the same for every tribe, even the far-flung ones like Bunyip and the Pure Ones.
                                      The corebooks disagree with you. The thirteen main precepts are universal, but the Litany is not composed solely of them and it is explicitly mentioned many times that there are more rules.

                                      Werewolf First Edition, page 42: The following are the basic tenets of the Litany; there are many others, some varying by tribe to tribe.
                                      Werewolf Second Edition, page 33: The following are some of the basic tenets of the Litany; there are many others, often varying by sept or tribe.
                                      Werewolf Revised Edition, page 37: Most modern werewolves prefer a straightforward culture with little patience for legal subterfuge. To simplify matters, the Litany can be summarized in 13 basic precepts.
                                      Werewolf 20, page 50: In its full form, it is as much an epic poem as a legal code. Chanting it in its entirety can take hours.
                                      The block of text explaining the Litany is generally the same across all editions with minor changes, but it is consistent. The Litany is a long oral poem that takes many hours to recite. It contains many codes, laws, and traditions. It is best know by its thirteen common precepts, but there are many more, and each sept and tribe has its own variations. The thirteen precepts are merely a simplification to aid all Garou (and us the players).


                                      Originally posted by The Cat Came Back View Post
                                      They're the same in 2017 that they were in 1207, which was the same as they were in 5 BCE, and i imagine if we go back further they're still the same.
                                      Again this is wrong. We know there is one specific example where the Litany did change in history. And that is the rule "Ye Shall Not Eat the Flesh of Humans". Multiple editions make it clear that this was added to the Litany only after the Concord that ended the Impergium. Before that time, it was not part of the Litany.

                                      Werewolf Second Edition, page 34: This portion of the Litany was first sung in the post-Impergium days; the Stargazers are believed to be responsible for its insertion.
                                      Werewolf Revised Edition, page 39: The Law: This portion of the Litany was first sung shortly after the Concord.
                                      Werewolf 20, page 51: Not long after the Concord, Stargazer mystics noticed that that many werewolves took a bit too much pleasure in devouring human flesh.
                                      It is clear that this law was introduced to the Litany in a historical event. People know when it was added and who argued for it. So we know without a doubt that there can be changes to the Litany. Furthermore, while we can assume that for the most part the basic thirteen tenets of the Litany are extremely old (and even this portion of the Litany is around ten thousand years old) and not likely to change, there might be very well be lesser portions of the Litany which are much newer and perhaps even added recently.

                                      While most Tribes are likely very traditional and prone to not revising, changing, or adding to the Litany, this certainly does not apply to the Glass Walkers. And as human society changed radically in the last several centuries creating far greater challenges for the Garou and presenting challenges that simply could not be expected by the Garou's ancestors, some of these Glass Walker innovations created in response are probably slowly creeping into the other tribes once their benefit becomes self-evident. Not to the point of revising the thirteen basic rules, but certainly addressing certain things. Possibly at some point, these new rules will become codified as a minor or even major precept during a Concolation or Grand Moot.

                                      Originally posted by The Cat Came Back View Post
                                      The Garou are not a hugely complicated or legalistic people, their body of law really is just these thirteen rules.
                                      The first part of this sentence is true, but not the second part. Garou oral law is certainly not like that of modern states with shelves of books codifying the law and adding more every year from legislators. It is custom handed down. But even custom can change over time.

                                      We have plenty of examples of what there oral law codes would look like on a general basis because such common law systems of illiterate barbarian peoples are well known. The best well known are the various Germanic and Scandinavian systems as they were recorded by literate witnesses and slowly converted to a Civil Law system. Examples include the Lawspeakers who were there to orally recite the legal code at political and judicial gatherings. By modern standards such codes are primitive and bare, but they still required actual experts who could memorize them and then let the people know what their law actually said.

                                      Besides giving precedents and examples of correct interpretations of the law, the Litany probably covers lots of things that Players and STs usually don't care about - how Garou can adopt one another, how inheritance works, how new Silver Fang houses can be created, appropriate chiminage needed to end blood feuds and re-establish peace when doing the Rite of Contrition, what offenses are to be met with the appropriate Rites of Punishment, jurisdiction issues between septs, and laws governing relations with kinfolk.

                                      The corebook states that the Law ability can be used in regards to the Litany and even allows a specialization in it. If the Litany was just 13 rules, this seems like overkill. But it's not, it is an entire corpus of law. It is simple compared to modern state systems, but complex enough that experts are occasionally needed. In sections describing the Litany we hear again and again that it is possible to use various "intricacies" of the law to protect one's self.

                                      Werewolf Second Edition, page 33: The Fianna are the true masters of the Litany, and even their Ragabashes know most of its intricacies – ideal for extricating themselves from various situations.
                                      Werewolf Revised Edition, page 37: Masters of Garou law can cite dozens of examples of precedent, but as fewer cubs leam to chant the details, more argue ways to bend the rules in their favor.
                                      At the same time, we know the Garou are not patient with legal obfuscation as you mentioned. They may like a smart argument or use of precedent, but not overly "clever" or "technical" defenses that clearly go against the spirit of the law. And Garou judgments certainly won't last more than one or two night's discussion at the most. The long drawn out multiple proceedings of modern US legal system certainly won't happen. But there is enough complexity for the Garou to use legal experts.

                                      Werewolf Revised Edition, page 74: Members of Stag's tribe are often called to be judges, for their memories are long and they arts familiar with Litany precedent as few others are.
                                      Werewolf Revised Edition, page 82: Because {the Shadow Lords} are so enthralled by the politics of the Garou Nation, they consider themselves guardians of the Litany, or at least the "correct" interpretation of it.
                                      Most matters of the Litany will be easy to adjudicate. But at some points in time it seems the Garou do go to legal experts so they can roll their traits in Law 5 with specialty in the Litany.

                                      Originally posted by The Cat Came Back View Post
                                      What you get after that is a body of lore about how various tribes, septs, and individual garou have seen and interpreted the Litany. These aren't changes to the Litany, they're not secret rules. They're just a body of thought and practice on the subject by Garou. Think like how the Talmud relates to the Tanakh, or how Tu Yu and Meng Shih relate to "The Art of War"
                                      Except they aren't just commentary on 13 basic laws. You have it backwards. The 13 precepts come out of the Litany; they are just simplifications and summaries. The long form of the Litany is not commentary on 13 rules. The Litany does change occasionally (probably as a result of Grand Moots and Concolations) as we know from the prohibition of eating human flesh. The Litany does include other laws we don't see in the 13 precepts. They may not be as important as them (and certainly not as interesting to PCs and STs), but they do exist. And tribes and septs do have their own version with additional rules just for them.

                                      Where there are gaps in the Litany, it is completely appropriate for STs to fill in their own answers. And different STs can have different ideas. Some ideas may be better thought out than others, but each ST determines the reality of their own game world. But the idea that the Litany is JUST those thirteen phrases and nothing more is simply not canon.

                                              For the needs of the game, the basic thirteen rules are more than enough to handle almost everything that comes up in game play. So for all practical purposes, they are the only laws PCs need to worry about. Most STs and PCs simply won't be interested in matters outside those points. Nobody wants to spend time in game going over trivial matters like the equivalent of Garou parking tickets or even probate. (Sometimes weird things do come up, and often it is best if the ST simply rules "this is how it is handled" and moves on - this allows them to do that.)

                                              Anything else would either be 1) flavor text or 2) an ST contrived plot point. About the only time I could see it meaningfully impacting most kind of games is if the ST makes it clear some gray area of the law is being used to screw over someone the PCs like (or sabotage an action/strategy/decision supported by the PCs), and arguing in front of a sept or moot would require rolling a PC's Law ability to convince enough people that the Litany is OK with this. An extended challenge contested by the NPC could be dramatic in a courtroom like scene.

                                              The real value of this to STs is that it gives them another tool to use to come up with plot. For the most part, any variations of the Litany likely comes up most often when it comes disputes between septs, especially when each sept is of one tribe as there is a very strong tradition of what the Litany is. The actions of members of one sept is totally supported by their understanding of the Litany, but the other sept with a slightly different understanding disagrees. Thus both sides feel justified in an ongoing feud. Or let's say there is a conundrum these septs want to get out of, and they are looking for an excuse to finally end this conflict. Who they do go to? Do they ask the nearby Silver Fang king to mediate? Do they send a delegation to the Fianna and ask them to send a respected Philodox so he can make a ruling? What happens if one side appeals to a Fianna to make the decision, but the other asks intervention from the Shadow Lords? Are the sides asking for arbitration, mediation, or simply face saving? Garou don't have a sophisticated court system so there is no appealate or supreme court to decide these things, but sometimes experts are brought in. Or if the PCs really piss off one sept, they can flee to another sept whose opinions match their own. Or they are following a "criminal" Garou to punish him and find him safe at some other sept which tells them the guy they're after is OK so piss off. It is also a good way to distinguish septs from each other. They all have the same basic law, but obviously Silver Fang septs are different from other tribes which are different from each other, particularly the Urrah tribes.

                                              As an ST, I like tools that help me generate meaningful conflict within the system. This is one of them.
