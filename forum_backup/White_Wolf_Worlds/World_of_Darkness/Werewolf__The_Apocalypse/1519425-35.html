            We arrive at a cemetery within Jacksonville. We are there for Jacob’s Mom’s funeral. There is an older woman who lets us in and takes our names. There is a man who comes up to us, wearing a scarf, which Jacob notes is odd – the man is his brother, Saul. They were fighting last they spoke. Underneath the scarf is a large scar on his neck, as if a large animal attacked him. The funeral itself is small; not many have shown up beyond Jacob’s close family, such as his brother, Saul, and their Grandmother, Alice. Gran suggests Jacob gets ‘things in order’ as they talk. Saul also seems thinner, but doesn’t seem to recall when he last ate. Jacob asks Owen and Cassie to investigate how his mother died and who did this to her, then the three head out to lunch. Cassie has a Spiritual Companion, Panther. She can communicate mentally with her so long as Cassie stays on the Physical Realm. Jacob’s mother is named Sylvia. She died in a car accident or other some such car wreck. Cassie goes to sus out the security footage, and the computer crashes. The security guard recommends seeking out her Insurance Company; Oliver Gavins, the owner of the Company, seemed to want to find a loophole for the money to go to her family. The Insurance Company is called In The Shade, which seems to reference Thunder in its slogan. It seems to be a family-owned company; the secretary is named Jamie Gavins. Oliver is Jamie’s grandfather. As Owen and Cassie meet the CEO of the Insurance Company, he wears a nice white-gray suit and is quite tall. His office is at the end of a long corridor, which strikes us as odd. Cassie tries to pull information from Oliver, who claims that someone named Sandro Phillips attempted to embezzle funds from the company. Oliver is a Shadow Lord. While we don’t know what is up with him specifically, he asks us about whether or not we’ve ever taken human life. Cassie answers, Owen does notBack at lunch, Jacob notes that Saul still doesn’t mention the scar; however he seems to hold onto the silver cross still. Saul taps his pockets frequently enough that Jacob asks about it. Saul explains that while in Iraq he began to pray. He seems to have trouble explaining that he was hit while on duty there and it hurt him so much that he began to read the bible. Jacob learns that the scar was created not by a Vampire; but perhaps a werewolf. Then finally Saul reveals how he got the scar in Iraq. He helped a few kids get water from a well when a large man with a rune tattoo by his eye arrived. The man was aggressive and yelled at the kids but Saul tried to stop him; this caused the enraged man to attack him. Saul doesn’t know if he was high on drugs, but the man bit into his neck before running away. Jacob hacks into the security system and discovers that his mother was possessed by a spirit. Specifically, she has been possessed by a rogue Wyld spirit. We’re also warned that Hunters are all over Jacksonville. With Spindle’s help, Jacob discovers the IP of the Black Fury who edited the footage. Cassie speaks with Panther, who appears to be injured. There is a Wyld Spirit, called Haze, who pretends to be a cigarette in the physical realm; in the Umbra, a purple smoke. When smoked, Haze will possess its Host and makes them do ‘dumb shit’. Jacob searches local smoke shops and discovers a man, seemingly soaked with blood. Other customers don’t seem to notice this man. He buys a pack of cigarettes and then heads to an alleyway to smoke. The smoke appears as a deep purple and we realize that we are looking at Haze.

                                                    Or, to pick something closer to WtA as an example:

                                                            Hey guys,

                                                            I've never been as much of a werewolf guy as I've been a V:TM guy but my friend and co-author, Michael Suttkus, absolutely loves them. So, I'm going to go over both his and my thoughts on the matter from what I could tell.

                                                            1. I don't mind the vilification of the Garou Nation

                                                            I think some of the complaints aren't particularly valid. As early as my 2nd Edition Werewolf Storyteller's Guide, the dark and awful parts of the Garou Nation were a major part of the story. Albretch was doing his best to reform things but it was a place where all of the abuse of kinfolk, subtle corruption of the Wyrm, and so on was there. Garou shouldn't be "good guys." All WOD protagonists are antiheroes against worse.

                                                            I don't get the complaints about the wedidng. Garou frenzy. They kill innocent people. It's why they're monsters. Monsters fighting something worse.

                                                            2. The vilification of the Get of Fenris isn't too surprising but disappointing

                                                            I once had a conversation with a developer about how W:TF had a white supremacy problem, particularly in Europe. It was were assholes took advantage of Pure Breed, the violence, and elements in the game to advocate their own nasty views while claiming to be "just playing in character." Addressing this with things like wiping out the Swords and other groups in Revised felt heavy handed because, "I mean, are fans too dumb to realize these are the bad guys?"

                                                            Well, it turns out player comfort getting compromised and bringing RL hate to the game was a bad idea and they put RL player comfort over in-game and I don't blame them for that. Still, I think they could have done this better.

                                                            Still, if I was the writer, I would have split the Get rather than wholly corrupted them. I would have made them The Defenders of Joro (Norse Earth Goddess) and had the "evil" Get join with Red Talons and Silver Fang to make the The Pure equivalent. That way you can have your decent Get and just say, "We hate the racists. A lot of us are Slavs, gays, or other minorities."

                                                            3. I feel a lot of these changes would have been fine in-universe

                                                            The Black Furies accepting men, the Fianna being the Hart folk, and so on didn't have to be retcons and could have been described as happening as the result of reforms within the Garou. They could also have been what helped bring about the split.

                                                            4. I like redeeming BSD

                                                            I feel like the BSD being redeemable is something that adds an interesting story arc. I'm pretty sure that redeeming Fenris should be much-much easier, though, because I view the latter as a political decision but they seem to have gone full spiritual corruption.
                                                            Still, I think it makes a hell of a fun potential PC background.

                                                            5. The Umbra being off limits

                                                            I think this reminds me of what happened to Mage: The Ascension. People want gritty street level games and feel like adventuring in the Umbra detracts from that.

                                                            6. No Genetic Component

                                                            My headcanon is this is mote just an acknowledgement that the Garou being with humanity since the Impergium means everyone has a little werewolf blood in them.

                                                            7. The Fera are now all mysterious

                                                            This is just bullshit and ruins my games where the players played a lot of Fera.​
