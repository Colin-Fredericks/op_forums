                An issue the book is running into is that the writers don't really seem to have even a clear idea of what Garou-culture is like. Questions like what is happening at a Moot, how does the Galliard's duty expresses itself or what the Garou's relationship with spirits looks like... instead of exploring those things, the writing runs away from offering concrete ideas on those topics. Like this sidebar on pg 207.

                Context Matters
                Remember that animism is a real-world belief system practiced by millions of people in one form or another both throughout history and today. Be appropriately respectful at your table. Werewolf does not suppose the Garou’s specific animistic lens is the “truth” of how animism works in the game world — people’s faiths and perspectives are not to be reduced to game mechanics. The relationship between the Garou and the Umbra is but a single, fictional expression of animism.​
                Instead of actually exploring real animistic beliefs and tying them to Garou culture, this sidebar shows how the book is avoiding the topic completely. It's saying "Please be respectful of real-world animistic beliefs which we won't get into in this book and if any portrayal of animism in this book comes off as offensive... well, actually it isn't even the "truth" in the game-setting, so there you go."

                    As I noted in the Release Thoughts thread, creating a fictional form of animism that might resemble but is clearly distinct from any real world animistic belief system requires actually fully describing it in the game text. It's sort of basic "show don't tell" stuff: we need to see how the Garou practice animism in a way that's significantly different from existing real world beliefs as to not be offensive, not just be told that they practice a fictional religion but never actually see how it's different and if it's really different enough.

                                            Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post

                                            Initially yeah but as the game developed they moved away from that. By the time of Revised they're even incorporating gnostic heresies into their outlook. It's also important to comment they represent an ancient coptic pagan heresy rather than contemporary Egyptians or even modern Coptics. If I wanted to actually address the Conan stuff it makes serpantis typhonicbeastis or give them another disciplines (probably setite sorcery or potence)
                                            Like I said earlier, the gameline tried to move away from the pulp roots, but I don't think they ever succeded. The clan was rooted in a fantasy concept that doesn't exist in real life. And even where they tried taking the clan in a different direction, it was still pulp fantasy with a patina of a historical group of people that no longer exist. Pushing the clan in an entirely different direction isn't whitewashing anything, because again, we're dealing with a pulp fantasy group with a few ties to an ancient group of historical peoples that don't really exist anymore. Setites are absolutely not, and were never intended to be a representation of modern day Coptic Egyptians or any other existing modern day group and I don't think pushing them in that direction would have been good for anyone.

                                            Whitewashing hurts people removing people from the cultural dialogue, but nobody is being hurt by the civil war among the Followers of Set or the fact that the clan is no longer 100% bad guys out of Conan the Barbarian wearing Egytpian cosplay. Just like nobody is being hurt by the Giovanni no longer being only a bunch of Italian mafiosos.

                                            In werewolf, all of the Tribes were based on various cultural groups. But in vampire only the 4 "outsider" Clans were based on (negative) cultural stereotypes (Ravnos = Roma, Giovanni = Italian Mafia, Assamites = middle eastern assassins and then the Followers were based on Conan the Barbarian and only dropped in Egypt because it's a desert with snakes). This was always recognized as problematic, not just the negative stereotypes, but simply having 4 clans that were so closely tied to very specific culture or fantasy elements made it much harder to work with those clans than the 9 that weren't. I think it was a smart idea to try and strip out a lot of the cultural baggage from those 4 clans and pull them up to the same universal standard that you see in the other 9 clans.

                                            Note that I wouldn't agree with doing the same thing in Werewolf, because in Werewolf the cultural connections have been fundamental to all of the Tribes since the beginning. Werewolf and V20 are different beasts, and what might work in one wouldn't work in another.

                                                I'm glad that JS pointed out the passage about residential "schools", even if just in passing. Even though everything in that passage is correct, given the context of how they were treated by KM, that Paradox would have the nerve to drop this description of a real-world atrocity (even if it was written by Indigenous authors) into the text with zero context or actual connection to the game just to say that "Look! I know we treated a real Indigenous author like crap and actively belittled everything they suggested, and removed all agency and representation from the groups actually affected by this atrocity, but! We brought it up, so we're totally not racist!" It made me so angry I almost just quit reading the W5 book entirely. While I'm not against bringing up serious subjects like this in games, there's just something so gross and cynical about how it was handled here.

                                                Maybe it just hits very close to home because where I am now, there are a lot of survivors of these "schools" and the kind of things they went through still keeps me up at night sometimes. It's definitely a subject that deserves more respect than being used as a shield against racism accusations. But I don't blame the authors of the passage itself. Like I said, the information is correct, and this subject matter is important. Any education on the subject is better than nothing, I guess. This is on Paradox and KM for actively refusing to back this passage up with respect anywhere else, despite being told repeatedly that they should.
