                                Well, the Garou regularly deal with entities too powerful to be mind-controlled by anything less powerful than Gaia herself, so they have an objective check on the memories and history. It is the World of Darkness, not the World of Grayness, so there are always objective 'evils', there are just no objective 'goods'.

                                In WtA, the Garou are not an objective 'good', but the Wyrm is an objective 'bad'. Garou are epic heroes, so they cannot be objectively 'good', and they often have to choose between two 'objective' evils. That, however, does not mean they are supernatural serial killers, just that they live in a world with precious few simple answers.

                                For example, a lost child stumbles onto a Garou moot. It is probably a safe bet that modern Garou will not kill the child (at least not with the people I have always played with), but they have to do something with her because they cannot just return her to her parents because of the Veil.

                                Do they call upon a spirit to erase her memory? Do they send her to an Umbra Realm to become an Ephemera in service to their Totem? Do they adopt her into the Tribe? Do they take her to Baffin Island and let Gaia make the choice? Every choice has a cost and a risk associated with it.

                                    Originally posted by PixiekillerEin View Post


                                    but like.... would adding nuance to that, the existence of mind control, the possibility that people are lying, not serve to improve it at all?
                                    Ah, you're asking for Doubt to be an element of the Lore, and that's an X-Rated Theme. Short answer; yes. Doubt is a great theme which serves to improve everything, whether the Lore is true or otherwise. It's one of the biggest and most realistic ways to generate conflict between people who agree with eachother. Me and James both agree that Nazism is bad, but can either of us be sure that Jim is a Nazi? If there were no doubt, there would be no meaningful or nuanced conflict. It would just be two people going after a literal Nazi after immediately agreeing to do it.

                                            Originally posted by PixiekillerEin View Post

                                            So at least for me, adding nuance is completely fine. Saying the Setting itself (and authors) is/are lying to you takes a great deal away from the game since you are removing the foundation it's built on.

                                            I have no problem with the concept of having characters disagree on the nature of the cosmological conflict (there are entire factions within the Garou Nation that having differing views, to say nothing of the expanded Fera Breeds). I do have a problem with the idea that the entire setting is a farce. Because then what is the point of playing the game if you start with the premise that everything the game is based on are lies?

                                            Oh, also worth clarifying maybe. I am speaking from purely a Werewolf game/Setting perspective. This has nothing (zero) to do with the greater World(s) of Darkness as a whole. How Vampires or Changelings or Demons, etc. view things and what themes are relevant to those games make no difference in my stance as it pertains to this thread/subject. Vampire has themes that reinforce the idea that everything is or can be a lie. That isn't the same premise as Werewolf. And both games can be enjoyed for various reasons. For me, I enjoy both games for their differing themes.

                                            Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post
                                            My point is that something which is presented as Word of God is still optional because it's ALL optional. I don't enjoy invoking the Golden Rule since it isn't something that you normally need to say, it's just something that most people accept and are happy with, it's a rare occasion when it actually needs to be stated that the lore and rules exist for your use and that "canon" is a nebulous concept at best.
                                            Some is, some isn't. Any information presenting IC is 100% option, because the IC bits in the books are meant to be flawed. If you have information given in a Setting section or a rules section, that's not so much up for debate. That's the authors telling you how the system or Setting work. And I fully recognize that some books (especially earlier ones) have writers that just don't understand the setting, or have a....non-traditional view of it. When something pops up in a book that goes against the basics of the Setting, you kind of have to write it off as nonsensical (Examples: a Vampire diablerizing a Garou; CoG revised; etc.). That is technically against canon, but that happens sometimes.

Spirits are wrong. The spirit world itself kind of has to follow as being a grand trick (since proof abounds there of the Setting's premises).                                                 Entire thread seems to be vampire players expecting werewolf to have the exact same source of angst and despair as VtM. You can absolutely have uncertainty in werewolf, but half the respondents here seem incapable of inserting ambiguity without completely gutting the core of the game, IE, fight a losing war to rebalance the Triat. Making the Garou Nation wrong about everything since year 0 isn't the right way to do it.

                                                My players have had to make difficult choices, and none of it required me to dangle the possibility of the Wyrm actually being in the right or for Gaia not existing or that their totems have all been hoodwinked by some mage 10,000 years ago. Those don't add anything.

                                                What if your friends are poisoned by an insidious Wyrm venom? Do you mercy kill them or do you seek out a cure, possibly having to fight your former allies who think your friends should be put down for the good of the sept?

                                                A kinfolk girl undergoes her first change, but it turns out that she was actually sired by a Black Spiral Dancer... and that there are terrible prophecies about what happens when Whippoorwill claims her as his own. Do you do your best to teach her to stay on the straight and narrow? Do you kill or maim her? Do you banish her into the deep umbra, hoping that distance will stop the bane totem from finding his prey?

                                                The good and right ruler of the tribal sept is slain in a challenge gone awry, and now your pack's rival and harshest critic is in charge. Will you fall in line, only to get placed in some of the most deadly missions 'to test your mettle,' or will you rebel against the new leader and stage a coup? How close, if at all, will you cleave to the Litany? Are you attempting to seize control yourselves, or just taking down someone wholly unfit to lead?

                                                And regardless of how and why you do what you do, the Weaver is still ascendant, the Wyrm is completely berserk, and the Wyld is dying but doesn't care. Kinda bleak, really, but you are fighting too hard to notice... and if you stop, the despair of Harano beckons...

                                                Without getting into gory detail, I think that shit is all pretty nuanced. The Wyrm = bad, Gaia = good, but how her servants act and prosecute the war is still totally up in the air. No need for mind-controlled spirits or vampire conspiracies here, boss.
