    Well, "should adhere to" as opposed to "must adhere to". My problem is usually with characters who want to build characters that are of a given tribe/culture, but oppose one or more fundamental (in my opinion) aspects of that tribe/culture - for (in my opinion) no other reason than being a special snowflake.
    I usually have no problem with characters that tone down or ignore one such aspect, in favour of other aspects of the tribe (or the character, as breed or auspice aspects).

    And, for obvious reasons (namely LARP), I have to maintain a coherent game world with a lot of players than an average troupe. Characters that are opposing the tribe/culture they claim to belong all too often create problems for the other players who have characters of that tribe and that is a game element they usually do not appreciate. And for the player who has the opposing character it is generating game exposure on the expanse of the other players, and that is not fair either.

                            heinrich

                            The problem is that you're treating Tribes as monolithic in their cultural natures; despite the clear evidence this isn't true. The Tribes all have Camps which show a large amount of philosophic variation in the Tribes. The books tell us that the Tribes are full of internal conflicts over which aspects of their Tribe are most important, who's the "truest" expression of Tribal values, and so on.

                            Painting it such that Tribes are essentially hive-minds where everyone must conform or they're killed/cast out as Ronin is not how the books state things are, not something that's logical about the nature of Tribes, and something that sounds incredibly boring. The way you talk about Tribes, they're not what the books seem to be talking about, they're fanatical cults where non-conformity is clearly worse than any Litany violation.

                            You're also ignoring the huge number of other factors that play into Tribal membership; such as breeding/parentage, the scarcity of the Garou necessitating numbers over vague ideological bickering, and as Ana Mizuki mentioned the role of the Tribal Totems and associated spirits.

                                I'm not sure how common or uncommon certain camps within a given tribe is, and surely some tribes are more tolerant than others. I'm not saying that each tribe has for every topic one narrow standpoint from what that they may never diverge. Obviously there is a bandwidth of opinions for every issue that is acceptable for each tribe, but there are still opinions that are not. And certainly not meeting the tribally acceptable bandwidth in one area doesn't call for expulsion from the tribe. How good one fits into a tribe is more of a multi-dimensional correlation type of situation.

                                And sure, breeding/parenting is certainly a factor for tribal membership (for some tribes more than others). If one argues that the tribe should not turn down an applicant due to the scarcity of garou in general, someone else will comment, that the applicant should go to a "catch-all" tribe like Children of Gaia or Bone Gnawers but not be allowed to weaken the Get, Fianna, Silver Fangs, or any more traditional tribe with his sub-tribal-par characteristics. Catch-all being a exaggeration, for these tribes have an in-game reputation for having a broad bandwidth of what is acceptable, that other tribes don't have.

                                "Cynicism and callousness are increasingly common, and the numbers of ronin (Garou who turn away from their pack and tribe) swell with each new moon." (from Werewolf 2nd) kind of tells us a little what the fate of a garou with "Burn Out" flaw might be. Sure, here is a shift in the Setting from different tribes competing for mundane and spiritual resources in present day to revised's working together attitude.

                                As for Tribal Totems: well, I see the chicken/egg problem here. Does/should a totem give clear instruction to let a garou into the tribe, no questions asked? If not, if the instructions are vague, vision-like, prophecy-ridden and what not, don't the garou have an obligation to check if the interpreted them right? Also, wouldn't due to the tribe/totem-symbioses the standards the tribe enforces be the same the Totem enforces? So, would the totem maybe only intervene, if such qualities weren't obvious in the cub to the garou and the totem wants to give them a hint?

                                Prophecies or divine intervention make nice story elements, but I don't think it is an all to common occurrence. And they are a kind of thought-terminating, if garou in game would rely on determining who is fit for the tribe an who not, why educate, and why have tribal specific Rites of Passage? Obviously, as a LARP-ST, with a lot of players starting as cubs I detest that players of a given tribe shift off the responsibility to judge whether or not a young garou is fitting for their tribe to the Totem aka the Storytellers.

                                And obviously there is a mention of a Get of Fenris, Fenris and the garou who educated, trained and trailed (Rite of Passage) him accepted, despite the fact that he was a reincarnated Stargazer soul who once Fostern left the tribe an joined the Stargazers. So, what a Totem allows and what not, in the game world is vague. Of Evan-heals-the-Past, who was declared Wendigo by ancestor spirits, it is said that for the first years he still was constantly tested by all Wendigo elders he met for the first time. And as a high ranking theurge in Wendigo TN paraphrased Spirits are unreliable, ever changing and contradicting, also circumstances change and visions are misinterpreted. One has to form one's own opinion an make one's own decisions."

                                In the end, it depends on the tone of the game of werewolf you want to play/ST. Certainly some games can or should allow more leeway with the characteristics a tribe member should present than other games. One has to find a level the troupe is comfortable with.

                                    Very interesting discussion!

                                    I handle it in a fair way, i create a NPC that is fairly stereotypical and let them have success that is at least similar to the one that the PC has. He most likely actually likes the PC, but other tribe member might - from time to time and without being facetious - ask the PC why he is not more like the NPC or when he fails he gets things to hear like "Even NPC makes mistakes sometimes". Very effective and it helps the character actually play said member of a tribe.

                                    Contrasting your PC with such a NPC will make the player invested in him. Maybe he will unconsciously start to dislike him and try to denounce the NPC or they become strong friends but the player will engage with that PC and it will strengthen the chronicle.

                                    From my experience that is way more effective than have other tribes member being consistently being negative towards the PC.
