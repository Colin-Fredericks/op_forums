                                    Originally posted by etherial View Post

                                    It's one way that GMs limit the presence of Fera in convention games where you simply cannot vet every player but also want to accomodate every player.

                                    Org games (MES and OWBN) have approval systems in place.

                                    A lot of the mechanical changes bring MET:WTA more in line with MET:VTM, but I just can't imagine anyone wanting these sweeping setting changes introduced through the LARP book.

                                    It's also tied into a concept they call the "economy of cool." The idea is that, while they want people to be able to play unusual things as the setting allows, they also want to make sure that people aren't dominating the scene with too many cool things. Rarer types of Changing Breeds cost merit points to prevent people from playing the strange thing AND getting a full load of other cool things through as well, thus edging out the person playing a "regular" concept (i.e. Garou) with those same "cool" merits. In Africa, werecats are just uncommon, and thus don't cost as much because it's more likely to see them, and they're not as special. It also encourages people to play the more standard and common character types, since those characters have more freedom to them, and presents a trade-off between relatively unusual powers and having more flexibility.

                                    It also acts as a useful tool for organized games, since they can alter the rarity levels. In the MES, where we use the BNS rules for Vampire in two games (one for Camarilla, Anarchs, and Independents, and the other for the Sabbat), there are two different rarity sets for the two different settings, to reflect the "unusualness" of a particular concept in each one - where the Crusader bloodline has a point cost in the core book and the Cam/Anarch venue, it's the default in the Sabbat venue and doesn't cost any XP. This allows the game runners to tailor the game setting to fit their expectations and personal story.

                                    If you're doing a home game, on the other hand, and you don't like rarity merits, you can choose to get rid of them or alter how the cost is paid. I'm in a Dark Ages Vampire game run off of BNS - none of the base clans are any rarer than any other (except for the Daughters and the Gargoyles, who are off limits), and playing a Bloodline costs XP, but NOT Merit slots (so, you can pay the points to belong to the True Brujah Bloodline, but still have seven points worth of general or clan-specific merits). There's still a cost, but it comes from the starting XP pool, not from the options you can use merits for.
