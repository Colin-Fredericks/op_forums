    Rank 1 Black Spiral Dancer Gifts

    $ Bale Armour: Activated for 1 Willpower, lasts for a scene, and applies a 1 die penalty to all incoming attacks. Successful Brawl attacks lead to the attacker taking a level of Bashing damage. Relatively cheap resource, lights a big area, draws a lot of attention, slight protection, harms attackers (though not ones with soak and regen). This is decent enough to be Green, since it's cheap, and the bonus stacks well with other defensive measures.

    $ Bale Aura: Costs 1 Willpower, lasts for a scene, lights a good area and those in the light will Frenzy with one fewer success on a Rage roll. This is a great way to push people towards Thrall of the Wyrm. Handy for corrupting others but not that useful generally speaking.

    $ Bane Protector: Essential for BSDs and apparently taught during their initiation. This Gift allows a Dancer to petition Banes for aid. Spend 1 Gnosis, Manipulation + Leadership (difficulty 6), more successes means a longer duration for the 'agreement' with the Bane. Given the number of Banes in cities and their sheer strength, this is pretty great.

    $ Repress Taint: No Gnosis cost! Roll Perception + Subterfuge, difficulty 7. Successes increase the difficulty to detect taint, one per success, to a maximum of 9. This compares very favourably with the Level 5 Skin Dancer Gift Mask Taint, which BSDs also get for some reason.

    Resist Pain: See the Rank 1 Philodox list. BSDs with this Gift may feel the pain but not suffer any downsides from it.

    Resist Toxin: See the Rank 1 Bone Gnawer list. Absolutely necessary for survival in many Wyrmish locales.

    Sense Wyrm: See the Rank 1 Metis list. Find friends!

    Shroud: See the Rank 1 Elder Brother list. European Garou only encountering this Gift from BSDs, and then meeting the Elder Brothers for the first time and seeing them using an identical Gift, probably didn't help.

    $ Spiral-Shadow Dance*: Passive +3 initiative when in poor lighting. Solid Amber so long as you don't have any favourite Gifts which generate light.

        Rank 2 Black Spiral Dancer Gifts

        $ Ears of the Bat: Your ears become strangely shaped in all your Forms. When you choose to activate this Gift, your ears unfold to be like a bat's, and you gain echolocation. You can sense the size and shape of everything within the range of your hearing, and you can beat supernatural forms of hiding (e.g. Obfuscate) with a Perception + Occult. Great incentive to use Lupus.

        $ Grave Claws: Your fangs and claws become anchors to the Low Umbra, guaranteeing that individuals you slay in that scene will become Wraiths. The only way to free such a Wraith is to kill the killer, or to journey into the Shadowlands. This costs 1 Gnosis and the transformation of your natural weapons lasts for one scene. The Wraith is mystically bound to you and can't harm you, but it's haunted by its darkest emotions (i.e. its Shadow would be very powerful and it'll suffer quite badly in terms of the Wraith mechanics). Feels like a good plot point for a Silent Strider PC!

        Going to leave this unrated since it's more of a NPC power, and if a PC uses it the result will be 100% dependent on how the ghosts wind up being run. Mostly this feels like a trophy of how mean you are. It could stop someone from rejoining Gaia as an Ancestor Spirit, for example, which could hurt the Garou Nation, but that's a very tenuous benefit. Arguably this could get you loads of Renown in the Garou Nation, as you can show off your wailing entourage of Garou Ghosts. So yeah, if you want to make your collection of anguished dead Garou your 'thing' this could be the inspiration of your weird Deed Name, sure, but it's more aesthetic than mechanically useful.

        $ Hidden Killer: A slight upgrade on Hidden Killer. See the Rank 1 Red Talons list. If you have a piece of a Garou you may alter your victim so that investigation reveals that other Garou, and not yourself, as the killer. For example the exact shape and pattern of the claw marks may change to one more indicative of that other Garou's height, weight and strength, while any fur left on the victim would match your framing target.

        $ Submit: A slight upgrade on Kneel. See the Rank 2 Black Furies list. Targets resisting the power suffer a -2 penalty to dice pools which can only be avoided with Resist Pain. This fixes the main problem with Kneel; with that Gift it either works (stuck kneeling) or does nothing (they spend a point of blood, or Gnosis, or equivalent and just avoid it). Here if it 'does nothing' the pain of resisting the impulse to kneel still impacts the scene with the -2 penalty.

        How good is a 'free' -2 penalty? Well that really depends. There is still an activation roll, and this Gift takes up time, but if you can get the -2 penalty to go off it could pay huge dividends across a fight. A universal -2 debuff which you can apply to almost any enemy, and not just in fights, is pretty good. Just remember that spirits don't take wound penalties, and Resist Pain (and similar effects) will make this ineffective. Kneel remains poorly written and messy, but Submit is definitely an upgrade, just not enough to be Green.

        $ Wyrm Hide: Spend one Gnosis, roll Manipulation + Leadership (difficulty 6), and get a bonus point of Soak per success, up to a maximum of 3.

        Weirdly, this Gift also lets you treat Lethal as Bashing so long as you don't botch the soak roll. Which... mostly does nothing! It might help if you need to heal normally without Regeneration, or if healing which only works on Bashing is applied, or if you half Bashing for some reason, or regenerate it more quickly for some reason... all in all a bit odd, but not entirely useless.

                            Rank 3 Black Spiral Dancer Gifts

                            $ Beautiful Lie: This is a mass memory alteration Gift which is specific to atrocities and terrible crimes. Costs one Gnosis, Wits + Subterfuge (difficulty 6 typically), on success people develop vague deluded memories which cover up what really occurred. Arson becomes a tragic accident, a slaughter by a pack of Garou becomes an attack by feral dogs, and so on. With 3+ successes there's even evidence!

                            Pretty strong, and helps BSDs with the kinds of things they consistently need to do. Given that BSDs are meant to be a secretive Tribe which is difficult to learn about, Gifts like this explain how they manage to stay under the radar.

                            $ Claws of Corrosion: This is 'one of the greatest Black Spiral Gifts' according to Book of the Wyrm, and I can easily believe it! With this Gift a Garou can turn a spirit into a Bane with claw attacks. On a successful attack you must spend a point of Willpower, Gnosis and Rage and make a Intelligence + Occult (difficulty 6!) roll. The spirit gains Corruption points equal to your successes, slowly loses them over time, and if its Corruption ever exceeds its current Essence then the target begins transforming into a Bane. This means the Gift is great against spirits which are already badly injured, or already quite corrupt from exposure to Wyrm taint or recent upheaval in its 'domain'.

                            Having a Gift which fundamentally changes the nature of a spirit at Rank 3 is something Gaian Theurges would be very envious of! The Gift is expensive and the impacts aren't immediate, but the Renown possibilities are fantastic. You could enter BSD legend as the hero who turned a mighty servant of Gaia to the Wyrm's service.

                            $ Foaming Fury: Anyone you bite, as a passive benefit, must roll Stamina (difficulty 8) or go into Frenzy. If you spend a Gnosis right after attacking you get to pick whether the target flees or goes berserk. Great ability, extremely handy, but do note that you can't turn it off, and it does influence your behaviour. Assuming you like the idea of your BSD acting like a rabid dog or twitchy fox in his mannerisms, this Gift is great.

                            $ Gift of the Tainted Totem: This has all the problems with Banish Totem, but comes with the complication of replacing the Totem with your own. That makes it better (you taint your enemies and cause them to panic as a Wyrmish spirit takes root in their Pack's soul) and also worse (the benefits of having a Totem remain available, including whatever unique abilities your Totem comes with).

                            A clever Theurge hit with this Gift will figure out the Ban of the Totem and break it as fast as possible in the hope of getting the 'tainted totem' to leave as fast as possible. Regardless, this is more of a NPC power, and while it'd be very interesting to experience this as a player, it isn't much use to a PC, even in a BSD game, unless it is a highly psychological one with an emphasis on slowly breaking down a cast of recurring 'heroes' to villainy.

                            Purifying the underlying spirit magic of this Gift so that instead of forcibly pushing your Totem onto an enemy you could temporarily lend your Totem to an ally would be pretty interesting! That sort of thing is normally the work of Rites, but picture a situation where two Garou packs work together and absolutely need to share some power or connection. If I were playing an Elder Brother Theurge who encountered this Gift I'd probably be drawn towards understanding it and breaking it down to something useful, for good or ill.

                            $ Ichor Blade: Hand Blade, but dripping with green Wyrmish poisons. Costs one Rage and transforms an arm into a poison blade. Dexterity + Melee to hit, difficulty 6, and on a hit your target will suffer a -2 penalty for one turn per success. This can be negated by Resist Toxin or Resist Pain, but most foes won't have those. Handy for a melee BSD, and the painful toxin is a nice angle, but the Gift suffers a little given the plethora of incredible melee weapons in the game. That said if you want to leave your Brawl at 0 for some reason, this could be handy.

                            $ Patagia: Reflexively unfurl flaps which allow you to glide on the air. The details of the Gift include high speed and options to speed up or gain altitude with Rage. Inferior to true flight, but the fact that it happens with no cost when you're falling or require it for some reason is pretty great! I really can't see why a non-"deforming" version of this Gift can't be learnt by Metis or Lupus from a Flying Squirrel spirit, so if you're desperate for this Gift perhaps you could look into it.

                                Rank 4 Black Spiral Dancer Gifts

                                $ Crawling Poison: After a successful claw or bite attack you may roll Gnosis vs the target's Stamina (both difficulty 8). If you beat your target they lose Regeneration for an hour per success.

                                The problem here is the Gnosis roll. It'll block you from spending Rage in the same turn, so now you're giving up a lot to block regeneration. Situationally useful against a foe who is extremely hard to damage and who is going to take a while to kill due to high Soak or unusually powerful regeneration. Typically you're better off just rushing them down.

                                If this Gift works against the Remain Active version of Regeneration it's better, and useful against Garou enemies, but the difficulty 8 Gnosis still an issue; that's a tough dice pool especially at that difficulty!

                                $ Howl of the Bane*: See Call of the Cannibal Spirit. This Gift, however, allows a BSD to summon any Bane with an Essence of 30 or lower. This makes it somewhat more defined than Call, which summons a spirit which lacks any stats in W20 to the best of my knowledge.

                                The ability to call upon ANY Bane with an Essence of 30 or lower is pretty neat, all things considered. This would let you summon a 30 Essence Furmling (see below), a 30 Essence Scryer (excellent spy), a Scrag (send it to possess a mortal), and so on. You could even get a weak (only 30 Essence) Nexus Crawler! Or you could evoke a Bitter Rage and send it after a Pack to send them into wild frenzies. The flexibility here is great.

                                The problem is that the roll is still difficulty 8, the summoning takes 3 turns, it costs Gnosis and Rage, and if you botch the summoned spirit tries to kill you. Still very limited, unfortunately, but more worth the risk than the original. If for some reason you can reduce the difficulty of the roll this could be Green.

                                $ Hungry Rust: Spend 2 Gnosis and put a Bane inside a machine. Intelligence + Crafts, difficulty variable depending on complexity. If someone uses the tool (shoots the gun, starts the car etc.) it fuses with them horrifically. This can be resisted with a Wits + Occult roll on the part of the wielder, requiring more successes than the BSD. Humans are stuck fused, Garou can rip free.

                                The ability to fuse a Garou enemy into the driver's seat of his car so he's a horrible part-werewolf-part-car monster is hilarious, but the intense setup for this Gift makes it tricky to use. Requires setup, and it's expensive, and the roll might be very hard, and the results are... well the results are HORRIBLE for the character who you target, but that doesn't necessarily make the power that great. The Gift sounds quite fun to use, if you're playing someone evil, but it isn't that great in terms of utility.

                                Open Wounds: See the Rank 4 Shadow Lord list. The flavour of the Gift changes slightly but the mechanics are identical.

                                $ Stolen Hide*: This turns ghosts enslaved with Grave Claws into armour. Each ghost worn as armour grants two additional levels of Bruised health which go 'first' when you are attacked. Any ghost whose health levels (worn by you) are removed is permanently destroyed.

                                How good is this? Well assuming that you can use Grave Claws to set up before a big battle this could be immensely powerful. The problem is that Grave Claws costs Gnosis and Stolen Hide costs Gnosis. For 2 points of Gnosis you can, with rolls and setup, gain 2 health levels. Now you could, in theory, stack up 1000 bonus health levels using 500 enslaved ghosts. That would cost you 1000 Gnosis, but you could do it.

                                So, in essence, this Gift is entirely at the mercy of the Gnosis economy like no other. And while you can regenerate damage done to the 'stolen hide' of ghosts, it's very limited. If you take one point of damage, you could regenerate that. If you take two, that ghost is used up and gone, and those health levels are now missing. It's powerful but hard to utilize well. It also does mean losing the ghosts you've enslaved, though chances are this is the best use for them, and a good threat to use against the ones you do keep!

                                Call it Amber, potential to be Green if you get access to insane quantities of Gnosis, and call it a day.

                                $ Summon Wyrm Elemental: See Call Elemental, which is covered in the Rank 4 Elder Brother list. This Gift is first given to BSDs in the Corebook under the name Call Elemental, with a simple note that BSDs will summon Furmlings, H'rugglings, Wakshaani or Hoglings instead of the typical results. Summon Wyrm Elemental refines this slightly, changing the name of the Gift, keeping the same mechanics, and noting that many BSDs don't bother to placate the spirit they summon, instead stopping with the summoning roll and then doing what they wish while the elemental causes havoc. This is a handy use for the Gift, but of course the best results require placating the summoned spirit.
                                Hogling (Smog)
                                W3, R8, G7, E18-40
                                Charms: Create Wind, Updraft

                                Not useful unless you need to stop or start a storm. Just a nasty air elemental.

                                Furmling (Balefire)
                                W5, R10, G5, E20-40
                                Charms: Blast (Fire)

                                Identical to the Fire Elemental, but missing "Create Fires" as a Charm, which is likely an oversight. Its Rage and its Blast make it horribly dangerous. As a Bane with a Rage of 10, and embodying the concept of balefire and toxic fires out of control, simply summoning one of these and running away laughing is probably a great move for a BSD.

                                H'ruggling (Sludge)
                                W9, R3, G5, E17-40
                                Charms: Umbraquake

                                Basically just a worse Earth Elemental. Earth gets Armour, this doesn't, and for some reason its Rage is worse.

                                Wakshaani (Toxin)
                                W3, R7, G7, E17-40
                                Charms: Blighted Touch, Flood

                                Blighted Touch has a chance of temporarily or permanently making someone damaged by the Bane become dominated by their worst personality traits. All in all this is a Water Elemental with the healing, Willpower and Gnosis reduced. A small buff to Rage doesn't really make up for it!


                                Notably none of these Spirits have a Gift which explicitly allows them to spread corruption, but that's fine. The important thing is that a flood, storm, earthquake or fire started by these guys should be corrupt and do the Wyrm's work, in the most obvious sense.

                                Despite this Gift still being good, the options are significantly worse. A Sludge or Balefire Elemental may well be useful in a fight, but some of the best things about Call Elemental are lost here.

                                    Rank 5 Black Spiral Dancer Gifts

                                    $ Balefire: A neat supernatural ranged attack with no cost. Roll Dexterity + Athletics, base difficulty 6, treated a bit like using a gun. Each success on this test inflicts a point of Aggravated damage which can only be soaked at difficulty 8!

                                    How good is this? Well a typical claw attack is Dexterity + Brawl, inflicting Strength + 2 + Attack Successes dice of damage, roll damage at difficulty 6, soak that damage at difficulty 6. How Balefire compares to a conventional attack will ultimately depend on your Strength, and the Soak of the target, and whether you have an applicable spec, and so on. All I can say is that Garou can roll HUGE damage dice pools thanks to a Strength of up to 9, +2 and beyond from their claws, and loads of successes on the attack. Swapping that out for an attack where your successes just automatically inflict levels of damage is a handy option, but keep in mind that you lose a few things in exchange.

                                    The damage you inflict is hard to heal and comes with horrific mutations. Not only do you have to heal the Aggravated damage as one normally would, you have to physically remove the damage with fire, acid, surgery and so on, which will likely come as a surprise to a Theurge or Child of Gaia trying to use Mother's Touch on the injuries as usual.

                                    Assuming you use this ability correctly it's a solid Green due to its utility and lack of cost. It's best leveraged against enemies whose soak makes them relatively resistant to normal attacks, since the +2 difficulty to soak has a much larger potential impact against a large dice pool.

                                    $ Cloak of Anthelios: The idea of a stolen piece of sun magic is very fun to me. This is basically a corruption of sunlight, and the name of a brand of suncream. Spend 1 Gnosis, effects last for a scene, and unlike the original Gift this doesn't hurt Vampires, as they are servants of the Wyrm.

                                    While using this Gift you gain +2 dice of Aggravated damage on your Brawl attacks, anyone facing you has +2 difficulty to attack tests, and if you deal significant damage you can put a Wyrmish mark onto your victims which makes them reek of Wyrm taint. Pretty great all in all!

                                    $ Mask Taint: See the Skin Dancer list. I rated Mask Taint as Amber for Skin Dancers. For BSDs... well it's somewhat redundant given Repress Taint, which is Level 1. But note that while Repress Taint makes it harder to detect your taint (up to a maximum difficulty of 9), this Gift fully represses it. The roll is still hard, and the Gift is pretty mediocre... but it being Amber seems about right.

                                    The White Howl*: A Gift where the spiritual teacher is dead and the Gift is now only passed down Garou to Garou sounds pretty cool! This Gift would be far better for Gaian Garou, for what it's worth. This is a massive AOE which inflicts unsoakable Lethal damage equal to successes on a difficulty 6 Charisma + Primal-Urge. The only creatures harmed are Wyrm-tained creatures, including the BSD using this Gift. The negative effect on Gaians is that they all lose a point of WP per success, and if they reach WP 0 they go into Harano.

                                    Potential uses for this Gift are fights with other servants of the Wyrm, or with anything you've tainted. You could also use this to destroy a huge swarm of Banes which is opposing you. The potential of throwing a whole Moot into Harano is also neat. The trouble is that if you get more successes than you expect you can just immediately kill yourself, or your pack, or your allies or servants.

                                    This Gift mostly exists to show how awful the loss of the White Howlers is, in a way, since the first thought you have when reading it is "this Gift would be great in the hands of Gaian Garou". So, that's the ideal use case of the Gift. If you restore the White Howlers chances are you'll get a lot of Renown as a result (sounds like a fast track to Elder or Legend if ever there was one!) and this Gift would be the most important thing to get once you reach Elder status.

                                    How balanced is this Gift if you're playing a White Howler in modern times? Well the cost is 2G 2R, and the damage is "only" Lethal. But it's unsoakable, and a Charisma + Primal-Urge is relatively easy to boost up. There are a number of Gifts, specializations, Merits, Backgrounds and Rites which could influence your dice pool or difficulty. Say you optimize your dice pool to 10+ and you get the difficulty down from 6 to 4... you're looking at ~6 unsoakable lethal to every Wyrm-tainted creature within range of your howl. Barring harming innocents who have been tainted temporarily, this is amazing. What about the Willpower cost for Gaian Garou within earshot? Well if you've restored the White Howler Tribe then that shouldn't be an issue! If you can use this for Gaia, I'd call it a solid Green. The cost balances it, somewhat, but it's absolutely a horrific nuke befitting a Level 5 (or 6!) ability.

                                        Homid Gifts (Wyld West)

                                        Jam Gun (1): Only works on guns, and only on a single gun at a time. Costs Gnosis. Requires a roll, Manipulation + Crafts, though the roll is only difficulty 5. The target gun is jammed for one turn per success. Naturally this is inferior to Jam Weapon, which effects all weapons present and has no cost, but this Gift may be slightly superior if you only want to take out a single enemy weapon. This Gift could absolutely save lives, and it could swing a fight, so it's not terrible. The specificity is handy. And comparisons to Jam Weapon, arguably one of the best Gifts in the game, are a tad unfair. Call it Amber, nice and easy.

                                        Spirit Brand (2)*: Lets you explain the whole "getting around with horses" situation, but given that, it would make way more sense to make this a Level 1 Gift. Still, this comes with a wide variety of bonuses. Usable on any animal (other than wolf kinfolk). Lasts for multiple months. Causes the animal to serve you loyally and follow orders. Tracking the animal is at +3 difficulty and it gets +2 Stamina. The details of the Gift's execution are all pretty reasonable (Intelligence + Animal Ken difficulty 6) and it only costs one Gnosis.

                                        Assuming that having a horse or equivalent is very important to you, or that an animal companion like a dog, wolf, or similar is critical to your character, I'd call this a solid Green. But in most situations it's distinctly Amber.

                                        Weave of Steel (4): This one is interesting! It can strengthen any manmade object or structure, so for example a coat could become armour or a rusting disused bridge can take the weight of a heavy carriage.

                                        The roll is Strength + Crafts (difficulty 7), the cost is a point of Gnosis. Most objects gain +1 health level per success, while worn items grant +1 soak per success. There is no duration given, so it seems this is permanent! Do note that "armour" created with this Gift grants no protection against Bashing, full protection against Lethal, and half protection against Aggravated, generally speaking. I'd wager that this Gift would be best used on real armour to 'fortify' it, but if I were STing I would limit the bonus soak you could grant to +1, or perhaps +2 in extreme cases, especially since you can apply the benefit permanently! This Gift would be best used on Fetishes and sacred relics of the Garou to ensure that they are never destroyed, assuming they are valid targets (manmade, using crafted materials).

                                        Metis Gifts (Wyld West)

                                        Rattler's Bite (3): Spend a Rage before biting. If the attack hits and deals damage, the damage after soak is doubled! There is some risk here, but the ability to inflict a very heavy blow with a single bite is pretty great. Stackable with other biting Gifts and superior to options like adding some damage. The ability to possibly one-shot a dangerous enemy, especially by ambush or with setup from your team (blinded, immobilized etc.) is great.

                                        Note that this is Rank 4 in W20. Not sure if this is an error or an intentional buff to the Metis list.

                                        Lupus Gifts (Wyld West)

                                        Healing Sleep (3): This Gift lets you sleep off any damage, including Aggravated, which is pretty neat. Spend a Gnosis, roll Stamina + Primal-Urge, after a full 24 hour sleep you're fully healed. I'd be kinder to Gifts like these if they could apply to the whole Pack!

                                        If this were free I'd be quite happy with this, after all, using this Gift could save your pack a point of Gnosis since less healing is needed. But this Gift requires Gnosis anyways!

                                            Ragabash Gifts (Wyld West)

                                            Spider's Song (1): This Gift is Level 1 in the Wyld West rather than Level 2. See the Rank 2 Ragabash List. Telegraph lines are treated like landlines, and you don't need to understand Morse code to use the Gift. Stronger because it only costs 3xp and you can get it at char-gen, weaker because messages are much less common and important now.

                                            Man With No Name (2): Leave behind fading or even no memories of yourself when you move on from a town. The Gift's mechanics are a little involved, but the net result is that more successes means less of a memory of you is left behind. The problem is that Garou move in a pack; unless the Ragabash rides a horse into town and all of the other Garou remain in the wilderness? This Gift would make a Lone Wolf Ragabash concept stronger, but nothing in the game supports that! The Gift is free to use, so adding an optional Gnosis cost to apply it to your whole Pack would make good sense.

                                            Ghost Town (5)*: Hide a town or settlement of some kind, making it and its occupants invisible. People will remember the place as abandoned. Locals can't see outsiders, outsiders can't see locals. The duration of 'one Story' is very rare and pretty useful! The ability to hide a settlement of Garou Kinfolk, or a tribe of natives, from those who would harm them is most key. The cost of 2 Willpower and duration of a Story means you could use this ability in perpetuity to keep a family or town hidden forever. Handy, if you're playing a stationary campaign, or a story where protecting innocents and settlements, or indigenous peoples, is very important.

                                            Could be anywhere from Red to Green depending on the Chronicle.

                                                Black Fury Gifts (Wyld West)

                                                Song of the Seasons (1): Ignore any temperature extremes which aren't attacks. Lasts for a day and costs 1 Gnosis. Any attack roll based on temperature is made at +1 difficulty against you. Pretty unusual for a Rank 1 Gift, but not gamechanging.

                                                Dispel the Golden Waste (4): This Gift lets you banish gold to protect an area from prospectors and gold rushes. Roll Gnosis, difficulty 8, on a success all of the gold in an area (no size given) turns to dust and escapes to some unknown location. Assuming that you are aiming to preserve the sanctity of nature in the Wyld West, this Gift is handy, but it falls firmly in the "should have been a Rite" department.

                                                Fianna Gifts (Wyld West)

                                                Prospector's Luck (3)*: Costs 1 Willpower, roll Perception + Alertness (difficulty 7), scan an area of a half mile radius per dot of Gnosis.

                                                More successes give more info, and you can only use this Gift in a given area once. Which, oddly, means that you're actually better off using this with a lower Gnosis score. With 1 success and 1 Gnosis, you know for a fact that there are precious metals somewhere in a half mile radius. With 1 success and 5 Gnosis, you know there are precious metals somewhere in a 2.5 mile radius. That's 25x more area to cover. What this means is that the Gift wasn't very well considered unfortunately!

                                                Red by default, given that it has a cost, can only be used once in an area, and the poor interaction with Gnosis increasing. Could be Amber or even Green if you find yourself in an unusual "get as rich as possible!" campaign where you try to become a Rockefeller.

                                                        Iron Rider Gifts

                                                        While this effects every Tribe, and not just the 'Iron Riders' of the Wyld West, it's worth noting here that certain Gifts are rare or not yet invented in this time period.

                                                        Rare Gifts: City Running, Control Complex Machine, Diagnostics, Signal Rider (telegraph lines only)
                                                        Unavailable Gifts: Cybersenses, Jam Technology, Plug and Play, Tech Speak

                                                        The losses effect Homids and Iron Riders mostly, however there are alternate and variant Gifts to take their place.

                                                        Well-Oiled Running (1)*: Costs Gnosis, protects objects from entirely natural wear and tear for one day per success. It's a difficulty 7 Stamina + Crafts, and you can't effect anything bigger than a covered wagon.

                                                        How good is this? Well, it only works on natural stuff. Say your pack are sheltering inside a covered wagon during a horrific storm which could easily kill an ordinary human, and for them, would at least leave them injured, scattered, and acting without rest. The Iron Rider could spend a Gnosis, do the difficulty 7 roll, and if they succeed the wagon will be 100% immune to the storm. That makes this Gift very ST-dependent. Not everyone gets how dangerous weather is, or how much sand and dirt and tiny insects mess with your stuff. If the ST remembers that the Wyld West is very hostile to Weaver-work... this Gift could actually be very good! Though the 'Gnosis tax' which comes with keeping your stuff in good shape could become a little annoying.

                                                        I'm going to charitably call this Amber, but it could very easily be Red if the hazards you encounter are only supernatural or manmade.

                                                        Iron Coat (2): This is a full-on upgrade of Steel Fur (see the Rank 2 Glass Walkers post). While I don't think this Gift is as good as the other 'armour' Gifts, I do think it is better written and should be used instead of Steel Fur.

                                                        The important changes are that Iron Coat gives you +1 soak, since you get +2 soak for 1 success, rather than +1 soak for 1 success, and from there it increases by 1 soak per success. That bonus point might be handy. The Gift also functions for Homids and Glabro, just not fully. A Homid subtracts 2 from the bonus soak, a Glabro subtracts 1. So, for example, if you got 5 successes on the activation for this Gift, you'd get +6 soak in furry forms, +5 soak in Glabro, and +4 soak in Homid. The ability for a man or woman to turn the tiny invisible hairs covering most of their skin to steel is very fun to me, and I love the image of an Iron Rider's beard turning to metal wire! The sneaky use case of using this Gift before shaving to get big quantities of metal is also quite funny to me, though presumably the metal will turn back to hair later.

                                                        All this said, the Gift still suffers from the +1 difficulty to all your Dexterity rolls. That's pretty terrible given that Dexterity is dodging, hitting, and by successes carrying over, damage. Big improvement, but still weak. Like I said with the original, increasing the difficulty of Dexterity rolls is a cardinal sin and you'd need a huge benefit to overcome it. If for some reason you're stuck crouching at an engine, doing some stationary work, and you need the bonus soak while you're unable to dodge at all... well this Gift could have benefits. Tied up and completely helpless? Might as well get +4 soak! Fighting in a place with no room to dodge? Might as well get +4 soak. Can't see the enemy at all, so you can't attack them or dodge? Might as well get the soak. So with all that said, this Gift stays Red because on top of the Dexterity debuff, you also have to roll at Difficulty 7 to activate it. So you might get very little bonus Soak in exchange for the loss in mobility.

                                                        Pulse of the Railroad (2): Allows you to listen to anything happening along a railroad by pressing your ear against part of it. Perception + Science, base difficulty 4 (within 20 miles). The difficulty increases for every 10 miles. So if you wanted to hear something happening 80 miles away you'd need to make a difficulty 10 roll. Another problem is that 1 success isn't immediately a perfect result, you need more successes to hear everything clearly over the 'chatter' of iron spirits. Like many other Gifts this one is afraid of handing the player a win - if you invest in this Gift it should do its job reliably, and being able to overhear the prospector arguing with his brother about the 'devil voice' in the cave should just be a given if you pass the roll with 1 success.

                                                        Another problem is that you have to select a specific area for eavesdropping. The Gift should let you listen along the track sequentially, working your way down the line until you reach something of interest, perhaps at a greater difficulty, but still. Assuming you play this Gift exactly as written, it's unfortunately quite poor. The limited locations it can spy on, the fact you're limited to sound, the fact that you have to pick a location to spy on and can't work your way along the track, the fact the difficulty spikes horribly if you're 60+ miles away (not a big distance for a wolf!), and the fact that you need more successes to get a clear listen over the spirits... it is sadly Red. But I can see a very strong Gift in this with a few little tweaks.

                                                        Repel Metal (3): Spend 1 Gnosis, roll Strength + Science difficulty 7, and more successes means heavier ferrous objects get knocked back. Since this only works on magnetic metals it won't repel a silver object. Despite this being spirit magic. Personally I would make repelling iron and other ferrous metals free, and repelling any other metal costs Gnosis since you need to 'charge' it. The distance you repel objects is fixed, and the Gift only effects a relatively small area. Sadly this is pretty bad! Yeah you could theoretically knock the guns from the hands of a huge gang of enemies... but they do get a resisted Strength to hold onto them, and you're paying a Gnosis. Compares very poorly with, say, Jam Weapons.

                                                        Ultimately this Gift falls victim to having a Gnosis cost when one really isn't necessary. It would also be much improved if you had the option to push or pull depending on your needs! Having a cowboy's gun fly from their hand straight into yours is a great image.

                                                        Gift of the Iron Horse (4): While touching railroad tracks your run speed is doubled and you gain a +1 to your Physical Attributes. This means that a Lupus Garou with a base Dexterity of 5 can run very, very fast with this Gift, thanks to the 2x speed and the +1 Dexterity, as well as bonus Stamina to keep them going for longer.

                                                        This is very niche but also pretty fun! If you can draw a fight onto railroad tracks then the +1 to your Physicals is pretty great. I would say that you could easily make this a Level 1 Gift though. Plus, as an Iron Rider, chances are that you'd be taking the train, or a wagon. If you're going to make a Gift like this, I want it to give your Hispo form, while on rails, the dragging power and speed of a steam train! The Gift has very little ambition in the way it's written, so sadly it gets a Red.

                                                            Red Talons Gifts

                                                            Dust Squall (3): Amazing focused debuff, definitely worth grabbing in a modern game. Spend 1 Gnosis, roll Charisma + Primal-Urge (difficulty 6). A dust devil goes after a single target for one turn per success. Anyone hit by the dust devil is at +2 difficulty to all rolls, and any rolls relating to sight or communication get set at difficulty 10. Hitting the most dangerous foe with this Gift prior to an ambush or assault is a great idea.

                                                            Stargazer Gifts

                                                            Threads of the Weaver (1): Lets you peek into the Penumbra, but only to see the work of Weaver-spirits. Why not just Peek, or Step Sideways? Well this is easier... kinda? Peeking is a Gnosis roll against a difficulty of the local Umbra. This Gift is a Perception + Enigmas, same difficulty. So the dice pool is likely better. But the result is niche; all you get to see are the webs and wires and railings left in the wake of Weaver-spirits. You also get to make a Wits + Enigmas (difficulty 8) to try and figure out what those trails might represent or signify, like "a powerful Weaver-spirit is marching its forces Westward".

                                                            All in all, you're better off peeking. If this Gift's difficulty were fixed at 5, sure, I could see the point. Because the big problem with this Gift, and with trying to spy on Weaver-spirits by Peeking, is that the local Gauntlet will be pretty strong in a place where the Weaver is at work. Not certain, but likely. So unless you're on the wild hilltop looking down on the fort or train station, chances are your difficulty will be quite high anyways. If you're going to write a Gift specifically to spy on the Weaver, ignoring the high difficulty you'd normally face from a thick Gauntlet is probably quite important!

                                                            Counting Coup (4): A great pacifying attack. Originally created by the 'Pure Tribes', this Gift has been learnt by the Stargazers from native Ancestor Spirits.

                                                            Dexterity + Brawl/Melee, and spend 1 Gnosis if you hit. Instead of dealing damage you completely 'break' your opponent's will to fight, assuming you get more successes on the attack roll than the target currently has in temporary Willpower. So, for example, with 7 successes on a Dexterity + Brawl you could spend 1 Gnosis to pacify an enemy with a temporary Willpower of 6. This even ends Frenzy! Enemies with no minds are unaffected, but this works on humans, animals, Garou, Fera and so on.

                                                            Presumably this Gift doesn't work on spirits at all, as they don't have a temporary Willpower stat. If it does, use their permanent Willpower. Great Gift, well worth buying, but should probably also be on the Children of Gaia list for Wyld West!
