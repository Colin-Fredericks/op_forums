                                                  I think we can agree, that in 2nd and revised it was easy to learn whether a child was garou or not and that some children, like Anna Kliminski, were trained to be heir/heiress to their garou parent's legacy from early childhood on. That is: even pre-change.

                                                  W20 changes that aspect of the game. Not only evident with "Scent of the true Form", but also page 41, which states, that virtually any kinfolk could be garou (which is given as one reason why some tribes revere their kin). The same page also states that being garou is genetic.

                                                  So, in my opinion, this part of W20 is crap. Period.

                                                  The Test Vial (in W20) has no limitation about pre-change garou, but also doesn't state that they register as garou, either. It is up to the ST to rule on that - where revised had a clear ruling in the FAQ.
                                                  In any case, the Vial is simply copy&pasted from Kinfolk: Usung Heroes. And since we don't know if the person who was copying and pasting, is the same one who wrote page 41 or the limitation in "Scent of the true form", this pretty much can be attributed to bad editing and/or not enough crowd editing aka open development. In any case, I assume garou would have wanted to know if their offspring was garou long before test vials came into existence, so the talen shouldn't be the only means of determining the answer to this question with relative certainty. Some herbs, rites, gifts, or other magical mumbo-jumbo should be out there. But, as I pointed out, W20, especially on page 41, clearly sets a mood towards "garou don't know".

                                                  So, the question was, "How would the world/culture of the garou be, if they can not (and never could) know if their children are garou?"
                                                  And here is my take on that:

                                                  The "Baptism of Fire" would not exist, or would be performed on any child. If it only works on garou, but doesn't impart the knowledge if it worked onto the performing ritemaster, the garou culture regarding kin families and offspring from one-night-stands could remain relatively the same, compared to a setting where the garou know. About when the first change is to happen, garou would get a kin-fetch spirit's alert and be there for the new garou and prevent any Veil lifting as good as they can.

                                                  But, if that wasn't an option either, then the game world would be different, in my opinion. Having lost cubs without any means to find them would have lead to the Veil being lifted centuries ago. That is unless you rule in favour of divine intervention by Totems and such. But really, if some random people would become garou one day, sure some would hide the fact and be afraid of the government or big corporate, but at least one would hope he is save or gets famous if he demonstrated his powers on live TV.

                                                  Only means to prevent that would be to forbid (and punish) all male garou, who impregnate a woman, who isn't aware of garou and who then removes the child from garou supervision. Although kidnapping such children and raising them, would probably solve the problem of them becoming lost cubs.

                                                  Sex with kin obviously is the best option, as the Curse prevents garou from bringing humans into the fold (not withstanding a Rite which could). But wait, not all garou have their first change at puberty (also page 41). In earlier Editions such garou were crazy Lunatics, today it seems totally okay that they first change in their adulthood (which I find okay, from a character design standpoint). But, if only 1% of all garou doesn't change in puberty that means that one in 10.000 kin is secretly garou and will have a metis child, if having sex with a garou or another of the adult un-changed garou. With teenager sex, the chance of having sex with a garou and therefore become a metis-parent is 1 in 10. Great odds. So, the whole outlook on metis and metis-parents in the garou culture might change. I mean, metis by teenager pregnancy would be relatively common.

                                                  As far as educating the offspring goes, kin and garou kids alike would get the full garou treatment, if the parent is in hope of a successor. This would lead to kin that is probably way more knowledgeable about garou and possibly more apart from normal humans. Also, possibly more kin secrets would be taught amongst the kin possibly raising the number of shaman-like sorcerers amongst their ranks.

                                                  All in all, I have to say, that a garou culture, where garou can't discern if their children are garou, could be interesting, but it wouldn't be WtA!
                                                  And it would have needed more changes than the write-up on page 41 and the changes to "Scent of the true form". In my opinion, an author here wanted to impose his view of the game world onto us and didn't really put the effort in to make it coherent. And it stinks.
