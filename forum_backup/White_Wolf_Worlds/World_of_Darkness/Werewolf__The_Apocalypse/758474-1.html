                        One of the keys to being a good Ragabash is knowing your limits and not doing things that will get you fucked up. There is no special shield for the Ragabashes being assholes just because they are Ragabash. This is something very important to remember when you go around playing Mr. Asshole. A good Ragaash is one that causes chaos and does not end up a victim to the consequences of his tricks. A bad Ragabash quickly collects enemies, battle scars, punishment rites and renown losses. Note that the Ragabash is supposed to be the contrarian, and that most septs will be conservative, so being a Ragabash who is pro conservatism is missing the point. You go from being the challenger to being the supporter of the status quo. Heck you go to being Troll for the status quo.

                        As for the rest, I'm quite willing to agree that the lot of a Metis is a harsh one, and that they have a hard time in their society, however, it should be stressed that Garou are rare and that their society is based on meritocracy. The rarity itself means that you will want to keep the metis around at least as long as possible to be an effective combatant. The meritocracy means that once they earn their recognition, it's a hard time to actually keep them down without a systemic abuse and backstabbing behind the scenes.

                        There are smaller septs who are quite against metis, who hate the mere fact that they exist, but most such septs will not be keeping such Garou, they are likelier to kill them or toss them out to fend for themselves then to actually keep them. The Litany tenet on not tending to anyone's sickness can easily be twisted into this. Why would they waste effort and resources on such a creature? Just to use them as punching bags?

                        If it somehow does conspire that a metis in such a sept grows up, or has the misfortune of a large sept that can spare some resources on getting him up to being cannon fodder, why does that metis stay? What does he have to gain? Let's say that he is a whipped weakling and that he has been turned into a self fulfilled prophecy, he is the punching bag for the sept as is. Why would that sept need a Ragabash to mock him and show how bad he is? Where is the renown in getting a victim to suffer further? Who rewards it? Why? And while I could get a NPC sept that exists to showcase the evils of xenophobia, I'm deeply disturbed by the idea of players that seek to play this kind of character.

                        In a sept that is more oriented towards integrating the metis (Even if just they need more bodies), the Ragabash pushing this party line will not have universal support for his attitude. He will be in the right to challenge the status quo, and his philosophy might get traction if he can actually get the other Garou to learn something from it. However, if he just picks on the metis or the kinfolk without actually having a point, he will end up losing renown and making himself into a troublemaker that might even be worse then the metis. At least the metis know their place and don't kick a fuss... this guy just drags them out into the spotlight with his crap. And no one wants the metis to be in the spotlight. This might mean that he actually helps the Metis of the Caern with his atitude.

                        In a sept where the metis are treated as mostly equals, I can see the biggest benefit of a contrarian. He runs the risk of being sanctioned by his superiors, but he may have actual wisdom to impart, and when he actually proves a lesson, he will be raking in renown for showing them that while they may not be wrong to embrace the metis, that there are risks and problems that can be prevented by actually thinking why the metis in the past have been a problem.

                        These same thoughts apply to the kinfolk issue as well. The extreme of that argument ends with kinfolk breeding camps to churn out new Garou, and that's not only missing the point of kinfolk, but truly and utterly horrible and beyond anything the Garou would ever do.

                                        No, it's not incompatible. Go for it. It's a piss poor Ragabash that can only "question" things by adhering to some (for a Garou) left wing stereotype agenda.

                                        Odysseus is probably a good example of a Ragabash character in real myth that has trickster like traits, but is essentially on the side of the powers that be. He is the complete henchman of Agamemnon that does all the dirty work, uses guile to get people to do the things he wants them to do, and deceive the enemy.

                                        If you really want to be a "questioner of the ways", there are all sorts of ways to do so. After all, in past times when all Garou treated metis like crap, demeaned kinfolk, etc., Ragabash probably weren't all 21st century Western liberals. They found other ways to question the Garou.

                                        The entire point of the "questioner of the ways" isn't too undermine the Litany or Garou society. It's too make sure their thinking doesn't get so hidebound that they are tricked by their enemies or are unable to adapt.

                                        An easy way to be a "conservative" Garou is to take the "liberal" talking points and bring them to their logical, but absurd, conclusion:

                                        1) Is the sept being too nice to the Metis? Well, they obviously know better than Gaia since she cursed them. What else do they think she did wrong?
                                        2) Is the sept too accepting of kinfolk as equal? Well, maybe it's time for them to stop being slackers and do all the things the Garou do. Which of course will get them killed and therefore useless to the nation.
                                        3) Is the sept too welcoming of bone gnawers? Well, maybe more tribes should be like them - not keep track of their kinfolk, accept criminals and lazy to boost their numbers, care so little of the Litany they engage in cannibalism, etc. Oh, those are actually bad things? Then why are we not holding the Bone Gnawers to the same standards we hold ourselves?

                                        Democracy and egalitarianism has its bad side too - mob rule, lowering of standards, undermining of traditional morality, etc. - just like hierarchical society does.

                                        After all, hasn't the adoption of these modern liberal ideas been the same time the Wyrm has grown in power? Is it just coincidence? Isn't it possible - just possible, it is these ideas that allow evil to flourish in the name of "tolerance"?

                                        If you want more ammo for such a view, do an internet search on the "dark enlightenment". You'll learn a lot of how "neo-reactionaries" think.
