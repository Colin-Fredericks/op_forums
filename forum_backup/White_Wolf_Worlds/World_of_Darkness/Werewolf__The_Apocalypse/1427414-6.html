                    I believe the limitations of tribal acceptance of certain new members is something that is due to both tribal culture separate from the totem and the totem itself.

                    Many years ago when I first joined the forum, I asked this specifically in regards to Silver Fangs and the need for Pure Breed 3. There wasn't any consensus. So it comes to ST fiat based on what they feel is correct given the situation.

                    We know that Griffin simply doesn't accept any homids - period. Not in the tribe and not in the pack. And we get other spirits as pack totems that also reject homids as members. This is clearly something to do with the spirit. We also know that spirits have certain taboos that they cannot violate, or they weaken and fall into slumber. Likely, such bans are related to such things. The spirits in question simply can't be the patron of certain Garou because it somehow violates their very nature.

                    We also know that some discriminating tribes reject candidates out of hand. Stargazers simply won't accept Garou who can't handle certain philosophical inquiries They require a certain level of intelligence/wisdom and interest in such questions. The Get of Fenris don't accept weaklings - their preparation of cubs even leads to death sometimes, and the tribe is find with it as it winnows out the unworthy. At the same time, is there really just a case of the tribal culture, and the totems in question would accept people who are weak in mind or body? I would say no. Chimera just won't accept someone who can't figure out certain problems that require Enigmas. Fenris won't accept physical weaklings. The tribes developed these challenges because they know what their tribal totem's expectations are - and in fact may have witnessed the displeasure of their tribal totem at one point at being presented with unworthy candidates for acceptance into the tribe. This doesn't mean the tribe's selection criteria is perfect - they might cut off candidates who could be acceptable. And they might allow candidates in that fail and anger the totem. But it works well enough in general that the totem is satisfied.

                    Even the Fianna have certain criteria for their members, and they aren't known as a particularly discriminating tribe. They still expect candidates to display certain mastery of skills before they become members. But this may just be cultural, and not something Stag is particularly concerned with (other than as a totem of Respect, he has certain expectations that tribe members present a good image). In this case, slipshod septs might allow members in that violate minimal standards, but the tribe likely acts against such septs before it becomes too much of a problem.

                    Then we have weird things like the Wendigo not accepting candidates who aren't indigenous American Indians. This was even mirrored in the pack totem's ban to help indigenous people's. This seems to have fairly weak IC/setting justification, but was probably included simply because the original designers wanted a tribe exclusively based on Indians as a hook. But there isn't anything obvious about a spirit of terrible winters, and hunger driving people into cannibalism to be "native only." Other than the ridiculous pack totem ban (which I never liked), this clearly seems to be a case of tribal culture. And White Wolf immediately subverted that requirement in second edition corebook when they introduced Evan Heals the Past. Now there may be some justification for this, like somehow members of the Wendigo must be ancestrally linked to one of the original Wendigo tribe members so many thousands of years ago. It could have been part of reciprocal chiminage. But I don't believe any justification was ever developed in the books (though I could be wrong, please enlighten me if so).

                    So there's a spectrum. Every gaming group will come to its conclusions as to where "this" case lies on that spectrum.

                    But if it is possible for Group X, that was previously forbidden to be in the tribe, to become members now, we should ask ourselves why there weren't in previous years? Why haven't we heard about that Silver Fang father who illegally used a rite to make his Pure Breed 2 Garou son into a Silver Fang? Why weren't we told about those Anatolian eunuchs worshipping Cybele that became Black Furies? Were there such cases, but simply hushed up? Or is the evidence of absence in previous books evidence that such things never happened. Or it is only now that such special snowflake style things happen?

                    No doubt we might see stuff like this in future material. But let's ignore that this is just a retcon, and does not reflect what the game originally stated. That's not necessarily a deal breaker for an individual gaming group. There are all sorts of stuff I find stupid in canon and ignore in the games I run. I may think this makes for a better game. But I don't pretend its canon.

                    I think retcons needlessly divide the fanbase and creates all sorts of other problems. We don't need official rulings to comfort a particular group of the fanbase by rejecting existing lore. Just keep silent and allow each individual ST determine what is true in their games.
