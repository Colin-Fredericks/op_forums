    Elder Brother Gifts

    Counting Coup (4): As above.

    Eyes of the Thunderbird (5): Spend 1 Gnosis, roll Manipulation + Occult difficulty 7. For every success you create a bolt of lightning inside your eyes. You can either release all of these bolts at once, or release them as you go, but no matter how many you release at once it counts as an action. The bolt hits automatically and deals 5 dice of Aggravated, so an average of 2-3 Aggravated before soak.

    If for some reason you have lightning inside your eyes when the scene ends, you take 5 dice of Aggravated for every unused bolt. Also, while your eyes are full of lightning, you can only see your intended target and no one else.

    It is not 100% clear whether getting hit by 2 bolts would mean soaking 5 dice of Aggravated twice, or 10 dice of Aggravated once. If it were the latter then it would be called out specifically, however, so we are left to read that getting hit by 5 bolts would mean 5 x 5 dice of Aggravated damage, so say 2, 4, 4, 4, and 0 damage. If the target can't soak Aggravated at all that would be 14 Aggravated, utterly obliterating them. If they can, and that seems quite likely, it could be anywhere from 0-14 Aggravated.

    An Aggravated damage ranged attack is always nice, and being able to shoot a bunch of bolts all at once with no roll to hit is great, but the cost being Gnosis, the roll being difficulty 7, and each bolt dealing 5 dice of Aggravated... this feels like a great Gift to smite weak enemies, and one which will be much, much weaker against powerful foes. And at Rank 5, powerful foes are likely the main problem for a PC in a WTA game.

    Younger Brother Gifts

    Dust Storm (2): This Gift summons a pretty large dust storm, which is actually pretty neat, unlike many weather Gifts which are higher level and worse. If you're in the wrong place for a sandy dust storm then the Gift will substitute leaves, bark, snow, detritus and so on, though this makes it harder to use.

    Roll Gnosis, difficulty based on where you are, minimum 5 in the desert and maximum 10 in a city. On a success you cover an area of up to a mile! Anyone inside the effect halves their Perception dice pools, so for example a Perception + Alertness of 10 would be reduced to 5.

    The storm messes with machinery, puts out small fires, and will sometimes make larger fires grow unexpectedly due to the sudden winds. This is very much an era-specific Gift in some ways, given that desert and plains are more of a battleground in the Wyld West than they are in modern games. The lack of a cost is nice, needing to roll Gnosis can be very nasty for Homids.

    Assuming a good Gnosis pool, such as 3+, and assuming the right setting... this is pretty neat. Could be as bad as Red depending on the location, but Amber feels fair for a rough average. The trouble is that this Gift lasts for 2 turns per success. On a Gnosis roll which may be very, very difficult. I don't care about changing the weather if it lasts for less than 30 seconds. In fact that's more of a Veil risk, since freak weather starting and then immediately stopping is weirder than it carrying on for a few minutes or hours. If this lasted longer it might be Amber, but as it stands it has to be a Red.

    Burning Tumbleweeds (3): This Gift requires tumbleweeds, which actually don't arrive until the 1870s based on my VERY brief research. These species are invasive, by my understanding, but I welcome being corrected if there were any present in the American South prior to introduction of foreign plants.

    Anyways, let's assume that tumbleweeds exist in the world and in the penumbra, because otherwise this Gift won't have been invented yet. The cost is Rage, the roll is pretty easy, but the problem is that we're just told that the weed becomes a rolling fireball and heads in the direction of your choosing due to a stiff breeze. We don't get told what kind of damage it'll do on contact or how far it travels.

    All in all easier to leave this unrated. Having a mining station suddenly be set on fire, with subsequent explosions, is very neat. But we have no mechanics to roll that scenario out. And "I use the Gift, and now the whole camp is on fire" isn't a very satisfying interaction! It'd be more interesting to have the party gather up tumbleweeds, set them on fire, and use a spirit to blow them through town.

    Counting Coup (4): See above. We're told that the Tribe have all but stopped using this Gift, marking their increasingly violent and isolationist stance in the face of the Wyrmbringer invasion.

    Tornado Rider (5): Summon a tornado and ride it! The Gift doesn't let you ride natural twisters unfortunately, which would be a great passive benefit, and very useful in some parts of the Umbra.

    Spend 2 Gnosis, roll Willpower, difficulty 6. Nice dice pool, nice difficulty. The twister then forms on the following turn and makes landfall two turns later... which is a little slow, but still, the results are worth it. You pick the rough direction the twister goes in, and you get to ride it if you wish. It will destroy any object "within reason" which it touches, and inflicts 5 automatic levels of damage to any living thing in its path, who might take even more damage if they're thrown into or off something.

    Finally, the gift ends after a minimum of 4 turns since forming, 3 base +1 per success, so with a Willpower of 8 you might expect roughly 4 successes, resulting in 7 turns of twister mayhem.

    Simply being able to cut a path of destruction through a huge gathering of humans or spirits, destroying carts and wagons and buildings as you go, is very powerful indeed. The image of a Werewolf riding that twister is also extremely intimidating! I'd rate this as a poor method of travel. The tornado will move at 10-20 mph based on very brief research, but possibly as fast as 60 if the wind is blowing the way you want to travel. If the tornado is very fast, and you get a good duration, you might travel 0.7 miles before it fades, or about 1.1km. That makes this a very handy mobility option within a small area, and it's a good way to deal with enemies and obstacles in your path, leaving a trail of destruction for a pack or other allies to follow!

    I'm only rating this Amber because it's expensive to buy at Level 5 and expensive to use at a cost of 2 Gnosis. It's definitely better than the average Level 5 Gift.

            If you were looking to play a character who has access to a variety of other excellent Gifts, combinations include:

            Metis / Ahroun / Children of Gaia - access to Sense Wyrm, combat Gifts and healing Gifts along with many utilities.
            Lupus / Theurge / Fianna - extremely long list of Gifts including Spirit Speech, Sense Wyrm and all of the excellent stuff Fianna get including Persuasion.

I'd much prefer that a player stuck with Glass Walkers, Red Talons, Shadow Lords or Silver Fangs / Younger Brother, and instead explored the relationship with the sub-clan to access their Gifts if it improved the experience or added to the story.

            Remember that WTA is a team-based game, so really don't feel like you need to glean anything from this summary. You could cover a niche which other PCs don't, you don't need to be a Munchkin. That said, I like having lots of options, even if I end up leaving most of them on the table in the long run.

                    Storm-Eater Gifts

                    All of these Gifts exist only so long as the Storm-Eater does, meaning that they do fade from history, though they could still be passed down from Garou to Garou, or potentially learnt in the Umbra.

                    Galliards

                    Song of the Storm (5): This Gift lets you essentially unleash the raw power of creation and change with some sense of direction. I'm reminded of Unleashing in CTD. For example you could point to a dying packmate and intend for them to receive 'help', but whether that takes the form of teleporting them to a safe place, summoning help to defend them, a wave of healing energy, them transforming into a more robust form, or literally any other helpful outcome, is in the hands of the Wyld (and the Storyteller).

                    Identify a target and a goal, spend 2 Gnosis and roll Wits + Expression, and a random event takes place driven by the Wyld nature of the world which drives things towards your goal. The example given is that if you want to stop a train, 1 success might turn the coal to lumps of ice, while 5 successes might turn the track into a giant spider which throws the train off the track and kills the guards.

                    While the effects of this Gift are entirely at the Storyteller’s discretion, remember that it is a Level Five Gift, and should work in the Gift user’s favor. The Galliard’s player should rightly anticipate just how random the Gift’s outcomes may be, but shouldn’t feel discouraged from using such a hard-won prize.
                    This Gift is a great 'help me' button, akin to Totem Gift, and it's an excellent tool for the Galliards. Any Gibbous Moon learning this Gift in the time of the Wyld West would do well to ensure it's passed down to other Elders who are worthy custodians of the power. The side-note on how the power should help the character and leave the player feeling satisfied, along with the roll being pretty achievable at a difficulty of (Gauntlet), makes this pretty great.

                    Black Furies

                    Wyldstorm (5): With this Gift a Black Fury can create a powerful Umbral Storm and push it through the Gauntlet. The resulting storm is very clearly unnatural.

                    Spend 1 Rage and 1 Willpower, and then roll Manipulation + Enigmas against the local Gauntlet. 1 success produces a minute-long storm which might tear apart a camp, while 6+ successes creates the equivalent of a major hurricane which lasts for hours.

                    How to rate this? Using this is a giant Veil Breach, of course, but it's so incredibly destructive that the Veil becomes somewhat moot. If a Rank 5 Black Fury were to, say, destroy the city of New York using this Gift, I think the Veil would be in the top 5 list of concerns, but it might not be in the top 3. This Gift, very unusually, has the potential to kill millions of people.

                    Yes, you won't find a hurricane on record which has killed more people than the low "hundreds of thousands" range, but that's because hurricanes appear as tropical cyclones, progress naturally, make landfall, and people are hunkered down by the time they get there, including evacuations and emergency efforts being prepared. A full-scale giant hurricane with spirit lightning appearing in, say, the most densely populated part of Manila, or right in the middle of London or Tokyo, would create an unprecedented disaster which would most likely have enormous repercussions for the entire world, and the entire Umbra.

                    Given the sheer destructive potential of this Gift I do have to give it a Green, but having a nuke in your pocket is a little tricky since there won't be any situation in which you benefit from using it. This Gift's existence being known could play a role in diplomacy, but not necessarily for good, especially since it's difficult to envision a use of this Gift's upper limit of power which would help the Garou Nation. That said, to be fair, it's easy to imagine using this Gift to destroy a great weapon of war, an aircraft carrier, an oil rig in construction, an area overwhelmed by Fomori, and so on. Just be careful you don't get more successes than you bargained for - consider splitting your dice pool!

                    Iron Riders

                    Quell the Storm (5): This Gift uses the power of the Weaver to quell the Wyld storms of the Umbra. Spend 2 Gnosis, roll Gnosis (difficulty 8). One success would slow a strong wind or stop a breeze, five successes could completely remove an Umbral storm.

                    Alternatively you can target a Wyld spirit. The Gift is the same but instead of rolling Gnosis difficulty 8, the difficulty is equal to the spirit's Gnosis, so anywhere from 3 to 10, most likely somewhere in the middle. The targeted spirit loses 5 Essence per success, making this much worse than an attack, but it's worth noting that a gesture and a word of power completely calming a Wyld spirit until it disappears into stillness is, narratively, very different from ripping it apart with your claws. There may well be Wyld spirits which thrive off the chaos of battle, story-wise, and who are best dismissed with a power like this. Very niche, but possible.

                    Whatever you use the Gift for, it can't target the same storm or spirit more than once in a given scene.

                    Here the difficulty being so high, and the pool being Gnosis, are real issues. Even a difficulty of 7 would be a huge improvement. And while calming a giant Umbral storm is a very big deal, it's also not a common problem to face (well, outside of the reign of the Storm-Eater of course!) and it is very hard to achieve with 5 successes. Even with Gnosis 10, the odds of you getting 5 successes with a difficulty this high aren't great. In fact they're quite bad. Between the cost, the excessively high difficulty, and this being a Level 5 Gift, it sadly gets Red.

                    My suggestion to improve this Gift would be that spirits and shapeshifters who are subservient to a Weaver-associated Totem (Cockroach, Queen Ananasa etc.) make the Gift easier to use. So, for example, if there is a small army of telegraph wire and railroad spirits ready to help calm the storm the difficulty will drop significantly.

                                Wait. Ahadi and Hengeyokai.

                                One last push!

                                Gifts of the Ahadi

                                These Gifts are available to the 'Ahadi', a young organization of shapechangers working together in Africa. Founded to fight against a Simba dictatorship, the Ahadi promote teamwork between the various Fera.

                                If you are a member of the Ahadi you can learn their Gifts at 5x level cost. So these Gifts are essentially always "out-of-list". They can indeed be learnt by various Fera, but for the purposes of this list the most important thing is that they can be learnt by Garou. If the Ahadi are still a very young organization then it's possible that only the lower level Gifts will exist, so ask your ST. And of course these Gifts can make for great inspiration, or you can simply copy them, if you wind up 'making your own' Gift in the course of a game.

                                Finally, a unique thing about these Gifts (at least according to the Mokole, who you would expect to be accurate) is that the new ones all have two animal teachers who work in concert.

                                Rank 1 Ahadi Gifts

                                Blood Webs: Costs 1 Willpower and lasts for a scene. Anyone inflicting Lethal or Aggravated damage against you with a "hand-to-hand" attack needs to make a Dexterity + Brawl roll, difficulty 9 (!), or get covered in sticky red webbing. Every time this happens a character gets a -1 penalty to Dexterity rolls, which accumulates over time.

                                Removing a single point of the penalty takes a full action and a Strength + Athletics (difficulty 7).

                                What this means, essentially, is that if you're being struck repeatedly (say by an enemy with Rage actions) then your foe is going to get less and less agile, hitting less often, dealing less damage, dodging fewer attacks, and getting slower as well. If you have plenty of soak or regen this could be a very good defensive mechanism. Ideally you'd suffer a single level of Lethal damage to trigger this, but unfortunately it's more likely that you'll be taking Aggravated damage when this goes off. I wouldn't suggest deliberately letting yourself get hit! This Gift is a handy debuff against foes which will make them suffer for hurting you, but don't mistake it for something you should directly weaponize, unless you have a means of healing or regenerating Aggravated damage.

                                Solid stacking debuff which will stack well with any difficulty boosts, and wound penalties.

                                Dragon Musk: Produce a powerful musk which helps your pack to find you and upsets enemies.

                                Spend 1 Willpower. For the rest of the scene characters, apart from your 'pack', within 5-25 yards (depending on Rank) add +2 difficulty to all Perception and Stamina rolls, including Soak. Your 'pack' apply a -2 difficulty modifier to finding you.

                                Increasing the soak difficulties of all enemies within 10 yards of you, at Rank 2 for example, by 2 is a really important effect. When you're fighting Spirits with a Willpower of 10, Wyrmish Shapeshifters with a Stamina of 8, turning the difficulty of Soak from 6 to 8 can be a very big difference! Solid green.

                                Ogun's Blessing: Spirits of metal help you with warfare. Spend 1 Willpower and for the rest of the scene you add +1 die and reduce difficulties by 1 when you do anything using a metal object for warfare. Examples given include shooting enemies with a gun, chasing foes with a vehicle, or using a lockpick to break into an ammo dump.

                                Somewhat situational, and certainly more powerful if you're in a country which is at war, but Garou are always at war. A big advantage of this Gift is that it would reduce the difficulty to swing a Klaive by 1, and grant a bonus die to swing it, which for a Dexterity 4 Melee 4 Garou would take their swing of a Grand Klaive from 8 dice difficulty 7 (2.9 successes average) to 9 dice difficulty 6 (3.9 successes average). Your chances to hit also go from 83% to 94%, and the odds that someone will successfully parry or dodge drop significantly.

                                Assuming you make some use of Melee, Firearms, Drive, Athletics (thrown weapons) or similar, chances are this Gift will do quite a lot for only 5xp.

                                Sense Wyrm: See the Rank 1 Metis list. Only noteworthy if you're from a Fera species which doesn't learn Sense Wyrm at all, in which case becoming Ahadi may grant you access.

                                Spirit Speech: See the Rank 1 Theurge list. Ditto; if your Fera species doesn't learn Spirit Speech for some reason then this will help.

                                Three Thousand Tongues*: Understand and speak every African language which is spoken today. Very handy, but doesn't cover reading and writing. There is an activation roll (Intelligence + Academics) but there is no cost to activate, so you can likely get this to work whenever you like. Given how many different languages are spoken in Africa, in an Ahadi game this could be even more important to have than Spirit Speech. Green, with a * naturally.

                                    Rank 2 Ahadi Gifts

                                    Anansi's Guile*: Simply a rename of Beast Speech, and a Rank higher than it normally would be. See the Rank 1 Galliard post. Since this will cost 10xp here, only useful if this Gift would normally be inaccessible. If you can simply learn Beast Speech as an out-of-list Gift for 5xp (since it's a Level 1 Gift) then don't bother with Anansi's Guile, which costs 10xp.

                                    Red based on cost; if it's your only choice for talking to animals then it may be Amber.

                                    Fury Jaws: Spend a point of Rage prior to biting. If the bite is successful then the target takes a -3 dice penalty to anything they try to do other than attack you, due to the intensity of the itch from the bite. This lasts for a number of rounds equal to your Rank +1, so 3-6.

                                    Assuming 3 rounds... it's alright? Forcing someone to focus fire on you, especially an enemy which isn't going to want to punch or claw or chase after you, is pretty neat. Use this on a tricksy enemy and 'taunt' them into only using attacking moves, nice. It's a handy option. But a -3 dice penalty isn't impossible to overcome, and the duration is pretty short. Decent concept, stronger and stronger at higher Ranks, worth getting if you're keen on bites.

                                    Impenetrable Veil*: This Gift strengthens the Delirium response. Roll Appearance + Primal-Urge (difficulty 7), and for every success reduce the Willpower of onlookers for the purposes of Delirium by 1. So, for example, with 5 successes a Willpower 8 human (Curiosity) will be changed to the Willpower 3 response (Disbelief).

                                    Handy ability to help you to overcome ordinary humans. No cost, and the roll is manageable. Solid Amber, could be Green in a game where human enemies and threats are absolutely key.

                                    River's Bosom: Costs 1 Gnosis, requires a roll, and grants you situational invisibility. So long as you remain perfectly still and submerged in water you're invisible. Neat, but a Gift this niche shouldn't cost Gnosis. Attacking ends the Gift, naturally.

                                    Spirit of the Fish: See the Rank 2 Elder Brother list. Useful if you normally would not be able to access this Gift at all.

                                                Rank 5 Ahadi Gifts

                                                Marriage of the Sun and Moon: Go from being weak to silver to being weak to gold, or visa versa. Since we are ranking these Gifts for Garou, what does it mean to go from being weak to silver to being weak to gold?

Damage you can now soak, to be fair, so if you have a good soak pool that could make Klaives significantly less deadly. Carryin silver will now have no effect on your Gnosis, so you can weigh yourself down with insane quantities of silver bling, silver Fetishes, silver ammo and as many Klaives as you can get your hands on.

                                                Hunters are going to be less of a threat, as are angry Kinfolk, certain Garou enemies, various supernaturals who appreciate the importance of silver (Mages, Vampires, Changelings etc.).

                                                The problem is that this Gift costs 2 Gnosis and lasts for a single scene. Not until the next sunrise, not a day, not 12 or 24 hours, a scene. Sadly that makes this pretty bad. Yeah, you can trigger it when someone with a Grand Klaive runs at you, but it's still going to slow you down by wasting a turn of Rage actions. Red, but with a tiny tweak on duration this might even be Green.

                                                Thousand Forms: Unfortunately this makes Brother's Skin somewhat redundant. That said, Brother's Skin allows the user to become Crawlerling Form (if Rank 4+ with a Werespider in the 'pack'), and it would allow a Garou to enter a form outside of Thousand Forms' size limit, if applicable. So if one of your 'pack' has a natural animal form which is bigger than a bison then Brother's Skin might see another use there. This Gift is wonderful all the same, and really speaks to the spirit of the Ahadi; an Ahadi Elder who can take the forms of almost every beast on Gaia's back is a representation of the unity the Changing Breeds need.

                                                Anyways, for more on this Gift, see the Rank 5 Ragabash post.

                                                Unity of All Beasts: Choose one of your Gifts. That Gift becomes a 'bonus' Gift offered by your Totem, but only one member of the group (besides yourself) may use it at a time.

                                                So, for example, you could put a Gift like Mother's Touch into the pool. Now in any given turn any member of your 'pack' may use Mother's Touch. If you decide to replace Mother's Touch with a combat Gift, say, Spirit of the Fray, then with each passing round one of your allies would benefit from the +10 to Initiative bonus of the Gift.

                                                The ability to nudge one of your rare or unusual Gifts into the pool so another ally can wield it, possibly better than intended by combining it with their strange Forms and Gifts, is amazing. It's also easy to imagine that this Gift-sharing would make it easier for your allies to later learn the Gifts, if the ST allowed them to be taught with the 'hard way' method.

                                                        Hengeyokai

                                                        Garou in the Beast Courts gain a few changes worth noting.

                                                        - Hengeyokai Garou receive access to the General Hengeyokai Gift List and the an additional Auspice List, both cost 4x Current Level
                                                        - They can exchange one of their three starting Gifts for a General Hengeyokai Gift
                                                        - They can exchange their starting Auspice Gift for a Hengeyokai Auspice Gift

                                                        Assuming there is at least one General and one Auspice Gift you want, the exchange is sensible. What you're losing costs 3xp, what you're gaining costs 4xp. Exceedingly character-optimization brained, but I like noting these things.

                                                        Before we get into what these lists are, I do want to address a side-bar I find funny:

                                                        WAIT A MINUTE, MY HAKKEN GETS FIVE GIFT LISTS?

                                                        Effectively, yes. Two of those lists (general Hengeyokai and Beast Court Auspice) are more expensive than native Gifts, but they’re still less expensive than, say, picking up a Silver Fang Gift.

                                                        So what gives? Why are hengeyokai so much stronger than Western shapeshifters?

                                                        The answer has nothing to do with Asian mysticism or superiority, and everything to do with history and hard work. The Changing Breeds elsewhere in the world have spent all of history since the War of Rage in isolated, balkanized enclaves when they weren’t either going to ground to avoid discovery altogether - or tearing one another’s throats out. The Fera retreated from the world, mistrustful of the Rage of the werewolves and of each other.

                                                        The hengeyokai, while not without their shortcomings and problems, managed to hold it together to a far greater degree. They came together and built something. They took their strength, and made it the strength of their brothers. Beast Court characters have more advantages than other shapeshifters because they’re the beneficiaries of thousands of years of hard-won unity and back-breaking spiritual labor undertaken by their ancestors. Because of this, Beast Court characters have some perks to emphasize this point - unity and understanding bring concrete benefits, while the wage of infighting is death. Perhaps, if there is any time left before the Apocalypse, the Sunset People could learn from the Hengeyokai.
                                                        I find this quite charming. "It isn't that they have 5 Gift lists because they're better, they have 5 Gift lists because they're better!"

                                                        I'm being facetious, and I appreciate that the history is very different. Ultimately it's totally harmless that Asian Garou have a leg up; there are fewer Tribe choices (only two that I'm aware of - Hakken and Stargazer, and maybe Boli Zouhisze at a push, though they aren't members of the Beast Courts) and the bonus Gifts are only slightly cheaper than they would be otherwise. The main benefit, in fact, isn't to do with experience, it's to do with accessing Gifts you'd otherwise struggle to justify accessing. Getting ahold of the most important Gift associated with a Breed, Auspice or Tribe requires clout, and it risks looking like you're trying to act outside the role you're expected to act within. So your Metis Hakken Ahroun being able to get Persuasion is a pretty big deal. It costing 4xp instead of 5xp barely matters, but the fact that they didn't have to plead their case like a loser is a big advantage.

                                                        Hengeyokai General Gifts

                                                        Remember that these cost 4x Level xp, so the main improvement is that you can access them easily. I will note that I'm not a fan of a 'General' list existing for the Beast Courts, since it makes some Gifts so ubiquitous that it lowers the stakes and makes the setting feel less cohesive (really hard to imagine certain threats flourishing in a place where every shapeshifter can get Sense Wyrm for 3-4xp!), but that's personal taste.

                                                        Balance (1): See the Rank 1 Stargazer post.

                                                        Create Element (1): See the Rank 1 Metis post.

                                                        Mindspeak (1): See the Rank 1 Galliard post.

                                                        Sense Wyrm (1): Ah great, now everyone has it. A sign of a society with its priorities in order, to be fair. See the Metis Rank 1 post!

                                                        Blaze Talons (2): Costs 1 Gnosis, roll Strength + Primal-Urge. For every success your forelimbs are set afire for two rounds. Damaging Brawl attacks using your forelimbs gain one automatic level of Aggravated damage.

                                                        How good is a bonus level of Aggravated? It's roughly akin to +2 dice, but more consistent. Fire also scares Vampires and various other threats.

                                                        Courtly Speech (2): Renamed from Speech of the World. See the Rank 2 Homid list.

                                                        Resist Temptation (2): See the Rank 2 Stargazer list.

                                                        Sense Balance (2): See the Rank 3 Philodox list. Accessing this early is a big improvement, and here you're paying 8xp (2 x 4) instead of 9 or 15xp (3 x 3 or 3 x 5).

                                                        I rated Sense Balance as Amber with a * marking it as a potential Red, depending on the table. Getting it at Rank 2, and for 8xp instead of 9xp, makes it a little bit better. But honestly if you're Peeking through the Umbra and you can't tell an area is heavily Weaver or Wyld aligned, or you can't tell a Drone/Gorgon from a regular person, you may need to invest into Perception and Abilities before you think about sensing Triat energies with Gifts. I maintain that most of the time you're better off relying on common sense and inferring from what you can see in the two worlds.

                                                        Still, solid Amber here, especially given the variety of relationships with the Triat held within the Beast Courts.

                                                        Clarity (3): See the Rank 3 Stargazer list.

                                                        Part the Wall (3): Irrelevant to Garou; this Gift allows those who normally wouldn't be able to step sideways to do so. Unless you're a Metis whose 'deformity' is that you can't step sideways on your own, this Gift won't do anything for you.

                                                        Waking Dream of Unity (3): Spend 1 Willpower, up to 30 willing individuals present at activation enter into silent telepathic communion. Excellent power for managing large battles and perhaps a social event or similar. Great for espionage.

                                                        Exorcism (4)*: See the Rank 3 Theurge list.

                                                        Mindblock (4): See the Rank 4 Silver Fang list.

                                                        Walking the Bone Road (4): Modifies the Stepping Sideways mechanic so that you can step sideways into the Low Umbra instead of the Middle Umbra. The main utility of this, for my money, is being able to use stepping sideways to escape from enemies who can step sideways themselves. If you're fighting a Garou, as a Garou, and they vanish into thin air, well you're going to step sideways to chase them... only to find they're nowhere to be seen.

                                                        Incredibly strong ability, well worth getting on anyone who is allowed to purchase it. The bonus utility of being able to easily access a whole different layer of reality is also neat. The best use of accessing the Low Umbra in a typical Werewolf game is avoiding Banes whilst also avoiding the real world, in my book, but that's still an excellent result.

                                                        Elemental Gift (5): See the Rank 5 Lupus list.

                                                        Way of Ten Thousand Cities (5): Renamed from Assimilation. See the Rank 5 Homid list.
