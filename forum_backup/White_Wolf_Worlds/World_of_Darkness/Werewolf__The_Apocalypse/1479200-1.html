    Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
    In my chronicles, I treat man eating in a similar way as drug abuse. In the general Garou population, about 5% of Garou have eaten people in the past five years, and 1% are pretty hard core ongoing eaters. Overall, there's probably 10-15% of Garou who have done it at some point in their lives. Incidents are probably higher among the Ronin.

    Now some of those Garou only did so by accident, or just once or a few times and never did so again. (It frequently happens with First Changers/Lost Cubs before the Garou find them and teach them how to control it.) But it's done enough that the crime is in the Litany for a reason. The notorious camps like the Man Eaters, Eaters of the Dead, and other canonical groups make up most of the 1% who do so continuously. But the rest is spread throughout the Garou tribes. The difference between being an addict who needs help and an internal enemy that needs to be destroyed mostly depends on whether they justify it (like the Man Eaters and Eaters of the Dead do) or admit they have a problem.

    The Garou break their own law just as much as there is crime in any human society. Most Garou follow the law. They aren't hypocrites. But there's always a criminal element of varying degrees. And most of their criminals don't become traitors. Just because they break one or more precepts of the Litany doesn't mean they're falling to the Wyrm.

    I like a certain amount of "normal" criminal behavior that results in Philodoxes actually using the Rites of Punishment in the sept. Otherwise the Philodox have a lot less to do.
    I personally do think this happens at times, First Change being common. But the playerbase I play with is close enough to their kinfolk and human side that I don't think it would work with them. There just isn't as much of a disconnect between human and garou to justify it not being seen as a Great Horror.

    Mind, Red Talons and some Ronin communities definitely do engage in willing cannibalism in my HC. Red Talons because they do not accept the Litany as written and the Ronin because that's what werewolves do, right?

    Also, agreed on Wolf being a good example of garou vs human behaviour.

They found the new character struggling for control (the player didn't know what game he'll be playing, he only knew that the setting will be modern and I tried hard to convince him that his character becomes psychotic) and bring him to the Foundation. Then one day I run a one player story for him (the therapy) and another for the pack who tried to cover their tracks from new guy's family on one hand and on the other tried to find out if they are Kinfolks (they weren't, he was an effect of his mother's romance). But at the same time his father was gradually slipping into Wyrm services as an Omni TV employee. In the end, when the Foundation staff agreed for a meeting, the new guy face the same choice as Nicholson: either he will return to human society (they will supress his nature with a fetish) or accept his nature and help his parents.                                                 For many septs, it's not something I bother with at all. But sometimes I'll decide that 1 or 2 members of some sept are secret man eaters (or some other crime). Sometimes it's just background description that doesn't mean anything (OK, you travel to the next step to share the news, and you learn that one of its members have recently been ostracized after being discovered to have eaten someone). That adds some flavor to the game, and very often causes the PCs to engage more with the NPCs. Or it's a sandbox element the PCs could discover and be the ones to bring that Garou to justice. But these crimes are incidental along the same lines of discovering a friend, coworker, or family member was secretly a meth addict.

I really liked the character of Bone Juice in Rage Across New York, and wanted to see additional NPCs like that in other Rage books (like Mother Pasta in Australia).
