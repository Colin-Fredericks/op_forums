    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    I mean, I personally imagine the Garou should leave an imprint on human society rather than just the Spirits themselves.
    I agree that it makes sense, but then there are some problems from a Doylist perspective:

    1- You have to account for the other splats, and then you run into the problem of how much you'll let each be the factor behind something and how it'll translate into the others. Can you answer this without pissing off some players more than it's worth?

    2- You have to take into account the impact of such interpretations on irl cultures. The Wendigo are in the current place, but the first big crisis was actually the Get of Fenris, and a lot of players still don't take well the whole thing. Then Chechnya remembers us what ignoring real causes to invent fictional ones can look like.

    3- You'll have a lot of work avoiding stupidity. Again, look at the High Tongue for an example of a mess, as few players I know really buy the idea that a salad of cool words is actually derived from the same proto-language and werewolves from far and wide use them in the same way for tradition.

    Not that it isn't a valid undertaking, just expressing that making it into actual canonical material is hard. And here we have the added problem of the setting being already running, so thinker with it without overruling previous canon doesn't makes things easier.

    Personally I like how Forsaken dealt with the Tongue aspect, keeping to faux-Summer instead of grabbing random worlds all around the place. But it is only one possible take and have its obvious limits.

                            #3: The High Tongue isn't a Fianna invention. All Garou have an instinctive knowledge of it upon their First Change. Since it's an innate part of them, it has to be tied to their creation, which in WtA cosmology would mean the great spirits of Gaia and Luna or the pre-imbalance Triat level of spirit. It's not something the spirits actively teach them, but something their creators put in them.

                            That said, while there's no mechanics to this beyond rolling dice for certain skill checks that would be true for any language, the Fianna are the Tribe describe being most active in elaborating the High Tongue into a more expressive language over the base level of mutual understanding all Garou have, and spreading their version of it to the Garou Nation at large. This gets to the idea of the High Tongue developing dialects; the core language remains the same for every Garou, but artistic flair, cadence, idioms, and so on can develop among Garou communities. Russian High Tongue would be noticeably different from the Fianna based High Tongue of Europe its colonial descendant countries, as would the High Tongue of the Pure Lands, the Emerald Courts, and so on.

                            The intrinsic core language makes sense as something that keeps them from diverging to the point of being separate languages, but otherwise this sort of drift is actually fairly normal; not just in humans, we see this in most socially verbal animals where vocalizations are taught on some level.
