          I don't like the use of the word "Lost Cub". WtA seemed to use the term for cubs, who weren't raised in close proximity to the tribe (and most likely aware of their werewolf heritage before the Change). Garou who on the other hand did not undergo a first change for some reason were called Lunatics. The way they use "Lost Cub" now, is more akin to the later, than the first. And while the mechanics allow for cubs of all ages, which is good for LARP, I find the terminology unsatisfactory.

          Also, the Delta slice clearly states, that Lost Cubs (and by extension possibly all pre-Change garou) can have off-spring with Garou. The way it is written it implies non-metis. I find this is wrong. It is a deviation from what 2nd edition suggested and revised clearly states towards the mess that W20 made. This change is totally not necessary. I furthers the image of the first change as some event that changes a person spiritually and possible genetically, rather than the breaking free of a condition one was born with.

          Also, Lost Cubs are described as being second class garou, although it is not mechanically that they are weaker or such. So it establishes another in-setting need to look down on the PCs of other players because the Setting says so. I found that most players have a hard time treating a metis or bone gnawers like a second class garou, but doing so with other homids, that are indistinguishable from themselves seems like a bad design choice that gets hand-waved to move on.

          The Bite itself seems pretty straight forward. It allows to change a Lost Cub or Kinfolk into a garou. Costs a gnosis. The "victim" makes a Mental+Survival challenge against 10 or dies. If successful there will be a 28 day sickness and then a first change. This first change will determine auspice, so garou now can pass produce garou of a certain auspice with an incubation periode of one month. That is great.
          Auspices before were omens, signs that indicate future prospects and a garou usually had the soul/demeanour/apptitude for a the auspice he was born under. Certain Rites not withstanding garou couldn't choose their auspice. But now, a kinfolk can ask a garou to bite her during the full moon and 28 days later (one lunar cycle) she will fist change into an ahroun. If Mr. Ashtray would have know that it was that easy. For the Bite is according to most theories something that has always be possible.
          The following sentence made me smile: "Once the septs began to reform, they quickly discovered a tidal wave of Lost Cubs, in greater numbers than had ever been seen before. Evidence quickly mounted implying that the majority of these new Lost Cubs came to be as the result of a werewolf biting a seemingly random human."
          As if a bunch of werewolf would bite humans or kinfolk on a regular basis, in a way that they survive (so not in combat, where one would punch/bite again, if one bite wasn't enough to kill) and invest a Gnosis for no reason at all. This then leads to a mystical sickness and first change, which in turn leads to more people being bitten. Sounds silly. I mean, during sex one might invest a Gnosis in one's actions to, well, make the experience more spiritual and also one might just bite the partner a little, if one likes that. But besides that, situations where garou bit people, who in turn became garou in numbers that translate to "tidal wave" just don't happen, do they ?


          All in all the Bite is a way to turn Kinfolk and Lost Cubs into garou. It serves a means in LARP, as 30-something players possibly also need starting characters and you probably don't want to start as a regular late-bloomer cub, if you just want to try out the game. So, you get a pre-change Lost Cub, play two or three sessions and get the Bite after that. But this whole problem is not new and LARP chronicles have dealt with that need before.
          As I said at other topics, and during W20 open development, one should have just changed 'Scent of the true form' to require a roll to detect pre-change garou (instead of no roll to detect all-kinds of garou). Then it would be perfectly reasonable to have garou mis-detected as kin or even kin mis-detected as garou. All kinds of strange character backstories would have been possible. Lunatics were also still around, so the change was in no edition required to be at the age of puberty. As for forcing the first change, garou could have been creative with inducing stress situations, using their hulking crinos forms and intimidation based fear-inducing gifts. There really was no need to invent and possibly retcon into existence, a bite leading to a one month illness leading to a fist change or death.
          Also, the Bite is a great way to torture supernaturals, like mages or formori, possibly even vampires, who suffer one month of pain, thinking death would be a better option, until they emerge unchanged. Also, it is a death sentence for any human.


          All in all, it is a huge change in the WtA Setting and I don't like change in the Setting. I like change in the rules to better underline the Setting and to make the game smoother.

                  As for the Delta Slices rules on the Curse. I like that the extend to which the Curse affects people is tied to the time the person is exposed to the garou, not willpower. It is also only in effect if the garou gained Rage within the last 24 hours, which is fine. I feel it should apply to all animals, not just wolves, and it should affect other supernaturals. Kinfolk are now immune, not just lesser effected, which makes sense if you want kin-players in your LARP game. The Curse is lesser in Glass Walkers and Bone Gnawers (for humans only) and Red Talons (for wolves), which I find okay, although I think Glass Walkers don't care about the humans much (more about inventions, the city as a living thing, and such) and Children of Gaia should have the lesser Curse instead.
                  I don't like that the Curse is tied to Impergium and Delirium in the write-up, as opposed to being solely tied to Rage. Also a short sentence on how the other Fera cause Curse would have been nice.

                  Actually, a short sentence about the other fera would be nice at the end of all universal rules, like Frenzy or Wyrm Taint and so on. Could just be like: "These mechanics apply also to Feras A, B and D. C and E have different systems in their write-ups at page XX."

                              The downtime rules are nice. Would houserule some stuff, but I like the examples. They help putting stuff into perspective.

                              A system for tracking by scent is also nice, something that was kind of fuzzy before. I find there should be examples on how certain conditions change difficulty, like freshness of scent and such.

                              I really like the renown mechanics. They allow for renown to be spend to influence other garou indefinite ways. Certain gifts or sept offices and such might allow for additional ways to use renown traits. Others can refuse with Willpower or defined other mechanics, but such is considered inappropriate in most cases.
                              There are, instead of temp. and perm. renown, not innate, abiding and fleeting renown. Innate corresponds to your rank and returns (either every session or every day, text has both ways mentioned). Abiding is similar to that, but it comes from circumstances other than rank and is treated as innate only if this circumstances prevail. Fleeting renown is not regained automatically, so if you spend it, its gone. New renown in this category is either earned from the sept or global positions, awarded by the ST or other circumstances (like a Galliard telling an inspiring tale about you during moot).

                              EDIT: Quickly browsed over the Moinkers, but it looked good. Way simpler and still more comprehensive than the renown loss/gain tables from the tabletop.

                                      I like the Challenge section and the examples of what can be part of a contest of gamecraft.

                                      THE GAROU NATION
                                      Well, here is some kind of revised history at works. "High King" was usually used for the Arg Righ, that Fianna-King. No it is used for the bearer of the Silver Crown, stating: "Historically, the Nation has been led by High Kings from the Silver Fangs. The authority of these rulers was conferred to them by their possession of a powerful fetish called the Silver Crown, and by the support of the Council of Tribes. Structured in many ways like a sept, but on a much larger scale, the Garou Nation is both a global community and a standing army devoted to the sole purpose of protecting Gaia and defending the Material Realm from the Wyrm."
                                      But that is not how WtA was, was it?
                                      I mean, there was no bearer of the Silver Crown between the fall of house Winter Snow and Albrechts return of the fetish. Also there wasn't a Concil of tribes on a global (or even western) scale. The four classical houses in a Glass Walker Sept (Rightful justice, urban defense, etc..) had global meetings, and the Black furies hat their tribal council, but an all tribes senate like body was never mentioned in WtA.
                                      And it doesn't fit with the supposed conflicts the tribes hat with each other, and their battle for territories.

                                      "The High King stands at the pinnacle of authority within the hierarchy of the Nation. He has many privileges and responsibilities that range from leading the Garou as a whole to war, to banishing a member from the Nation and rendering them Ronin. It is his responsibility to be an example to the rest of the Nation and lead them in the unending war against the Wyrm. He may also assign special missions to the Silver Pack, and call upon the aid of the Legends of the Garou to ensure even the most difficult and dangerous of tasks is accomplished."
                                      That doesn't fit with how Ronin were presented or Silver Packs.

                                      "A councilor chosen from each tribe within the Nation represents the beliefs and desires of her tribe to the High King as well as offering counsel on the myriad decisions that must be made every day in order to keep balance and order between the septs throughout the world. A position on the Council of Tribes also offers a limited check on the power of the High King, assuming that the tribal councilors can agree on what those limits should be. The role of councilor is a powerful one within a tribe’s own structure, for it is the councilor for a tribe that may cast out one of her own tribe members, rendering them Ronin from their tribe, though not the Nation as a whole. The councilor for a tribe may also forgive one who has been made Ronin, welcoming them back into the embrace of their tribal totem and family."
                                      Here they are, werewolf own Justicars. Or Camarilla inner circle members. Does the tribal council also meet in Venice?
                                      Actually they meet in the High Kings home sept, it's later in the text...
                                      Albrecht and his pack vanished, supposedly sacrificing them self's to protect the remaining caerns when the Age of Apocalypse started. Phoenix predicted their return, but the Sivler Crown is once again disappeared.
                                      I wonder how long it will take for an game by BNS or White Wolf to elect a new High King...
                                      From a design standpoint I can't make sense why this was written as is...

                                          So, moving on...

                                          "Although they only officially serve as advisors to the High King, the Councilor from each tribe is also considered the highest authority for her own tribe."
                                          So, every tribe has a Arg Righ now?
                                          What about the Black Furies with their strange governing body, or the Children of Gaia with their dual-leadership?

                                          "The Council of Tribes is responsible for interpreting the Litany, and it can even make adjustments to these laws with a unanimous vote from its members."
                                          Yeah, like "Don't get caught"
                                          But seriously, wasn't it a plot point in the Silver Crown Novel that Gaia/Luna gave the inspiration to the Litany to the garou in Pangea, for they could not ever get unanimous vote with their tribal differences? Sure, the older DA werewolf states, that the Veil was later added to the Litany by Silver Fang decree, but that statement is iirc never repeated. Having even the theoretical ability to change the Litany seems wrong to me.

                                          It follows a list of the Council members, the Margrave, Roger Daley (formerly presumed dead) and Bronn MacFionn being the only ones I recognized...


                                          It follows the description of the two faction that now make the garou nation: The Sanctum of Gaia and the The Concordat of Stars.
                                          The former is traditionalist and seeks victory over the Wyrm without the help of Fera or humans. Their way has proven effective against Harano that was plaguing the garou. Spirits from the dawn times should be the garou's only allies. They are realists enough to understand that they wouldn't make it without weaver-creations, but avoid their use as often a possible. Wearing only self-made cloths from animal-hide, they hunted themselves makes them interesting to LARP.
                                          "The Sanctum of Gaia points to the Ages of Myth and Legend, when humanity and werewolves alike lived in a symbiotic relationship, with the Garou guarding the land and the creatures Gaia birthed within it."
                                          That is interesting, was that before, after or during the Impergium? Well, my standpoint on the Impergium is something I posted elsewhere already.

                                          "The Sanctum reluctantly participates in the recent cease-fire between werewolves and vampires."
                                          I smell cross-over LARPing....

                                          Its member tribes include the Silver Fangs, the Fianna, Get of Fenris, Wendigo, Red Talons, and the Black Furies.

                                          Concordat of Stars consists of the Shadow Lords, Stargazers, Glass Walkers, Bone Gnawers, Children of Gaia, and the Uktena.
                                          Leaving the Silent Striders unaligned emissaries and diplomats - and messengers (since the Sanctum is not using modern means of telecomunication, they are suddenly necessary again...)

                                          This faction believes in working with the fera to re-balance the triat. When their members and some Gurahl discovered the shard seeds, they gained membership and influence to rival the Sanctum of Gaia.

                                          "They seek out other supernatural creatures, such as mages, fae, and even vampires, to create alliances and treaties, supporting the overall goal of ensuring that a world remains in which they can all find a way to survive. They point to these alliances, including vampires coming to the aid of a caern under siege and driving away the minions of the Wyrm, as proof that such cooperation can and will succeed."
                                          Smells like more cross-over friendly LARP. Or even tabletop in the "one world of darkness"...

                                          And there are the Antititrubu, of course, meaning, that if you want to be part of the faction not being the majority of your tribe, buy the "Camp Defector" merit.

                                          "Fera may choose to undergo the Rite of Acceptance and receive membership within a sept. These shapeshifters are then considered members of the Garou Nation, with all the rights and responsibilities that entails."
                                          So, the Council I never heard of before had a tight vote to turn the garou nation into the Beast Courts (light). Great. Moving from playing werewolf to were-whatever for the sake of cross-over.


                                          With so much time explaining how the tribal council would not get an unanimous for for a new High King, it is kind of silly to give a page to High King versus Council conflict. Well, turns out the Council might veto the King with unanimous vote. It all read to me like "Obama versus Congress" or "House of Cards" for garou. Wasn't it a major plotpoint in Silver Crown, that the crown could command any garou? How would than someone vote unanimously against the king?

                                          The Council versus Septs page is then basically telling us a Sept Leader in a Prince and a justicar is higher up than he is, although the Sept Leader/Prince is basically the local ultimate authority, except when he isn't. Argh. Moving on.

                                          Ronin now are only Ronin if expelled from High King or Council. Septs banishments are only temporary. Good to know, that the governing body that didn't exist in WtA before had such power, and that simply leaving the garou nation doesn't make you a Ronin, as before, but you actively need to be thrown out by a global committee (or at least the guy who represents your tribe at this committee).


                                          All in all the whole Garou Nation chapter sucked. I liked WtA in 2nd and partly revised, when the garou lived in localised septs and cared for their region. Global cabals and government bodies weren't needed and left to VampireTM and MageTA. All this is presented as if it had always been there, not as something that developed with the Age of Apocalypse. This makes me wonder if 'Shattered Dreams' will retcon us from beloved TNG/DS9/VOY-universe to ChrisPine-universe and explain all these stuff that according to Martin were written with his participation...

                                          Really, I hope the rebrand that stuff as "Werewolf: Age of Apocalypse" so I can tell people, we play "the Apocalypse" not "Age of Apocalypse" and they will get the difference.

                                                      Sept Chapter is next.
                                                      I liked the different titles for Sept Leaders by tribe. With the Age of Apocalypse most septs are mult-tribal. Sept offices have certain ways to spend renown, as mentioned above. The some sept and moot offices now double as the council of Elders of a sept, now named council of auspice. They also have a kind of legislative power, confirming some Sept Leader decisions (like wartime, within 24 hours of being declared) and other sept positions.

                                                      From a LARP standpoint that makes sense, involving as many players as possible with certain duties. Also, the sept offices have each some bulletin points on what their duties are, and this reads fairly good.

                                                      All in all the sept chapter is a nice read and fairly compatible with WtA, as it was before. The proceedings of a tribal court and trail are presented and there is some information in punishment. Only quickly browsed most of it, but it all reads ok.

                                                      Next chapter is Packs. It presents several types of packs, how they work amongst themselves and what roles one has to fulfil in a given pack. It is as I quickly browsed over it a nice read. Not really necessary,but a fairly good inspiration for different group dynamics and I guess can be used for tabletop as well, if one wants to design NPC packs with certain uncommon structures.

                                                      So, this all follows with mechanics like Rites and Ideas for story development. I'm not going to read them now....
