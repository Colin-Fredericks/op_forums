    Is Command Spirit broken?

    05-03-2022, 10:29 AM
    In our last fight the PC's were facing a bunch of banes. The theurge used command spirit on the biggest, beat its gnosis with one success, and commanded it to attack all the others. In the next encounter he did the same with a drattosi.

    I'm wondering if there's more nuance to this that I'm missing? Can Command Spirit be used to order banes to attack each other that simply? Getting one success against their gnosis rating is relatively easy, and it feels like it's made spirit combat (other than swarming the PC's with dozens of banes) much less dangerous/interesting?

      I think it is one of the many Gifts that suffers from being ambiguous. Depending on your ST it can feel extremely weak, or broken, because what "one command" means has no guidance to it.

      The big control on Command Spirit is that it costs 1 WP per command (and you can only spend 1 per turn without some other power increasing your potential there). If your ST lets "fight all other bane's present," as one command? Yeah, that's pretty broken. If each "spend an action to attack that bane," is one command, it's questionable how great of a use of your Gift that really is; esp. since the other enemies can hear you barking orders and even if they are surprised at first (though it is just a level 2 Gift so... not that surprised) they should catch on quick to what's going on and adjust.

              Command Spirit is like Dominate, but the spirit knows it is being compelled. There are a few things to remember.

              It takes Willpower spend for each command given. Each successive command requires another Willpower. While not mentioned in the Gift, I would assume something like commanding a Bane to attack each other would only be effective for one round, not ongoing until one or the other was defeated. If the Theurge wants the Bane to keep attacking, I'd have them spend a Willpower every round. After all, the Bane does not want to do this. (If it was a Gaian spirit that was warlike and prone to supporting the PC, that spirit - even if reluctant at first to engage in battle - might continue to attack. But a Bane would clearly want to resist.)

              Clear wording is essential. Spirits would need to obey the letter of the command, not its spirit. And hostile spirits will always attempt to foil the Theurge's control over them by exploiting any leeway in the interpretation. So at the very least, the Bane must attack the others. But it doesn't need to use its best Charms. It doesn't need to roll its entire Dice Pool (some roleplay comes into this, a Scrag might attack full on because that it's nature, but a more cunning and smarter Bane might not). Or maybe it attacks the Bane, but also the Garou by employing a Charm that can affect multiple targets. You only need to have the spirit obey the letter of the command, nothing more.

              Now once that happens, players begin to do annoying things like say "fight to your full potential" or similar crap in an attempt to defeat the spirit's evasion of the Theurge's intent. But you can twist that around too. Have it leave so it can slumber first and regain all its lost Essence, but don't worry in several years time it'll come back and attack those other Banes. Or it says it can't attack at it's full potential unless the PCs donate it Gnosis since it already spent some temporary Gnosis. Or similar things. Attaching conditions to commands that would otherwise be concise can often backfire.

              Another issue is when players begin to recite legal paragraphs in attempt to clearly communicate what they want. Remember that a combat turn is only about 3-5 seconds. If they insist on being a lawyer, remind them that the first 3-5 seconds of reading is done and move to the next person. That's what they did with their turn. When it comes back next turn, they can add another 3-5 seconds and so on.

              As an ST, you reward the behavior that you want from your PCs, and don't reward behavior you don't want. You want to make sure a gift like Command Spirit works the way you want, not the way your players may want.

              Talk with the players to make sure you are on the same page. Most players can come to an agreement with an ST as to the scope. And one thing I often do when there is an interpretation difference between what the PCs want and I want, is to let them know the same standard is going to apply to them. If they want a real fight between that big Bane and other Banes from a use of Command Spirit, what happens if a BSD Theurge commands the pack totem to attack the Garou? Or when a vampire uses Dominate on them to fight each other. I find players become a lot more reasonable in their interpretations when they are talking about standards for everyone, not just what is most advantageous for this scene.

              Now, spirits twisting things around mostly comes about when the spirit is hostile towards the Theurge. Friendly or allied spirits may sometimes need to be coerced, but are more likely to obey the spirit of the command even if they would prefer the Theurge went about such requests through chiminage rather than coercion. But to a certain extent, the spirits would recognize the Garou as fighting for Gaia and the natural authority of Theurges - after all Command Spirit is not some forbidden lore, but a standard low level Gift the spirits agreed to teach a Theurge. Friendly spirits can always complain afterwards that they felt the Garou acted inappropriately. So I'd limit twisting the Theurge's commands to hostile spirits (which might be more than just Banes, a Pegasus spirit might be hostile towards a Get of Fenris theurge, or a Griffin spirit to any homid Garou), or if the spirit really, really, doesn't want to do that they're commanded. Likewise, a Bane might want to fulfill the Garou's command if it is something they'd want to do anyway, or they think they can begin corrupting the Garou.

                      A spirit's Gnosis can be all over the place. By itself, it does not indicate how powerful or useful a spirit is. And they certainly weren't designed for such a rule in mind.

                      Let's look at the 4 Elemental Wyrms:
                      Hoglings - 7
                      Furmlings - 5
                      H'rugglings - 5
                      Wakshaani - 7

                      Should Rank 3 Garou be able to Command Furmlings and H'ruggling, but not Hoglings and Wakshaani? Is there any real reason a Rank 2 Garou cannot Command any?

                      Likewise, we can look at standard elementals:
                      Air - 7
                      Earth - 5
                      Fire - 5
                      Water - 10

                      Should water elementals only be able to be commanded by Rank 5 Garou?

                      Or at the sample animal Gafflings included in W20 corebook:
                      Deer - 6
                      Falcon - 5
                      Snake - 8
                      Wolf - 5

                      Granted you can always change these stats, but why should Snake gafflings require a Level 4 Garou to command it?

                      Furthermore, there doesn't seen to be that many spirits out there that have a Gnosis of 4 or less. So Rank 2 Garou who can buy the Gift won't have many opportunities to use it.

                      I think you'd need to re-stat a lot of spirits to create the effect you intend.

                      You want Command Spirit to be useful, but not game breaking. Players want to feel that the Gift they bought helps them so you can't kneecap it. I think it's best to talk with your game group and figure out an acceptable standard.

                      Maybe they don't want a Willpower spent every round to make a Bane attack its enemies because it's too expensive, but they'd be fine if the command holds only for 2 rounds before spending more Willpower. Or that one round is fine, but the spirit must always roll its full dice pool. And they'd be find with that if a BSD Theurge commands their pack totem or caern totem to attack them the same way. Or come up with some other type of standard, that satisfies you and them. Maybe the rule is Gafflings only at Rank 2 and 3, but Jagglings only at Rank 4+. And maybe it'll take a few more encounters before everyone is satisfied the standard is working out.

                      But assuming good faith, this is something your game group can likely settle for yourselves. And that may be different from other gaming groups and STing styles.

                          I think the best solution is to make successes matter. Make 1 success enough for a simple action that can be done in a round, and then increase the number of successes you need to roll for actions that take longer, or require more complexity. There's a lot you can do to make the Gift more nuanced so it sits nicely at its Rank while being useful without overpowered, but there's a cost in how complex you end up making the Gift. There's a bonus to keeping any house rule to a level of simplicity.

                              While I agree that players and STs will come to some understanding, like Black Fox suggests, I have some objections.

                              Sure, the Gift adds in W20 that "to the letter" passage to the Gift, that wasn't there in revised - but it is a can of worms.
                              Communication by language, for living beings, is dependant on context. That even translates to sign language. There is no need to use spoken language after all, for the 'Command Spirit' Gift. Why would there be, since the command is funnelled through "Spirit Speech" anyway? So, the rank 1 Gift manages that the spirit understands what the Theurge wants to convey - not Garou tongue, not spoken human language, not gestures... (expect the spirit understands Garou/human language, in which case the command is reaching the spirit on two channels ).

                              Fun fact: The "some spirits are to alien to understand Spirit Speech" passage is gone in W20. So it reads "[..] permitting the Garou to clearly [..] speak with any spirit he encounters." Making the whole "by the letter"-thing mood - the Theurge is able to speak to the spirit clearly what wants to say.

                              God, I hate W20.

                              If spirits didn't have a Gnosis score to substitute for Wits, they might act as a computer and interpret commands without any context. If that was the case, Theurges would give commands like a programmer does:
                              DO inifinite TIMES attack(SELECT entity WHERE entity.type EQ bane ORDER BY entity->relativeDistance(self)).

                              So, I think, unless you deal with a really stupid spirit, the spirit is able to understand intend of the command and follows it.

                              I can also totally see a being that is tricky by nature (or by Nature, as the game stat goes, or Demeanour) would try to get out of a command by following it to the letter. That is true for any Gift or power that allows to command, imo.

                              Is there even another mention of "clear wording is essential when dealing with spirits", anywhere? The Gift states "it is always essential", yet I can't remember it being mentioned anywhere within WtA books. It just doesn't fit the shamanic nature of garou mystics, it is a hermetic thing, I'd argue.
                              Also, as only "some spirits may attempt to twist the spirit of issued commands while obeying them to the letter" why would Theurges adopt such a behaviour when dealing with spirits. If only some spirits attempt this, not even succeed in doing it - how common is this really?
                              Is there a Wits roll to formally understand the command and another one to twist it? What difficulty would that be? Does the spirit get a couple of rounds to think about a way to twist the command while listing to the Jeopardy jingle before it follows the command?

                              The whole "by the letter"-addition is to cater to some trope and another thing, I assume, that was a quick edit by some author, not discussed within the development team thoroughly. For the most neutral spirits it wouldn't make a lot of sense to twist out of a command, for the garou would rip them to shreds anyway. For instances, as described, when a bane is commanded, I would still argue, that only those would twist out of the command for whom such an action is part of their nature.


                              That said, Gifts that cost more than one Rage, Gnosis and Willpower usually allow to spend those in any combination on activation. The need to spend a WP per round is something I don't see. Per separate command, sure.


                              That said, I'd think that the command is performed until the task is finished or the scene ends - depending on the command.
                              Commanding a spirit to deliver a message would not automatically end, while en route, since it is a new scene (or on arrival but before delivering the message).

                              Requiring additional successes for Commands that go against the interests of the spirit makes sense to me. The spirit should also simply be free to act normally, or confused, if ordered to something that it is incapable of doing. If the spirit is forced to fight its allies, allow for Gnosis rolls after the first round to break free. The difficulty should be high and decreasing each round.
