    Body part loss goofiness

    09-01-2021, 12:51 AM
    So, we all know that if a part of a garou is removed, it'll return to its breed-form.

    Well, here are two questions about that that have amused me for a while;


    1. What would metis blood register as? Since garou blood returns to its breed form, metis blood would naturally be metis blood. But what IS metis blood, essentially?

    2. A homid losing a tail. Since we humans don't really have tails, what would happen to the tail once its chopped off?

          1- I don't think Metis blood would be a Veil Breach. Without deep screening it's indistinguishable from human blood, and under deep screening it is either an amalgam of two species' blood, easy to brush off as resulting from an animal attack, or its own thing, which maybe indicates a new species, maybe is still indistinguishable from common blood.

          The most interesting question wouldn't be the blood itself, but DNA. And then, not only for Metis, but actually for all Garou and kinfolk. DNA structure determines a lot about the possibility of mating, especially the number and typology of chromosomes. This is a very interesting topic where the books are mostly silent (since they talk something, but never go in details). Is kinfolk DNA different from normal DNA for their species? What about a true Garou? How does the mating with two different species is affected by that? How does it affect the offspring? This all must be explored before even talking about Metis DNA.

          2- I go with Heavy Arms here. We DO have tails, or at least their vestigial structures, and sometimes something goes wrong and people are born with real, natural human tails. True human tails are boneless, but have all other tissue and are pretty well known by science. Some of them even move! A human tail would raise a lot of ruckus among common people, so the Garou would probably try to get rid of it, but in the medical community it would be seen just as an accident or impromptu surgery. A piece of human coccyx with the local skin would be more likely, though, matching the wound that would be left in the Human form, unless they're a Metis with an actual tail in human form.

              I guess it depend what you mean by "deep screening." If you put human blood and wolf blood under a basic microscope could you tell the difference? Nope. Humans and wolves have significantly different concentrations of major dissolved components, and completely different blood cell antigens that result in totally different blood typing. You'd need a reason to try rather than dismissing it as a mixed sample, but it there's some pretty basic tests that would reveal that the blood cells have human and wolf markers on them.. which would then prompt a lot of questions.

                  I have to re-read Book of the Weaver, and if it isn't there s somewhere else, I know of this group, but IIRC the book gives very little detail, even because the few I saw came from a very poor grasp of how DNA works. The kind of thing that, to be honest, was acceptable in the 90's, but is so easy to verify today that is suspension-breaking for a lot of people.

                  By deep screening I meant actually testing the blood. It depends a lot on circumstances, but even in large quantities, without a body it is unlikely to be examined or tested in a lab, so it is unlikely to be found out as anything beyond "blood". If tested, we would have to define if it is a mixture or a hybrid thing. If it is a mixture, then it wouldn't ever be a Breach because the simplest explanation would be that it is, indeed, a mixture, blood from two distinct sources. An animal attack is likely to produce such things, and at most the examiner would ask why the hell someone was carrying a vial of wolf blood around.

                  Hybrid blood is a little different, because it would point to the existence of something new. But, at the same time, it wouldn't give a proper clue of this thing. Chemical hybridization isn't the same as body hybridization, so they would find indications that this blood is mammal and such, maybe its diet, but the blood alone isn't nearly enough to figure out werewolf.

                  DNA is the most pressing matter here because in all those situations the thing that really may come to be a problem is DNA composition, starting with chromosomal distribution.We have 46 total chromosomes (23 pairs). Wolves and dogs have 78 (39 pairs). Chromosome counting is the first thing to look at before making a proper DNA test. And what's more, chromosomal pairings are the main thing to define if two creatures can have offspring, so given Garou ability to have offspring with those two different species, Garou DNA may be quite alien even without being a Metis.

                  So, does the Metis have two sets of DNA (like a genetic chimera, a condition that exists)? If so, is it nicely divided so wolf cells have wolf DNA and human cells have human DNA? Or is it random and the examiner may come across a human cell with wolf DNA? Does the Metis have just one DNA type, and so half its white cells have the wrong type of DNA? Or worse, the examiner finds a blood type that matches no known creature, but inside it have known DNA? Or is Metis DNA something weird, different, maybe because all Garou DNA is different, or maybe because it is an impossible hybrid?

                  And depending on the answers to those questions, we may now live in a time where every single DNA sample left behind may become a full breach. Or it may not be a problem at all.

                      Two important points, courtesy of the Revised Storyteller's Handbook:

                      1 - Cloning a Garou or other changer results in a Kinfolk clone.

                      2 - Cloning metis or otherwise producing them outside of a living union of two Garou (or two of the same changers that normally have them) is impossible and results in a stillborn fetus.

                      So whatever chromosomes that Garou born of two have, they can theoretically be cloned and grown in a lab. You won't be getting a viable birth out, though, because of that last sticking point of "requires living, half-spirit mother possessed of Rage". Not having that is a dealbreaker.

                                                      How frequently a Lupus character assumes a leadership position compared to a Homid? How many important characters are Lupus? They are there, but what is their proportion? How many werewolves thrust the decision making of a Lupus? How much of an impact and a voice do they really have? There are many ways for a group to be revered and still be seen as beneath you. Condescending attitudes are a well known blight for minority groups around the world.

                                                      And a Lupus will frequently have to deal with a series of minor consequences of their situation that, together, makes their lives shorter. Decisions involving them are frequently taken without their active participation, as few think they're able to comprehend the situation. As Self-fulfilling prophecies go, it is common for the thought that they won't understand something to lead to things not being properly explained and them becoming unable to understand the picture. They are left with simpler tactics and sometimes without key information.

                                                      This treatment is a frequent subtext in the books, maybe something the authors aren't totally conscious off, but something that makes perfect sense for the Garou society and their many internal contradictions. It doesn't bode well for Lupus survival in the long run, as it don't bode well for the survival/success of many other victims of this particular form of prejudice.

                                                      Something similar happens with mating with wolves. Any book you see a reference to this happening, either it is a Lupus Garou or it is described as an effort. Garou do mate with wolves, but with which real frequency? I'm not denying the Lupus do it, either, but if they are in danger it is obvious that they shouldn't be the only ones doing that. By the way, it is considered good for the Garou to avoid having a lineage purely Lupus or purely Homid, and yet lineages purely Homid are common. And what can be said? How many non-zoophile people would stomach mating with a wolf just for social obligation? This is to ask a lot.

                                                      It is a question of theory versus practice. Homid Garou should treat Lupus as equals and fight for their recovery, and should mate with wolves frequently to replenish the Lupus population. And seriously, the number of wolves world-wide isn't a good enough explanation. A female wolf can have 4 to 6 cubs a year, and their packs are tightly bonded, so it isn't at all hard for a werewolf to stay in touch with a pack and have cubs every year. As small as the wild wolf population became (and it isn't as small anymore), it was never small enough to explain the lack of Lupus.
