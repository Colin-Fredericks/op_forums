    Originally posted by Shadeprowler View Post
    If I might add ,don't forget good old Gunnhild from Clanbook: Tzimisce Revised, who is based on Gunnhildr Gormsdóttir a quasi-historical figure who appears in the Icelandic Sagas​
    I had completely forgotten about this character. But by 2001 when this clanbook had come out, I had determined that the Revised edition didn't scratch my itch, and was increasingly ignoring Revised canon and beginning to work out the first details of what I actually wanted to see in the chronicles I ran. So I hardly remember about anything in the Tzimisce revised clanbook.

    While I do think it's good to look at existing characters and wonder how they could be part of the Host, I am also leery of the idea that simply because a vampire is in northern Europe that it means they must be a follower of Odin. He's not the only powerful methusaleh in the region. He had to have enemies, rivals, and so on while he was active. And of course, Gunnhild would only have been a neonate or ancillae before Odin went into torpor. She's not Gangrel (or Brujah), the clans most associated with the Norse and Odin. And even her mortal origins (in the clanbook though not in history) place her outside of Scandinavia (though her life is integrated with the historical Norse).

    Originally posted by Shadeprowler View Post
    Blood magic, rune magic and a nice dose of Koldunism, connectio nto the fae, Odin and all...?
    Any Fenris would have a very hard time against the Mother of Kings and her allies
    She is a discount Baba Yaga technically​
    I agree with your general assessment, but I think my preference would be to make her a vampire enemy of the Host. She's already established to be in the Sabbat. She is best associated with Finland, not the actual Norse lands of Scandinavia. So I am inclined to place her among the enemy Sabbat of Finland aligned with the Malkavian methusaleh Louhi. Not among the mainly Ventrue anti-tribu lorekeepers that are somewhat alienated from the main sect and possibly open to defection (in my reading). So I think the character is best used as another vampire who would oppose Odin's Host.

    Of course, it is possible that in her very young days - centuries before the Anarch Revolt - she might have been a memberin the Host, and could still exchange favors with them in the early days of the Sabbat, but I think any such loyalties would have withered from her centuries spent in the Sabbat. When the Walkure began to take in the mid-20th century, they probably found that Gunnhild's loyalties were now very different. But it is possible some boons might be owed by either side, creating an interesting tension.

    I also admit that a strict reading of the revised clanbook does not explicitly state she is Sabbat. Just that she was "hospitable" allowing the Sabbat/Tzimisce to survive before they fled to the New World. So a case can be made that she had her own reasons for helping them, and does not indicate a member of the sect. Still, she'd be a Tzimisce elder post-Anarch Revolt. If she wasn't affiliated with the Sabbat itself by that point, I would think they would most likely destroy her. Any non-Sabbat Tzimisce should be an enemy. So I'm inclined to more likely make her some kind of Bishop or Archbishop of Finland for the Sabbat, overseeing their activities there and helping Louhi.

    However, I do like the idea that regardless of her loyalties now, that the Get of Fenris could very well believe she is an important member of the Host. As you explained, there are a lot of thematically appropriate elements. They would think its stupid to not believe that the "Witch Gunnhild" or "Mother of Kings" isn't one of the Host, or perhaps even one of its most important leaders. The Get of Fenris simply do not have the knowledge needed to understand vampire politics. So I would think it would be a good idea for the ST to present things as if Gunnhild was a member of the Host. And if for some reason the PCs actually investigated or attacked her and her own forces, it'd be up to them to figure out the truth. It's good for the setting to let the Garou be ignorant on a lot of things.

    I recognize this is more a matter of taste though. Different players will be inspired to do differently, and there are all sorts of justifications that can be made. Whatever an ST does with Gunnhild, I do think this is a great call out which will add to the Host's flavor and use in a chronicle in some way. So thanks for posting this.

        I'm a bit surprised that none of the posts on the Changing Breeds produced any comments when the fae posts did! Even if only to hear, "Nah, that's a stupid idea."

        Anyway, I'm moving on to the next bit. There are many human cults dedicated to Odin and his other mythological names. They are distinct from historical Norse or contemporary neo-pagan worship of Odin. The cults of the Host are the direct descendants of the original secret society of retainers and allies created by the vampire All High to serve him. This society dates back to the Germanic Migration Age and earlier. However, these human cultists find it useful to recruit members from modern neopagan groups, provided they can “correct” them of the “erroneous” lore of the mythologists and folklorists, and introduce the “true” and “historical” (albeit secret) rites and beliefs of the cult of the All High that they have preserved for millennia. And since there is a historical continuity going back to the All High in the setting, they'd have the "facts" to prove it.

        This secret society of “Odin” worshippers has survived for a thousand years since the All High went into torpor. The mortal followers are closely connected with supernatural forces like the Ulfhednar and the Black Magicians of the Aesin. In turn, the humans provide numbers for protection, a source of Glamour for the Aesin, and future kinain. Some serve as Allies, Contacts, Retainers, and Herds for the Walkure and other vampire followers. Others as the kinfolk for the false changing breeds. These supernatural creatures provide the verifiable magic power of the Odin cult. It is rare for specific mortal cults to serve more than one supernatural group of creatures, and each faction has different needs and desires. There can be certain groups that may not be under the "control" of supernatural creatures, but to be members of the Host they still need to be in contact with them. Maybe there is some kind of ritual that can be done that is the mystic equivalent of a telephone ringing among those who swore loyalty to the All High. And many are likely ignorant of the true nature of any of them. A Walkure may be accepted as an actual valkyrie, and not considered to be a vampire.

        Some mortals are also able to practice a form of sorcery that has survived the decline of magic. Such knowledge is not widespread and usually limited to only a few with the talent, intelligence, patience, and time to learn it. Both men and women practice this magic. Sorcery Paths often include those of Divination, Enchantment, and Herbalism/Brewing. Maybe Cursing, Healing, Hellfire, and Weathercraft to spice things up. I would try to keep the more blatant powers limited to the standard splats, and keep mortals more to a supporting role.

        In actual history, many kingly lines of the Norse claimed descent from Odin. So it’d be real easy for these mortal cults to claim they are the actual descendants of Odin as justification for their secret society. It’s part of the family secret or ancestry. Some may consider themselves descended from the Ynglings, and thus the true kings of Sweden or whatever. Whether that’s actually true or not is irrelevant. It just needs to be what these people actually believe. It’s not like anyone could actually verify such claims. Although in this age of easy DNA testing, I’d probably state that these families do heavily share a common paternal gene that is not present in most of the general population. But that isn't necessary. There can be all sorts of people in Scandinavia, England, Germany, and other places that might trace their lineage back to "Odin".
        ​
        Mortal members of the Host likely view their participation as purely a religious matter, and be happy that Odin and his children/allies favors them with any attention. But I think they should consider being prepared for Ragnarok to happen at any time to be an important obligation, and it'd be a good way to distinguish them from similar groups. I think it might also be interesting to see individual members of this secret society to be in the same organizations that some Get of Fenris kinfolk might join - historical societies, ancestry groups, neopagan religious groups, etc. Could lead to some interesting kinfolk encounters especially since on the surface people repeating the standard legends of Norse mythology won't be seen as "odd" to these kinfolk who are used to people always getting them "wrong". Someone who claims Fenris is a demon wolf and that Odin is great will just be accepted as normal. And then the ST can do the big reveal later on.

                I'm glad you like it.

                There is a small Heretic group of Wraiths called the Companions of Hermodr that have connections with the necromancers of the Host. In mythology, Hermod was the son of Odin who traveled to the underworld to obtain Balder’s release from Hel. So I think Hermod would fill the role of either a Ferryman, Shining One, Reaper, or at least a wraith with lots of Argos. I would make Hermod someone who was part of the Host long ago who became a wraith after he died, and he managed to re-contact the All High. Perhaps even someone who volunteered in some kind of ritual sacrifice by the All High in order to create an agent in the Underworld. There can be other Heretic groups associated with Norse mythology, but this group is the one related to the Host.

                These wraith Companions mainly act as a spy network, keeping the Host informed on the happenings of the Underworld, particularly the Far Realm of Hel. One of the All High’s concerns is that the dead will cross over the Shroud during the Apocalypse, and the leaders and best scholars of the Host follow events in the Underworld with keen interest. The Host of the All High would oppose any group seeking to bring down the Shroud. Ragnarok, of course, has much relevance among Wraiths although they know the concept as Doomsday. Doomsday is a core idea in many Heretic cults. Perhaps the “Loki” who will sail Naglfar with the monstrous hordes of Hel is some kind of Malfean. (Now I'm thinking there could be some sort of Spectre Nephwrack cult that presents itself as servants of "Loki" attempting to escape his prison in Hel.) This is something that will occur some time after Gehenna happens, but is still obviously part of Ragnarok. The All High is concerned with more than just the Jyhad, but the everything concerning the End of the World. I think this also distinguishes him from a lot of other vampires whose interest just comprises Gehenna and seem to believe the Antedeluvians will rule forever afterwards.

                Very few Companions of Hermodr remain. After the Christianization of Scandinavia, recruits plummeted. The group lost many members after Stygia issued the Proclamation of Reason and banished all Shining Ones, one of which was Hermodr. The surviving Companions went underground. Their recruits are now mostly limited to wraiths reaped from mortal cult members who are already adherents. Though in recent decades as neopaganism increases, the Companions have been able to find a new small source of recruits. Whether the wraith Hermod himself is still around, or Transcended, or fallen to Oblivion; is immaterial to the current status of this group.

                    Given their small numbers and the importance of their mission in the Underworld, few Companions are able to help their compatriots in the Skinlands. Indeed, many of the older Companions now lack Fetters and cannot even remain in the Shadowlands. However, a few are able to manifest in or affect the Skinlands, and they might be used in an extreme emergency. A more likely scenario of assistance is when the necromancers of the Host summon a wraith and ask for them to provide knowledge or to spy on others. I wouldn't overdo it, but this can provide a slight edge against the PCs - and perhaps attributed to a vampire's Auspec, fae Art, or something more mundane.

                    In exchange, the Host must assist the wraiths in terms of what matters to them - protecting Fetters, creating Memoriam, maintaining Legacies, preserving Haunts, and other work that supports Wraith Backgrounds. It would take a lot of work for an ST to build all this up for use in game, but it could lead to some really interesting set pieces of seemingly abandoned or lonely buildings in Scandinavia maintained by the Host to do just this, or perhaps a minor local folk museum.

                    I should also note that Odin and the valykries also serve as psychopomps in Norse mythology, and this might be reflected as some kind of Necromancy that they know which allows them to enter the Underworld and perhaps interact with their allies. I would probably keep this knowledge very limited though, perhaps just to Odin and one of the Walkure. The Host after all is still quite small and only makes up a small fraction of each supernatural splat.

                    There’s also potential ties back to the fae with the concept of Adhene like the Keremets, which are dead souls trapped in the Dreaming as opposed to going to the Underworld or Transcending. Perhaps some of Hermod's companions are Keremets, and there are Black Paths of Balor that lead to some of their hideouts in the Underworld.

                    It might be interesting to introduce the Host in a Garou game not by typical means, but by interaction between this wraith group and Garou like the Ivory Priesthood, Silent Striders or Uktena who know the Descent into the Underworld rite, or even a pack of Owl. One thing I want is to have many different ways of introducing the Host of Odin into a Werewolf game from sources that are not typically associated with vampires. Done properly, PCs might think these are all different groups until they begin to notice certain connections or common themes among them.​

                        The next thing I want to bring up is how the Host might be impacted by the death of Baba Yaga (assuming that happened in your chronicles). Baba Yaga was building her own - much greater - armies in Russia as the same time the Walkure were waking up and exploring. And while Odin and Baba Yaga did not have same goals, this does not mean some of Baba Yaga's followers would not want to join, link, or ally with the Host. Even if you accept canonically in your own setting that Baba Yaga was killed by Nictuku, there is still the question of what happened to all those forces she was building back in Rage Across Russia. The two forces are very different in their motivations and goals, but individual members might have reasons to work with both methusalehs. So we need to step back and review what we might want to pull from that sourcebook.

                        We know the Odinndohter were sent to explore what was going on behind the Shadow Curtain. They could have contacted some of the armies of Baba Yaga after she was destroyed and convinced them to join the All High. The Army of Night and its “General” Viktor was made up of 25 vampires. Per the sourcebook, their job was to control the cities and use human bureaucracy to support the Shadow Curtain by keeping out outsiders. There could be individual vampires who will prefer working with the Host rather than the Camarilla, Sabbat, or as independents. I suspect this part of the Baba Yaga’s Army just went back to being regular vampires and either joined the Camarilla or remain unaligned and independent. But there are still some named NPCs associated with her. One was the 6th generation Gangrel methusaleh/elder Viktor who was embraced in 6 BC, and who was blood bond to Baba Yaga. Now that the bond is gone, he could join the Host or be its enemy.

                        Then there is Sergei Voshkiv, Baba Yaga’s 5th generation childe embraced just in 1991. Voshkov did not seem to be a member of the Army of the Night and ran a separate group, the “Eyes of Baba Yaga”. The Eyes are (of course) an intelligence group of Nosferatu of Baba Yaga’s line that are lead by Sergei. The text actually states “childer of Baba Yaga”, but given their leader is also a childe, but just a neonate, I think it makes more sense these are much weaker generation Nosferatu of her line that could be intimidated by Sergei’s powerful generation and closeness to Baba Yaga. I don’t think he’s a good candidate for the Host given his backstory, but he’s likely still out there doing something. His stats in Rage Across Russia are utterly fantastic given his young age, but I would associate that with help from Baba Yaga. Now that she is gone, his augmented powers are likely back to where they should be for such a young vampire. Young vampires of potent generation are a diablerie target risk.

                        I think the real question is what motivated these 25 vampires. Was it a fear of Gehenna/Apocalypse, and they hoped Baba Yaga would allow them to survive? Was it a love of the Russian motherland a desire to protect it? Was it fear that Baba Yaga would devour them if they did not help her? Was it love through the Blood Bond? That will determine whether they would be willing to defect to the Host. Unless one of the powerful vampires join, I don't think this will noticeably improve the Host. But any new vampire members would allow them to expand their operations in the former Soviet Union and gain new recruits.

                        The Host might also be helping “Typhon” Incarna break free of Grandfather Thunder, though perhaps the spirit uses an alternative name like “Donar”, “Thor”, or “Thunraz” among the Host. For those who don’t have the sourcebook (and spoiler warning), this was a spirit of Grandfather Thunder’s brood who wanted independence and was helping Baba Yaga to get it by corrupting the Shadow Lords to the Wyrm. He had a sept and several packs following him, and some of the members had outright joined Baba Yaga (in secret). If you go down that route, much depends on whether you base Typhon as he was in Rage Across Russia or as written in Revised Shadowlords Tribebook (which does not mention any of this), or decide to make those descriptions as two different spirits. Personally, I think these two different depictions of Typhon are incompatible with one another, and was part of Revised era attempt to dissociate the sourcebook with the game (which Baba Yaga's destruction in a Vampire sourcebook fulfilled). But if you want to go down the route of "Typhon" as portrayed in Rage Across Russia, I think the spirit - assuming he was not found out and destroyed by Grandfather Thunder - would be looking for a similar ally and the All High could be it. This would give the Host some potent new allies.

                        Perhaps one or more of the Verbena helping Baba Yaga (there were four in a chantry), who are more interested in supernatural lore or information concerning the end of the world than they are about concerns over contemporary Russia, might join. The All High and some of his followers possess many occult secrets and magical knowledge which might tempt these mages.

                        I don't see the All High as trying to work with the Zmei in Russia or try to free Koschei. I think he'd be opposed to both since he wants to prevent or survive Ragnarok. But here there is an opportunity for the Host to serve as secret or dark quest givers to the Garou. Perhaps they even do so with their new Shadow Lord allies (through Typhon) in order to promote them in ranka nd prestige.

                        Lastly, there are also ongoing negotiations between the Host and the Ravnos elder Durga Syn and the forces she compiled to fight against Baba Yaga. Their goals are not identical, but there are some similarities. I would think that the discussions have been honest, and it is possible that Durga Syn could rise to be an important leader among the Host. The big obstacle is that for most of Durga’s existence, the Gangrel methusaleh has been in torpor, and she has no way of knowing if he would be a similar menace like Baba Yaga. For now the negotiations continue as Durga Syn considers her options and whether it is wise to associate herself with such a being like the All High.

                        Of course, if Baba Yaga still exists in your chronicle, then the activities of the Odinndohter complicate things for her, and that is its own post.
