    Originally posted by Sergeant Brother View Post
    Some kind of "Anarch" movement within the Garou nation sounds like a great idea to me. Instead of making every Garou faction super woke and "tolerant" it would be far better to acknowledge how incredibly intolerant and rigid Garou society is and to emphasize that in combination with a focus on a younger generation of Garou who want to want to resist the hyper-conservative elders.
    What's strange is that early Werewolf very much plays up the fact that there is a rebellion brewing within the Garou Nation. Young Garou are either jaded about traditional Garou values because they clash with modernity, or they realize the old leadership is failing badly and need to be removed if the Garou are to win or simply survive. There is very much an intergenerational struggle going on. And of course, there were Ronin Garou who were the Caitiff of the setting. So there is totally supposed to be an equivalent of the Anarchs in the sense of discontented youth wanting to make change.

    I think that doesn't get traction for several reasons. The first is that fairly quickly the fans decide the Garou were good guy superheroes and effectively jettisoned the complications of Garou society. If you portray the Garou as good guys, then you undercut the need for "Anarchs" to change the system.

    The second is that the renown system really affected how players portrayed their characters. In order to get the higher level Gifts, you need the higher level ranks. And to get the ranks, you need the Renown. And by following the Renown, you end up being good Garou. Now it is totally possible to figure out a way for corrupt Garou to game the system, or for discontented "Anarch" type characters to still gain Renown. But we're never told within the game itself how that happens so the players don't have models on how to run it in their games. So they default to being sincere.

    A third is that in concentrating on the tribes and the difference between the tribes, we don't get any sense of how these young, discontented Garou organize or protest or just complain. There isn't an "anarch" camp in any of the tribes, nor a multi-tribal camp. So the game actually doesn't have any examples for PCs to model themselves on. So many players don't. They gravitate to the models that are provided which mostly support the system.

    For example, in none of the online or LARP games I've played, I never see examples of bad leadership at the sept. I don't see bad elders obviously failing, yet clinging onto power. I don't see the Bone Gnawer PCs acting up and complaining about the Garou leadership or Pure Breed that I always see with Brujah or Anarch PCs in online or LARP vampire games.

    While I do see PCs criticizing certain aspects of the setting, it's more theoretical because those things being criticized aren't actually portrayed by any of the NPCs or PCs of that chronicle. Silver Fang PCs aren't entitled assholes. Shadow Lord PCs aren't treacherous, ambitious pieces of shit. Bone Gnawers aren't self-serving jaded jerks that don't give a fuck. Glass Walkers aren't heretical Garou with very different ideas on what it means to be Gaian. Instead, there's a very strong conformity of everyone honestly trying to serve the sept. And I think it's mainly Renown that is causing it. The desire to "level up" means everyone behaves. To the point that even STs don't have NPCs act in ways the setting implies many NPCs act.

    So there's a disconnect between the setting as original portrayed and the mechanics in the game of how one increases in power. That's why Ronin never get played. In contrast, Vampire divorces status from the ability to increase in power, so I see a much wider variety of play styles and character concepts.

    I don't think that means that Renown has to change. We just need concrete examples of viable character concepts and how they were still able to rise in Renown and gain Rank so they can get the better Gifts. How can certain Garou rig the system to advance faster than they should? How can characters wanting to defy their elders and drive change in society still able to rise in Rank? Once players have other models, they'll start roleplaying in a more variety of ways.

        The Bone Gnawers range heavily from barely members of the Garou Nation but don't want to be Ronin, to members trying to work with the Children of Gaia and Glass Walkers to get more respect for the "lower" Tribes. The Rat's Teeth are kinda the default Gnawer PCs because they actually see the value in working with the other Tribes directly; giving them reputations as both important to the Tribe not all getting cast out as Ronin, and sell outs to the rest of the Garou that see them all as scum.

        There's a lot of reasons here.

        I've been part of games where we've trying to facilitate this sort of dynamic:

        1) Garou society, being more militaristic, is more regimented. Cliaths can't challenge Adren (or higher) no matter how much of a screw up the Adren really is. Which means you need to go above the Adren in the chain of command and find someone higher Rank to do something about it. This leads to massive strain on how the Sept leadership is even functional. Sure the players can get rid of one broken stair or something, but if you want this conflict to continue past one plot arc, other higher ranks and Sept officers have to have been in on it (at least to the point of actively turning a blind eye to it) until either the PCs have no chance against an ingrained Sept that is unified against them even if they're right, or by the time you've cleaned up all the corruption and nepotism and so on, there's so little Sept left that it can't operate effectively any more.

        2) Players don't like to keep going on IC about stuff that they can't change. Well bred Garou can't be made to be less well bred. There's no way to turn off their bonuses. A Gnawer with PB 0 vs. a Silver Fang with PB 5 sitting around grumbling all the time isn't something players want to engage with because that's the rules, so the Gnawer (and Walker, and etc.) players just move on because they'd rather be proactive and thus more fun to play with.

        3) As you went into with Renown, the game has a lot of places where rocking the boat just isn't a good idea. An Elder can still face consequences if they travel to a level 5 Caern to learn a new Rank 5 Gift, but don't pay their proper respect to the local Sept and its customs. This leads to a lot more, "work within to push for change," than full on Anarch style counter-movements.

        4) Between 13 Tribes, 5 Auspices, and 3 Breeds, the Garou just don't need political faction to be an essential part of character variety. There's a lot of dynamics already in play that are, to many players, interesting enough without trying to copy early WtA's stronger affinity for retreading VtM's political conflicts.
