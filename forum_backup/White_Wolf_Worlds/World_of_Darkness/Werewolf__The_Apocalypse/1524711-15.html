    From an out-of-character perspective, we know the Cult is doomed. But we're also led to believe that the world is doomed anyway. Nobody is fighting to save it other than the Cult. And from an in-character perspective, well, maybe the Cult could save the world. It's not likely but it's more likely than what anyone else is trying (which is essentially nothing).

                        In one place the W5 corebook does talk about a way that the book's negative outlook on Garou in general can be addressed (from the sidebar "Changing Ways" pg 47):

                        Tonight, Gaia needs more than murderous maniacs… which is one of the tragedies of the Garou, in that they are so prone to Rage. Rage cannot solve every problem, however, so each Garou needs to find how they can best fulfill their spiritual charge — or whether they must abandon it.
                        (Emphasis mine)

                        So, step one to dealing with the Age of Apocalypse: Don't be a Garou. You're instantly "winning" the game by just turning to your ST and say "My character loses the wolf, never shapeshifts again and gives up on being a Garou.". And that's it! Problem solved! No lengthy gaming-sessions and handwringing over what to do as a Garou with the slow realization that being a Garou means being a failure and any effort to change things for the better is futile.

                        What's entirely missing from W5 is its Anarch-equivalent. The Camarilla-equivalent, the Garou-Nation, has been blown up and with the BSD and the Cult of Fenris you have two Sabbat-like factions which are considered to be bad - but W5 has no dedicated "Let's do something new."-faction. So practically all the paths a Garou can pursue have already been deemed pointless or been thrown into the "That's evil and wrong."-box.

                        And without that, the book's overly pessimistic outlook makes it hard to understand why anyone would even wanna play as a Garou in the first place.

                                With apologies for everyone I couldn't respond to because of my mishaps with posting, my short version of assuming this goes like this.

                                "The key to understanding this is that this is about going past the Apocalypse in Werewolf: The Apocalyspe."

                                I write for a fanzine called GRIMDARK MAGAZINE and it's basically about incredibly bleak and antihero filled fantasy or sci-fi. It takes its name from Warhammer 40K and it reminds me a bit of a discussion I had about how to do the best Tabletop RPG Warhammer 40K game you could do. Most people assume that it would be about Space Marines, secret cults, and the extreme measures to save the world.

                                My answer would be, "Failure."

                                The ideal Warhammer 40K game I'd love to play, preferably with Inquisitors is coping with the concept of failure on a massive scale. That the problem is impossible to deal with individually and the rot is intense and humantiy's Imperium WILL be destroyed by its various enemies. Maybe not today, maybe not a thousand years from now but inevitably and probably deserves to be. Knowing that this is absolutely impossible to prevent--what do you do?

                                * Try to fix things anyway?
                                * Go down in the blaze of glory?
                                * Try to find individual joy?
                                * Preserve some small fragment instead of the whole thing.

                                I feel less off-put by W5 because I get that they're attempting to essentially put the Garou in a position of having freedom to do a lot more of what they want to with their powers. The war is over and you lost. Maybe there will be something else or maybe you just have to make time with what you have left. I write a lot of Noir and Punk books and that attitude applies to Cthulhu Armageddon (which is set after the Great Old Ones rise and humanity is going extinct--and there's nothing that can stop it), Moon Cops on the Moon (which is about being on a corrupt cyberpunk dystopia as the one honest cop), and Lucifer's Star (which is about a pilot whose side lost the Galactic Civil War--but they kind of had it coming).

                                Or to use another example, FIREFLY.

                                Mal and company aren't going to overthrow the Alliance. They have irretrivebaly lost. However, they can survive themselves for a time.

                                It's very DARK SOULS original recipe I suppose.

                                Yes, you can do a big "What if we did X, Y, and Z and resurrected Gaia!" but you can also do, "I can't save the world but maybe I can save these campers!"

                                    Yes, you can do a big "What if we did X, Y, and Z and resurrected Gaia!" but you can also do, "I can't save the world but maybe I can save these campers!"

                                    Because what differs WtA from Warhammer 40k or Dark Souls, is the focus and the themes. 40k, at its most hopeful, is about saving a planet or some people. But more often it is about the meat grinder that is the universe and how even the factions with the best intentions end up fighting because of intolerance. Dark Souls is about entering a pre-ruined world and figuring out why it happened, with a side of trying to fix things on the side.

                                    What neither of these games have that WtA does, is the role of the Apocalypse and the pollution of the environment in the game. In 40k, you got worlds upon worlds upon worlds. Destroying one is not the end of the game, you can find another Death World to hang out in. In Dark Souls, everything is already ruined and your job is to put together the pieces of how and why. But in WtA, the very title hints at the focus; the Apocalypse. Not the aftermath or how we got here, the very end of this one world.

                                    In addition, even in W5 a massive part of the game focuses on the way the planet is suffering and how people are hurting other people. The Wyrm, Fomori, the BSDs, the Weaver, etc. are all symbols of some way humanity and nature are being hurt. But the world is not destroyed yet, the end hasn't come yet. The tagline is When Will You Rage, meaning; When will you take action against all this wrongness?

                                    WtA is about CARING so much that it makes you RAGE. This does not apply for 40k because its a large scale game. This doesn't apply to Dark Souls because everything was already lost.

                                    So when that sort of game presents action as negative and sitting on your butt positive, you get why most of us have issues.

                                    Teal Deer; WtA is not the same genre as Dark Souls or 40k, and that is why the non-action annoys people.

                                        There's also the issue that unlike Dark Souls and WH40k, WtA has to work with one huge limitation: Its wider context/setting is our world in the present. With Dark Souls and WH40k and its completely fictional settings you have a lot of room to express specific moods and themes in its worldbuilding. There's a strict limitation to how far you can go with the "Age of Apocalypse"-bit in W5. You could get a lot more out of the setting if the setting were actually a world in the middle of a global Apocalypse - but it isn't. It's our world. Right now. Bad stuff is happening but nothing world-ending-bad is happening at the moment. I'd wager the majority of people in Omaha, Nebraska don't believe they're living in an "Age of Apocalypse". That's where W5 just got way too ambitious and didn't consider the implications of its worldbuilding. There are a couple of contradictions that the W5 corebook failed to resolve but in this particular case it's this: If the world is actually ending (experiencing the type of Apocalypse WtA is about), then you can't have our real world in the present as a backdrop. If you have our real world in the present as a backdrop, the setting won't come anywhere close to deserving the moniker of "Age of Apocalypse". The previous editions got this right: "It's looking bad out there and it seems like it can only get worse." That's where you want to be in terms of the titular Apocalypse.

                                        W5 would have to go way further in its worldbuilding to deserve a description like "grimdark". In the way it's presented in the W5 corebook the game is currently not grimdark. And what is in its stead...? Who knows... That's part of the issue. Age of Apocalypse is a central part of W5 but the W5 corebook isn't very committed to that aspect - outside of shitting on Garou constantly, of course.

                                                PHASE FOUR PART TWO

                                                Put a Trace on That Artwork

                                                After the initial online 'discussions' about the way W5 was going, Renegade/Paradox decided to preview some of the Tribes with their write-ups, based on the results of a poll. Surprisingly, the Glass Walkers won. I honestly didn't think they were the most popular Tribe, but I never saw the tabulated results. Anyway, soon after the preview came up, someone noticed how one of the figures looked familiar. Now, tracing an image to create your own art is accepted, especially when things like tight deadlines are involved. However, the subject involved doesn't like his image used without his consent. A whole new round of speculation began, that only increased when more previews came out with artwork that looked like it had been traced from known individuals.

                                                Now, this could mean a few things. Let's give the benefit of the doubt and say that the art department hadn't gotten around to okaying the said artwork because of unexpected results in the polling. They were just getting around to it when, boom, they had to preview it and found out about the tracing. Renegade quickly announced that any depictions would be fixed in the final version. At the very least, this meant that the editing process was very rushed. I have already mentioned the many errors that crept in that weren't caught in time. When a fixed deadline is involved and problems come up, you need to change the deadline. Instead, only the suspect artwork got changed. A company that can't bear to adapt to sudden problems isn't one that garners much trust.

                                                A second possibility is that the art director and artists never communicated about certain possible issues. The art director should have told artists to make sure that got permission to use the images of any models they used, even if it was secondhand via tracing. Or make it less identifiable, at least. The artist should have informed the art director about their methods of creation, especially with techniques like tracing, so any issues with public figures could be avoided. Either way, this lack of communication does not bode well for a company and their freelancers. If basic communications about such basic standards aren't followed, what else are they not doing correctly.

                                                Third, maybe no one understood about the feelings about the subject in question. Just plain ignorance. Not a great excuse, but a relatable one. Considering the recent debacle about the Fenrir, one would expect such research would be made to prevent more online flaming, not to mention litigation. Again, if something like standard research isn't conducted by a company, what should a player expect about the quality of that company's products.

                                                Of course, maybe no one cared about doing any such things at all. Laziness. This isn't a promising outlook either. Considering how much of the rest of W5 was left empty, this cold be the case. No one cared to do anything else, until observant people spoke out and brought this problem to their attention. It did get W5 a bit more attention than his it would have had at the time.

                                                This brings up a final possibility. It is the least likely, but still within the realm of plausibility. What if the artwork was never the finished version, and it was never going to be published? There is a school of advertising that says "any publicity is good publicity." While the known artwork did get changed for the final product, some of the other artwork that wasn't previewed may have been just as noticeably traced as well. It has been pointed out that at least one of the wolf illustrations looks like it was traced. Some of the other figures are in somewhat familiar poses, if not outright known. The artwork got altered and inserted rather quickly, relatively late in the process, especially when other errors were overlooked and not corrected.

                                                I am not sure which of these possibilities would put this situation in good light, especially that last one. Either we are dealing with a company that doesn't know what it is doing, doesn't care what it is doing, or doesn't mind possible litigation to trick potential buyers into looking into its new product.

                                                THWarted
