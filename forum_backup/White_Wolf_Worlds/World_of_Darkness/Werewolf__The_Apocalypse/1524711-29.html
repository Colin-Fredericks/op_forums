                                            I'll do it even i I agree it's an unfair framing:

                                            WtA shouldn't focus on angst because there are other emotional dilemmas for games to focus on. Werewolves aren't broody types like Spider-man (though main continuity Spider-man is long since past that, and it was mostly a thing during his teen and college years), Daredevil, or Batman. Their powers are not accident's of fate or circumstance. They were chosen by powers of deity level as champions of said beings' cause. If we're talking comparisons to heroes, they're Superman* living in a world of cardboard, heirs to a legacy of a ancestor that knew they would be far stronger than humanity, and charged them to lead righteously. Superman of course has doubts, and flaws, and so on, but you don't associate him with angst and brooding. Much like another on point example, Captain America, their primary dramatic emotional conflicts are when these strong seemingly virtuous ideals they grew up with, and embraced, turn out not to work in an imperfect way. Harano was added to the games as a "fail state." Garou that give up on the ideals they were chosen to lead the fight for - to wallow in angst, and brood in depression - have failed to endure the emotional trails of having power.

                                            When will you Rage? Is not a theme of broody angst. It is a question of how you will use you power when the time comes, and even how you figure out when the right time even is. There isn't a question of if such power is necessary, or if the Garou are right to have it. They were chosen for this task. Their drama is in living up to that burden.

                                            * - and hey, the things Superman has done to Lois Lane to protect his double identity are pretty resonant with how the Garou can treat their kin sometimes.

                                                You know, it occurs to me that for all the insistence that the World of Darkness is about being grimdark - about players playing monsters - that's really not all that true for most of the game lines.

                                                There's dark subject matter at play, obviously. That's why it's the World of Darkness. And every game line gives players ample opportunity to play bad people.

                                                But most of the games are, by default, sympathetic to the factions positioned as "Protagonists". Gaian Garou fight against the Wyrm. Hunters (big and small 'H') fight monsters. Wraiths want to get their affairs in order to pass on. Changelings just want to survive in a reality innately hostile to their existence. Mummies are immortals that need hurt no one to live forever, and also oppose Apothis. None of these splats NEED to be evil.

                                                With the exception of Demon: The Torment and Kindred of the East (who are just another kind of vampire), Vampire: The Masquerade is kind of the outlier in WoD.                                                 From a certain perspective, we as WoD fans only really assume it as a whole needs to be Grimdark because Vampire: The Masquerade was the first.
