                Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post
                Does something have to be true to matter?
                If you seriously think your character's philosophy, historical beliefs and religion need to be correct for the RP and story to matter, then I don't know what to say.
                I think these two are very interesting and good discussion points in general. I think the value of truth in roleplaying would be a terribly interesting discussion/thing to read about.

                Again, not to be critical, I think it is inherently flawed to compare Vampire and Werewolf in these regards. Vampire is giving you options for a wide area of worldviews, origins, purposes, etc. Your Bahari might find worth in their mindset or the myth of Lilith. But fundamentally that was a choice the character made as well. Mage is doing very much the same thing. The war between Vampire Sects is about ideology, and can be further adjusted with Paths. The war between the Technocracy and Traditions is also about ideology, also tempered by Paradigms.

                A Werewolf isn't choosing to be a Werewolf and they don't really have any other mindset they can adopt. The battle against the Wyrm doesn't leave room for much moral ambiguity (on a broad level). Pentex and the Black Spiral Dancers (broadly) are just evil. It is almost like a bait and switch for Werewolf because the setting, themes, etc are actually seemingly built upon various accepted truths. I think a big degree comes into what changes are being made, obviously. But the purpose for their existence and the nature of the Apocalypse are rather important. It lacks agency if certain things aren't correct.

                Now, I want to take a look at the concept in general. Does something need to be true to matter? No. BUT, I think some value or justification needs to be given to make those experiences matter. Again, I really want to avoid scenarios or specific examples because I know it'll sound unfair. Like I said before, and you said earlier, "As for those who have commented comparing it to a "it was all a dream" twist, that's only if the storyteller is incompetent. The spirits who tell you how to live your life are the creations of an evil Leech can be an awful twist, or a good twist, depending on how it's delivered, forshadowed and brought into the tale." Most stories can be good or bad depending on how well the ST presents it. The thing is, I think there is a grey area and that this truth versus meaning situation isn't so cut and dry.

                Taking your Bahari as the example, they take meaning in because fundamentally the Path still imposes various philosophical value. Same with something like Native American Mysticism. I'm not sure (and this isn't a knock against them) that the Garou belief system holds the same innate value beyond the purpose it serves. It ends up feeling like a dupe if the character was wrong. I'd arguably say that saying one view of Mage is right can hurt as well, but that'd be a bit specific on the paradigm. Now these things being wrong doesn't mean a character is worthless because they were wrong. But I'd posit that it is inherently less satisfying if their sole worldview and purpose is fundamentally wrong. This is why I think people are suggesting the idea is similar to 'it was all a dream' scenario. It doesn't need to be a stark parity between THIS MATTERS and THIS NO LONGER MATTERS for story and RP. But a gradient of what is enjoyable, typically with people enjoying being right, success, etc. You can lose or be wrong and have fun. But Werewolf isn't presented in a way that easily allows being wrong about certain things and also be quite as fun. People can easily get emotionally invested in that sorta thing.
