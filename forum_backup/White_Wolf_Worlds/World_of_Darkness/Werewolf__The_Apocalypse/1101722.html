          In other news: water wet.

          The numbers for such things were just sort of made up and once they were in the books, even after people figured out they didn't add up well, they were the numbers. It's a bit of a dead horse.

          Though that bit of snark aside, the numbers aren't actually that bad. They don't work, but they're not as grim as you're saying either. Also the Garou are supposed to be dying out so the unsustainable numbers are probably the idea; even if again, they don't add up well.

          10% (which isn't a set rate, but lets run with it anyway) isn't the replacement rate. The base replacement rate is the number of children that need to be born to replace the parents. In a sexually reproducing species, the lowest possible rate is an average of 2 children per female for the total global population (in a local population it can go lower via migration), however this is then modified by the mortality rate of children before they reach child rearing age. So humans living in a society with high levels of medical technology and common access to it, the replacement rate can easily be between 2 and 3, as long as most adults have children. A replacement rate of 2.1 would imply a very low child mortality rate, high fertility rates, and most women reproducing. Lots of species have very high replacement rates because they have very high pre-reproductive ability mortality rates. Salmon give birth to a lot of eggs just to maintain a population because very few of those eggs will survive to reproduce.

          The issue with applying population statistics to the Garou is that the Garou don't work like a species. Garou don't mate with other Garou to produce a stable population. Garou mate with kinfolk, humans, and wolves to produce both more Garou and more kin, and kin can also produce Garou without mating with a Garou.

          So it isn't as simple as saying a female Garou needs to give birth to at least three Garou children (figure that despite Garou health and magic, 1/3 cubs isn't going to survive the violent lives of Garou to mate), and at a 10% birth rate would then have to have thirty total children over their lives (though for lupus Garou that's not such a difficulty). You also have kin producing children in kin-kin pairings. If you assuming a 1% birth rate of Garou in kin-kin populations, 300 kin females having one child would have the same impact as one Garou female's contributions to a stable replacement rate.

          This becomes the crux of why the numbers don't add up well: we don't have good figures on both lupus and homid kin numbers compared to the Garou population, as well as how to judge their replacement capabilities compared to average members of wolf and human populations (do they out produce because the Garou are actively influencing this and and spiritual influence, or under produce because they're targets of the Garou's enemies?). There's a bunch of variables, and most assumptions play out poorly when crunched (mostly that way more of the human population would have to be kin then the books imply).
