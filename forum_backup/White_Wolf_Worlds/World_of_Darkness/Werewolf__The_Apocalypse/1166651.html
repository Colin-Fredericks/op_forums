    Rage and the Curse versus Human Ties

    12-11-2017, 04:00 AM
    I have for a long time now noted that, that a lot of people often ignored the Curse and the Rage the Garou have in favour of being employed and having strong ties to humanity. I figured after Black Fox's Rage across New York retrospective, that I'd put up a thread discussing this issue.

    This might sound like a small gripe, but I think it has some long reaching implications, especially when it comes to kinfolk and the role they and garou play in their society. As well as pondering if the Curse should be used more like Primal-Urge is used in Werewolf the Forsaken.


    With the Kinfolk, as a long time forum and chat player, I have seen a particular type of kinfolk pop up. Often female, often young, often basically mere breeding stock for their garou mate. Now, I must note that these games are played 24/7 and so have the chance of playing out relationships in much more depth than in sessions. I have no problem with this. What I have an issue with, is that these kinfolk are often totally under the thumb of their garou mate and they often do not contribute to the sept much aside from some off-screen stuff.

    Now, Kinfolk are subservient to their garou, but the kicker in the books is that they also do a lot of work that the garou just cannot do because of the Curse. So when the garou are belittling them, they act like condescending dicks. To the chat/forum kinfolk, garou belittling and controlling them feels justified at times.

    I do think this behaviour from the players in these very old communities comes from the garou pcs wanting to interact with humanity a lot. Leaving the kinfolk pcs in a situation where they end up being limited. This is also a factor in some sourcebooks, where the Curse is ignored in favour of the NPC having a job or good ties to humanity. The newest MET book outright lists jobs that different garou of different auspices and tribes can do. In general, unless the ST particularly pushes for it, the Curse seems to be often ignored by the players and the writers alike.

    This brings to mind one question, is the Curse something that you guys would miss if it was removed? For me, I would like it to be used, but often my fellow players would not agree with this. The mentality I have seen is geared to garou that more often are more likely to do a lot of grunt work themselves and are less likely just to focus on spiritualism and fighting the Wyrm.

    In this, I must admit that a system similar to Forsaken's Primal-Urge would be a much better overall, allowing the PCs to interact and have stronger ties to humanity overall unless they choose to sacrifice that for more power. Given the mentality against the garou being moochers being so strong among the player base (from what I have noticed) and the heavy focus on taking part in human activities, this would work very well.

    Of course, this would further limit the KInfolk. But honestly, I think Kinfolk are much better as NPCs than PCs if the players and the STs aren't going to focus on their work.

      I think the thing is that the Curse isn't that bad at lower Rage. Even at Rage 4 (that's starting for a Galliard base) only inflicts the Curse on ~48% of the population. And Willpower is by no means evenly distributed (a Rage 4 school teacher is in a lot more trouble than a Rage 4 SWAT officer).

      I feel like part of the issue is that the core books and Kin books tend to emphasize the Curse beyond what the mechanics say, while most of the other supplements actually follow the mechanical implications (not always, there's lots of stupid things like high Rage school teachers in the books). Among Garou that don't bother to raise their Rage much (and this really does seem to be a divide in players; either you keep your Rage low and can generally interact with humanity, or you go for lots of Rage and accept the Curse and avoid people), holding a job is probably not such a hard thing to pull off. Rage 1-2 characters barely suffer the Curse at all. Rage 2-3 characters can easily hold down jobs as long as they avoid a few where you're going to deal with low Willpower people a lot (or if you do, it's not really going to matter), and Rage 3-4 you can probably swing a job if you're careful with what career you go into.

      The core book especially seems to expect you to buy up Rage for the significant power it means in combat and for Gifts, despite the drawbacks. Play experience tends to teach people to leave that to the Ahrouns and people that want to go very combat heavy. Which goes beyond the Curse, since there's also the Beast Within, and Frenzy.

      I would love to see the mechanics reflect the setting the game layouts out in the core book more, but it would probably need a pretty significant rethinking of the mechanics.

                  I think the Curse is very important and emphasize it in my chronicles. It's one of the few things in the game that helps bolt the setting into the typical horror movies and stories we expect in the World of Darkness as opposed to the high fantasy adventure the game can too easily become. Establishing the status quo early on helps the PC incorporate their character into the setting appropriately and guides them in their future XP spending.

                  I typically play tabletop so I don't have an issue with PC kinfolk. All kinfolk are NPCs whether they are GM characters or part of the Kinflk background. If there's issues, it is because the players don't adequately think out their character creation and don't buy the Background and become frustrated when they can't do something because they only have their Garou and no kinfolk to use.

                  Sometimes that Garou PC does need to interact with humans for routine story purposes, and I allow PCs to spend Willpower to subdue the Rage within them to alleviate the Curse for a scene. When that happens, mortals are only affected as if they were kinfolk, and kinfolk don't notice anything at all. Since Willpower is a finite resource, this isn't something I've seen abused.

                  I think when it comes to online games, a major problem in general is that players are looking for something to do. They want characters that can participate in a scene if they're interested. They don't want to sit back and do nothing because some other PC should be the one doing it. It can really kill the scene and enthusiasm for the game, if this or that PC isn't around so we can't advance. In similar circumstances, many plots involving the umbra are difficult because unless there is a theurge present, interaction with the spirits can be difficult. Sometimes games get around that by having spirits speak the Garou Tongue or the vernacular, but that ends up crapping over the theurges like how Ana described happens to kinfolk.

                  The Curse should quickly cripple most Garou's ability to interact normally among the Flock. Rage 1 Garou are fine. Rage 2 Garou only affect 10% of the population, which is probably made up mostly of small children, the elderly, and the meekest adults. Most of those can be avoided on a regular basis, and the few that aren't will be seen as the odd ones to the Garou's normal social peers. These Garou can pass in human society without many problems. (In practice though, many Theurges will soon be acting in strange ways to humans as a result of their spirit interactions).

                  Rage 3 Garou will start finding it to be very difficult. 30% of humanity is a large number, and now there are all sorts of people who need to be avoided. At that stage, there will be a noticeable enough amount of people actively avoiding the Garou out of fear that questions will be asked, police will show up, and normal social interactions can be poisoned (your coworker Bob used to like you, but once his wife met you and decided she hates you, he hasn't been as friendly). At that stage certain kind of jobs can still be done, provided social interaction is minimized. It also helps if the job in question allows both some privacy or isolation as well as attracting coworkers who would have high Willpower to begin with. These would be hard jobs in isolated places; but are also the kind of resource extraction jobs many Garou would find problematic. There would also be jobs involving illegal activities, private detectives, security, or other violence oriented jobs that the Garou could be selective about it. This couldn't include actual law enforcement though - police have to come into contact with too many regular people. Long term office jobs aren't feasible at this level. Certain kinds of third shift jobs that otherwise wouldn't work in normal business hours could work. So interaction is not impossible, but is limited. The Curse is affecting the character's life in a meaningful way.

                  At Rage 4, the Garou would have to be making a living off the land by themselves, and hopefully with kinfolk helping them. This would be the mountain man hermit, or someone working on a family farm or ranch. In today's world, YouTube performer could probably also work. So would highly illegal activities like "pscyho mob hitman" or "third world mercenary." The aforementioned Rage 4 SWAT member would probably be OK IF he just had to deal with his immediate coworkers, but he doesn't. I don't think he'd ever be able to reach that spot to begin with. I highly doubt he'd be able to get through a service academy, high school or college, the first few years on the beat, or even ordinary life without some kind of incident that kills his chance to get that. There would be why too many complaints about that "maniac cop" scaring half of the city, or general complaints that at the least would mark him as a poor team player. Psychologists evaluating him would mark down all kinds of things in his profile that would ruin his chances. Same thing for the military - he'd be washed out of boot camp as unfit unless we're talking Third World warlord militias.

                  At Rage 5 and above, the Garou would not be able to hold down any job at all except in the most extraordinary or isolated of circumstances. Interaction with the Flock would be minimal. He'd be the crazed loner everyone avoids except a few high Willpower individuals he might have befriended, or who volunteers to see the guy when needed because everyone else is crapping their pants at the mention of the guy.

                  Exceptions exist in small towns and villages (and organizations) that may be kinfolk only. In that case, maybe there is a local policeman who is Rage 5 that is effective at scaring away unwanted intruders. Or a bartender Galliard who likes talking to his customers. Or a community leader who is a Philodox in an isolated "religious" community similar to the Amish or Mennonites. Or the owner of a small business that employs only kinfolk, and is small enough to avoid the eyes of the government on their employment practices. But in all of those cases, these Garou would be isolated and sheltered from ordinary humanity. But even in kinfolk only towns, ordinary humans appear because kinfolk to kinfolk matches sometimes produce ordinary humans who would need to be removed at a certain time. And of course, at some point in time a regular human shows up in town because their car had a flat, or a government agency decides to audit, or a nosey reporter shows up for some reason.

                  The Curse isn't a "I don't like you feeling," it is a "My life is in danger if I stay around you feeling." People can tolerate someone they don't like. They can't tolerate someone they feel danger from. It creates too much stress. Even among Kinfolk, the Curse isn't eliminated, it is just lessened. That gives the ST some leeway on how to interpret things for individual kinfolk and scenes, but the mass of kinfolk will still have a negative reaction the more Rage the Garou has.

                  That is not a problem for me. It pushes the Garou into the wilderness, liminal spaces, and isolated locales that the horror genre puts werewolves in.

                              That is ironic because Garou actually begin the game with their Garou nation occupation already determined - their auspice. Furthermore, Garou can easily survive without a human job. Just live in the wilderness and hunt deer in lupus form. Garou have job duties and responsibilities to the Nation, and you don't need a sept office to do so. Ragabashes can be scouts over known Wyrm lairs. Ahrouns can be sentries. Theurges should be learning rites, creating talens, and creating ties with spirits. Galliards need to master a huge amount of oral lore and heritage. Philodoxes should be studying the Litany, mediating disputes, and making sure their tribe members are upholding tribal ideals. And that is just generic auspice duties. Packs are supposed to have quests, and pack members should be working with their totems to embody that purpose.

                              Garou should have a full time job just being Garou. If a Wyrm menace appears, a spirit needs help, or there's a big argument between septs or packs that could spiral out of control, what does the Garou say to explain they can't make it because they have to get to work on time for their 9-5 job?

                              Now this can be taken too far. It's possible for a Garou character to be so removed from human society, that they become a fantasy adventurer that seems to live in a different world entirely than the actual setting. This is why it can be beneficial to include other elements that help bind them to the real world - you can use the natural environment, ordinary animals as NPCs (although these too can be affected by the Curse, but various Gifts can help overcome that), and even spirits to ground them. And the occasional high Willpower human or kinfolk (knowing or unknowing) NPC can do the same.

                              I can understand why STs may be concerned that not having a job makes Garou too much like fantasy adventurers, and they want the PC to have ties to the human world. But that is exactly the function of kinfolk!

                              The PC I am playing now is a high school drop out who lives in the backyard camper trailer of his extended (knowing) kinfolk family. A buddy's PC (an ahroun) was disowned by his family because of his "emotional problems" which caused him to be ejected out of multiple schools (the last one was a military school he was sent to in order to install discipline) and is now squatting in an abandoned hunter's shack in the woods, but he still gets hand outs from his family secretly because they (unknowing kinfolk) all know the stories of their family's history of "mental illness". Another is a trust fund child who has bought a wilderness estate out of inherited funds, who quit his old job because of a "nervous breakdown" and his inability to lead a "real life" causes problems with his parents and siblings. Despite none of these three characters having a job, we all have plenty to do in interacting with the human world through our kinfolk and neighbors, and focus to varying degrees on Garou issues. One low Rage PC does have a job, but he basically rents out some stuff and have other people work it. But for the most part we're recluses who live on the fringe of society, but we do have strong human relationships through our kinfolk.

                              In some of these cases, the PCs did not bother to buy Kinfolk. So they don't get control of relatives in game, but the ST still roleplays their relationships out.

                                                  Want an education but the Garou hours arent university friendly? Then one of the kinfolks had that position in the university and she would see that you can continue and justify your absences and would assign you a kinfolk to accompany in campus to help you in case you have an "accident"

                                                  Want a house to live in? The local head of the Glasswalker (who is also a kinfolk) owned lots of properties (having garous to back your business interests is useful) can rent you one.

                                                  And those are practical concerns, then there the whole subculture angle. Using the example of the game, the kinfolks there were many childhood friends or at least known from the established families. They had things like a mini league of tennis which the Garou weren't invited because if they did "they had to let them win", it was nothing official or super secretive but more like an inside joke among them.

                                                  Or the players (playing their kinfolks) organized a D&D night and the whole scene was them playing their kinfolk and talking.

                                                  Even plots can revolve around the kinfolks and use them to include the Garou PCs.
                                                  Example: the local GW company had a race team and were competing in a local race and one of the competing teams was pentex subsidiary who were cheating. The race itself was important for the kinfolks themselves (not in the grand scheme of things) as they all cooperate to make it happen. So the PC got involved without stealing the kinfolks show by helping from the sidelines (also because it was kind of illegal to switch drivers in the middle of a race that way). The PCs all got behind that with more investment than fighting a hive, because they knew the people involved and they wanted to help them.

                                                  Also, don't fear to showcase different personalities to the kinfolks. Not all of them are submissive, most kinfolks raised in the nation are like slaves, they know who is their "owner" but also know how much safely they can push their leash. The Kinfolk of my PC was had a dismissive attitude to most Garou edgy but never crossing the disrespectful safe in the knowledge that her connections to both of the most powerful Kinfolk families meant that as long as she never outright insulted a Garou she was untouchable and any Garou who tried would bear the anger of the Sept alpha and an elder.

                                                  Even if just about mating, have an NPC kinfolk (normally a sister/brother or even mother/father) explain to them how the systems works. Many PCs also play the equalitarian Garous who treat kinfolks and Garou the same, in the name of fairness.
                                                  My PC was one of those (he was a lost cub) so then a kinfolk calmly explain to him that he was "one of those Garou" (and that phrase by itself does wonders to showcase the inner culture of the kinfolk btw), that he was dangerous because he didn't get that the rules are also there to protect kinfolks and them pushing them aside put the kinfolks in danger. That when a Kinfolk goes to his "owner" to ask permission to mate with a Garou sometimes is that oppression is real but other times is because if the kinfolk doesn't want to be with the Garou, the kinfolk can deflect the anger of rejection to the Garou owner. That telling "no, I don't want you as a partner" is one of the things that most hurt one´s ego and telling that to a person with anger issues, who can transform into a giant monster and rip you apart and whose life in the society you live it is more valuable is a scary and dangerous proposition.

                                                          How the Curse is handled is one of my main complaints with how the game is commonly played. I’m currently playing a Metis Ahroun, his job in his birth Sept was blacksmithing and maintaining weapons for defense. The ST dropped our pack into a town and expects my character to interact with the townsfolk. I tried to explain how that won’t really work for my character and most people will go out of their way to clear the area any time he shows up. Instead the nice little old lady next door tries to give him cookies. Sure, that may work for our Theurge and Ragabash, but the massive Get Ahroun? I took almost no social skills to reflect how a socially stunted and abused individual would have major issues interacting with anyone, let alone society at large.

                                                          Kinfolk are a great way to get a pack of Garou interacting with NPCs and gives them an emotional investment in the game. Not everyone in a pack can interact well with humans and even those that can will be colored by their association with that weirdo everyone knows must secretly be a murderer or thug. Trying to role play the Curse or get others to do so is difficult because not all players or even STs are aware of how big an impact it should have on the daily lives of the Garou.

                                                          The Garou are monsters, monsters trying to save the world, but monsters nonetheless. The Curse helps prevent the stereotype of furry superheroes. It makes players think about the requirement of cultivating working relationships with Kin. Garou may be incredibly powerful in many regards, but the Curse takes some of that away and presents new and unique challenges to take on during play.
