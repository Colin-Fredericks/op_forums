    Coterie Embraced a Kinfolk: Advice

    05-14-2020, 12:49 PM
    In the game I run my players threw a curve at me. A friend of the teen that they're Guardian/Adopted parent of was suffering nightmares, bouts of temper, feeling like they were being watched. The kid was a kinfolk on the verge of turning into a garou and their first change and was being observed by a pack of bone gnawers.

    The idea was they would take the kid in for a few days, keep an eye on him, get attacked, and either fend off the lupines or, more likely, take the kid and run from these mysterious pursuers. They would harry and herd them and then when they were pinned he would undergo his first change and they would subdue him and then the older lupines would take him away.

    Instead, thinking the kid was marked and now both the kid and their teen were in the know about things supernatural and what little they did know about lupines being that they can be relentless, decided to embrace them to give them an edge in survival. I figured it would be a slim chance that the embrace would stick, kinfolk being resilient/blessed against such things. I rolled and the kid survived the embrace.

    Thoughts and opinions would be welcome. What would the lupine's response be? Retaliation and rage seems appropriate, but what scale? Full on war? Does this include just that one pack or does it include all of the bone gnawers in the Culver City/Greater Los Angeles area? Also, what about the other vampires in the area? Will it drag others into the mess?

    Thank you.

    PS: I thought this was implied, but just for context, the kid didn't know he was kinfolk.

                                      I think Koronus is talking about the Kinfetch spirit that is sometimes "placed" upon newly born kinfolk during the rite of Baptism by Fire. (Koronus can clarify if he is thinking of something else.) Kinfetch spirits are notorious for eventually getting bored and wander off. Sometimes they abandon the kinfolk before they change, this is probably more true for late changing Garou as the spirit decides he isn't going to change. Other times after the change, they simply inform the closest Garou they find instead of finding the right tribe. So if I was ST, I would not agree that the kinfetch would continue to track the kinfolk "until final death". That's very debatable. If I were ST, I'd likely rule the kinfetch would just leave. Furthermore, the rite is not universally done because of the sheer number of kinfolk. It is far more common of certain tribes and lineages than others. Bone Gnawers would likely be one of the tribes that does it the least. So you need to figure in all these factors in order to decide if this is even an issue or how to run it.

                                      However, per your story, we definitely know there was a group of Bone Gnawers actively looking at this teen, and that they knew he was either a kinfolk or Garou soon to have his First Change. We can proceed from there.

                                      As ST, I would definitely have that pack go after the vampires. They would likely inform their sept of what had happened unless there was some crazy reason not to. They would then go off to kill the newly embraced kin. They would view one of their own being a vampire as horrifying and sacrilegious. They would also likely target the Sire for revenge. All the other coterie members would just be collateral damage.

                                      Whether other Lupines would join in, and whether the conflict would spread to other vampires depends on a lot of things. The Bone Gnawers are notorious for being lax about their duties and generally not caring. If individuals had a personal reason for caring about their kin, they would likely get involved. But if not, they might be very passive - willing to pass along information, but not do any actual fighting. Another question is whether there are other tribes locally involved. They might be more willing to hunt or push to expand the conflict to other vampires. Still another is what context in local Lupine-Vampire relations did this happen in. If this is one more incident in a series of provocations, it might be spark a full out assault. If this happened out of the blue in otherwise good terms, the conflict may not spread.

                                      Another thing to remember is that very often the Bone Gnawers and Nosferatu pass information on to each other, both being despised members of their own societies. This is usually downplayed since Revised edition, but was very much an element in earlier editions.

                                      If I was ST, this is how I'd run it based on the information you've given, and not having access to anything else that may cause me to think differently. That BG pack will try to kill the new fledgling and Sire. Any other coterie members (or NPCs) will be attacked if they get in the way. That pack will be assisted by a few other BG members who likely won't physically fight. They'll use spirits to try to find and track their prey and inform them when it is safest to do so (but remember that in a city, there are lots of unfriendly spirits). I'd also inform the vampire PCs of things like stray dogs, city pigeons, and the like seem to be following them or at least looking in their way. It's creepy, and if they have enough Animalism, they might find out why.

                                      Second, in order to try to keep the general peace, the BG information pipeline to the Nosferatu goes into effect. The Nosferatu are informed that this specific coterie, especially this person (the Sire) has offended the Lupines and will be hunted down until final destruction. Any vampire who does not want to be involved should step away from them. The Lupines who tell them this, of course, is informing them that they are doing this as a "favor" to their friends so they don't get intentionally slaughtered. They wouldn't tell the Nos any other information. They don't want to educate potential enemies on concepts like kinfolk, kinfetches, how new Garou are made, etc. If the kin was illegally embraced (no permission from the Prince or other appropriate elder), they might let that slip only to encourage other vampires to hunt them down too. The idea is to make all the other vampires socially abandon and isolate the PCs to their fates. That limits the likelihood of a greater conflict they don't want, and makes it easier to isolate and kill their true prey. Word should soon spread to the PCs they are in trouble and they see everyone slam doors in their faces. Note, I highly recommend that such a notice ONLY happens after a first attempt to kill the PCs happen. The lupines don't want to tip their hands and alert their prey. Notice should only come after the PCs realize they are being targeted and t he cat is out of the bag.

                                      The BG pack should be smart - trying to hunt and kill their vampire targets during the day as much as possible.

                                      The real question is what are you prepared to do as ST? What "should" happen is that the NPC fledgling and the PC Sire gets killed and things go back to normal. The other PCs who survive play on, and the player of the newly killed PC creates a new character. Or that the targeted PC leaves the city in order to survive and rolls up a new character anyway. Lesson learned and everyone agrees that this outcome makes the most sense and there are no hard feelings.

                                      In reality, we all know that in many gaming groups, deliberately trying to kill a PC (even for actual mistakes) leads to bad blood. Players don't like losing their PCs especially for things they don't know about (how was I to know that NPC was a kinfolk?). So you may be tempted to roleplay things out so that the vampire PCs beat the BG pack somehow and everyone lives happily ever after. Unfortunately, that is very unrealistic (unless the PCs really, really work their asses off and actually earn it - I always believe in rewarding those who earn things) and make the lupines look like they aren't a threat. That likely subverts the setting which is a bad thing.

                                      So you may want to encourage alternate solutions that still teach the PCs a lesson. The newly embraced NPC needs to be casualty. The PC Sire might survive if the PCs come up with a great plan to pin the blame on some other vampire, fake his death, temporarily exile him and bring him back secretly later, or something else. Something that allows them to "win" by saving the PC for another night, but not deflating the menace of the lupines.

                                      Another option is that while the BG pack successfully kills the NPC childe, that the PCs escape. And rather than keep attacking short term to kill the coterie when they are on their highest alert, is to temporarily lay low to lull the PCs into complacency. The BG pack them becomes a long term menace that continues to monitor the PCs and will attempt to slay the Sire at a later time. If the BG pack ever loses track of them, I'd have them interrogate and possibly torch an NPC vampire last seen with them that can update them to their whereabouts or other information. This is more of a slow burn tactic, but at least preserves the enmity of the BG pack, establishes them as an ongoing menace that will not go away, and serves to continue isolate the PCs from the rest of vampire society (perhaps encouraging the Players to come up with a solution themselves).

                                              Definitely seconding all of what Black Fox said and figured that to be helpful instead of simply repeating what they said and the reasoning I'd add some context about the garou to explain why they would behave this way.

                                              The Garou are spiritual warriors, flesh and spirit made one, who adamantly believe that they were chosen by Gaia, the spirit of the Earth Mother, to defend Her body against all threats. The garou, even outcasts amongst them such as the Bone Gnawers, are fanatically devoted to Gaia as it is both their nature and they see spiritual evidence of Her divinity everywhere.

                                              The Garou defend Gaia from the predation primarily of the corrupted Wyrm. Garou cosmology is defined by The Triat, three spirits of primordial forces shaping reality. The Wyld is creation, a vast chaotic maelstrom that spews forth life and existence and one that modern Garou feel the need to protect alongside Gaia. The Weaver is structure, the force that takes what The Wyld creates and spins it into the structure of reality, however the modern Weaver is considered mad as it intends to calcify creation and lead it to stagnancy. The Wyrm is destruction, the force that removes structure to create room for new life and matter, but the Wyrm has fallen and is now corrupted as it now pursues the corruption and defilement of all the world, including Gaia, until it all finally collapses into oblivion.

                                              The Garou see themselves as divinely appointed champions of Gaia as her Wild Children who must War against the Wyrm (and Weaver) in order to protect their Mother and all of Her sacred creation.

                                              The reason why Garou loath kindred, aside from a long history of bloodshed and betrayal, is that they view vampires as minions of both the Weaver and Wyrm. They are stagnant and apart from the natural cycle of life, reflecting Weaver taint. They are plagues who cause strife and pain in their never ending search for blood, reflecting Wyrm taint.

                                              The Garou care for all life in the abstract but for themselves and their families (kinfolk) with violent passion in practice. The idea of becoming a vampire is a horrific separation from Gaia's love and an active harm done to the Mother's body, one any good Garou would rather die then be party off.

                                              The embrace of a kinfolk is not only horrific in that it propogates vampirism, feeds the Weaver and Wyrm while harming Gaia; it is horrific to them in that their own family, those with the blood of Gaia's blessed children and the potential to further that blessing, has been so defiled and ripped bodily from Gaia's bosom and the love of their kin.

                                              The Garou consider it a great tragedy and many Storytellers sing ancient tales of kinfolk and Garou who became vampires and had to be destroyed. It is one of the highest sorrows possible for the garou.

                                              But while the kinfolk will be tearfully destroyed, the mourning family has nothing in the way of sympathy for those who cursed their kin and everything in the way of hatred and blood. The average Garou would accept death in the pursuit of destroying the one who spread the curse of vampirism to her kin. They would hunt the damned to the end of the earth and rest only when their hatred and sorrow was quenched in the ashes of the vampire.

                                              Many Garou who undergo this experience also become prolific vampire hunters in general, having experienced the pain and horror that the kindred cause. This is one part of explaining the ancient conflict between vampires and werewolves.

                                              All that aside, like Black Fox said, consider the needs of your own story and chronicle first. This was all simply to provide a greater understand of why werewolves react the way they do.
