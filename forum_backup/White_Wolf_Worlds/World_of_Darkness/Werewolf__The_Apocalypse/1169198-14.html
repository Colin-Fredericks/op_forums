                                Technically, most if not all Werewolves would be classified as Obese in Crinos and Glabro forms. Using the numbers (admittedly pulled off a website and not out of the books, so check me on this) going from Homid to Glabro adds 6 inches and 100% to 200% in weight, and going from Glabro to Crinos grows in height by half and adds another 100% to 200% in weight. Both weight increases are stated to be all muscle, but it doesn't actually matter for this calculation.

                                As a starting point I chose someone who is 5'10 and 140 pounds. That's a 20 on the BMI, which for that height is the middle point of normal (BMI 20.1). Going with the lowest possible weight increase, this werewolf's Glabro would be 6'4" and 280 pounds. That's a 34.1 BMI, which is Obese. Shifting up to Crinos takes the werewolf to 9'6" and 560, which is an Obese 30.3. Even if we assume that the weight and height increase to Crinos should be based on the Homid weight and height, that's still 7'11' (small for Crinos) and 420 pounds, which is 32.7 BMI. I redid the calculations again using a Homid in the low end of Normal (BMI 18.5) and the only thing that changed is that the 9'6" Crinos form became merely Overweight.

                                None of this really means anything except that the BMI makes no sense, especially not when applied to giant wolf men.

                                                    The text specifically said something even weirder, that Garou prioritize muscular growth over fat storage (page 12 under "Enhanced Physique"). Which is not how macromolecules and nutrition work; the filaments that make up muscles have a rather different chemical structure apart from glycerin and fatty acid chains, and they are not mutually exclusive anabolic reactions. Also, if you live in a cold-ass winter wonderland, as wolves tend to do, not having fat storage is a terrible problem. Seasonal fat increase is necessary to survive for most animals (especially for the Gurahl, who tend to hibernate as their kin also usually do, and the Mokole, though they can usually get away with storing it in their tails), so if the Garou are life "as Gaia intended", it's odd that their natural cycles are being inhibited. In general, the explanation is the kind of overthinking into ridiculousness that marked Exalted 2nd edition at many points; the game didn't need to describe metabolic oddities for Garou and Fera. Especially since they seem to make it rather difficult to disguise one's self as a normal human, which is the entire point of most Homid forms among the changers. Since Garou are not Ananasi or Rokea, though, it seems overdone.
