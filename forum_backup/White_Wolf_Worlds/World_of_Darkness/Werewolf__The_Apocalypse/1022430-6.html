                    Psychic Vampirism is much better than Soul-Stealing in my opinion because Psychic Vampirism is more flexible and does not cost Willpower. With Psychic Vampirism 3, you can steal bashing health levels and use them to heal and, when you are healed, change them to two points of Willpower each. With Psychic Vampirism 4, you can steal lethal health levels and use them to heal bashing health levels, lethal health levels, or use them to downgrade aggravated damage to lethal damage and, when you are healed, change them to three points of Willpower each. Of course, you can do different things with Soul-Stealing than Psychic Vampirism (such as boosting Health Levels with level 4 and stealing Gnosis with level 5), but I would rather have Psychic Vampirism myself.

                                                    Well, one possible reason why people do not have all of the answers is the rarity of supernatural creatures. While lesser supernatural creatures in totality may be uncommon, greater supernatural creatures in totality are rare. Targeting the right person, even if they do not have supernatural defenses, is nearly impossible just through random selection (something on the order of 0.01% of the global population are greater supernatural, and half of that population is defended by the Mists).

                                                    Greater supernatural creatures also usually have some sort of gimmick that protects them from mundane perception (the Delirium for Fera and Possessed, the Fog for Wraiths, and the Mists for Changelings and Chimera). Especially in the case of the Mists, it is very easy for everyone to forget that they dealt with Changelings or Chimera within a few minutes after witnessing them in the Fae Mien (this is even true for supernatural creatures, which is a mixed blessing when Changelings or Chimera are trying to find allies that they do not have to Enchant). In the case of the Delirium or the Fog, 86% of the mortal population will forget that they encountered the greater supernatural creature and another 12% of the mortal population will rationalize the experience, meaning that only 2% of the population will accurately remember the event (and their associates will think that they are insane when they talk about ghosts or werewolves). A Sorcerer would find it exceptionally hard to convince their superiors that greater supernatural entities exist (perhaps with the exception of Mages and Vampires) just because their superiors are cognitively incapable of remembering if they witnessed supernatural events.

                                                    For example, the average FBI agent has a Willpower of 6 and possesses no Occult Knowledge, so he will be Conciliatory when affected by the Delirium and the Fog, and he will forget the events minutes after they happen. Since he will most likely forget or rationalize the events after they occur, the majority of greater supernatural creatures will probably not bother to kill him, as dead (or missing) government agents tend to bring around people asking uncomfortable questions. An FBI agent affected by the Delirium will beg for his life but, after the Garou finishes her mission and leaves, will forget the encounter and will report the massacre or sabotage to his superiors as if he just stumbled upon the scene.

                                                        That still only addresses human interactions with the Supernatural. The setting doesn't work as written because the various major Supernaturals have undefendable means of spying on one another (and they certainly at least have idea on where the others are located).                                                         Animal spies can be used against all earthly Supernatural fortresses (Caerns, Chantries, Havens) with little way to defend against them. Projection offers a backdoor into those places. The Umbra serves as a backdoor into most places. The only reason Mage Chantries get to be even somewhat defensible is because of the Ritual casting game mechanics (generally added together with OOC knowledge being sprinkled into Mage defense setups).
