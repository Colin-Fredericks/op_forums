            Originally posted by idpersona View Post
            So Ahroun (and Galliards and maybe Philodox) Garou just don't work in city Caerns unless they stay on the bawn or in the Umbra?
            ​Pretty much, yeah.

            ​Now that's not to say that in-setting there can't be Ahroun in the city -- just that they face severe problems due to the sheer ubiquity of humans. There's going to be just as many Ahroun in a Glass Walker sept in a city as a caern in the darkened wilderness in Sibera. It's also not to say that Ahroun would have no place or standing in an urban sept -- nobody is going to be able to do spirit combat like the Ahroun will, so their value to a caern's defenses is frankly priceless. Your Warder is going to almost certainly be a full-moon, and that dude is going to always want more of his fellow full-moons to fill posts among the sept guardians. And beyond the Bawn, if the sept needs a sort of Garou SWAT team to take care of specific threats, the Ahroun are perfect for that.

            ​That also doesn't mean that in specific cases, an Ahroun is a crippling anchor when dealing with humans. If the human in question (let's say a Contact) has a high enough Willpower to deal, then no problemo. Alternately, if the pack needs some who specifically terrifies people, to do some good old fashioned intimidation, the Ahroun is the obvious choice.

            ​But outside those conditions -- in general? No way. Unless the Ahroun is sitting back at the caern practicing martial arts or whatever while the rest of the pack goes out and actually does story-related stuff -- he cripples the pack.

            Originally posted by idpersona

                            Originally posted by Uniform Two Six View Post

There's nothing in the description that indicates (to me at any rate) that it would affect the Curse's tendency to cause a human to "avoid contact as much as possible".
                            "Success lowers the difficulty of all social rolls by one for the rest of the scene, and allows successful rolls to have uncommonly strong impact (such as changing long-held political views, or causing an addict to seriously reconsider the course of his life)."

                            The second half is the important part.

                            If Persuasion lets you convince a person that's opposed to marriage equality to support it with a single roll, it can convince someone to ignore their desire to run from your angry friend for a conversation.

                            I'm not sure where there's room for disagreement on interpretation. The Gift is more than a -1 difficulty boost, but also a narrative boost in what you can do with your social rolls in terms of the level of their impact. You're going to have to make a stronger case here.
                            ​
                            Meh... The mechanical description doesn't, but there's narrative that even when the Garou is in the Penumbra, sometimes humans get the creepy feeling that they're being followed or watched.
                            These are separate things. Peeking might give someone the creeps. Rage doesn't inherently change this. This doesn't support your assertions that the Curse lets humans detect Garou without actually being aware of the Garou's presence. Even if they have an ill feeling, it doesn't equate to blowing the Garou's attempts at stealth; people get the chills all the time without it being anything.

                            If the Garou is watching a human with a pair of binoculars, then I'd agree, but I'd rule that if they're trying to tail someone, either they keep enough of a distance that they risk losing him in the crowd, or they get close enough that the Curse risks giving the werewolf away.
                            There's a lot of middle ground here (esp. when taking various level 1 Gifts into account that do things like extend the range of your senses).

                            And again, you're neglecting a pack working as an actual pack. A Galliard sits back and uses ranged senses (so they don't upset people and reveal the tail) to aid their packmates on the ground using mystic forms of communication (like Mindspeak or Pack Link) to allow them to play mobile eye-in-the-sky (and this is something that Glasswalker Revised paints as a common role for GW Galliards). This isn't having them sit at home in the Sept, because they're being active in the pack's efforts and directly contributing (however you want to mechanically model that).

                            Is an Ahroun going to be the best choice for stealthily trailing a human through a crowd? No. But that's why the Garou function in packs with Auspices that have strongly defined default rolls. The Ragabash is the sneak/scout that's got all the stuff to help them follow up close. Part of playing WtA has always been balancing the fact that because of Auspice roles, the pack is very likely to have one member that's much better at a set of tasks than the rest of them, and juggling that spotlight time.
