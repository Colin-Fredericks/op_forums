    Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
    One more question before I dive in: the group also has a Stargazer, so I would like to involve Chimera. Does she have a role in CtD? If not, is there an entity she could substitute?
    No role, but nothing barring it,either.

    CtD doesn't usually deal with this kind of entity, but they totally can exist out there. For most Changelings it wouldn't be distinguishable in the Dreaming from any other Chimera (in their terms, a dream-thing that isn't part of a regular enough structure to be a fae). They don't have gods or something, and they accept the nature of the Dreaming without ever questioning if there's something actually guiding it in any way, shape or form, they accept its (mostly narrative) logic at face value.

    So the totem may have any role whatsoever you want, and probably only those who deal with spirits will be able to notice. But at the same time, since you want this to be a place that surprises your players in how it works differently from the Umbra at large, limiting Chimera's power over the place may be a good idea. It may still be vastly knowable, and able to help in many ways, just not in the ways the PCs expect.

    Keep in mind that a few Changelings, like the Nunnehi, deal with spirits and even have totems. But they deal with spirits through normal Umbra, as a somewhat distinct thing.

    Black Fox's cosmology on both posts is the state-of-the-art you'll find on the WtA part of the equation, as he already knows a lot about the rest of the WoD, but tackles this from the WtA perspective. Changeling will help you big with the part on making it different and surprising, and maybe adding fae NPCs if you feel it will be fun.

                        Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
                        As is, the whimsy (I hope that is the right English word; it's not meant negatively) of the CtD setting would clash somewhat fiercely with the tone of the campaign so far, and moving the presentation more towards a Lovecraftian experience in general might be a solution to that. Maybe the malfunctioning pair of Silence and Night has begun to let raw material from an 'outside source' through - I consider nightmares and bad dreams part of the normal state of affairs, so having cosmic horror as an alternative for 'taint' is a good option. A high level source like a Neverborn (which could further connect Underworld and Dreaming) poisoning Wyld's input or something along those lines.
                        Yes, this is the correct word, it isn't inherently negative or anything.

                        But I want to raise a few points here: first, Lovecraft's Dream Cycle and his Dreamlands are quite whimsical on their own. While you'll see a number of horror things, it's not your regular cosmic horror. Which brings the second, you can't actually take whimsy out of dreams. That's a lot of what gives them their weirdness, as they refuse to work under "rational" or "natural" rules.

                        Third and tying to the other two, a lot of people don't get what Changeling's whimsy is (or at least can be), and how it can be used in non-whimsical plots.

                        Working with dreams in a "serious" game will probably focus on shock, contradiction, weirdness and contrast. Shock comes from how quickly dreams evolve into nightmares and how extreme they can be. This is not necessarily Alice in Wonderland, it can as easily be It, Call of Cthulhu or Nightmare in Elm Street, or even simply a Slasher movie. The Joker is as valid a dream entity as anything else, and fae can be as violent and gore as any Werewolf, but with far less reason.

                        Contrast comes from how apparent whimsy can reveal horror or terror without an actual change. Make-up made with human fluids, fancy furniture with internal organs begging for mercy, a laughing clown that helps the Pack while snacking on living children that may well be, indeed, living children from the Waking World. Or any situation that while it resembles a game or something like that, is as serious as it gets for either tragedy or horror.

                        All over the place in a dream there's weirdness, things that don't follow the logic PCs expect. They do follow some logic, though, the point is for you to define what this logic is. In this sense, the Umbra can be thought as one kind of dream-logic, as many things resemble the material world in some way, but they don't work as in the material, so for a newcomer they make no sense until they learn how spirits work. The difference with dreams is that the logic of one isn't necessarily the logic of another. You can design a logic based on the mental state of a dreamer, or a story genre, or a word-game, whatever you want for that particular dream.

                        Finally, you can use the more benevolent aspects of dreams to contrast with more serious stuff to have your PCs know what's at stake. I personally find horror and drama too bland when it's pure cynicism, grimdark with nothing to contrast the darkness, and it's definitely not what Werewolf is built upon. In this sense the dreams are just another aspect of nature. Whimsy is also a version of idyllic, which is the main contrast of Werewolf. It's nature as it pertains to dreams when things are balanced and corruption is held at bay. It doesn't have to be extreme or all over the place, just be there enough for the PCs to know what is being lost as the world tumbles towards the Apocalypse.
