        Well, legal adoption usually requires an in-depth background check by the state authorities, for criminal behavior and financial stability, usually after a few years of taking care of children in the foster care system (ideally showing the state authorities that you have the compassion and discipline to take care of a young child). The process is usually abbreviated if the child is a relative, like a cousin or a grandchild, and the majority of states only require a cursory background check, to make sure that the person adopting the child is not a sex criminal and to make sure that the person can take care of the child. It would be nearly impossible to keep the adoption of a non-relative secret from anyone, as the relevant state authorities usually interview close associates of the adopting parent. Of course, this is the average state. Some states, like New Jersey, are well known for cutting corners though, usually resulting in some horrific consequences for the children.
