    I think the Umbra needs to exist in the context of an animistic worldview. It's about the shamanistic and ritualized dealings with various categories of spirits, both good and bad. For Garou these practices are just even more real because they personally can enter the spirit realm. And the Theurge Auspice specializes in dealing with these ritualized communications with spirits or practicing the right Rites to sanctify a hunt or honor an Ancestor spirit before summoning it.

    Just making it a horror realm where entering it means going through hell misses the mark, I think. At that point, the Theurge Auspice becomes superfluous - in the same way Galliards become irrelevant when W5 degrades the effectiveness of oral history in the name of getting away from deeper lore/worldbuilding.

                PHASE THREE PART EIGHT

                Storytelling

                The main considerations for storytelling remain mostly the same, although there does seem to be slight change of focus on the emotional well-being of the players, as opposed to the long-range development of the characters. This might be due to the 'Nordic' style of playing that is favored by the developers. While subtle, this change leads to shorter, one-and-done stories, instead of longer campaigns. The rules favor this, such as the new experience tracks for Gifts that are complicated yet still unstructured to a degree.

                Strangely, the trigger warnings are incredibly detailed and include outside links to other sources. It might be a generational thing, but I've never had so many potential problems called out. Not really a bad thing, just somewhat unusual

                Antagonists and Others

                I've already mentioned spirit antagonists in the section on the Umbra, so this part will focus on the more corporeal enemies, as there really isn't anything that could particularly be called an ally since Kinfolk have been removed.

                Pentex is still around, although they are now an international holding company rather than an actual corporate entity, although this is a fine distinction. Instead of a servant of the Wyrm, Pentex is now beholden to Fly as its Patron, who even offers the executives Gifts. Sure, there are still Banes and Fomori about, but the interaction between Pentex and the Wyrm is different. [As an aside, it was the Fianna who initially named the Ban-possessed Fomori after the enemies of the Fae allies of the Tribe. Since that connection is now gone, as well as the cultures that spawned them, how did the name come about?]

                A common ally of Pentex is the Black Spiral Dancer Tribe. Somehow, their origin remains about the same. The White Howlers went into a cavernous area to confront the Wyrm, only to wind up corrupted instead. Now, the exact time this happened is unknown, but it was in the fairly distant past. How did every Howler across the world wind up in the same place at that right time, even if it was in the Umbra? How does the Tribe manage to continue, if there is now genetic component to being Garou? Is there 'recruitment' drives to convert other Garou? Does the BSD's new patron Bat manage to trigger the transformation and hope the new cub chooses a path of corruption? Doesn't really make sense. Also, the new BSDs see themselves as ushering Gaia into the next phase of the cycle so that something new will be born, as opposed to just making things worse. Odd philosophy. At least it seems to be easier to cure them, even without Erebus.

                There are also two other Tribes that have left the Garou Nation. The Stargazers left to try new means of saving Gaia, under a new unknown Patron. For some reason, no Garou has sought to ask who their new Parton is after Chimera left under mutual terms. Stargazers are likely to help in many situations, just not in the expected ways. (The other Tribe, a reimagining of an old one, will not be mentioned here. Look for this Tribe to be highlighted in Phase Four.)

                There are new types of lycanthropes beyond the Garou, and the old Skinwalkers. No one really knows how many of these shifters came about, and that is actually a good idea. A variety of wolf-changers outside of the Garou adds a layer of intrigue that was missing from previous editions, but was added to the CoD Werewolf game. This is the best new treatment I have seen in W5. While previous editions had only one known way of becoming a werewolf outside of being born one, and that had the prerequisite of being Kinfolk, having multiple ways suggests that there are mysteries beyond even Gaia and that Garou really don't have everything figured out.

                The Fera still exist, or at least some do. Apparently, they still know their cultural history, but they aren't telling the Garou anything about why they really don't like each other. Only a raven-shifter and a spider-shifter are mentioned, so there is no way of knowing what else is out there. Not much is told about them. For instance, we aren't told if they have a form similar to Crinos, only that they can shift into an animal form, swarm for spiders. More vagueness. Here, this isn't as helpful as it was for the other werewolves.

                Other supernatural splats get write-ups, longer ones as their details are being 'fleshed' out, as it were. While the vampires feel similar, the other three don't. This is especially true for the fae character who is not acting like a Changeling at all. I fear this will bode even more changes to the other lines, perhaps greater than what happened to the Garou.

                A few mortal hunters and animals are mentioned. No major changes from previous encounters, although I would say the hunters feel even more narrowly defined and more generic than in previous editions. Much of the part on animals has been pasted from V5 with a few changes.

                THWarted
