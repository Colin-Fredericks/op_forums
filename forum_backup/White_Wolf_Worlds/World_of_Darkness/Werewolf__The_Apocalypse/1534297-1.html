                                            Originally posted by Ravnos View Post

                                            I always felt the premise of WtA was that the Garou can't save the world (or weren't ever able to) because there is too much in-fighting, conflict, raging at each other.
                                            Both physically and philosophically
                                            And this was valid both for tribes and individual Garou mindsets.

                                            It can get lost often because WtA has a lot of premises, and I always felt the books tend to push the players to have different primary objectives (fight the Wyrm and maybe the Weaver)

                                            It's also a metaphor of sort for our world. We don't save the world because we're very much embroiled in our differences. Simplistic as it may be, it's not really far from the truth
                                            I'm not a fan of turning Garou into too much of an allegory for what we do in our world. That's why I think "can't" is the wrong way to look at it. If anything, in WtA the Garou have the best chance at saving the world compared to any mundane human activist. And the main-reason for that is that the setting is animistic. The animism in WtA isn't just a matter of opinion but a fact. The Umbra is real and a very important part of the struggle. Garou not only have a better understanding of the reality of the setting (aka Gaia and the Triad) but they also can interact with and counteract the powers that reign in the Umbra. A human activist can't do that.

                                            The Garou aren't struggling because they can't do better but because they need better tactics and strategies to keep up with what the Wyrm, the Weaver and (occasionally) the Wyld is throwing at them. That's where the struggle is. And of course there are internal political struggles to go along with resolving that problem.

                                            The W5 corebook was way too pessimistic in that regard, in my opinion. Especially because I don't think any edition of WtA is well-suited for this take. After all, "can't" only makes sense if the idea is that there's something fundamentally wrong with Garou, not on an individual level but far more significantly in what they are as a group of people and/or what their nature is. And then you have a game with five Forms with the highlight being Crions, the "war-Form", supernatural powers you get from making pacts with Spirits, focusing on fighting the Wyrm - but supposedly in this conception of "can't" all these things I've listed are a waste of time and don't amount to anything at the end of the day. Maybe these things actually just even make things worse. That would certainly fit into a more pessimistic take on the WtA-setting. But that would be such a drab game! Using that premise the players are set up to lose. Why make the game about Garou then in the first place? If doom is the only thing awaiting them, why would you play that? And even W5 occasionally mentions the idea of preventing the Apocalypse. Since the end-result of the "can't"-take is for WtA to not be about preventing the Apocalypse. But that kind of WtA would need to be radically different in my opinion to embrace a concept like that (even W5's somewhat different premise is different enough for that, I think).

                                            Also, in the context of environmental activism which is one of the closest real-world themes one can bring up with the WtA-premise, it doesn't feel right to me to make a central tenet of such a setting: "You can't make a difference, don't even try.". Again, this is a setting where an animistic conception of the world is the correct one. So Garou are the primary agents who can change things for the better. So to attach "can't" to that doesn't feel right to me.

                                                    Here's the writeup for the two Concordats from WtE Preview materials if anyone interested:

                                                    First we got the Western Concordat who are characterized as conservatives that clings to the traditions of the garou nation as a lifeline in those uncertain times and even some camps are slowly starting realize that they might need to change to adapt to they new circumstances.


                                                    The Western Concordat are myriad of contradictions. One the one hand, they are made up of some of the most fierce traditionalists out there, such as the Skysingers and Fenrir, led and formed by the Silver Fangs. On the other, they also count amongst their ranks such radicals as the Glasswalkers, and are often forced to work outside and even against their own traditions in order to have their goals realized. The places where this collection of tribes agree are the most revealing as to their ultimate aims and philosophies, but even that can be difficult to parse from an outsider’s perspective.

                                                    The traditions that they uphold have been effective in the past in both combating the Wyrm, its minions, and the spiritual grief of Harano that has swept through the Garou tribes. While others criticize the hierarchies that the Westcons rely on, they cannot fully appreciate the deep sense of purpose that fighting under a single banner, or for the glory of the heroes of old instills. There is nothing quite like seeing your king, your leader, the monarch imbued with Celestine power leading a charge and fighting alongside you to inspire warriors to claim the greatest victories against the Wyrm imaginable.

                                                    This sense of pure focus, purpose, and confidence can only be experienced, which is part of the reason why outside criticism of the Silver Crown is not often taken as seriously as from within. The biting words of Black Furies and Bone Gnawers are all well and good, but they do not strike the same chord in the Western Concordat as arguments made from its own people or those who have left the faction for another. Broadly, every tribe in the Westcons are united through tradition of some form: the Skysingers and Fenrir reject the Weaver and are committed to fighting and destroying the Wyrm, the Silver Fangs and Skysingers harken back to their own traditions and old ways to resist the spirit-sickness of Harano and work to reawaken Gaia.

                                                    Where the Glass Walkers fit into these traditionalists is not so clear in terms of actual philosophy, but more in their actions. Originally forming part of the Eastern Concordat, the children of Cockroach vehemently opposed the Hearthbound, changing allegiance to the Westcons and finding common ground with Skysingers in their poor treatment of their kinfolk and their marginalization of warborn. This political, rather than philosophical, decision also intentionally alienated the Red Talons from the Westcons, supposedly in order to stop the faction from descending into extremist rejection of humanity. It would not be a stretch to say that the one tradition that unites a large swath of the Western Concordat is just the old discrimination against kinfolk and warborn, the Get of Fenris being the outlier here. The history of influence the Silver Fangs have held over the Westcons cannot be understated.

                                                    Not only was it held together through the herculean efforts of Jason “The Wyldstorm Rider” Wyrmslayer, but such cries for unity and strength echo the deeds of High King Albrecht in his efforts to bolster the Garou Nation against despair in the face of Apocalypse. This pattern of powerful Silver Fang leaders has continued through the Age of Heroes within the Western Concordat, and their aim to reunify the Garou Nation through a return to the traditions that held fast during Apocalypse has garnered much support from Skysingers and Fenrir alike.

                                                    A return to these traditions and the hierarchies they stand on, however, also brings with it the half-scabbed wounds that those hierarchies have wrought upon others in the past. For example, the monarchist bent of the Silver Fangs is in direct opposition to the Dawn Tribes’ philosophy of not bending the knee to kings or colonists. It also causes problems when the more autonomous political system of the Skysingers clash with notions of loyalty to a crown, echoing the historical colonization of Celtic peoples by empire. All of this isn’t to say that the Western Concordat is doomed or useless.

                                                    It is undeniable that the Western Concordat field some of the most dedicated and loyal warriors around, and the hierarchical nature of the faction means that any one group of these fighters can be dispatched and relied upon to work effectively with little oversight. Many a bane has been defeated and many a powerful formori has been slain before it can wreak havoc by the mobilization of Westcon troops in their territories, and the territories of those that would have them. The Shadow Lords may have once been the tacticians and careful planners of efficiency of the Westcons, but it would be a mistake to assume that all of their abilities and knowledge left with their exodus from the faction. Unity and strength of purpose is what led the Garou Nation out of the Age of Apocalypse with the pleas of courage from High King Albrecht, and so it is no surprise that the Westcons believe those are the things that will lead it to ultimate victory and the revival of Gaia.

                                                    It would be foolish to say that this faction cannot change, and in fact change is already happening with the different reactions to the Hearthbound and changing times of the Age of Heroes. Traditions will change in order to adapt to different circumstances, and perhaps all it needs is a powerful leader to rise from the newest generation of Garou and seize renown, leading the Western Concordat to true unity, and true strength.
                                                    This is Contrasted by the progressive Eastern Concordat who are concerned with integrating the kinfolk into a more active role in the fight agenst the Wyrm and some of them that the actual way forward is to find a way to purify the Wyrm and undo the damage the weaver did to him and some bleaves that they should kill the current incanation of the Wyrm and weaver and some beleaves taht they simply have to kill the Wyrm so he can be reincarnated in a purer form.

                                                    heir numbers dwindled to just 12 in total, the Stargazers merged with the Children of Gaia and pushed the tribe towards assuming a role as teachers of the Garou. Josef Konietzko, Margrave of the Shadow Lords, saw an opportunity for the Silver Fangs to lose political footing and quickly helped bolster these efforts, rallying the Black Furies, Bone Gnawers, and (initially) the Glass Walkers into joining them.

                                                    The Margrave leveraged his influence over the creation of the shard seeds to create a new faction built around the idea of tolerance for the Fera and working with the Weaver to protect the Gaian Realm. His vision, along with that of Daly and others, shaped the Concordat into a faction devoted to adapting to changes in the world and understanding how and why these changes took place. They dream of a world where the Garou are leaders, driving humanity forward in directions that ultimately support the war against the Wyrm. Using his powerfully charismatic presence, the Margrave bound together the tribal alliance into a powerful political organization on the Council of Tribes Eastcons happily use the information and power they wield to hold space for humans, especially those who could deeply impact the world around them.

                                                    This faction urges a stronger and more thorough integration of Kinfolk into Garou society as warriors, healers, and visionaries, much like the Dawn Tribes. They do not want to hide their Kinfolk away, keeping them from the world with the excuse of protecting them. Instead, Eastern Concordat members wish to teach and prepare their Kinfolk, giving them knowledge and tools to take a more active role in the world in which they live, embracing the ways and guidance of the Hearthbound to integrate kin into their societies. Through their kin, the Eastern Concordat actively seeks influence in the mortal halls of power, leveraging humanity itself to benefit Gaia and all her children.

                                                    Eastcons believe that keeping up with the modern era is of import. Stagnation is another form of corruption, and by refusing to change with the world around them, the Garou have let this corruption take root and fester. Only by learning and growing can they embody the balance they seek to forge anew in the Triat. Initially strongly supported by the Glass Walkers, Cockroach’s children shifted alliances to the Western Concordat, both in opposition to the Eastcons’ willingness to work with the Hearthbound, and to shift the Overton Window away from the Red Talons, who they believed were close to pushing the Westcons to violent extremism. Of the Westcon tribes, the Glass Walkers still maintain the best relations with this faction, often bridging the communication gap.

                                                    Eastcons believe that the entropic aspect of the Triat must exist to allow for healthy ends within the Gaian Realm and the Umbra. The Weaver’s webs have driven the Wyrm mad with pain, changing his nature to corruption and decay. This taint prevents the Wyld from recycling what remains into new creations for the Weaver to form and fashion. The corruption that has overtaken the Wyrm must be rooted out and destroyed so that it can return to the role for which Gaia created it in the first place. Some members advocate the destruction of the current incarnation of the Wyrm, allowing the Wyld to forge a new Wyrm, untainted by madness, restoring the balance.

                                                    Others believe that some ritual or artifact may release the madness haunting the Wyrm, allowing it to see its purpose and resume that function more clearly. No one knows for sure the right course of action, but all seek to find a way to restore Gaia’s original vision before it is too late. Where the primary domains of the Dawn Tribes and Westcons are largely relegated to the remaining wild spaces across the world, Eastcons are relatively metropolitan. They hold most (but not all) of their domains within humanity’s population centers, more often than not focusing much of their efforts on fighting the forces of corruption that target marginalized groups. While nowhere near as integrated into human society as the Glass Walkers, you’ll hardly find a city in the Pacific Northwest that doesn’t have, at minimum, a pack of Eastcons operating within it’s city limits.

                                                    The Children of Gaia and Bone Gnawers are tied for possessing the largest demographical representation across the Pacific Northwest. Bone Gnawers operate virtually anywhere that other Garou neglect: inner city flophouses, mutual support networks, union halls, underpasses, and forest squats all comprise their domains. The coggies consider all of Gaia their domain, and their fellowships fill out almost everywhere else, from inner-city secular Jewish communities to forest Pagan enclaves, they exist close to places of conflict, particularly between Garou and close to places of Power that require aid and education on the creation and maintenance of Shard Seeds. The Black Furies can be found in all places of suppressed human voices, from social workers to civil rights activists, their roles as avengers of women and oppressed voices ever-constant. The Shadow Lords meanwhile play their cards close to the heart, with strongholds found speckled throughout the lightning-prone High Deserts of the Pacific Northwest
                                                    .
