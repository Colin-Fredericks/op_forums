    Christian Werewolves

    05-02-2021, 11:18 AM
    Hello!

    Have any of you played with this concept? (either as players or storytellers)

    I'm running a chronicle set in the 1910s with werewolves as main antagonists of Mages. I introduced some Black Furies from the Order of Our Merciful Mother who want to seize a Node (to turn it into a Caern) from Celestial Choristers by taking over their Monastery thanks to an order from the Pope.

    I wanted to go beyond the "we use christianism as a tool for Gaia" idea stated in the Black Furies book and started wondering how werewolves could believe in Christianism for real. I hoped to find some info about this in the Dark Ages book but there was nothing around.

    Any insight would be helpful.

      The revised Glass Walkers tribe book discusses the issue of Catholicism among the tribe, specifically among the Wise Guys camp and their rituals.
      The Book of the Weaver also touches on it a little, mostly about how in Werewolf's cosmology, human religion is generally considered an outgrowth of the Weaver, specifically its aspect that deals with Conformity and Dogma. (Whether this is true or not is a matter of personal opinion and what the Storyteller ultimately decides to go with.)

      Just personally, I've long had a faction of the Shadow Lords (mostly Spanish and Italian in origin originally) who from the Dark Ages on believed the Christian God, especially from the Old Testament, is actually Grandfather Thunder, and have this hardline dogmatic and judgmental take on Catholicism.

      The single biggest thing is that, by nature of their half-spirit, half-flesh existence and the Garou Nation's overall beliefs about Gaia, the Triat and so forth (beliefs which seem to be objective based on how spirits and the Umbra work), Garou who still adhere to Christianity are generally going to be seen as heretics (or their equivalent) by most mainstream Christian denominations. To a degree, they would have more in common with older Gnostic sects and their ideas about certain spiritual truths which are only available to an elect chosen elite, in this case, the Garou (who literally possess Gnosis). But a lot of the general rules about morality, ethics, and personal behavior from Christianity would probably be unchanged.
      ​One of the more likely concepts a Christian Garou sect would come up with is the idea that the division between the material world and the Umbra is the result of Mankind's fall in the Garden of Eden, and that when the Bible speaks of angels and demons, its talking about the spirits the Garou deal with all the time.

      You may also have some that hold to some sort of hold over from ancient pagan beliefs, with the idea that the Christian God and Gaia are consorts to one another, similar to the way Uranus and Gaia are in Greek origin myths.

      Back when Demon: The Fallen originally came out about 20 years ago, a common theory was that the Malakim (if I'm remembering their name correctly) angels created after the fall to battle the fallen could've been the actual origins of the Garou if one wanted to try to fit them into Demon's more Christian-derived cosmology.

      What is tolerance? It is the consequence of humanity. We are all formed of frailty and error; let us pardon reciprocally each other's folly. That is the first law of nature.
      Voltaire, "Tolerance" (1764)

          Naturally like you said there are some tribes who have connections with the development of Christianity. I’d argue that connections could be made in several places but to get into the meat and potatoes lemme spin some of my thoughts.

          Naturally human-born are related to the ambient human cultures they move in, especially if they are raised in human society or are lost cubs. So you have the natural bleed over of human culture into werewolf culture. As of the 1910s you have a long established tradition of schism between Catholics, Protestants, and Orthodox. It’s also not long since (iirc) the third great awakening of the 1800s with new protestant sects and revivals blooming across America alongside the development of Mormonism. So you’ve got a period of development in the general culture of the time.

          Now about the practical theology of Christian Garou, there are a number of things to consider. On a cultural level you have a number of thing to consider. Biblically wolves are often used to represent predatory influences and in the New Testament Jesus of Nazareth warns His followers that it will like being hounded by wolves to follow Him. This alongside warnings of wolves in sheep’s clothing as allegory for false prophets. This may make it seem like a Garou would feel like a feared outsider, however there are stories to the contrary as well. Saint Francis of Assisi famously tamed the wild Wolf of Gubbio and brought it into peaceful coexistence with the people of Gubbio. I could easily see Saint Francis or the Wolf of Gubbio becoming hugely important symbols, especially for Catholic Garou. The idea that through Christ they can quell the fury in their hearts and obtain a peaceful life, perhaps taking on a self-appointed guardianship of the faithful. I can easily see the ‘Wolves of Gubbio’ being an interesting cross-tribal camp for Christian Garou.

          Now as a Garou, you are awake to a spiritual world. However awareness of that world does not mean that the Garou cannot also believe in an even higher power. Perhaps a Christian Garou interprets Gaia and the Triat as simply manifestations of G-d, representing an aspect of the Divine Spirit which the Changing Breeds alone are privy too. In that sense Jesus of Nazareth could become an allegory for their own physical-spiritual existence as He was mortal and divine (according to your sect’s specific theology), as well as offering an example for them to pursue as sacrificial figures for the sake of the world. This gives Changing Breeds a unique opportunity to seek out places and people of power suffused through obvious faith, as it would be reflected in the umbra or could be potentially used to effect their spirits.

          I can easily imagine Garou, especially of higher rage, trying to use their faith to restrain and guide their frenzied hearts toward useful ends or in pursuit of inner peace. However I can also imagine the Garou coming to the conclusion that through their connection to the ephemeral world, they have been granted by G-d the spiritual insight to seek out and destroy threats to the flock of humanity. They might even persist to have their very Garou frustrations with humanity and human survival, believing that humans have become corrupt and have failed in their stewardship of the world, which is a problem the Garou must correct one way or another.

          Christianity is a two-millenia old faith which has evolved and adapted over the centuries. It has manifested in a great spectrum of ways. This would be the same for the Garou as they interpret the messages of Christianity through their own unique perspectives and needs, both political and spiritual.

          Hope this is helpful.

              I think it would be fine for Garou to also be believers in any human religion in addition to their Gaian beliefs.

              People like to say the Garou have evidence to support their beliefs and humans don't, and that is true... partially. But it's really a case that Garou have a lot of evidence for a lot of things, but a lot of their religion is also based on pure conjecture. There's no proof that the reason for Evil in the World is that the Weaver trapped the Wyrm. It's just a story. There isn't even proof that the Wyld, Weaver, and Wyrm exist as distinct entities. It might just be a case of the Garou anthropomorphizing the impersonal forces of Dynamism, Stasis, and Entropy. So it's perfectly fine for a Garou to come to a realization that the Gaian religion might not explain everything.

              In addition, in the World of Darkness, there's also lots of evidence in favor of actual human religion. You can meet angels and demons in the High and Dark Umbra (the books tell us, the reader, that they aren't "real" angels or demons, but how would someone know that IC?). True Faith allows people to perform miracles. When even ordinary churches might keep vampires out, to many people that would provide evidence that human religion is also real. If there is a Garou history of an incident in in the Middle Ages of a Christian saint walking into the sept whose True Faith protected him from any spirit and lowered everyone's Rage so much that nobody could change, that would seem to be like a lot of proof that something very real was behind that human. And this probably happened more than once or something similar.

              The real problem isn't that there isn't evidence. It's that there is too much and too contradictory. If you have Buddhist monks, Christian saints, Hindu sadhus, etc. all performing miracles, what does that tell you? What does it mean that this person could perform miracles, but these other believers of the same religion couldn't?

              Plus, any kinfolk community already has the issue to trying to balance the Gaian beliefs imposed on them by Garou, and fitting in with their human neighbors which includes religious worship. They'll likely try to reconcile their two faiths a lot sooner than the Garou and be the source of local versions of the syncretic religion.

              I imagine a lot of Garou believe in a mix of their Garou religion and whatever human religion is dominant in their region. The result would be considered heretical and syncretic by any religious standard, but it'd probably make more sense to their Garou believers based on all the evidence that they have. They'd just need to keep their mouths shut around humans and repeat only what their supposed to believe. Among Garou, I think it'd be a more interesting story. There would certainly be Garou "fundamentalists" that stick to only the religion as portrayed in the sourcebooks - people like the Red Talons, Silver Fangs, and other traditionalists. But there would certainly be a lot of members in other tribes who are either more tolerant or such heretical beliefs, or possibly even share them. You might even have entire septs that combine the Garou religion and the local human religion.

              Why wouldn't the Children of Gaia be interested in Jesus Christ as a figure of peace? Why wouldn't the Bone Gnawers be interested in a religion that proclaims the poor will inherit the Earth? Or Stargazers consider the teachings of Buddha? Why wouldn't any number of Garou, seeing evidence of True Faith and other miracles, not try to reason why this is possible and insert unofficial, but seemingly plausible, explanations of why into the Garou religion and reconcile things?

                                          I mean, it’s not necessarily the hardest thing.

                                          Whether a Garou views Gaia and G-d as being the same or as G-d being the Universe, including Gaia, Jesus Christ offers ultimately the same thing to Garou as He does to humans.

                                          At the end of history He will come again, defeat death, and usher in the resurrection. Whether that involves the creation of a New Jerusalem or a return of all souls to G-D’s Being or whatever else a specific theology may teach, it only requires a few tweaks to work for Garou.

                                          For one thing it reinforces the pre-existing casual relationship Garou have with death, as not only will their soul persist they will one day return to life during The Resurrection. I can easily see Garou arguing over whether Jesus will return after they defeat the Wyrm or if He will return to lead them in the Final Battle against the Wyrm. Regardless, it can be used to motivate them to seek out and destroy The Adversary (including the Wyrm) and anything else standing between them and the Return of Christ.

                                          I’d also say that Garou and their relationship with Gnosis would create a particularly strong relationship with them and the sacrament of the Eucharist. Garou live in a spiritual world and the sacraments and rites may take on a very strong role in their spiritual lives. Perhaps you even allow a camp of Christian Garou to develop sacramental rites, such as gaining gnosis from their Communion with G-d just as much as their traditional kin regain gnosis through spiritual harmony with Gaia and the Living World.

                                          The Garou are very spiritual and very militant so I’d say considering Jesus Christ as both a central spiritual figure and an archetype of The Lion and a Conquering Messiah would be very potent symbols to the Garou mindset and instincts.

                                              Garou are half flesh and half spirit, so they're in an anomalous state compared to humans. It's like in the old fairy stories when fairies ask if it is possible for them to be saved and get to heaven, and the usual response is "no, fairies don't have souls so they can never reach heaven." Not all the stories answer it that way though.

                                              Possible answers include: 1) yes, Garou can be saved they just won't become ancestor spirits, 2) no, Garou can't be saved like humans, but God will still somehow take care of in an appropriate way to our purpose, 3) that is something that will be decided based on events in the Apocalypse, so we should strive to behave like good Christians, or 4) we don't know. There are probably lots of other variants out there.

                                              I recommend reading up on the werewolf trial of Thiess of Kaltenbrunn. The accused werewolf there incorporates a lot of strange beliefs in an attempt to reconcile Christianity with his views on werewolfery. I think it'll give you some inspiration on how that might turn out.
