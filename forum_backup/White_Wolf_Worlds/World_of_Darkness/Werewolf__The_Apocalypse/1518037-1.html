            Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
            Werewolf already had LOTS of internal antagonists that provided direct and immediate conflict. That got downplayed in Revised in favor of heroic fantasy when everyone was expected to team up under the signature NPCs to follow the metaplot, but you just need to examine the original books to get an idea of what is out there. There was no need to designate one of the Garou tribes as the enemy (and if you want that, there is already the Black Spiral Dancers so you're just duplicating an existing conflict, not giving the ST a new tool).

            First, there was already a lot of legitimate conflicts between the various tribes based on the nature of their totems, the typical tribal approaches to problems, and historical grievances. These exist primarily so the PCs can help the Garou rise above them and concentrate on the real enemy, but if the ST wants they can become a major issue in a chronicle.

            Second, there is internal competition for the scarce resources between Garou - cubs, kinfolk, and caerns are all being fought after, stolen, or conquered.

            Third, there is the Ronin - a big mass of rejected, unaligned, or lost Garou. You can do something with them. You don't even need to transform all Ronin into being something specific. You just need a particular group (or groups) of them to do something that causes issues.

            Fourth, every tribe had a camp that was clearly intended to be antagonists to the rest of the tribe and the Garou Nation as a whole.

            Fifth, you can play up the issue of man eating. It must be in the Litany for a reason. And there is no reason to limit it to the Man Eaters camp of the Bone Gnawers, though they are the largest and most organized group.

            Sixth, there are all sorts of secret antagonists described throughout the books that have gotten very little attention. Ever since Hakaken was introduced as a pack totem for the Black Spiral Dancers, we know there are cults of Shadow Lords who worship him. But what are these cults like? What about various totems that aren't exactly Wyrm tainted, but are heavily distrusted by the Garou. For that matter, what about even legitimate Totems like Bear whose followers get hit hard? Why can't that be showcased, or have various packs of Bears get together to do something in response? Show us exactly why packs of Bears are distrusted thousands of years after the War of Rage when other totems don't have the same problem.

            Seventh, you had differences in points of views between the different breeds.

            Eighth, the game has a long tradition of occasional Garou "prophets" who arrive spouting prophecies, challenges, claims to authority, call to action, and so on. A spiritual leader creating a movement to do so something can lead to lots of conflict.

            I can go on. If you want more internal conflict, it's all there without the need to delegitimize any of the existing tribes.
            Well, it's like how the Camarilla used to be a powerful organization that was rumored to have tentacles everywhere, but now in V5 it's a weak and feeble thing that's turned Amish because it can't even manage a decent cyber security team. And, oh, all the Elders have run off from everywhere because mysterious metaplot reasons that'll probably never be touched on.

            I don't think these people are very imaginative or understand any of the material they're writing about.

                        Okay, this is a bit of a conspiracy-theory but this is theoretically what I could envision happened here:
                        • We got that version of W5 headed by Hunters Entertainment but that iteration (for whatever reason) falls apart (maybe we're gonna find out more in the future about what exactly happened here...).
                        • Then we got the next attempt at W5 headed by JA.
                        • What this development-team wanted to do is something along the same lines as H5: The game is called WtA but it's a personal horror street-level gothic punk game where you play werewolves but you are not playing a Garou and Garou are not part of the setting.
                        • Then Paradox told them that they wanted them to develop something closer to V5: It's an update but it's still recognizable as being in the same vein as those previous editions.
                        • This new development-team, though, really didn't like the idea of revisiting old WtA-lore and dealing with legacy-elements from previous editions.
                        • So what we got is this stitched-together version of old legacy-elements from previous WtA-editions, the Hunter-version's attempts at "updating" the setting (like V5 did) and bits and pieces of what the new team actually wanted this game to be.
