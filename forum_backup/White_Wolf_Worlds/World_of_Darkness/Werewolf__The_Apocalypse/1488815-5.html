                        The problem is that WtA started with shallow stereotypes, and the efforts to build them into fully fleshed out subcultures was a mixed result. Most of the Tribes don't have a function, they have a niche. The important distinction being that they don't have any real practical claim to that "task" but that they've socially crafted the narrative that they're the ones to do it.

                        The Get aren't the "marines" of the Garou Nation. That's a narrative they push (to help expand them past their origins as Viking Werewolves) to claim a niche in Garou society. Is there any actual demonstration that they're the best front line soldiers of the Nation? Not really. They don't have any innate bonuses to give them an edge, and even their Gift List doesn't advantage them to any meaningful degree.

                        The Striders might play the role as "messengers" but that's just a convenience for their nomadic life style. Being nomads came first, and being messengers is something that at least by the invention of the telegraph, they stopped really being particularly "best" at. The Glass Walkers and Bone Gnawers especially have much better methods of fast long distance communication because they don't need to physically go anywhere to just as securely send a message to an ally somewhere else.

                        Sometimes the functions do translate better, but in the end the functions are post hoc assignments to try to make the Garou Nation more sensible as an organization, not something inherent to the Tribes as they're constructed.

                        Additionally the books did build out that the Children of Gaia originated in the Fertile Crescent as their traditional lands. The dominance of monotheist cultures later helped push them out of prominence among earlier empires and smaller local cultures, and being part of the center hub of trade between the silk road and the Mediterranean Sea meant they easily spread. Unfortunately this never got really explored with the mythologies of relevant cultures treating "unicorns" more like aurochs than horses, and those would have created a strong bond between the Children of Gaia and Apis in the ancient past and fits very nicely with their adoption of certain mentalities after the War of Rage (which could also explain a shift in Unicorn itself from a powerful wild animal of strength and virility, to the more classical European take).

                                For all that this thread's based in mingling Forsaken and Apocalypse in a not good way, the simplest solution is to.... follow Forsaken's lead in all this. Just flip the names around.

                                Lodges take the place of Forsaken Tribes: Overarching cross-background philosophies that guide "what" you do if Auspices are "how" you do it. Tribes take the place of Forsaken Lodges with a more local focus that mixes human cultural issues with specific aspects of some number of the Lodges. The Get of Fernis are a Tribe started in northern Europe that only allows in members of the Lodge of Soldiers (so even Ragabash and Theurges are dedicated to combat applications of their roles), and train to be the most deadly military force (not individual fighters) of the Garou Nation..

                                You can then cut the Lodges down to 4-9 or so of them depending on your preferences, which impact most of the mechanics currently associated with Tribes, while the Tribes mostly serve as color to help keep the WtA setting intact. But it still says a lot about a Tribe depending on how many Lodges are common within it, an to what degree that balance looks like.

                                This sort of approach makes keeping the setting as close to what it should be as possible easier, while also allowing some pretty big fixes to slide in without too much rocking of the boat. It also helps explain smaller groups like the Siberakh and Singing Dogs because "Tribe" is whatever you want it to be to help portray the setting well. There isn't a need for any mechanically oddity around Tribes dying off, forming fresh, or forming a small different version of things in an isolated area.
