      Garou are not banned from learning Path magic, but it is generally rare and unclear how much is cultural, practical, and a limited capacity. PC Garou require a 7 point Merit to do this, (not all Fera do), but it's left up to the idea of being "innate" or something that could be trained over time. It mostly exists to keep reflect the rarity aspect (mechanically it's not at all worth it).

      Is there a particular reason why you want to go this route? A base-line Theurge has easy access to their own methods of both instant magical healing and creating minor herbal concoctions for practically (if not usually better) results at a tiny fraction of the investment. I could understand if you're not sticking strictly to WtA conceits for a mage PC's related NPCs, but it sounds like you are.

              Unless your game involves a lot of magical travel... the fact that you PC and their companion NPC live that far apart feels like a bigger issue than anything else.

              If the question is, how lore-appropriate would it be for the cousin to come down to Oakland to hangout with your PC and study magic? The answer is not really. As I said, a Theurge has very little benefit in terms of practical skills to learn from such instead of just using their Gifts and Rites. It's even less likely if Path magic is already common enough in the extended family that it's likely that there are other Kinfolk in the Oregon commune that know it and could just teach it there.

              A big factor is that the Garou put a lot of social pressure on the Garou to stay part of Garou groups: pack and Sept specifically. Running off for a few hours to hang out of family across town is one thing, spending months away, or doing regular huge trips, or overusing the Moon Bridges, is a fast way to get the Elders pissy and the spirits to start revoking Renown.

              I feel like you and your ST might have written yourselves into a bit of a corner here and might need to retcon things unless there's more you haven't said yet that could address matters.

                  According to 1997's World of Darkness: Sorcerer, the answer is "No", one of the Changing Breeds cannot normally learn Sorcery (the exception being some Bastet though that is always subject to ST approval). The idea is that Sorcery is something only living humans are capable of, and the Garou (and other Fera) are only partially human and partially something else. It just doesn't work for them. They might do everything correctly, but it just doesn't produce any results.

                  I would also add that even if they were capable of learning Sorcery, that a Mage does not know Sorcery, they know Sphere Magic. The two are not the same even if they sometimes produce the same results. Attempting to teach Sphere Magic to non-Awakened does not result in Sorcery, it results in nothing. Your character's mother obviously does know Sorcery and thus could potentially teach it, but the Garou will simply be incapable of performing it as explained above.

                  So that is the canon answer as far as I can tell. Like all things, STs can decide otherwise. Other rulebooks that the one I consulted might also have changed things.

                  It would probably be more appropriate for a Garou NPC to learn additional Gifts (like rare Gifts or perhaps Gifts unique to your ST's chronicle) that provide similar benefits to those paths, assuming your Mage knows Spirit Spheres, knows various spirits, and can introduce more spirits to that Garou that she may not be aware of. However, since the Garou in question is a Theurge, she probably already has the necessary skills and friends (sept members, pack totem, etc.) to already provide that knowledge.

                                          It's important to remember that there's more than just scale. Infrastructure and geography play a big part of time to travel that doesn't always translate to things like the raw distances involved.

                                          In the US, major cities frequently have very bad traffic infrastructure for their current populations and it spills over into the major highways. Starting in a major urban area like Oakland can add 30 min to an hour to a trip just because you're leaving/arriving at a bad time of day. While I live in a very different part of the US: I took public transportation to get to high-school. Not the fasted option, but no traffic. I could drive to my school in 15 minutes if there was nobody else on the road... but morning traffic regularly bumped the drive to over an hour... and light rail let me do it in 25 minutes consistently. Big cities in the US are also generally very unwelcoming to the kind of vehicles you want if you're regularly driving from an agricultural/timber/etc. small town in for something. For whatever reason US folks are allergic to utes (I never quite understood that, they make way more sense for most US people than our huge pickups do), it means there can be all sorts of weird things like trying to park something that's great for driving around timber land while in the city.

                                          Edit: also I think the Story Teller put them that far away so I wouldn't just cast "Werewolf Companion" to solve every problem.
                                          I understand the instinct to do that, but it still feels like it's gone a bit overboard in this case. As you noted, it's gone from "can't have a werewolf solve all my problems" to, "the werewolf might as well not be on my sheet." The Garou live pretty busy lives. There are dozens of narrative reasons a Garou can be stuck between their work for their Sept and wanting to help their Kin-mage cousin, and it's simple enough to use the Elders being disapproving of dodging Sept duties too frequently to keep things in check.
