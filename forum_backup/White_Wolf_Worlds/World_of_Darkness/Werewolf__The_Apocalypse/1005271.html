    Metis Social Status VS Kinfolk Social Status

    11-19-2016, 03:48 AM
    One very blurry issue that has not really been properly addressed across the books.

    Kinfolk are second class citizens 99% of the time in Garou Society no matter how accomplished/educated they are in Human Society.

    Heck, even Bone Gnawer Kinfolk who can hold jobs and have Level-3 Resources have to drop EVERYTHING for their illiterate, never-even-ONE-Dot Resource Garou Crackhead-cousins if they drop by on Saturday Night riddled with bullets, and have to just nod their heads and smile while missing out on Junior's Piano Recital.

    And yet they are afforded respect, honor and love because they provide the vital role of being able to give birth to/sire the children of the next generation of Garou.

    Whereas Metis are CONSISTENTLY described as whipping boys and objects of mockery not just for their physical and/or mental deformities, but for the fact that they bring NOTHING reproductively to the table: They are literally just eating up oxygen and food, waiting to die while not being able to give the Garou more children for the future.

    So: My Question Being.

    In terms of Social Status, a Kinfolk, particularly a financially stable, politically connected and educated one, would surely have a higher spot on the social totem pole than a Metis-Liability, right?

    I would imagine that ESPECIALLY in the Fianna, that disgusting tribe of whore-mongering child-abusing/abandoning hypocrites, even the KINFOLK get to bully the Metis Children and expect nothing but grateful nods from their designated tribal punching-bag?

    Curioser and Curioser....

      Depends on the tribe, honestly. The books state that some tribes give their metis for their kinfolk to raise, since they have the time to do it. Usually this amounts to the metis being in a basement and only fed at most. For example, to Silver Fangs, kinfolk are servants so they get to deal with the lower caste.

      For Fianna, though, I imagine they just throw their metis into some distant part of the woods and try to forget about them.

      This is of course just about the child metis, adult metis are still accepted as part of a tribe. That can mean they get to be less of a punching bag to fully accepted members. And that does include watching over kinfolk at times. In fact, given their sterility, metis are perfect for being the ones handling things with kin as they are very tied to tribal ideals and sterile.

      For my part, I've had these situations with my metis and their kinfolk ties;

      - one lived in a family like a guard dog until they changed, then they were taught to be a servant and were subservient to the kinfolk.

      - one was sent to build a monastery with a gaggle of kinfolk monks.

      - one was raised by their kinfolk uncle and view them as family.

      - one lived with a pack of wolves and garou and acted as a guard.

                                      The books have generally presented Metis as "out ranking" kinfolk, because the Garou promote a hierarchy where Garou > Kin. Even the lowest Garou is still a Garou.

                                      Of course the informal social weight is far more complicated. Tribal attitudes, character history, etc. are all going to matter. The kin mate of one of the Sept Elders that has decades of service to the Sept with a reputation of going above and beyond in their duties while never using their mate's status as a cudgel, is going to have a social advantage over a Metis Cub that's still in training for their Initiation. In a more traditional Sept/Tribe, that means the kin is still going to be expected to do things like defer to the Metis in terms of order of eating, or seating around a table, but the Metis would learn pretty quickly (if they hadn't already) that trying to use their rank to push said kin around is just going to result in a lot of higher ranking Garou making that Metis' life hell.

                                      Edit: Also, the Fenrir don't respect all Metis and Kin. They respect merit. A Metis that proves themselves is welcomed into the Tribe. It's not a universal tolerance.

                                              Sounds pretty much exactly like the kind of faux pas a more traditional Garou might face around any of the less formal Tribes.

                                              The least reasonable part of the scenario is a Shadowlord (or really any Garou) that's trained and trusted as an emissary being stupid enough to not recognize the situation; esp. insulting an Ahroun's mother (that's just a bad idea in general because that's how Frenzies happen). While most Garou would not consider such deference to a kin normal, they know better than to risk pissing off a Sept of rage filled warriors by upsetting the boat... because a severe ass-whooping is pretty much the best case scenario. The Shadowlord might think all of that was strange, and perhaps even Litany breaking - but they're never going to say it out loud or act on it and expect a clam rational response.

                                              For Garou that see kin as family first, an outsider insulting and threatening one of their kin is an attack on the whole family and will be responded to in kind.

                                              For Garou that see kin more as property first, an outsider and threatening one of their kin is an attack on the host Sept's honor - you don't hit someone else's kin, if you have a problem you take it to the local Alpha/Elder - and a similar result ensues.

                                              Also, most Garou are raised to some level of meritocracy. If a kin is being honored so thoroughly, the default assumption is that there's a good reason for it; that said kin earned it. There's also always the possibility that the kin in question is also seen as blessed by one of the Totems, Luna, or even Gaia (ex: being a Kami) and there are further reasons you don't see on the surface going on.

                                                      I'm not sure what you're trying to say.
