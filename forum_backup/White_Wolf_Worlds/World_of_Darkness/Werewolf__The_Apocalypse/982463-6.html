    Ah, understood, I had not been thinking of the female Lupus angle. My, that would make for an interesting family reunion. Hm, do female Lupus have limited fertility like female wolves do (a week of estrus during the winter)? And is the pregnancy for a female Lupus equal in duration to that of a female wolf (two months)?

    It takes around three years for a wolf to reach adulthood, so I imagine that there would be some rather interesting social dynamics occurring within the household. The Lupus female would have to be with the wolf pup for around twelve hours a day during the first three months and, quite frankly, she would want to be with other wolves to allow for proper socialization. After the first three months, the wolf pup could be around humans and, because it would be Wolf Kinfolk, it would be more sapient than a normal wolf, allowing it to mimic domesticated behavior after its mother explained things to it. After age three, however, wild instincts would probably kick in and, while it would probably not be as dangerous as a normal wolf, it would probably be looking to joining with pack of wolves as soon as possible.

    Of course, if the pup ended up being a Garou, things could get very interesting. While the wolf pup would be less connected to its wild instincts because of its interaction with its human father, it would be quite familiar with human society and, to a certain extent, human technology. It might even consider its human father to be its true father, which would cause some weird social interactions, especially if the pup ended up being a female Garou. As far as I know, there is no particular incest taboo in Garou society that would prevent a lupine daughter from interbreeding with her human father (other than her lupine mother might kick her rear end).

            About "the Rite". Well, it comes from "the guy". First mentioned in Valkenbourg Foundation, it reads in the characters entry:
            Thaumaturgy: Samuel Haight knows several Thaumaturgic spells and rituals, most of which can be found in the Vampire: The Masquerade rulesbook. Movement of Mind 2, Lure of
            Flames 1, Weather Control 1. He also knows a rite: the Ritual of Sacred Rebirth.
            Showing, that when first presented, the Rite, wasn't even a Rite, but a Blood Magic Ritual. In his 2nd edition appearance when the Skin Dancers were introduced in WoD:Outcasts, the write-up of the Ritual hadn't changed much. It wasn't until revised's PGttG that the Ritual was called a Rite and explicitly stated, that it could be used without knowledge in Thaumaturgy, although Rituals 5 and Rites 5 are mandatory for Skin Dancer characters.

            So, what White Wolf did in revised was to elaborate on Outcasts, by creating a foundation that allowed the skin dancers to exist, without the need for Vampire or Mage:Sorcerer revised as means to explain how this Ritual turned Rite would work, when all others didn't. And they did so by saying "This Rite, just does just does work".

            So, I find of "I want Skin Dancers in the Setting" a fruitless example for an exemption of the "Kinfolk can't enact Rites" rule, for it wasn't intended to be that exception. The reason, that murderous practice is even a Rite is just so that authors could keep the Skin Dancers, without explaining to us who Haight was (for everyone hated him in revised era), without involving Sorcery, Vampire, Book of Mirrors infernalism rules or any other crossover mechanics that would make sense when it come to the unspeakable act to become a werewolf, but were all left out for sake of "no crossover, please".


            -------

            I totally agree that Kin are weaker than ghouls. But I don't see the point. Kinfolk are also weaker than kinain or mages. Some kin can be sorcerers, but would one allow one sorcerer in an all-kin-chronicle? Psychics ?
            If one plays a Kin chronicle, the setting for that game was used to be that of the Unsung Heroes. People who are everyday Joes or Janes.
            There is, imo, no need for expanding gifts, reworking existing gifts into Kin-versions, not mentioning the original kin gifts, reprint the psi-stuff and Rites that do or do not bring magical effects.

            Sure, the book might have been shorter, but that wouldn't have bothered me. And I would have very much liked to see a statement: "There are some Kinfolk, who learn magic or are born with psychic abilities. Those are rare amongst humans and only slightly more common amongst the Kenning Kin. WoD20: Sorcerer and Psychics will go into detail an provide unified mechanics for characters like this."
