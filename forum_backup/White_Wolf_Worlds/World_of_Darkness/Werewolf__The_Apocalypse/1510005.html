      Croatan Song is a whole book about the life and sacrifice of the Croatan. The Howlers got a tribebook as a part of W20. All three of them can only be used as ancestor spirits and ghosts in most games. The Bunyip got murdered in Austrlia in the mid 19th century. Howlers fell during the Roman conquests of Britain. Croatan fell in the early years of colonisation of America.

      They were all based in very specific locations and they all died in very specific times. Unless you're running Croatan Song or Past Lives you're not very likely to encounter them. Of course a chronicle built around them before their extinction is valid, but quite limited as they were all very hidebound and not prone to interacting much with the Nation at large.

      What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.

          I have toyed a bit through the years with redoing Rage Across Australia and the first thing to go was the Bunyip as a tribe, things are a little more convoluted so to speak.

          Australia and neighboring isles have a tribe of its own, the Yowie that have an acrimonious relationship with the Garou Nation that can make hardliner Wendigo seem moderate by comparison.

          (As an aside, for my more recent games i sketched out a number of "apocryphal" tribes that are not recognized as such by the Garou Nation's leadership for a variety of reasons, though they have caerns & numbers in their own, sometimes quite large, parts of the world)

          Part of the tension relates to issues of dispute over aboriginal and dingo kinfolk - but much also steems from resentment over the extermination of the Djankerrk, another fera, the werethylacines, favored children of the Bunyip totem and also the threatening of the children of the Rainbow Serpent.

          ​Bunyip did once have shapechanging children of its own, though they are elusive and mysterious to the Yowie themselves - and possibly related (somehow) to the creatures better known to a few in contemporary times as Anurana....​

                  AFAIK

                  White Howlers - they aren't extinct, rather dormant. They went to Mafeas to slay the Wyrm, were corrupted and return as Black Spiral Dancers at the end of II century. The survivors and Kinfolks faced a rather grim choice: join or be killed. Some of the Kinfolks fled and were adopted by Fianna and Get of Fenris tribes. So genetically there are White Howlers but they isn't a tribe of White Howlers and those who carry that gene are members of other tribes. Also their Totem is still alive (but weakened) and there's a legend about one White Howler sleeping somewhere so technically there's hope that this tribe can be reborn. But you actually can't play them

                  Croatan - they sacrificed themselves to stop a great Wyrm beast and their totem is nowhere to be seen at the early colonisation era. So the tribe is gone. On the other hand you can meet a Croatan at the Midnight Circus.

                  Bunyip - the tribe members are dead, their lupus Kinfolk too (last thylacine fell in 1937). But during Rage Across Australia there's a possibility that players can help Rainbow Serpent (Bunyip Totem) revive and there should be human Kinfolk too (at least genetically) so technically there is a possibility for Bunyip to return but it would be a long and painful process (just like with White Howlers) and there would not be any lupus Bunyip.

                  But canonically those tribes are extinct and you cannot play them during modern times (except as Past Lives, just like in Past Lives sourcebook scenario).

                              So with the Lost Tribes, here's some stuff to know about restoring them:

                              The Children of Gaia and Uktena AKA Elder Brother Tribes are particularly interested in this. There is a belief that the Garou are suffering because they're playing with an incomplete team; just as all 5 auspices are needed, all 13 original tribes are needed. The Earth Guides and Seekers of the Lost Tribes are particularly invested.

                              When it comes to recovering a dead tribe, you need a few things. Genetics is the relatively easy part; there are Kinfolk and Garou who carry the blood of the Croatan, for instance, in the Elder and Younger Brother Tribes. The problem is that bloodline is not enough. The Tribal Totem must be invoked so that the Cub can be brought into the Tribe; in the absence of Turtle, who is lost to the Garou at present, a Homid born to the Uktena who traces a Croatan bloodline won't be able to become Croatan.

                              If you could gather the following:

                              - Garou and Kinfolk who are part of the Lost Tribe's bloodline
                              - Ancestor Spirits of the Lost Tribe
                              - The Totem of the Lost Tribe
                              - A working and pure Caern associated with the Lost Tribe
                              - Knowledge of the Tribe's initiation rites

                              Then resurrection would prove possible. It's a challenging enough endeavour that out of the three tribes that have died, none have been restored. Determining such distant ancestry is also difficult; the Rite of Heritage could easily tell you that a cub is the child of a pure-bred Black Spiral Dancer and a pure-bred BSD Kinfolk, but it couldn't tell you that an Australian cub is descended from the Bunyip unless you got an ungodly amount of successes, due to the way the Rite's mechanics work.

                                  Of the Lost Tribes, the White Howlers could come back. As already pointed out, in the WH TB, there is a story of a legendary White Howler that just kept coming back from death that is sleeping somewhere and would one day return. In W20 Umbra there is a Black Spiral Dancer that has been in a sea of silver within the Realm of Erebus for years now that has been slowly becoming purified from the Wyrm's taint, with the section ending with the Dancer drifting closer to shore lately, implying that the purification could be nearing it's end and that they could be the first of new White Howler Tribe or possibly a new Tribe altogether.

                                  While it was never finished and released, the protagonist of the abandoned Heart of Gaia video game was of the White Howler lineage. So the idea of them returning isn't something new in WtA.

                                  Further the Lion while weakened is still around. And for that matter, while some of the WH Tribal Totems also fell into corruption, others have disappeared and no one is sure what happened to them. It is possible that they could be found and the Lion's Brood could be restored, aiding to strengthen the Lion. Of course it probably would piss off the Griffin to lose one of his Brood.

                                  Though as it's been noted, restoring a Lost Tribe would be a lengthy, challenging and frankly legendary endeavor. Honestly I'd probably do it as a generational chronicle, requiring the efforts of multiple packs spanning decades if not a century or two to bring a Lost Tribe back to life. Especially since even if they get the Tribe back and alive, there would be a period where their numbers will be dangerously low and require support from other Tribes until their numbers can reach a stable level.

                                  Still, to achieve that would be a shot of hope and a genuine triumph for the Garou.

                                  If of course you just want to play those Lost Tribes in a modern game without going through all that, the obvious answer is to talk with your group about altering the setting. The WH TB does talk about ideas of playing WH in modern games, with three possible ways to do it, Spontaneous Rebirth, the Great Quest and Never Fell. The Great Quest is akin to what we talked about, the idea of the Lion or Garou looking to restore the Tribe approach the pack to aid them in this endeavor. Rebirth is a scenario where a pure untainted White Howler is born and found, resulting in the Lion and BSD seeking them for obvious reasons. Never Fell is where the White Howlers never fell or at least were never completely lost. While they are WH specific ideas, one could alter them to fit the other two as well.

                                  Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. They're indomitable. Indomitable.

                                          I feel differently. Certainly WtA is a game where you're fighting a losing battle with a number of handicaps born of the mistakes of your answer but increasingly I've come to the opinion that the fight is not doomed and that if the fight is truly doomed, what's the point? Why not just succumb to Harano and end it?

                                          Everybody seemed to assume that the Prophecy of the Phoenix was the end of the world but then W20 came out with the Eighth Omen comic, revealing an final part of the Prophecy and making me realize something I had never thought about when it came to the Prophecy. Why is it from the Phoenix? A creature that is famous for dying and being reborn? If it was truly a sign of the end of the world, wouldn't it have come from something related to Death? What if it always as W20 paints it, not the telling of their Doom but a call to battle, that even in the darkest hour victory can still be won.

                                          There was also a little fiction bit from Apocalyse the final WtA book before W20 that's always stuck with me, especially it's end.

                                          “This is it, isn’t it?” asked Pei. I looked up from the floor at her and nodded. Sitting in front of me, Halley played with her blocks. She stacked them with her tiny, chubby hands until they tumbled over. She giggled and started stacking again. I scooped her up and blew bubbles on​ her belly. I wanted to hear that laugh again. Gaia’s own music wasn’t more beautiful.

                                          I stood and looked at Pei. Behind her, in the hall, her husband was stuffing a duffle bag full of diapers and clothes for Halley. I liked Jim, but there was always the awkwardness between us. He was a good father to Halley — he could be there for her in ways I couldn’t. He would be “Papa” and I would be “Unca Mike.” Some nights, that burned.

                                          I held my daughter, smelled her hair and handed her back to Pei. “It is. Get to the caern.” I gave Halley my keys. She jingled them and laughed. “Take my Jeep. The roads are crazy. I’ve got bottled water and some canned stuff in there for the three of you.” Jim muttered something about car seats and ran to the garage.

                                          “Are we going to make it?” asked Pei.

                                          I looked at Halley. The Harano that had been nipping at my heels dissolved. “Nobody knows how our stories end. I doubt mine reads ‘and they all lived happily ever after’. Maybe hers will.”​
                                          ​
                                          Here we have a Garou who knows the Apocalypse is here and doesn't except to survive but he doesn't succumb to despair from it, rather he looks to his daughter, to the future and finds strength in that. That even if he is to die howling in dark, his daughter will survive, that the world will survive. The Battle may be costly, many will die and the World as we know it will cease to exist but the World will survive. People, Human, Garou and Fera will survive.

                                          After all, most of the scenarios in Apocalypse or the wider Time of Judgement don't end with the world dying. Damaged and changed, sure but it still endures.
