                    Originally posted by garhkal View Post

                    Care to post all of the Cha Creation rules on them??
                    Character creation hasn't been finalized yet. What I've got thus far goes to around 80 pages, but most of that is background stuff (creation myth, social structure, camps, relations with humanity, etc.). Moreover, I'm still in what I call "mental-dump-mode" and much of what I have is in dire need of editing (which will happen once I'm comfortable that I have every potential good idea down).

                    Basic character creation will be as follows:

                    Attributes: 7/5/3
                    Abilities: 13/9/5
                    Advantages: Backgrounds 5
                    Finishing Touches: Gnosis 1, Willpower 4, Rank 0 ("pup"), freebie points 15. (Currently we're discussing making it 21 freebie points -- arguments being made for and against).

                    Gnosis is simply for certain reflexive rolls (resisting vampiric embrace, for instance) and cannot be consciously used at character creation. Form statistics are the same as for the Garou, and forms are referred to by the same names except for the Lupus equivalent which the weredogs call "Canis". Weredogs cannot purchase the following Backgrounds: Ancestors ( although that's also under discussion), Fetish, Kinfolk, Mentor, Pure Breed, Rites, or Totem. Weredogs may purchase Paths in Hedge Magic, and may do so as any human sorcerer would without suffering the experience point penalty that Garou do. Weredogs have no Gifts at character creation, and gaining Gifts and Rites is intentionally hampered by the setting. Weredogs regaining the use of either Gifts or Rites should be the focus of an entire chronicle (weredogs are no longer party to the Great Spirit Pact, and the spirits are generally hostile).

                    Other important points are that a weredog's Crinos form does not inflict the Delirium. The Veil still applies, but only to a degree. All human observers generally remember the event, but will rationalize. More humans remember everything about the encounter and are less likely to rationalize than if it were a Garou (discussions are going on about giving witnesses between a +3 to +5 bonus on the Delirium chart as to whether they rationalize or remember). Moreover, as humans recognize weredogs as protectors on a subconscious level, weredogs have a strange anti-Delirium effect when in Crinos. If the number of Garou (or other shapeshifters) in Crinos in a scene is less than double the number of weredogs in Crinos in that scene, the Delirium is negated (the Veil is not).

                    So, that's basically character creation in a furry nutshell. We (my gaming circle and I) are only now beginning to get around to the nuts and bolts of character creation, however. For instance, as the weredogs are barred from most Backgrounds, we're coming up with replacements. Most are shamelessly plagiarized from other WoD books, but the following is an example of an original one:
                    (I'm putting this in "Spoiler" brackets so I don't create an obnoxious "wall of text")
                    PACK BONDS
                    Like their lupine cousins, dogs are also highly sociable creatures with elaborate, cooperative social structures. As such, the weredogs, like the Garou, also share a spiritual link with their packmates. However, just as wolves and dogs differ from each other (particularly where humans are involved), werewolves’ and weredogs’ Pack Bonds operate in slightly different ways.
                    One of the most obvious ways that a werewolf’s ties to his packmates differs from that of a weredog cousin, is that the Garou link with their packs by means of a pack totem. Rather than linking directly to each other, the totem serves as the spiritual locus of the pack and the various packmates link directly to the totem and by extension, indirectly through the totem. Weredogs, being much more tied to the physical world and with far more tenuous links to the spirit world, do not form packs this way. Weredog packmates link directly to each other.
                    The next way that the two differ is in just who can be a party to a weredog pack. Just as dogs often form extremely tight bonds to humans, weredog packs are likewise far less choosy about who counts as a packmate. As such, ordinary dogs and normal humans can join a weredog pack (and benefit from Pack Bonds).
                    Lastly, the strength of a werewolf pack’s bonds is determined by the pack as a whole. As weredogs link directly to each other without need of a totem, the strength of the bonds between specific packmates determine how the Pack Bonds work on a case-by-case basis.

                    Pack Bonds (at least for a weredog pack) are a complicated issue, with many distinct parts. Pack Bonds also represent perhaps the greatest “magic” powers the weredogs have.
                    First off, a weredog pack can be thought of as having four distinct elements: First and most important are the actual weredogs themselves. This block determines how large the various other blocks may be, and by extension how large the pack as a whole may become. The next element are the humans in the pack (if any). The pack may have as many humans as there are weredogs (so a pack with three weredogs may also have up to three humans). The third element are the dogs in the pack (if any). Again, the pack may have as many dogs in it as it has weredogs. The last element are the supernaturals in the pack (if any). Weredog packs can include just about any sort of supernatural being imaginable. Wraiths, Changelings, Vampires, Mages, Werewolves, Garou Kinfolk, Ghouls, and other Changing Breeds are all potential packmates. The pack may have as many supernaturals in it as it has weredogs. So a pack that has five weredogs could theoretically have up to twenty packmates (though this is unlikely as non-weredog supernatural packmates are extremely rare).
                    Non-weredogs are subject to certain restrictions. The potential packmate-to-be must both implicitly trust all the existing members of the pack and, in turn be implicitly trusted by the pack before the Pack Bonds will slip into place. A human intending to infiltrate the pack by feigning friendship will never establish the connection necessary to bond with the pack. Likewise, if any member of the pack is suspicious or in any way distrusts the potential new packmate, the bonding will also fail.
                    Potential recruits cannot be bonded in any way to anyone or anything outside the pack or the Pack Bonds will not fall into place. Humans or Ghouls who have been blood-bonded, Garou with pre-existing Pack Bonds (any dots whatsoever in Totem), Hegenyokai bonded to a sentai, or any other similar situation make it impossible to bond to a weredog pack.
                    If (through combat or accident) the number of weredogs in the pack falls below the number needed to sustain the total in any of the other three elements, the excess number of non-weredogs are not lost. Rather, no new non-weredogs may join the pack (including remaining elements that are not over-populated) until either a new weredog joins the pack, or a member of the over-populated element leaves (or is killed) and the pack is again back in balance.

                    One of the most devastating things about Garou packs is their capacity to perform pack tactics. Weredog packs may use pack tactics just like Garou do, although in practice, this works a little different.
                    First, any member of the pack who wishes to participate in pack tactics must have at least one dot in Pack Bonds to do so. Secondly, the number of pack tactics that can be accomplished has nothing whatsoever to do with Gnosis (a good thing since weredogs generally don’t have that). Third, pack tactics are learned by individual members, not the pack as a whole. Fourth, for two (or more) packmates to perform a pack tactic, all participating members must know that particular pack tactic. Fifth, the total number of pack tactics an individual member can know is equal to his number of dots in Pack Bonds (whenever a new dot in Pack Bonds is picked up, the player should annotate what pack tactic his character wishes to learn).
                    Some non-weredog packmates are severely restricted in what pack tactics they may participate in. Specifically, some pack tactics require participants to be in certain forms or require the superhuman speed and strength of a hybrid form. For example, a human packmate could learn Fur Gnarl. However, he would not be able to participate in the opening move (which requires claws or fangs). He could however, take part in the second move (by using a rifle to target the newly vulnerable patch on the pack’s adversary, for example). Which pack tactics (or which elements of a pack tactic) may be used by a packmate is entirely at the discretion of the Storyteller, and should make sense given non-shapeshifters’ limitations.

                    (To avoid confusion, the following term is used: Just because two weredogs are part of a pack does not mean that either or both have any dots in Pack Bonds. For the purposes of this subject, “packmates” refers to any member of the pack with a number of dots in Pack Bonds equal to or greater than the character in question.)

                    The following are the powers that are associated with each level in the Background. Generally, these powers function only with packmates who have at least that rating in Pack Bonds. For instance, a member of a pack has one dot in Pack Bonds and another has three. The two packmates can only use the powers with each other afforded by one dot in Pack Bonds. If however, another packmate has four dots, then the packmate with three dots can make use of all powers of one, two and three-dot levels with that packmate (and vice-versa), while the one-dot packmate may use only the powers of one-dot level with both his three-dot and four-dot packmates. The four-dot packmate may not use any of his four-dot powers until one of his packmates gets at least four dots in Pack Bonds (and then only with that packmate).

                    ● In the event of an ambush or a surprise of any sort, if any packmate succeeds in the Perception + Alertness, or Wits + Primal Urge roll and the surprise fails, all packmates are assumed to have succeeded at the roll (even those not capable of making the roll). All packmates are aware if a packmate is wounded or in distress (though not the packmate’s location). Characters normally vulnerable to the Delirium (such as most humans) are now immune.
                    ●● All packmates’ general direction and vague distance are known. If a packmate is wounded, the extent of his injuries is roughly known.
                    ●●● All packmates’ general locations are known. Packmates can send simple instructions or requests to other packmates (“go there” or “attack that one”) as a free action. Any packmate whose initiative roll is lower than that of the pack alpha (assuming the alpha is engaged in the combat), or that of whoever is leading the combat (if not the alpha), may use that leader’s initiative roll as if it was their own. The exact injuries of all wounded packmates are known.
                    ●●●● All packmates’ exact locations are known. Packmates may silently communicate with each other through their pack bonds (restricted to simple concepts as if they were communicating in the dog-tongue – across any distance). Mental pictures, the sound of a target’s voice, and a prey’s scent may also be shared. A single Knowledge, Skill, or Talent may be shared with a packmate (although not while it’s owner is simultaneously using it). Another packmate’s wound penalties may be temporarily absorbed (multiple packmates may absorb part of the wounded packmate’s total, effectively spreading out the wound penalties across the rest of the pack).
                    ●●●●● Packmates may silently speak to each other through their pack bonds over any distance as if they were face to face (effectively mental telepathy). Packmates may allow a packmate to see through their eyes, hear through their ears, smell through their nose and otherwise fully share any or all of their senses. Any single Attribute may be shared with a packmate (although not while its owner is simultaneously using it – the form modifiers of the owner do not transfer however, only the base rating). Packmates may absorb a wound level per turn into themselves (even aggravated wounds), essentially sacrificing themselves to heal their packmates. This means that a weredog may absorb the lethal damage done to a human packmate, and then heal it using his regeneration (another peculiarity of this is that a werewolf packmate may absorb the wounds of a human packmate who is shot with silver, and regenerate it as it is merely lethal damage, not aggravated). By spending a number of Willpower points, an equal number of points of a packmate’s Rage may be absorbed for a day. Only packmates who do not naturally possess Rage may absorb such points. The Rage points may not be used by the one who absorbed it. The packmate who absorbed the Rage points may give them back at any time before the full day has elapsed (its owner has no say in this), although the Willpower spent is not returned. Alternately, the Rage points’ owner may pull upon them at any time and regain the full total. After a day, the Rage points return to their owner unless a packmate spends more Willpower to absorb them again. This power generally goes unused as most packmates have no Rage, but should a Garou or member of the Changing Breeds join the pack (for whatever reason), it allows the pack to temporarily relieve their packmate of the debilitating effects of Rage (including The Curse).

                    As you can see much of this needs serious editing. Also, some of this seems seriously over-powered, so as our discussions continue, some of these things are going to get pared down a bit.
