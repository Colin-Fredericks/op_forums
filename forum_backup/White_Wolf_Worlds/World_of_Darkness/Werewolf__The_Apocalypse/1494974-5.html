    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    Have you ever tried to run a WtA game with 4+ Fera types at the same time with the different Renown types? It's a complete pain in the ass. There's a reason Beast Court games override individual Fera Renown systems for one unified one instead.
    Still sounds like less of a pain than Exalted 2nd edition. Which is really the lion's share of my ST experience (for almost a year!), so I'm less likely to see that as a deal-breaker, personally. Then, though, I was blessed with players who knew what all their Charms did.

    But if they're going the Forsaken route, Renown might be easier to track in that it is purchased instead of banked up. Not necessarily great, there, if we're going for Apocalypse flavor. Alternately, it might have a simplified track where you just check off a set number of milestones to get a new dot.

    That's what I'm talking about. If you keep expanding the types of Renown, and you expect to use all of them at once, it gets unworkable. You end up reducing them in some fashion.

    Even in the existing Fera schemes, you have to treat Duty, Obligation, Honor, and Obedience as distinct things even though... they're very minor and nuanced differences that all revolve around similar broad strokes concepts. It's not bad world building, but it's not great game design because they're not like Nature/Demeanor where it's just flavor on what a person or society values, but ultimately it's a loose guideline on one mechanic's application. They're in-depth and highly relevant systems to a lot of stuff.
    That's how things were. W5 will have little of the Was, from what we're being shown. As such, the Renown can be moved further apart, if need be, so that it's not a series of edge cases.

        Well, having played plenty of WtA and Exalted 2e, let me put it in Exalted terms.

        The Garou and Fera are a lot like the different kind of Exalted. They all work on the same basic set of rules, but each have their own set of unique quirks to deal with. While they share more generic Charms than the Exalted do, each group adds their own need Charms to learn/have your players know on top of the innate powers and weaknesses.

        The Renown categories though, are like having each have their own different set of Virtues. Instead of thinking about how a combat might require a Valor roll, it's Valor for one PC, Bravado for another, Glory for yet another, and so on. Even getting past the bean-counting of how the old Renown worked, and moving to something more like how Renown works in Forsaken, juggling a completely different set for multiple PCs is just piling on the ST workload.

        There's a reason Scion dumped that exactly way of doing it where every PC had a pick of Virtues from a big list, making it a chore to judge how each character would react to situations based on that.

        What I've been trying to get at, is that if you add the Fera to the default game, you end up reducing features like custom Renown sets for them, because it's too hard to have as many as there are in WtA now. W5 would be correct to reduce the number of Renown traits even at the cost of how it used to differentiate the Fera.

            Honestly not sure equating renown to virtues is comparable.

            In my experience at least, renown wasn't hard to keep track of because most of the work is on the Player's part to remember and they only need to keep track of their own character. Players are actively Trying to earn renown so their character can advance and renown is only issued for pretty grand and obvious acts on the player's part(putting it in the same ballpark as bonus XP).

            Sticking to the old system also meant player's gravitated toward the mindset of their character. A Nuwisha is going to actively try to be funny and informative because he needs humor and teaching to advance. Anananasi is going to be more withdrawn, thoughtful, and by-the-book because they're graded on their cunning, wisdom, and obedience to the law. Garou are going to be high off their own adrenaline most of the time because two thirds of their renown code is "fight the thing, but look awesome doing it" and "hold up the old ways, which mostly end with: fight the thing in fair combat". The system encouraged people to actually Role-play rather than just roll-playing the character.

                        The problem is that the players don't say how much any given action is worth. There's the charts in the books (which are supposed to be suggestions, and even then frequently offer up ranges the ST needs to assign).

                        When everyone's on the same Renown, you can just say, "that was worth X Glory, Y Honor, Z Wisdom," and it's as simple as passing out XP, and there might be a few individual modifiers that might come up (even though the books generally recommend that Renown is shared by a group).

                        Just having a Garou and a Bastet fight side by side against a squad of fomori means you have to look at the fight from Glory vs. Ferocity (and depending on how it goes it might interact with other Renown traits too). They're no 1:1 charts as each puts slightly different emphasis on different actions. You can easily run into situations where a fight is 5 Glory for the Garou, but 7 Ferocity for the Bastet, after having run through the fight against both charts. Every additional Fera type that uses different Renown means a new chart to run a scene through, and a different set of numbers to give to each player individually.

                        None of this matters to the players being good about keeping track of their numbers.

                        Also, it feels like we're not talking about the same rules here. The Renown charts are full of things that are not "grand and obvious," if the books didn't say Garou get 1 temp Honor ever month they perform their regular duties at the Sept... would that be obvious from the general description of Honor? It's certainly not a grand act of Honor.

                        Sure if you completely toss the sample charts and eyeball everything it's going to be easier, but that just sounds like what I was already saying: you end up reducing the individuality of each as the similar Renown traits are going to lump together quickly. Without the larger sample award charts you're not going to have the examples of where Glory and Ferocity, or Honor and Duty, diverge.

They're so used to games where the ST kept tight control over it, they never bothered because the ST just assigned numbers and they moved on, so there was no point to them to learn it. They had a vague idea for a general sense of what's "good" and "bad," but beyond that they wouldn't have a clue about why one fight got them 4 temp glory and another got them 8 even if the first one felt vaguely more "glorious" in the mind..

                                I've also been in games where the ST considered trying to haggle for a bit more here or there behavior worthy of kicking people out of the game because they don't want to have to deal with everyone trying to "game" the system (which you already noted does not need players to try to milk it for Renown even faster than it already goes).

                                So, basically, you know exactly what I'm talking about, but you got lucky with your group actually being into the Renown charts for the most part,and instead of realizing that for a lot of people that "barely shows up Ananasi player" is probably half the group of otherwise awesome and engaged players you don't want to turn away because those big charts aren't their thing.

                                From a game design perspective, making people learn those charts to play a multi-Fera game is not worth the end result of rewarding them for playing in their niches; playing to your niche is basically the whole point of such a setup. The game does not actually need 17 different Renown traits, the vast majority of which could easily be collapsed into 4-7 of them depending on your inclinations, and have everyone just use one standard list that would be bigger to make the Tribal Renown links we're seeing in the preview more sensible, and giving everyone favored Renown traits would let the Fera (even if it's a W5 STV book) express their interests that way.
