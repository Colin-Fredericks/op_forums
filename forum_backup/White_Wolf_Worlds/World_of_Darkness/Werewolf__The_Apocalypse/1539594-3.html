        I feel like we're now at a point where the writers can't decide whether the standard-scenario for W5 is the PC-Pack with one or two other Garou and/or one other Pack around at most with the Litany/Septs/Garou-Nation not mattering much or if we're just going back to how things used to be with Septs, mutliple Packs around and the LItany and stuff like Moots mattering a lot. The problem is that you can't do both of these things at the same time. Also, why would the Garou-Nation be considered to be fallen when two isolated Packs feel the need to still commit to Garou-traditions/rituals like doing a Moot and therefore are fine with using locations like some random cabin?

        The way Touchstones end up being described feels more like a burden than anything else. It's like somebody forced the issue on the game and then no writer ever came up with a justification for why exactly they're in the game. According to the text, Garou in the setting don't care about Touchstones and on a game-level they also simply exist because somebody somewhere decided that all WOD5E games need to have Touchstones. Without any thematic relevance, Touchstones just end up being some random human who's occasionally an inconvenience and sometimes a "nice shoulder to cry on" (recovery mechanics) and them getting hurt triggers Frenzy or Harano checks. The text only mentions how Garou would lose themselves to Rage without Touchstones... which isn't a thing in the system. It's simply assumed that Garou-PC need a connection to humanity. And it just feels lame to see it laid out like that when CofD 2E should've offered more than enough examples that Touchstones are better off being thematically flavored at least if that mechanic just has to be included.

        It also comes off as cheap if the Gaian cults end up being this assembly of humans who worship Gaia and in return some become immune to Delirium and their children are very likely to be Kin (future Garou). At that point, W5 jumped through all these hoops with Kin only to come around and re-introduce Kinfolk again. Except the explanation makes less sense now. Both Kin and Delirium-immune humans... just happen. At least Kinfolk and Wolf-Blooded people had a place in the setting. Here with Kin and Delirium-immunity it's just there because the ST said so because within the setting there's neither rhyme nor reason to it.

        The issue of the text feeling like the writer was just using different words to restate superficial explanations from the corebook has come up two times already now. Hopefully this isn't too big of an issue with the book. They should've just done W5.5 corebook instead if the writers at Renegade feel like basic elements in the W5 corebook need to be revisited and revised.
