    Renown & Rank, a Deep Dive

    03-10-2025, 06:02 PM
    What is Renown?

    Renown reflects how well a Garou meets the expectations of society. It's a mechanical and narrative element which ties together all of Garou society, contributing to changes in Rank.

    Renown is split into permanent dots (rarely changing) and temporary points (constantly in flux). There are three kinds of Renown, Glory, Honour and Wisdom.

    The systems of Renown, like I say, are both mechanical and narrative. Much like becoming Prince in VTM, or ascending through the Nobility in CTD, are bound by politics and monstrous actions, WTA can at times be far more similar than people expect. There is backstabbing, infighting, and actions which would make for a poor leader in a sane society may well be rewarded in this one. All this to say, if you find yourself looking at the Renown system and thinking that it's unfair, corrupt, riddled with holes or fundamentally unfair, I would call that a Feature, not a Bug, of the setting.

    Because Garou are heroes, albeit monstrous ones, you will see quite a lot of defenses of the Renown system, and associated elements like the Litany and Rank. Just to get it out of the way: criticism of the Garou Nation, and Garou, is a fundamental aspect of the source material. Garou society is explicitly framed as unjust and having deep problems, plenty of which are of their own making. None of that is a criticism of the game, or indeed your characters past and present. I'd argue that it's part of what makes them great!

    Is Renown Objective?

    In a word, no. Much as any decision a government makes is inherently political, any award or removal of Renown is based on values and judgements which come down to the group or individual. If you don't believe me, look at extremist Camps.

    Do Mother's Fundamentalists, reporting to Fundamentalist Elders, reciting their deeds furthering the Fundamentalist cause, suffer losses of Renown? Not at all. Actions which would be dishonourable in a mainstream Sept may be "mission complete" elsewhere.

    You'll see something similar with Tribes. What targets are considered 'glorious' to destroy will depend on the tribal majority. The Get of Fenris consider Sidhe to be their ancient enemies. The Red Talons have great antipathy for humans as a species. Silver Fangs might see any unruly Pack, Camp or Sept, or even Kingdom, as a valid target for warfare, to push their claim to rule over the entire Garou race.

    There is, however, also the matter of incomplete information. W20 establishes that the spirit world will witness your acts even if other Garou are unaware of them. If you break the Litany, for example, you will lose Renown even if other Garou never find out. If you pretend to have dishonoured yourself for honourable reasons, such as pretending a Metis cub is yours in order to protect Gaia in some fashion, the Storyteller may well rule that the Honour on your sheet winds up being better than what your fellows believe of you. Whether a Garou framed of some crime loses Renown or not is also a nebulous area, but given that loss of Rank and Renown can be forced by Punishment Rites and the actions of others, it's clear that framing can have tangible results for the dots on your sheet.

    So, here we see an apparent contradiction, at least at first glance. We know for a fact that there are Elders in every Tribe. We also know that Garou of Ranks 4-5 are an expected feature of a Sept. So there is a presumably consistent (we'll get to that!) spiritual observation of Garou behaviour which is part of the flow of Renown. There is an inconsistent political observation of Garou behaviour which is part of the flow of Renown. We can only assume that the latter is influential, or takes the driver's seat, since if it were secondary to the opinion of the spirits we would see the Renown of certain groups 'lagging' due to their dishonourable or unwise behaviour.

    It's possible to resolve this however! The mistake is to assume there is anything homogeneous about spiritual judgement of a Garou's actions. Ancestor Spirits vary massively between Tribe, Auspice, Camp and so on. A Mother's Fundamentalist who secretly assassinates a Kinfolk patriarch of a rival Fianna family may well gain Honour since the opinion of their own warped Ancestors, and the Red Stag, matter more to their spiritual standing than others.

    Subjectivity is also baked into many of the reward examples themselves. Defend a criminal who is proven guilty? Dishonourable. It actually turns out they're innocent? Retroactively, what you did is now honourable. So your status is determined by the ultimate verdict! How things "come out in the wash", whether a strategy works or not, whether an accusation lands or not, whether a scheme pans out, play a big part. Which means that Renown is, in part, for winners, and in a more meta sense for those who have good luck when rolling d10s. That brings in issues of spin and public relations.

    W20 does also highlight that the Renown expectations will vary depending on Auspice and Tribe. A Ragabash can get away with certain lies and tricks since it's expected, but by the same measure they may struggle to gain Honour they rightly deserve at times. A Fianna's bardic works might make a bigger splash Renown wise than those of a Glass Walker, who comes from a Tribe which places relative emphasis on genius and innovation over storytelling. The reverse might be true if a Fianna discovers something of technological interest!

    So, all this to say, Renown isn't objective. There's a complex interplay of spiritual, legal, political, cultural and contextual factors at play. That is good and interesting, and helps to feed into themes of Personal Horror. And if you find yourself balking at what I've written, just keep in mind that there is one thing we don't see in WTA, to the best of my knowledge. Very experienced, very successful Garou who are beloved by everyone in their Sept and who have many, many victories under their belt, but who are stuck at Rank 1-2 with no clear explanation. If Renown is objective, fixed or fair in some way which Garou fail to understand, if it reflects some pure and inalienable Gaian reality, then you would expect to find Garou who keep putting out hit after hit but never get their flowers because their Totem, or Ancestors, or perhaps a force beyond them, knows that at their heart they are truly lacking in Honour or Wisdom. The observable reality of WTA is that Garou who get on well, and are seen to succeed, accumulate Renown.

    Glory

    I shall be valorous
    I shall be dependable
    I shall be generous
    I shall protect the weak
    I shall slay the Wyrm
    Glory is gained largely through destroying the enemies of the Garou, especially those that serve the Wyrm. Victories which are clean and impressive are particularly valued. Galliards and Ahroun, the Auspices with the highest Rage, have the strongest tie to Glory as it is the major determinant in their rise in Rank.

    This is arguably the simplest category of Renown. Garou are a warrior culture, they see themselves as Gaia's ultimate weapons, and so the only pertinent debates will be over what is an ally and what is a foe. How much Glory was awarded in the War of Rage? How much in the War of Shame? When the Elder Brothers were maligned as being 'of the Wyrm' due to ignorance, were the Garou who conquered their Caerns and unwittingly released Banes kept imprisoned for centuries found wanting in their Glory?

    Given that Garou society is monstrous and historically they have made many terrible decisions, I would argue that it is more likely that Renown was reinforcing this cycle rather than slowing it down. If the invasion of North America by European Garou was failing to pay dividends in Glory due to spiritual disapproval then I find it unthinkable that this would go unmentioned. My takeaway would be that if you and your Sept believe your enemies were 'of the Wyrm' chances are you'll be getting enough Glory that you think you're right due to the actions of your fellow Garou. The function of various Rites and Renown Rewards serve to muddy the waters enough that even if spirits were curbing your rewards you likely wouldn't notice, and to be honest I'm not sure that they would have been in many cases.

    A core problem with Glory is that it rewards making enemies, so long as you don't appear outright foolhardy. It rewards violent solutions over peaceful ones. It rewards behaving in a way which leads to others Challenging you, so long as you are sure you will win. After all, you do typically get to pick the competition when you are challenged. It rewards bragging, and having the gift of the gab, and having access to things that need killing. All of this forms a series of what we call 'perverse incentives', where the intended function of a reward doesn't match with the actual incentives it creates. Might does make Right for Garou, so the fastest way to accumulate Glory is to be the strongest you can possibly be in your "Challenge bracket" (1/2, 1/2/3, 2/3/4, 3/4/5, 4/5).

    Glory is lost by picking battles you can't win, unless this forms a heroic sacrifice. It is also lost for cowardice (including perceived cowardice!) as well as costly defeats. It's important to note that there is every chance you could accumulate Glory for quite a long time without ever truly being tested. It falls to the Storyteller, or perhaps an Ahroun of greater Rank, to watch for this. Circumstance might see an Ahroun repeatedly confronted with enemies who though theoretically fearsome were never any threat to them. And fighting something which is no threat to you does not show the courage one only finds when their life is on the line.

    Honour

    I shall be respectful
    I shall be loyal
    I shall be just
    I shall live by my word
    I shall accept all fair challenges
    While there are many ways to gain Honour, many rewards for this trait are tied to fulfilling the tasks which are expected of Garou. Protecting wolves, Kinfolk and Garou. Being honest. Acting as a mentor. Honour is the core measure of a Philodox, most especially. Theurges and Ahroun need very little Honour at first, but suddenly find themselves requiring huge amounts to access the uppermost Ranks.

    There's something important to keep in mind about Honour, however. It's Garou Honour. To a Garou it may be honourable to engage in murder of the innocent (to protect the Veil, Litany or Gaia), to perform acts which most humans would consider unseemly or barbaric, and to uphold a system which is, to be frank, deeply dishonourable.

    The Garou Nation believes that Might Makes Right. It believes that it is dishonourable to allow your loved ones to 'suffer' your sickness and disability. It encourages its members to commit ritual suicide. In many Septs the grasp on bodily autonomy and consent is very tenuous. Changing Ways gives an example of an Alpha who constantly belittles and mocks the only other Garou in the pack who could possibly challenge her, making her the hazing victim of the entire Pack, talking her down at every opportunity, bullying and even attacking her. We have a canon example of this victim going to various people for help and basically being told, with varying levels of sympathy, that might makes right and that if she wants this to stop she should either leave the Pack or Challenge her abuser... a fight she will likely lose given that her confidence and life path have been so thoroughly wrecked by the person she would be fighting. This isn't presented as an abnormal situation.

    Basically when you're working with Honour Renown, forget everything you know about Honour. Most of the time, "ah, but if you really think about it, isn't it more honourable to-" won't fly. The exceptions we're shown are exceptions. The Children of Gaia are exceptions. The Glass Walkers are exceptions. The Bone Gnawers are exceptions. The Black Furies are exceptions. Your favourite canon NPC is an exception. And each of them gets things right (from a human perspective) which the others get wrong. A Child of Gaia may have the right idea about not punishing a victim of rape or abuse, but have missed the memo on vaccines. A Philodox may rescue Kinfolk from domestic and child abuse but consider their abusers to be fundamentally righteous, even if they're taking their rights 'too far'.

    A tension between what is truly honourable (interfering to protect a victim from a bully) and what is Honourable (advising the victim to either fight back or shut up) makes for great drama. Are you going to rage against the machine, or rage against those who are stopping it from working with their constant moaning about what's 'fair'. Honour is best explored in a Chronicle where both the Storyteller and the players remember that the 'Honour' of an ancient warrior culture is somewhat alien to us. It's also important to remember that there are many, many things which you'd imagine would cost you Honour but don't. Bullying a packmate doesn't cost you Honour by default, so long as you do not talk down your pack as a whole.

    Wisdom

    I shall be calm
    I shall be prudent
    I shall be merciful
    I shall think before I act and listen before I think
    Possibly the most controversial given the Garou Nation's track record with rash and unwise behaviour. W20 makes a point to say that Glory and Wisdom are often at odds with one another in Garou, and that mastering both takes something pretty special. Glory is often awarded most generously when you pick every battle you possibly can, so long as those risks pay off. Wisdom is at times awarded for taking the high road, going to the root of the problem, outwitting a foe instead of destroying them, or preventing battle from being necessary in the first place.

    You can get Wisdom for purification, for uncovering the Wyrm, for understanding and following the spirits. Problems do arise however. Much as what a Garou considers Honourable may not be very honourable, what the Garou consider Wise may not be all that wise. Metis lose Wisdom for keeping their deformity hidden. Essentially, the state mandates that if you refuse to be a victim of prejudice, you are showing a lack of wisdom. Making use of modern methods which may show great wisdom can cost you Wisdom. You're asked to take part in a Rite, but you know of a reason it would be a poor decision? Well you'd better take part, as ordered, or lose some Wisdom. You're offered a promotion out of favouritism? Turning it down is unwise.

    Frenzy, a result of circumstance in most cases, costs you Wisdom. Which makes Ahroun rather unwise for being born on the full moon, various Garou unwise for being attacked by a Bitter Rage, and even more Garou unwise for existing in a culture where hazing and bullying are relatively common.

    Meanwhile it's relatively easy, if you have the favour of a Theurge mentor, to gradually work your way through learning the various low-level Rites and farm up Renown. Now of course the ST should arbitrate here. Just as it isn't impressive for a Rank 4 Ahroun to slay a group of weak Fomori, and thus the Glory reward should be minimal if any, it also isn't very impressive for a Theurge who is a quick reader to master their 10th Level 1 Rite of the year. But it does stand that learning these Rites makes you more 'Wise'. Now I'm sure that there is wisdom in taking time for such things, and there is wisdom in the lessons you learn from performing these Rites for the first time... but ultimately you can substitute demonstrating true and enduring wisdom such as giving someone lifechanging advice for the slog of working your way through the backlog of Rites you'll probably find quite useful.

    Wisdom gets lost for things like wrongly assuming something is Wyrmish, losing or breaking a Talen or Fetish, being too 'modern', breaking the Litany, and being foolhardy.

    That's it for now. In future I'd like to cover Punishment Rites, how the advantages of your own circumstances can influence your Renown journey, and ultimately how it can be hard to build up Renown if you're solving problems created by the people deciding whether your actions were Honourable and Wise or not. I'd really welcome feedback! If there are any interesting examples of NPCs in the Rage Across books who are in weird situations with Renown, or any published adventures with really weird Renown rewards suggested to the ST, I'd love to hear about them, and anything in that vein.

      In my opinion the renown system has always been something that’s more interesting if it’s RPed out on some level, and I think that’s a big part of what Moots are useful for both IC & OOC. The Moot is the regularly occurring interval where Garou are all encouraged to gather and go through the ritualized recitation of their deeds and to receive recognition for having gained renown, as well as being the places where packs and war parties can form for the basis of performing deeds worthy of renown. Akin to what you mentioned, I think the Moot is a fantastic way of demonstrating the Garou way of politicking, with the elders sat nearest to the center and at the most liberty to direct matters and with the young turks sniping around the edges, attempting to whip up their own renown, or setting the stage for their plans.

      (Vancouver by Night isn’t a perfect book but one of the reasons I always liked it was for the character of Gut-Tooth, the prophet of Gaia, on the werewolf side, who seemed to me like a great example of the kinds of antagonists who an ST can have thriving within the Nation itself)

      Something that I think is interesting to encourage with PC packs, who tend to start young and largely unestablished, is to have them be in a protectorate with a handful of septs and loners. This variety gives them the chance to triangulate with the sort of Garou most willing to tolerate their methods, however unorthodox, or alternatively shows them the benefits of being inside the tent pissing out. Ostensibly any Garou who learns the rites of recognition for permanent renown and raising rank, regardless of their standing with the predominant local leadership, can perform these rites. This creates an interesting possibility in my opinion where, the hypothetical imagined pack of rebel youths imagined by early editions, can seek out the means of advancement by sacrificing the benefits of the leading septs in exchange for aligning with the weirdos or spiritual patrons earned on quests.

          Nice deep dive.

          I'd add under the subjectivity of Renown, is that broadly the game presents Temporary Renown as something mostly biased towards the judgement of spirits, and Permanent Renown more in the hands of the Garou themselves. The spirits can reward you with dozens of temporary honor, but if no Garou will perform the Rite of Accomplishment for you, your Permanent Renown stagnates, your standing in the Nation doesn't go anywhere, and your Rank can't go up (which is also in the hands of other Garou rather than spirits). The game does leave the option of the spirits just acting on their own (this is mostly a dial to allow for different styles of play to be functional). This helps explain a lot of the dichotomies to Garou life without putting too much pressure on the ST to make it unfun to play. Since the ability to determine temporary Renown in-character basically doesn't exist, NPCs need to be convinced you've done enough things to warrant a Rite of Accomplishment, which is were so much bias can enter the equation. Does the NPC give you your Rite quickly because of your breeding, or demand more of you because you should be living up to your bloodline? Do they understand your Auspice duties to forgive a bit of less than perfect behavior or not care? Etc.

          This isn't a hard line of course, but as a broad framework it's a bit easier to understand how these dynamics manifest.

                      Rites and Renown

                      Regardless of whether an individual Garou is gaining and losing Renown due to the influence of Garou or Spirits, or in many cases a combination, the process of gaining permanent 'dots' of Renown requires the input of the Garou's 'elders'. This is where Rites of Accomplishment come in. Meanwhile a Garou's Renown can be reduced, rightly or wrongly, through the use of Punishment Rites.

                      W20 does establish that in unusual circumstances, when a Garou's accomplishments have no doubt whatsoever and their elevation in Glory, Honour or Wisdom should go without saying, that a permanent increase can happen without any Rite or outside interference.

                      In very rare cases, the character’s courage, wisdom, or honor is so exceptional that there is little doubt that the werewolf has increased in standing. If that is the case, you can simply decide to award a point of permanent Renown without the Rite or the challenge. This should happen very infrequently, reserved for cases where the tales of the character’s deeds will last for a long time.
                      Failing this, Rites of Renown are required.

                      Rites of Renown

                      These Rites are performed by rolling Charisma + Rituals, so if you have a choice of which NPC handles your elevation, pick someone likeable or persuasive.

                      Rite of Boasting: a great example of a Rite which allows Garou to 'game' the system of Renown somewhat. You can boast for yourself, or your whole pack, that you will achieve something boastworthy. If you do it you get bonus Glory, if you fail to do it you'll lose Glory. The potential bonus or loss is equal to half the number of successes, so with 10 successes you could stand to gain an additional 5 Glory, or lose 5 Glory. Naturally the bonus can't exceed the total for what you're boasting about doing. If taking out a certain threat nets you 3 Glory, the absolute maximum you could get for it with Boasting would be 6.

                      Here we immediately see a place where discrepancies in culture, personality, and what role you are expected to fulfil within your society begin to creep in. A Bone Gnawer Metis with a withered leg bragging that he will claim the head of the Spiral Dancer due to be hunted that night will realistically be met with a level of heckling, disbelief and sabotage which won't occur when a Homid or Lupus of a more 'respectable' Tribe makes the boast. From what we're shown of the Garou we can infer that such a boast, from the Metis, would likely result in someone else going out of their way to secure the kill and claim the head themselves, so as to better 'instruct' their lessers on the importance of not making a boast they can't fulfil. A popular individual who has the right social 'mandate' to make such a boast, like a boisterous Lupus Cliath of the Get, may find that people who like them and are charmed by the idea of them succeeding do what they can to facilitate it, within reason.

                      When you get into the weeds of this, you start to find that it isn't just that Might Makes Right, by the same measure Right Makes Might. Much of an Ahroun's power, most especially, comes from how swiftly they can accumulate Glory. If your ability to accumulate Glory is influenced by the degree to which your society sees you as a 'good fit' for it, then things like disability, class, Tribal affiliation, family importance, ancestry, Pure Breed, attractiveness, hell, neurotype, will begin to play a disproportionate role. I'm a tall and assertive guy, and to me the idea of bragging about how I'm totally going to do X and Y and Z is absolutely mortifying, so a level of passive discrimination is immediately at play. Of course such boasting is just as likely to backfire as it is to work, but when it does work, you're then slotted in at Fostern or Adren, at which point you are expected to handle more dangerous missions and hunt for more powerful foes, with the assistance of more underlings and access to more powerful tools; again, Right makes Might.

                      Rite of Wounding: mostly this Rite serves as a means by which Cubs, and more rarely Cliaths, get some of the temporary Glory they require. The only especially noteworthy thing about this Rite from a sociological perspective is that some Tribes and Septs ensure the wound scars by rubbing ash into it. Part of the culture of the Garou is a focus on having survived battles, and bearing marks of those battles. You could see this as a kind of Machismo, an interest in being a warrior rather than a looker. It should go without saying that a Garou whose Homid form has distinctive scars now has a disadvantage in certain situations. They are more recognisable, easier to describe, and a random hunter is more likely to think "huh, he looks the type". Of course there are also marginal benefits, like potentially looking more intimidating to someone who has never been in a fight! We can only assume that Garou who choose to scar their underlings, or who ask to be scarred themselves, know this and consider the warrior culture to be more important than marginal losses in strategy.

                      Rite of Accomplishment: this Gift turns 10 temporary Glory, Honour or Renown into a single permanent point of the relevant Renown type. Immediately this serves an important function in pacing a Garou's ascent. No matter how glorious your deeds in a given month, short of some legendary accomplishment for your current Rank which enters into local myth immediately, you will at most gain 1 Glory, 1 Honour and 1 Wisdom with each passing month. And that, to be fair, would be an exceptionally fast accumulation of Renown! NPCs pass years and even decades without increasing Rank, so we can only assume that there is a normal amount of waxing and waning at work, otherwise the distribution of the Garou population in terms of Rank would be very top-heavy.

                      Accessing this Rite is not a given. If someone refuses to perform it, and has enough clout to make it unlikely that others will perform it, you might find yourself running around a Moot trying to do favours or make promises. Push comes to shove you may have to Challenge someone to access this Rite, which could be very difficult if you are Rank 1 and there aren't any Rank 2 Garou who know the Rite in the Sept. Given that it goes against Garou Tradition for you to challenge a Rank 3 Adren, a Cliath in that position might just have to accept that they are powerless and need to attend a different Moot next time.

                      This Rite, of course, can fail like any other. A Charisma 1 Rituals 2 Theurge (silly thought experiment, but feasible given that this is a Level 2 Ritual!) has a 87% chance of succeeding, or 74% if someone heckles the proceedings to try and stop you from receiving the Renown. With a more reasonable dice pool of 6 the odds are 96% and 88%. So, assuming that the Rite is rolled procedurally, there may be a 4% chance that you won't gain the Renown you're owed, or a 12% chance if someone heckles you. What's important to note is that if the Rite fails then you, the recipient, are generally blamed. Garou superstition holds that there must be some hidden fault in you. A Botch is worse; you are expected to undergo some kind of contrition before you can have any Rite of Accomplishment performed, even for other Renown types, due to a grave inner failing.

                      What's important to note about this Rite is that ease of access is everything. A Silver Fang whose mother is the Master of the Rite, and who has all the right Backgrounds and Merits, will likely have an easier time accessing this Rite at a Moot, and may well have it performed by someone more competent. While every Rite demands silence and attention, there may well be more fanfare and a greater sense of 'occasion'. While this isn't necessarily an advantage at most Moots, it's worth noting that the sooner you get your Renown, the sooner you can Challenge to improve your Rank. And the sooner you have an empty temporary Renown track, the sooner you can begin earning Renown again, and a Moot is a great place to do so with challenges and riddles and advice to give and conflicts to resolve, along with hunts and special Rites and so on. As I said, Right makes Might.

                      Rite of Praising: with the Rite of Accomplishment in mind, it can be easy to wonder what happens when a Cliath's quick thinking and bravery are pivotal or important in saving a Caern from invasion. This Rite handles that situation quite well. You get your relevant Renowns bumped up using the Rite of Accomplishment, and you may well have even gained another permanent point of one or more Renown types at the moment you did this great deed if the Storyteller wills it, but you may well still 'waste' a large amount of temporary Glory or similar. And so, given that you've gone far above and beyond what would ever be expected for your Rank, you receive this Rite as a special congratulations. You're employee of the month, have some Talens or a Fetish, and a nice social dice pool boost for a few months.

                      I find myself wondering what happens if this Rite fails. Perhaps the takeaway is that those who decided the Garou was worthy of special congratulations were mistaken, and there is some hidden truth which makes the praiseworthy act more pedestrian than they had realised.

                      What does this ultimately mean? What is the functional influence of Rites of Accomplishment? You could think of it as a kind of pressure valve. Outside of exceptional circumstances, a Garou's ascent is moderated, guided, and paced by the Garou above them. The difference between a rising star who rockets up to Adren in record time and an exceptional Fostern who "still has much to learn" would be the whims of Elders. Given Tribal, Camp and familial conflicts, preferences based on Auspice and Breed, political considerations and even the timing of a new Garou's 'arrival' into a Sept, it seems absolutely inconceivable that Elders wouldn't have their thumb-claw on the scale as it were.

                      Rites of Punishment

                      We're told that these Rites are performed only when necessary, and when conventional punishments (and presumably the standard losses of Renown) don't do the trick. Charisma + Rituals, difficulty 7 typically, and a failure tends to be interpreted in Garou superstition as a sign that what they were trying to punish is not serious enough to warrant a Punishment Rite. It's also noteworthy that W20 tells us that at times the spirit world will cause an attempted Punishment Rite to automatically fail due to its injustice, automatically warranting an Honour loss on the part of those performing it.

                      I get into some examples below, but I think it's really important that PCs are shown NPCs suffering these Rites, and encounter (directly and indirectly) characters who have suffered such punishments. You could have an Elder talk about how a certain punishment set them straight and is the reason they are the hero they are today, for example, or the PCs could come across Garou whose otherwise promising careers have been destroyed by cruel and unusual punishments. Hell, a great villain would be a Philodox who has become an unwitting pawn of the Wyrm as they become harsher and harsher towards those who are straying, coming up with convincing and powerful arguments for why small infractions must be taken seriously (gotta be tough on crime!). This would be a great side plot in a story which emphasizes injustice as a major theme, for example one where the players are dealing with a Pentex subsidiary which builds private prisons and then does everything it can to ensure they get filled.

                      Note that there are Punishment Rites in W20 which I won't cover here as they aren't relevant to the discussion of Renown.

                      Rite of the Jackdaw: this Punishment Rite has little to do with Renown and more to do with breaking a promise of secrecy... though that, of course, does tend to lose a Garou some Honour. This is a simple punishment which makes the target begin to blab all their personal details, like who they have a crush on or which Elder scared them so much as a kid they wet themselves.

                      Rite of Ostracism: a punishment used for 'lesser' crimes, all present declare that they've never heard of the punished Garou, and they are temporarily banished from their pack, Sept and so on. Everyone agrees to ignore them, and people only discard the punishment if absolutely necessary. It's also noted that some will "accidentally" attack or harm the banished individual. Receiving this punishment costs you 1 Glory, 5 Honour and 1 Wisdom, which could do almost nothing (if you have some temporary to spare!) or be absolutely devastating and temporarily knock you down a Rank. Note that this punishment is for 'lesser' crimes and that it seems to be considered normal by the Garou Nation at large; we are given no indication that it's rare or odd or old fashioned.

                      As a result, my storytelling tip is to have an Ostracised NPC in your game! Perhaps they're the only one who would be able to help the PCs, but given that they won't exist until the next full moon... your choices are to break the punishment and seek out this Garou who 'doesn't exist', or to sit tight and wait. Giving succour to a Garou in need who has been punished in this way might be a strong defining character beat for a new PC, as would callously ignoring them to stick to the rules. A Child of Gaia who will pour water down any parched throat... unless they deserve to be thirsty of course is very different from a Child of Gaia who will help others even at risk to their own status, and highlighting that difference early on could be very interesting.

                      Pretty juvenile as a punishment, and ultimately unproductive as ostracising a person only serves to further alienate them. Banning this punishment in the name of recognising and taking responsibility for every single Garou who has not betrayed or left the Nation would be a step in the right direction, but presumably this Rite must have advocates or it wouldn't exist! Make sure to have NPCs who have values which align with these punishments and who see them as inherently right and proper, and who are deeply offended by the suggestion that this ancient tradition stops. You can't just have ambient badness in your Sept with no nameable NPCs who are in favour of it.

                      Stone of Scorn: this one is where I really start to cringe. We are told, and should take seriously, that Garou sit around doing this for hours. Just talking shit about someone. Someone who has committed some serious crime of course, but presumably that's already cost them a hefty amount of Honour at the very least. We're told that this Rite "often" takes a whole night and that the tales told become more and more extreme as time goes on. Gaia is dying, corporations are growing fatter and fatter while poverty intensifies, Banes are reaching new record concentrations with each passing month, and we're told that Garou sit around for a whole night saying mean things about someone who broke an important rule.

                      Patience for this needs to be extremely limited. A senior Philodox metes out punishment (demotion, a new chore, a whipping, an unpleasant solo quest) and the matter is closed. Instead we have a tradition where Garou delight in excessive and even childish cruelty.

                      Oh and the target loses 8 Honour and 2 Wisdom, naturally. If you want to show the weakness of a particular Sept you could have some theft, sabotage or desecration take place while everyone is howling with laughter at this Rite, only for the Sept at large to blame the person who was being punished for the 'distraction'.

                      Voice of the Jackal: a Garou who has shamed their whole Tribe or Sept is punished using this Rite, which gives them a shrill high-pitched voice like a jackal. Until the punishment is ended you subtract 2 dice from all social rolls. This costs you 2 Glory and 5 Honour. If the one who punished you won't end the punishment, performing a great duty for Gaia can reverse it.

                      Meeting a NPC who has suffered this Rite might help to introduce the PCs to what the Garou Nation is like, or what a given Sept would consider a crime which fits this punishment.

                      The Hunt: this Rite is used as a means of honourable execution when a Garou needs to be killed but their Honour remains intact. Garou who die in The Hunt are forgiven of all their crimes.

                      It's worth noting that a society where being forgiven in death is important is very different to the one most of us live in. The idea of your honour mattering when you are no longer alive is alien to us, but it isn't so strange for the Garou who have Ancestor Spirits and so on. The fact that such Garou aren't sent to fight the Wyrm tells its own tale; this isn't a series of punishments crafted with a logical plan, it's more a collection of traditions which are so deep-rooted that they're (seemingly) impossible to change.

                      Rite of the Omega Wolf: this is used when a Pack wishes to punish their Alpha for failure. It's very funny to me that this Rank includes spitting on the Alpha, or even pissing on them, but the writeup implies that at the end of the Rite they're still the Alpha! Naturally it isn't guaranteed that any one member of your Pack will have the guts to outright challenge you, but this Rite is funny to me because it suggests that you could be too cowardly to defeat your Alpha (even taking it in turns to Challenge them until they are worn down) but brave enough to grab them as a group, tie them up, and haze them, and then go on obeying them. My assumption would be that the Garou Nation's stance is "if your Alpha is such a failure, you should be able to take them in a Challenge, no?"

                      The Rite is self-defeating, for what it's worth. The problem is poor leadership, so you give a -3 dice penalty to the Alpha's leadership, which will only make things worse.

                      Satire Rite: used as a more serious version of the Stone of Scorn, this Rite is basically a blistering satirical work intended to shame you so terribly that you drop in Rank. Given that a whole huge gathering of Garou needs to play along with it and let you do it, this Rite is probably very, very rarely used. I would point out that it only makes sense that a Garou who needed to be reduced in Rank would get there through losing enough Glory, Honour or Wisdom that they automatically drop their Rank... but this is more immediate, so it serves a function.

                      What adds insult to injury is that the song enters into collective memory. A great plot-thread for a Galliard PC would be them learning a song which originated from one of these Rites, which is a bit like a limerick or folk tale at this point. They could later meet the Ancestor Spirit of the Garou who was satirized and hear their side of the story. Alternatively the subject could still be alive, and have moved to a different Sept where they've regained their lost rank or perhaps exceeded it. Finding them could be hard given that they would need to change their Deed Name at the first opportunity. If you wanted to show the spitefulness of an Elder, you could have a Galliard's Challenge for gaining Rank be to find the Garou described in the song, which might lead to the satire reaching the Garou's new Sept for the first time. "Ah, makes sense, he's sent you after me to sing the song. Spread it here if you wish, there's some excellent rhyme, just know that if you do I will hunt down that old bastard and kill him, or die trying."

                      What can we learn from these Rites? Well, a lot of time and effort goes into them. They are quite excessive and passionate, and clearly driven by a creature filled with Rage. None of these Punishments are quiet, proportionate or somber. They're visceral, base, highly public. This serves an important function on a wider level. It isn't just a punishment for a wrongdoer, it's a warning to any who might emulate them.

                      It's also important to note that there is no strict Garou legal code. The Litany is like the Ten Commandments, there are rules there, but we don't have a minimum and maximum sentence for a given crime which is defined precisely as when you blah blah and blah with strict terms to distinguish it from other similar crimes. So again nepotism will rear its ugly head. Who needs 'making an example of'? Who 'has already suffered enough - they already lost Renown, and now they've formally performed a Rite of Contrition'?. In a society which is explicitly described as unjust and gripped by various bigotries, it's easy to imagine that the demographics of who gets Punishment Rites (Metis, Bone Gnawers, members of marginal Camps, and so on) will differ from those who simply suffer the loss of Renown and move on. Who has people ready to cover for them? Who has a sage mentor ready to tell them the right apology and gift to offer up to make the whole thing blow over? Who gets wrongly egged on to stand up for themselves after a small error, causing it to spiral into a bigger deal than it should have been?

                      Of course certain protections can backfire! Pure Breed means high expectations. Being from a powerful family, being surrounded by loyal Kinfolk, having success after success, may well mean there are people who want to take you down a peg. It might not even be you they're hoping to harm, but whoever you're seen as being an extension of. A Cliath (of any background) who finds themselves suffering a Punishment Rite might be part of a larger scheme they know nothing about, their misdeed becoming a proxy battle between different Garou who are fractured on ideological or ethnic lines, or by past quarrels.

                      The overall picture we can take from these Rites is a society which is, at times, lacking perspective. Suffering seems to be key to Garou existence. Changing Ways tells us that beatings are a common punishment, and that when Garou sleep with one another execution will be on the table for discussion. For all the Garou talk about being Gaia's front-line warriors, they seem to act like they have soldiers to spare. Keeping a Garou totally ignored for a month. Wasting a whole night chatting shit about someone. Letting an honourable criminal die in a hunt rather than die in battle against the enemy.

                      When we look at Renown Rites we also see a huge range of potential outcomes without ever being explicitly discriminatory. Prompt, competent and warm Rites of Accomplishment might see a Tribe's Renown rise very rapidly. Begrudging last-minute Rites may see your ascent slowed considerably. It would be easy to imagine that if this were occurring it would be pointed out, but keep in mind that the fastest possible way to lose Honour and ruin your career is to question this system. W20 explicitly tells us that complaining is a great way to get yourself in even more trouble. This is a society where questioning being born into a rigid caste system based on your horoscope has serious consequences - you'd better believe that pointing out systemic discrimination and corruption is risky at best and shooting yourself in the foot (or head) at worst.
