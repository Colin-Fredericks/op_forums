    [W5] Returning What Was Taken

    12-27-2023, 09:03 PM
    I have some thoughts on rules approaches for W5 and how to reintroduce concepts that it threw out with the bathwater. I have to stress, however, that I do not have a copy of W5 in electronica or paper, only the pdfs of V5 and H5 from that Humble Bundle promotion that they had a few months back, so I'm not sure what might be retreading ground or not. You can pretty well assume that my suggestion for accommodating these changes, however, is "get rid of Harano/Hauglosk". In fact, even if you don't use them, I'd still suggest getting rid of Harano and Hauglosk as degeneration mechanics!

    Primal-Urge

    At the top of the list is adding in a kind of "power stat", in the form of Primal-Urge. PU then takes over most shapeshifting from Rage, as it used to, in the form of Primal-Urge + Resolve rolls. It also adds a bonus to some of the less beefy forms to make them really good at an aspect of conflict, which for Homid involves getting people to talk by loosening up (social engineering, in other words), and for Glabro and Lupus involves taking advantage of cover, artificial and natural, respectively, to hide or to duck behind to avoid attacks. Crinos and Hispo do kind of get a benefit from it, but this is rolled in with frenzy changes.

    Breeds ("Birth")

    If you're keeping the Crinos-born as disabled, my suggestion for a name-switch would be a reference to a certain smith god using the Roman name to match the mostly-Latin naming scheme of forms. So, it's Volcanus, not a certain titaness that bore Athena in Zeus's head. Also, I dispensed with "breed" in favor of "Birth", as in Birth Form, to step around the name of the Crinos-born in one way if not another. Those born in Crinos and Lupus also recover from disconnection (q.v.) more easily, but I haven't hammered out quite by how much.

    Regeneration

    Garou aren’t some kind of unliving offense to Gaia that has to “rouse” a stolen force in order to heal – it just happens. Outside of their Birth forms, with the exception of those born in Crinos, a Garou will heal two superficial levels of damage per turn. In any given form, she will heal one aggravated level per day. No roll is required.

    Up next is how to handle the Garou's spirit side. I'm assuming that you're dumping Umbral damage and the need to use a rite to enter the Umbra, incidentally.

    Disconnection


    While suffused with Umbral energies as half-spirit beings, Garou do not possess an infinite well of these reserves. When calling upon Gifts, appeasing spirits, or otherwise expending spiritual energy, the character must check to see if there is a widening gap in her proximity to her spiritual side.
    Disconnection ranges from 0, where the Garou is incredibly spiritually in-tune and vital, to 5, which sees her looking inexplicably worn out regardless of her physical health and a pale, flickering shadow when in the Umbra, which can become a difficult place to get to while in this state.
    Calling upon spiritual energy to accomplish a task is an Essence roll, which can accomplish a great many things but always entails rolling a single die. A success (result of 6+) does not result in a noticeable increase in Disconnection. Failure increases Disconnection by 1. Essence rolls cannot take place in the same turn that the character spends a level of Rage, and vice versa, unless a Gift calls for it.

    Effects of Disconnection

    Being disconnected makes passage through the Umbra more difficult, and each point increases the time necessary to Step Sideways by one turn. Disconnection also acts as a negative dice pool modifier to rolls to sense spiritual presences or to utilize subtler and more introspective Gifts.

    Recovering from Disconnection

    The most common way to recover from being worn of spiritual energies is to meditate in a spiritually compatible place, preferably in a caern. Roll the character’s Primal-Urge + Composure + Caern Rating; every two successes alleviates a point of Disconnection. This can only be accomplished once per day, and meditation over concurrent days increases the required successes by one per the total number of days relying on this method.

    Another method is known as the Sacred Hunt, where a form of spiritual prey is pursued by all the members of a sept. At the conclusion of the rite, all Garou who participated alleviate all levels of Disconnection.

    Finally, spirits can be cajoled to directly supply their reserves. Provided that the werewolf has not requested it of any spirits for an entire month, she may make a persuasion roll, modified by the temperament and attitude that a spirit has for her. Each success allows her to alleviate one point of Disconnection.

    Causes of Disconnection:

    Utilizing various spiritual powers (number of checks dependent on Gift or other power)

    Not stepping sideways into the Umbra for a year (check every year, on the same day of the year).

    Subsisting on highly processed foods for a month (check repeats monthly).

    Not saving a wolf from human actions or Triatic minions (check each incident). Fera swap this out for the appropriate animal of their kind.​


    Rage

    This is, by far, the largest section. I'll divide it up.


    Rage is a state of inhuman fury that Garou nearly always fall into, at least on some level. There are six discrete levels of Rage that werewolves can experience, ranging from Calm, a state that few, if any, werewolves feel for an extended period of time, to Apoplectic, where the character is constantly on the edge of frenzy but still maintaining tenuous control. Intensifying Rage involves hurt, shame, embarrassment, or other intense emotions, while decreasing it typically requires venting this ferocity toward a specific end. Completely losing control and sliding into a frenzy is the ultimate form of this venting, though it is also possible for Rage to level off until finding a scene full of aggravating circumstances and individuals.


    Levels of Rage

    0 – Calm: The Garou is mostly unhindered by any hints of inhuman anger – but can still be normally angry. There are no mechanical differences with the “normal” state to note other than that if some stimulus provokes a frenzy in the character, she gains a +2 bonus to resist… but even if she resists successfully, her Rage increases by one level.

    1 – Vexed: Starting here, the werewolf begins to experience supernatural levels of emotion… and agitation. At this level and those higher, add half of the character’s Rage level, rounded up, to the difficulty to resist frenzy. However, this comes with a decidedly dangerous upside, in that the Garou can apply their Defense against Firearms attacks without penalty even without cover.

    2 – Furious: The Garou can now add her Rage level to Dexterity + Athletics tests out of combat, or once per turn in combat. She may also reduce her Rage level to apply this bonus to every Dexterity + Athletics test she makes in combat until her next turn.

    3 – Fiery: By taking a Dexterity + Athletics test to move, the Garou can move up to ten yards per point of Rage, passing the distance as if flashing between two spaces. If she lowers her Rage rating by one, she does not need to roll and may move in this fashion up to her Dexterity times before her next turn. This movement can be accomplished in any direction, so long as the werewolf has the means to do so.

    4 – Raving: At this point, the character must resist going into a frenzy over more inconsequential inconveniences, such as being informed of delays or setbacks, having her way blocked by unfamiliar individuals, or simply being confronted by non-Garou authority figures. However, she may also attack every opposed character in close combat with a single attack, or make two attacks against a single close combat target.

    5 – Apoplectic: The werewolf must resist frenzy every turn while in combat, or every scene while out of it. It is plainly obvious that she is dangerous, due to her fitful breathing, ruddy countenance, clenched jaw, and pulsing veins. However, this feverish state also allows her to casually oppose the extreme speed of those that would normally deny her an effort to defend herself, such as certain vampires. By opting to make two attacks against everyone in arm’s reach or three attacks against an individual, she can vent this level of Rage and lower her rating back to being only Raving.

    Spending Levels of Rage

    While some levels of Rage have unique expenditure circumstances, Garou at any levels of Rage above 0 can shed one level to do one of the following:

    Activate a Gift that requires one or more levels of Rage (no spending levels that you don’t have).
    Ignore the result of being stunned in combat.
    Instantly change to any other form without the need to roll.
    Recover from being at Incapacitated or lower health. This automatically causes the character to frenzy but restores her to her last two health levels regardless of how many more she should be suffering, which can save her not only from being inactive but also from dying. This option can only be used once per scene.


    Gaining Rage


    Upon seeing the moon the first time since she last woke up, the Garou gains a level of Rage. If it is her auspice moon, she gains two levels.

    Critical Failures and other humiliating experiences roll a variable number of dice, from one to five. Each success increases the character’s Rage level by one.

    Betrayal can sting straight through to the soul. Experiencing it from a close character such as a packmate or touchstone increases the character’s Rage by two levels.

    If suddenly thrown into a tense situation from rest, the Garou gains one Rage level.

    If a Garou would gain Rage but is already Apoplectic, she remains at the same level but can gain the benefit of expending as many levels of Rage as she would have gained without actually decreasing it. It also requires a frenzy roll at a -2 penalty.


    Consequences of Rage


    Rage is not a subtle thing, and its temperamental nature makes those without it wary and fearful of those who do – and they can feel it radiating off of Garou and others like them, like a wash of heat on a road in the sun.

    For every point of Rage presently higher than the character’s Composure, she takes a penalty to all Social rolls not geared toward frightening others or, oddly enough, unhinged carousing.

    If a character’s Primal-Urge + present Rage are higher than a non-Kinfolk human or animal’s Willpower, they feel ill at ease around the werewolf and will seek to avoid her. This is known as the Curse, and tends to make life among normal humans or wolves difficult. Kinfolk frequently act as intermediaries, where a pool of highly focused and unflappable workers is not available.


    Frenzy


    There are two ways that Rage overcomes Garou – the berserk frenzy, in which the werewolf assumes Crinos, stands and fights (or typically slaughters and massacres); and the fox frenzy, where Rage fans the flame of fear and causes the Garou to change into Lupus and flee with all speed. Resisting either state requires a Willpower test. Characters are not out of the control of the player, technically, in either case, but the player is obligated to play out a character who is out of control, and out of control really means out of control. Having a Primal-Urge at least equal to the current level of Rage can keep the werewolf from attacking members of their pack, but that’s mostly the extent of control, barring Gifts that alter the way frenzy works.

    Berserk frenzies last until enemies or the Garou are dead. Fox frenzies last until after the Garou manages to flee, evade any pursuers, and hide for at least a scene. Garou tend to be much more susceptible to going berserk than being overtaken by the fox; generally, only the most hopeless situations stir the latter significantly enough for it to require a roll. Additionally, if any opposing forces are armed with silver, increase the difficulty of any of these triggers, berserk or fox, by one.

    Sample berserk frenzy triggers:

    Provocation
    Difficulty
    Friend injured by an aggressor
    2 (serious injury 3, killed 4)
    Lover or Touchstone injured by an aggressor
    3 (serious injury 4, killed 5)
    Packmate injured by an aggressor not in the pack
    4 (serious injury 5, killed 6)
    Insulted
    2 (by perceived inferior 3)
    Public Humilation
    2 (3 if in front of higher-Ranked character)
    First injury in fight
    1 (serious injury 2, injury that would be lethal to a human 3)

    Sample fox frenzy triggers:

    Provocation
    Difficulty
    Excessively weird circumstance outside of comfort zone (without pack present)
    1
    Confronting a Nexus Crawler (re-check each time it warps reality)
    2
    All other packmates die in battle
    3
    Confronting a Maeljin Incarna or Talon of the Wyrm
    4 (5 in Malfeas)
    Confronting a Triatic Wyrm Avatar
    5 (7 in Malfeas)

    A Garou can temper their actions in frenzy for a turn by spending a Willpower point. After spending three such points, the frenzy breaks and the character is considered to have shaken it off and cleared her head. However, while in a berserk frenzy, if the Garou kills an opponent, roll Composure. A critical failure indicates that she stops fighting for a turn to begin eating that unfortunate. If the character has lingered in Crinos for days without eating sufficiently larger meals, this occurs on a simple failure. Only grossly inedible characters will be passed over, in favor of the nearest likely-edible character. On the next turn, repeat the roll; a failure indicates that she continues eating, while a success snaps her out of it and puts her back in the fight.

    When not tempering her actions, a Garou gain a bonus to frenzy-viable actions equal to her Primal-Urge value, a like penalty to actions that aren’t supported by the frenzy, and double that penalty for actions that go against the character of a frenzy (if the dice pool is reduced to 0 or lower, the action cannot be attempted). The character of a frenzy involves turning non-packmates nearby into piles of meat or scrap on the ground or pursuing nearby non-packmates for berserk frenzies; for a fox frenzy, the bonus applies to actions that would put you further away from whatever prompted it, which can include fighting if there is no other way out, but a fox frenzy prioritizes escape over that; only actual unavoidable fights will receive the bonus, and the Garou will also be more concerned about making sure that the other party won’t be able to pursue them than with necessarily killing them; a quick, crippling blow over a sustained fight, in other words.

    At no point is it necessary to remove the player from control of their character; if, for whatever reason, the player keeps attempting to undertake penalized actions with dice pools above 0, the lack of success should stand on its own. Talk out any differences over declaring the actions of the player’s character.​


    And now for an adaptation of 13th Age's Escalation Die and Aberrant 2e's Collateral pool...

    Carnage


    This track, running from 1 to 10, denotes the level of danger in the present scene. It should not be used for every scene; combat is a sure one, and also highly strained negotiations, but it can be omitted if the Storyteller and players feel that it wouldn't fit. For scenes where it is used, it is a narrative tool that helps to guide the horrifying violence that follows Garou and Fera around like their own shadows. Specifically, it’s all about turning otherwise nondescript levels of damage into maiming and dismemberment. The Garou can, of course, heal this damage easily, but it still hurts like hell. Humans, naturally, are out of luck, and even vampires are in for an unpleasant time if they hang around werewolves, assuming that they don't simply end up suffering the final death.

    For every turn that any Garou in the scene spends in Crinos or Hispo, increase the Carnage counter by one (or two in an uncontrolled berserk frenzy). If particularly hazardous weapons or substances are present, such as high explosives or intense harmful environmental hazards, increase the count by one or more per use or turn as appropriate. The count stops at 10, unless you’re in a hopeless situation against godlike entities, where it usually starts at 5 and might not have a ceiling.

    When no characters are in Crinos or Hispo and no attacks occur, reduce the Carnage track by one. One or more characters entering fox frenzy will also reduce the Carnage value by one per turn of unrestrained flight.

    When Carnage is in play, crippling injuries are the norm, even for superficial damage (count superficial damage as half its value in aggravated for these purposes). Instead of rolling a d10, add the present Carnage rating. Note that for Garou, Fera, and spirits, 13+ denotes incapacitated but still able to regenerate. It does increase the difficulty to avoid battle scars by 1 each time in a scene it occurs, however. In this state, any additional damage can be terminal for changers, barring a typical roll for recovery.

    While Garou and Fera can regenerate from lost limbs, this isn’t certain. Roll Primal-Urge at the end of the scene, with a difficulty of 3 if the character used Rage to recover from being incapacitated or killed. Failure means that she fails to recover from the most severe injury.​


    And here's something that plays into truly old school loresheets...

    Rituals and Rites – A System Topic


    The bedrock of Garou society, knowledge of their customs and practices also give rise to what outsiders would potentially consider “magic”, though to the Garou, they are a sacred part of their spiritual world. Each point of this loresheet must be purchased concurrently, i.e. no two dot level without the first, no third without the second, etc. Fera will also possess this Loresheet, but for their own specific culture and practices.

    Lore

    1 – You have likely just been introduced to Garou culture and have some idea about why it is they perform some ceremonies. Some key utility rites are available, such as Talisman Dedication or Purification.
    2 – A solid working knowledge and practice of how to function in Garou society. You can perform more advanced rites such as summoning spirits.
    3 – Beginning to dive into the more advanced and esoteric elements of being Garou, you can lead some of the more significant ceremonies during a moot for your own tribe. At this level of knowledge, you might be able to seriously punish lawbreakers to your culture, or reach an agreement with a spirit for it to empower a talisman or instrument.
    4 – Your knowledge reaches even other tribes of Garou, allowing you to handle moots at even multi-tribe septs. Rites at this level include tying your perceptions to your sept’s territory or conceal it from interlopers.
    5 – You’re certainly the apprentice to your sept’s Master of the Rite, and you might even be able to build a new caern, or empower a legendary hero with greater power from the Umbra in recognition for their nigh-impossible deeds.

    *Bonus: Your rating in Rituals is equal to the highest level of this Loresheet that you have purchased, and comes with one rite of that level or lower.​


    (Like a broken record, I'd also suggest ditching everything about rites in W5 in favor of a legacy edition. This just facilitates that with a pseudo-skill a la Weapons of the Gods kung fu styles and pseudo-skills.)

                          I viewed the special dice replacing part of a pool as less a unifying mechanic for all WoD games in an edition and more just V5 and H5 spinning their wheels.
