    Head Canons & Home Brew Part 2

    06-06-2023, 06:54 PM
    Bringing it back in a new form instead of necro-ing the original. (Original is here.)

    The place for anyone to share their own ideas, alterations, or changes to the Werewolf setting.


    As I mentioned elsewhere, lately I've pondered the idea of Hunters in Darkness and White Howlers as alternate names for the two Native American tribes. However, as part of a wider effort to remove human-culture derived names from all of the tribes, I'm still trying to figure out what would take the place of the Fianna and Get of Fenris (as well as the Croatan and Bunyip, as well as what would be the alternate origins of the Black Spiral Dancers).


    What is tolerance? It is the consequence of humanity. We are all formed of frailty and error; let us pardon reciprocally each other's folly. That is the first law of nature.
    Voltaire, "Tolerance" (1764)

      Something I was considering that might help with that:

      Have "Lodges" that work like the Silver Fang Houses as localized versions of much broader Tribes that have associated Totem spirits, rename the Silver Fang Lodges into something else.

      The Fianna, canon White Howlers, Get of Fenris, and Younger Brother are all Lodges of the same Tribe (White Howlers being the original shared name works fine). Older Brother, Nuwisha, Kitsune (I know not everyone will like those), and the Bunyip (though I house rule them to the singing dogs and dingoes, not thylacines) are Lodges of the same Tribe, with the Ghost Council name being a good one IMO. Middle Brother, Stargazers, and the Shadow Lords are Lodges of the same Tribe (I like the idea that the Shadow Lords actually started in Central Asia and followed the Turkic migrations west, and Hunters in the Darkness would work as an umbrella name for these.

          Black Furies
          Greater Patron Spirit: The Gorgon
          Theme: Rage for the vengeful.
          Tribute: Once per month, an Avenger must openly declare vengeance against someone, or alternatively, publically accept being the target of another’s vengeance.

          Bone Gnawers
          Greater Patron Spirit: Rat
          Theme: Rage in defense of the downtrodden.
          Tribute: Once per month, a Scrapper must destroy the assets and wealth of a designated enemy, or alternatively, liquidate her own wealth.

          Circle of Gaia
          Greater Patron Spirit: The Unicorn
          Theme: Rage against defilement of purity.
          Tribute: Once per month, a Paladin must destroy a Triatic spirit, or alternatively, use pain to atone for her own past misdeeds.

          The Wild Hunt (replaces Fianna)
          Greater Patron Spirit: Stag
          Theme: Rage until ecstasy and catharsis.
          Tribute: Once per month, a Horned must immerse in any intense activity where everyone else ends up losing reason, or alternatively, she is left unconscious.

          Get of Fenris
          Greater Patron Spirit: The Fenris-Wolf
          Theme: Rage against the false gods.
          Tribute: Once per month, a Vargr must destroy a manifestation of religious faith (including living devotees), or alternatively, something related to her human upbringing (if any).

          Note: The Get are the Lupusborn-dominant Tribe.

          Glass Walkers
          Greater Patron Spirit: Honeybee
          Theme: Rage until a breakthrough is found.
          Tribute: Once per month, a Hand must create something subjectively innovative, or alternatively, destroy one of her previous creations.

          Red Talons
          Greater Patron Spirit: The Gryphon
          Theme: Rage against oppression and suppression.
          Tribute: Once per month, a Marked must …… (undetermined yet)

          Note: The Talons are the Crinosborn-dominant Tribe.

          Shadow Lords
          Greater Patron Spirit: Grandfather Thunder
          Theme: Rage until another submits.
          Tribute: Once per month, a Condemner must seize victory without revealing herself until the outcome is sealed, or alternatively, do so while revealing every one of her steps to do such.

          Silent Striders
          Greater Patron Spirit: Owl
          Theme: Rage to make the past remembered.
          Tribute: Once per month, a Ghost must reenact the legacy of an ancestorial figure, or alternatively, spend one full night in the deeper Underworld (called the Tempest by necromancers).

          Silver Fangs
          Greater Patron Spirit: Falcon
          Theme: Rage until inspiration is stoked.
          Tribute: Once per month, a Lordling must seize victory while leading others as a superior, or alternatively, challenge leadership from a clear superior.

          Stargazers
          Greater Patron Spirit: The Qilin
          Theme: Rage to attain clarity of mind.
          Tribute: Once per month, a Reader must …… (undetermined yet)

          River’s Eye (replaces Uktena/Older Brother)
          Greater Patron Spirit: Serpent
          Theme: Rage in defiance of the hidden.
          Tribute: Once per month, a Whistler must expose an enemy’s secret, or alternatively, her own.

          Winter’s Maw (replaces Wendigo/Younger Brother)
          Greater Patron Spirit: Brother Winter
          Theme: Rage in defense of the self and selfhood.
          Tribute: Once per month, a Devourer must consume some part of someone or something that has slighted her, or alternatively, expose herself to extreme environments harsh enough to harm her.



          Black Spiral Dancers
          Greater Patron Spirit: Nightjar
          Theme: Rage to tear down everything.
          Tribute: Once per month, a Cursed must destroy a Changer or a Kin.

              A slightly alternate take on the Ratkin, as Gaia's Plague, meant to keep human populations in check. The initial Wars of Rage (in their case, against the Bastet as much as the Garou) wrecked them, sending them off the rails. The explosion of the human population after the end of the Impergium is believed to have driven them spiritually insane on some level.

              Auspices (based on the classic bodily humors, in fitting with the fact that they undergo a spiritual disease as part of the first change, and that they are more or less meant as a plague on the human world)
              Sanguine - Extremely extroverted and hyperactive. The most social of the Ratkin, often involved with kinfolk and those outside the nest. They frequently maintain connections between the breed around the world, and are those given the unwanted task of interacting with other changing breeds. Many of their gifts allow them to pass as normal (at least for a while), go where they aren't wanted, and convince others of absurd untruths.

              Choleric - Extroverted and short-tempered. The most violent of the Ratkin, acting as fighters and killers. They defend their nest-mates with an insane ferocity, using any means necessary to win and survive. Their gifts are primarily combat oriented, but as much involving hit and run tactics, escape and evasion, and ambush as actual physical damage.

              Melancholic - Extremely introverted and anxious. The most intellectual of the Ratkin, fixated on their plots and schemes. They help maintain the breed's history and lore, such as it is, and help develop plans to destroy their enemies. Their gifts are mainly focused on uncovering information, remaining undetected, and sabotage.

              Phlegmatic - Introverted and cold. The creepiest of the Ratkin, mystics who delve into the secrets of decay, disease, and derangement. The religious leaders of their kind, devoted to the culling of humanity and destruction of all who have wronged them. Their gifts involve interaction with spirits, the powers of entropy, and care of their nest mates.



              The Contagions are widespread social subgroups of the Ratkin. ("The Plague" refers to the spiritual disease which infects them during the first change, spoken of in religious reverence, and so isn't used to describe something as mundane as a subculture.) Having existed as long as human civilization, they've constantly evolved and changed over the millennia, with new ones spawning and old ones falling away as the whims of time dictate.

              Desert Rats - Paranoid survivalists who prefer to stay isolated from the rest of the world. Their nests often resemble militia compounds or are based out of abandoned Cold War bunkers or silos. The oldest surviving nests are tribes of bandits in North Africa and Asia.

              Sea Rats - Cutthroat pirates who have been sailing the oceans for as long as humans have had boats. Their strongholds are often hidden islands or abandoned ships. Gangs will swarm ships, often killing everyone on board and stealing everything they can carry off.

              Swamp Rats - Inbred, xenophobic and often violent dwellers in swamps and wetlands.

              Tunnel Rats - As much explorers as survivors, they've long dwelt among the caves, catacombs and modern infrastructure of the world. Many have taken to constructing and expanding their warrens, even into the umbra.

              Street Rats - The most common Ratkin in the modern urban world. Members often dwell among the homeless and other street people.

              Pack Rats - Obsessive collectors of anything under the sun. They horde and jealously guard their treasures, often competing to have the biggest or most unique collections. Anything goes in the acquisition of thing, including theft and murder.

              The Rat Race - A fairly modern phenomenon, paralleling the rise of office culture and bureaucracies. Members are usually among the lowest levels of office workers, as well as janitorial staff and other invisible cogs of the modern corporate world. They work to sabotage the companies they work for.

              Plague Rats - An almost cult like culture devoted to the exploration and spread of disease among the human populations. They work to spread - and sometimes develop - new diseases in an effort to ultimately return the world to an Impergium like state.

              Lab Rats - Mad scientists and survivors of human experiments. They develop bizarre weapons and tools while waging war against human science and other enemies.

              Mall Rats - Spawned during the late 70s and 80s, members once spent their time crawling all over human shopping malls and other places of commerce. However, since the collapse of malls in the United States, those who remain continue to haunt the locations, long after they've been abandoned by man.

              Jungle Rats - Primarily rat-born, they prefer the savage jungles and forests far from humanity, carving out their own strange kingdoms.

              Rats in the Temple - Unique among the Ratkin of India, they are centered around the Karni Mata temple in Rajasthan. They take a slightly less antagonistic attitude toward humanity and other changing breeds, and are more focused on battling the forces of the Wyrm. Other Ratkin find them odd, if not insane.

                          Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
                          However, as part of a wider effort to remove human-culture derived names from all of the tribes, I'm still trying to figure out what would take the place of the Fianna and Get of Fenris (as well as the Croatan and Bunyip, as well as what would be the alternate origins of the Black Spiral Dancers).
                          I've long been in favor of expanding the tribes beyond human culture. Tribal culture should be based around cultic devotion to their totem. I also believe all the tribal names are essentially poetic expressions, and there have been many different versions over the years. Their "true" name as such is probably something like "Children of Stag", "Children of Unicorn", "Children of Rat" and so on. The Garou tribes predate all human ethnic groups that exist today - when the ancient Get of Fenris were in Scandinavia around 8000 BC, they certainly weren't referring to themselves through the lens of Norse culture - the Germanic speaking peoples didn't even exist then, nor did their ancestors live there. And I've long thought that it was extremely lazy to use the totem name as the tribal name for the Uktena and Wendigo. Every other tribe gets some fancy name, but they don't? So I'm good with Ghost Council being used for the Uktena (or if you want to call the totem the Horned Serpent or Underwater Panther). It's about time they got something more poetic.

                          The "true names" of any of these spirits are going to be secret anyway, so whatever name is used is only going to be used by others to identify it. It's not even as if "Stag" is going to be universal because that is still in English. And not even if we hire a linguist to invent the Garou Tongue will that be the "true" name of the spirit.

                          However, I smell the strong whiff of some kind of Pol Pot inspired "Year Zero" brewing around the changes in W5 that seems to seek eradicating everything that does not fit the new philosophy (which will inevitably later be condemned by some future generation, coming sooner than the current version of the Red Guards think). So I'm not in favor of "removing" human-culture derived names. It is almost inevitable that homids will draw on their own human heritage when discussing their Garou culture as well. It's fine for copyright purposes or sourcebook clarity to use one dominant name for each tribe. But I would prefer to keep the original names as one of many names being used currently and historically in the setting. Let everyone feel they can use whatever name they want, and discourage the tendency for some to claim using another name is somehow "wrong".

                          = =

                          I think other names for the Fianna are very easy. Revised Fianna tribebook already gave us lots of versions - Hounds of the Horned One, Skysingers, and Night Claws. Although of these, I think only Hounds of the Horned Ones is relevant. But the term Fianna simply means a warrior band. So Soldiers of Stag work just fine if you want something alliterative and suggestive of the original name.

                          Fenris is a bit different, because if you want to universalize the name Fenris, you need to first determine what exactly is the Fenris spirit to the Garou? It is simply a variation of the Big Bag Wolf - the most dangerous wolf that all others fear - perhaps the greatest Jaggling of Father Wolf that grew so powerful he became an Incarna of its own? Is it as the Wolf of the Apocalypse who disappeared (imprisoned, sleeping, bound - voluntarily or involuntarily, etc.) for now but whose appearance will signal the start of the Apocalypse? And early legendary Garou who became so mighty and famous he became an Incarna and assembled a tribe of his own (Hakkaken likewise became an Incarna so it is possible)? Some kind of Undeworld Wolf/Dog that guards the entrance of hell? A combination of multiple of these? Once you figure out what you think Fenris should be, it's much easier to give him an alternate name that could be used, and then different versions of Get of that Spirit would be good.

                              Some tribal head-canons and thoughts I’ve had… (Part 1)

                              Black Furies
                              •I think the idea of replacing the chief spirit patron/totem with Gorgon is interesting but I can also see the argument for Pegasus as being part of that
                              •I think that the ‘Furies’ element of the Black Furies is useful to embrace on a few levels:
                              -1. it allows the tribe the imo interesting thematic tendency of being the avengers of primordial laws and taboos (Kinslaying, Matricide, [improper] Cannibalism, Defilement of sacred spaces, etc.); while the focus of this has become the Patriarch as the usurper of the Matriarch’s former role, it also applies to other taboos
                              —1a. I think that the taboo focus creates some interesting overlap with Older Brother but I’d say the difference is that OB guards/maintains taboos while the Black Furies seek out and punish violators
                              -2. I think that the image of the ‘Furies’ allows a connection to be made with Cthonic elements (which ‘Black’ also helps with. I think it allows the tribe to establish connections with cthonic powers (Night, Plagues, Life Death Cycles [The Demeter-Hades-Persephone Drama], etc.
                              •I envision the Black Furies as having three archetypal scenes associated with them:
                              -1. “Barbarian steppes”; the Furies are associated with Scythians, Amazons, and other wild-outsider peoples who lived on the periphery of the Hellenic world and their civilized compatriots (Egypt, Mesopotamia, etc.)
                              -2. Sacred places; the Furies also guard and conceal places of power, particularly those closely aligned with primordial life forces (primeval shrines of the Earth Mother and the like). The Furies guard islands hidden in the mist, remote glades, and the hearts of deep jungles
                              -3. Cthonic darkness; the Furies also gain power from the caverns and cave mouths where the cthonic forces can be contacted and which have connection to the more dark/cthonic side of the Mother principle
                              •The Black Furies are on the periphery of ‘civilized’ areas; they would go into the cities of the Hellenic world with mystical disguises but represented the prior order to the forces of social power the early polities harnessed to distinguish themselves from the old mother-cults and primordial societies

                              Bone Gnawers
                              •The Bone Gnawers do not have any one particular origin but are originally composed of Garou and their kinfolk who were outcast from their societies of origin for a variety of reasons and causes (being Garou-born, being cowards, being criminals, being taboo-breakers, being disabled, being too old, etc.); the earliest Bone Gnawers scraped to survive and would eventually come under the auspices of the Scavenger Kings (Rat, Jackal, Crow, Vulture, Coyote, etc.)
                              •The auspices of the Scavenger Kings is why the Bone Gnawers have lost their ancestor spirits and pure breed; they have forsaken their old taboos and been broken off from the old communities of spirit they came from and have become united by the pragmatic egalitarian-survivor principle of the scavengers
                              •The Bone Gnawers have developed a historical connection with outsiders and outcasts but with Rat’s focus in particular being on survival and the importance of survival - one doesn’t necessarily need to fight direct battles as long as they fight to survive
                              •The Bone Gnawers are historically the Garou who would remain somewhere after taint had overwhelmed it - this is for instance the case for why Bone Gnawers took over traditionally Silent Strider septs after their cataclysmic confrontation with the Cult of Seth; this is also why modern Bone Gnawers are so often found in cities

                              Children of Gaia
                              •Black Fox has a good thread about Unicorn that I’ve taken inspiration from for how I handle the Children of Gaia
                              •The Children of Gaia are a leadership tribe like the Silver Fangs or Shadow Lords; the Unicorns believe in the necessity of a restoration of a pure and undivided Garou and that the whole of the Nation must be reunited under a single great tribe (whether the appropriate totem for this tribe is Mother Life [Gaia], Wolf, or Unicorn is a matter of debate)
                              •The Children of Gaia will accept any members who are willing to undertake pledges to their principles and cleansing, which is why they’ve maintained their Ancestors and Pure Breed while other tribes who have broadly opened their ranks have not
                              •The Children of Gaia has a number of camps dedicated to uniting various groups (Humanity & Garou, Human Groups, Humans & Wolves, Garou Groups, Humanity & Spirits, etc.); the ideal of the tribe is the eventual restoration of all the world into the harmonious Circle of Mother (Gaia) which they believe was the original and primordial state

                                      Some more tribal head canons and thoughts (Part 2)…

                                      •In general: I think that what many tribes do is that they are particularly prominent examples of what all or most Garou do; for example the Fianna are not the only Garou that have sexual relations/unions with spirits but they are particularly notable for it

                                      Fianna (Horned Ones)
                                      •Compare/Contrast the Fianna with the Fenrir below
                                      •The Fianna are the tribe which most highlights communal ties with their kinfolk; Fianna are deeply invested with familial links (both by blood and ritual custom), hospitality, communal honor, etc; more than any other tribe Stag emphasizes the importance of family (due to Stag’s position as a Patriarch and icon fertility/virility/pride) and so Fianna are closely involved
                                      •Stag as a totem for the Fianna represents Virility, Pride, Fertility, Family, Passion, Experience, Fecundity, Generosity, Natural, etc.
                                      •Stag is associated with nature and the wilderness like most Garou totems are; but in particular Stag is associated with the virility, fecundity, and heaving excess of life which gives the Fianna their reputation for excessive love of life, insatiable sexual appetites, teeming hordes of children, clannish pride for their relations, proud boasting and rivalry (Stags bellowing and clashing antlers), etc.
                                      •The pride of the Fianna (Stag’s Antlers) and their love of life also plays a role in why this tribe in particular has historically been associated with the composition of legends, songs, and master-crafts
                                      •Fianna also embody and highlight another Garou tendency, namely to have relations with spiritual beings; for the Fianna this is highlighted by their regular, ritual, and passionate communions with earthy wild spirits and their Fae associates (and with Stag’s pride in particular this also means that they must boastfully represent their Fae ancestry)
                                      •The Fianna are a tribe where ritual customs such as sleeping with ritual horses or going on quests to enter mystical sexual relations to insure fertility and bounty for the people are represented

                                      Fenrir
                                      •Compare/contrast with the Fianna
                                      •I view the Fenrir as embodying the archetypal form of the ‘Koryos’ more than any other tribe; a band of warriors who have left the customs of regular and fruitful society, leaving behind the life camps/peace camps of their people, to join together in semi-nomadic war camps
                                      •The Fenrir kinfolk are left to largely regulate themselves with semi-regular check ins or old wolves who are established enough to settle down outside the war-bands but not old enough or weak enough to be voluntarily (or involuntarily) euthanized
                                      •The Fenrir are dedicated to the principles of War-Wolf and Death-Wolf; warriors who consecrate themselves in a willingness to kill and an acceptance of their own death in order to be fearless in battle and to terrorize their enemies and foes
                                      •While the Fenrir are known for their fearless direct battles they have no scruples launching midnight raids on their enemies with silent claws or with whirling noise makers; regardless the Fenrir are the tribe which most embody the archetype of the psychedelic chewing killers who desensitize themselves from fear-of-death through dissociating from self and the glorification of valor (sometimes this valor is what one imagines, the glory of direct battle, but it can also include midnight terror raids and silent killing)
                                      •The Fenrir are led by the most capable, fearless, and brutal of their elders who also take up the role of ritual-doctors for the lower warriors who are brought into the war band
                                      •The Fenrir are far less associated with the Scandinavian aesir gods and far more with the wilderness-mystic gods of the Vanir, wilderness spirits, and giants embodying natural chaos
                                      -(TBH… under this view I think certain manifestations of Odin actually fit rather well amongst the Fenrir; less the God’s manifestations as a law-keeper and enemy of primordial chaos but more in his roles as a one-eyed cthonic death-warrior god of the wild hunt)

                                      Glass Walkers
                                      •The tribe of Cockroach has certain similarities to Bone Gnawers (who live amongst the developed wreckages - in Garou terms - of human civilization) and the Children of Gaia (who also want to communicate and develop human society towards Gaian ends) while the Glass Walkers are increasingly concerned with the powerful systems developed by Humanity-Weaver and put to use
                                      •The Glass Walkers are the closest tribe to the study of the Weaver and of humanity in relation and how they and the systems they generate might be useful for the Garou Nation and Mother Gaia (any Garou who uses language or a klaive is participating in the Weaver’s world whether they like it or not, the Glass Walkers embrace that and carry it on to its logical extremes)
                                      •Glass Walkers are the tribe most at risk of falling into the taint of the Weaver; which namely would occur of servicing the systems of the Weaver and developing their power for the sake of that power rather then constraining and putting it to the benefit of the Mother and being able to better sabotage the Weaver’s machinery from within
                                      •The Cockroach is a survivor, an ingenious survivor, which sheds any detritus and flourish for the purpose of better survival; the Glass Walkers have embraced the Weaver and Humanity and have shed their Ancestors and Pure Breed in order to better maneuver the cold engines of the Weaver

                                          I try to match the Black Furies to the Greek legends of the Amazons as well. I think it was a mistake for the game to match the Black Furies to the Greeks. The writers clearly wanted to link them to the Amazons, but forgot the Amazons were actually barbarians to the Greeks, not part of the Greeks themselves. I don't mind some connections to the Greeks, because the legends show multiple cases of intermarriage between Greek men and the Amazons, but the majority of the Black Fury kinfolk should match up to where the Greeks located them - primarily (but not exclusively) with the Eurasian steppe (from Ukraine to Central Asia), areas around the Black Sea in the Caucasus and Anatolia, and "Libya" (North Africa).

                                          I am fine with the Black Fury totem and even using the name "Pegasus" for it. But if I ever wanted to emphasize the difference between the spirit and Greek myth, I'd use the term "white winged horse" instead.

                                          The color white, like silver, is a lunar color. It connects the spirit to the moon, just like how the Furies say "Artemis" (another Greek term, but for our purposes is just an Incarna of the Celestine Luna that is capable of interacting with the Garou and is known by lots of different names) sent Pegasus. This is a lunar spirit.

                                          It being winged demonstrates the spirit is a messenger from the celestial heavens - an important intermediary between Luna and the material world. That means the spirit is a divine messenger, bringing the wisdom of the moon to the Garou. And the moon has long been a symbol of the feminine because of the correspondence of the moon's cycle to menstruation. It being winged is extremely important to its function. This all fits with the standard Black Fury narrative as well.

                                          Lastly, it is a horse spirit. The Furies are said to be one of the oldest tribes - perhaps the second oldest tribe. We see the horse as a domesticated animal now, but back then it was not. In fact, the horse would not be domesticated until quite late compared to other animals, long after the Impergium ended. So at the time, an equine spirit would be seen as something of the Wyld. An untamed and undomesticated animal. The figure of a "white horse" shows up in a lot of mythologies, usually representing a being of exceptional powers, divinity, or excellence. So it would be an appropriate symbolic or real carrier of the power of the sacred feminine from Luna to earth. That power isn't just a representation of the feminine - it is a power that protects and avenges. Wild horses can be incredibly dangerous so it can be an appropriate totem for a tribe of Furies/avengers.

                                          (Unicorn bears many of these attributes as well, but in this case the color white does not represent a connection to Luna, but as a symbol for purity.)

                                          As long as we remember "Pegasus" is just an obvious name for the spirit given it's description, and go back to what mythic associations its description would have, we can accept the spirit as something that long predates the Greeks. On the other hand, switching Gorgon for Pegasus doesn't seem to me that it solves any "problems" with the spirit and merely substitutes one cultural specific being for another.

                                              Some more tribal head canons and thoughts… (Part 3)

                                              Red Talons
                                              •Black Fox has a thread about Gryphon as a totem which I think has some great Red Talon ideas; in particular their association with the primeval wilderness, acting as guardians, and relations to leadership

                                              •Red Talons are a relatively reclusive tribe, mostly found in the last great wild places where great wild packs are able to form

                                              •The Shadow Lords imo benefit from being as global as the Silver Fangs who they ‘shadow’ (heh) but like the Silver Fangs they can also have a tribal center in the deep caucasus mountains and/or Siberia
                                              •I view the Shadow Lords as taking on a role of diametric opposition with the Black Furies for reasons that should become more clear below
                                              •Grandfather Thunder, the Shadow Lord spirit patron (totem) is in my opinion a prime fit for being associated with primordial storm-father/storm-warrior archetypes (Zeus, Jupiter, Thor*, Indra, Yahweh, etc.)
                                              •Grandfather Thunder in the distant past probably originated in a pair along the lines of Father Sky-Mother Earth with the pre-eminence placed upon Mother Earth (a form of veneration the Black Furies in particular will fiercely uphold) and over time, in certain places, Father Sky and related manifestations began to take on pre-eminence as Fertilizer & Life-Giver over Mother Earth as part of a father-cult overtaking a mother-cult
                                              •In the WoD we also know that at some point along the process one of the seeds of the Weaver would adopt the form of the Patriarch, a spiritual representation of the father-cult which would emerge from the Storm-Fathers and would form the basis for a Patriarchal Law-Giver Order; an order which the Shadow Lords would recognize the power within and adopt for themselves
                                              •The Shadow Lords would have a role defined by their proximity to power and their role as the stewards of authority and power in spiritual terms (whereas the Silver Fangs would be the ritual-vessels of power); for the Shadow Lords this would mean strictly maintaining the power of the highest authority, crushing dissent, and a willingness to use whatever tactics necessary
                                              •The Shadow Lords and the Black Furies both upheld some of the darkest ritual duties of the father-cult and mother-cults respectively, albeit with different approaches and philosophies. Both of these tribes held the silent knives and night killers who would uphold the respective taboos of one spirit-cult or the other
                                              •The Shadow Lords adopt the role of ‘villain’ whenever it is necessary for the power of the Silver Fangs and/or the Nation
                                              •The Shadow Lords closely follow the Silver Fangs and are also consumed by their own dedication to power and authority. The Shadow Lords traditionally are the star council which watches the Silver Fangs for any signs of weakness, impurity, impotence, cowardice, or incompetence - should the signs of this be discovered the preparations are made for the Killing of the King rituals; by which the spirit of the Silver Fang is cut off before it can decay and so is passed along to the successor
                                              •A core divide of the Shadow Lords in the modern era as the Silver Fang decline and decay becomes more pronounced is over their role. Some Shadow Lords believe that they must remain to the old role of supporting the Silver Fangs lest they lose themselves to power-madness as the Fangs have. Other believe, either out of genuine interest or self-interest, that the Silver Fangs must be usurped and the legitimacy of the Nation be removed from the Silver Fangs and placed into the body of the Nation itself (which may or may not be headed by a vanguard council of Shadow Lord elders)

                                              •The Silent Striders are the tribe who maintains the ancient, secret, and eldritch taboos, bindings, and mysteries of the Land of Magick prior to their cataclysmic confrontation with the Cult of Set in the past sands of time
                                              •The Silent Striders, under Owl, are now dedicated to being the silent guardians of dead mysteries and secrets (as well as the mysteries and secrets of the dead); activities such as guarding ancient death scrolls which could unleash elder demons of the Settian cult or tablets which contain fragments of the earliest dictates of the magicians or the last words of the pre-historic god-kings
                                              •The Silent Striders now are dedicated both to the keeping of their ancient mysteries but also in general the acquisition of other knowledges which they can spread amongst their fellow Garou in exchange for brief respites at their hearths and camps
                                              •The Silent Striders are feared, reviled, and pitied by the other tribes; their knowledge and gifts are recognized but they are fundamentally a tribe of Post-Apocalypse and considered as such
                                              •The Silent Striders have a similar niche to Older Brother or the Stargazers; but is made distinct by a greater interest in the dead and of mysteries which are not simply old or dark (like Older Brother) but are truly Dead and of Epochs which no longer exist (and according to mortal historians may never have existed)

                                                  My slightly alternate take on the Gurahl, Gaia's Succor.

                                                  The auspices.

                                                  Spring: Succor as the Healer. They are called to encourage and nurture new growth, as well as recovery from past trauma. This includes mental and spiritual injury as well as physical, and a special emphasis on children. They also work to heal natural sites, communities, and parts of the Umbra.

                                                  Summer: Succor as the Guardian. They are called to defend and protect their charges, and to help them learn to defend themselves. While their focus is often sacred sites, natural areas, and communities, it will also at times involve specific individuals, powerful fetishes, and lost lore.

                                                  Autumn: Succor as the Teacher. They are called to instruct and guide those in need, helping them understand and accept their place in Gaia's greater world. Often wise beyond their years, and instinctively able to understand others, many work to encourage their charges and help them grow into their true selves.

                                                  Winter: Succor as the Priest. They are called to tend to the greatest and gravest of spiritual matters, including guiding the sick and dying to peace and helping the spirits of the dead pass over. They council against grief and loss, and help make way for the new growth that is to come.

                                                  And the Lodges, the social groupings which take the place of tribes, and are not really bound by geographic location.

                                                  The Lodge of the Green Earth, nurturers and protectors of growing things, including ecosystems and children.
                                                  The Lodge of the Healing Heart, ambassadors and peacemakers devoted to soothing conflicts and building bridges between people and communities.
                                                  The Lodge of the Star Mother, devoted to the arts of astrology and prophecy, providing guidance and encouragement to those in need.
                                                  The Lodge of the Whispering Winds, mystics and umbra explorers who spend much of their time in the Umbra and healing spirits, including the restless dead of the Dark Umbra.
                                                  The Lodge of the Sleeping Kings, historians and protectors of sacred places, including the lairs of ancient Gurahl elders who have been slumbering for centuries and millennia.
                                                  The Lodge of the Cave Bear, an all-ursine sect fiercely devoted to protecting nature and their kinfolk from humans and other forces. (They are the most likely to have secret ties with the Red Talons.)

                                                  In addition, there are a few regional groups that are more culturally oriented rather than involving specific mechanics or foci.

                                                  The Jambavana are the Gurahl of the Indian subcontinent. Among the local changing breeds, they are usually seen as being akin to the Brahmin caste (alongside the Makara and the local Stargazers and Children of Gaia), and are skilled demon fighters.
                                                  The Okami are the Gurahl of Korea, Japan, Manchuria, and parts of China. They are often part of the Beast Courts of the Emerald Mother. (Not extinct.)
                                                  The Artio are - or were - the Gurahl of the European subcontinent, but were mostly destroyed by the Garou during the various Wars of Rage. Only a handful still exist, doing everything they can to stay hidden. Many are part of the Lodge of Sleeping Kings.
                                                  The Otso are the Gurahl of Fennoscandia and northwest Russia. While decimated similarly to the Artio, they have slowly recovered to a better degree, mostly owing to pure stubborn determination.​
                                                  The Goah are the native Gurahl of the Eastern Woodlands and Great Lakes. In the days of the Pure Lands, they were the ones with the closest ties to the Three Brothers and other breeds. (There are different groups in the Southwest, Pacific Northwest, and other regions.)
                                                  The Nanook are the native Gurahl of North America's Arctic regions, mainly of polar bear stock. Powerful hunters, they are also strict enforcers of spiritual taboos.
                                                  ​The Nandi are rumored to be Gurahl native to East Africa, but no one has actually seen one.
