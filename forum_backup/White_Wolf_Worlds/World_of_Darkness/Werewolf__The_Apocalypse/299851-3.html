        I would like to point out vis-a-vis the "Evil" thing. Werewolf is meant to be a game of Savage Horror, you ARE playing a Monster, a terrible monster that can reduce a bar full of people into a web sticky mess because someone spilled your pint. You scare people just by walking down the street and they don't even know why. You'll butcher people who are in the wrong place at the wrong time, because while humanity matter they don't matter "That Much". That's the truth of it for all Garou, even the ones who care, because the War and the Apocalypse are far far more important. Rage is far more powerful than simple good intentions.

        Sure, you're a Hero, but only to the Garou and the cause they fight for. Some poor guy gets a bane in him through a bad batch of King Beer and it starts causing other banes to spawn. You can't cleanse him, you can't save him, you have to kill him it's the only way to make the bane vulnerable. The Litany is upheld, a threat to Gaia is removed, your deeds spread through the Nation, and you just killed A Guy. The RedTalons are just seeing a deeper corruption and the terrifying thing is, they are not really wrong. The rest of the Nation isn't protecting humanity, they're just trying to circumvent the threat that Mankind can pose to the efforts of the War and directly to the Tribes. The Talons certainly want to go too far and are almost vindictive in their desires, but they remind the other Garou that really and truly humanity do need to be bled.

                Not so much, Lian. The entire reason the World of Darkness did so well to begin with was because you got to play a Monster. It was at it's time unique in this and most of the things around then didn't really have the same moral granularity (they could but it wasn't a solid default) with things like Cyberpunk and Shadowrun coming close but even with them it was expressing how your character wasn't a monster of some sort. It's not an In Universe view at all, it's a core game premise for a game of Savage Horror. That the Talons require a "buy in" to be played is a solid reflection on that. A great many of the Tribes require a Buy In to be effective in their flavour and almost every character requires it too, this is why I like to play with the optional Tribal Flaws at times. I'm a massive SilverFang fan and every last one of them that I have played has been Mad to one level or another and in every case it's been a very carefully chosen and represented madness, by rights people shouldn't enjoy playing any mad character because that is alien to them but they seem popular enough. Handling Rage right out the gate by the same logic should make Werewolves unpopular to play, remember that anything that might make a normal person angry or annoyed or shout or swear might make a Garou frenzy and rip things into pieces.

                There are plenty of people out there who enjoy all these aspects of the game and there are plenty who enjoy the RedTalons? Why? Because it presents a challenge, it presents a need to do something else and really think about character actions and how they differ from your own. It's a reason to understand things that would not otherwise enter your mind and to express that in a storytelling medium.

                                                            Thing is, Heavy Arms, exactly the same applies to all the Tribes. Why is there a tribe of Kings? Of Shady power hungry manipulators? Of street born guttersnipes? Of weaver linked city wolves? Of angry natives? Of mystical natives? Of Song and mythology? Or Warriors? Of female warriors? Of medative martial artists? I can do any of those with any other tribe. The entire point of the Splats in WoD is to represent parts of the Mythos from which the game line is inspired and buildt around. Each Camarilla clan represents an aspect of the Camarilla concept of "Vampire" and each Werewolf Tribe gives an identity to an aspect of the Garou.
                                                            The Silverfangs the Alpha concept, the leading hero, the failing fight with a glimmer of hope.
                                                            The Shadowlords Do what Must be Done and exert strength and power and beware the cost.
                                                            The Fianna are the Songs and Tales that build Legends and a connection to Otherworlds
                                                            The Black Furies are the nurturing she wolf and protection of the pack with ancient wisdom and fury
                                                            The Uktena are the dark secrets of the world and the unseen sacrifice made to keep the Wyrm at bay
                                                            The Wendigo are the tribal history of Man and the distrust of any Not Garou
                                                            The Glasswalkers are the Wolf in Sheeps clothing
                                                            The BoneGnawers are the Omega and the underdog fighting against bad odds
                                                            The Stargazers are the disconnect of knowing truths the rest of the world will never see
                                                            The Red Talons are the Wild and Primal Rage of the Werewolf, unleashed and untamed
                                                            The Children of Gaia are the balancing act within every Garou
                                                            The Get of Fenris are the relentless warriors ready to face any foe.
                                                            The Croatan are the ultimate sacrifice, the line in the sand
                                                            The WhiteHowlers the empty sacrifice born of being the Lone Wolf or Hubris
                                                            The Black Spiral Dancers the failure and surrender and the traitors.
                                                            The Bunyip are the way that a Werewolf is different from those they may share much with (and the results of their own prejudices ).
                                                            The Siberakh are a hope based in prophecies but also the ultimate isolation of the Garou.
