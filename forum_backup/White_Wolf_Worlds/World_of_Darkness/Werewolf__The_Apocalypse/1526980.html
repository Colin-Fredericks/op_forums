    [W5] Fixing the caern system

    12-17-2023, 08:57 AM
    This thread is a spinoff from my previous thread about house ruling W5. (By the way, in case anyone is interested, you can find the latest version of the house rules [PDF, Dropbox link] here.)

    In this thread, I would like to build on Knightingale's cool idea of adapting V5's domain and coterie system to a caern and pack system. I've re-familiarized myself with V5's rules and had a look at my old 1st-W20 books (mainly the rulebooks, Caerns: Places of Power, and Guardians of the Caerns).

    To recap V5's domain and coteries system:
    • Each member of the coterie adds one (or, depending on ST decision, more dots) to the shared coterie pool. In addition, players can decide to put dots from their own advantages pool into the coterie pool. Dots in the coterie pool can be spent on domain or on other advantages/backgrounds/merits that can be used by the whole coterie.
    • Domains are defined by three stats: Chasse (size of the domain), which determines how difficult hunting is (the bigger, the easier); Lien (coterie's integration into the domain), which adds dice to social rolls with people living in the domain; Portillon (security against intrusion or disruption), which reduces dice from enemies' dice pools to intrude or gather intelligence. Chasse is limited to five dots, I couldn't find limits for Lien and Portillon.
    • Coteries are basically group templates that have certain prerequisites (e.g., certain amount of Chasse, Lien, or Portillon dots; certain advantages/backgrounds) and allow the sharing of advantages/backgrounds/merits. In the Players Guide there are specific coterie merits.
    • The Players Guide also expands the domain system by adding merits for Chasse, Lien, and Portillon. These count towards coterie requirements and are basically a bit more flavorful variation of the basic system.
    That's it, basically. To my knowledge, there isn't a more abstract system to expand the domain, beyond buying more advantage dots, putting them into the coterie pool, and spending them on the domain (either dots in the three stats or merits), coterie assets, or coterie merits.

    Now, the question is, how could we apply this to W5?

    I think it's safe to assume that instead of Chasse, we can use the caern's power, which influences the difficulty to step sideways. A variation of Lien could add dice to social interactions with spirits, and Portillon could be used as is. So far, so good...

    However, in V5 there is one coterie per domain, so the question is how this should be handled in W5. After all, a septs usually consists of several packs. So, should the pack pools all be pooled together for spending on the caern and the shared advantages/backgrounds/merits? EDIT: Personally, I'm not sure. Adding all the pack pools of several packs together might make it too easy to build very strong caerns. I feel there has to be a point of diminishing returns if you have too many packs in one caern.

    EDIT: Another issue is whether to bring back moon bridges. I'm tending towards yes, but maybe as an additional set of merits, not as something a caern automatically has.

              I will need to find a better moment later to write down all of it but some ideas:
              • Pack-Type is prioritized as it is about what the group does
              • Some Pack-Types are associated with caerns but not all
              • Caerns have their own mini-char-sheet
              • The bigger structure horizontally is Sept and non-Sept/outsider: There's a difference between helping a Sept that protects a caern and dealing with a dormant caern.
              • The inspiration for what Pack-Types do is to emphasize what PCs do. For example, the core-scenario from the W5-corebook of a Pack awakening a caern on their own shouldn't be about raising the caern-level of the thing. Instead that Pack-Type should be about gathering contacts and allies because you build the Sept first and then turn to get the spirit of the caern to grow in power. The idea is that a caern at level 1 is an egg and so the priority is protection.
              • Meanwhile, for Pack-Types serving Septs the Caern starts at five dots (with caerns having a 10 dot rating now) and dealing with the caern's spirit is different. Depending on your Pack-Type serving the caern-spirit might be what you're doing. Something like that.
              • In regards to Pack-Patron-Spirits, rather than making it about buying dots... the whole thing is put on a separate track. Your Pack-Patron-Spirit wants you to act a certain way (within the bounds of what your Pack-Type is) and you accrue specific Pack-Patron-XP that way which you then spend on Pack-Patron specifc benefits. And what I'm thinking of is something along the lines of a Blades in the Dark Crew sheet.

                          Hm, that's a very good point, and it hits the heart of W5's problem. However, I wouldn't focus too much on this stuff... it's a waste of time to be honest!

                          The main reason we have this problem in the first place is because V5 wanted to be a continuation of the old metaplot. The problem is, the new books never seemed clear on what their respective splat's eschatology is actually supposed to mean: the literal end of the world, the end of an age, or the end of a people (Kindred, Garou)?

                          For me, believing in the Apocalypse being inevitable, seems like a direct path towards harano or hauglosk (your/our redefinition at least), because it means throwing down the towel based on anxiety about the future, about an idea what the future might hold, instead of living in the here and now. For me, the lesson should be not to become paralyzed or sidetracked by fear (i.e., the assumption of certain doom), but to act now, even if it's only small steps, because in the end the small steps add up, and the future is decided by the gradual process.

                          In that sense, I feel my house-ruled W5 should be focused on what a pack can do here and now, within its own sphere of influence, to do its part in changing the current course. And, sure, if the old approaches weren't effective enough for changing the trend, then it raises the question what new approaches might work better. And for that, we have to define the old approach and possible new approaches first. Since it's a reboot, I feel we are pretty free to decide on the nature of the old approaches and why they didn't work.

                          EDIT: But the thing is, all this was already in the legacy editions. We already had the various different approaches to tackling the problem. So, W5's spiel about how the young now have to do everything differently seems disingenuous (unless you retcon the old guard being very single-minded). Functionally, to me, it means we don't have to pay attention to the whole Apocalypse business, because it doesn't change what packs do in comparison to older editions. PCs will do the same shit, we've been playing for the past 30 years, because nothing really changed effectively.

                                  The caern’s bawn houses a group of Gallain who have organized a private militia to protect the area. Each dot of Gallain Watchmen supplies four Average guards and one Gifted boss (see Supporting Character Templates, p. 102).

                                  Warding Glyphs (• to •••••)
                                  Prerequisites: Power •• or more; Wards •• or more
                                  Along its perimeter, the caern possesses Warding Glyphs barring other supernatural forces. These glyphs cannot be actively deactivated, but the caern’s Rite master may allow individuals to pass. Discuss the limits of the Warding Glyphs with the Storyteller. Choose one type of supernatural creature: fera, vampires, mages, ghosts, fair folk, etc. Each dot of this Merit adds one to the difficulty for this type of supernatural creature to enter or magically scry the premises. This Wards Merits must be bought and increased separately for each type of supernatural creature.

                                  I'm thinking about the Pack Types now. I've come with a few rough ideas:
                                  • Caern Guardians (protecting a caern from attack and outside influence)
                                  • Callers of the Wyld (serving the caern's patron spirit and seeing to its needs)
                                  • Community Builders (influencing human communities, raising awareness)
                                  • Hunting Party (hunting down a fugitive enemy)
                                  • Infiltrators (infiltrating enemy territory to gather intelligence and possibly assassinate)
                                  • Investigators (investigate the Wyrm's influence and identify targets for attack)
                                  • Monkey Wrenchers / Saboteurs​ (sabotage enemy infrastructure or operations)
                                  • Sept Envoys​ (improve diplomatic relations with other septs and caerns)
                                  • Spirit Questers (uncover hidden knowledge or find allies in the spirit world)
                                  • Strike Force (attack specific targets, usually based on the information from other pack types or the Wyrm Foe)
                                  • Truthcatchers (investigate and settle disputes among the members of the sept)
                                  • Urrah Troubleshooter (keeping trouble away from the Garou from humans and keeping up the Veil)
                                  ​For non-sept pack types:
                                  • Nomads (traveling packs without association to a sept, trying to survive)
                                  • Fugitives (hunted, justly or unjustly, by their former allies)

                                      A couple of ideas:

                                      For non-Sept-Packs:
                                      • Don't know what a good name would be but it's the main-scenario of the book: You have a Pack that finds a dormant or lost caern and reawakens it. But instead of the focus being on the caern itself, the focus is on gathering allies and contacts both in the physical realm and in the Umbra. Instead of just buffing the caern, the group-dynamic is more about populating a new Sept. So, the caern-spirit is weak compared to the kind of spirit "Callers of the Wyld" deal with and instead things like the Amenity "Kin-Beacon" and dots in Allies and Contacts are emphasized.
                                      • Umbral hunters: The idea is that this is the Pack-Type for when you want to go full-fantasy and have PCs spend more time in the Umbra than in physical space. Another idea I had is if that Pack-type has a close relationship with Starving Remnants as in them having Rites that keep these Packs from becoming them and maybe there are sacred hunts where the Pack competes with Starving Remnants and allows them to either "move on", turn into a friendly Spirit (they find their own niche of existence within the Umbra) or if the change happened only recently, they can be returned to their physical state.
                                      • A wandering Pack of storytellers and lorekeepers (with a lot of Silent Strider members naturally) that go from Sept to Sept. (Hmm, it's probably more like a more Garou-ish interpretation of the Nomad-template, I guess)
                                      Unrelated other ideas:
                                      • What do you think of the idea of turning the Pack-Patron-Spirit into a Loresheet? I would have to take a look at all the Loresheets whether there's a way to streamline the process of creating one, so people can have their custom Pack-Patron-Spirits. But for established Patron-Spirits from previous editions, it should be easy to come up with five dots of benefits that fit the writing.
                                      • I also think one system that W5 needs is a way to deal with investigating Spirits. Wyrm-sense was removed so you can't tell the difference between whether a disease-spirit is of the Wyld or the Wyrm. The book has that part but the part where now Garou are supposed to investigate Spirits first to figure out what the deal is needs a system. I think the Project-system from the V5 corebook and the V5 Players Guide could be repurposed for this. Just as a way of allowing a group to "zoom out" while dealing with a Spirit. That allows STs to make Spirits a more general phenomenon and not having every Spirit be there for important narrative roles like antagonist, ally or whatever. You can just have spiritual activity and if a player cares about it, you have a quick system to abstractly resolve the process of engaging with the Spirit and if nobody cares... well, that's that. But that way an ST can promote engaging with the Umbra and Spirits more.

                                                  Okay, I'm currently writing the pack types and I need your help. I'm currently surrounded by my whole extended family and currently have little capacity for the rules bits.

                                                  It would be a great help if you could suggest appropriate Prerequisites (including the caern's Power, Harmony and Wards rating), Possible Extras and share your ideas for possible Common Pack Advantages and Flaws (like Coterie Advantages and Flaws in the V5 Players Guide). Thanks in advance!

                                                  Here's the first batch:

                                                  Caern Guardians
                                                  The pack has been chosen by the Warder, the sept’s chief guardian, to protect the caern and its bawn from outside attacks and influence. Guardians are loyal to the Warder and are obliged to follow their orders over any given by other sept officials. They often receive powerful talismans to aid in their tasks and hold a place of honor within the sept.

                                                  Callers of the Wyld
                                                  Members of this pack are tasked with attending to the needs of the caern’s patron spirit. They keep up the good relationship between the patron spirit and the sept by performing the necessary propitiatory rituals, listening (and sometimes deciphering) the patron spirit’s wishes and demands, and fulfilling whatever tasks it gives them. When the caern’s patron spirit is content, the caern and the sept can better harmonize, allowing both to grow stronger and more united.

                                                  Community Builders
                                                  Realizing how intricately Garou are connected with wolves and humans, more and more packs of Community Builders have formed in recent decades. These pack usually focus on building sustainable communities and networks to protect and preserve wolf populations, to educate the human public about the importance of wolves to their respective ecosystems, and to raise awareness about environmental issues. Some Community Builders also work hard with human activists to lobby for more environmental protection laws in order to tackle systemic imbalances of power that favor the Wyrm.

                                                  Ghost Healer
                                                  These packs specialize on rescuing, redeeming, or in some cases hunt Starving Remnants. When there are signs of a Starving Remnant haunting the Spirit Wilds near the caern, Ghost Healers search for the lost spirit. Sometimes they find a newly lost Garou whose physical state can still be restored. Often, Ghost Healers are too late unfortunately. In these cases, they try helping the Starving Remnant to “move on” by teaching them to control their Rage, thus ending their confusion and allowing them to find their own niche as local spirits. In more difficult cases, the Ghost Healers perform a dangerous, but sacred hunt chasing the Starving Remnant deep into the Umbra. There, in a mysterious spirit wilderness called the Eternal Hunting Grounds, the Starving Remnant transforms into a wolf-shaped hunter spirit and becomes a naturalized part of the spiritscape. Only if a Starving Remnant is too malicious and dangerous to be kept alive, Ghost Healers hunt it down and destroy it.

                                                  Hunting Party
                                                  These packs locate, stalk, hunt down, and kill specific targets, usually by order of the sept’s Council of Elders or its Wyrm Foe, who coordinates the sept’s packs and plans strikes against the sept’s enemies. The targets are usually servants of the Wyrm, but they can also be dangerous humans, fugitive Garou, local vampires, or rarely even rogue spirits.

                                                  Infiltrators
                                                  Hunting Parties and Strike Forces can only do a good job, if their intelligence is accurate enough. Infiltrator packs are tasked to enter enemy territory or to embed themselves in enemy organizations in order to gather such intelligence. Rarely, when aiming at a high-ranking target that is too difficult to eliminate for “outside” packs, Infiltrators assassinate the target from within, often burning their cover in the process. It is no secret that Infiltrators have one of the most important and dangerous jobs in a sept, yet are often eyed with great suspicion by the sept’s more conservative elements.

                                                  Investigators
                                                  While Infiltrator packs work behind the enemy’s lines to provide crucial intelligence, Investigators packs do the hard legwork from the outside to uncover the Wyrm’s influence and to identify vital targets. Often, these packs have good contacts among journalists, watchdog organizations, and law enforcement agencies in order to always keep an ear to the ground about where the servants of the Wyrm will spread their corruption next. As soon as a viable target is identified, Investigator packs usually brief the assault packs and coordinate their attacks.

                                                  Monkeywrenchers
                                                  Monkeywrencher packs specialize on sabotaging the enemy’s infrastructure and operations. While this often means directly shutting down Pentex factories, burning down secret laboratories, or destroying logging equipment, more urban packs also hack enemy computer systems and infest them with Weaver-infused viruses or sabotage a company’s value on the stock market. Both Monkeywrenchers and Infiltrators work covertly, but the former tend to be more offensive and take a more hands-on approach, whereas Infiltrators usually stick to subtle intelligence-gathering in order to avoid raising suspicion.
