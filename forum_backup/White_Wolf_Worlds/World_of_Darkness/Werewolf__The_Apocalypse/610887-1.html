            About the "environmentalism is a privilege of First World people"... I see a certain amount of skirting the argument here. Personally, I see it exactly the other way round. In the First World, whatever inconvenience arises from environmental destruction, we can just throw money at the problem and life goes on. Climate changes, our crops don't grow properly one year, rainfalls flood a town? Let's just buy the tech to circumvent the consequences. Let's buy food from somewhere else, let's buy the medicine and vaccines against diseases that spread in devastated areas. Let's buy our fuels from other places in the world, no matter under which circumstances they're won and how many people get sick / cancer / have to be relocated during the production. Let's drive the climate change with all the energy that we waste and let the Third World, who barely contributed to it, bear the fallout while we get next to none. Watch islands slowly sink in the ocean and natural disasters kill hundreds of thousands. Let's just watch how hungry people destroy their environment, their forests, their soil just to survive. Environmentalism done right isn't just finger-pointing and being smug, it is essentially about tackling those problems by the root and realizing our own part in causing them in the first place. So I call major BS on that argument.

            Then, the Lupus should be atheists thing, it's even mentioned in the source book that what the Garou have isn't religion in the classic sense. It doesn't require belief, since Gaia and the spirits are absolutely real and tangible in that world. It's as if you could visit Jesus any time you want, have some coffee with him and talk about whatever.

            Tool use: What's the argument here? Garou do that. Half human and stuff.

                Originally posted by Elfive View Post
                To put it another way, Werewolf the Apocalypse is not a setting in which the horror movies are wrong and werewolves are the good guys. It's one where the horror movies are right and werewolves are still the good guys anyway.
                Well, it's a setting where the horror movies got it right, but the moral ambiguity of the setting is so deep and pervasive that the very distinction of "Good" versus "Evil" is largely meaningless.

                If a Garou warrior (say, a Red Talon) goes out and slaughters a bus full of schoolchildren, simply because he can, does that make him (in the context of the World of Darkness) one of "The Bad Guys"? If you say yes, then what happens if the very next thing he does is sacrifice himself in a battle against a Wyrm creature that would otherwise have gone on to destroy or corrupt the area? If you say that the fact that he was willing to sacrifice himself for the spirit of the Earth makes him one of "The Good Guys", does that then mean that him previously murdering a couple dozen schoolchildren is a moral act, or that it otherwise just goes away?

                The werewolves are monsters for the most part -- and the World of Darkness is a fairly complicated place.

Because killing innocent people is bad by definition. The fact that the same Red Talon sacrifices himself against a minion of the Wyrm does NOT redeem him. History is replete with people doing the right thing for the wrong reasons and the wrong thing for the right reasons. Yeah, that Red Talon may have sacrificed himself and saved lives by killing that Wyrm minion, but that still doesn't bring those kids he murdered back to life. A noble monster is still a monster. Absolutely, that Red Talon is still a Bad Guy. After such an atrocity, one just cannot be redeemed. Some things just can't be forgiven. Once you cross the Moral Event Horizon (and killing a bunch of innocent kids is a textbook example of that), you're not coming back. Other Red Talons might not care, but plenty of the other tribes would throat that Red Talon on the spot for that sort of atrocity. A Child of Gaia, Black Fury, Bone Gnawer or Glass Walker sure as hell would.

                            Of all the Tribes, while none of them are immune to falling to the Wyrm, it always seemed to me that the Red Talons are by far the most likely to fall. Genocidal urges and ideologies tend to have that kind of effect in the World of Darkness. Go figure.

Granted, their religion isn't conventional but they do place great faith in Gaia. And by any reasonable measure, they are terrorists in that they use asymetrical tactics in attacking sites staffed by civilians. Not every person working in a factory owned by Pentex is going to be a fomor. Some of them are just mundane humans working normal jobs who happen to get caught in the crossfire. The Furies are also pretty apathetic when it comes to the Talons' mischief -- the two tribes are more often than not in lock-step about taking modern technological society down a notch. The Children are probably the biggest bunch of hypocrites. They preach the whole non-violence and peace-with-the-humans line, but they know exactly what the Talons are. Still, they are also dedicated to inter-tribal unity (including with the Talons), so when anything that the Talons do backfires, the Children are more than ready to give them healing and places to hide, rest and recuperate. Even when the Red Talons led the pogrom against the Bunyip and utterly exterminated them, the Children of Gaia kept on banging the unity-drum -- and then snapped up all the now-vacant caerns that they could get their paws on.

                                            Now, of course, the argument there is that the Garou need every tribe and every single last Garou warrior in the fight against the Wyrm, so they can't waste precious resources in "pointless" internecine fighting. It sort of highlights the complexity of the situation facing the Garou. But nonetheless, it brings to mind the old adage: All that Evil needs to flourish is for good men to do nothing.

                                            I have a hard time thinking of the Garou as heroic when half of them seem to think that murdering innocent people is perfectly okay, and the other half are too apathetic, contemptuous, indifferent, or just plain wrapped up in their own ideological B-S to do anything beyond the occasional mild verbal rebuke.
