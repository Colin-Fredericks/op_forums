            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
            Ronin don't normally make the choice to be part of Garou socitey.
            The books don't really portray this as the majority of Ronin being forced into it. It's generally discussed about evenly left or ostracized.

            Take a look at the iconic Ronin in Albretch.
            He was never a Ronin. He was exiled from his home Sept, and moved to a different one. He was never stripped of his Rank and Renown, and he was never ritually stripped of his Tribe. Treating ever political exile as a Ronin is not how the game operates.

            WHich I hope you can do the game.
            Ronin packs (with the different names they've had over the years) aren't really going to add much besides having ally NPCs working with you given what else we know about the game. And, you know, the four people fighting alongside you thing.

            Such as?
            W20, for example, makes zero mention of Ronin operating as mercenaries for the Garou Nation, and being like Shadowrunners with the Tribes being the megacorps. W20 presents the vast majority of Garou literally shunning Ronin completely and never stooping to doing anything with them besides chasing them off. It is a rare and usually small and desperate Sept that does such things, and they still charge the Ronin unfairly for access to their Caerns and any sort of help (like teaching Gifts).

            Basically, all the interesting stuff that Outcastes give them to do isn't acknowledged at all, because the default game doesn't consider Ronin to be things you play except for the possible redemption arc.

            Ronin aren't cops that don't have to play by the rules. They're cops that have left/were fired under such shameful circumstances that no respectable person would hire them, all the current cops hate them and punish them harsher if they don't follow the rules to the letter, every reporter would love to get a scoop on their further downfall, and the only people actively seeking to offer them a hand are the worst and most vile criminals in town.

            Edit:

            And that's where I don't see it following Bloodlines. In Bloodlines you're an illegal Embrace, but it's treated as being more like a Metis. Your sire is punished, but the Prince decides to spare you to use you as a tool. A Ronin gets booted out of the Protectorate instead of offered a job, with an apartment, and people helping out learn stuff.

                                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                        Personally, I felt the Garou Nation should make regular use of exile as a way of dealing with people.
                                        Why? From the perspective of a TT or LARP game, exile rightfully made a rare option. A dead character means you make a new character. A character stripped of their rank and status can regain them. An exiled character is either a dead character, or a character that is removed from play leaving the player with nothing to do with the rest of the group for however long it takes to reverse their exile.

                                        In universe the reasoning for ostracism being worse than death to the Garou is discussed very thoroughly. The Garou are so social it's easily ingrained in them that death is better than being shunned. The Bone Gnawers are even stated to get a boost in numbers out of the Garou that are too close to choosing to be Ronin for any other Tribe, but still desperate enough for staying part of the Garou society that they don't want to cross that line, and the Gnawers allow them to stay with an acceptable level of loyalty to at least the Tribe.

                                        Given you CAN join factions (and learn their gifts) I'm inclined to think it won't be the kind of "Ronin" which is treated as a shunned outcast but more a valuable asset that you remain apart of it.
                                        We'll have to wait quite a bit I think before we see how this goes exactly.

                                        A "Lone Wolf" versus a outcast and that's the way it should be. Ronin are inherently cool after all.
                                        You don't have to play a Ronin to play a lone wolf. I had a character in a larger format game that had three packs die on him over many years of play, and as a higher Rank member of the Sept essentially just did his own thing most of the time and nobody really cared that he wasn't in a permanent pack because even Garou can understand being standoffish at that point. He'd gather up any stragglers from the Cliaths and lead temporary packs on missions and that sort of thing when numbers were needed on top of experience.

                                        I really don't find anything inherently cool about the Ronin. Probably because they're clearly designed as cautionary tales more than things that are fun to play, but WW could never ignore something that you could technically play getting a book.

                                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                        Clearly you treat finding Erebus a different matter than in my games where it's about as easy as finding the Holy Grail and is semi-mythical.
                                        OK... but that's not how the books describe Erebus at all. Entering and finding redemption isn't exactly easy, but finding Erebus isn't any harder than any other Near Umbra Realm. The Holy Grail is coming out the other side cleansed fully of your sins, not getting to the front gate.

                                        A Ronin can very often be a person exiled for no damn good reason and who stands for their own beliefs.
                                        Not really. Becoming a Ronin requires a Rite (the easy one for those that do it by choice, and the harder one that forces it). As one of the most dire punishments in Garou society, the spirits that empower the rite to make someone a Ronin are going to demand it's for good cause. A Garou can get exiled from a Protectorate and such for no good reason (see Albrecht) but that's not the same as being a Ronin.

                                        Say, a Garou who refuses to abandon his Homid religion.
                                        There's multiple examples of characters (even Camps) that manage to walk the line between human religion and the Garou cult. The basic version is that as long as a Garou upholds the Litany and their duties, there's nothing to punish for believing other stuff too.

                                        A Garou who needs his pack and tribe is probably understandable but the one who puts what is right above companionship is a figure who deserves respect.
                                        Sure, and the one that balances both is even more commended. This is handled in the Renown charts, not an issue of Ronin vs. regular Garou.
