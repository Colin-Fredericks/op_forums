                                  Originally posted by Mahjemm01 View Post
                                  And what does "progressive socially" mean?
                                  Well, the actual definition really isn't that hard:

                                  In political theory "progressive" designates a position that advocates a fundamental change of the current system with the intent to create a better system. This is in contrast (along a spectrum of positions) to liberalism in that liberalism aims to do more technical changes that are aimed at adjusting the "letter" of the system to better reflect the "spirit" of the system.

                                  Not trying to get into a particular debate, but this issue illustrates the difference well:

                                  A liberal position on the 2nd Amendment to justify laws that increase regulations would be based on the full phrasing of the 2nd Amendment and the need to reinterpret it in light of the country no longer using a militia based military system that was supposed to regulate weapons; leaving the government to pick up the slack

                                  A progressive position on the 2nd Amendment to justify laws that increase regulations would be based on the idea that the 2nd Amendment is no longer applicable to modern society (no militias, a completely different level of destructive technology, etc.) and is essentially inapplicable so it should be changed or gotten rid of as not working.

                                  Again, in political theory, "social" refers to issues based on social norms and mores rather than technical debates. The temperance movement and subsequent Prohibition laws in the 20s were about social mores being made into law to criminalize those that violate those norms (abusing alcohol is morally bad, so alcohol needs to be banned). This is contrast to policy debates over things like, say, economics where it is a competition between different economic theories and which to implement to maximize the country's economic health.

                                  So someone with a socially progressive political position, is one that's advocating for overt change on one or more social issues.

                                  Obviously, like most politics, this can take a huge number of variations (including people that should be called right-wing progressives, but progressive movements are so tightly tied to the left do to the right being theoretically founded in conservative thought which is fairly antithetical to progressive ideology, so they get different names like "radical right" instead).

                                  -------

                                  That all said, WtA has always been an extremely socially progressive game as it is (I'd argue the most of the WOD games). The Tribes are very focused on a number of social issues (feminism, indigenous rights, homelessness, racism, political activism, etc.). The game has long since made a point that the Wyrm cannot be defeated in straight up combat because it has corrupted human social institutions so thoroughly that the Garou that seek to fight the Wyrm holistically have to engage in progressive social causes.

                                  Mage, as a contrast, is more of a socially liberal game. It has a strong focus on multiculturalism (which is a socially political issue), but ultimately the main PC groups do not advocate changing how mages organize and govern themselves on a major level. Each Tradition/Craft is largely left to its own philosophic outlook, with only enough adaptation to facilitated cooperation with their allies. The Traditions are not out to make the Hermetics to stop being elitist asshats, just that they keep it down enough to get along to fight mutual enemies.
