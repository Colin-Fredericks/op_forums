    Not all Garou born had the Baptism of Fire performed for them especially in more modern nights. In earlier editions, there was an implied backstory that many Garou approaching their first change became runaways, driven by some inner need and wanderlust, and that other Garou somehow found them. Spirits and such may have played a role, but it doesn't require divine intervention by Incarna. It could be Ancestor Spirits guiding the newly aborning cub to somewhere he will be safe (this would be the easiest go-to scenario). There could be Galliard genealogists tracking extended family lines and asking spirits or kinfolk to keep their eyes and ears open, or that crazy "great uncle" who appears every now and again. There might be cryptic family secrets laid around that only make sense to people on the cusp of change, and which points them to the right direction.

    I agree that lost cubs create a greater risk something will go wrong, but I think there are ways around it.

    Originally posted by heinrich View Post
    But wait, not all garou have their first change at puberty (also page 41). In earlier Editions such garou were crazy Lunatics, today it seems totally okay that they first change in their adulthood (which I find okay, from a character design standpoint).
    Not necessarily Lunatics. Lunatics are people who repress their wolf instincts to the point they can't transform, and the Beast drives them mad as a result. Not all late changing Garou fit that category. There could be various explanations on why they don't change during puberty, but which don't make them Lunatics. Instead, the Beast wakens only at a much later date. Perhaps they needed a specific trigger which they lacked during puberty. I think the likelihood of becoming a Lunatic increases the longer the Garou to be first changes, but it's also entirely possible that there are people should have transformed during puberty, but didn't because they were a Lunatic (they repressed the wolf even at such a young age). I think it's two different phenomenon, but there are some correlations.

                                        It is a basic thought experiment. I mean, where is the practical application? Sure, we as outside observers of the game world know that there are differences between kin and pre-change garou, but those are for the most part only relevant when the Firsting is near or happening.                                                             Originally posted by heinrich View Post
                                                            Like I said, a level of uncertainty would have been fine and it should have come in the form of "Scent of the true form" being not absolute in discerning pre-change garou, but providing false positive and false negative results. The gift not working at all and leaving us hanging in regards to other means of detection, there is absolute uncertainty. And while it makes nice stories - it prohibits the possibility of "One sibling was chosen, the other was not"-scenarios. Along with possible changes to garou culture. So, the change didn't just give, it took away, too.
                                                            There is an easy way to accomplish this. With Scent of True form, the Garou automatically detects a pre-Change Garou as kin. However, if they roll, they have the chance of detecting the pre-Change Garou as really Garou. If the Garou botch the role, they get a false positive or false negative. It's probably best to have the PCs always roll - if they get no successes, then give them the automatic detection - don't give them the automatic success to begin with and then decide whether to roll.

                                                            Botches, of course, will be rare, but I think it makes the uncertainty stories even stronger. After all, Garou will expect the initial test to be correct. In the example Asmodai gave, there is no real reason to groom offspring to become great Garou - 9 of 10 will fail. Why make the investment until the child changes? On the other hand, if the Scent of True form is supposed to work, then of course Garou will take a false positive to raise the child as Garou - they have every reason to think so! Then when the child never changes, it is much more a crushing disappointment.

                                                            Of course, it is always possible to have other Garou shotgun the gift until there is good consensus. However, that is very much a metagame attitude. In actuality, what is more likely is that Scenting a newborn child for parents is a great honor, like being a godfather. It's a honor to do it. You don't take away that person's honor by assuming he'll botch it and having lots of others do it. Besides, botches (or even normal failures) are so rare it's not like Garou expect it to be wrong.
