                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                    Also, Stephen King's "It" with Pennywise as a Deep Umbra Wyrm thing that takes over a town, corrupts the population, and gets eaten by a giant turtle at the end is not actually that far from what a Garou would think a fairly normal occurrence for them to deal with...

                                                    Werewolf gets a lot of focus on the environment business but they live in a Lovecraftian world. It just so happens they're Ghouls versus Humans.
                                                    I would not look to works like 'It' or Lovecraft's corpus to define Werewolf. There are some surface similarities between the Wyrm and the alien gods of the Mythos, but the underlying themes are fairly dissimilar. The inherent meaninglessness of life in the face of cosmic horror featured in Lovecraft's universe doesn't jive with Garou cosmology at all. The Wyrm is a disastrous, maddening evil, but you can fight against it. You must fight against it, as the Garou have done for centuries. Gaia (the soul of the world) is worth fighting for, and it may be possible, albeit with intense personal and societal sacrifice, to overcome the ultimate enemy.

                                                    I think people that compare WtA to Lovecraft kinda miss the boat, and can't see the forest for the tentacles. Garou aren't Ghouls, the Wyrm isn't Yog-Sothoth or the Wyld Azathoth- nothing is closely analogous, and rightly so. Different stories, different messages. WtA has more heroism and less cosmic, otherworldly horror. When the horror does come, often it isn't existential terror, but rather personal sins committed due to unfettered rage or misplaced zealotry.
