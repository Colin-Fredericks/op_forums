                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                I appreciate what you saying, but for me it is always important to not anthropomorphize when dealing with non-human animal behavioral studies. We can't ask a lioness about how she feels regarding what biology demands of her and her social role in her pride as it relates to her biological sex.

                When dealing with humans, it is very easy to demonstrate the separate of sex and gender, because we can show how different groups of humans consider a given behavior "masculine," "feminine," or, "neutral." For example there are modern human cultures that consider relatively heavy applications of make-up a "feminine" behavior (ex: most Western mainstream cultures), others that consider it a masculine behavior (ex: some of the Saharan indigenous tribal groups model masculine sexual displays after birds which includes being more colorful via make up), etc.

                When dealing with non-human animals that gets extremely difficult to suss out; if not impossible. There are very few animals on earth where we can observe disparate social groups that have clearly developed their own cultures to even begin to try to look at if they have gender that can vary from social unit to social unit. When we study orca pods - which display some of the widest diversity in social cultures of non-human animals - we just don't see any real evidence of "gender" that can be separated from sex. Even when there's some separation, there are other factors. For example in pods that have more "individual skill" focused cultures (that is, their life strategies tend to focus on each individual hunting and so on for themselves) mothers do far more teaching. In pods that focus more on communal skills, teaching is... more communal. But all orcas seem to actually treat those teach methods the same way, with the time spent on each being more important than any sort of gender role that varies from pod to pod.

                We can't really say that "males and females behaving differently," establishes gender in non-human animals, because that abandons the idea of socially constructed genders: lots of non-social animals have sex based differences in behaviors. How can they establish genders if they don't form social units?

                Sure, but that raises the problem of an inconsistent definition. There are, for example, individuals in the transgender community who have no interest in dresses, or female typical gender displays, and only seek treatment due to mental and emotional tension regarding their body, but we don't call these people 'Transsexual' until after they've received hormones or in many cases, received surgery, but no one is going to deny that they're transgender. There are a lot of people in feminism who are adamantly against the notion that gender can be anything but social construct, my own anecdotal experience suggests that, unless we're going to start redefining what sex and gender actually mean, then there are parts of gender that are inborn, gender being a spectrum, though, it's difficult to nail these things because there is unlimited possibility for variance.

                And I don't so much consider it 'anthropomorphizing animals' as it is shrinking the britches of humanity, which in general really likes to set itself again or apart from the rest of the animal kingdom. In my own research the context has been that gender refers to behavior and personality, any behavior and personality, sex refers purely to the biological and physical characteristics of the person place or thing in question.

                But I majored in Psychology, not Gender Studies, so I could be wrong.

                                                        Personally, as I stated before in this thread, I do think it is important that there is a discussion in the setting about Black Furies and trans people.

                                                        Mainly, because Black Furies do in fact nicely how a tribe based on ideals of equality and rights can end up fighting itself over what defines their focus.

                                                        Especially, like the Children of Gaia, Black Furies have this divide between them the new blood and the older generations. With the new blood being active in protests and issues, while the old guard usually are more spiritually focused. And especially in Furies, that old guard tend to be very alien to the new blood. Many Crones embrace being witches, and they even have a rite meant to curse entire bloodlines, among other things.

                                                        So having Black Furies be divided on the matter of trans people makes sense. Of course, the setting -itself- should not be against them, only just the npcs. Still, having the tribe fight over this, even as a background event, can add to the game by showing that aspect of the tribe well.
