    Besides the snake and crocodile fomori that may be found at a Temple of Set, I want to mention another possibility: the so-called Set Animal or Typhonian Beast. It is an unidentified totemic animal of Set. Set is often depicted as having the head of this animal. People nowadays think it is either a fictitious animal, an unknown extinct species, or... an aardvark. For our purposes on this thread, I'll consider it as a type of animal fomori based on sighthounds like the Saluki or Greyhound.

    I'll assert that Set developed this fomori for the original purpose of killing the Silent Striders in Egypt thousands of years ago. They became so iconic that the Egyptians began depicting the god with the head of them. Get a pack of them, and they can kill a Garou. Maybe not able to kill a pack, but they might buy enough time for the Setite vampire to flee using Obfuscate. So for our wilderness temples, maybe there is a kennel that contains a lot of these animal fomori, just in case.

    I would have this fomori animal possess a Delerium similar to the Garou in that people who look upon it normally see an ordinary sighthound. Or perhaps the fomori has a bestial mutation that transforms it from looking like a normal sighthound to the Typhonian animal. In any case, it's a good excuse to give a Setite temple something that might give Garou PCs some pause. But I'd probably not include them in a Vampire chronicle - maybe they don't thrive in city life, but need to range in wilderness spaces.

            In WoD, the Typhonian Beast, Typhonian Animal, or Typhonic Beast was the name given to a creature of powerful malice created by Set by dark magic. The animal's own long-extinct species survives only through its association with the Antediluvian, while its original name is long-forgotten.
            The creature known as the Typhonian Beast was a ghoul, a monster created from a now-extinct animal. The creature was described as destructive and hungry for blood. It is said that the Gangrel Antediluvian held it imprisoned with its powers of Animalism, preserving the creature's life because it was the last animal of its kind in the world.
            Under the guidance of their clan-founder, the Gangrel of the ancient city of Ur began training the man named Enkidu to be a childe of the Antediluvian, one that would be able to be the guardian of the several rare creatures contained by the Antediluvian within its flesh. In the century of training that followed, the Setites and their allies eventually attacked the city of Ur, called by the Typhonian Beast as Enkidu struggled to contain it within his body, as his sire taught him. The battle against the invading Followers of Set, desperate to retrieve the Typhonian Animal, was ferocious. Enkidu fought fiercely against the beast but was pressed to the point his rage made him monstrous. Wounded, he vanished into the night while the city burned - but the Setites were denied the Typhonian Animal, whom they believe could lead them to Set himself. After that night, although Enkidu would occasionally encountered other Gangrel, he never met his sire again. Throughout the ages Enkidu has learned with the wisdom of spirits of the wilderness, that only by holding onto his Humanity could he continue to contain the Typhonian Beast – that never ceased its struggle to escape. In an effort to maintain his morality, the ancient Gangrel has splintered part of his personality into another form. Thus, he can transform himself into this form and maintain a mental grasp on human concepts.

                        Originally posted by Asmodai View Post
                        Honestly, I generally so tie the Setites with urban centers and exploiting city structures that It's easy to forget that you need quiet places to perform your rites and do all the vile shit you need to do without witnesses and easy methods of disposal and/or storage.
                        Yeah, I think it is very easy to fall into the idea of Setites only as an antagonist in Vampire, and that means having them act like the other vampires - putting them almost exclusively in cities. But if you consider them to be an antagonist for all the game lines (as I think they were originally intended), you need to start thinking about them differently. The Setites have become "trapped" in Vampire for obvious reasons. As later developers wanted them to become more viable as options for Vampire PCs, they gave no thought as their potential use as antagonists in other game lines.

                        I think it is easy to forget because while the first few game lines mentioned the Setites a lot (Vampire, the original Mummy book, Werewolf), the later game lines did not. As enemies of the Mage version of the Cult of Isis (which are incorporated into the Order of Hermes), the Setites should have been mentioned in some capacity. Maybe not as much as the Tremere, but given that much of Hermetic lore is based on Hermes Trismegistus which is a combination of Hermes and Thoth, the Setites could have provided some kind of foe. The Book of Madness mentioned various vampire infernalists of the Sabbat, Baali, and Tremere, but no mention of Setites. It is a curious omission. I wonder if the writers were beginning to become leery of the game's Howardiana by that point. I am not surprised Wraith did not make the Setites a big opponent as they pretty much had to create the entire Underworld setting by scratch, and consideration of antagonists in the Skinlands was secondary. But given Osiris makes for a Far Shores figure, and stylized Egyptian magic should involve Necromancy, it is again surpising there was not some mention of the Setites in books like Quick and the Dead or Mediums like the Giovanni were. By the time Changeling came out, I think the game was in the midst of distancing itself from its early Howardiana and there may have been second thoughts of making the Setites on the big bads for everyone, but even here I could see some kind of "giant snake" opponent simply tied to the Fomorians that could somehow be leveraged to the Setites in some capacity.

                        Because the Setites stopped being mentioned, I think it was much easier for their involvement with the greater World of Darkness to be forgotten, and they "shrank" to just being a setting element in Vampire.

                        Originally posted by Asmodai View Post

                        Unless I was running an actual plot for Bubasti PCs to somehow free their feline ancestors from the Setites, I would mostly avoid any involvement of the Kyphur except as setting lore. If anyone should free them, it should be the Bubasti. If anyone else, like the Garou did it, I would feel the same way I would if I picked up an issue of Batman to find out Superman showed up and sent the Joker and all other Batman villains to the Phantom Zone, and now Gotham is a much better place and doesn't need Batman anymore. There's a certain logic to that given the interconnected setting of the DC superheroes universe, but that's not why I would read Batman comics.

                        As I stated in the twelfth post of this thread, I would probably limit the use of the Kyphur among the Setite faction to explain the involvement of some corrupted (or reluctantly coopted) Bubasti among the Followers of Apophis. Some Bubasti "serve" this faction in order to get access to the Kyphur. Having the Garou PCs witness or figure out that a Bubasti has stolen some artifact from a museum, or is performing some services for some hippie commune out in the hills, could get them involved in a plot without revealing too early that the Setites are up to something. Setting up a Bubasti as an enemy of the Garou PCs because of some involvement with the Setites is both an obvious use of them, but something I think PCs would be shocked to learn (at least the first time).

                                            I had no idea that DAV20 gave us more examples of the Typhonian Animal. I thought this was something never addressed, but now it seems like several writers have incorporated it for the game. Just makes Serpentis even more of a list of random powers than it already was!

                                            I also had a realization about the story of the Enkidu. Enkidu is more popularly known as the "Noah". Since the Gangrel antedeluvian held extinct animals in its body, and passed those down to Enkidu, it is possible that those extinct animals are supposed to pre-date the Flood. That means Set might have created the Typhonian Beast even before the Flood. It was not a creation while he was actually in Egypt. If so, the Typhonian Animal might have all sorts of weird abilities lost since the Flood, and is not just a mundane animal that was ghouled. It might be more like a Bygone.

                                            Now of course, whether an ST wants to go down that route depends on how they interpret "the Flood" and reconcile the story of Set and Osiris in Egypt to the traditional Caine story. For some STs the above would just be ridiculous. Some STs are very historical and materialistic minded. But it could work for others.

                                            In my case I hold that there was a Flood in the sense of some cataclysm that corresponded with the Severing, and thus the pre-Flood world was filled with all sorts of magic and spirit that simply no longer existed on Earth after the Severing. That is why there is no archaeological proof of its existence - all the evidence is on the other side of the Umbra somewhere. I also hold that there are, in effect, two "Sets". The first Set is the actual antedeluvian of the Clan. The second Set was the mortal brother of Osiris in Egypt embraced by the antedeluvian (and thus of the 4th generation). All of the antedeluvian's other childer perished, so the brother of Osiris became his only childe. (Eventually this would change like when Kemintiri was embraced) As the years went by, this childe often served as surrogate for the antedeluvian when it would disappear for whatever reason. Eventually the two figures would become merged and their distinction forgotten. So in my setting, I could have the Typhonian Animal be an artifact of the pre-Flood world, and all other attempts were to try to emulate it (like the fomori sighthound). I'll have to give this some thought.

                                            Another weird thing about the Enkidu is its "Sabrina" form. This human (typical Gangrel) appearing form is part of Enkidu's human personality and is an attempt to hold onto his Humanity. The Sabrina form also has access to certain Disciplines and Abilities that the Noah form does not. The Sabrina is taken by need whenever the Enkidu needs the social skills of that form. We're given two reasons why the Sabrina is form is used 1) to hunt in more civilized areas where the monstrous Noah form would draw attention, and 2) to locate any Setite in the area so the Enkidu form can destroy it. The interesting thing is that the Sabrina form has Faerie Lore 4 and Lupine Lore 5. If we take everything seriously, the only reason for the Sabrina form to have those Lores is to help her destroy Setites. That may mean some interaction with the Lupines to turn them against Setites she's discovered. It is said that Enkidu has almost no contact with Lupines outside the cities, so that might imply it is the Glass Walkers or Bone Gnawers that have been so manipulated to move against the Setites. So if an ST wants to use the Enkidu character as a means to propel plot, using the Sabrina form is one tool to do so.

                                            I still find the Enkidu character very gonzo and bizarre. It holds some attraction from me, but is so over the top I don't know if I'd ever use it in an actual game.

In terms of physical abilities, he is probably one of the weaker mummies, but has boatloads of magical bullshit.

                                                    I will use as inspiration the stories about the legendary schools of black magic like the Scholomance located near Sibiu, Romania (previously known by its Transylvanian Saxon name of Hermannstadt), the Cave of Salamanca in Spain, the Black School of Wittenberg in Germany, and many others. In all these stories the students go to the school to learn knowledge and wisdom, including magic, but the last one to leave the school upon graduation must give his soul to the schoolmaster who is the Devil. In our case, the “Devil” is actually the Bane Mummy and he arranges for the last student to sell his soul to Apophis (and receive demonic investments or other powers through an infernalist Pact).

                                                    This Bane Mummy teaches anyone who wants to learn - mortals, vampires, Bastet, whomever. The people who leave do so with a lot of Knowledges and certain Paths of Sorcery, Thaumaturgy, etc. It’s random chance who will be able to leave the school last so it can be a very tempting and corrupting opportunity.

                                                    Usually the stories say that the curriculum lasts between 3-7 years, so it must be a very thorough education. You are learning philosophy, theology, literature, the classical languages, mathematics, science, etc. as well as magic and lots of supernatural lore (true for the WoD, not the fake stuff). The students here leave with a high level of Academics, Enigmas, Law, Medicine, Occult, etc. plus knowledge of various magic and other supernatural abilities. It’s a good excuse to justify NPCs having certain knowledges at rank 5 if they once attended the Black School. And the students may be able to learn things they cannot do anywhere else like learn ancient Sumerian and Hittite or learn how to read Linear A. Although the Bane Mummy has a curriculum, he allows much independent study. And he doesn’t care what they do with such knowledge. If they want to use it only to protect people from black magic, that is just as fine with him as someone who begins learning Infernalist paths on his own. He just wants it disseminated because once it is out there, who knows what will happen?

                                                    The “cult”, put simply, is the school itself and its students, particularly those who have sold their soul. Those will be the ones who will make the arrangements to speed up the Bane Mummy’s resurrection. We can call this cult “the Black School” but as we have seen it has had many local individual names throughout its history. The location of the school changes every so often, so the Bane Mummy travels and sets up the school someplace else, explaining why there are so many variations of the story. I’d even have the Bane Mummy often introduce himself as Satan or some other figure as needed. The students can decide if they want to accept the story or not, he doesn’t care.

                                                    The Bane Mummy does this because 1) it gets souls for Apophis and thus long term slaves, 2) it pushes out a lot of forbidden and dangerous lore out there, and 3) it might lead to additional allies depending on what the students do afterwards. The potential for corruption is high. The Bane Mummy might occasionally ask certain students (only those that had the “right” mindset thatcould make them allies) to help or assist the cults of the other Bane Mummies.

                                                        The Bane Mummy takes very few students - most legends of the Black School say it is seven, nine, or thirteen at any one time. And the classes last seven years or so. Of course, the Bane Mummy cannot spend his entire time teaching students. He may need to take time off to gather additional occult resources, expand his own knowledge, arrange for the secret building of the next school, visit old friends (past students, other Bane Mummies, certain elder Setites), or just go on vacation and enjoy life. Who says one must spend eternity always working?

                                                        So there may be quite some time between separate classes. So the Bane Mummy probably graduates only one or a few classes before it depletes in Ba and waits for its resurrection. It might stay in a local area for several lifetimes before moving on. And while we know some of those locations from legends, he probably has many schools elsewhere and has spent only some of his time in Europe. He probably had a school in Egypt (perhaps near Alexandria where he was first made) early on, perhaps one in North Africa, and he’s now slowly making a circuit in Asia. No reason to keep the Black School limited to Europe or the Mediterranean.

                                                        I would make the Black School one of the primary sources for learning the Thaumaturgy Paths of Setite sorcery. One can learn such magic from individual Setites of course, but you’ll likely have to pay boons and only know whatever Path or Paths your mentor wants you to learn. On the other hand, you can learn the full package from the Bane Mummy provided you are accepted, you have the talent, and you put in all the effort that is needed. It is a high risk, high reward option for certain Setites who know about it. You have a 6 in 7 (or 12 in 13) chance to keep your soul! Who wouldn’t accept those odds? Plus of course whatever he extorts from the Setite clan as a whole to hold these classes. This could become a big part of the backstory of some Setite NPCs.

                                                        The magical traditions of the other clans lack the resources that the Tremere clan has at its disposal. So this Bane Mummy arcane master and teacher represents something that helps the Setites make up for that. Yeah, very few Setites get this level of teaching compared to the in depth study that most Tremere receive, but it can produce some potent blood sorcerers.

                                                        Of course, it is not limited to just Setites. Sometimes he accepts students from other vampire clans. He’s taught Koldunic sorcery to Tzimisce, accepted Tremere as his students, independent Sabbat, and other vampires (including Dracula if you want to make the Black School the actual Scholomance and not just inspired by it). These non-Setites might be taught alongside the Setites, or maybe he holds separate classes distanced in time. I imagine he keeps his mortal students and vampire students in separate classes so there isn’t the issue of one group of students exploiting the other. He takes his responsibility to his students seriously (good for him).

                                                        Maybe this is one reason for the existence of the Order of the Wyrm within the Tremere - some of its members were originally students of this Bane Mummy, and this is where they learned of the Al Azif - or of the Eye of the Serpent instead. The Black School is definitely an asset of the Setites, but it isn’t limited to them.

                                                        I would make teaching mortals his main passion. This is where the greatest possibilities for corruption lie. He maybe holds a class for vampires once in a lifetime, and may extort favors and Apophis pacts from the Setites to simply hold the class. And when he does, he may invite vampires from other clans as well. Or he may hold a vampire class and not even invite any Setites just to remind them that he is independent. Or not teach any vampires for centuries.

                                                            Other members of his “cult” would include various Banes, and I would make some of them be able to materialize. Depictions of some of these black schools describe mysterious hands appearing out of the shadows to hand students what they need. These Banes probably appear as normal people after materialization, though they may have telltale signs indicating their true nature. Scryer Banes would be useful. There may be Scrags that can be used in defense of the school. Maybe a single Dream Maker used only when necessary. And invent your own Banes to accomplish what is needed. And of course, an enterprising student with the right Paths could learn to summon these same kinds of Banes.

                                                            The cult would also include anyone needed to maintain his students - people responsible for shopping for food and consumables (or act as Herd for vampire students). All the prosaic needs that a school must fulfill for its students. Maybe these are dedicated humans, or people the Bane Mummy has hired to perform tasks when needed and are discarded when they are not needed anymore, or kidnapped and compelled by supernatural powers. Or such help may be an element of the Ushabti Hekau.

                                                            I think the most likely reason why PCs would encounter this Bane Mummy is when he is not teaching students, but during his time off when he is hunting down obscure books of forbidden lore, archaeological treasures, and artifacts of power. Or when it is looking for the school’s next location and is arranging things to prepare for the first class. Or he might just be used as background element to explain a very powerful sorcerer, and perhaps the PCs can learn of the Black School’s existence and attempt to hunt it down and destroy it. Even if the Black School itself is not involved, it can be something used to justify something else and build up some lore in your chronicle.
