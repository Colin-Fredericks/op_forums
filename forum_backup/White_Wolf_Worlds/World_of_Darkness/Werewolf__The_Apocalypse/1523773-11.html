                        The reason I mentioned it is not just because these two templates have Appearance 1. Of the remaining three, two more have Appearance 1 and the other is Appearance 2. The Rust Shaman definitely has a reason why the character is Appearance 1. The others don't. There's no real reason for such low traits across the board except "Um, they're Bone Gnawers so who cares."

                        This is not a major issue and can be easily fixed, but I find it annoying. In my experience players rarely want their characters to have App 1 unless that is part of their specific character concept. They at least default to App 2 and may increase from there. Because Attributes are so expensive to increase with XP, I find most players choosing to increase Abilities instead. So getting the Attributes "right" for their character at creation is generally important.

                                    Vigilante Wolf (Lupus Philodox)

                                    Animal abuse is one of my button issues, so that will probably color my reaction to this one.

                                    This is also another of those write ups that appears to be longer than average.

                                    This one isn't really gender specified either. They were born to what I presume was feral stray with a high percentage of wolf blood. The runt of the litter, they were the only one spared when their mother and pack mates were all horribly abused and murdered by some group of humans. "Street punks" who did it just for amusement. Naturally, this left a lot of psychic scars and emotional baggage.
                                    They managed to survive, partially thanks to help from more empathic and humane humans, all of whom seemed to carry a distinctive scent that marked them. Obviously, this is meant to imply Bone Gnawers and/or their Kinfolk. Eventually they grew up powerful enough to dominate and command most of the other stray dogs in the area.
                                    Then the First Change happened and they learned that humans tend to abuse other humans as well before finally being able to fight back against his abusers. Now they're a roaming urban vigilante who is loosely associated with The Hood camp.

                                    Equipment is a trench coat and fedora, a light pistol, hand cuffs, and matches. The coat is mentioned in the write up as being dedicated so they don't lose it when shifting. I confess that this leaves me with the image of a person walking around the city streets naked under a trench coat, which honestly sounds more like a flasher than a vigilante. But with the fedora, I'm guessing at least some of the inspiration behind this character came from the 1990 film Darkman. Not that that's a bad thing.

                                    The image is of a somewhat ragged and scruffy looking Crinos with blood on their hands. There's nothing really outstanding about it one way or another. It fits the character and does have a nice savage quality to it, I think.

                                    For Attributes, Physicals are primary, with a 3/4/3 spread. Socials are tertiary with 3/2/1. Mental is secondary, with 4/2/2.
                                    I'm a little torn on the Socials. One the one hand, I can see the character as perhaps having various scars and the like from their very rough upbringing (and also the Darkman influence), and a certain degree of Manipulation is needed for good Intimidation rolls. On the other hand, I could just as easily see them as looking fairly normal and having a low Manipulation to represent how straightforward and blunt they are. I guess this is one of those six vs half a dozen things.

                                    For Abilities, Talents are primary with two extra dots from freebies. 3 each in Alertness and Dodge, 2 each in Brawl, Intimidation, Primal Urge, and Streetwise, and 1 in Athletics.
                                    Skills are secondary with two extra dots from freebies (again). 3 each in Stealth and Survival, 2 each in Animal Ken and Firearms, and 1 in Leadership.
                                    Knowledge is tertiary with two extra dots from freebies (yet again). 3 in Investigation, 2 in Enigmas, and 1 each in Law and Linguistics.
                                    By and large, I think these are fine. I don't really think they need the Firearms skill. Law is also perhaps a little iffy. I think I'd probably take those two Firearms dots and put one into Leadership and the other into either Brawl or Streetwise. The one from Law I'd be tempted to use for Backgrounds instead.

                                    Said Backgrounds are 3 in Allies and 2 in Contacts. From the write up, I think the Contacts are mainly the Barking Chain. At least some of the Allies might be the stray street dogs they still command the loyalty of. This is part of why I think a slight boost to Leadership might be useful. This also reminds me that I wish the WoD games had at some point talked about the idea of having animal allies, be they wolf packs, incredibly well trained horses, or whatever. If I'd decided to spend those freebies on Backgrounds instead of Abilities, I might've gone with more levels in Contacts, or possibly Pack Totem. A one dot Mentor who helps the character better understand human society might also be useful.

                                    Rage and Gnosis are the default levels of 3 and 5, respectively. Willpower has been raised to 7 with freebies.

                                    I mostly like this character. It's not perfect, but I can't exactly put my finger on what it's flaw is. It does have a fairly strong backstory and motivation. I think there's a case this might work better as an Ahroun, but at the same time I think the way the write up shows how they've experienced both abuse and kindness at the hands of humans works with the Half-Moon ideas about balance and justice.

                                            I like this template in a lot of ways with one big exception. It is very hard to see this character as an actual lupus - meaning a wolf. Its description of its life as an animal points more towards being a dog. There is no problem with Bone Gnawers having lupus members. But why would a wolf pack be living in a city? Does it make sense that some kinds would actually mess with a wolf pack? Or that the wolf mother could not have seriously injured or scared this group of kids/teens who messed with her cubs? "Street punks" won't normally screw around with an actual wolf pack or den. None of this seems like it is talking about wild wolves (who somehow found themselves in a city for some reason). It naturally reads like they are talking about dogs.

                                            I remember there being a LOT of confusion about the nature of the Bone Gnawers in the nineties. A LOT of players had the impression that Bone Gnawers were basically dogs, not wolves. It doesn't help that page 29 of the tribebook actually shows a picture of a dog. (Even in the 1e corebook there are images of Bone Gnawers that seem much more like dogs than wolves, though this might simply be because of the lower quality of art in that sourcebook.) Even terminology like "the Barking Chain", which this template references, creates the impression of dogs as opposed to wolves. Wolves don't really bark. Everyone says that wolves howl, but dogs bark. I understand the term may be poetic, but combined with other things, it creates a lot of confusion.

                                            I don't see the lupus of the Bone Gnawer being urban based. They are probably one of the tribes that are most wolf-starved. If you are going to assign a camp to lupus, the Hillfolk are more natural. The Bone Gnawer as described are VERY homid based, so there actually is not a lot for the lupus breed to go by. The tribebook just ignores the issue rather than engage it. It's major weakness that is not sufficiently addressed even in the Revised version.

                                            Outside of this issue, the template suffers from not explaining exactly how a lupus gets Allies and Contacts. I think it is perfectly fine for a lupus to have human Allies and Contacts, but the template description must address how the lupus crossed this divide. You may only need two sentences or so, but it should be explained. It doesn't. It just assumes you have them.

                                            Lastly, and I may be unfair about this, it irritates me to read this:

                                            "You do not threaten the Veil. You wear a black trenchcoat that is Dedicated to you."

                                            Great, that must be all you need. Black trenchcoats solves everything.

                                            For the tribe and auspice, this is a solid template. But I have major problems for it as a lupus breed character.

                                            Again, this template has an Appearance of 1.

                                                        Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
                                                        But WoD is not our world in the sense that there are semi-sentient wolf kin running about nevermind fully sentient lupus garou. There are two (or four if we count Furies and Coggies) tribes of werewolves regularly found in the city. So I can totally see high content wolfdog kin being a thing in the cities. You got smarter than normal wolves, who already are smart and you get wolves trying to find easier time where the Trash is.
                                                        If this is actually something that is an element of the game setting, it is such a departure from the real world that it really needs to be documented and explained explicitly somewhere (preferably a major book that won't be overlooked - like in the corebook or players guide, not wait many years for something like Book of the City (which doesn't describe anything like that). What is the extent of them? Are they found in all major cities? Only cities that have urban caerns or a large population of Urrah? Is it something unique to the Bone Gnawers, or do the Glass Walkers (and perhaps even other tribes) have them as well? Is this only a kinfolk animal thing, or are there other such scavenging wolf packs. Is this well known among humans, or is it somehow kept secret and unknown except for urban legends and the like?

                                                        Should we consider them some kind of fictional subspecies of canis lupus that are perhaps smaller, more slender, and have more doglike or coyote-like features?

                                                        What exactly do these carnivores eat in the city? Wolves need to eat a lot of meat - about 5-15 lbs per day - and there are no large hoofed animals that make up 90% of the wilderness wolf diet. Cities don't even have a lot of small mammals like rabbits, beavers, raccoons, or large fish like salmon. Yeah, some of those animals are around in the city, but not enough to support an ongoing population of wolves. Eating rodents and birds won't be enough. So are they eating people's pets like urban coyotes often do? Really going after food waste in garbage cans (if you think raccoons are bad)? Scavenging off (or perhaps hunting/killing) people living in the street? US cities typically don't have a large greenbelt or wilderness nearby that such wolves could live off of and then go back and forth to the city. It's just a lot of suburbs. A good ST can figure out answers for these in a way to make sense (maybe the Bone Gnawers move them back and forth, monitor them so they know when to provide aid, have their members regularly provide food (either real or through the Cooking Gift or by a Fetish), have kinfolk run restaurants or collect in common building so they can dump a lot of food waste, etc., but some thought needs to go into it to help us suspend our disbelief.

                                                        Not only would this be a prominent setting element for the Werewolf game, it has other impacts as well. Some kind of wild scavenging wolf packs in cities would interest a LOT of vampires who have Animalism. How does that impact the Garou in return?

                                                        I am not necessarily against this idea. As you explained, there is various lore in the game that can support the concept. And the idea of wolflike urban predators has a good presentation in the book and movie Wolfen which was Whitley Strieber's more "scientific" take on the werewolf myth. So it's firmly in theme. I think it can add to the game. If an ST wants to make it a thing in her chronicle and does a good job of presenting this, I am not against it. I could easily enjoy provided it is kept limited and doesn't force other changes like a domino.

                                                        But the actual setting materials don't present us with this. We're not given enough as STs and PCs to assume it exists. Instead, we have to create head canon in order to justify things like this. I don't think the setting actually intends it. It isn't necessarily against it either, but I don't think the creators actually considered this. So when we have things like this, it needs more explanation. Again, no reason not to make sufficient changes to allow it, but to pull it off, an ST will need to put some thought into it. If people want to talk about, it could be its own thread.
