    Mokole- For too many different reasons.
    1) I'm an a herper by hobby (Currently own 17 different herps and that's not counting my dart frog tadpoles). To be able to play as a werereptile was a big plus for me, so I was excited to buy both the Mokole and Nagah breed books. The only thing that takes the Nagah from being tied with the dragons is that I can't find anyone who wants to play an all Nagah game and well they don't mix well. W20 giving us the Ao was what took it over the top for me (big time turtle guy)
    2) Archid form- you can base entire concepts around this form. For me building the archid isn't all about more power, but to have it be a reflection of the character's persona. I have a shinning for example that I have turn into a large bipedal chameleon for example. Color change rotating eyes and a long tongue don't exactly make one a battle tank, but fun none the less. The customization here threw the Mokole instantly to the top slot.
    3) Culture- I have been a big fan of a lot of the cultural stuff written about the mokole. Considering their kin the "8th sun" to me always made me read that they have a close relationship with their kin and see them more then just property or breeding stock but vital members of the Wallow. I was amused to read in revised Rage Across the World about how Mokole applied to NGO's and the UN for various grants and aide to help different areas of Africa where their kin could be found. The idea that they were thinking outside the typical shifter box amused me.
    4) Purpose- I love the idea of being the memory, and how this could shape your character's out look on life. Mokole can remember being different races/genders/and even changing breeds. It gives them a unique in site into the world. I also think gifts like "Walk Into My Eyes" and "View the Seed" are interesting and can be fun to use in game, along with rites like "Burn the Library" and "Silence of the Oracles" can not only be useful but can really open the door for a lot of fun role playing. I think its a shame that the Mokole didn't get more gifts and rites along these lines then what they did, like maybe an ability like lvl 3 Auspex or lvl 3 Mytherceria.

        I cant say I have read all the tribe-books and am well informed on all the tribes but so far my favourite are the Ananasi, probably followed by the Nagah

        I actually do play an Ananasi right now (see other thread) and I just love the different outlook on things when compared to the other breeds and also the alien-ness of the spider-shifters. They also have quite a lot of very interesting abilities and gifts. Yes even if you don't go for the most efficient ones for fighting
        Its interesting how they see it as their duty to restore balance to the Triad, to even possibly get rid of the Weaver so that Ananasa can take her place (and do what she was made to do) even though Ananasa is held prisoner by the Wyrm yet they still seem to think she is totally uninfluenced by him and totally tricking him by being in contact with her children without him noticing.
        I also like all the options for subterfuge and stealth you can get.
        This time around I opted to not boost any abilities at character creation but instead put lots of freebies in merits and stuff like that that I wouldn't be able to learn later in the game. It gives me even more basic abilities to play with and helps explain the continued survival of the character in the time before the game started.

        I'm not even a great fan of spiders in real life. Actually I guess I even have a mild case of Arachnophobia (though much less severe than several other people I know, maybe it shouldn't be called that at all in my case, don't know) but on the other hand I still find them very interesting animals nonetheless.

        Had to decide between Ananasi and Nagah for the current game. As its a Hengeyokai based game a Nagah would have been somewhat easier to include but would have brought with it a lot of other difficulties. Like having to be reeeeeaaaaally careful about not letting any outsider know that you're a Nagah.

                    Conceptually, I think I like all the breeds. However, I do feel the earlier breeds got the shaft because they were just stupidly focused variants of Garou with little to actually give them their own character. Gurahl are all misunderstood CoG. Nuwisha are all Ragabash. Corax were an early attempt at being different, and their concept is cool, but they got shafted with a super short book. The Ajaba even got a little chunk in a book about their biggest enemies. It took the creation of the Ahadi to actually make them worthwhile and raise them from the status of the occasional Villian and punching bag. Mokole are possibly one of the worst offenders because they really, really do try to be special. And then lose a lot of their oomph thanks to a rather poorly written Breed Book. At least if you focus on the cool things (Archid, Sun, Mnesis, the Streams, some of their artifacts, their Metis) you can play up a pretty cool Breed.

                    It's a shame the Kitsune didn't get more variety, they kinda ended up as Garou lite thanks to the fact that they got a mini-breedbook, but I did like their mythology and interaction with the Hengeyokai. That reminds me that I never actually read their writeup in the W20 Changing Breeds, and I probably should. I think I am probably the most lenient towards them of the ones who got handed the cliff-notes ball.

                    The winners in the Breed pool are the latter maniacs - Ratkin, Ananasi, Nagah and Rokea. They are all quite a bit harder to crossover with anything else, but that's kinda the point - they literally burst with flavour of their own and can carry whole games on their shoulders.

                    Ratkin are batshit insane, and their breedbook manages to paint a brilliant image of eschatonic revolutionaries getting ready for the wrong war of the Apocalypse. Every book that makes the Garou perspective positive in comparison has to be downright special. And the Ratkin manage it by being gleeful about all the chaos.

                    Ananasi. Ok, I never understood how you get from Grandfather Spider to the great Spider Queen (something I've actually used in my games to create a schism at one point, actually). However, Ananasa is a brilliant idea. All the shifters had their gods in a rather... hands off holding pattern. You got get as the great unifier, as the grand planer and as a comparativley reasonable objective for your games. Then add an utterly alien mindset, and you're faced with a rather interesting group of characters who can be anything from heroes, villians to antagonists. Good, job whoever wrote them, because these guy make sense, even if it's a bit of work to actually integrate them into a game.

                    Nagah. You know how I complained about overcrippling specialisation and how it's a poor excuse to make a good breed? Nagah take that concept and own it. With a very broad and colorful look on the snakes in mythology, and a whole complete game mode that's based on playing them and just them, and a deep seated criticism of what they do and what they did. There is room for some amazing interactions and stories there. It's a bit sad that you will probably never get to play them, but it's still a small price for a really enjoyable book. Also, I do feel that this is what the Corax and the Nuwisha could have turned out if someone actually gave them the time and pagecount they deserved.

                    Rokea. Oh momma. These guys are simply amazing. They take the whole game and completley reskin it, terming everything in the perceptions of someone who doesn't understand anything beyond the Sea. It introduced the problems of the Rokea, their rudimentarry politics, the acquatic reinterpetation of the Triat and one of the coolest visuals ever - Giant Gaian Kami-Caern whales. These guys are off. Their logic and values are skewed, and they really, really make sense. This and the Ananasi books may actually fight for my two favourite Werewolf supplements.
                    Bonus points for actually running a Rokea oneshot at sea in the middle of the night on a rocky beach.

                    What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.
