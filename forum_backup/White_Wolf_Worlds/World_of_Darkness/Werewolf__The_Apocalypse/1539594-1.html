                                        W5's priorities are just not that well-aligned with what WtA is. They have this focus on humanity, for example. So you have your warehouse with abusive working-conditions or a hunting company or some other industry-site like an oil refinery or pipeline. Then you reveal to the players that a ), it's a bad thing and b ), the leadership and security personnel are all Fomori. Then the PCs do their thing, go in, destroy the thing, kill the Fomori, earn Renown... It's the "radical goth-punk game of environmental horror where you can finally do something" (that's the way it's presented occasionally). But then W5 is just so proud of itself, it thinks it's just so profound to then point out how this one action hasn't solved climate-change, hasn't truly fixed the underlying issue and doesn't it say SO MUCH about the flawed nature of Garou that their Rage hasn't solved everything magically? Except the W5-setup is rather than profound quite banal. You put a bad thing in the setting, the proverbial nail that sticks out and needs to be dealt with, you put a hammer in the form of Garou-warriors into the hands of the players, they use the hammer on the nail and then W5 thinks it's interesting to point out that not everything in the world can be solved with a hammer - except the solution is always the hammer because that's what W5 gives to the players. There hasn't been a single W5 adventure that hasn't culminated in "And now the PCs have to kick this big bad guy's ass.". Therefore, what W5 perceives as profound only even exists on the level of when you talk about the game not in the way that it plays and what happens during the game.

                                        But you could reframe it as "The world has been getting worse, the Wyrm has been getting stronger and if it's a contest between Bane-Spirits/Fomori/Pentex/BSD and humanity then humanity is gonna lose. That's why you need Garou.". You can't do that, though, because that would put the focus more on the Wyrm than bad humans which in turn would make things more spiritual again, give the Umbra a bigger focus. And Paradox doesn't want that.

                                        Also, my suspicion is that lore-wise this supplement will be a let-down overall. Because ultimately W5 doesn't like WtA-lore. "Re-imagination, not continuation" and all that... If any writer were to go too far in re-introducing old bits of lore, that's where then these paragraphs appear that argue stuff like "Maybe the Garou Nation never existed, ever considered that?" to counter-balance things. It will just be this tug-of-war between writers trying to add anything of substance to the setting but then having to adhere to the idea that WtA-lore is bad and needs to be avoided. And yet, since W5 also isn't a huge departure a la WtF, the old lore is the most obvious thing to add to the game. Like, trying to explain Septs... easiest way to do that is to just pick up the WtA 20th Anniversary and do a copy-paste. Similarly, in online-conversations when new players asked about clarifications and more lore... the easiest way to deal with that has been to just refer them back to the previous editions. But my suspicion/fear is that there's just this editorial stance coming from Paradox that looks at a supplement like this one and would have insisted on making sure WtA-lore is avoided/undermined as much as possible.
