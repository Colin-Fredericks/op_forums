    Thoughts on W5 tribes

    10-14-2022, 01:39 PM
    This is not a thread about if the wholesale changes are good or not, this thread is focused on the tribes and what flaws I have seen as they are presented in the preview and Justin's comments.


    Black Furies- Furies might be a tribe prone to fighting, but I feel they would serve better as a Wisdom tribe. Once you get past the angry young Furies, the tribe has a lot more spiritual stuff than stuff dedicated to combat. A larger flaw I see is the inclusion of men into the tribe. Furies are a tribe focused on the mysteries of womanhood and the deeper concept of the Earth Mother as a mother. If this concept does not fit W5, I would have preferred the Furies to be omitted than changed like this.

    Their updated glyph is all right, though I liked the original more.


    Bone Gnawers- The only two issues I see here are the tribe being listed as a tribe of Honour and the implication that you can now be a rich Bone Gnawer. Even the Gnawer verbs hint that the tribe is not exactly honourable. The Gnawers are a tribe tied to being in the worst places and thriving, things like Honour and dignity do not go with it. The tribe name and totem HIGHLY also imply they are not a tribe of manners. The best renown to tie to the Gnawers should be Glory.

    Children of Gaia- The Coggies should be a tribe of Honour, as they more or less hold a leadership position.

    Fianna- Maybe a name change. Otherwise, I don't see anything worth changing.

    Galestalkers- Change the Ban to something not related to eating meat. The name change and the totem change are fine and needed.

    Ghost Council- Nothing worth commenting on from me.

    Glass Walkers- Why did their tribe patron change to the spider? What was wrong with the cockroach?

    Red Talons- The Talons also suffer from the push for inclusivity and a mismatched renown type. But I think as a tribe, they should have been cut entirely. I like the Talons, but over the years I have gotten the message loud and clear that most people don't like Talons, see them as disruptive to their games and just generally only good for NPCs at best. There is a reason why in Forsaken the Predator Kings (a tribe clearly based on the Red Talons) were made an antagonist tribe.

    Shadow Lords- They should be a tribe of Honour, not Glory. Also, what IS their new glyph supposed to be?

    Silent Striders- Nothing much to comment on, their new glyph looks fine.

    Silver Fangs- From what I understand, the focus of W5 is very low level and the characters are struggling to survive. In that setting, the Silver Fangs make very little sense. The tribe is defined by their rulership and also their inner corruption. This tribe could be better used as an antagonist (and was in Forsaken.) Their new glyph is fine.

    These are just my thoughts on the matter.

      I've been thinking about how to make sense of the choices of verbs and what they say about how the Tribes are portrayed. And this is what I came up with:
      Glory Honor Wisdom
      Stealth Shadow Lords Bone Gnawers Glass Walkers
      Fight/Confront Black Furies Galestalkers Ghost Council
      Community (Get of Fenris) Silver Fangs Silent Striders
      Protect Fianna Red Talons Children Of Gaia
      Now the logic here is that there are two traits: The Renown and a specific way to express the Renown. The way I got the four categories on the left is:
      • Stealth are Tribes that have verbs about doing things secretly or using subterfuge (SL: scheme, BG: Eavesdrop, GW: Hack)
      • Fight/Confront are Tribes that look to engage enemies (BF: Overcome/Demolish, Gale: Ambush, GhoC: Subvert)
      • Community are Tribes that do something that benefits other Garou (SF: Inspire, SiSt: Deliver (ie being messengers))
      • Protect are Tribes that have verbs for defending (Fi: Protect, RT: Ward, CoG: Heal)


      Now as for Renowns, I think there are also similarities:
      • Glory Tribes have aspirational verbs about achieving some future goal (SL: Envision, BF: Avenge, Fi: Reclaim)
      • Honor Tribes are about contributing a service to Garou society (BG: Witness, GS: Track (ie tracking down enemies), SF: Motivate, RT: Purge)
      • Wisdom Tribes deal with the occult secrets of the world (including the Umbra) (GW: Research, GhoC: Unearth, SiSt: Scout, CoG: Puzzle Out)



      Not 100% sure about all of this but that's how I could envision what the new Tribes are like in W5. And I'm not really sure how to make this work without changing some Tribes massively.

                          OK.

                          So first off some general thoughts that go beyond each Tribe by itself:

                          With our current level of information, there's just too many Tribes. It's obviously they want to keep the original set in the setting (even if not all of them playable or in the core book anyway), but there's so many changes going on why some things are deemed important to keep and others aren't is hard to wrap your head around. Without something that more obviously delineates each Tribe's game-function to replace the ethno-geographic-cultural links that separated them before, it's very unclear why global anyone-can-in-theory-join organizations have so much overlap.

                          Conversely, there are too few Renown traits for this setup. Lots of them make sense, some don't make sense based on assumptions about how those Tribes were in the past but could make sense if the Tribe are heavily redone, and others seem very hard to justify no matter how you reinvision the Tribe (esp. without making the previous point worse). Long before W5 was even something anyone was thinking about, we had threads about three Renown categories not being enough just on how the old Renown setup really screwed over Galliards that didn't act like back-up Ahrouns (Galliards had a high Glory requirement, which makes a high-concept level of sense, but the books made the non-combat sources of Glory via inspiration, storytelling, etc. pittances in comparison).

                          The Favor/Ban thing sounds fine in the theory, but we need more examples. It's also hard to judge them without more examples, and knowing what the new Gifts look like and if Rites are even a mechanical thing. The Shadow Lord favor feels very lack luster if assumptions around what Theurges did in the past remain remotely viable. Even if we look past the glaring issue of North Wind being attached to the replacement Tribe for Younger Brother, it seems like one of those things that's basically either never real problem, or a gigantic pain in the ass,as it doesn't seem to have any dials attached to it (a common problem in the old WtA Tribe flaws). Again though there's glaring holes in what we know. How bad is it to break a ban? Is W5 moving the Garou in a similar direction as the Uratha where all of them have to hunt regularly and become more strict carnivores over time, thus North Wind is just making an aspect of that worse? Or is it really just going to stand out like a hangnail compared to the rest?

                          In general the glyph redesigns feel like change for change's sake. The Gnawer one is the only one that's an improvement to me (and it's just more legible now), the rest are either minor changes that don't seem to add anything, or overt changes I don't understand. It feels like there's a lack of interest in the glyphs as part of an overarching communication system though. The changes to the Clan icons and addition of Discipline ones in V5 is generally fine, because there isn't the implication that they're regular parts of vampire culture. You can imagine that vampires might slip such designs into larger ones as claims or threats normal people wouldn't get ("Hey, neonate, don't just go in there, look close, you can tell that's a Malkavian's place, and they're announcing that they're using Auspex to spy on people that go in") but you wouldn't expect those designs to be carved into trees or stones to relate more complex ideas.

                          By Tribe:

                          Black Furies - We just don't know enough about how they're being handled in the light of what else we know. I'm not opposed to them being slightly less radical feminist (I kind of liked how BNS WtA capped Rank for male Furies as it leans into the idea that membership isn't the same as equality), but we really need to see how the Tribe of Gorgon operates in this new context. Glory doesn't bother me for them, esp. if Glory finally does get better at the non-combat side of things. Gorgon feels like a strange change to me that I'll need to see more to pass judgement on beyond it being in stark contrast to the idea of less culturally linked Tribes. The new glyph isn't bad, but it's also bland and doesn't say anything about the Tribe.

                          Bone Gnawers - Another Tribe that needs a lot more previews on what on Gaia they do with the changes that are being presented to us. I really hope we're not returning to the old "jackal's curse" thing where Rat demands they're all poor, but it feels like treating joining the Tribe more as taking a vow of poverty than actually fighting against the oppression of the exploited classes is where we're headed thanks to that Honor pick. As I noted above, this is the one glyph I actually like.

                          Children of Gaia - Nothing to really go on yet. One of the Tribes is a pointlessly altered glyph but at least no one that causes oddities. I don't have a problem with them being Wisdom over Honor in theory, but it feels like what the Renown traits actually mean has changed enough that we'll have to see.

                          Fianna - Definitely wrong to keep the name. The glyph change is minor but bothers me because it adds a bunch of odd fine detail work to make, and it added a spiral of all things to it.

                          Galestalkers - Bad in the unavoidable comparison to Older Brother, otherwise one that really adds to the feeling of there are too many Tribes. The glyph doesn't communicate anything useful to me.

                          Ghost Council - Similar to the Galestalkers as they obviously only exist to replace Older Brother, but don't really seem to have a meaningful place with that context being removed, and another glyph that doesn't really seem to say anything. Horned Serpent meeting the absolute lowest bar of fixing Younger Brother's poor use of the uktena myth by translating uktena into English so you can't link it to one specific people any more.

                          Glass Walkers - Echoing the confusion about the Patron change.

                          Red Talons - Echoing the issues with justifying why the existing in the new setup and the odd choice of Renown trait. The minor change to the glyph is a great example of dismissing the idea that these are used as active forms of communication: why have two of the claw marks have a break in them, and who would make the motion to cause that?

                          Shadow Lords: Hopefully they can explain the glyph. I actually don't mind the idea of Glory here, because again I want to see Glory as boasting and self-aggrandizement that goes into a culture that puts focus on oral history being more prominent. I don't like the presumption that leadership = honor... politicians struggle with being honorable people.

                          Silent Striders: Another Tribe that seems like it wouldn't be here if not for legacy reasons. I don't like the glyph as being overly complex for a Tribe that still focuses on a lot of movement and travel. It's really complex if you think about trying to sit there are draw it with claws.

                          Silver Fangs: Glyph is the only thing to have a real thought about. The "it's a crown!" thing feels too on the nose, and it runs afoul of the idea that the Tribes aren't linked to cultures because crowns as marks of leadership isn't a universal thing in that sense. It's busy and complicated, and lacks the laconic nature of the old "we're in charge because we're powerful," one.

                                                      I think we need to be prepared for a significant shift in what the three Renown traits really mean in W5.

                                                      Sure, based on H5, we might just see some things stated and not line up at all with the mechanics, but if Renown is being handled significantly differently, what sorts of actions and lifestyles to associate with each of them is likely to change to.

                                                      The current selections can be made consistent in application for the most part... if you accept Justin's comments about things being changed and apply that to what exactly each represents.

                                                      For example, if we see a Tribe linked with Honor as an indication of a steadfast adherence to a duty or code of conduct in its membership over other considerations, it makes more sense why those four Tribes are the ones that have it compared to the more personal Honor of the older editions. If the new version of Honor focuses on accepting less ideal lifestyles because it's for the good of the many, the Gnawers are honorable for watching the places none of the others will, the Galestalkers and Red Talons are honorable for living off the grid to protect places without adopting tools that might defile them in the long run, and the Silver Fangs get to play on the One Great Man tropes where at personal sacrifice they accept the duty to make the hard call so nobody else has to suffer for that decision. All of that is a conceivably coherent take on Honor, even if a very different one from older WtA.

                                                      There's also a design level thing I can respect that if the Children of Gaia, Shadow Lords and Silver Fangs are the "leadership" Tribes, that each having a different Renown help differentiate their different approaches to leading: The Coggies want to lead through intellect and thoughtful decision making (Wisdom), the ShLs want to lead by crafting a narrative that makes them the best choice (Glory), and the Silver Fangs want to lead because of a sense of duty to endure the weight of the crown (Honor). If they're all Honor, it's less appealing in a few ways.
