    *What*?

    What?

    That is some very slanted, very ultra-biased bullshit somebody crammed down your RPG throat. And I am very sorry to read it. How utterly bizarre.

    "The garou are not sentient because their behaviour is controlled by a dice roller" -- Yeah, and? Of course they are sentient. The fact that dice are used to sometimes mimic the conflict between the various aspects of themselves (beast vs. man, et al) doesn't mean they aren't sentient -- it's just a game mechanic tool to help guide/simulate roleplaying. Or do your friends think that vampires (with their Humanity/Paths, and various Virtues) *also* aren't sentient, as they have a similar (if thematically different) "dice-roller" aspect? (Your friend(s) are nuts.)

    "The[y're] unnatural, because of their supernatural abilit[ies] and the fact they cannot create healthy offspring with their own kind" -- Unnatural? -- a matter of thematic perspective. In the Real World(tm), they would be unnatural, true, but that is neither here-nor-there in the WoD, where all sorts of critters exist. In *that* world, they are, in fact, "natural" in the context that the laws of Reality(tm) allow them to exist and do what they do, sans an "proper" breaking of scientific understanding (the "science" of WoD hasn't caught up with the Science(!) of WoD, which easily understands how Garou, and other Fera, exist). As far as reproduction, that is a human-bias if there ever was one: the whole point of their cross-reproduction is for the *uniting* of two aspects of life on Earth (Gaia/the Middle Umbra); that is, it *IS* natural for them to reproduce the way they do (alternating between human and lupine lineage/parentage), and unnatural for them to directly breed with each other (which is contextually the same as two full-siblings having offspring together... save that the spiritual aspect of the Garou ensures that such a coupling *will* result in "something wrong" with the Metis that results, whereas the incest of two siblings "merely" has a high chance of genetic defect). (Your friend(s) are nuts.)

    "A bunch of bullshit about how [d]ogs have a definite [evolutionary] advantage over wolves , how tool use IS an evolutionary advantage, and a bunch of other garbage." Again, bizarre. Dogs have advantages over wolves primarily because of their domestication by man, *not* because of any inherent genetic superiority over wolves (save, again, for the domestication factor that is present in most dogs). Wolves are on the butt-end of things, currently, because of man, primarily, and not because of any other non-man-made factors. As far as tool use being an evolutionary advantage... well, *duh*, of course it is. But the thematics, as expressed in WW, tweak that concept a bit because the supernatural/paranormal is confirmable as "real" in that setting, and therefore the "tool-use" of the werewolves (fetishes, glaives, and the like) are mirror-valid to the tool-use of man, meta-conceptually. (Those two groups obviously disagree vehemently on various salient points, but the *concept* is valid for both.) (Your friend(s) are nuts.)

    "oh [and] some insanity about how environmentalism is the product of people who are not struggling to survive, and often considered gross [hypocrisy] by those who are." -- This is bullshit on a level that is nauseating to the extreme. I am not even going to directly respond to it because YOUR FRIEND(S) ARE NUTS....


    "ANOTHER [piece] of bullshit she had, was that lupus should be [atheistic], since animals don[']t have religion...." -- *Animals* may not, but *Garou* (as a sentient species) certainly could/do. (Your friend(s) are nuts.)

    "Oh and [an]argument that Garou are INFERIOR to humanity that I found really really sick" -- Well, if I didn't have the above context of where your friend(s) were coming from, I 'd ask for an elaboration on this point because -- meta-conceptually -- there *is* a valid argument that can be put forth relative to that, if one delves deep into the metaphysics of the WoD and looks at things from a certain POV... however, given the further up contexts, I can safely assume that whatever would be said by her would be pure bullshit... leading me to the conclusion that your friend(s) are nuts....

    *****

    The Garou, as a group, are as much anti-heroes as heroes, good guys as bad guys, know what they're doing vs. fucking up left-right-and-center, as most any other splat in the WoD. That is, many/most of them are trying to do what they *believe* is right and correct in order to save the world/Gaia... it's just that, like mortal man (and many other splats), they can be (and often are) wrong about salient details, have their own personal biases, and otherwise aren't as wise or understanding as they should be. That doesn't make them villains right off the bat -- it just makes them flawed relative to their goals/ideals. Not unlike most sentient beings in general.

    They can easily be heroes, and many are, from a narrative POV, and the game setting, as presented, assumes that relative to their individual and cultural understandings. Doesn't mean they don't/won't make mistakes, approach things from a mistaken perspective, or at times fall to the lure/rage of Dark Forces(tm) -- but by-and-large, they are the heroes (or at least protagonists) of their setting. Any other view ignores the narratives and themes presented in their (and other) books.

        At the risk of starting another two-month argument with various other posters, and slightly off topic, I'm not sure I'd agree with that assessment (I do agree that the Talons' extra-curricular activities make them the villains, and no amount of self-justification or compensation can change that). While the Talons, Shadow Lords, and Get were the worst of the bunch, I've always looked upon the entirety of the Garou Nation (with the possible exception of the Stargazers) as being part of the problem too. In the above scenario, the Walkers probably wouldn't do anything unless the Talons did it in their immediate territory (and if they did, it would be to use Gifts and Rites to plant / create false evidence, or to intimidate witnesses into silence, and otherwise inhibit the authorities, rather than do anything against the Talons). The Gnawers probably wouldn't care unless it was a bus of poor, inner-city schoolkids, or BG Kinfolk. The Furies are also pretty apathetic when it comes to the Talons' mischief -- the two tribes are more often than not in lock-step about taking modern technological society down a notch. The Children are probably the biggest bunch of hypocrites. They preach the whole non-violence and peace-with-the-humans line, but they know exactly what the Talons are. Still, they are also dedicated to inter-tribal unity (including with the Talons), so when anything that the Talons do backfires, the Children are more than ready to give them healing and places to hide, rest and recuperate. Even when the Red Talons led the pogrom against the Bunyip and utterly exterminated them, the Children of Gaia kept on banging the unity-drum -- and then snapped up all the now-vacant caerns that they could get their paws on.

        Now, of course, the argument there is that the Garou need every tribe and every single last Garou warrior in the fight against the Wyrm, so they can't waste precious resources in "pointless" internecine fighting. It sort of highlights the complexity of the situation facing the Garou. But nonetheless, it brings to mind the old adage: All that Evil needs to flourish is for good men to do nothing.

        I have a hard time thinking of the Garou as heroic when half of them seem to think that murdering innocent people is perfectly okay, and the other half are too apathetic, contemptuous, indifferent, or just plain wrapped up in their own ideological B-S to do anything beyond the occasional mild verbal rebuke.
        Entirely out of emotional reasons, simple pragmatism and the Litany would prevent any of those tribes being apathetic towards this.

        Veil Shall not be Lifted- Pretty simple, such a killing would risk the Veil.

        Respect Those Beneath Ye, all are Gaia- Even if some tribes argue against it, this is still the law. Needless killing of humans is not allowed, and any CoG, Gnawer or Walker philodox worth their salt would get the Talon for breaking this tenet.
