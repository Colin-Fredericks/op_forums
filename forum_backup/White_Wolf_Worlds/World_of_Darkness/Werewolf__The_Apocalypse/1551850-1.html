                    The problem is that this is exactly how we got W5 in the first place, regardless if we don't like how Paradox did it.

                    Werewolf isn't Goth-Punk and never has been, to begin with. Paradox thought instead of rolling with the game as is, they were smarter than the game's actual fan-base and could make it better by making it more Vampire. They recreated the setting following their own ideas of what the game should be without any regard for what people who already liked the game think.

                    Paradox's whole mess comes from forcing into the game their own limited views of what's the right way of being WoD. Which was already a disaster for Vampire, but was even worse for a game that, lo and behold, isn't Vampire.

                    How do you expect to tackle the problem with the same attitude and get another result? You can do marginally better by not being as moronic in many issues as they are, but that's about it.

                    I'm all in for bringing back the old, pre-5th ed, setting. I'm also all in for keeping the current setting and doing with it the best we can, as it has its own fans, unless those fans want something else. Just ignoring the current fan base seems to me like making the same mistake again out of spite, and this very thread is proof that at least sometimes that's exactly that. Now, trying to "solve" the current setting by doing the very same path again?

                    For whatever it's worth, a similar but more respectful take is also exactly what the New World of Darkness, currently Chronicles of Darkness, is. If the idea is to just have a brand new setting, this boat sailed 20 years ago and did very well, except for the strong resistance of cWoD fans who wouldn't change settings at any cost.

                                The WoD does not need another edition, and likely wouldn't need another anniversary rehaul for a long time. Its philosophy was really more in line with having a spiritual sequel; an effort by fans influenced by it, turned game designers, to hit a lot of the notes of the WoD games, tweaked to also account for the new creators' points of view and what's important to them.

                                Continuing the WoD on with new editions is even more corporate a decision than the nakedly terrible corporate reason for buying the IP from CCP is, because someone thought that Bloodlines 2 would be a slam dunk and gateway into their own Marvel EU, only to find that nothing was so easy, especially when you hire people who kind of hate every part of said universe but Vampire and think that it absolutely needs to be more like Vampire.

                                The WoD is dead. It died in 2004 with the Time of Judgment. The 20th anniversary editions were just a QOL and equivalent of old games ported to new platforms to archive the experience for the future. We should all learn to move on and utilize bits of it in new games with new settings.
