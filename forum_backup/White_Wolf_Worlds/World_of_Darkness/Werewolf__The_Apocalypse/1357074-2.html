        Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
        Which parts specifically, out of curiousity?
        When the game first began, it made an attempt to include elements of classic werewolf tales of gothic legend, books, and movies such as Lunatics. The emphasis was very much on the real world myths and legends in the setting as well as unique creations and ideas. First edition Storyteller's Guide had many pages of antagonists which were based on urban folklore. DNA was just an example of a group that could act as hunters against werewolves (with both a motive and a semi-plausible high tech devices power set within the setting).

        In Revised, much of what tied the game to actual werewolf lore (real folklore and popular culture) was abandoned, and the Garou became much more of its own thing with few ties to real lore, but entirely sitting in unique White Wolf elements. DNA became a force of the Weaver instead of just a high tech group of hunters. And the subject of the setting stopped being the real world and increasingly high fantasy elements not found in real world, and not obviously derived from them.

        And of course, there was emphasis on individual characters being flawed, and the various tribes and septs were flawed too. These were real people. The Curse was frequently mentioned (although in terms of character stats, it lead to a lot of contradictory nonsense between the stats and character description). Personality flaws and failures of leadership were embedded in individual characters.

        Look at Rage Across New York. Mother Larissa is clearly failing in sept duties as her entire sept outside the BGs are formenting a rebellion against her. Man Eating is tolerated by the tribe in a "don't ask, don't tell" kind of way. They have deals with several groups of vampires. (The Bone Gnawers simply do not come out good in early editions.) Septs and tribes are plotting against each other. Various individuals besides the BGs are making deals with groups of vampires. There are plenty of unsympathetic Garou.

        In Revised, things clearly shift into Lawful Good versus Chaotic Evil mode. Problematic camps and individuals get removed offstage (although all this happens with septs and characters we've never seen before; the game never addressed the huge failures of leadership with Mother Larissa, probably because she was too much of a high profile BG to suffer such a fate). Game stops being about the interactions between these flawed characters, and telling gamers to get on the railroad so they can collect their plot coupons.

        In short, in first edition you could introduce a new player to the game, and he could find lots of things he would expect from his knowledge of popular culture and folklore. In Revised, if you introduced a new player to the game, there'd be very little he'd recognize other than you have people who can turn into wolves.

            -In general I've had the idea stirring for awhile that rumors of 'Cryptid Breeds' would be an interesting thing for the setting. I don't mean like Bigfoot or Mothman (although...) but that the modern changing breeds spread rumors of vestigial breeds that remain. Corax trade rumors of were-fish sightings from Indonesia, rural Canadian Gurahl occasionally report seeing were-badgers treading old ancestral paths, their are Russian Silver Fangs who swear by Helios that they've seen were-falcons in the skies overhead.

            Whether or not there is any validity to these claims is the domain of the ST naturally, but with the Kami and such, I simply think the rumored possibility adds some interesting flavor to the setting.

                A new idea that's appealed to me:

                BIRTHRIGHTS OF THE TRIBES
                •Once the tribes were one great people without nation or divide, all one brood under Wolf. Yet one by one they took totems and divided into tribes, each with specific homelands and broods and heritages. From those days onwards, the tribes recognized their own and the land recognizes them as well.

                •Tribes gain -1 difficulty to actions taken in relation to their heritage. For example: The Fianna. Within the lands of Celts, they have a -1 difficulty to step across the gauntlet. They have a -1 difficulty on social rolls with those bound by spirituality or blood to their Celtic homeland. Spirits of their homeland are also considered to be positively inclined towards them, offering a similar -1 difficulty. However this does have an inverse at times that The Delirium effects those same people (assuming those people were effected by The Impergium) as though they had one fewer permanent willpower.

                •Exceptional Tribes:
                -Mediterranean women are granted a resistance of one higher WP against Black Fury Delirium while men suffer as though they lost a second additional WP.
                -Bone Gnawers treat their homeland as the Middle East and Levant, however aside from a -1 difficulty to step sideways in the Middle East as well as in Urban Environments (with -2 in Urban Middle Eastern Environments) and lose most other benefits unless they are those miraculous few with the Blood of The Jackal, Pure Breed, or Ancestors.
                -Children of Gaia treat their material home land as the Indus River Valley and India proper, which all of the tribe gain, yet only those with CoG Pure Breed or Ancestors gain the other benefits.
                -Fianna additionally gain a -1 to Social rolls with fae/changelings of European descent.
                -Glass Walkers share a homeland and urban feature with the Bone Gnawers.
                -Red Talons treat their homelands as whichever rural forest from Eastern Europe to Siberia that their kin have occupied. They do not gain the benefits in relations to humans, barring the extremely rare cases of the exceptional humans with a noteworthy amount of Neanderthal and other lost tribes of humanity within their blood, which Griffin calls out to.
                -Silent Striders have naturally lost their connection to their homeland of Egypt, yet humans of Egyptian heritage still recognize them spiritually.

                                    My own take has always been that one of the aspects of the Gothic-Punk part of the setting is that there is - and probably has been since the late 50s to one degree or another - a sharp divide between the mainstream culture, with its blockbuster action films, low brow comedies, and kids movies, its cookie cutter sitcoms, lurid soap operas, and crass reality shows, its popcorn thriller novels and melodramatic teen fiction, its flavor of the minute pop stars and so on, and the counterculture of small press/independent films, fiction, and music. The mainstream is very bland, very banal, and very conformist (with the value of what is considered "conformist" changing from decade to decade, but it always comes back to accepting what the people in positions of power and expertise tell you is so), while the counterculture rebels against that, sometimes mindlessly, sometimes trying to get you to question your own preconceptions (sometimes even asking if your "rebellion" has just become another form of conformity).

                                    And my own head canon sometimes insists that the original Terminator film was given limited release and then suppressed by the studios, only to become an underground cult film from traded bootleg VHS copies. Probably the same thing happening to Near Dark as well.

                                    Added: Also possibly that Prince was known entirely for his club concerts and underground mix tapes among the Midwest.
