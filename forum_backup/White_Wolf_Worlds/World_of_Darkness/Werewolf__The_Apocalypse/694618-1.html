        Ok, so the thing about elders disfavoring the month-long route to learning Gifts is because it's dangerous, not because of an unreasonable bias like I at first thought. That makes sense. And this sort of companionship between teacher and student has led to more than a few metis being conceived? Wow, that's something I hadn't considered. It makes sense after a fashion, though.

        It's probably an unconscious bias on my part, but whenever I envision a mentor-student relationship, it's always an older man teaching a younger male student so the romantic element doesn't enter into my thinking at all. But (with some tribal and/or sept) exceptions, the Garou females fight alongside their male brethren as equals, so it's entirely possible for a mentor-student relationship to be male-female or female-male. (Homosexual liasons among Garou teacher-student relationships may or may not exist, but they don't lead to metis being conceived, so I'll leave that alone.) So it's entirely possible for romantic feelings to crop up in that situation. In light of that, I have to disagree with Asmodai about Litany breaches in this sort of situation being excessive, as he called it. Considering that Garou best relate to other Garou before they can relate to humans or wolves, it makes sense that romantic feelings might crop up in a Garou mentor-student relationship. Just another facet of the tragic nature of the Garou, I suppose.

                                        The legend about the first gift (in one of the anthologies iirc, Drums around the fire maybe), states that if you see a spirit do something (or possibly only suspect it is able to do something) you can petition to get that power taught...

                                        So, being instructed by a member of a given tribe doesn't seem necessary to lean a gift. Keeping secret what spirit teaches a certain gift certainly seems to be a way to protect tribal gifts, but a skilled theurge or pretty much anyone who is as creative as the author who designed the gift for the rulebook can come up with a spirit that logically should be able to bestow the feat to the character - so keeping something secret pretty much everyone could figure out on his own ain't that effective.

                                        Whether or not spirits enforce the tribal tie regarding their gifts is unknown. It might be the case if a gift is taught by a vassal of the tribal totem, but for more independent spirits it might be possible, that the gifts are taught regardless.

                                        Finally, it could be the case that spirits find it to exhausting to bestow gifts to people you don't have a natural knack for them and therefore need the extra convincing. If the rules regarding the instruction ability are applied to gifts, the spirit also would need to invest more time. But since the time to teach gifts is a lot less than other teaching the rules should not be used without conversion.
