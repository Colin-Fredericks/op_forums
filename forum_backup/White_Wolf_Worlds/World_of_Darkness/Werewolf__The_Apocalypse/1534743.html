    Fetishes/talens as gift replacement

    04-25-2024, 08:58 PM
    One of my pc play as a Bone Gnawer Ragabash. He like to create thing like sharp metal whip, deployable shield and so on for the other pc.

    He recently learn the rite to create Talen and discovered Paws of the Newborn Cub gift. As there's no silver fang pc and no one at rank 5 this gift is out of range for them, but he had the idea to create Talens that could give them access to those inaccessible gifts.
    As the st i don't know what to think of the idea, on one side a talen is a 1 time usage and must be create with correct materials, on the other hand give them access to any gift that way seems a bit... overpowered?

    Secondly if i were to make a weaker version of that kind of gift would the duration be fixed or define by the activation roll (and in that case how to fix the difficulty since it should be the willpower of the target as a gift and the fetishe gnosis as a fetishe)?

      Typically Talens are not at the level of a rank 5 Gift, and usually do not duplicate a Gift's exact powers. Talens tend to be useful, but not very powerful, magic. A Talen that would approximate the power of Paws of the Newborn Cub (removing supernatural powers from an opponent for one turn) seems overpowered to me. That Gift also requires the Garou spend two Gnosis, while Talens just require a Gnosis roll to activate. I would just tell your player a flat "No" on this.

      I would allow a Talen that would make a specific type of power more difficult for one's supernatural opponent to use. There are several examples of Sanctified Plants (which are essentially Talens). that add a +2 Difficulty to a specific type of magic (meaning faerie magic, vampire disciplines, etc.). There's also an example of a sanctified plant forbidding a specific kind of creature if they fail a willpower roll against the spirit's Gnosis (sanctified wolfsbane against Garou, sanctified garlic against vampires, etc.).

                  A big balancing factor for Fetishes is their Gnosis rating and if their effect is based on how many successes you roll.

                  Any Fetish can be activated by spending 1 temp Gnosis, but this only counts as having rolled 1 success if that matters. For a lot of them, it doesn't, as their effect is binary.

                  If you want to make a Fetish that can suppress a target's supernatural powers, you can require a number of successes equal to the level of powers you can deny. Spending a Gnosis would only stop level 1 powers, while you'd need to roll for better. Gnosis 7 or 8 (the difficulty of the activation roll) would make stopping higher level powers very unlikely even for perm Gnosis 10 PCs.

                  Duration is also a big factor, as scene-long powers are easier to use without running afoul of the Rage + Gnosis ban. If you have to activate a Talen or Fetish every action you want it to impact, then you can't spend Rage and use it at the same time.

                  Crossover is a bit more complicated for real balance than this example, but these are ways to accommodate ideas without a no or a useless item.

                              For anything homebrew, your table matters more than we do in the end.

                              The books generally treat Gnosis 8 as the highest for Fetishes/Talens as that's supposed to be for the most potent Fetishes. If the PC as Gnosis 10, they're averaging 2 successes per activation at diff 8 (pesky 1s), so diff 9 makes bothering to roll pretty meaningless. At that point it seems to me it should just be Gnosis 8 and have a flat duration so the choice is spend Gnosis or roll to save Gnosis but have a chance of failing.

                              While yes, you don't want to completely overstep on the Gift, you also don't want a Fetish that's worthless. Crafting high end Fetishes isn't exactly easier either. If you want a decent chance of it working you're putting a lot of RP effort into it (or dropping a lot of permanent Gnosis and thus XP into it).

                                  Originally posted by Cain Loup-Noir View Post
                                  The main concern of my pc is that he cannot be very usefull in combat because of it's low dice pool and his tribe does not give him a lot shine in that matter while 2 other pc (including a Fenris) are monster in combat.
                                  What is the real issue for the player? Is it that he regrets not building a non-combat character and hopes creating fetishes is a way for him to become one? Or is he not able to contribute to the game because the things he did design the character to do are not coming up in the chronicle?

                                  I'd be reluctant to let a character have access to a level 5 gift simply because as a non-combat character he wants to be as effective as a combat character. I don't think that is a good way to approach fetishes and talens in general. Their creation and use should be their own part of the game, not a stopgap measure because a player wishes they had more or better powers. Besides while the ST can handwave a lot of the effort and time spent to creating such a powerful fetish, in many games this would take a long time that it would not be a good solution to make the character feel useful now.

                                  Is he generally satisfied with the game, but just feels bored when combat comes up when he has nothing to do? If so, maybe all you need to do is redesign how you handle combat encounters by making sure there is some specific task he can do during the combat while the other PCs dogpile on something. Maybe there are lesser powerful foes you can add that he can take on, or he can do some other contribution based on the Gifts and Abilities he do have (fighting oriented or not).

                                  Not knowing anything about the exact situation in your chronicle makes it hard to comment, but at first glance it seems to me that maybe the chronicle needs to include encounters and scenarios that allow that PC to shine while the combat characters don't have much to contribute in order to balance things.

                                  One thing I notice about many games that allow for a wide divergence in powers and abilities is that combat specialized characters can easily hijack the game. They don't find many things challenging so the GM escalates and pumps up the combat encounter to challenge those specific characters, but that in turn makes the other characters feel even more useless. Werewolf, in particular, often has a concentration on combat (Glory renown) that causes short shrift to encounters and scenarios that prize solutions that lead to Honor and Wisdom renown. D&D style play that emphasizes combat often causes PCs of certain auspices to feel neglected. Combat encounters are obvious to an ST. Encounters or scenarios that would lead to Honor and Wisdom are not as obvious, and some STs struggle with that.
