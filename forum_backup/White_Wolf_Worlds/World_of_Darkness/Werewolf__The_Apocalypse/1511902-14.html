    Rank 5 & 6 Ragabash Gifts

    Thieving Talons of the Magpie: I think it's easy to get a bit confused with this one.

    Think of a random enemy. A mighty BSD Alpha. One of the Urge Wyrms itself. A Duke or Duchess of Malfeas. A Nexus Crawler.

    Now imagine depriving them of a single supernatural ability, one carefully chosen for best effect. You could steal a Spirit's 're-form' Charm, which may be useless to you, but would stop them from escaping. You could rob a Mage or Vampire of whatever ritual they are performing. You could steal a Nexus Crawler's ability to reshape reality to its will, and turn that power to your own designs.

    But here's the problem; you have to get THREE SUCCESSES on a Wits + Larceny roll, difficulty of the target's Willpower, and for every turn you want to keep the power in your possession you have to spend a Gnosis!

    This is sadly Red, despite its immense potential. It won't reliably work on any power worth stealing, because it will belong to someone with decent Willpower.

    My fix would be to make the theft last for one scene by default, and be extended by a whole scene if you spend a point of Gnosis. The Gift should require you to pluck something from the target as if you were pickpocketing them, and thus should be quite easy if you can take them unawares. So the test would either be a Wits + Larceny, difficulty determined by the ST ('stealing' the power as if it were a wallet in their pocket).

    Thousand Forms: This Gift lets you take the form of any animal within a size range of the smallest birds up to bison. You spend a Gnosis and roll Intelligence + Animal Ken, difficulty 6. For the rest of the scene you can roll to transform without spending Gnosis. Turning into a supernatural animal such as a Griffon or Pegasus or Unicorn requires a difficulty 9 roll and costs 2 Gnosis rather than 1.

    This is... neat. The stronger version of the Gift is very hard to use, but turning into a Dragon with all of your Gifts is a pretty neat trick. The main appeal is the flexibility granted by being able to turn into any number of disguises and utility forms. Posing as a rat, lizard, raven or big cat could have any number of effects when it comes to interacting with Fera. This Gift allows a Garou to become a fluid problem-solver who can remain out of reach, slipping into tiny spaces, blending into the environment, producing venoms, charming wild beasts, showing respect to spirits, playing tricks, performing assassinations, and so on. The supernatural side of the power could be better, but it's usable; if you get lucky it could produce a pretty spectacular result!

    And, for Legendary Ragabash...

    Firebringer*: Ultimately useless to you, personally, but potentially very impactful to the Garou Nation.

    In theory.

    Firstly it needs to be a power which can be used ON you. There are many great and wonderful abilities in the WoD which this could work with, such as a Nexus Crawler's reality-bending or a powerful yet corrupt Gift of the BSDs, the memory-altering powers of Dominate, the purification of Pyretics, the total rebuilding of a person or thing which comes with Naming, or even simply being able to learn a True Name with a glance. But you, the Garou in question, can never learn this ability. Instead you spend a point of permanent Gnosis and become the first teacher of this 'new gift', which winds up having a level and spirit-teacher the ST thinks is appropriate. And again, it has to be an ability which can be targeted onto a person; many, many powers of the WoD will be out of reach as a result.

    So you perform a great sacrifice for the Garou Nation, and now new Gift exists. But is the Garou Nation in trouble because it doesn't have the right gifts? In my honest opinion the Garou Nation is in trouble because every time it checks whether its gun is loaded, it does so by shooting itself in the paw.

    This one is ultimately impossible to rate, which is fine. If you have a NPC use it, great, if you have a PC use it for the good of the Pack and the whole of the Garou Nation, great. There are some really impressive Fera Gifts which would be amazing to import using this power! It would be very good, however, to have an example. Pick a pretty neat Gift and mention that it was first created using this power, to give some context for its history in the setting. This goes unrated, for obvious reasons.

        Rank 5 & 6 Theurge Gifts

        Feral Lobotomy: Just use Beast Mind. You are a Garou, you can kill your enemies, doing a really hard roll and spending loads of Gnosis to permanently kneecap someone's Intelligence doesn't matter.

        Malleable Spirit: Now this is what I want. A Garou and a Spirit enter into a resisted roll of Gnosis vs Gnosis, with no cost or restrictions on when it can be used.

        The Spirit's difficulty is the Garou's Gnosis. The Garou's difficulty is based on what they're trying to do. Shifting dots around is difficulty 6 (e.g. a spirit could be calmed by having its Rage funneled into Willpower and Gnosis), changing disposition is difficulty 7 (e.g. a spirit could be made to love the Theurge or fear them, or hate the pack they guide), and entirely changing the type of Spirit is difficulty 9 (turning a mighty Bane into a Gaian spirit).

        This is exceptional, but to be quite honest it's too good, especially at extremes of Gnosis. If you had a Gnosis of 10 you can expect to roll 10 dice against a difficulty of 6-9, resisted by 1-10 dice against a difficulty of 10. There's nothing stopping you from trying again, nothing stopping you from using the Gift repeatedly, and at this point Spirits become clay you can remold at will. This can partly be controlled by the fact that the Umbra is full of many billions of spirits and that messing with the ecosystem unwisely has consequences. A Theurge who used this on, say, one of the Wyrm's most favoured servants would become public enemy #1!

        This is very obviously Green, but it requires some kind of guideline or limitation. The simplest one is to say that the spirit must be in some way willing, even if under duress. Flat outright refusal means this power won't work, but a Spirit which has promised to submit to this ability if they are defeated, outwitted, or break their word etc. would be a valid target. That would result in a situation where a common use is to offer this as a 'way out' to a spirit when they are at the brink of total defeat (though not all spirits care about such things, as many lack individuality), or to bargain with a spirit so that they can be transformed (e.g. many Banes are shortsighted and driven by immediate, ravenous desires. There are likely Banes which would agree to be turned into Naturae in exchange for sufficient Chiminage, since all that matters to them is that they get to fulfil their desires, and it's unlikely that they will understand how their desires will be changed by being transformed.

        Ultimate Argument of Logic: This Gift convinces someone of a fact, true or false, about the nature of reality. Manipulation + Performance requiring 3 successes to work, and the difficulty is the target's Wits + Enigmas, so a difficulty of 7+ is pretty believable. For Spirits you target Gnosis (Wits), which could be 10. For important Fera and Garou you might face a difficulty as low as 4, or as high as 8, who knows.

        But you need 3 successes, and the result is... potentially pretty big, but hard to define. Making a Pentex exec (perhaps Wits 4, Enigmas 0) believe that Pentex is about to go bust and that he needs to dump his stocks immediately could form a pretty important part of a plan! So if you try to use this Gift on targets with a high difficulty, expect disappointment. But it's cost-free gaslighting which you can spam against the whole world of low-Gnosis spirits and no-Enigmas mortals and supernaturals.

        The wording of the Gift is a little odd; you convince people of an 'aspect of existence', true or false, and the examples given are pretty cosmic and big. Can the Gift work for things like "I am your brother", or "you need to sell your stocks now?" I don't think it can unfortunately! It's more for big, giant, all-encompassing things like "Capitalism is bad".

        When you have the Capitalism is Bad power in your back pocket, ready to convert most of the people you run into, you do then come across the problem of the power changing things about the setting too easily. NPC Rank 5 BSD Theurges can, RAW, walk around telling Garou that Gaia has betrayed them, that Gaia is dead, and that the only goal which can be accomplished is trying to survive the Apocalypse. That doesn't sound like a fun ability to be on the receiving end of for a player!

        The lack of a cost, or limitations, or duration, or anything, makes this one a bit of a mess. It's arguably incredibly strong, it won't work at all if the difficulty is high most of the time, but when it does work it could allow you to completely change the expectations, beliefs and allegiances of far too many spirits, supernaturals and mortals.

        "It's a Rank 5 power, you need to be wise and sensible to access it!" is an argument that can be made for any silly Gift at this Rank, and I get why people make it, but you do need to account for PCs being driven by players, and for characters going insane, winding up corrupted, or having their normal patience stripped away by an absolute emergency. That's before getting into the 'Wisdom' Renown of the Garou Nation having a tangential relationship with being wise in a conventional sense.

        I'll call this one a wash and leave Ultimate Argument of Logic unrated. It needs significant reworking.

        As In The Beginning: Tearing a hole in the Gauntlet and allowing a horde of friendly spirits through is always gonna be good. It isn't well-defined but it's very strong and it works well. There really isn't much to say about this one beyond the mechanics making sense (a cost of 3 Gnosis works, the difficulty matching the local Gauntlet works), and it being a Wits + Occult roll adds up pretty well with the stats you'll likely have invested in to reach Rank 6 as a Crescent Moon.

        Reducing the Gauntlet by 1 in the area, permanently, is a fun bonus. That alone grants this power some neat utility which could make things better or worse. Given how hellish the Penumbra is in many places, making the Gauntlet weaker could be a very bad idea! The flavour opportunities for how this Gift might vary by Tribe are also nice.

            Rank 5 & 6 Philodox Gifts

            Geas: Enforcing a Geas and binding a target, or a group, to fulfil some task is a pretty nice high-level ability. We are told that the request cannot go against the target's nature, so presumably you cannot task a group of BSDs with destroying the Wyrm.

            We aren't really given any indication of how attempting to resist the compulsion works. Is it literally impossible? Sure, easy to go "yes, it's literally impossible" but how does that manifest? What happens if you're slow? What happens if you don't give the mission your all? This is one of these things where a lot more information is needed.

            The cost and the roll are fine, but this remains unrated as it doesn't actually give much of a guideline at all. In trying to implement this Gift, the ST essentially ends up writing their own homebrew version of the power, since the Gift is incomplete.

            Wall of Granite: While it's hard to picture this Gift playing out, since all I can see is horrible uncanny CGI, this Gift is pretty neat. You create a small barrier of stone which can completely protect you and a number of allies up to your Gnosis score, which also moves with you! With a Soak of 10 dice and 15 health levels it's very unlikely that it will come to harm, though personally I would have put a minimum threshold for damage.

            A single Gnosis point is spent, and there is no roll; the wall simply forms. I love this because even if it breaks you could erect another one! This Gift is amazing for protecting your Pack or defenseless allies, holding a position, protecting an important object, or buying time to escape via Stepping Sideways or some other method of transport. The uses are pretty flexible and even in an outright fight this grants you a great defensive option to hide behind while spirits or mind controlled enemies cause chaos outside.

            Break the Bonds: Immunity to supernatural coercion, Strength 15 when dealing with 'bonds' to herself and others, and for free you can banish the mental bonds others are held by. This requires a roll, Manipulation + Leadership at a difficulty of 11 - the target's WP, but you could free a lot of supernatural slaves in the WoD using this method. Spirits bound into slavery, supernaturals and mortals held by the blood bond, victims of Dominate and even those under a Geas. This Gift makes you the potential worst enemy of the Ventrue, Silver Fangs, Sidhe and so on, which could be great fun.

            This Gift has an absoluteness to it which I expect at levels 5 and 6.

                Rank 5 & 6 Galliard Gifts

                Fabric of the Mind: With no innate cost (we'll get to that!) you can imagine, storytell and dream a new being into existence. This being is limited only by your imagination and your successes, with an extended Intelligence + Performance slowly adding more and more dots of Attributes. You could, in theory, tell the tale of the mightiest Garou to ever live, granting this imagined being a Gnosis, Rage, Willpower of 10, 10 in every Attribute, and a plethora of Abilities, then send them into battle to win a glorious victory for Gaia.

                There's a problem with this, however.

                We're not told what happens when you Fail or Botch. We're not told how getting Gifts, Charms or other Traits onto this creature works; is a Level 3 Gift worth 3 successes? What about Charms? What about innate features of a being you might describe, like a Spirit being ephemeral or a Garou having regeneration?

                And then there's the difficulty. Difficulty 8. There may well be difficulty breaks you can use here, and a Performance spec in Storytelling would help for 10s granting 2 successes, but come on. Unless Failing or Botching does nothing at all (which would be very silly) that high base difficulty is going to cut your legend short.

                My version would be to sum up all of your Social and Mental Attributes, Willpower and Gnosis, along with your dots in Empathy, Performance, Subterfuge, Expression, Intimidation and Etiquette, to get a total number. Bit of faff to add them all up, but once you've done it you've done it, and the number will only change when you improve one of those Traits. That number is a value which you can distribute between Traits for anything you create, with a base of 1 in all Attributes already applied. You roll Intelligence + Performance once, with the difficulty determined by what you're describing. A mortal or Kinfolk is difficulty 5, a Garou is difficulty 6, another supernatural being is difficulty 7, and a legendary creature is difficulty 8. You allocate stats and the being you've evoked comes with anything you'd expect it to have by default, such as regeneration, stepping sideways, flight or what have you. It lasts for one scene per success.

                Anyways, back to the RAW Gift. The creature lasts for 1 scene if you spend 1 Gnosis, and so on, with you being able to spend Gnosis as it goes to keep it going. The horror of your creation potentially becoming aware of its impending death and begging you to keep it alive aside, this Gift could be great, but is held back by a lack of thought and testing. As written, it's Red, though if you roll nothing but 10s with the right specialty you can expect to create a being beyond compare. That simply isn't statistically reasonable however.

                Head Games: A Gift of complete emotional control, Galliard Elders can completely rewrite the emotions of a target with a Manipulation + Empathy check against a difficulty of the target's Willpower. You could make someone look upon their own child with utter disgust, or with love they've never felt. Given the problems Garou have regarding the other Tribes, their Kin, and their feelings towards the Fera, this Gift could be responsible for great good (moral qualms aside!) or great evil.

                The implanted emotions don't last beyond the scene itself, but they can stick around if reinforced. Basically this Gift could permit a breakthrough where one would otherwise be impossible. Keep in mind that emotions are not mind control, however. A BSD who feels warmly towards you might decide that you are destined for the spiral, or some other awful fate. Emotions and core values are different; a person who controls and mistreats the things and people he loves is a dangerous person to be admired by! This opens up a lot of interesting narrative space within the game's story; that Elder may well resent and dislike his Metis son, but even if you remove those negative emotions, all of that history and all of those messed up values still exist. "I love you, but I can't be seen with you" is arguably more painful to hear than "I don't want to see you".

                A solid Green. Just be careful what you wish for.

                Break the Bonds: See the Philodox list. Very strange to see a Level 6 Gift repeated!

                    Rank 5 & 6 Ahroun Gifts

                    Kiss of Helios: Spend a Gnosis. Become immune to natural sources of fire and heat entirely. Artificial and supernatural flames will never deal more than a single point of Bashing damage. To top this all off, you can boost your Brawl damage by 2 dice of Aggravated.

                    If you take account for the possibility of those you grapple being harmed by the flames, that many creatures of the Wyrm fear and shun fire, and that many enemies use fire as a weapon, and finally that +2 dice of Aggravated damage on an Ahroun can add up to a lot of additional damage across a whole round... this is pretty good!

                    Abusing this power to the absolute maximum requires similar thought to Master of Fire from Homid's list. Unfortunately this Gift makes MoF entirely redundant, so I would suggest refunding 3xp to a Homid who wound up with both.

                    Strength of Will: Spend a point of Willpower, roll Charisma + Leadership difficulty 8. All allies within 100 feet, and all packmates within 100 miles, regain 1 WP per success.

                    Assuming you have no way of reducing the difficulty, this is risky, but definitely worth having. The benefit provided by reinforcing the Willpower points of many, many allies is great. Your immediate goal should be to stack your dice pool and reduce the difficulty as much as humanly possible, and ideally you should have a way of recovering Willpower yourself, whether through a Fetish, ally, Gift or Charm.

                    All said, this is a great Gift. Given that you can feed WP to allies within 100 miles, this is a handy way to reinforce your friends when you can't be with them. You could be miles away and granting many, many points of WP to your packmate every single turn while they struggle in a duel against a mighty servant of the Wyrm. This also feeds into the idea of the Ahroun as a leader not only in war, but in all things. Providing a pack with endless WP is a huge boon to any project, whether it is spying, ritual, legal, performance or warfare.

                    Unstoppable Warrior: Anything which builds upon the basekit of the Garou, and passively improves it, is great. Very few Gifts do this, annoyingly. This Gift lets you regenerate and heal from Aggravated damage as if it were Lethal damage, unless it comes from silver. This means that you can take 6 points of Aggravated from claws, and 6 turns later you'll be completely back to normal, with no special healing, no Fetish, no Charm, no Gift. If you have anything which speeds up your natural regeneration or lets your body heal Lethal very quickly, this will help make the best use of this Gift.

                    It isn't exciting, but it's cool, and useful. Solid Green. I could wish for more, like it adjusting the Remain Active test you take when you would be 'killed', but it's fine as is.

                        Rank 5 Black Fury Gifts

                        Gorgon's Gaze: A truly terrifying ability which is hard to acquire due to the rarity and danger of the Basilisk in the Umbra, this Gift allows you to paralyze a foe or even turn them to stone.

                        Rolling Appearance + Occult against a difficulty of the target's Willpower causes them to freeze for one turn per success, absolutely paralyzed, and with 5 successes you turn them entirely to stone. The cost is 1 Gnosis.

                        This... sadly isn't that great. By now you've had opportunities to get freezing powers, paralyzing powers, banish-in-fear powers. If your Appearance is 2 and your Occult is 3, the odds of you getting 5 successes are very slim. Don't get me wrong, the sheer AURA of being able to glance at an idiot mortal and turn them into a statue is great, it's a terrifying thing to be able to do, but you can clown on people with a Willpower of 3 when you're a Cub, most likely. The requirement of eye contact only makes it more niche.

                        Extremely cool, but probably one I wouldn't bother buying, unless there are a significant number of low Willpower high importance NPCs knocking about. It's perfectly reasonable for an Elder to have a surprisingly low Willpower, at which point this becomes less weak.

                        Thousand Forms: See the Ragabash list.

                        Wyld Warp: A pretty neat summoning power with a difficulty based on Gauntlet, which is much better than having a difficulty of 8 as so many high level Gifts do. Spending a Gnosis and a Rage lets you roll Wits + Enigmas, and the more successes you get the more intense the impact of the arriving Wyldlings. Their behaviour is unpredictable but beneficial, and likely extremely powerful. Even if the Gift just causes all of the fodder to be torn apart, thrown out of windows, sent flying into the sky, leaving the Big Bad for you to fight alone, that could be an enormous effect. Plus you could get healed, or find your Gnosis/Rage restored, and so on. A random effect table would be neat, to be honest, to help guide the ST towards something genuinely random, but as it stands this is a cool effect.

                                                Rank 5 Bone Gnawer Gifts

                                                Riot: Immediately I find it interesting that the spirits provoking a riot are 'malevolent'. It's a relatively regressive framing off the bat and makes this feel more like a BSD Gift. Regardless, the riot which forms goes after a target of your choice. However, it tends to spiral out of control and have unexpected results, which makes sense for a riot.

                                                Wits + Subterfuge, difficulty 8, with a riot radius of one mile per success. Cost of 1 Gnosis, naturally.

                                                You could say this about a lot of Gifts, but I would argue that this is a very good example of something which is better to do organically. Using Kinfolk, using community leaders, using a variety of Gifts to reach a larger goal, invoking spirits. This Gift isn't terrible, it can fulfil a lot of different needs (distraction, pinning down a business etc.) but I would argue that it very unwisely puts the "protectorate" of the Bone Gnawers at immense risk.

                                                When you start a riot with supernatural mind control, against a target of your choice, you put the lives of many marginalized people into the hands of fate, and more directly, the cops. Communities which are disproportionately likely to contain your Kinfolk, and your future Tribe members in rare cases. Like many Level 5 Gifts this feels like an ability which a sensible Elder will almost never use since it takes a nuke to the problem.

                                                All in all I'd call this a solid Amber, it isn't bad by any means, and it could have huge positive effects, but the inherent risks are also terrible.

                                                Survivor: I really wish this was passive.

                                                Survivor lets you become immune to certain requirements, like needing food, sleep, water. You also don't have to worry about extreme temperatures, natural disease, and natural poison. Special Wyrmish stuff has half the normal effect. Turning it on costs 1 Gnosis and a difficulty 6 Stamina + Survival, lasting for one day per success.

                                                By spending a second Gnosis point you can get a 10 round effect where you have +3 Stamina and ignore all wound penalties. The Gift then expires. When this Gift expires, either after (successes) days or after 10 rounds, you have to sleep for 8 hours. Pretty terrible in a battle!

                                                My immediate thought is that the Gift should make you inherently immune to natural threats (needing food, water, sleep) and that its active form should cost 1 Gnosis, last for a scene, and grant special benefits like immunity to Wyrmish toxins, bonus Stamina, and various other things. Still, the Gift isn't bad by any means.

                                                How good is +3 stamina for 10 rounds? Decent. Not amazing for this point in your career, but good. But falling asleep the moment those rounds end? Awful.

                                                So, ignoring the special 10 round version where you spend a second Gnosis, how does the base effect fare? I would argue that if you're a Rank 5 Bone Gnawer you should be able to handle surviving. A Gift which makes you not have to worry about stuff like this should apply to your whole pack, or a large group of mortals, since those are the people who are going to need help with such situations. An Elder Bone Gnawer should be helping a whole family of Kinfolk to ignore that they're going hungry for a few days while they run far into the wilds to hunt for food, not making it so they personally don't need a snack.

                                                Even if you're in a place like Malfeas where a good meal is hard to come by and impossible to keep clean of Wyrm taint, this Gift ONLY helps you. Your allies aren't going to benefit, which is just... far too little at this stage in the game.

                                                    Rank 5 Children of Gaia Gifts

                                                    Halo of the Sun: I love the subtle solar theme the Children of Gaia have going on. They're one of the Tribes which enjoy using the Greet the Sun Minor Rite, for example. It could be more present, but this Gift is a great way to bring it home.

                                                    Spend a Gnosis, the Gift lasts for a whole Scene. Thank Gaia. I am tired of Level 5 Gifts where you randomly have to pass a difficulty 8 Appearance + Science roll or whatever the hell. The benefits are pretty great:

                                                    - people facing towards you suffer +3 to all their difficulties
                                                    - Vampires take Aggravated damage every turn from the sunlight
                                                    - +2 dice of Aggravated damage to your attacks
                                                    - Homid and Glabro deal Aggravated

                                                    I would suggest including Lupus in the final point, but all in all this Gift is great. Becoming a beacon of raw sunlight which is difficulty 9 to hit is great on its own, and if you have anything else increasing the difficulty to hit you then the difficulty might hit 10. A problem with this Gift is that your allies may fall victim to it, but if they are stood to your side and facing away from you they shouldn't be effected. Enemies which don't rely on sight will likely be unbothered, but the vast majority of enemies do need to see you thankfully, even among Spirits.

                                                    I'm such a Coggie fanboy that I would probably include some passive taint-removal into this Gift, as Helios' raw light burns away corruption all around, but the Gift is great as it is.

                                                    The Living Wood: And we crash right down to earth. Spend a Gnosis, do a difficulty 8 roll, and for every success you bring a tree to life, wandering around like an ent. What are the stats? We don't know. We aren't even told if they're meant to be powerful allies or not. This is impossible to rate since we have no metric for what these trees should be like.

                                                    Notably there isn't even a time limit. RAW this Gift may well animate a tree permanently, which is very funny to me.
