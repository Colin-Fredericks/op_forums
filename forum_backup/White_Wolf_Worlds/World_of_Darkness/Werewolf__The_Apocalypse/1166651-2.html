            Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
            I think the Curse is very important and emphasize it in my chronicles. It's one of the few things in the game that helps bolt the setting into the typical horror movies and stories we expect in the World of Darkness as opposed to the high fantasy adventure the game can too easily become.
            This. Definitely this. I remember my first werewolf game. I run it. And I remember how my players (mostly D&D players) were sure as hell excited about "oh wow we get to play werewolves in this game, sounds awesome" but when I explained how the Curse works... well, living as Garou suddenly sounded less awesome. Even players that usually aren't much invested about their characters genuinely felt bad for them. The Curse sucks, and so it's great. You're a monster and people around you feel it.

            As you said in another post, it's not Vampire but many players want to play WtA in a similar way. And I can get why. Vampire has generally more appeal for a bigger audience. The fact that in a vampire game you have many occassions to play many scenes where you pose as an "average human, but with a twist" while your surroundings stay fairly mundane, I'm sure that's a big draw for a lot of the audience.

            Also I understand that people playing WtA 24/7 want some other stuff to do and want to be more immersed into their alter egos and probably having a dayjob helps them feel their characters more real because they become more "human", more "normal".

            The recent poll about what races are the most played among the current D&D players tell us that the most played race is human. I'm for the more escapist side of fantasy, so it really puzzled me how the plain human was the more favoured choice. But if we take into account the desire of losing one-self into their alter-ego I can get it. I don't share the desire, but I get it.
            It's more normal, more understandable, more easy to *become* that person.
            And also yeah, having a dayjob makes the character more believable. The occupation, the role in human society is an important building block for a believable human character.

            But hey, while I can get why some players like that I personally dislike it.
            It's WtA and for me it's about being a monster. Heroic as only the more tragic heroes can be, but still a monster. Sure, it's an awesome and cool monster (let's admit it please) but also it sucks greatly being one, and there is no escape. Because as a Garou you're shunned by everyone else. You're shunned by human and wolf society. You're not welcome. Yet you're tasked with saving everyone else because some old wolf geezer said you so. Because if you won't do it no one will do it. And yet people will hate and fear you while you're trying to save their butt (funnily this aspect of it sounds much like X-men). The stakes are the highest possible: the world is dying and who's in charge isn't doing shit about it (sounds familiar?). From a Garou perspective mankind is in charge of the world now, and humans are only doing more harm.
            And it boils down to the question: when will you rage?
            When will you be so fed up of everything that you will just go out on a killing spree? Or join the wyrm because to hell with everything? Or embrace red talons ideology and begin human extermination? But you don't want to do those things, they're terrible things. So when will it be that your rage and your moral restraints clash? Will you go crazy?
            Will you go mad by rage?
            The alternative is to Lose the Wolf permanently. You can give up, no problem. Who cares about the world?
            I like the Curse, it adds another factor to the maddening rage-fueled existence of this human-beasts hybrids that I love.
            Seriously a lot of stuff of the setting seems to be designed to make Garou existence maddening. Wolves and humans fear you and will forever do it, but you can't mate with other Garou. You were never human, you just thought you were. Yet you must coexist with them and protect them and absolutely not kill them because your religion says so. Rage is good, but also bad. The moon is good, but also bad. The Garou Nation is made of allies, but also isn't. The Nation could win the war, but it's too busy bickering with each other. Avoiding Apocalypse is hopeless, yet it is THE good fight so it's worth fighting... but you're left with asking yourself what's the point.

            Don't get me wrong. I think it's great. But I also think it's the point of the game. If some people like something different it's fine.
            It was just my rant about why aspects like the Curse or the ecoterrorists or the "captain planet with furries" aspects of the game should stay.
            And I'll also add that they don't need to be much nuanced. Sometimes simple and straightforward works the best. Ok maybe not as simple as "captain planet with furries", but you got it.

            Also for me that I'm able to play only 2 or 3 sessions per month of any RPG... Well, the idea of having to lose precious session time for playing out dayjobs or even having to deal with them for a few minutes... Let's say I'd immediately make a rage roll, but not as a character ahahah

            "Ever since the Followers of Set rebranded themselves as The Ministry, I can barely keep a straight face around them." - Ramona
