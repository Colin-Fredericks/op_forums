                                    Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post


                                    I understand what you're saying, but it's not an ideology I was made accustomed to growing up. I was taught that gender was biological, rather than psychological. So please forgive me for not understanding properly.

                                    Psychology is biological, especially if you're an atheist. Because there is no 'soul' your brain is responsible for your identity and consciousness. Your brain is a body part, like your liver or your heart, a very complex body part, but a product of your biology like everything else. Who you are as an individual has a lot to do with your neurological development. The reason why gender as a social construct is considered to be non-biological is because much of it is social, in other words women and men behave the way they do because society tells them that is what they are supposed to do, and then they get into the habit of doing that, and conditioned behaviors diehard.

                                    There is plenty of evidence to suggest that gender has a biological origin, identical twins are more likely to both be trans is one prevalent example. This does not mean that women are predisposed toward being less aggressive than men, or liking dresses. It means that a transgender person, often early on, will associate with women or men because 'that's what they are', and will pick up those behaviors because 'that's what women and men are supposed to do, right? I would like to fit in."

                                    As an example, when I was in elementary school I had a lot of issues where consciously I understood I was supposed to be a boy, but conditioning hadn't really taken hold yet. When asked to separate into lines of boy or girl, I would often find myself accidentally in the line for girls before running to the back of the boy's line and hoping no one saw me. There was one instance where we were asked to give presentations and at the end we were supposed to bow (for boys) or curtsy (for girls) and at the end of my presentation I automatically curtsied and tripped over myself trying to correct it halfway through the movement. There was another instance where I was kicked out of the boyscouts because I kept referring to everyone as she - even though I'd been reminded more than once where I was. I can remember very vividly forgetting that the same way you might forget you picked up an early shift at work.

                                    The thing is, in these instances I didn't consciously seek out these behaviors, I did not consciously 'want to be a girl'. Identity wise I was a girl, so when exposed to gender defining behaviors I would fuck it up and then feel very embarrassed due to my behavior.

If I spot a woman on the street with overwhelming accuracy we can predict that her chromosomes are XX, and if we check that will be the case.
