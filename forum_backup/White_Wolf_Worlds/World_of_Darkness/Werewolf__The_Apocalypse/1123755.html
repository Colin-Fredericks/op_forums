    Pure Breed Problem

    08-01-2017, 11:38 PM
    While working on a game of WtA, I had a player bring up some serious concerns about the Pure Breed trait. They think it's dumb, and the mechanical bonuses illogical. In their words:

    The queen can be the queen all she wants. If she walks up and kicks my dog and slaps my kid. I'm gonna beat her to a pulp. All the noble breeding in the word is not gonna stop that. And will not give her any benefit at all.

    If a pure blood garou of any level, walks up and just breaks their neck, are either of your characters gonna care at all about their lineage? Or will they frenzy and make garou paste. any onlookers too, can't care at all about random murder. so screw the bonus dice to whatever would be involved.

    It's all social interaction, which, according to the entry, is unlimited and unstipulated if its social and involves garou. You win, no matter what, and that's my point. That is wrong. If the book elaborated, and said if its an unjust or dishonorable stance, then you LOSE dice equal to your pureblood rating, then yes, I could probably get behind it, but for it to always be a plus, no matter what, is flawed and stupid.
    They'd prefer for it to be removed from the game. Thoughts on this? It's not the first time I've heard people claim PB is broken, or doesn't make sense.

      The only thing you'd really lose in the setting is the obvious mechanical advantage of the Silver Fangs being the alpha tribe. Without Pure Breed to bolster their Social Rolls, it would be much easier to ignore them and thus effectively dethrone them. But other than that, it's not like the game falls apart.

      However, one of the major themes of the game is that the characters who get the most advantage out of Pure Breed may not actually be the ones who make the best leaders or actually deserve this kind of status. When some obvious high Pure Breed Garou (most likely Silver Fangs, but most tribes have some members with high Pure Breed) shows up and takes over simply as a result of these extra dice, it is unjust and it is resented.

      And that tension can be fun to roleplay through even if the PC(s) get the short stick end of that. That dynamic does add something to the game, and I like that. One of the pleasures of playing a Bone Gnawer is to be outright contemptuous of those kind of unjust "tricks".

      Pure Breed is a function of supernatural power of some kind (as it only occurs as a result of the tribe's totem) so it may be "illogical", but only in the way any other kind of mind or emotion controlling power is. Pure Breed represents a connection to a higher spiritual world and it is this experience of the numinous which results in social deference from those who don't have it to those who do.

      I don't think Pure Breed is broken in the context of the game as your players do. Although I do understand them not liking the idea that their PC who has no Pure Breed is at a supreme disadvantage in social contests with an NPC with five extra dice because their character has Pure Breed 5. (Although there are ways to mechanically compensate for it to a degree - using Ancestors to get more dice, spending XP to boost the most important social traits, spending Willpower for successes). But ultimately this seems to be a complaint that "I don't like the fact that my character would be disadvantaged in certain situations." Well, that sucks of course, but some games are "unfair" like that. If you play Vampire and have a character with bad Generation (13th as opposed to 8th), you have all sorts of negatives accordingly. That is just part of that game. And yes, I've heard many players complain about Generation as well for the same reasons.

      To modern people, the idea that the "lower classes" would automatically "defer" to their "betters" is extremely hard to understand or tolerate, but wasn't uncommon even half a century or ago. Even in cases where you had peasants revolts and the like, in cases where they actually negotiated with kings of nobles, they spoke very respectfully for the most part. Only in severe cases of massive social change did things get topsy turvy (like in the French or Russian Revolutions) and such status privileges become completely overthrown.

      But I'm not quite sure what your players are arguing about. Yes, of course, if another Garou (even with Pure Breed) physically abuses or kills another Garou, it makes perfect sense why the encounter would go into physical combat and ignore social challenges. So what? The higher Pure Breed Garou know that. Only an idiot would push certain things to the point where justifiable violence against them could be pursued. Does the player think any Social Challenge is meaningless because the other party could always resort to violence? Probably not. Certainly society distinguishes which situations should not result in violence, and which situations where violence is either accepted, tolerated, excused, or otherwise justified.

      The flip side to Pure Breed is not that there is negative game mechanic social penalty if the Garou does not upheld their standard. The flip side is that high Pure Breed Garou have a social obligation to act accordingly. And if they don't, Garou do become resentful against them, dislike them, form conspiracies against them, or begin to disagree publicly against them - and that includes other high Pure Breed Garou, not just the lower breeds. But this is more in the realm of the ST reacting to what happens in game than giving an automatic penalty. This is one of the major reasons why more and more Garou are unhappy about the leadership of the Silver Fangs - it explains why the Shadow Lords and others are looking to make a change which is a major element in the setting. And it is one reason why the Bone Gnawers are always pissed off against the other tribes.

      Rather than doing away with Pure Breed mechanics, you may want to discuss with your players how you may handle those situations where high Pure Breed Garou develop a bad reputation for failing to act like they should. Maybe you might give them a penalty to the Difficulty numbers to certain or all Garou who know that werewolf does not measure up. Or allow a system where PB dice decline according to differences in Rank. Or saying if the Garou spends WP he can ignore the effects of PB for that challenge or even whole scene (since WP is a finite resource, I personally like to allow players to use it for various reasons - if it is important to them they have that option, but since regaining WP during a story is generally hard PCs have to think hard about it).

                  There's a few different issues here.

                  On the "broken" issue, the baseline rules are a bit extreme in bonus with a very ST whim penalty with not mechanical or even general rule to structure a drawback on. This is made worse by any number of Gifts and Merits that can add even more dice or reduce the difficulty of social rolls. This is, however, the easier part of it to fix. W20 Rage Across the World, introduces both the Sept Standing Background (which lets characters, at least for their home Sept, earn bonus dice to compete with Pure-Breed), and a cap on the total Background/Merit bonus dice you can apply to a single roll at 5 above your base-line roll (so if you have 6 dice, but are currently at a -2 penalty, you can benefit +7 from social Backgrounds and Merits to cancel the -2, but anything more would be wasted). This addition alone takes a lot of the wind out of complaints about Pure-Breed. Adding in something to enforce the idea that Pure-Breed means having to live up to expectations should pretty much address any lingering problems. You definitely don't need to remove it.

                  The issue with it not making any sense is boiled into the fact that it's two different concepts smashed together and the compromise is always lacking in narrative logic. PB is both a physical (for a supernatural being) trait that governs looks and progeny, and a mystical trait that confers social authority. By choosing to link/maintain the linking of these two concepts, PB has always had counter intuitive issues attached to it.

                          Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post

                          I disagree because I don't think Social Rolls are as powerful as you seem to describe. There are practical limits in what a Social Roll can accomplish and this can change over time or in different circumstances. Having high social skill is not the same as magical mind control.

                          Something as simple as "Can you open this door for me?" should lead to very different results depending whether you are talking to a hotel employee as you enter the building, another passerby as you enter a store, a person alone in their home confronted with someone they don't know asking to be let into the house late or night, or a security guard under orders not to let anybody in. You can go from - "no roll is needed, you are let in" to "OK, roll with standard difficulty" to "The person isn't going to let you in. I won't even allow a roll."

                          In real life I have seen some circumstances where one person has - in game terms - a big dice pool for Social Rolls, and there are others with a much smaller dice pool. Yet it becomes obvious over time that this one person is actually a selfish jerk, a sociopath, or simply someone whose values are beyond the pale (say a Holocaust Denier). In the beginning, before this truth is actually realized, the person with the big social dice pool is quite successful with getting people to agree with him, do what he wants to do, or simply like him. But over time people begin to learn the truth about this person and begin to feel exploited, ignored, used, or become appalled. They begin to challenge that person - hoping the person is actually reasonable, concerned, or just misunderstood.

                          Now because that person is highly skilled socially, he often or even always "wins" these arguments. But the "win" is usually just that the other person doesn't know what to say. They can't articulate it in a way that causes them to win the argument because the other person is so skilled at rhetoric. No matter what they say, they'll ultimately feel like an idiot and often begin to simply say nothing at all. But at the same time, they aren't actually convinced by this person - they just don't know how to respond effectively in rhetoric. Even while these people are silent and not disputing the other person (or disputing very poorly), it is obvious they actually are not convinced and are seething in resentment or outrage. In many cases, they are looking around the room or table and hoping someone else, anyone else, will step up and smack the crap out of this guy orally.

                          At that point in time, no matter how charming or persuasive this person is, people begin to react in ways that they ignore his high social skills. In some cases they simply ostracize the person and remove them from their social circle. In other cases, they simply put up their shields and simply zone out the other person when they begin to act in these offending ways. Despite this person effectively "winning" the argument tactically, they actually fail to convince anyone. In these situations, what I've seen is that if there is someone new to the group and scene and observes this, they are usually convinced or at least have a favorable impression of the jerk. These new guys haven't become "immune" to his charm, and therefore don't know why the group is treating this guy so badly or ignoring well made points. Their learning curve is just beginning, but they will get there eventually. In some cases, the person is actually threatened and told to leave him or the entire group alone.

                          In other words, if someone treats you like crap, no matter how skilled he is socially, you KNOW he is treating you like crap. If someone is recommending a course of action that will get you killed for no reason, a mutiny will result of some kind barring some other reason that keeps their loyalty outside of that person. Someone who is a Holocaust Denier, no matter how charming or skilled with words, won't actually convince you the Holocaust didn't happen - even if your own lack of knowledge and debate skills makes you lose that argument on a theoretical points basis.

                          These kinds of people don't become unstoppable social steamrollers. Instead they usually have to move from social circle to social circle as they burn the relationships they have with others and need to find new victims. If they do have a group of friends who stay with them, it is usually people who already want to do or believe what this guy wants, or sycophants who are attracted to bullies.

                          Garou with high Pure Breed are in the exact same dynamic.

                          Yes, a jerk Garou with high Pure Breed can steamroll dissent - but only selectively, or in the very beginning, or for a little while. If the person is clearly acting against the interests of their social group, that begins to decline over time. In some cases, very quickly.

                          Even in cases where Social Rolls are not being used on a short term tactical basis, but going for extended successes in order to win over hearts and minds, there are still going to be limits. No matter how much the jerk Garou insists he has changed his heart and is different now, so let's please please be friends, unless actual behavior changes as well, some of those success aren't going to count, or become reset to zero, or some other modifier.

                          It is going to be a very rare person who gets constantly screwed over by a high PB Garou who continues to like them. Most people won't. They might admit the person is charming, or very distinguished, or given any number of social attributes that reflect their high Social dice pool. But they won't actually like them because they know they are toxic.

                          At the same time, it is also possible for these kind of jerks to be like Eddie Haskell from the old Leave it Beaver episode. Haskell was a selfish jerk who used people, but he also acted very differently to different people. He was very polite, helpful, and respectful to parents and authority figures, and then acted like a little shit when they weren't around. So such a Garou's reputation might be very different in different social circles.

                          If you want to translate this into game mechanics, it is changing the Difficulty from the standard 6 to a higher value - 7,8, 9, or even 10 in certain circumstances - because other people (perhaps only a certain group) now dislike the person intently. And furthermore, even what the jerk Garou is allowed to do in social rolls against people who know what kind of person he is should likely be limited. If that person wants to become designated "leader" for some quest, and the other Garou absolutely won't allow that person to lead them, then no Social Roll can achieve that result. The ST needs to adjudicate the scene so that only a lesser kind of success can be achieved, or at least stagger that it can only be allowed if a minimum number of successes is rolled at high difficulty. And of course, I would always allow someone to simply spend a WP to ignore someone else's mundane social success if they are very opposed to it.

                          So I think there are actually some very easy tools for STs to run Pure Breed and not have it lead to absurd conclusions. I think the ST and PCs can probably find some sort of balance.

                                      This reminds me of when my friend wanted to run an epic level Forgotten Realms campaign. I chose a Paladin and with enough stuff and boosts and what not got a 32 charisma. The first thing that happened was I had players scream "IF HE STARTS ROLLING TO MAKE ME DO THINGS I QUIT" (...i just wanted to smite well....)

                                      this leads me to what I've always ruled is that you can't force players to react to social dice unless gifts/disciplines/ect. are involved. I have never met a player who wants to be told "no, you in fact still like that guy". It never makes for fun role play. I tend to use social dice for NPC's reacting to NPC's and players. This way you can have that smug antagonist who always seems to have friends or influence, and make the players free to react to them how they want.

                                      I also keep situation in mind. At some point an NPC with a high social pool may have done things that for me, mere social dice are impossible to fix.
