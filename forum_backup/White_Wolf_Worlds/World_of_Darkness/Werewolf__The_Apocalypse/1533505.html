    Koschei as an enemy faction in Werewolf

    04-03-2024, 03:02 AM
    This is another thread of making the various Talons of the Wyrm as actual factions of the Wyrm that can be introduced into regular chronicle play. I like creating individual factions of the Wyrm with their own lore and antagonists in order to make the setting more interesting than simply making everything part of PENTEX or the Black Spiral Dancers. Diversity of antagonists is good, even if the Garou see them all as minions of the Wyrm. The previous threads can be found here.

    Narlthus
    Thrassus

    And similar threads to beings who are not actual Talons of the Wyrm, but lesser beings.

    Setites
    Odin

    At first glance, it does not seem Koschei needs much developing. After all, of all the Talons of the Wyrm he has received the most attention being a core element of the sourcebook Rage Across Russia. However, we get fairly limited information about the Talon (although more than any other Talon). Instead, most information is about Baba Yaga with Koschei merely being a plot coupon or macguffin. Furthermore, Koschei is limited to being a figure whose relevance is limited to Russia. If you run a chronicle in North America, western Europe, North Africa, or anywhere else, it doesn't seem like you can utilize the Talon.

    So this thread is about trying to universalize the Talon while preserving his unique lore. To me, the key is to keep Koschei as a kind of dark fairy tale type creature. He is the Koschei of Russian legend. He is also something like Sauron from Tolkien. Or any other "Dark Lord" character like Voldemort among many others. Something more accessible than the more cosmic horror we built around Thrassus. Look for archetypical aspects of the Koschei legends that can be found elsewhere. So what can we do with him? Well, these are my thoughts.


    "She comes of your father Adam's ... first wife, her they called Lilith. And she was one of the Jinn. That's what she comes from on one side. And on the other she comes of the giants. No, no, there isn't a drop of real Human blood in the Witch."

    - C.S. Lewis, The Lion, the Witch, and the Wardrobe (1950)

    It is known by many names.

    He Who Points.

    Tsar Koschei.

    The General of Winter Unending

    Koschei is “He Who Points”. It is a primal manifestation of the Wyrm, a Talon of the Wyrm who serves the Eater of Souls. Tsar Koschei is the General of Winter Unending, a malevolent figure of the Apocalypse prophesied to lead the armies of the Wyrm against the forces of Gaia in the end days. "Koschei" is not its real name, but given its presence in the Eurasian forests, it became popularly known by that name, and it remains the name he is best known by, but not the only one. Some Get of Fenris believe the "Loki" of Ragnarok who leads the forces of the world’s destruction is in fact this Talon of the Wyrm. Its true name might not even be known, so to the Garou it is simply "He Who Points".

    Alone among the Talons of the Wyrm, Koschei chooses to assume a mortal form, that of an old, withered, cruel man. However, it is not human, but an eldritch being of the Wyrm that predates humanity. It either possesses a mortal, transforming the body over time, or uses its own power to create such a form. It often appears to be nothinbg more than a powerful Ferectoi. It assumes this shape in order to infiltrate and corrupt mankind, and the other supernatural beings who have the shape of humanity like fairies and the Changing Breeds. Its true appearance is unknown.

    The Pact of Koschei is comprised of the various vassals of this Talon of the Wyrm. Its vassals are often particularly powerful, supernatural creatures, but also includes humans it has transformed into fomori. It makes pacts with those who are desperate enough for power or aid that they agree to be its loyal, immortal slaves in exchange for power, knowledge, and influence. The vassals of Koschei are some of the most dangerous opponents of the Garou because they recruit and lead many other lesser Wyrm minions in the service of Koschei. Garou legend warns that Koschei will lead a vast host of Wyrm minions against the Garou on the Plain of the Apocalypse in the Near Realm of Battleground.

      Although the soul gem of He Who Points is guarded by a small group of Uktena Banetenders in Siberia, and it is best known as Koschei in the lands of Russia, He Who Points is known throughout the world by a variety of local names that have entered human culture and Garou legend. The Talons of the Wyrm are so ancient that all the Garou tribes have myths and legends from those times regardless of the ethnic heritage of their homid breed.

      In western Europe, it has been responsible for the Bluebeard legend. Elsewhere in Asia, He Who Points has been called Div-e Sepid. In the Americas, one name has been Lox. All depict some kind of greedy or lustful monster or sorcerer who imprisons women and difficult to kill permanently. None of these aspects and their followers, however, have been as well known, numerous, or dangerous as those Wyrm minions who follow its aspect of Koschei.

      = = =

      = = =
      As a Talon of the Wyrm that arrived on Earth in the earliest days of pre-history, I do not believe Koschei should be limited to Russia. I have no problem with the Talon being most active within a certain geographic area, but even the boundaries of modern Russia does not reflect the boundaries of historic Russia (Kievan Rus) when the legends of Koschei first took form.

      So I think it behooves us to look for similar figures in other cultures. Traditional monsters or sorcerers who have a fondness for mortal women or treasure, and are difficult to kill because they can come back to life.

      Baba Yaga is its most powerful vassal, but other parts of the world should know of the Talon in some capacity and have other vassals of lesser power and influence. That way we can extend the followers of this Talon beyond the confines of Rage Across Russia. That may be its most important battleground, but not the old one.

But since he is the Talon of the Wyrm we know the most facts about, I wanted to expand it into a whole faction.                   He Who Points has seduced and recruited powerful vassals to serve as his dukes and knights all throughout the world. It prefers immortal, or at least long lived, servants for he needs them to be present at the Apocalypse. It has had many vassals who raise armies on his behalf - vampires, mages, fomori, fae, bygones, and other kinds over the many thousands of years since his arrival on Earth. The most important of which was Baba Yaga, the hag of Russia. But they have also included the Giant Who Had No Heart in His Body, the terror of Scandinavia; Cú Roí Mac Dáire, the sorcerer of Ireland and King of Munster; Telpochtli, the Young Man of Mesoamerica; Ahasuerus, the Wandering Jew; and many others. Few have survived to the modern age, but all have been terrible foes of the Garou while in Koschei's service.

                  Koschei is the Wyrm's envoy to the Fomorians and resultingly a major menace to the Dreaming. Many chimerical creatures and Bygones, rudely shut out from the mortal world by the Weaver, have flocked to its cause. But its alliances with the dark forces of the Dreaming are not the end of these pacts, just the beginning.

                  Koschei's best known pact is with the Hag of Russia, the Nosferatu vampire Baba Yaga. It is even said he may have made a pact with the Nosferatu progenitor himself. As a result many vampires of this lineage have entered its service. Some speculate it is the activities of these vassals who are responsible for the legend of the Nictuku among the Nosferatu.

                  Not all vassals of Koschei have supernatural power to begin with. Koschei make these mortals a special kind of fomori, his Sworn. I'll detail these fomori later in another post.

                  I've looked for a lot of other cultural examples of monsters/villains who have similar attributes to Koschei. The two things I look for is 1) an immunity to death by placing its soul in an impossible place to find, and 2) an obsession with kidnapping women. I'm often torn whether they should be alternate names of Koschei, or names of vassals under him.

                  The legendary figures I think are most similar to Koschei are the stories involving the Norewegian folktake The Giant Who Had No Heart in His Body and Cu Roi Mac Dare.

                  This version of the fairy tale has various changes from the actual story, but is close enough to get the point.


                  There isn't a cinematic version of Cu Roi Mac Dare, but the essentials of his immortality can be found here.


                  In my imagination, He Who Points is a much more human figure than the other Talons of the Wyrm, more accessible. While I see the other Talons as a figure for cosmic horror, I see Koschei as a much more understandable figure. A kind of Sauron lording over the Nazgul. Or the White Witch of Narnia ruling her minions. It would make Koschei an important figure in earlier ages when legendary monsters and such were more present. But a lot of modern day urban fantasy can also find ways to present such a figure appropriately.

                                      As an side, I want to note that pointing one's finger can be a threatening or aggressive gesture as it targets or challenges someone. It can also be used to accuse, stigmatize, or blame someone. (Satan, of course, is the great accuser of mankind). Many people associate someone pointing at them to be an attempt to lead or control them them. Its an action people do to animals, or to indicate social authority over someone. It is something associated with leadership and dominating other people, making them subordinates and not equals. Many people find it extremely rude or insulting if someone points at them.

                                      I will also note that in earlier times, the gesture could be interpreted as the person putting a hex on someone. And since Koschei is seen as a sorcerer in Russian folklore, this might perhaps be most apt.

                                      But in all of these connotations, Koschei being called "He Who Points" is indicative that the Garou consider him a major threat. It doesn't just need to be a pedantic assignment of a Talon to one of the fingers of the hand. It can be associated with his role as a leader of the Wyrm's forces during the Apocalypse, a corrupter of Gaian forces, and as a warlock that hexes the Garou.

                                          I imagine most vassals of Koschei are existing supernatural creatures seduced by Koschei. But I don't think we should be limited to that. So here are the Sworn of Koschei, who are equivalents of the Nazgul to Sauron. If you can't imagine an existing vampire, corrupt Changing Breed, Mage, wraith, fae, bygone, or some other creature as fitting, you can use this fomori.

                                          The Sworn of Koschei (fomori)

                                          The Sworn of Koschei are human fomori selected to be direct vassals of the Talon of the Wyrm. They are all highly capable, ambitious people whose hearts are beholden to one of the Urge Wyrms under the Eater of Souls. They appear human although their minds have become utterly alien. These fomori are not disposable pawns, but important vassals who will lead armies under the banner of Koschei. Koschei only provides his rare fae treasures granting immortality to these servants.

                                          Some Sworn are created as a result of their lust and greed upon possessing one of the legendary treasures of Koschei. Other Sworn are created as a result of a Seeder bane identifying a human whose heart is already towards the Eater of Souls. There are also humans who transform into Sworn as a result of their own occult investigations into the secrets of the Wyrm. Other vassals of Koschei who see promise in such mortals may also give special knowledge that results in them becoming this type of fomori. The Sworn of Koschei have been mighty bronze age warriors, wise women, tribal chiefs, petty kings, rich merchants, ruthless bankers, ambitious wives, and politicians. All were governed by their greed, ambition, and lust - the vices of the Eater of Souls - all enslaved themselves to Koschei.

                                          The base powers of the Sworn of Koschei are as follows:

                                          Powers: Armored Skin, Darksight, Eyes of the Wyrm, Gifted Fomor, Homogeneity, Regeneration, Sense Gaia, Spirit Ties

                                          Gifts: Persuasion, Master of Fire, Smell of Man, Falling Touch, Bane Protector, Resist Pain

                                          In addition, each Sworn will have individual custom powers as a result of their unique circumstances or purposes.

                                          In the Umbra they appear almost invisible but surrounded by shadows.

                                              As a servant of the Eater of Souls, Koschei knows greed, lust, and appetite for power are powerful weapons to corrupt humanity. Koschei had its servants in the Dreaming create magical treasures which it gave to its mortal servants and extend their mortal life like its other servants. Of course the price is them becoming enslaved by its will. In every case Koschei bestowed these treasures as a token of their pact and submission to Koschei after they are transformed into fomori, its Sworn. The treasures of Koschei are creations of the fae that cannot truly be destroyed by mortal means. Any that appear to be broken or destroyed simply appear so as Glamour, and will appear as new again whenever it is safe to do so.

                                              These treasures can be rings, bracelets, pins, brooches, necklaces, pendants, or any other adornment. All obtained the glory and great wealth they wanted as well as occult powers because of these treasures. All surrendered their will to Koschei as a result. The life extending properties of these treasures only work as a result of one of the Sworn making a pact with Koschei. Otherwise they are inert and powerless. The treasures do not work for any other kind of fomori or other being.

                                              Koschei does not need to be free or awake to make such pacts. Any human possessing one of these treasures can be put into psychic communication with Koschei. These always first occur in dreams (usually forgotten by the time they wake) when Koschei can interact, evaluate, and tempt the mortal. If he find the mortal unworthy, he will subtly encourage the human to sell, give, or somehow "lose" the treasure so a more worthy mortal may find it. If he finds the mortal worthy, the dreams gradually change as the human begins to embody the vices of the Eater of Souls into their life and grow in power. Only after such corruption has taken hold into their heart does Koschei reveal himself further in dreams and eventually while the person is awake in order to guide them towards becoming fomori and making a pact with him.

                                              Garou legend holds Koschei has only a finite amount of these fae treasures, and the Garou have documented which of them they have destroyed in Arcadia. But Arcadia has been closed off to the Garou for centuries, and since then none of Koschei's treasures have been destroyed. Instead, they have been hidden in the guarded vaults of the Black Furies, Uktena, and others.

                                              While each of the treasures of Koscehi extends the life of the Sworn fomori, how it does so is special to each treasure. Some bestow such power naturally, but its possessor ages naturally until they appear as an ancient, withered old man or crone. Some require the Sworn to drink blood or consume human flesh to enable them to regain their youth and vitality. Others are talismans required to perform blasphemous Wyrm rites sacrificing the innocent or relatives to gain their appearance.

                                                  Vassals of Koschei typically begin recruiting other, lesser, supernatural creatures to work for them. While many supernatural creatures are already prone to recruit their own kind or already have servants and pawns, the Sworn of Koschei fomori usually lack such ready resources. They must make do with what they can.

                                                  There are many packs of chimerical wolves terrorizing the Dreaming and certain Trods of the Penumbra who owe loyalty to one of Koschei's faerie vassals. Some malevolent Bygones like dragons (such as the Zmei), cockatrices, basilisks, ghuls, manticores, perytons, and harpies who have fallen to the Wyrm also serve vassals of Koschei. Provided they have survived. These likewise either reside in the Dreaming, special realms in the Umbra, under the protection of corrupted Mages, or the Realm itself provided they have access to special powers or pacts that protect them from the forces of Banality and the Weaver.

                                                  Many Dauntain are attracted to the King of Eternal Winter such as the Cursed, Black Magicians, and Apostates. For different reason they view Koschei as one of the supreme forces of banality who will bring about their desires towards the Dreaming.

                                                  Also important are lower fomori fit to be slaves to the Sworn of Koschei. These lesser fomori are known to make allegiances to more powerful and smarter fomori. Newly made Sworn will need time before they can subjugate or attract such lowly minions. One's reputation is not made overnight.

                                                  A very rare number of Banes known as Dream Makers serve Koschei, and a very small number of his servants are aided by one of these Dream Makers. Other Dream Makers continue to serve Koschei, but as independent followers of the Talon rather than as a servant of one of its vassals.

                                                      So with a faction like this, everything really depends on how interesting is the individual vassal who commands whatever local Wyrm forces in the chronicle. Simply being a servant of Koschei is not enough.

                                                      Baba Yaga is a great example of such a a vassal. She's well known in folklore. She has an entire sourcebook based around her. She makes a great antagonist. She's the kind of figure you might expect leading her minions onto the Plain of the Apocaylpse on Battleground under the banner of The Talon of the Wyrm.

                                                      Even if other vassals may not be at her stature or power level (I think she should be considered to be the most powerful and influential of Koschei's vassals), they still have to be compelling and interesting to players in that dark fairy tale feel. So now is a good time to give some examples of another vassal of Koschei. I'll start with one and post others later. Interested in seeing if people contribute any of their own ideas.

                                                      Countess of Shrikes

                                                      The Countess is a sidhe fae, not a Changeling, who resides in the Far Dreaming able to survive both Banality and Bedlam as a result of her pact with Koschei. She made the Pact during the Shattering in order to preserve herself in its disastrous aftermath. She has attracted many Dauntain to her court, specifically Apostates, Black Magicians, and the Cursed who seek to further the cause of Endless Winter. They believe her survival due to strange magics of banality will allow their people to survive. Many dangerous chimerical creatures serve her. Some members of the Shadow Court exist in a secret conspiracy on behalf of the Countess and her master. Through her Changeling minions, her reach can even extend to the Realm. They try to convert more Changelings to follow the Countess, and sometimes kidnap them to forcibly convert them. Her minions often act like bandits among the Trods. Her followers' conception of He Who Points is of a primal figure, perhaps one of the Fomorians, who is the General or Lord of Winter Unending. Once a feared figure, but whom they have now placed all their hopes.

                                                      The Countess of Shrikes hates mankind for bringing order and reason to the world as it upset the balance and drove the Wyld out of the world, and thus the fae's ability to survive in the Autumn World. She believes the Shattering of the Gates was a defensive move to protect Arcadia, and that the Gates will only open once more with the ending of the world at the Apocalypse. The old world has already been lost, so the only thing to do now is end this world as soon as possible. She delights in sending raiding parties to the Realm that furthers the Apocalypse. By victory over the Garou and those fae or changelings stupid enough to oppose her, she will ensure the survival of her people and their return to Arcadia. A part of her regrets such battles against old allies, but one must do what one must to survive.

                                                      Frequently mistaken as a Lost One, the Countess of Shrikes can in fact leave the Dreaming and is immune to Bedlam. But she prefers to dwell in her home, the Castle of the Impaled Heart located in the Bullydale Wastes of the Far Dreaming, and sends her fae minions and enchanted slaves to do her bidding throughout the Dreaming and the Autumn World. It is said that the Countess of Shrikes can sometimes be found in the Arcadia Gateway trying to convince Queen Marianna to join her. But for now, the Queen sees the Countess as simply a rebellious vassal of hers that will one day be brought to heel, but not right now.

                                                      The machinations of the Countess of Shrikes and her minions are reasons for Changelings to call upon the aid of packs of Stag.

                                                          Here is another example vassal of Koschei.

                                                          The Tiger Prince

                                                          The Tiger Prince is an ancestor spirit of Bastet Khan from the pre-historic Maru-Diklah War that ravaged the Khan. As part of the losing side, he made a Pact with Koschei as he lay dying. As a result, he survived as a Wyrm corrupted Ancestor spirit that still "guides" and occasionally possesses what descendants he has today. He is one reason why the Khan have destroyed so many of themselves in fraticidal war. He also utterly controls a Den Realm in the Indian jungle which he bestows to a favored Khan descendant. The ancestor spirit manages to rule small band of Khan kinfolk descended from him and those Khan they can produce, typically one every few generations. Since the Khan themselves are almost extinct with around twenty current members, this is not as meaningless as it seems. The Bastet, however, are ignorant as to the actual corrupted nature of this Ancestor spirit when they use his real name. They recognize there is a dangerous spirit known as the Tiger Prince however. The Tiger Prince’s cult most often refers to He Who Points as Hiranyakashipu.

                                                          The influence of the Tiger Prince extends beyond his kinfolk and Bastet descendants. The Ancestor Spirit has gathered a small brood of corrupted spirits (including Jamaks) and Banes around him. Several insane ancestor spirits are part of his brood. A variety of man-eating Changing Breeds provide the spirit known as the Tiger Prince homage in exchange for his favors, protection, and service of his brood. This spirit brood teaches many Gifts to these corrupted Changing Breeds. It is this group of man-eating Changing Breeds that now provide him the most influence since the severe decline of the Khan.

                                                          The Man-Eater camp of Bone Gnawers, some of the Eaters of the Dead of the Silent Striders, some fallen members of the Bringers of Light camp among the Shadow Lords, some corrupted nests of Ratkin, and various individual Garou and Fera who engage in man eating see the Tiger Prince as a powerful spirit ally. There are also some human cannibal occult societies that includes the Tiger Prince as one of their occult benefactors. The Tiger Prince hopes this cult will one day make him an Incarna of man-eating. Some Garou Theurges speculate if this happens it will be one sign of the Apocalypse.

                                                          The portion of the Umbra where the Tiger Prince normally resides is a pocket realm of the Legendary Realm accessible only by the Bastet. There the Tiger Prince relives his revised version of the Maru-Diklah War where he triumphs and mercilessly tortures and destroys his enemies. It is only here that the true identity of the mysterious “Tiger Prince” is known by those spirits and rare Bastet who manage to find and enter this pocket realm, at least until they perish.
