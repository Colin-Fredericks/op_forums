    The Ghost Council is supposed to take on "unconventional methods" of getting the job done, but in a game where you won't even be straight up about what convention is, this has no meaning at all.
    The way the Tribe is presented in W5, it's an outright evil Tribe. I saw the writer of the Book of Hungry Names game talk about how he thinks their shtick is "using the Wyrm's tools against the Wyrm". And then of course in Scent of Decay, the last adventure is about how the Horned Serpent Patron Spirit encouraged a Garou Pack to let the Wyrm run rampant in a small town as a way to "study the Wyrm".

    This one and the Red Talons with their Plague Dog character-concept, I have a hard time seeing what's the difference between those two Tribes and BSD in W5. Especially because Shattered Nation describes how Black Spiral Dancers can sometimes be your allies in the fight against Pentex and how they still care a lot about their White Howler legacy. Honestly, the BSD in W5 sound more reasonable than the descriptions they have for the Ghost Council or Red Talons in my opinion.

                    Yeah, it's weird, isn't it? The W5 version of BSD is very aware of and interested in its White Howler legacy but according to Shattered Nation the 80s and 90s are considered "ancient history" by the good Garou...

                    I could stomach a lot of W5's changes if it weren't for the bad worldbuilding underpinning it. Nearly everything feels like it got caught up in the contradiction of trying to produce a new edition of WtA and having a deep resentment for WtA as a franchise. The main-phrase for W5 is "re-imagination, not continuation" but while there are changes - none of them are effective. The changes range from incoherent, half-assed to simply mean-spirited. It's worse for H5 but both the released W5 corebook and H5 corebook are just an anchor around these gamelines' necks. Neither the H5 nor the W5 corebooks give you worldbuilding you want to expand, these books offer worldbuilding you want to reject. Both these corebooks offer such stringent limitations on what the game is supposed to be while offering little to no support for even doing the specific thing they're aiming for.

                    Just think of this: In the way the W5 corebook describes its setting, every adventure put on a page by Renegade or in the corebook is a meaningless sideshow. There's the whole idea of W5 decrying Garou as bad and incompetent but then every single adventure afterward is all about how the good thing the PC-Garou-Pack should do is to be a bunch of traditionalists. "Maintain the status quo." is the core-message of all the material released thus far. In other words "The bad guys are bad, so you have to fight them - but at least be polite about it. There's such a thing as "going too far", you know.". And the name for that is Hauglosk. "You think you can change things for the better by being proactive? Sounds like you're suffering from Hauglosk." No, what W5 envisions you should do is have the players take over a caern and just sit there, protecting it and deal in street-level-adventures that concern that caern. Does it matter that it's the so-called Age of Apocalypse and the world is closer to the Apocalypse than ever...? Nah... don't worry about it. And actually worrying about that and getting angry about that is a sign of Hauglosk - which is bad. And that's why you shouldn't do that.

                    These days it doesn't feel like the Garou you're supposed to play in W5 are radicals. They're more like a morality-police. You fight against Fomori because they're "bad people" and you fight against other Garou because they're "bad people". And what are you fighting for? That everything's fine and hey, if you want to change things, that's okay... they feel bad, though, because the way you're doing it is wrong which is why it needs to get shut down.

                                            The discussion of types of packs neglects to mention an obvious one. Well, a few obvious ones, from when W:tA was about a war. Because while it isn't always the best tool to use, sometimes you've got to go in like a loud team of hooders from Shadowrun - another game that's had issues about "to shoot the suit, or not to shoot the suit..."

                                            I do believe that, in the end, SR6 has come out on the side of "fuck 'em, shoot!"

                                            The section Litany mentions that the Garou know it, and that one of the ways that they might is from glyphs etched onto warning stones in the Black Forest. Well, there goes any justification that the text has for ignoring that Garou do, in fact, write down their history. Regrettably, it proceeds to ignore the half of the tenets that can be used to counterbalance the exploitable ones; we've been through that on the forums, but suffice to say that the text would have a damned difficult time arguing against not eating humans or The Veil Shall Not Be Lifted. Especially so for that last one, because it spends so much time pulling for the status quo that it wouldn't be able to exist without it.

                                            The Changing Ways sidebar is still a slap in the face. And it still makes for a less good version of Revised.

                                            We get some prominent septs that really do not make it seem like it's a reboot, as they name check some prominent ones from Caerns: Places of Power and Rage Across the Amazon, with the last example referencing the war that the Garou were waging there up until recently. There's some hand-waving about how the BSDs managed to move an entire hive.

                                            Moots are now apparently more occasional things than regular meetings to revitalize the caern. Seems unnecessary. The text hits the "Garou getting together magnifies their Rage!" drum pretty hard. It makes me long for the days of a certain rite from CoG Revised. This is how low W5 is, especially considering that there's no tenet against the Rite of Clouds and Rain in this edition, and it'd be easier to mechanically define it.

                                            Harano is somehow both watered down as a game concept yet also made more severe. In prior editions, just to recount things for potential readers who didn't know, it only happened under extreme circumstances and its mechanical effect, while a character was afflicted with it, was to halve every dice pool you roll unless you succeeded at a scene-by-scene Willpower roll (tn 7). There were also mechanics to recover if you did something significant. The presentation in W5 makes it a one-way slide with no chance of fighting back and also undersells what it is by not talking about how it's a product of the extremes of being Garou.

                                            Hauglosk is presented as the opposite, though it's noted that, again, in legacy, Harano can result in a sudden burst of of activity in response to what the Garou is going through. In general, those psychological states are probably a bit too cumbersome to make a regular part of the game the way Vampire deals with Humanity. Also, Hauglosk is another one of those "guess what the people who made this game think of you?" things, and the answer to that guessing game is... not very great, since the storytelling chapter likens it to falling down the ecofascist pipeline and together with certain comments made about various attempts to write the Get make it pretty apparent how bad it is. Again, well trod by now, but it needs to be repeated as long as the W5 crowd keeps denying that it's the case.

                                            The auspice and tribe descriptions have glyphs associated with them, but remember, Garou history is only ever transmitted orally! And it's not like oral history could, say, preserve a 100,000 year memory of where stars used to appear over the horizon!

                                            The Black Furies are now the new Get of Fenris, the Bone Gnawers' tendency to help the down and out people they live around is only communicated through their tribal patron's ban, CoGs are oriented around mystery instead of peace, the Ghost Council seems okay hanging out with BSDs for information, and not even the Silver Fangs have any sense of history to them. But at least they have The Legend of Polk High! And everyone has plenty of musicians in the Galliards!

                                            Chargen and Traits spends way too much time giving a rundown of the 1-5 dots and cracking jokes. You know, if you wanted to make your game seem modern, you might not want to copy the format that the games had in the 90s. Just... Saiyan.

                                            People will swear up and down that Appearance isn't a thing but much like CoD, it just got folded into Charisma. Also not a thing that makes you think of a modern game: Charisma including your looks!

                                            Composure 5 has a standout dated reference with 5 dots being "You probably wouldn't have paid 44 billion USD for twitter".

                                            That's not an example of Composure 5, book. That's an example of Composure 2, maybe even 1.

                                            There's a lot of finger-wagging about the applicability of Specialties. While I can get where it comes from in some cases, like wanting it to not apply always, ragging on people for a fighting style that they'll probably use often seems like a very 90s thing to do.

                                            Academics jumps from talking about BS degrees straight to doctoral ones. That felt like a bit of whiplash.

                                            Backgrounds and Merits. Why Both?

                                            Loresheets only allow you to take levels of one Loresheet at chargen. That's not how Loresheets should be used as a concept. I know, because I read Weapons of the Gods ~20 years ago. The structure of the concept is meant to engage a character in various parts of the setting, and multiple avenues of that may, in fact, be necessary.

                                            Resources notes that high Resources can have your character involved with despoiling of the world and that it could be hypocritical. Oh, now you have a problem with capital, book. How convenient.

                                            Spirit Pact notes that Ghost Council and Red Talons Garou make pacts with banes. (Picks up flamethrower, ignites it) Whelp, time to burn this motherfucker down.

                                            Renown is 1-5 and purchased; the chagrin process for when you act against it reduces its effective rating until you're sufficiently more like your heroic image or what have you. Like everything else, the descriptions of Renown take the time to remind you that Garou are horrible and cannot succeed after every description.

                                            Taking a moment again, let's all give the sidebar on Wolf touchstones a hearty fuck you for not knowing very much about wolves beyond lifespan and that they operate in different ways, which the text interprets as wholly lacking volition in a number of ways.

                                            XP is based on multiples of traits to raise it. Once again, very 90s. So modern. Very updated. Also, it's the developer's interpretation of it, so instead of current rating x multiple it's new rating x multiple. Milestone improvements are a brief suggestion.

                                            Much like Weapons of the Gods, there's a suggestion to make a relationship map. I find the one that's sketched up in the book distracting but also kind of hilarious because all but one of the names are culled from Rage. Like, not just W:tA or anything, but characters that showed up in Rage, the card game. Anna Kliminiski is noted as the mentor of Zachary (Ellison), Susan (Anthony) and Syntax operated under Jonas (Albrecht) and Raina (Fader - a Corax, and the only non-Rage character), and there was also Thomas (Kachina) as a bit of the odd man out. Note that Thomas was Younger Brother in past editions but a Galestalker here; everyone else is of the same tribe.

                                            Again, not exactly selling this as a reboot. Especially since there actually are hints of past characterization there - the Shadow Lords treat everyone as objects, Thomas doesn't like Syntax (a Glass Walker), Syntax is playing a dance around the Shadow Lord, and Susan Anthony, who was a kind of a (figurative) maneater, views Thomas as an object as well. In fact, everyone seems to share this standpoint about him, which comes across as really, really unfortunate given that the character was, you know, Younger Brother (and there's no real indication that he's not of mixed Italian - Native American descent anymore, if you happen to know the name and tribe associations).

                                            But it would line up completely with, you know, everything in James Sembrano's recounting of their treatment during this project.

                                                    And now for the Rules chapter.

                                                    The system doesn't go back to the old days of variable target numbers, but suggests that it might change from its fixed target of 6 on occasion. A light version of stunts from Exalted exists, where if you have a well-thought out response or action, you can reduce the difficulty (read: the number of successes required just for basic success on the roll) or possibly even just succeed. Health and Willpower operate as per Storytelling rather than Storyteller, so immediately, there's a great big compromise to the durability of the Garou, who could previously get mountains of soak dice. Also, it makes you roll temporary values, because apparently some lick fan wanted symmetry with all WP rolls and Dominate or something.

                                                    Like the CoD, only two types of modifier to rolls typically get used, and they're dedicated to specific circumstances. Difficulty is for external circumstances, while dice pool alterations are internal, character-based ones. You can also Take 10 Take Half on a roll to just succeed if no one wants to bother with potential swing on a roll. We're also told that W5 will let you do this in combat, something apparently frowned upon in V5 and H5, kind of like how Forsaken can just dispatch less powerful adversaries in certain forms in W:tF 2e. 10s are not doubled... unless you roll a pair of them, kind of. These are "critical wins" and how is it that modeling yourself after D&D is more modern, exactly? I'm calling that as very old school, if not 90s.

                                                    Not meeting the difficulty is introduced as winning with a cost. Well, at least one thing from no earlier than last decade got in. Willpower allows you to reroll three failed dice instead of getting a success. Is there a reason to spend it?

                                                    Combat and most social interactions are modeled as "conflict". This grinds my gears on turbo, because it's like Exalted 2e social combat speedrunning Willpower loss, and I also wholly reject the notion that social interaction is an exact mirror of tearing someone apart, or perhaps just beating or shooting them. Instead of having a Defense rating like in CoD, the system opted for a defense dice pool that you don't have to set aside actions to have. Getting ganged up on will eat away at said pool, but it's still reflexive. Combat, at any rate, is now a jousting contest where you want to get more successes than the opponent, like Shadowrun melee combat. Except that you still have to split your pool to attack multiple targets.

                                                    Initiative isn't used, instead having an order of action that follows from which action you use. V5 also set up the mandate of not making combat last longer than three turns, so if you came here to tear apart a crowd of foes, the writers want you to know that if you had fun doing this before, it was wrong and bad, and you should feel bad.

                                                    Damage has been flattened into superficial and aggravated. What, exactly, those two are depends on how tough you are; humans now take agg from almost everything that can break the skin or bones, and that matters for them, while for monsters, a lot of things that might have been lethal are now superficial. Also, Garou in other forms can make Rage checks to heal damage... any type of damage. Even aggravated. Crinos heals double time. This doesn't really have the feel of potentially dying but making a come back with a Rage roll to recover from the damage on the brink of death, though.

                                                    Also, someone was apparently incensed at the existence of Master of Fire, and went out of their way to emphasize fire being Serious Bad News, even though Garou have always had Kiss of Helios in the Gift section.

                                                    Feats of Strength now rely on Athletics. Yes, they kept the one dot "you can crush a can!" feat. Amazing use of word count and space, that.

                                                    Rage is no longer related to a permanent value, and runs one to five. Technically 0 to 5, because losing the wolf can happen really often, which is dumb, because they don't let vampires eliminate that last bit of Hunger unless they kill someone to do it. Rage checks can result in a rapid loss of it, since each die has a 50% chance to do so. Also, like Nightmare from Changeling, having Rage replaces dice in actions with Rage dice, which is basically the only manifestation of botching that remains in the system, and it can get you on a 1 or a 2, two of which will auto-fail you. How I long for a wild die system instead.

                                                    Since agg is the new lethal, you don't die immediately from being incapacitated with it, and can instead try to heal it, at the cost of two 50% Rage loss rolls per level. While knocked out from agg and out, you'll only die if you get hit by fire or silver. Or, like, someone uses lots of explosives.

                                                    Silver is a special case on its own, because it gives you Rage for every level you suffer. This is presumably why Silver Claws burns you for a level of damage each turn, accomplishing a similar end through an annoying means. At any rate, this is probably a good time to mention that you take Willpower damage if you're at Rage 5 and you gain Rage. Combined with the "Stamina does not soak, it just adds more HLs" model of play, this means that you're going to run out of WP fast if someone is using silver, unless you make a bunch of Rage checks. Homid and lupus don't take damage from silver.

                                                    The Five Forms are still here, but not really. As has been mentioned, no one involved in the production of the book apparently can tell the different between a wolf and German Shepherd, apparently, which harms the work via ignorance. The persistent insistence that Hispo is a dire wolf is also a bugbear that I'll refer the reader to the 1e AD&D Monster Manual about to clear up that mess. Glabro gains a lot because the forms all have blanket bonuses to all physical tests; lupus loses the most, because it use to be fast. Hispo was quizzically given the role of overland movement ace, which isn't good on any level (like the Veil, for starters).

                                                    Crinos. Crinos, Crinos, Crinos. The Vampire stans have had their eyes fixed on you for so long that they've lost sight of everything but stat boosts and a lame attempt to balance the game. Some even believe that limiting the time in Crinos is the key to making the game play more like a "traditional" werewolf, because it's the "win button" or something.

                                                    If that's the case, why is it that no werewolf in a movie gets punted out of the form for the rest of the night that they go on their rampage? Because according to the rules, Crinos induces frenzy, frenzy burns out all of your Rage after increasing it to 5 for the duration (hardly a good thing, recall; any increase in Rage is still going to hit your temporary Willpower), and it never lasts for more than one scene. Shorter, if you run out of targets. After that, you lose the wolf. So tell me, then, if you're brave enough to get a login and reply to this post on this forum: how does this model the nightly rampage of the Wolfman and company? You know, where they're rampaging out for the entire night?

                                                    And you're only worried about this because... someone might play mini golf or have tea in Crinos, and you think that looks silly? Or is it because you think the game is broken because of the stat boosts that vampires don't match? In this game, which is about something vastly different than V:tM and has never treated Crinos as the end-all, be-all, because there are a number of Gifts that alter it in some fashion.

                                                    So fuck temporary Crinos. It's clear that any intention for game balance or modeling of fiction has gone far, far awry to appease some bizarre form of concern trolling about a game.

                                                    Harano and Hauglosk are an annoying, double jeopardy, feel bad mechanic. You're offered some kind of risk and reward mechanic for being able to refill all your Willpower or Rage depending on which point you voluntarily take, but since you can't reduce them without a particular Loresheet at five dots, it's clearly not much of a reward. It just makes Werewolf more like Changeling, if you were in a Changeling chronicle where you were speedrunning accumulation of Banality and couldn't reduce temporary Banality with Glamour.

                                                    The Delirium does have a condensed chart with only three necessary entries. Finally, something that's caught back up with 2005 in gaming. It tries to chip away at the rationalization that humans typically engage in when they see recorded media of Crinos, though, so it's clearly trying to open up the SI to act on werewolves even though they don't really have the numbers, expertise, or equipment to do this.

                                                    Shit, do I hate the forms in this game. Nothing learned from Forsaken 2e beyond "Crinos blenderizes low-threat targets easily", and the only concern the writing seems to have is hemming in the capabilities of every form to a very, very irritating degree. It tries to set some of them up as having a purpose, but gives little to no support for this. At least lupus, it seems, can still communicate with other Garou when they lose the wolf. Small graces, cold comforts. I'm hating something that's making me think better of CoG Revised, personally.

                                                            If that's the case, why is it that no werewolf in a movie gets punted out of the form for the rest of the night that they go on their rampage? Because according to the rules, Crinos induces frenzy, frenzy burns out all of your Rage after increasing it to 5 for the duration (hardly a good thing, recall; any increase in Rage is still going to hit your temporary Willpower)
                                                            You can't really gain Rage during combat. It says:

                                                            Provocation, Harm, or Humiliation: If the Garou suffers harm, pain, or other emotional or physical agitation, they gain a point of Rage, maximum one per
                                                            turn. Note that once combat begins, most damage and injury don’t yield additional Rage; low-level provocation, such as insults and trash talk, don’t provoke further Rage, either.
                                                            And I think you have to run this game that way. You can't get Rage during combat and combat has to be "three turns and you're done".

                                                            First, why no Rage-gain during combat...? It's all about the Crinos-Form and Frenzy. Say you enter the Crinos Form in combat willingly. Then you have to pay 1 WP per turn or enter Frenzy. The first hurdle here is that if you were to get into a situation where you can't pay 1 WP and enter Frenzy... you're fucked. But if you include the idea of gaining Rage during combat, that would mean that once you enter Frenzy, you're in Crinos and gain Rage 5. But during Frenzy you can't use Gifts and you're stuck in Crinos. So no Rage-checks from Gifts or shifting. Aside from Regeneration you're stuck with Rage 5, so any Rage-gain would immediately turn into WP-damage. And then both in the former scenario of entering Frenzy because you can't pay the WP-cost or in the latter scenario where Rage-gain while having Rage 5 creates WP-damage, you run into the same issue: After you've killed the source of the Frenzy you have to do a WP-test whether you're about to try to kill everything in sight. But with the WP-damage involved in this process, you can't give Rage during combat because then you would almost guarantee that the Frenzy will go out of control.

                                                            Second, why the three turns and you're done...? Since gaining Rage during combat could lead to problems, you gain little to no Rage during combat. Of course, the first obvious issue is that narratively it's a bit lackluster (like a player asking "What do you mean I don't get Rage when I get attacked in combat...?"). But the other is that with that in mind between shifting, regenerating and using Gifts, even going into combat with Rage 5 might get used up pretty quickly... unless you stick to "three turns and you're done". That's why I was so baffled by the Isle of Splintered Dreams suggestion of setting up a combat-scene that mandated 8 turns or more.

                                                            And finally, the biggest irony of all is that W5 has created a solution that is anything but. Even if the complaint is that you don't like the idea of Garou being in Crinos for lengthy periods of time, the system of W5 is pushing for the opposite extreme. And we know that this came up during playtesting. Outstar talked about how in one of her playtesting-sessions nobody shifted into Crinos. And if you're dealing with particularly risk-averse players, it's obvious why they would never shift into Crinos. Especially if you're stingy with giving out Rage, you might refrain from shifting, using Gifts and regenerating because you don't want to be caught with your pants down due to losing your wolf.

                                                            Of course, even these risk-averse players might get further confirmation that they should avoid Crinos because of Frenzy. Because "severe provocations" can trigger Frenzy-checks. What are "severe provocations", you might wonder...? Who knows. It's all up to the ST to decide that.

                                                            The only concrete example of Frenzy-checks is the harming of Touchstones. And so hurting Touchstones is a double-whammy because the PC has to do a Frenzy-check and a Harano-check. And it's quite the thing what W5 supplements suggest you do with that. In both Scent of Decay and Red Tundra it's suggested that when you're dealing with a Pentex-front, you should take players' Touchstones and make them employees of the Pentex-front the PCs are about to attack. Like when it says in Scent of Decay:

                                                            Side Hustle
                                                            Storytellers looking to add some personal stakes to the story should consider an unexpected encounter with a pack member’s Touchstone or another Relationship Map connection. The fulfillment center is always hiring, and bumping into the character will complicate this scene no matter the approach the pack takes. The Touchstone took the job because they need more money, either to supplement their regular income, or because they lost their job and didn’t tell the pack member yet.​
                                                            Of course, neither Scent of Decay (like in this excerpt) nor Red Tundra end up addressing the full mechanical consequences of putting a Touchstone into harm's way like that.
