    Originally posted by DarkKing View Post
    The original plot for W5 actually sounds like it could have been halfway decent. A new Savage Age taking place in a post apocalyptic future would have been way more metal than this lukewarm version that tries to get us to sympathize with the fucking Fomori.
    When W5's key-terms are things like "The Garou Nation has fallen", "Gaia's dead/dying" and "Age of Apocalypse", it does feel like a natural conclusion to think of a post-apocalyptic setting. Especially coupled with the corebook's pessimistic opinion on Garou in general.

    But there are two sections in the book that I think describe well what the game aims for:

    There's a bit on caerns and the players' Pack on pg. 191:

    As a game, Werewolf: The Apocalypse assumes that the players’ characters will have their own caern, or that their pack will belong to the sept of a specific caern.
    So you already got the assumption that you're gonna be tied to a caern. And then there's a section concerning the usual scope of W5 games on pg. 212:

    The other axis is that of systemic / individual. Systemic antagonists are large, overarching entities or ideals. Systemic antagonists are massive and often decentralized and, in general, cannot be directly defeated. They can certainly face setbacks at the hands of the characters, but total defeat is almost never an option. Most systemic antagonists have local proxies that can be defeated, thereby hurting the global antagonist’s plans and relieving tension — the “little victories” that mean so much in Werewolf, because they address the player characters’ immediate goals. The Wyrm is one of the best examples of a systemic antagonist, as is Pentex Group.
    And there you have your "street-level only"-perspective by saying that it's all just about these "little victories". And so far, the kind of adventures that have been put out by Renegade can be sorted into two categories. The first one is "Small town, humans doing bad stuff and... would you look at that! It turns out the people in charge are Fomori!". This is all the Pentex-related stuff or some people just doing some bad stuff to the environment, other people and whatever. And the second one is "Hey, there's some other Garou over there... hang on a sec... They're Bad Guys!". And the second one is where you get Cult of Fenris, Black Spiral Dancers and the like.

    There are two that stand out, though, for being particularly weird in my opinion.

    The first one is the last adventure in the Scent Of Decay book with the rival Pack. Because that's about a Gaia-loyal Pack that has pledged itself to the Horned Serpent Tribal Patron Spirit. They're on a mission to deliberately let a small town fall to the Wyrm to study it, though. And the Horned Serpent supports them. This, it turns out, brings the Players' Pack into conflict with them because they want to stop the forces of the Wyrm in that small town. Very weird take on a Pack loyal to the Horned Serpent. Also it made me laugh that at one point, you encounter the Horned Serpent with the rival Pack present and the Horned Serpent is like "I want to hear both Packs about what they did and why." So you make your case and the rival Pack make their case for why they're right but then the Horned Serpent is like "Hmm, I've changed my mind. Whoever can recruit this one person who's a Kin first will get my support."

    The second one is "Rotten Home". The players' Pack is sent by their Sept to another one in order to build an alliance. The PCs get there and the Sept feels like a cult. Then you're asked to join a Legendary Rite that is all about telling the story of how the Weaver corrupted the Wyrm and that's what led to the dismal state of the world. Then the leader of the Sept tries to explain that fighting against the Weaver is more important than fighting against the Wyrm. Then the adventure essentially concludes with the players supposedly having to take a stance on this. One of the options is to actually start a debate over which one is worse, the Weaver or the Wyrm? According to the adventure, you have this evil heretic with evil ambitions... but all that's going on is there's just some creepy dude who wants to sell you on his elevator-pitch for what he calls "A really cool idea". And you can beat the crap out of him if you don't like his elevator-pitch. Thought that was a weird adventure.

                                    Here's the truly crazy part about the whole "oral history" thing and forgetting their culture and history: The most recent supplement Moonlit Path addressed this. There's a sidebar in the Introduction called "Oral History" and here's what it says:

                                    Much, even most, of Garou history is lost. To speak of an oral tradition is a misnomer. Rather, it comprises multiple traditions separated by geography. Writing down the legends of the Garou is frowned upon as threatening the Veil, and many werewolves see study and research as wasting valuable time when they should be bringing the fight to the Wyrm where many of them fall, taking their knowledge with them. Compounding the problem is that the Garou are not a culture one is born into. Rare is the one who hears the stories of Gaia’s chosen from their childhood. Those joining their ranks are usually young adults, already preoccupied with the concerns of life in this modern world.
                                    (You can find the entire sidebar here. It was part of the preview for Moonlit Path,)

                                    God, I forgot how this attempt at an explanation makes it even worse. First, the history was at times vague in previous editions because it was oral history, then in the W5 corebook it seemed to blame oral culture for why Garou forgot their history but with this supplement it now has morphed into "Actually Garou don't even really have an oral culture. The real issue is that memorializing Garou culture and history is just too hard, you know. So why bother?".

                                    The same way the key-terms like "Age of Apocalypse" and "Gaia's dead/dying" make a post-apocalyptic setting feel like a natural conclusion, I always thought that the natural conclusion to this whole "Who knows?"-business was to wonder why anybody would even bother to call themselves Garou then? If most werewolves barely know anything about the Garou's culture and history, and what little is known is all connected to their failures and how those are tied to this dismal state of the world... why bother calling yourself a Garou? Most of the Spirits (according to W5) don't like Garou, the other Shapeshifters don't like Garou, the Cult of Fenris don't like them, the Black Spiral Dancers don't like them etc., etc. And the Garou apparently don't even care to preserve their own culture and history. It doesn't seem like they even like themselves!

                                    And to return to the original intent of this thread, that's why W5 is so different to 1st edition. That's why I call it "antipathy". I really don't get the impression that this edition even likes the idea of Garou. And yet it's so bizarre that despite this, you're supposed to play the most vanilla bogstandard version of a Garou in this edition.
