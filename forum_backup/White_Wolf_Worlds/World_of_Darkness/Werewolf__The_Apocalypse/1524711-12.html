                            OVERALL GRADE: C-/D+ (lowered to D- if personal feelings on the Umbra are added in)

                            Whenever a new edition of a game comes out, especially one that is tagged with numerical subtitle (such as 'Fifth Edition'), one feels the need to compare it to the previous editions. Although W5 clearly states that it is more of a recon rather than a 'real' new edition, that subtitle suggests otherwise. While many terms and core concepts are brought forth, many other things, such as mechanics and metaplot, were not carried over. In all fairness, this game should be titled Generic Paradox/Renegade Werewolf Game, or, less facetiously perhaps, Werewolf: the Extinction, although I'd prefer to hold that subtitle out for a new, different game in the WOD that I have had thoughts about.

                            Many problematic terms and ideas were either changed or excised for W5. Some sort of change for these things were necessary, even if they could have been executed a little better. However, other things that most players didn't have many, if any, problems with were also changed. Not all of these changes were integrated well into the existing systems. In fact, many feel like they were just shoved in without thinking about what it would do to greater gameplay.

                            A main issue is the vagueness about the history of the Garou. The designers leave hints about what may have still occurred in the current game, but it comes out almost like laziness without having even a trace of why this has happened. Maybe entire generations of Garou were wiped out in the final battles, leaving not enough to carry on history and traditions. Maybe the spiritual portion of Garou history was sacrificed in order to win, but leaving a hole in Garou society. Anything would have been better than the feeling that management held certain ideas that the designers wanted to work around however possible, even if that meant leaving empty portions within W5 that could be filled in later.

                            There are a few new kernels of inspiration within the game, but nothing feels fleshed out. While there is the possibility that secrets were being held out for future supplements, this weakens the structure of the core book. Even a slightly deeper dive into these concepts would have been appreciated, but not enough background was given to help players build upon these ideas. Maybe, but more would have helped.

                            Perhaps it is just a generational things, but W5 feels like it is more an entirely different demographic than mine. While that isn't a 'bad' thing, alienating the older, core group of fans with so many changes is. Near total recons, like that of W5, can appeal to old and new fans alike, if done well. This book just misses the mark on that one point. Too much was changed just enough to hurt many long-time fans without justification. A primary example is that of the Umbra, in what used to be a location where players could spend entire campaigns for up to three months at a time has been reduced to an afterthought that becomes unusable after a few turns/minutes. The only reason I can see for a change this major feels like managerial fiat, as these rules function only to justify a strict style of gameplay, as opposed to previous editions that allowed multiple outlets for stories.

                            Now, I'm sure with house rules, players could adapt the better parts of W5 for their own 'legacy' versions of the game. However, players shouldn't have to cobble together a mixed version of the old and new if the W5 just followed through with their own presumption of being an update for the game. As it is, W5 is more of a new game based on the old one than it is the latest iteration. As such, it is not the "Fifth Edition" of Werewolf, but it is its own thing. As I previously mentioned, it is an average game with some problems that should be resolved in future printings. It is not, however, an average update of Werewolf. Too much was not carried over, and too much was changed/added, to truly consider this to be 'Fifth Edition.'

                            THWarted

                                The fourth and final phase of this review will start tomorrow, and it will focus on some of the issues and controversies raised since the news broke in the fall of 2022 that W5 had a release date. While using information outside of a game itself is not always standard for a review, in the special case of W5, I feel that it is necessary to do so. The amount of behind the scenes information that has come out since then reflects on how W5 was made, as well as the gaming industry as a whole. As such, a review of W5 based on said information would be useful in determining just why W5 has proven to be so decisive. So far, I have found it to be a just-below average game, with many mistakes and iffy ties to previous editions and other WOD games. This final phase should help clear up why such things have happened so far, and if there is any way to improve on any issues that have yet to be reconciled.

                                        PHASE FOUR PART ONE

                                        This is going to be complicated. My presence in social media is not as great as I would like, so much of the information I'm basing this section on could be considered second-hand, at the very least. However, I still feel that I have enough of an understanding of what has been publicly going on to form an opinion and relate everything back to the creation of W5, as well as where the game is possibly headed. I know that this phase will have some mistakes, so bear with me. I'll be skipping some of the early stages of the creation of W5, as they are not really pertinent until later on in the release cycle.

                                        The Gutting of the Get

                                        When the announcement of the release date of W5 came out in the fall of 2022, it came with fanfare and many questions. The announcement started with a table listing the eleven Tribes, their Patrons/Totems, and lists of 'verbs' describing how the Tribes worked. Two Tribes were missing from that list. The first, Stargazers, had technically not been a part of the Garou Nation since Revised. The other Tribe, Get of Fenris, were more noticeable in their disappearance. In interviews soon after the announcement, the developers said that the Get would not be playable. Further announcements summed up the decision as "The Get were fascist. Nobody likes fascists. So, the Tribe is not playable."

                                        Now, there were many possible ways to interpret this. First, the Get could simply be erased from the game. No mention at all. This could leave holes, but it would be a simple solution. Second, the Get could be eliminated 'off-camera' by some means. There could be a few left, mostly the ones not as bad, although there could be worse ones active too, but the Tribe would only exist as individuals, Third, a surprise, new twelfth Tribe could be formed, based on the positive attributes of the Tribe and minus the negative ones. Other Tribes had such a restructuring, so having the Get reformed wouldn't be a big deal. This would be a big secret to be revealed closer to the release or kept hidden until the actual game came out.

                                        However, the designers chose a fourth option.

                                        The Get, now renamed the Cult of Fenris, were now an antagonist, albeit one with similar aims at times. For some reason, the Tribe managed to keep a human cultural name when all others were changed. Apparently, the Cult were initially a camp of an unnamed Tribe that eventually took over their parent Tribe entirely. This even though 'camps' were never defined in W5 (normally as a division of a Tribe based on philosophical, cultural, or familial bases). How a Tribe like could keep up and expand like this, when genetic ties were removed is hard to grasp. Sure, the Tribe's Patron could chose, but the Cult is defined by their complete degeneration to Hauglosk which a new Garou cannot have. An adventure has since come out suggested how the Tribe 'recruits' new members, but there are suggestions that it was originally written for the BSD instead of the Cult. Regardless, this was a big slap in the face of a large segment of older fans.

                                        Now, many were unsure of why the developers chose this route. Many players came out saying that they never played the Get as fascist, possibly the majority of players. There were elements that could be considered fascist by some in other Tribes, but the developers possibly felt like these could be ignored without problem. And, despite a trigger-warning notice very late in the book, on the next-to-last page of the rules, there is nothing in the rules preventing players from using fascist-like tactics to further their goals. Concentrating power as a way of rebuilding the Garou Nation would be a sound decision, even if it could end up badly.

                                        Also, many people seem to be confusing other forms of vileness with fascism, even when they barely share many qualities. It is as if just calling something 'fascist' is an argument ender instead of having true debate. When people began decrying this decision online, their detailed explanations were drowned out by just such statements. Logic seemed to evaporate when the topic was brought up. This might be a generational thing on my part, but I can't seem to connect with why and how fascism became such a big deal and contentious topic. Sure, it was bad, but it was mostly historical to me, not something showing up in my current life. Even in recent news stories, I don't see why so many see such a big threat. Sometimes, I think some are just misusing the term just to make the worst possible comparisons possible, instead of looking deeply into an issue and discovering exactly what to call something.

                                        Back when the announcement on the Get not being playable came out, I mentioned something on a W20 Facebook group about how the game would deal with the Get. My idea, before the total recon would be known, was that Fenris would eat Antihelios, maybe unwillingly, and sacrifice himself to destroy the Perfect Metis, who was an avatar of the Defiler Wyrm. Its death howls would wrack the Tribe. Some would survive and join other Tribes. Others would go it alone, trying to reform the Get as something else. Others would fall, becoming a new threat. Now, a lot of this wouldn't be possible in W5, but the part about joining new Tribes is actually part of a Loresheet. There is no way of knowing exactly when in the development cycle the Loresheets were, although they reuse art from earlier in the book suggesting a rather late timeframe, especially considering how much was still being edited near the end. Maybe it was made early, as an attempt to appease older fans. Maybe it was created after the brouhaha started about losing the Get flamed online. There is no easy way to know. However, posts like mine could have influenced later decisions.

                                        While there may have been greater reasons for changing the Get, and speculations have been made on this matter, nothing greater has officially come out on this decision. The changes made to the playable Tribes were also a cause for detailed online discussions, none were as derisive as those for the Get.

                                        THWarted

                                                In the end, thanks to the convoluted route of W5's development the Cult of Fenris we ended up with makes no sense.

                                                One developer's fanciful egomania plus a circuitous attempt to make sense of it in a new paradigm that ultimately failed... It's another wrong-headed idea where a WOD5E game shot itself in the foot like when the H5 corebook was so insistent on demonizing hunter-orgs.

                                                And it doesn't help that the traces of the original extreme idea of "Get of Fenris=Nazis" remain in the book.

                                                There were so many ways to deal with this but the version we got in the W5 corebook was clumsy, watered-down and unfocused while still being tied to this extreme idea. And it doesn't even fit into the paradigm of W5! The description of the Cult mentions Valhalla, their symbols in the sample-adventure are called runes, they're called the Cult of Fenris, for god's sake...! The same way JF Sambrano's idea for the Ghost Council got copy-pasted with some lazy re-writes, the Cult of Fenris is a lazy re-write of the Hunters-Entertainment-version. And it's lazy because it's clear that no effort was put into making this work. The worldbuilding surrounding the Cult of Fenris is pretty much non-existent and makes little sense. The text just mentions events that happen god-knows-when and those events... just happen because the writing is pretty lazy. The text has the vibe of "Who even cares about why any of this matters or happens...?!"...

                                                Of course, we can speculate why the Cult of Fenris ended up being this lazy piece of writing that makes no sense within the paradigm of the W5 corebook but in terms of reviewing the book, it can only mean one thing, I think: It's bad. The Cult of Fenris makes W5 worse. Forget all the meta-stuff, here's the W5 reason why the Cult of Fenris make W5 worse: They're a waste of space because - who cares?! The Cult of Fenris are supposed to show off the extremities of Garou-society... except it's the Age of Apocalypse and there is no Garou-society anymore. The W5 corebook makes a big stink about how little the Litany matters anymore and how much of a failure the Garou Nation has been. So why would somehing like "extreme Garou behavior" even be something interesting to address? It can't be too extreme, though, because you also still have BSD in the setting. Making Fenrir more extreme than BSD makes no sense (because that would mean sometimes the Wyrm followers are the better allies), making them as extreme as BSD makes no sense (because then why would you need the BSD as a faction in the first place) and if they're less extreme... then they aren't antagonists unless the idea is to kill every Garou who doesn't operate the same way the PCs do (which would make them the villains). Other than that, it would just be Garou having different opinions in a Sept. You don't need that Cult of Fenris faction for that stuff.
