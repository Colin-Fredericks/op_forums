                    Missionary (Metis Philodox)

                    So this one, like the previous two, begins with a quote from a song by a female artist. In this case, Tori Amos's Crucify. And it misquotes it. It's "spit in their faces", not smash.

                    Our first Metis character. And also the first character who is presented as being part of a Camp, specifically the Order of Our Merciful Mother. Which is apparently presented in their write up in this book as being "many of them Metis". So to a certain degree, I can't help but wonder if having your only Metis template be a member of said Camp is maybe not the best exercise in creativity.

                    The Order has always gotten a bit of a mixed reaction from fans. Personally, I rather liked it, as it recognizes in its way the deep influence the Catholic Church's reverence for the Virgin Mary has had on how Western Civilization would view women.

                    Anyway, our character is a Metis, and was apparently raised by a werewolf pack. She's had mystic visions of the Goddess since before her first change, and was exposed to Christianity by a sympathetic preacher. It doesn't say anything about if this preacher was a human, Kinfolk, or Garou. I feel like maybe that would be important, but maybe not.
                    Her sense of compassion led her to start doing charity work overseas, and she's started to learn more about the human (and I presume not-so-human) sources of much of the misery she works against. While mostly peaceful and calm, she has her limits and will fight if necessary.

                    She's described as having human allies as well as being followed by "the Mother's spirit". I'll go ahead and mention that her Backgrounds are a 3 in Familiar Spirit. I want to say this was from the first edition Players Guide, but I don't have my copy handy to check. She has no dots in Allies.

                    Her Metis weakness is Fragile Claws. This is also apparently from the Players Guide. It's also the same deformity as the Black Fury Metis character Iobe Fareye in Rage Across the Amazon. Apparently this character build opted for making her hulkingly big and strong to compensate for this.

                    Her equipment is a jeep, medical supplies, and a flak jacket (size XXL!). Flak jackets were pretty much obsolete as body armor by the 80s, although the name sometimes still gets used to refer to more modern body armor. Actual flak jackets are actually meant to protect from shrapnel and aren't usually effective against bullets. I'm not really sure a werewolf would need one.

                    The artwork isn't bad. A very tall, athletic, Plane Jane type, in the sort of clothing you'd expect an aid worker in the Third World to wear back then. The jacket looks like the same sort of old Army surplus jacket I used to wear in high school. (And that's not a flak jacket, btw.) Overall, it's a good image. Nothing all that fancy. But nothing about it is bad.

                    As with the last one, Physical Attributes are primary, and we have an extra dot purchased with freebies, for a 5/3/3 spread. I don't really have a problem with this. It's an interesting dichotomy, with the character fitting the classic Gentle Giant archetype. Socials are tertiary, with a 3/2/1 spread. Our second with an Appearance of 1. And to be honest, I don't really see it reflected in the art. She looks completely average. I probably would've gone with a straight 2/2/2 myself. That leaves Mentals as secondary, with 3/3/2. Seems perfectly fine.

                    Talents are secondary, with an extra dot, for Alertness 2, Brawl 4 (with a specialty in Wrestling), Empathy 2, and Primal Urge 2. Skills are primary, with Drive 3, Firearms 2, Leadership 3, Repair 1, Stealth 2, and Survival 2. Knowledge is tertiary with Linguistics 2 and Medicine 3. Overall, it's a fairly solid spread. I'm not sure Firearms really fit the character. Maybe spend that instead on another dot of Repair and a single dot in Melee?

                    Rage is 5. Again, the power stat thing. From a revised standpoint, it feels too high. Gnosis is the natural 3 for Metis. Willpower is a whopping 7.

                    Overall, this is - once again - not a bad character. But I'm not sure it's a great one either. I think it represents an importance facet of the tribe, in the idea of the feminine side of human religion, but I'm not entirely sold on this as the ideal Philodox. It's not that she's necessarily bad as a Half-Moon. But I can't help but wonder if it might work better as a Theurge or Ragabash, maybe?

                                I think this is a case of a decent concept in theory, but not a very good execution.

                                Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
                                The Order has always gotten a bit of a mixed reaction from fans. Personally, I rather liked it, as it recognizes in its way the deep influence the Catholic Church's reverence for the Virgin Mary has had on how Western Civilization would view women.
                                I like the idea of the camp of the Order of the Merciful Mother. I can definitely see the appeal of the veneration of Mary in Catholicism, and how that might appeal to certain Black Furies. But this should be a legacy of the European Middle Ages. I can see this camp once having been dominant among Black Furies (in the Latin West at least) as it would have provided a convenient means for Black Furies to fit in. But this camp would have been declining for a very long time, certainly since the Protestant Reformation. For it to still be a thing, it has to be because of a persistent, but very small, group perpetuating it a sub-culture in the tribe. Probably because the Order has reconciled its belief in both Gaia and Abrahamic monotheism, and this belief is strong.

                                Making such a group primarily Metis however, seems like a cheap way to give Metis their own thing. (aka "We need to do something for the metis, so let's do that.") I don't think that makes sense UNLESS we assume the Black Fury tribe as a whole dump many of their metis onto the Order to raise, perhaps because there is some Catholic nun orphanage run by the Order of the Merciful Mother out there that is dedicated to raising them. Otherwise Metis being the bulk of a group based on a human religion is just bizarre. Homids would predominate. So I'm left with the idea that the Black Furies essentially send most of their metis to the order, and this is perhaps why it is tolerated by the Black Furies even now.

                                We should probably assume that "Order of the Merciful Mother" is in fact an actual, but obscure, Catholic religious congregation or institute in the fictional World of Darkness that is solely comprised of Black Furies and their kinfolk exclusively. There are some Catholics that heavily promote Mary as the Co-Redemptrix, and this devotion strikes other Christians (and even other Catholics) as a bit heretical. I assume the Order of the Merciful Mother is very strong on such language and really emphasizes Mary as a figure near equal to Jesus. Maybe not quite heretical, but sometimes running close to it.

                                So if I was every going to run a character (PC or NPC) as a member of the Order, that is how I would run it. The Christianity of such characters should be legitimate in the modern day, not just a cover. If it was just a cover, it would have been discarded long ago and no longer considered a camp, but at best some odd beliefs of some individual Garou likely in a few specific Black Fury kinfolk families. So there has to be some small villages somewhere in Europe and Latin America that is composed of Black Fury kinfolk that are strongly Catholic with a heavy dose of Marian veneration. And these villages have to be providing the backstory of new Black Fury homids who keep the Order alive while incorporating Metis dumped on them and the extremely rare convert.

                                Familiar Spirit is indeed a new Background from the 1e Players Guide. It is basically a Spirit Ally with some aspects of Totem. At three dots, this spirit would be a Jaggling which she could speak to aloud. It can store give extra points of Rage, Gnosis, or Willpower (pick two). It always knows where she is and can she through her eyes as well as peek through the Umbra. For a roleplaying perspective, the ST and player probably need a long talk about what kind of spirit it is that fits this concept, as I don't think there are any canonical spirit that fits. Should it be more like an Enigmatic spirit or Epiphling? Maybe a nature spirit that evokes St Francis of Assisi preaching to the animals and the Wolf of Gubbio? This relationship is going to be very important to the character, but it still needs to be appropriate for this Gaian cosmology of the game.

                                Like you, I would not spend Freebies on Rage and instead use it instead to boost some Abilities and Backgrounds needed to shore up the character concept. But otherwise I think it's a solid build mechanically. It's the lack of development of the Order of the Merciful Mother which is bad, especially in how they integrate their Christianity with Gaia beliefs. This really cannot be done in a Template, but required more detail earlier in the Tribebook. As such, this concept would be very hard for an inexperienced player to pull off. The weakest aspect is why this character is a Philodox. How is she a Keeper of the Ways? Is it that she is preserving the ways of this unique Catholic-Gaian syncretic belief and spreading her understanding of it to others (perhaps not in a sense of converting others, but demonstrating the wisdom of the Mother)? Something else?

                                The character certainly is not any iconic idea of a Black Fury. But in terms of showing an example of a new camp introduced in the Tribebook, I think its acceptable. A problem however is that the Template description implies this character runs in a pack composed of other Black Furies of this order. Perfectly fine as an NPC concept. But if these templates are considered to be something the ST can hand over to a new player (someone's friend who just showed up and is interested in playing), this doesn't do that. The template should have some wording that sets up this character in some way being open to join a generic pack.

                                                Warsinger (Lupus Galliard)

                                                So, this one starts out with another song quote. But somewhat surprisingly, it's by Jethro Tull. I was honestly expecting another female artist. It would've made an interesting and appropriate theme, if nothing else. There's also a second quote from John Irving's The Hotel New Hampshire. I actually find this mildly annoying. The lay out for a template write up can only include so many words, about 500 IIRC. So, including an extra quote is effectively padding which wastes words that could be better spent on the actual character.

                                                That said, this is an interesting character. Born as a wolf, after undergoing the first change she decided that being human was much more fun and exciting. Especially human music. She taught herself to play guitar. Being a werewolf is one big party to her.

                                                It describes her as being "trained as a witch" and Rituals being her life blood. This is something I'll come back to in a bit. She may be part of the Moon-Daughters camp, but it's not exactly clear on that aspect.

                                                Her equipment, in addition to a trench knife and sawed-off shotgun, includes a "serious collection of guitars, amps, and hard rock CDs." Now you may be asking yourself, "Self, just how does a wolf-born Garou come to own a bunch of somewhat expensive musical instruments and equipment?" And the answer to that is apparently that she has 2 dots in Resources. There's probably a really weird and potentially interesting backstory behind that. Unfortunately, absolutely nothing in this write up mentions it. I'll also mention that she has 4 dots in Performance with a specialization in "Guitar". Lupus, of course, have restrictions on certain Skills and Knowledges they can use their basic points on, based on the idea that they'd have no way to learn them before their first change. Performance isn't one of those restricted Skills, but I sort of feel like specializing in "Guitar" is mildly violating the spirit of those restrictions. Likewise, I've always sort of wondered why Lupus didn't also have certain restrictions on some Backgrounds. Like, for example, Resources. So, all in all, this is just sort of a weird aspect of this character.

                                                The character image is really great. Probably my favorite of the bunch, to be honest. The same sort of early 90s GenX riot grrl archetype as the Street Rat, just as an attractive and hot wild child instead of an ugly and obnoxious gutter punk. She's wearing cut off shorts and a crop top, with tattoos (including one on her arm that's the tribe symbol that apparently turns into a dragon or something near the bottom). And holding a bloody knife. I like it quite a bit.

                                                Attributes have Physicals as secondary, and once again with an extra dot, for a 3/3/3 spread. Socials are primary, with a 4/3/3 spread. That leaves Mentals as tertiary, with a 1/2/3 spread. So she's meant to be kind of oblivious, I guess? The text doesn't really indicate anything about it.
                                                For Abilities, Talents are primary with an extra dot. Brawl, Expression, and Streetwise are 3 each, Alertness and Intimidation are 2 each, and Primal Urge is 1. (I always kind of wondered why Streetwise wasn't on the list of restricted abilities.) Skills are secondary with an extra dot. Besides that 4 in Performance, she has 2 each in Drive and Survival, and 1 each in Melee and Repair. Drive is one of the restricted skills for Lupus. (You'd think maybe Repair would be as well, but no.) Knowledge is tertiary, with 1 dot each in Enigmas and Occult, and 3 dots in ... Music? It's sort of penciled in at the very bottom of the official list of knowledges. Remember what I said earlier about the character being trained as a witch and loving Rituals. Well, she has no points in the Rituals knowledge. I feel those Music dots are a total waste and should've been spent to raise Rituals and Occult or Enigmas to 2. (Also, the character has a sawed-off shotgun, but no Firearms.)

                                                Backgrounds are 2 each in Contacts and Resources. Rage is 6, which seems mostly OK for this character. Gnosis is ... 3. Yeah, Lupus get that at 5 minimum. So, I've no idea what's going on here. Either a misprint or a mistake on the writer's part. But if the latter, then the Developer should've caught it during the redlining of the first draft. Willpower is also 6.

                                                Once again, I don't hate this character. It's actually pretty interesting. But I'm not sure it's the best example of a Black Fury, especially a Black Fury Lupus. Even with the Galliard aspect, I feel like this is stuck in a lot of the very early presentation of "Galliard = Musician/Performer". Especially the whole rock guitarist thing. If this character had been one of the templates in Ways of the Wolf (a book I'll be getting to very soon), I think it would have fit better. Especially if it had been the Ragabash one.

                                                I also find it kind of bizarre that we have a Homid character who is "more wolf than wolf" and a Lupus character who wants to be a human. I honestly can't help but wonder if it'd been better if they'd been switched, with the Theurge being a Lupus and this one being a Homid.

                                                Next (hopefully tomorrow), the Ahroun, and some final thoughts.

                                                After that, I'm debating if I should do Bone Gnawers or Ways of the Wolf next.

                                                    That said I do agree that I don’t know if it’s the best way to represent the idea of a BF Galliard, although I do think this one works as a cool character a new player could pick up and be “oooh yeah I want to do that”. I def think she should be the inspiration for a signature character in the vein of Spooky Tooth or Baby Chorus. Plus it’s definitely got the punk side of the Gothic-Punk motif.

                                                    That said, what would the archetypal Black Fury Galliard look like?

                                                    The two main ideas that come to me are the Mystic-Memory type and the Scab-Picker. The former is kind of taking inspiration from the ideas of muses, divine poets/poet-priests, and some oracle imagery - basically a mystical character whose function is to recall the sacred truths and to reiterate them to the tribe and deepen their understanding of the various taboos and laws which they uphold (and conversely why it is necessary to respond to violations of them). The latter I see as someone who’s more interested in knowing precisely what violations were done and by who. They’re the Galliards who remind the Furies of past trespasses in order to both remind them of why they fight and also to get them revved up in preparation of major action.

                                                        The Warsinger is actually my favorite template description in the Black Furies Tribebook. The character sheet not so much. Although I do believe it's important to have distinctions between homid and lupus Garou, I don't believe lupus characters must be prevented from embracing their homid form and interest and humanity any more than homid characters must be prevented from embracing their lupus form and the wilderness. I think the description straddles that edge well. There's a certain feralness about her behavior and look as a homid. A lupus Garou who embraces those aspects of the Wyld in her homid form through artistic expression is valid.

                                                        The character sheet undermines that though. It's a case where someone's description of their character and their character build simply doesn't match. Your criticisms are all valid. But to be fair, I know a lot of players whose spoken character concept and character sheet don't really match. It's not an unusual problem.

                                                        The specialization in guitar doesn't make sense for a concept called the warsinger and a reference that her howl as a wolf could not be ignored. I normally don't like new characters, usually depicted as being older teen, having abilities that allow specialization. Lost cubs who only change as adults, and thus had time to develop their abilities, are different. But they are fine in certain circumstances. If the specialization was "singing" and not "guitar", I would be happy.

                                                        I'd remove her 2 dots in Drive and move them to Stealth and possibly Animal Ken. Her three dots in Music is wrong for all the reasons you mentioned. I think as a long term objective for this character to learn more about human music and musical theory is great. In fact, I think a lupus Garou with a beautiful howl becoming interested in her humanity because she's intrigued by human musical culture is a great idea. (Sidenote, while wolves generally have superior hearing to humans there is one big exception. Humans are great at identifying changes of pitch. We're almost unparalleled at that in the animal world. Most animals are terrible at that except bats. So I think it would be a good angle for this lupus to get into her homid form by really liking this one aspect where the human body is obviously superior to lupus form.) But as a brand new character, she shouldn't have three dots. She should just be starting her exploration of human music. As you mentioned, these would be much better put into Rituals with possibly one dot in Law, just to represent some knowledge of the Litany.

                                                        Her backgrounds make no sense at all. Even with an abiding interest in her human half, I don't see how she gets Resources or Contacts. What is that supposed to represent? Being some kind of a busker? Rites would make a better pick once she has actual dots in Rituals. (Rites like Gathering for the Departed and Rite of Wounding would be especially appropriate - rites that can take advantage of her singing voice.) Outside that Backgrounds like Mentor (someone to teach her about human culture) or Fetish (Harmony Flute or one of the other musical fetishes) would be good.

                                                        This is definitely a martial character so 6 Rage is fine. But once again, if an ST is going to take the Curse seriously, that likely prohibits any ability for this character to fit in with human society. That is not a dealbreaker. You can be a lupus Galliard and learn about human music through intermediaries, but it goes against the Backgrounds she has. And if the player wants to spend more time in the human world learning about her homid form and human society, it'd make better sense to keep Rage at 4. It just depends on the type of roleplay experience the player wants. Certainly the Freebies could be better spent on aligning the character sheet better to the description.

                                                        Warfare and music have very strong ties. Soldiers have been singing as they march and go into battle forever. And music has been used a lot to communicate in battle, increase your side's morale, and decrease your opponent's morale. I would have stressed this in any concept called warsinger. Less MTV and more war chants (but there's no issue in her using more modern musical instruments to explore this new world). It would tie it in stronger with the Galliard aspect, and you could even incorporate the section on Howls in the corebook.

The early books are very raw and present a society for the Garou that later books slowly (perhaps unintentionally) depart from. So I think doing Ways of the Wolf early is better than doing it later (like after all the tribebooks).
