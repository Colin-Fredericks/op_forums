    Pure Breed

    04-14-2025, 10:23 AM
    A lot of npc even without pure breed are described with a classic, almost signature look in their wolf forms. All silent striders even without pure breed has jackals or anubi features, for examples. Almost every get of fenris is grey or greysh-white.
    Should be more common in the common era, except for some more isolationist tribes, to have a more various look in wolf form? Take again the silent striders for example. They should be one of the most mixed tribe among the ethnic-centered tribes (all of them except BoneG and GW basically), but I never seen a white pelt Strider, or a standars grey wolf.

    Am I missing something from the lore ?

    -'' We are the unsullied.
    We are the inheritors.
    We are the Pure ''-

      It's just one of many things that many writers often don't consider in their work. Writers very often seem to ignore the impact of Rage (and the Curse) on their character's interaction with non-Garou, the impact of Gnosis on how they interpret their local environment and nearby Penumbra, how kinfolk are integrated into the local community, and more things despite that these concepts should be affecting how they impact the setting. How Pure Breed or its lack impacts a character's appearance is just another example. It just becomes a stat without reference to anything else. Just view it as a common weakness that shows up in writing because it is overlooked as writers concentrate on more important elements. It's not an intended commentary.

      However, that is something that is very easy for STs to fix in their own chronicles if they want to do so.

      In my chronicles, I do have the look of NPCs be impacted by their Pure Breed or lack thereof. High Pure Breed leads to the "classic" look of their tribes. As Pure Breed goes down, Garou begin to look more like generic wolves. Usually Pure Breed 3-5 retains the classic look, while Pure Breed 1-2 simply shows "signs" of the classic look. That is an arbitrary distinction I use simply to explain why Pure Breed 3 is the lowest the Silver Fangs will accept.

      Now it is possible that a Garou without Pure Breed might somehow show signs of something that is a classic Pure Breed. That is mostly chance combined with the local environment. Garou who live exclusively in Arctic or subarctic will pick up a lot of traits in their lupus form normally associated with the Silver Fangs or Wendigo because that is natural. Silent Striders native to North Africa (or indeed any Garou) without Pure Breed might have a more lean or jackal-like appearance because that is what the local environment selects for. It won't be confused for Pure Breed because lots of other things tie into that, but at least there will be superficial elements in common.

      Now certain traits may be so generic that it really shouldn't correspond to Pure Breed. Greyish wolves are extremely common so its appearance on Get of Fenris without Pure Breed shouldn't really be a surprise. What distinguishes Pure Breed Get of Fenris from their kin who have no Pure Breed are probably other features.

      These might be appearance based, but could be things normally not mentioned in character descriptions. They might be the type, length, and texture of the fur, not just its color. Things that appear in their homid or Crinos features, not their lupus ones. Shape and proportions of the head, tail, bone structure, or other parts of the body. Things like the gait or stride when they walk or run. Their scent. The Garou's temperament, demeanor, or bearing. How confidant, alert, serene, or composed they seem even in emotionally charged situations. Even as humans we say things like this person has a regal bearing or displays class.

      One thing I also do is select one attribute associated with a particular line of Garou with Pure Breed, and have that show up in some format of the Garou of that line. It could be a particular nose shape, bushy eyebrows, strong jaws, chin dimples, piercing eyes, large hands or feet, a particular pattern that shows on their lupus form, or anything else that might distinguish that line. I am also quite fond of giving common Merits or Flaws to NPCs of those lines, particularly Flaws that mimic the classic "tells" of werewolves like Banned Transformation, Forced Transformation, Mark of the Predator, or Sign of the Wolf. It's a way to show Pure Breed in general while distinguishing the Pure Breed of this particular family from others.

          Part of the problem is that wolves don't have a lot of naturally occurring coloration patterns, and most of the variation we see is from cross-breeding with other canids (dogs and coyotes mostly). 13 Tribes is just too many to combine a naturally occurring wolf coat pattern and the concept of "pure breed" denoting a specific heritage. If "pure breed" is going to make you look like an archetypal wolf from a given area... most of the Tribes are going to end up looking very same-y in their fur.

          As noted very astutely by Black Fox, a lot of how we differentiate between different populations of wolves are other factors. Wolves from northern populations tend to be larger and stockier to help endure long winters and match their reliance on larger prey animals. Wolves in southern populations tend to be smaller and slimmer. They need to navigate different types of forests and usually mix their diets more with small and medium sized prey which leads to differences in jaw shapes; the smaller size also helps out maneuver competition like native apex predator cats. Himalayan wolves have a very unique facial shape that helps them hunt more effectively when they have to rely on a higher proportion of small mammals that like to hide in shallow rock crevices.

          If we look at historical records, Fianna and Get that have high Pure Breed would like quite similar in their coats, but Scandinavian wolves are more likely to be heavier built than wolves in the UK or France.

          W20 tried to resolve this by just throwing natural history to the wind and calling it all spirit magic, but that's hard on consistency within the game-line.
