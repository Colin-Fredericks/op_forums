                                                            04-27-2022, 02:37 AM
                                                            The 'Garou Nation' as a term for all of garou-kind has been around for a long time. But earlier in WtA's publication history the setting seemed to be more localised, with each sept being kind of unique and the game being quite different, depending on where you set the game to take place. With the tribebooks came kind of templates how tribal septs might work and it removed some of the individualism. The remark that there are local rules held in the same regard as tenets of the Litany is still there in 2nd Edition and iirc revised, but it is not given any example in the supplements, at least not that I remember.
                                                            With revised and W20 came even more globalisation into the Setting. Which might make sense, as the Garou are basically a secret organisation that has a desire to communicate between septs. Even if they still would only use Silent Striders as delivery man, they could consume human news broadcasts from all over the world and aren't as oblivious to things that happen far away, as they might have been 200 years prior.

                                                            Personally, I don't like this global garou nation connotation. But the terms meaning has shifted over the editions, as the Setting's focus changed.

                                                            ----

                                                            As for the Native American tribe's names. I think it is okay to change them, as long as it is framed as something that happened over the last two decades or so, but i would not totally hate a retcon on this, too, for it is a correction of a design flaw in the Setting.

                                                            That said, I find an approach to reduce the stereotypes in many cases rather unfruitful. I mean, sure, there is merit to show that the fictional werewolf tribe culture is more diverse than attributes described to highlight stereotypes one might attribute to Native Americans,.... or people of Irish descent (drunk Fianna), or eastern European descent (all un-trustworthy cut-throats), or Asian descent (philosophical Kung-Fu masters) or Italian descent (Wise Guys Glass Walkers) or what ever other stereotypes there are.

                                                            But, stereotypes didn't just spring into existence. There is a reason they developed - which might have more or less truth at it's core. And for a work of fiction, one has to give the text some freedom to explore and use these stereotypes and build character archetypes around them. For, it is a tabletop role playing game and has a need to describe some easily understandable roles. Using stereotypes and archetypes is a legit way to do this. Ever read casting calls for actors? They often describe roles using such expressions. Nuance is added to character roles later, the template has to be more generic.
                                                            WtA is not a thesis for a anthropology PhD and shouldn't aim to be. Sidebars elaborating on topics the descriptions might neglect, are totally fine, though.

                                                            But, that said, I find that the reproach that KotE is orientalism might be objectively correct, but still it is something that I want and need of that Setting. A disclaimer or warning that the game is that way could have been helpful. And sure, the Setting might mix a lot of East-Asian culture, legend, myth, philosophy into a potpourri of a game Setting. But so do all the Core game lines to some extend.

                                                            When monteparnas said, the two Native American tribes are as if there were only two European tribes I thought, that's true. But come to think of it, there are three Native American tribes in the Setting - and the Coratans might have been subject an unfortunate name choice, too. But there are also only a few tribes which borrow of European cultures, too. That the Get's mixture of stereotypical Viking culture with 20th century Nazi Germany is a mess we already discussed. Besides that the Fianna borrow extensively from stereotypes attributed to Irish immigrants to America. The Irish aren't necessarily attributed like the Fianna in Germany (apart from American TV Shows). The Black Furies borrow from the Greek myths and symbolism, but are hardly attributed a lot of stereotypes usually associated with the Greek. The Wise Guys too borrow from Mafia-type archetypes, but hardly other stereotypes associated with the Italian. The Shadow Lords mainly follow a generic character stereotype than actual stereotypes associated with the region the tribe is said to originate from. Same is true for Silver Fang houses and Red Talons. Children of Gaia borrow from stereotypes of a sub-culture mainly, not necessarily from a human culture of a given region or nation.

                                                            So, should I be offended about the fact that Nazi Germany is the main (but not only) fallback whenever stereotypical German elements are used in a character. Germans are often described as meticulous and punctual. Try to add that to the Gets.
                                                            What about the French and Spanish? Are thy represented in WtA tribes? Not really. The Spanish are all Lasombra anyway.
                                                            The Portuguese and Netherlandish? Well, the later are at least probably housing a Silver Fang house, right?

                                                            So, does it do the Native American tribes and Nations justice that they are only represented in two living and one dead tribe? Most likely not.
                                                            Could it be done better? Probably.
                                                            Is a more or less complete description of the diverse and distinct Native American cultures necessary within the need of a tabletop role-playing game to convey character concepts and archetypes? A more generic description might be sufficient, I think.
                                                            Could the different Native American tribes be handled better (within the scope of a tabletop RPG) by reverting to WtA's 1st Edition's focus on local sept's differences and local distinctions? Maybe.
                                                            Could the different cultures of East-Asia with the general KotE Setting be handled better by adding distinct groups of Hsien, Hengeyokai, Mages, Bygone, Chimera, whatever, but still leave the overall Setting intact? Maybe.

                                                            Is using the word "werewolf" from the Proto-West-Germanic language cultural appropriation and should the game be henceforth called "Lycantroph: the Nit-Picking"? Hopefully not.
