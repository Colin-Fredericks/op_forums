    Activating a Gift = 1 Action?

    08-01-2015, 05:55 AM
    Hello, it may seems a stupid question but I can't find anything in the corebook that tells me what kind of action activating a gift is.

    In some gifts (like Razor Claws) it's said that the gift takes "1 full round to sharp the claws", but what's the general rule? If, let's say, I want to use Lambent Flame do I have to spend 1 action?

    My first storyteller told me that activating any gift is a reflexive action, unless otherwise stated, but this seems overpowered, especially with higher rank gifts in which I could do tons of damages and then still attack at full rage.

    How do you handle this? Is there any reference on the core 20th anniversary book that I am missing?

    Thanks

              Firstly, I use Revised rules for dice-splitting (first action subtracts [total number of actions] dice, every further action subtracts one more), meaning that even if a gift has no activation roll, it will at least make the other actions more difficult unless the entire turn consists of diceless actions.
              As for the full round: It takes exactly that, one full round, with no possibility for action-splitting or rage expenditure for more actions. Personally, I'm not quite sure how they figured this would balance out (Razor Claws already took the full turn in previous editions when it was pathetically weak), but that's how I play it.

                      The "standard rule" is that anything that is not specifically a reflexive action is a standard action, and thus "uses an action."

                      Reflexive actions are as follows:
                      - Yielding your initiative position.
                      - Spending Rage
                      - healing damage
                      - soaking damage
                      - reverting to breed form
                      - speaking

                      There are a few limits there; "speaking" does not include oration or other speech-derived actions (lying to the mall cop is an action, not a reflexive action - even if lying to mall cops is a reflex for you as it is for me!) Rage has internal limits to how much can be spent in a turn. With the exception of soaking damage, you have to be conscious for this stuff, too. There are also other things that are "reflexive" but aren't actually actions, like rolling for Frenzy or making a Willpower check to resist something.

                      However, each Gift is unique, and as far as actions are concerned, there are numerous types.

                      - Automatic Gifts. These are Gifts that have a permanent effect once learned, like Spirit of the Fray or Balance. it also includes "at will" gifts like Scent of Sight, Resist Toxin, or Call the Breeze
                      - Clearly Reflexive Gifts. These specifically use reflexive actions to function. Hare's Leap, Flame Dance, and Lightning Reflexes are the only core book Gifts that are specifically reflexive.
                      - Apparently Reflexive Gifts: These use reflexive mechanics and "feel" Reflexive, but are not specified to be Reflexive. City Running, for example.
                      - Standard action Gifts: Pretty much the majority of Gifts.
                      - Full-turn Gifts: These specify that they take a full turn to enact, and so can be your only action (besides reflexives.) Razor Claws is the most famous example.
                      - Multiple-turn Gifts: These are Gifts that require extended rolls. The only one I noticed in the core Book is Roll Over, but I've seen them elsewhere.
                      - Mini-Rituals: The activation of these Gifts is measured in minutes, rather than actions or turns. Song of the Earth Mother and Inner Strength.
                      ​- Action modifier Gifts: These are the weird ones. Things like Black Mark, Falling Touch, and Liar's Face. These say "When you do X, you can use this Gift as well." it's not clear if you're using them reflexively, or if you are splitting a standard action; some require rolls, others do not, but they all clearly take place at the same time as the action you are modifying with them.

                      I usually err on the side of power in RPG's - more powerful characters are generally more fun for players, and it gives me license to make more powerful dangers for them to face in turn. So I would rule that the last category are in fact reflexive actions that are reliant on succeeding the action they modify (Rattler's Bite doesn't work if you don't actually succeed your bite, and Liar's Face won't work if you botch your attempt to tell the truth - it can happen!)
