          Do not go through that rabbit hole, you have better options.

          If your players don't have any idea on how to improve their chances (acquiring allies, amassing fetishes, bringing in mundane military equipment or any other way to do it), you can provide them spiritual help. You, as the ST have the power to put spirits on their side, maybe due to some fate of them or the significance of this Caern to the spirits (spirits don't have to act on the same assumptions as werewolves do). You can even place a Tribal Totem or other Incarna to help their side.

          And if in the end you decide what they need is more xp just give it to them. Do not, under any circumstance, put in the players hands a tool to draw XP when they want, as adjusting the pace of growth in the game is your job.

          You don't have to follow the advice on the book for rewarding XP. Use anything you want, totem blessing, deep experiences, a dream journey to unlock hidden powers, whatever to justify, and then just give them as much XP as you deem necessary, it doesn't matter if it is two, three, forty times the usual amount.

                          This is still attaching too strict constraints on how they get XP and that's not how XP really works or what it represents.

                          XP isn't a natural resource that exists in the setting and the PCs get through defined means. It is an abstraction for growth meant to be used by the Storyteller as a means to achieve three goals:

                          1- Control the pace of growth in the chronicle, so you know what to expect as time passes;

                          2- Reward whatever you consider adequate behavior, encouraging the players to act in certain ways;

                          3- Share responsibility with the players on their growth, so you give the points, they chose how to spend them.

                          But that's all an Out-of-Character thing. XP isn't a narrative tool, it doesn't exist in the story, the characters don't have a concept for it or know it exists. It is not meant to be given following clear criteria. Those are just guides.

                          Your situation is that you decided to throw at them a challenge that you think is beyond their current power level. This is your problem, not the way to give them more XP. How do you solve it?

                          If you're not satisfied with other ways to solve it (give them more resources and allies, weaken or postpone the threat, or just play it as is and capture or let them escape, change the chronicle expectations to let the Caern fall) and you decide that it really is with their power level...

                          Well, just do it. Stop rationalizing how they get XP and just give them XP. Do not attach extra XP to the little things they do during this time, just let them do things to actually improve the Caern and then give them more XP than usual. You don't need any reason to do so beyond 'I want to'.

                          Another option is to give them the things they would buy with XP. Just give it directly. Have some totem teach them new Gifts, someone teach them skills, or even increase an Attribute rating. Cut the middle man and just let them know this is a one-time thing, not something they can count on in the future.

                              Also, something very specific to WtA?

                                                      Honestly? Talk to them. See what they want.

                                                      You don't have to tell them everything of course. But be honest, tell them you feel like you've guided them into a corner, and you're not sure how to get out of it in a way that they'll enjoy. Maybe they'll be OK with some deus ex machina to make it through the situation. Players do understand that a new ST is learning and sometimes mistakes take correcting in hard to hide ways. Maybe they're OK with doing the best they can with what they've got, and if it goes belly up, so be it.

                                                      You can do this for decades and still find yourself in a bind. The vast majority of players I've ever gamed with appreciate being asked about how to deal with such binds (it helps maintain agency even if the game is going to be low agency for a bit to undo the bind). I've never had a player get nasty with me for asking their opinion on things.
