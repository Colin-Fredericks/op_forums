                        This doesn't work, because Sony had the complete rights to make Fantastic Four movies without Marvel's input.

                        Their license contract had a clause that was reasonably common back in the day in which the contract self-renovates as long as they keep making movies on this IP with a given regularity. As long as they keep making super-hero movies, they keep the license in effect.

                        And this is exactly the same situation: Marvel could have negotiated something different and keep a lot more oversight at the time of the license agreement. They chose not to.                         Bottom line, Marvel made a bad license agreement with Sony that let the later make shitty movies and harm the IP while Marvel had no oversight or participation, and as a result those movies are Marvel's only in the broadest sense, yet they are in the sense that this license agreement was their decision.

                        Their numbers are good, but not thanks to WoD. For their size and niche, their numbers are pretty good thanks to their actual games, the ones they either make or oversee the making. They're becoming a strong-if-small name in the Real-Time Strategy market and do have signs of a solid growth that an bring them to the big leagues in the coming years. Not long ago their shares had an increase in value that made a lot of people really happy.

                        On the WoD side, though, things aren't good. They're not terrible, but aren't good. The key point here is to understand that the fans are not their main goal. It isn't cynicism or anything, it is just that their declared strategy is literally to make WoD a multimedia IP through licensing. So their focus isn't to make good games for the fans (while in their minds they may very well be trying their best anyway), but to attract strong license offers.

                        But those licenses aren't coming. For their size and the size of their core business, the problem is one of Opportunity Cost. They are having positive revenue with WoD, that's hard to deny, but they are far behind what would be sustainable for them as a business. If the IP doesn't make a minimum profit, they'll have to ditch investing on it sooner or later to use this money in greener pastures they do have anyway. They can keep a weak investment as a pet project for a long time, but not forever.

You're not supposed to want to be a Garou, you're supposed to want to fight the fight they claim as their own, without their regressive baggage. Which you can actually participate in in the real world, because you don't need super powers to engage in environmentalism, anti-corporatism, or fighting government excess.

                                                            We watch superheroes because it's a positive emotional rush that lets us imagine how awesome we would be with obscene amounts of personal power. We watch topical horror movies to question how well we're really doing on being part of the solution instead of part of the problem. Both are important. You can't do your part in fixing things without taking care of yourself with some fun, or you'll just burn out. But when it comes to movies and profits? Superhero escapism beats topical horror.
                                                            I mean, I agree vampire in particular is going to have a hard time because the protagonists literally have to eat people to keep going and are therefore inherently not good for easy straight forward tales with simple morality. But I feel like it wouldn't be that hard to make a straight forward mage or werewolf movie that cuts out the political message and gives a story about a plucky group of underdogs banding together to fight the BBEG who is a mad archmage gone rogue or a powerful corrupted nature spirit or something.
