    BNS Kickstarter for MET Werewolf: the Apocalypse

    01-09-2016, 07:12 PM
    The KickStarter for By Night Studios' Mind's Eye Theater Werewolf: the Apocalypse has begun.

    The setting's story has some... interesting updates. The rules and setting have been modified to update the setting from the last 20 years, as well as to make them more friendly to massive, or multi-chapter LARPs too.

    But for those who may not be keen on the book, I'd like to point out THE PINS.


    They're offering Tribal, Fera, Rank*, Auspice, and Breed pins as Stretch Goals.

    You can pledge a $5 minimum and add the pins as you'd like ($20 each + s/h with discounts in bulk.)

    *We've never had an official Rank glyph before! I'm curious what it looks like...

    Plus, check out this awesome Bone Gnawer piece by Scott Harben.


    Update: Breed pins were added!

                          Originally posted by Cogliostro View Post
                          (...) and the theatre of the mind is much better suited to that then the LARP environment.
                          The simple answer, and I mean this without snarkiness, is that it isn't one or the other; you bring the mind's eye into the LARP and it really does a great deal of the work. Imagination rules in a LARP, and you have to be willing to let yourself get caught up in the inertia of it. For some types of people, this is a lot more difficult - nothing wrong with that, it's just how their brains are wired, and they feed off of visual cues more than imaginary ones - but not impossible. In cases like that it seems like a LARP that has a greater dedication to costuming really helps bridge it.

                          In my experience, Werewolf invites a lot more storytelling because a lot more has to be imagination-spun, from not just the ST but each player. It makes it more crucial that you have a good, involved ST - moreso than a setting where you get more of the feedback visually, say maybe Vampire or Hunter - who can not just paint these scenes, but yarn you into them and stir your feelings. When the mind in interested and invested in something, it is naturally more inclined to be involved and fill in blanks.

                          After a strong Storyteller, you need to be willing to tap into your inner child just enough to not worry about how silly it might look when you start menacing someone with your clawed hand, curling your lip and snarling at them, with your other hand making a "C" (this is what we've used in our LARPs to indicate Crinos form) but once you get into the stride of it, it doesn't really matter. Everything else falls away, and your mind learns to read these posture cues and again fill in the blanks. But you, as a player, have to be willing to relax your grip on pride and just go for it with confidence, and weave for yourself a solid head space of your character. So a lot of the responsibility is on the individual player, I feel, to not just participate but contribute to the immersion, and that has a meta way of tying into the themes of community in the setting as a whole. Special effects are then largely just supplied by the mind's eye.

                                  Parlor LARPs, to me, are more an extension of tabletop because beyond some costuming and moving around, the "action" takes place in your mind.
                                  Friends of mine have been working on some foam-combat-style LARP rules for W:tA, which I think would be much more beneficial to a W:tA LARP game than MET. But if you understand that the scenes are narrated just like TT (basically it breaks down into TT with Rock/Paper/Scissors), then the expectation isn't that it's going to feel like a frenzied scene OOC, but you'll have to use your imagination like TT.
