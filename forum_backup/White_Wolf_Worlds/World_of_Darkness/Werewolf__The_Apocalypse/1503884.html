    Black Spiral Dancer Gifts

    02-25-2023, 05:59 AM
    I got my hands on the book of the Wyrm and i'm creating a big antagonist for my party. On the tribe gift list i saw 2 interesting but strange gift: Rank 2 Grave Claws and Rank 4 Stolen Hide.
    Grave Claws allows to automatically create a ghost from any being killed by the BSD for 1 scene, it say that those ghosts cannot harm their killer but it does not say that they must obey him despite their bond. So that gift does not change anything for the BSD unless he get to Rank 4 which allow him to bond the ghost to him and get 2 additionnal bruise levels.

    4 questions:
    -As those ghost are tormented and bonded to the BSD is there any rule or powers that allow him to summon them and command them (not all but a few at the same time) or is there nothing the BSD can do without the Stolen Hide Gift?
    -Is there a limit to the Stolen Hide gift that limit the number of ghost the BSD can turn into Bruised level?
    -Wouldn't it be better to combine these 2 as Rank 5 gift that allows the BSD to turn it's victim as slave ghost summonable at will to attack and automatically being bond to him at will (but reducing the bonus bruised health level to 1 per ghost)?
    -What are ghosts caracteristics for their powers and actions?

          I rarely like crossover Gifts like Grave Claws because there are always issues working out the details. Here are some general comments. First, while the Gift says "any creature", we know there are lots of creatures that do not become Wraiths. Animals do not become Wraiths for example. The general rule is that Garou also do not become Wraiths though the ST can always allow it. So first the ST has to decide whether this Gift really works on just humans, or if other creatures can be affected. Second, if we go by Wraith, people who die don't just become Wraiths. Usually there is an intermediate period of time where the Wraiths Caul forms, and she either breaks out herself or more usually someone reaps her. So does this happen here? Or should we assume the Gift expedites this process so the wraith immediately forms? Is the true purpose of the Gift just to torment the newly killed victim, or something else?

          Based on language in the Gift description, it seems that the BSD killer becomes a Fetter (likely Rank 5 and perhaps the only Fetter) for the new Wraith, and that the Wraith has an exceptionally strong Shadow. There is also some compulsion that prevents the Wraith from hurting the Dancer, although it is not clear to me how best to emulate that restriction using Wraith mechanics. It might just be ST fiat then or a unique Flaw that pertains just to this case. The wraith's existence is not "normal", but solely tied to the Dancer and will Transcend immediately if the Dancer is killed.

          Based on the language of the Gift, it gives no power or control over the Wraith. In fact, the Wraith does not even need to stay around the same area of the Shadowlands as the Dancer is in the Skinlands. So the benefit of this Gift to the Dancer seems dubious to me.

          The issue of what kinds of Gifts and Rites work on Wraiths is another of those troublesome crossover issues. I know some crossover rules were published in the Werewolf: the Wild West sourcebook Ghost Towns and, in all places, the Oblivion book for MET LARP. From my understanding, the use of these Gifts and Rites are very limited. Spirit Speech does allow communication, but powers that would compel Wraiths to obey do not work (like Command Spirit of Rite of Binding). I would probably allow the Rite of Summoning to be usable so at least the Dancer could attempt to "negotiate" some sort of service from the Wraith. However, overall I don't think this is the best use of ST and Player's time. You have to figure out a lot of things that a Werewolf ST probably doesn't know, and even if the ST does have and know the Wraith rules, it is still asking a lot for her to master yet another rules set just for the use of this one Gift. Usually when people ask about Wraith mechanics because they want to portray a ghost in their chronicle, my go-to suggestion is always to be inspired by actual ghost stories and don't worry about trying to use the actual wraith mechanics.

          There's no problem with an ST integrating Werewolf with Wraith (or any other game) if that is what they want to do. But that is a lot of work just to accommodate one or a few Gifts that happen to make reference to Wraith, and the ST really just wants to concentrate on Werewolf stuff.

          = = = = =
          I think the Grave Claws Gift really only exists for the Stolen Hide Gift later on. While the Stolen Hide Gift says nothing about a limit to the number of Wraiths it can apply to at one time, I think it is a very bad design mechanic to allow multiple benefits to stack. If you do that, you are going to have ridiculous characters that have a thousand or more Health Levels. So I would limit it to only one use at any one time, although once the Health Levels from one Wraith are gone, I would allow the Dancer to use the Stolen Hide Gift again to gain back those two Bruised Health Levels. But is this a good use for just a rank four Gift? This seems like a lot of worth for relatively little benefit.

          I don't recommend combining the two into another Gift that allows the Dancer to command a Wraith because of the crossover issues mentioned previously. It's not fair to the ST to make them learn another game system, and too often players want to heavily abuse the notion of a wraith servant to strike at foes who don't have any means of fighting back. That is just a headache too.

          After reading them, I am not a big fan of these two Gifts. I also dislike the idea that Stolen Hide consigns the Wraith to Oblivion after its plasm is destroyed as a Health Level. That just seems extremely unfair to the person's soul, and is totally against the themes of the Wraith which are highly psychological. Despite it being a possible downer of a game, Wraith actually isn't that nihilistic.

          As an ST I wouldn't allow either of these two Gifts. However, I might allow a version of the rank two gift that creates an additional two Bruised Health Levels. Dancer needs to follow the mechanics of both - spend an initial Gnosis to activate the Gift, and if someone dies they can then spend a Willpower and roll Stamina + Primal Urge against a standard difficulty of 6. If successful, the Gift cannot be used again until the two additional Health Levels are gone. Use whatever flavor text you like. That doesn't seem out of bound compared to the benefits of other rank two Gifts. But I'd defer to the opinion of people better at game mechanics balance than me to decide if there are any issues to that.

                      I'm also of the philosophy that this is not a player facing gift and as such doesn't need a lot of scrutiny. Even then i doubt that it's actually supposed to be interacting with Wraith mechanics. It's a nebulous "ghost" of a person you killed, ghosts are exactly what the narrative needs them to be and serve as the fluff here. They probably don't have any mechanics beyond what the gift specifies, and if we wanted to play with the idea of these collected ghosts being used in a different way, we could just use the simplified ghosts from the antagonists section, or better yet treat it like the Ajaba gift "Shadows of Serengeti" just refluffed for the evil Spiral ghost collector.

                      Of course considering the fact that being able to turn the ghosts into a action multiplier, there should be a cost and/or roll of some sort. I'd definitely have it require a gnosis expenditure to make the character unable to use rage actions on that turn.

                      If you wanted proper full ghostly servants, I'd go with an approach like dino's with binding spirits.
