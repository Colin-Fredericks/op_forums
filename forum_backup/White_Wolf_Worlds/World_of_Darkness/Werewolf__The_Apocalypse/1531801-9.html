                                                        Originally posted by MrornX View Post
                                                        Well Look at Rise of Sky walker and Halo Infinite, doing a 180 in story direction worked out great /s
                                                        While I get the sarcastic intent....

                                                        Rise of Skywalker made Disney a giant pile of money. So it did actually work out great for the company. It also think it worked out pretty well for Star Wars fans that they decided to stop trying to do blockbuster epic movies and put way more focus on streaming shows. While the shows have been varied in quality, they're easier to watch on their own, and generally have made the fans happier than the movies getting pulled in four or five different directions by people at the top.

                                                        Probably more importantly, Rise of the Skywalker didn't actually do a 180 on the plot, it did a 180 on the themes in a particularly self-undermining way. A big reveal isn't a change in the direction of the plot, it's a change in the context of the plot. W5 doesn't even have a plot. So it's hard to really compare what the hardcore fan reactions are going to be to Renegade trying to push more legacy material back into W5 despite the original intent for it to be a new take on the game.

                                                        Halo fans generally don't have a problem with Infinite doing a 180 on the plot because it was a course correction of the most hated Halo plot in the previous game, which was a 180 in its own way (totaling in a 360 with a whiff instead of a no scope). Halo Infinite's plot has problems, but it's mostly that Microsoft demanded that 343 half-ass the story and put all the focus on the multiplayer live service side of the game (and that sweet sweet microtransaction cash) and leave the story campaign to wither with a bare bones story and no DLC to pad it out (despite original intentions for DLC by 343 once they got told to get the multiplayer stuff out ASAP and had to cut story content for time).

                                                        Halo Infinite is a lot closer to V5 than W5: an attempt to keep the continuity with the previous games, while 'resetting' to something closer to the original formula ("Master Chief and a ragtag group of survivors land on a mysterious artificial ring-world after losing a major space battle, and have to fight to survive, control the ring, and uncover the dangerous secrets it hides" applies to Halo CE and Halo Infinite rather purposefully). You don't have to question if any of the plot of the previous games happened or not (they did), even if you have a lot of questions about what the hell happened during the time skip (a lot of which didn't get answered in the game). Unlike Halo fans though, TTRPG fans are used to reading supplements for more answers; most Halo fans don't want to have to read six tie-in novels to find out lots of details that probably should have at least gotten a bit of exposition in the games at some point (and 343's whole run has been bad about this in all three of their Halo games, not just Infinite). The state of the Halo plot in 2024 is actually in a pretty decent place, if you've read all the books, comics, and so on instead of just played the games.
