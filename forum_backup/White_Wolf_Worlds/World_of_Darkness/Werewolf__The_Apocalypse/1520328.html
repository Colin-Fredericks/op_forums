    W20 Various Doubts

    08-10-2023, 06:30 PM
    Greetings,

    Lots of minor questions, these might be common knowledge but I am a bit ignorant on many details (also I might be just wrong):
    1. Can the Changing Breeds get the Animal Magnetism merit? There is a bit of overlap with the Sex Appeal merit that some breeds can get.
    2. How does the Cat/Fox Magic merit work? It states that "The character can use this talent to mimic Gifts and rites from any Changing Breed" but Foxes already "can easily learn the Gifts of other Changing Breeds as well, as long as they have a teacher for the Gift.​" and I think Bastet can also copy from other breeds.
    3. Mechanically, what do you use Appeareance for besides first impressions? Charisma and Manipulation seem more impactful long term.
    4. Is there any limit to raising Gnosis, Rage and Willpower at character generation? It is a bit absurd to have a paragon of spiritual and mental fortitude as a starting character but seems perfectly valid by the rules.
    5. Hengeyokai refer to the Wyrm as the Centipede, and the Weaver as a Spider, which animal represents the Wyld?
    6. Can Kuei-Jin (or any other supernatural for tha matter) make use of Fetishes? By using a rite or power, I was reading the Nushi mechanics and it seems feasible, but I dont see it described anywhere. And it seems something that Mages could replicate, but no idea which spheres (Spirit + Prime perhaps?)
    7. Can Packmates teach rites to each other? The Core book says that the ones that teach must be Elders, but what stops two packamates from trading knowledge?
    I might post further questions based on the answers.

    Thanks,​

      Black Fox I tried to keep your contribution, I was able to copy from the original thread now

      Originally posted by Black Fox

      I think there are some keys to when Appearance should be used versus the other Social attributes.

      One is whenever decisions are made based on visual information as opposed to the spoken word. Those are not necessarily limited to first impressions (in the sense it is the first time someone has met you). The silent person who stands up and stares at a rowdy crowd to be more respectful to those around them? Appearance + Intimidation works good for that roll. Even if the rowdy crowd has seen or even spoken to that person before and thus is not a "first" impression. Likewise if you want to conceal something on your body, but not have it be obvious - roll Appearance + Stealth. Dressing to fit in like you belong to a group and are not an outsider, roll Appearance + Streetwise or Appearance + Law (or whatever) depending on the social environment you'll be in. Want to sleep (or goof off) while on guard duty, but still appear like you are doing your job? Use Appearance + Alertness.

      Anything that might involve body language would be another. You can communicate a lot of basic things through body language even if you have an actual spoken conversation, which if transcribed would appear ordinary and innocuous. Anything from "I don't like and you're not going to get anything from me" to "You are in danger if you stay because I am in danger as well, get out and call the police" to "I think you're hot, let's get out of here and have sex." You might want to extend that to other forms of non-verbal communication than just body language. What happens if you encounter someone who cannot speak the same language as you, but you need to communicate to them somehow? Appearance + Expression might be the best choice. Or you want to comfort someone in emotional pain or grief but think talking is inappropriate at this time? Appearance + Empathy could work. Someone taking others on a physical adventure and wants to show/teach them what kind of gear they need to take with them and how to pack it and carry it on themselves? They may want to outfit themselves first and display it to others visually because that is more effective than talking about it abstractly - roll Appearance + Survival.

      Anytime you want to showcase physical perfection or elegance. A ballerina wanting to appear graceful in a performance might not be rolling Dexterity + Performance, but Appearance + Performance instead. Or you might need to roll both for looking great while performing a particularly difficult stunt. A gymnast doing a particularly difficult stunt may need to roll Dexterity first to simply do it, but then also roll Appearance so she looks graceful and elegant while doing it in order to get that 10 out of 10. Similarly a gunfighter may need Dexterity + Firearms to actually hit a target, but if he wants to do an impressive looking quick draw or other gunslinging stunts use Appearance + Firearms. Want to impress the rubes that your occult ceremony is spectacular? Select the right clothing, aesthetics of your ritual area, and your physical performance of the rites by rolling Appearance + Occult.

      There may also be times when Appearance is an attribute used on people or objects other than yourself. If you can make yourself look good, then you can use your aesthetic skills to make someone else look good. Want to turn the ugly duckling wallflower into a stunning Cinderella? Use Appearance + Etiquette. A cosmetologist putting makeup on a customer (or a mortuary worker putting make up a dead person to make them presentable for the wake)? Appearance + Crafts.

      Now in some of these cases, an ST or player may want to achieve the same goal, but not want to use Appearance but another attribute. And I think that would be fine too. Very often there are multiple ways of accomplishing the same thing, and I'll allow players to suggest different trait combinations than the ones I originally ask for. But Appearance can definitely be used more often in the game than just "I want to look good in front of other people and have them find me attractive."

          Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
          Can the Changing Breeds get the Animal Magnetism merit? There is a bit of overlap with the Sex Appeal merit that some breeds can get.
          Generally the core book Merits are open to the other Changers. However all Merits and Flaws are meant to be up to ST discretion.

          How does the Cat/Fox Magic merit work?
          It lets you buy Numina Paths from books like Sorcerer. However, so it can be useful for people that don't buy non-WtA supplements, you can use Gifts and Rites to mechanically represent this instead (the same way the sample supernatural antagonists in the core book use Gifts instead of the powers of their supernatural type)
          .
          Mechanically, what do you use Appeareance for besides first impressions? Charisma and Manipulation seem more impactful long term.
          RAW Appearance is a dump stat. You can push a bit via using ST discretion, but if you want Appearance to be useful outside of a few Gifts, you're going to get into house rules.

          Is there any limit to raising Gnosis, Rage and Willpower at character generation? It is a bit absurd to have a paragon of spiritual and mental fortitude as a starting character but seems perfectly valid by the rules.
          There's no mechanical limits besides your limited Freebie points and them all capping at 10. Depending on your starting splats, you are either spending the vast majority of your points to do this, or even not even able to get to 10s in them.

          Raising Rage at normal char-gen is also a horrible idea, even if you buy up Gnosis and Willpower to avoid the worst possible outcomes. Starting Garou don't have the tools of more experienced characters to help resist Frenzies.

          As usual, this is why the games rely on people to run them: it's the ST's role to look at a character and decide if they're appropriate for the specific instance of the game about to be played.

          Hengeyokai refer to the Wyrm as the Centipede, and the Weaver as a Spider, which animal represents the Wyld?
          AFAIK there isn't one. The Wyld is... somewhat opposed to such labels. The original book uses the term "goblin" to describe the Wyld distorted Yang spirits of the Asian Umbra, though I don't think they specified which word for such monsters in an actual Asian language they were going with that might make it easier to apply an guess on what a local term for the Wyld would be.

          Can Kuei-Jin (or any other supernatural for tha matter) make use of Fetishes? By using a rite or power, I was reading the Nushi mechanics and it seems feasible, but I dont see it described anywhere. And it seems something that Mages could replicate, but no idea which spheres (Spirit + Prime perhaps?)
          A lot of supernaturals can potentially learn to use Fetishe, though how varies considerably. Mages just use Willpower instead of Gnosis to attune and activate them. They only need Spirit to make them. Though most Spirit mages would caution against using a Fetish without Spirit, since that's a great way to piss off your Wonder and have no idea how to placate it.

          Can Packmates teach rites to each other? The Core book says that the ones that teach must be Elders, but what stops two packamates from trading knowledge?
          The tradition is that you seek such things from high ranking Garou as it relates the tacit approval of your reasons to learn the Rite. There's no mechanical reason to stop packmates from teaching each other Rites, though the disrespect for tradition could result in a mild Renown penalty and/or a denial of the normal Renown for learning rites.

                  Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
                  Greetings,

                  Lots of minor questions, these might be common knowledge but I am a bit ignorant on many details (also I might be just wrong):
                  Can the Changing Breeds get the Animal Magnetism merit? There is a bit of overlap with the Sex Appeal merit that some breeds can get.
                  It'd probably less cringe if you called it Sex Appeal all around, frankly.


                  Mechanically, what do you use Appeareance for besides first impressions? Charisma and Manipulation seem more impactful long term.
                  Exalted 3rd edition has some suggestions on that front, as it's just used when Charisma or Manipulation might be, but when you're using your presence and looks instead of doing much talking. It's also used for leading mass combat from the front (something you're more likely to see in Werewolf or Changeling than most other WoD games), communicating via gesture, or a passive effect that messes with the dispositions of characters with lower Wits. It's also used for some Gifts, though this is less common.

                  In the original 1e versions of WoD games, the gating effect of first impressions was also more sensible - instead of limiting an entire social dice pool, it limited the final number of successes that applied to characters who didn't know you. So in Werewolf, what Appearance for is for getting your foot in the door when your Renown isn't high enough or people simply just don't know you.

                  Is there any limit to raising Gnosis, Rage and Willpower at character generation? It is a bit absurd to have a paragon of spiritual and mental fortitude as a starting character but seems perfectly valid by the rules.
                  Sometimes you've just lived a life that put you there. That's part of why lupus start with Gnosis 5, and why werebears start with Willpower 6. Just think of it as the character type that might not be quite as gifted in terms of expertise, but who has heart.

                  Also, there's a possibility for using some merits to blunt the impact of high Rage. Calm Heart places your difficulty 2 higher; I can't recall off hand if there are any other, but definitely do not get Mars Midsky if you have high Rage...

                  Hengeyokai refer to the Wyrm as the Centipede, and the Weaver as a Spider, which animal represents the Wyld?
                  Some Mages in Dragons of the East framed the Metaphysical Trinity and Triat as a tiger for Wyld, phoenix for Weaver, and dragon for the Wyrm, but this is counter to how those animals are typically presented in Sino-xenic cultures, which tend to go for four legendary beasts, with the tiger representing a rather more Weaver-ish bent because of associations with the xing of metal and Dragon Wyld-ish because of being used to represent the xing of wood.
