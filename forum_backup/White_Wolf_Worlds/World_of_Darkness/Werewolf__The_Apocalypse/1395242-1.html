    Honestly I think that the thing with Black Furies and the Get of Fenris is that in some ways they are more liberal then the other and in some ways they're more exacting then the other.

    The Get of Fenris expect excellence and strength from all of their children, but they don't expect them to equal the Garou and where they fail, so long as they can provide communal support for the Fenris warriors there's a lot of leeway for them.

    The Black Furies mean while work alongside their kinfolk and give them many opportunities yet many of them probably would chafe under the doctrine of the Wyld and elements of conservative Fury ideology, particularly male fury kinfolk who must be born feeling like they started life on the wrong foot.

    Neither tribe are demons nor saints and I have a deep and abiding passion for both of them, but I can see where people are coming from that either tribe might have its own unique benefits and shortcomings to being kinfolk within.

                    Both the Get of Fenris and Black Furies are militant tribes and have been shown as ruthlessly killing humans when they've felt like it. So these are not nice people. Both have also been shown as capable of compassion and mercy towards their own favored groups. I gave my reasons why I placed them in my schema, but I'm not surprised these are the ones that some people dispute. That's why I noted for both of these tribes that their location might be surprising. I think that's OK. In an early draft of the post, I placed these tribes (and several others) differently before I gave serious thought to the list and revised them. In the end, I felt as a more ideologically driven tribe, the Black Furies would have to be more controlling of their kinfolk than the Get who are more of a military elite.

                    People will interpret them differently based on what aspects of the tribe they prioritize above other aspects. This will vary from chronicle to chronicle. Them and the Glass Walkers were the tribes I struggled with the most. I'm comfortable with this schema and I know how I'd apply it in any given situation (which is much too complex to easily summarize in a short post). But I wouldn't complain about some other ST categorizing it differently if I was playing in someone else's game.

                    So while I can defend my placement of these tribes in that system, I would not say that people who disagree with me are "wrong." If someone argued that the Bone Gnawers were extremely controlling, but the Shadow Lords were not, I'd be heavily tempted to say the person was flat out wrong. But the Get and Furies? Let differences live.

                                        I'm still putting down my thoughts on what that "control" means, but I think however a tribe applies that control, to any individual Garou that control over any individual kinfolk must vary according to their relationship to that individual kinfolk. So I'm putting kinfolk into various categories that determines how much control a Garou can exercise over them. This is based on some of the conversation we've already had here.

                                        At this point, I'm not putting down what a Garou can do to a kinfolk in each category. That will come later. Right now I'm just trying to get those categories right. Here is what I think those categories are. But I'm very open to changes and additions.
                                        • Kinfolk who are Immediate Family to a Garou
                                        • Kinfolk who are Extended Family, Close Friends & Neighbors
                                        • Other Tribal Kinfolk within the Sept's Protectorate that a Garou has been appointed "Guardian" over
                                        • Other Tribal Kinfolk within the Sept's Protectorate
                                        • Non-Tribal Kinfolk within the Sept's Protectorate
                                        • Kinfolk outside of ANY Sept's Protectorate
                                        The basic idea is that a Garou can exercise the most control over Kinfolk they are "closer" to. So the more distant the group is, the less authority one has. For those kinfolk that don't have a Garou immediately "over" them, then the sept in general exercises control. Sometimes they appoint guardians over them. But if guardians can't be found, then the sept still has some authority over any kinfolk in their protectorate. Lastly are those kinfolk who for any reason have fallen outside any sept's claim. These kinfolk are the most "free". Some might even be unknowing kinfolk. Still, they may still have some obligations to the Garou (in the Garou's mind at least). And there has to be some sort of law or dispute mechanism that handles attempts by Garou to try to "claim" them. Garou society doesn't want a situation where multiple rival Garou try to control these kinfolk and end up killing each other over them.

                                        These categories are exclusive. So if you belong to one one group, you don't belong to another. Kinfolk don't need to worry about more than one Garou telling them what to do. But that doesn't mean other Garou can't put social pressure on that Garou to get them to do something she normally wouldn't do.

                                        Related are two issues I think need to be handled separately as they may not fit into the general categories above.

                                        The first is how Garou claim mates especially in the sense of whether their mate might not want to be claimed; and how disputes between Garou to that mate may be handled; and whether any rival kinfolk for that mate matters. It is highly likely that a Garou will seek a mate outside of the kinfolk they "control". A Garou won't seek a mate within their immediate family. They'll be looking for someone else who is likely under the "control" of another Garou, and as a result that mate will change groups. So there will always be potential conflict there. Mating may not mean an exclusive relationship like a marriage. I could see in some tribes kinfolk being married to each other, but that a Garou still has sexual rights with one of them so there is a nice ready built family for any cubs.

                                        The second is some actual hardcore rights under the Litany that kinfolk should have. This can be very basic and very limited. It might only be stuff like "can't be killed by a Garou without trial", or that a kinfolk's "activities with the Flock that don't interfere with the Garou's protectorate aren't under Garou law". Or that a kinfolk is guaranteed to keep enough resources so that they can continue to survive. These rights should be things that are relevant to kinfolk as they were back in the Stone Age because the Litany would have been created back then and reflect that. So it may be of limited use now, but some things are eternal.

                                        So do you think the categories should be changed? I think once we have a general sense of good categories, we can distinguish what can happen in each one.

                                        ​And of course, feel free to comment on any earlier posts. We're not moving on from those discussions. Just adding another item to the agenda.

                                            Alrighty so looking over The Litany, I think that these are probably the tenets of it that are the most relevant for a discussion regarding kinfolk:

                                            •Respect the Territory of Another
                                            •Submission to those of Higher Station
                                            •The First Share of the Kill for the Greatest in Station
                                            •Respect Those Beneathe Ye - All Are of Gaia

                                            I would also argue that to a certain degree the tenets 'Accept An Honorable Surrender', 'Ye Shall Not Eat the Flesh of Humans', 'Do Not Suffer the People to Tend thy Sickness', 'The Leader May Not Be Challenged During Wartime', and 'Ye Shall Take No Action That Causes A Caern to Be Violated'

                                            So basically, an argument can be made that nearly every tenet of The Litany impacts the relationship between the garou and their kinfolk.

                                            I think that the categories you propose make a lot of sense and I'd say that they are most likely supported by the Garou Litany and I'll try to answer some of the questions you asked using the Litany as a guiding principle.

                                            Firstly 'You Shall Respect The Territory of Another'. I can very much see this as representing the claim that garou have over kinfolk seen as theirs. You respect another garou's claim over their family. You respect their relationship with close friends - unless it violates your territorial claim over your kinfolk. Further more these claims can arguably be expanded to your pack's territory, your sept's territory, and your tribe's territory. The question then becomes which of these territories deserves the most respect...

                                            Then there is 'Submission to Those of Higher Station'. I think we can all imagine who the garou believe to be of higher station in the relationship between garou and kinfolk. From there, the question becomes I think, how else does this apply. I would imagine it provides some answers to the prior tenets question: whose claim to territory is greater - the one who is greater in station. They are after all the best able to protect their territory. However, I can also see this becoming a matter of great tension. All things being equal, you should heed the greatest in station. Yet what happens if the Silver Fang lord demands the Right of the First Night from a fianna's kinfolk. Whose claim is greater? The Silver Fang whose station is higher or the Fianna whose claim to territory is more direct. Ask your local Philodox!

                                            The next tenet works hand and hand with the last one I feel. 'The First Share of the Kill to the Greatest in Station'. Invoked literally this already gives garou and high rank garou in particular a great deal of power, which they feel is deserved given they have the most power to offer their people's efforts. Speaking figuratively though, we see how garou can invoke this in nearly every facet of their kinfolk's life. Your monthly pay check? All yours - after your uncle takes his cut. You farm land? Tend it how you like - just make sure that your aunt never goes hungry. Your car? Sorry about what happened, but your cousin needed it. I'm sure those claw marks can be buffed out. He's garou ya know.

                                            Then we have the tenet that is meant to check all of these imbalances. 'Respect Those Beneathe Ye - All Are of Gaia'. In a perfect world, this keeps things stable. Garou ensure that their kinfolk do not want. They protect them from the Wyrm and outsiders alike. They insure the congeniality of the spirits. They work diligently to assure their kin's happiness. They fulfill their Noble Obligation.

                                            As any Bone Gnawers can tell you, this is not always the case.

                                            It's from the violation of this tenet, this most important tenet, that I imagine the vast majority of kinfolk defections can be attributed to. Everything else can chafe and be unpleasant, but if there is justice it could be bearable. If the kinfolk had what they needed, most could endure. When they are left to the cold, when they are treated like they are expendable, when they are ignored; that is when the kinfolk most often break.

                                            They leave and all the Nation is lesser for it.

                                            Each of the tenets can be argued to have implications of their own. Kinfolk should accept their position with grace - an honorable surrender. Garou should not exploit their kinfolk unnecessarily - eating their flesh. Kinfolk should always provide something to benefit The Nation - and not suffer it to tend them in their sickness or futility. They should not unjustly challenge the garou - for are the garou not leaders of kin?

                                            What I think is probably one of the greatest hammers in a garou's arsenal against the kin is the last tenet. Ye Shall Take No Action That Causes A Caern To Be Violated.

                                            The Garou Nation is an army and the kinfolk are the logistics and support branches. An army marches on its stomach and without the kinfolk, the garou cannot fulfill their duty. If the garou cannot fulfill their duty, caerns are threatened. If caerns are threatened, Gaia is imperiled. If Gaia is in peril, The Apocalypse draws near.

                                            "So attend your duties kinfolk.

                                            Your failure draws further The Apocalypse..."
