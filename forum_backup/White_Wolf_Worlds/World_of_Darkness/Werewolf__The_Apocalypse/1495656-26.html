    Originally posted by MrNatas View Post
    At the end of the day I have to ask 'Who is this game being made for?' and to me it just looks like the game is being made for Justin. Which more power to him, It just reminds me a lot of Monte Cook's World of Darkness.
    This was a familiar refrain for H5 too.

    I don't mind indie games. But they have a different set of expectations, as others have said, so you need to actually commit to making an indie game.

    I loved Over the Edge (1e and 2e), where you have, like, three traits and that's it. I also loved UA1e and 2e where you have four attributes and freeform skills ("Being a Sexy MF" was just as valid as Melee).

    Those games intentionally kept things simple (OtE especially), but the games worked because everything was built from the ground up to work that way.

    V5 is a bit more mixed, but H5 isn't. It's a trad system with chunks carved out and handwavium used to paint over the gaps as if they were a feature, not a bug.

    W5 seems like it's going the same way. If combat should only be one to three rolls? Then ditch health levels and just have narrative effects (essentially simplified Conditions) to handle the results instead.

    A narrative system would be markedly different to V5 (which is too fiddly) or H5 (which feels unfinished).

    E.g., you could have five traits instead of the dozens we have. Maybe a Virtue, a Vice, a Concept, a Background/Upbringing and a Role/Profession. Allocate some ratings to each of those: one five, one four, one three, one two and one one. Maybe you can reduce the five to a four to move another dot to one of the others. That's it.

    In a fight, your Vice might apply, or your Role, or your Concept. Roll whatever makes sense. Your Background could cover your upbringing, education and social clout. Your Role is what you do now, in the party, or the profession that you had while alive.

    Damage would be negative traits you gain and would have to buy off with time and feeding scenes. Feeding scenes could be RPed or you could commit your downtime to them.

    Hunger and Humanity could be tied to Vice and Virtue, or again, could be represented by negative traits you accumulate and have to buy off with remorse or penitence scenes. Again, these could be RPed or you could spend downtime actions on them.

    Instead of XP, you could have something like narrative arcs (like Invisible Sun or 7th Sea) where you improve certain traits by completing the steps on a quest that changes you as a character. Or maybe you just do the Fate thing of moving dots between traits at the end of every story, to reflect how you change.

    Even Exalted Essence managed to get its Abilities down to a list of 10... At this point, if you carved out Charms and the Power-based combat system, EE would provide a better chassis for a narrative game than H5 does. And that's saying something.
