        Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
        That said, I can understand why White Wolf people would be reluctant to follow through on it after Bridges departed.         Yep. Once a creative person leaves, then any unwritten ideas you have cannot be exploited. Only what was actually published. And if all you have is the idea is that this thing does nothing except pop up once the Apocalypse begins, it's an idea that is unhelpful to STs. The only thing you can do is have some bad guy attempt to find it (Azarea in Rite of Passage, Baba Yaga in Rage Across Russia). And that too is not very helpful. All scenarios should be designed with the idea of "What happens if the PCs lose?" because that is always an option. If the choice is only victory or the setting is destroyed and unrecognizably changed (because it is now the Apocalypse), it's bad because few STs want to completely alter their setting so radically. So it ends up being "the PCs will win no matter what mistakes or failures they make" which is just as bad. Players actually don't like that. They want their actions to matter, just that any mistakes can be recoverable in some way.

        Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
        The idea the Talons might exert influence indirectly, despite being imprisoned, was evidently never considered by the folks at White Wolf. At least not after Bridges left.
        That does puzzle me. It seems so obvious. The Call of Cthulhu RPG usually assumes the Great Old Ones are not active, but slumbering. But that doesn't stop various cults and creatures around them from doing things. I basically see the Talons of the Wyrm as a substitute for the Great Old Ones. That's why I try to build some kind of faction around these entities. That way they can be utilized during play without running a plot where either the PCs succeed or the world is destroyed. You need stakes that are less than that most of the time.

        Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
        Thrassus is a force of decay. This includes decay of recollection. The ties of cultural knowledge fray and disintegrate, until no one can remember why they stood vigil over a site. The souring of memory is especially bad for cultures who rely on rich oral traditions, like Garou.
        I had not considered the idea of memory damage really hurting the oral culture and traditions of the Garou. But it is an obvious angle to take. And it fits well with a lot of horror tropes. Each generation of Garou near a Thrassus site slowly having their memory degraded of what exactly is down there, or the rites needed to keep it imprisoned, or what to do in certain circumstances, can lead to a lot of interesting things. Nothing so much that the loss is noticeable or obvious, but it just happens so that after a few centuries what is known by the oral history is much degraded.

        Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
        Perhaps Thrassus has minions that fill a similar niche to those half-mortal minions of the Fomorians.
        You could create a brand new monster, fomori, or bane to do it; or simply make it something that all things belonging to Thrassus possesses. Simply make it a unique Power or Charm that requires an expenditure of Willpower or something else, and then consult the Delerium chart or make a Willpower roll to resist. It doesn't need to always happen. Just some of the time. It just takes some deft uses by the ST to create the proper atmosphere or obtain buy-in from the players.

                Using this kind of "wild goose chase" plot is of limited utility for Storytellers. You don't want players thinking they wasted their time too often. An occasional romp of little consequence isn't too bad. But you can't rely on that, lest the players stop caring. More often than not, the threat needs to be real. Which brings us around to the Talons, themselves, being a threat that's TOO real.

                I like the concept of the container of Thrassus leaking. That way, there's single drops of concentrated evil that can be separated from the whole, to be sought and fought over.

                One might have dripped on the ground and now is buried deep in the soil of a location. Poisoning the local groundwater and possibly gestating some subterranean horror. It's in the land, it's in the water. It's in the people who drink the water and consume the fruits of the land. It's below, and all around you. It eats at foundations of buildings, until they collapse into stinking sinkholes. It eats at the flesh, the mind, the memory. Causing cancers and blood poisoning and amnesia and domestic violence.

                One drop may have been consumed or injected into a person decades ago. In a plot to slowly incubate a spiritual evil within their bloodline. Really play into the Gothic Horror element, where some long-buried sin of the family causes physical, moral, spiritual, and mental decay over generations. If Thrassus, as a stand-in for the Prince of Darkness, is a kind of Anti-God, are its cultists attempting to create an Anti-Christ? (Or the Wyrmish equivalent thereof). Or did the drop of the Talon's essence grant power to the bloodline's founder, but this power comes at a terrible cost? Slowly corrupting, rotting the family from the inside out. Would eradicating this bloodline even be helpful, if so much of their blood literally spilt would simply release the corruption into the environment? Perhaps that's the long-term plan.

                Importantly, decaying oral histories would cause more problems than simply hiding sites of the Talon's power. It also degrades bonds of friendship between packs, between Cairns and Septs, between whole Tribes. Connections of loyalty, friendship, mutual defense, and honor. All severed or become frayed. The Sept cannot be counted on to come to the aid of their allies, despite promises made and pacts forged in ages past to do so.

                This eroding of memory could even be selective. Groups of Garou forget the bonds of friendship, but remember grudges. Every positive interaction between groups or even individuals forgotten, while every slight, error, or insult endures. Love withers, while hate remains strong.

                It's an insidious weapon against the Wyrm's mightiest foes. The Garou need no help forgetting their duty and clashing with one another. But it's help Thrassus is more than happy to supply.

Common themes or degeneracy and decay leading to madness. So what kind of things might we take away from this? One common theme are artworks inspired by such mythos entities that cause madness in people. These are not occult tomes that must be studied by occultists. These are, on their surface, ordinary plays, music, paintings, etc. that reveal some truth of cosmic horror.
                            • The symbol of the Yellow Sign - anyone who possesses or sees – even by accident – a copy of the Sign is susceptible to some form of insidious mind control or possession
                            • The play King in Yellow - creates obsession and delusions
                            • The opera Massa Di Requiem Per Shuggay - provoked a riot
                            Over the centuries there have been works of art created by cultists or victims of Thrassus that have a strange and terrible effect on people. The physical works themselves are ordinary. Plenty of people have seen, read, or listened to them without incident. But something in their nature are portals to the essence of Thrassus. But since this is not universal, there are plenty of skeptics about such “forbidden” art works. We might take this further and say that when certain works are posted online that the digital versions somehow lack that portal. STs can always put that effect in, but I generally lean towards things that make it easier for the ST to explain why the Veil, Masquerade, etc. exists.

                            The physical artifacts of such works are completely harmless. However, their creators somehow implanted the psychic residue of Thrassus in their works. How such a thing can be done is impossible for the rational human mind to understand. Given the right conditions when a manifestation of Thrassus intrudes upon reality, these works are triggered like time bombs when they come in contact with the human mind. Like hypnosis or a hallucination, they hijack the mind of some people who are spiritually senstive but lack sufficient psychic protection. The lack of protection could be because of low Willpower, a low level of the local Gauntlet, being Wyrm tainted, or have their natural defenses weakened by use of drugs or poison. Those who are considered to be Innocent in the World of Darkness are somehow immune to this, just as they are protected in other ways.

                            But when triggered in certain minds, the person succumbs to madness.

                            So now we only need to think of some iconic works that exist in the World of Darkness, or perhaps hijack some that may already exist in Mage or another sourcebook. Maybe there is a banned Qing Dynasty Chinese opera about the “legendary” Centipede King of Hang Chu (mentioned in Infernalism: the Path of Screams). Or there is a unique set of non-standard Tarot Cards printed in the 17th century (“the Florentine Tarot”) that had its own strange card games and methods of divination. That kind of thing.

                                With my association of Thrassus with certain elements of Hastur and the Yellow King, I am inspired by something in the Yellow Dawn RPG. For those who don’t know it, it was an independent RPG setting in a post-apocalyptic world where the essence of Hastur has somehow embodied itself in Earth’s reality. It’s mainly a post-apocalypse survivor horror setting and the actual Hastur/Cthulhu Mythos elements are minimal.

                                However, one aspect in are humans reduced to a pseudo-zombie status similar to what we see in the movie 28 Days Later with humans infected by the Rage virus. I think there are elements here that can be repurposed for us.

                                The essence of Thrassus can infect some people who have a high spiritual sensitivty, low willpower, and are located in a place with a low Gauntlet. When this happens, the presence of Thrassus can overwhelm the mind and drive psychological changes in people. Their awareness expands as they perceive the overlapping layers of reality - the physical, the penumbral, the cthonic, the astral, and others - all at the same tme. Their experience becomes an utterly confusing, non-Euclidean, chaotic meta-reality. Victims become incoherent. It breaks down their sense of self, and they become in thrall to Thrassus.

                                Besides expanding their consciousness, contact with Thrassus also gives them Rage. They become utterly psychotic as they enter the Thrall of the Wyrm. These people lose themselves in an existence of horror and rage. From the perspective of others, the people seem to be having schizophrenic hallucinations and become incredibly aggressive and strong, like users of drugs like PCP.

                                They do not become fomori. Other than possessing one (or in rare cases more) point of Rage, they have no supernatural powers. They just become psychotic monsters. Their cognitive and personality centers of the brain shut done, and they become frenzied and aggressive. Per the Thrall of the Wyrm, they are driven to attack, kill and “consume” their prey. This is not from hunger, mere compulsion. As a result, the vast majority of victims engage in biting or gnawing their victims, but do not actually cannibalize them.

                                This is not a disease or spirit possession. Nor can victims pass their “infection” to another. It is the human mind succumbing to the psychic presence of Thrassus. When some aspect of Thrassus manifests somewhere, it contaminates the place and the spirituallysensitive.

                                These attacks are extremely rare, but have been recorded in every part of the world in every culture throughout history. Every culture interprets these attacks in ways that make sense to them, often involving supernatural causes, demonic possession, or witchcraft. Modern psychiatry refers to it as Spontaneous Induced Rage Personality Syndrome (SIRPS), and understand it by reference to various stress and personality factors creating schizophrenic hallucinations. SIRPS does not appear to be inheritable or the result of infection. It remains a medical mystery.

                                Historically, most victims end up beging killed by unaffected humans. But as psychiatrists better understand the syndrome, they are developing ways to help their patients. Currently some patients diagnosed with SIRPS are being kept in induced comas and heavy depressants after being subdued. There has been some success in integrating restrained patients back to sanity. The biomedical company DNA is a pioneer of medical research in this area, but has only had limited success in curing victims.

                                Garou recognize the victims, whom they refer to as the Rabid, as a sympton of the intrusion of Thrassus upon the world, and it usually provokes local septs to begin an investigation as to the location and type of Thrassus’ manifestation and the reason for its arrival. Some Garou believe that during the Apocalypse when Thrassus finally escapes its fetish prison that large segments of the human population will succumb and could push civilization into collapse.

But elements present in the game include: people being liquified and coalescing deep underground as a cancerous mass of flesh; the protagonist losing big chunks of time (as if their memory or perception of reality is unreliable); people (robots this time) devolving into distorted, violent zombies; visions of other places and times; "cycles of guilt", etc. As befits a game taking heavy inspiration from series like Resident Evil and Silent Hill.

                                            If you want to play it yourself, it's obviously on Steam and other outlets.

                                            If you don't want to play it yourself, but want to experience it and its plot points, I can recommend a Let's Play by Bricky.

                                            If you don't have time to watch a whole Let's Play, Charlatan Wonder did a good (albeit spoiler-full) overview.

                                                Originally posted by Black Fox View Post

                                                In my last post I mentioned that some elements we’ve created for Thrassus could be evocative of the Cthulhu Mythos entity Hastur and the King in Yellow. Common themes or degeneracy and decay leading to madness. So what kind of things might we take away from this? One common theme are artworks inspired by such mythos entities that cause madness in people. These are not occult tomes that must be studied by occultists. These are, on their surface, ordinary plays, music, paintings, etc. that reveal some truth of cosmic horror.
                                                • The symbol of the Yellow Sign - anyone who possesses or sees – even by accident – a copy of the Sign is susceptible to some form of insidious mind control or possession
                                                • The play King in Yellow - creates obsession and delusions
                                                • The opera Massa Di Requiem Per Shuggay - provoked a riot
                                                Over the centuries there have been works of art created by cultists or victims of Thrassus that have a strange and terrible effect on people. The physical works themselves are ordinary. Plenty of people have seen, read, or listened to them without incident. But something in their nature are portals to the essence of Thrassus. But since this is not universal, there are plenty of skeptics about such “forbidden” art works. We might take this further and say that when certain works are posted online that the digital versions somehow lack that portal. STs can always put that effect in, but I generally lean towards things that make it easier for the ST to explain why the Veil, Masquerade, etc. exists.

                                                The physical artifacts of such works are completely harmless. However, their creators somehow implanted the psychic residue of Thrassus in their works. How such a thing can be done is impossible for the rational human mind to understand. Given the right conditions when a manifestation of Thrassus intrudes upon reality, these works are triggered like time bombs when they come in contact with the human mind. Like hypnosis or a hallucination, they hijack the mind of some people who are spiritually senstive but lack sufficient psychic protection. The lack of protection could be because of low Willpower, a low level of the local Gauntlet, being Wyrm tainted, or have their natural defenses weakened by use of drugs or poison. Those who are considered to be Innocent in the World of Darkness are somehow immune to this, just as they are protected in other ways.

                                                But when triggered in certain minds, the person succumbs to madness.

                                                So now we only need to think of some iconic works that exist in the World of Darkness, or perhaps hijack some that may already exist in Mage or another sourcebook. Maybe there is a banned Qing Dynasty Chinese opera about the “legendary” Centipede King of Hang Chu (mentioned in Infernalism: the Path of Screams). Or there is a unique set of non-standard Tarot Cards printed in the 17th century (“the Florentine Tarot”) that had its own strange card games and methods of divination. Arcana unknown in other forms of Tarot, all lavishly but obscenely illustrated. The original sets are all said to be destroyed, and it is now only known by a heavily expurgated version that is barely known. A Persian book of poetry of the 10th-11th centuries about Judgment Day, but so blasphemous that the ulema universally condemned and destroyed all copies (although lost versions always seem to be found). That kind of thing.
                                                On the subject of works of art that induce madness in their audience, we can take some inspiration from two works I can think of off the top of my head.

                                                First, we have In the Mouth of Madness, another of John Carpenter's "Apocalypse Trilogy" along with the previously discussed Prince of Darkness and The Thing. It's about a horror writer whose writings become a vessel for The Old Ones to return to Earth, by driving people mad, mutating them, and eroding the veil of reality. It's said near the start of the film that the effects are at least initially contained to "less stable readers" (though this obviously ceases to be contained later). Causing disorientation, memory loss(!), and paranoia (and, as the protagonist discovers, later ballooning into physical mutation, homicidal violence, and/or suic*de).

                                                Parallels to HP Lovecraft's body of work is all but explicit, and very much intended by the director. Lining up its imagery and plot to Thrassus is a no-brainer, and it's appropriate given the other work in this "Trilogy" that directly inspired Thrassus to begin with.



                                                Second, there's Videodrome, an 80s Canadian horror film about pirated videos of apparent (and, as it turns out, real) snuff films unfolds into a mind-conspiracy of "socio-political warfare" in North America. Where works of psycho-sexual violence beget more violence in their viewers, and which also has a distinctly body-horror element. Whether these are true mutations (or warps in reality), or simply hallucination. Videodrome is also where we get the line "Long Live the New Flesh", which is something to chew on.

                                                There's a bit of an element of this "technology", the titular Videodrome, being used for different purposes depending on who used it. (It's creator was killed after he discovered his partners were using it for malevolent purposes). So if we crib ideas from it, we could examine how a similar tool might be employed by Thrassus cultists, rather than being a force of independent, self-perpetuating malignancy (although I doubt any form of it could ever be benign).



                                                Speaking of self-perpetuating malignancy, though, we might as well bring up the Ring franchise. A story about a cursed videotape that kills its viewers in 7 days (except when the timeframe is different) unless they copy the tape and have it viewed by someone else. In the series, the tapes were created by a psychic girl named Sadako (with a different name in other languages), who was murdered and now uses the tapes to spread knowledge of her demise. (Later novels attempted to establish a more Sci Fi explanation, involving a virus, but I find this less satisfying than it just being a curse).

                                                This concept can easily be ported over to Thrassus. A cursed work that not only kills or drives its audience mad, but must be spread to others to avoid the worst fate. Turning the work from not merely a spiritial landmine, but an active artistic contagion. Each person having to spread it to the next to escape death. Possibly leaving them "merely" insane for their trouble. Slowly deepening the spiritual corruption in the world.

                                                            Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                                                            When were the pig-men first discovered?
                                                            I couldn't really say when the first pig-men were created, but I imagine you could do some neat things with the introduction of the Domesticated Pig to the Americas by the Spanish.

                                                            A species first brought as a food source and tool of colonialism, pigs were dispersed in many Caribbean islands, and later the mainland. Often small numbers were left behind on islands by the Spanish, knowing by the time they came back later the population would have exploded all on its own. For use as ready-made food for colonists or marooned sailors. Even natives at the time would begin raising them.

                                                            That is, when pigs weren't escaping captivity and breeding in the wild. Some Spanish explorers reported finding wild hogs that had reached places before the explorers themselves had.

                                                            Simply the presence of mundane pigs was an ecological disaster for the "New World". Islands stripped bare of plants and fruit, that had no natural defenses against the pig. Moreover, European swine brought all manner of diseases and parasites with them, causing mass death (when they weren't killing natives through starvation from ecological collapse). Pigs are STILL a troubling invasive species in the Americas. In the US, it's often pig hunting season year round, just to thin their numbers.

                                                            Combine all this with the fact pigs can survive on anything, including human feces, and we've got plenty of data points to start concocting some pig-men action. Hell, the introduction of pigs to the Americas might, in and of itself, be part of the Wyrm's plan to cause mass death. Especially in regions that Garou have presence and/or Kinfolk. Humans didn't need encouragement to bring their bacon with them, but it certainly furthered the Wyrm's interests.

                                                            Imagine an island suffering ecological collapse. The people - whether native or colonists - become susceptible to the influence of Thrassus due to the stress of their situation. One or more start feeding the dead (or the dying) to their pigs, or leaving them staked at the edge of the jungle as offerings to wild hogs (or to Bane spirits that dwell among them). The transgression converts swine into swine-men. Still largely contained by the sea, but able to do lots of damage nonetheless.

                                                            Perhaps some swine-men shamen - Wyrm-priests and Thrassus-speakers - take up where their makers left off. Using foul rites to whip up storms, poison the ocean, draw humans to them via corrupt dream magic (creating totems that act as signal boosters for Thrassus in the material world), or even shroud their island in a mental fog that causes it to escape mapping or trade routes. All while lesser pig-men maybe hop into rafts or canoes, to make swift night-time raids on surrounding islands. Claiming supplies and prisoners, for use as food, sacrifice, or "breeding stock" (though this last you might be inclined to de-emphasize for the sake of good taste).

                                                            Through the forgetting-magic, the island might escape notice for centuries. (Who knows? Maybe this has something to do with the "Bermuda Triangle".). The island becomes visited by pirates (vintage or modern) who stumble upon it by accident while looking for hideouts from the law. Or by settlers, castaways, or tourists looking to party. (Imagine a "Fyre Fest" style event, whose organizers bring their guests en masse to an island on no map by mistake.
