    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post


    In a normal human family, parents telling their teen aged children that they're going to join the family business just like their parents did, and their grandparents did, and so on, is a recipe for shouting, saying things that are hard to take back, and slamming doors. With the Garou it's a good way to have family dinner ruined by fangs, fur, blood, and death.
    Well this is a load.
    For starters, for 98% of recorded human history this is exactly how humans worked. Your dad's a tailor? Congrats, you've got a great opportunity to learn to be a tailor. You could not be a tailor, but that's going to be significantly more difficult than being a tailor. So best be a tailor. If you're the sixth son and nobody looks to be dying anytime soon maybe your mother can pull some strings and get you into masonry, because the mason never had sons, and maybe has a few daughters left around... but anything else? You're going to be at a disadvantage. In times of scarcity "follow your passion" is fucking stupid advice. You follow what's best for you. tradesmen were a minority that had more choice than most. Farmers usually had none (also: I talk with a lot of Indians as part of my job. The steryeotypes are real: if you're talking to an engineer, her mother and father will also be engineers 90% of the time. The one person I met that didn't meet the trend described the drama that occured, but most are just happy to be engineers)

    More pressingly... your tribe wasn't your fucking job. your auspice was your job. Tribe was more like your nationality. If your parents are both french are you going to scream and rant and rave at having to be a frenchman? Are you going to go through a five year immigration process just because you don't like to be texpectedly french as parents? Of fucking course not. So why would a child raised by Shadow Lords rail against being a shadow lord for the sake of not wanting to be told what to do?

            Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
            For starters, for 98% of recorded human history this is exactly how humans worked.
            I'm not even going to get into a big thing about how historically inaccurate you're being because it doesn't actually matter.

            For the vast majority of human history, humans got stuck with jobs because they didn't have a choice. Sure. That doesn't mean they liked it, and it doesn't mean they were ignorant of the idea that there were other ways to live.

            That's the important part. Humans aren't rage-monsters. They can accept that lot in life and move on, because what else are they going to do? The Garou are rage-monsters. The build up of frustration and resentment when they're told they have to be something they don't want to be (esp. if never even given the chance to try other thing out) builds up Rage, leading to Frenzies and death of loved ones. In the old lore of the game, the vast majority of Garou have Rage-killed a loved one (generally during their First Change). That's normal for them (and not really examined as a reason why the Garou are as messed up as they are when you consider how many of them have untreated mental illness from that). And the only real reason that it's that bad is because the Garou lifestyle maximizes the risks of it rather than doing everything possible to minimize it. It doesn't have to be that way, the Garou created their society that way and decided to keep to those Traditions despite thousands of years of watching other options come and go.

            Tribe was more like your nationality.
            The problem is that Tribal status for the Garou is never guaranteed. They don't have birthright citizenship. It doesn't matter how much your Shadow Lord parents want you to be a Shadow Lord and how much you want to do it, if you fail your test and now have to hope one of the less strict Tribes will take you in so you don't become a Ronin.

            What W5 is really changing here, is that the tests are done solely by the spirits. The Garou can't try to put their thumbs on the scales by giving favored scions of their lineage cake-walk trials. If you're going to follow Thunder, you have to get Thunder's approval and Thunder's approval alone. And, again, if you're talking about a society built to keep Rage in check, this is clearly a better method because it takes away sources of tension and stress. If the choice is out of individual hands, then they don't build up the expectations that they can influence the results, and then don't have to deal with what happens if the results they want aren't going along smoothly (and, yet again, "drama" here means Frenzies and death and shouting and shame and trying to get people back in line with family expectations).

            It's also rather notable how you're completely flipping the historical argument around as it suits you. The concept of nationality you're using in extremely modern.

                                    Originally posted by Knightingale View Post
                                    Yeah, but Forsaken has only five Tribes in consideration for that. With W5 it's 11. That would necessarily mean what the Patron Spirits are looking for in an individual Garou has to be more specific in order to distinguish between the Tribes. And that specificity would increase the chances of upbringing and legacy not being enough in producing a Garou with the specific qualities to be a certain Tribe-member.
                                    Forsaken also has the Lodges to help here, as they have a much more significant impact on their members than Camps do in WtA (at least the old books). As completely customizable, but linked to the Tribes, they can bridge the space between the very broad Tribes and single spirit associated Packs. Lodges can be used to both add variety so that being in the same Tribe as other members of your family doesn't completely cut you off from individual choices about what that means, or as an enticement to stick close to your family as the only way to access the unique secrets a Lodge offers.

                                    Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post
                                    Its more of a general trend than an absolute...
                                    I don't think it really holds up as a general trend though. Part of it is that the different games have different numbers of splat to pick from, how they come about in-universe, and what level of mechanic impact they have.

                                    Vampires have Clans. In theory Sect can be picked by multi-Sect games have never been the default way the game is setup. Yes, that does mean as a generality vampires get chosen by their sires.

                                    Werewolves have Tribes and Auspices, and had Breeds as meaningful choices pre-W5. Part of the issue here is that because Auspice is absolutely selected at birth for a Garou the game plays up Tribe as being something the Garou have a say in by contrast, and even moreso with Breed being something you couldn't pick either. Tribe was the one thing Garou had a say in on some level, not what was picked for them. The Garou as "born" applies much more strongly to everything but Tribe.

                                    A mage's Avatar and Essence are beyond their personal choice, but mechanically not really significant in how they differ (though that's Mage's approach to a lot of things). Most mages are equally chosen as they choose, because they learn from mentors (unless they're Orphans/Hollowers aka organized Orphans). You can't just say, "I want to be a Hermetic," and you get to join, you have to find a Hermetic mentor that thinks you're up to snuff to take you in and teach you their methods. While mages have a lot of reasons in modern nights not to be picky about enthusiastic initiates, most of them are still described as knowing that a mage that's ill suited for their Tradition/Convention/Craft/etc. can cause all sorts of problems. Saying that mages "chose" as a general trend is very much ignoring the explicit (until M5 changes something) fact that their main meaningful splat can absolutely tell them to get lost.

                                    The comes a point where there's just too many exceptions for something to be a general trend.
