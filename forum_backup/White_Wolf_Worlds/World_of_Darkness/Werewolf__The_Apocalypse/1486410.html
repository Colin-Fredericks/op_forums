    Multiple Fetiche

    07-14-2022, 09:39 AM
    From what i've read, you can only put 1 spirit in a tool/weapon/object to create a fetiche, but what about a composed tool?
    Let's take an exemple: you build an armor, you turn into fetiche each part of it (gloves, boots, helmet...) to create a single armor with multiple powers, let's say a wind spirit in boot to run faster, a war spirit in the helmet to increase accuracy with guns, steel spirits into the main part to increase it's resistance, and a fire spirit in the gloves to get additionnal fire damages to the punch.

    Would you allow that kind of thing and how would you manage to make it work? A single roll but with a certain amount of success to activate the entire armor (in that case 4 minimum), spend 4 gnosis point? Activate them 1 by 1?

    I've heard about the tattoo fetiche too, so a draw or a mark can be used to create a fetiche, then would you allows someone to add on a single tool multiple marks to give it multiple powers, and would you let it activate them all at once, or 1 by 1?

      I'm rather strict with fetishes, and as a house rule even count attuned fetishes to the total number of dedicated objects (ie permanent Gnosis limits the total number of dedicated objects and fetishes). One spirit per object, and composite objects (like your armor example) count as a single object. If the player wants to upgrade their fetish, they need to find a stronger spirit to bind into it. If they want multiple effects, they need to find a spirit that matches all these effects.

      I have multiple reasons for this, but mainly because I prefer characters to rely more on their innate powers, and because the mechanics get unwieldy after a certain number. If I had a player who wanted to focus on crafting fetishes as a character concept like this, I would probably cook up a house rule to pool the abilities of bound spirits and then activate them as needed for a fetish. I would still make them pay for each activation of a single power.

              So:

              1) A Fetish can house more than one spirit, though each additional spirit makes it increasingly difficult to do. Two is considered the height of spirit-craft, and three would be a legendary feat. Multiple spirits do not, generally, result in multiple powers, because the spirits have to be compatible with each other and work together towards a common purpose (that is, two spirit Fetishes are going to be stronger versions of single spirit Fetishes of a similar design.

              2) Characters can use multiple Fetishes at once, and even split up something into smaller components to make each a Fetish. However doing so requires each to be activated separately.

              3a) The spirits in Fetishes are sentient and sapient beings who do not appreciate being used as mere tools to grant powers. Spirits can refuse to be bound into a Fetish, and can rebel if they feel misused or taken for granted. Each Fetish a character is attuned to is also an NPC with needs they're responsible for seeing to, so the more they have, the more time they use up in their day keeping their Fetishes happy.

              3b) The Garou Nation does not see Fetishes as private property. Mistreatment of the spirits via Fetish binding is grounds to have your Fetishes taken away from you by your fellow Garou because you're mistreating spirit allies, and punishment rites might get brought into the equation.

              -------------

              So, basically? The intended controls here are meant to mostly be social. Imagine a character that invests in a dozen Retainers of varying dot values and specialties, and wants to constantly bring them around. Fetishes might address the logistical nightmare part of that, but not the social difficulty of managing all those NPCs as a cohesive unit. Fetishes can be complex in function, but no matter how many spirits you try to stuff in one, it's still going to have "one" power (even if mechanically it might have 2-3 related abilities) not one per spirit.

              I'd suggest reading Hammer & Klaive if you haven't already. It doesn't completely address everything here, but it goes at lot farther than the core books do.

                  If I remember correctly, klaives are the only example of a fetish that has more than one spirit bound to them. One for the normal ability of a klaive to do aggravated damage, and the second for any special ability the klaive, or grand klaive, may have. It's one reason klaives are so difficult to make.

                  So if you wanted to model anything, I would use that as my example.

                  But I would personally limit how many spirits and powers are possible. I'd limit it to two. The first that allows the armor to soak, and the second for one extra power that makes sense for the armor.

                  If I were the ST, I would not approve of things like an armor with a shoulder canon or gravity boots, and so on. That seems way too gonzo for how the setting and its fetishes are typically portrayed. And the time and chiminage needed to make one likely prevents a character from making multiple sets of them. WtA does not have the same assumptions about crafting like some versions of D&D.

                  If you want to make multiple fetishes that can be used together, you could do that. But each fetish would need to be activated separately. And of course, each fetish would need to be made separately and all the spirits separately negotiated with. Again, making even one fetish takes a long time and commitment. After he's created all these fetishes, he probably wants to do something else. Unless you intend your character to do nothing else for the next decade or more for making all of them.

                  RPGs in general do not handle crafting well. The rules to do so are included to help the ST and PC create singular items important for the character. They are not meant as any kind of economic simulation, and the crafting rules don't reflect the true difficulty of creating all these items. Since as written, usually the crafting rules would imply far more fetishes, magic items, artifacts, or whatever than are actually present in the setting. As an ST, I'd have no problem with a PC devoting a portion of his time crafting one fetish, or over the course of an entire chronicle a few more. But if he intends to craft out a bunch for himself and then others, I wouldn't allow it. Crafting a fetish is essentially a spirit quest by itself. It's not something that should be shotgunned repeatedly. I'd start running the requirements to make a fetish a lot more severely, and requiring a lot more roleplay than just time and some dice rolls.

                      Thematically, this is something that would really be more fit for a Technocratic Convention rather than Garou. As the others pointed out, the culture of the Nation and the nature of Spirits makes Fetishes something really special. The Glasswalkers used to have a faction called Cyberdogs who specialized on techno-fetishes for body enhancements like this, and that didn't work out well for them.

                      I can see the appeal of a weapon smith character as concept though. My advice would be to make the 'armor project' a long term thing for the player- if they fulfill their self-set goal after only a few sessions, it won't be very satisfying. Give them a Mentor smith who is on their way out and wants to continue their legacy with an apprentice, let them gather info on journeys to other Caerns, find rare materials they would need etc. Make the spirits of Fetishes characters that demand to be treated as individuals, with their own goals and personalities. As a journeyman, they could learn about all the points the others made in this thread and why crafting Fetishes is no trivial smithing.

                      Mechanically, how about this:
                      The armor set is a single fetish, as usual. Its function is that it can store and use 'charges' at will of the wearer. These charges are specifically crafted Talens for the armor, which produce the extra effects. For example, an 'air charge' would be Talen that temporarily increases speed.

                      Talens are single use, so you can limit the total uses of the special armor effects with 'slots' and cap the power level. The spirits bound to them are usually also a lot less intelligent (although still sentient), so the creation as tools is less 'controversial'. More intelligent spirits of the same kind would still take offense if they aren't treated respectfully.

                                                  Originally posted by Cain Loup-Noir View Post
                                                  You sure spirits can refuse to work in fetishes?
                                                  Yes. Such fetishes are typically called cursed fetishes. It's up to the ST when this might be the case, as it's done pretty much for narrative purposes. The spirit is angry in some way and therefore the fetish is "acting up." It might be because the spirit was forced into the fetish, the fetish is being maltreated in some way, the original agreement for binding the spirit to the fetish is violated in some way, or whatever else the ST thinks might justify for the spirit being angry. Asmodai phrase that it is angry because it is not longer being treated as a "sacred object" seems the right take here.

                                                  What are the affects if the spirit in the fetish is angry, making it cursed? Again, this is up to the ST. It might mean needing to spend more Gnosis, having a higher difficulty to activate, that it stops working entirely at times, that it subtracts to the dice pool instead of adding it, or some other very inconvenient response that makes the fetish very unreliable to use.

                                                  Basically a cursed fetish is the spirit letting you know that it's best if you destroy the fetish and release the spirit because it isn't happy anymore for whatever reason. It's possible the spirit could be sufficiently appeased that it stops being cursed and the Garou can go back with using it with no concerns, but in many cases I would say many Garou would think it best to just release the spirit.

                                                  Technically, the spirits in a fetish are in a kind of slumber, but it isn't identical to slumber as there have been many statements in multiple sourcebooks of the spirits in fetish being somewhat aware of its surroundings especially in regards of whether it is being properly treated. I imagine it's more like the spirit is shifting between a waking dream and light sleep, rather than the deep sleep that "slumber" usually is.
