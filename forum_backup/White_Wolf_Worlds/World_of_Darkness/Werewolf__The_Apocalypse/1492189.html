    Hunting ideas for a Mage/Werewolf crossover: The Trial

    09-02-2022, 06:55 PM
    Hello! I come looking for ideas for my Mage/Werewolf crossover.

    My players (mages) have been accused of killing several Garou in the area, but they're innocent. The killers are other mages who are enemies of my players too.

    Being wrongly accused, my players are about to be hunted down by local werewolves. Nonetheless they could send them a message before the hunt starts: "We are willing to be put on Trial to prove our innocence. We are willing to submit ourselves to your laws - Garou laws - and accept any punishment you choose if we are found guilty". They did this to show the wolves who the real enemies are.

    I thought local Garou calling some elder from a neutral tribe to preside a Trial on those mages would be a cool and unique addition to the story. Thing is, it is still difficult form me to imagine how the whole thing would go (scenery, structure of the trial, etc).

    How do you imagine something like that happening?

          Submitting to Garou justice just means getting killed quicker. The laws of the Garou don't give any provisions for anyone who is not a Garou, and while they have some protections for Kinfolk, they'd be trumped by the fact that it's Mages and that Mages are generally seen as dangerous, assholes or dangerous assholes.

          What could work is something else - have an experienced local pack or elder vouch for them, maybe they helped them once or maybe they are kin, or maybe even <shock> they're friends or lovers - it won't give them a lot of time, but it might give them a bit of time to prove to that Elder they are clean, who might help them by reorienting the hunt onto the actual culprits. Problem is, any situation like this will have some young turks trying to make a name of themselves (or maybe even the allies opponents?) and not listening, so the PC's might still have to avoid those long enough to prove what's going on.

          Another option would be allowing the Garou to catch up to the PC's and then before the Garou rip the Mages apart have something so horrible that they need to team up to fight it off (Nexus Crawler?). This might buy the Mages a bit of respect and trust, enough to have a conversation - even if it will be a rather edgy, angry conversation they will need to tiptoe around. But hey, they might make lifelong friends if they play their cards right.

          Do not under any circumstance try going to the Werewolves and talking with them at their base/home/church, because you will get horribly murdered for not just the fact that "they" killed Garo but that you are a Mage and Mages tend to desecrate and drain their holy places just to get a quick fix of Power (Quintessence).

              As the others pointed out, direct confrontation is not a good idea, especially with blood still running hot. Make this apparent to the players, and have them find an indirect approach.

              I would try to introduce a mediator:

              - Having a Garou friend as Asmodai mentioned would be the best option. If the players can convince them, they know best how to approach the Sept or pack and plead their case.

              - Next best option is Kinfolk. Garou will listen to their family, and if the players are lucky, there may be a Sorcerer among the kin they can contact via mutual friends within a Craft or Tradition. There's also a chance that the killers ignored the families of the victims (as Mages sometimes do with Sleepers) which could mean there are witnesses.

              - If one of the players is proficient in the Spirit Sphere, contacting local Spirits might be a good idea. Maybe there is even one that witnessed the event. For a boon, there is a good chance a Spirit will contact a Theurge in the Sept and speak for the players. How convincing this is however depends somewhat on the type of Spirit, a trickster Spirit might not be believed as easily as a Spirit of justice or truth.

              - A ghost as mediator or witness is a fringe option. Garou usually don't go to the Shadowlands even after meeting violent deaths, so having a Wraith as witness only works if one of the victims was actually mortal or kinfolk, one of the crime scenes was haunted and so on. Even then, to make the Garou listen at all you probably would need a Silent Strider at hand.

              If the players can't figure out either option, you can have an NPC approach them and present it. Kinfolk in particular would be a good option for that role.

                                                  Originally posted by Asmodai View Post
                                                  Submitting to Garou justice just means getting killed quicker.
                                                  This is probably the most accurate statement given the setting. The players are being very naive and making lots of assumptions about the Garou, their society, and legal system. They are trying to do the "right" thing, but this seems to be more based on what the players think of as being fair and just, and not knowing how the Garou actually handle justice.

                                                  Probably only the Children of Gaia would provide a real trial and crime investigation as the players are hoping will be done. It is more difficult to see why the Garou do not simply hold their usual speedy trial and come to a judgment that same day which likely results in the deaths of the PCs. It's not that the Garou justice system is inherently unfair. It's just not designed to protect the rights of the accused or absolve the falsely accused. Different values (speedy resolution, maintenance of public order, deterrence of future crimes) take precedence.

                                                  Garou courts are not like modern court systems. There is no presumption of innocence. There is no excluding of evidence because it is tainted, possibly false, etc. There is no lengthy trial that gives everyone sufficient time to prepare for a defense. There are no appeals. There is not even the benefit of being represented by legal counsel.

                                                  This is the equivalent of a foreigner being accused of crimes in a dictatorship and willingly giving themselves over to that government in the expectation they will be tried fairly or accurately. It's just a bad idea.

                                                  It's like willingly giving up your right to an attorney in the US system because you are innocent and therefore don't need a lawyer. Any lawyer would tell that person what a bad idea that is.

                                                  I see several ways to handle this.

                                                  1) The ST runs the Garou as they likely are. PCs are killed. Players are shocked how this happened because they are running their PCs in such a nice way. Why are the Garou so stupid as to not appreciate that? Game breaks up in anger.

                                                  2) The ST has mercy on the PCs and runs the trial the way the players are expecting. Events run their course. PCs are happy. Except now the players have a very twisted way of seeing the Garou which is counter to the setting.

                                                  3) The ST informs the PCs that their tactics are not a good one and will result in their PCs getting killed. Instead, the ST advises the players of the better way for them to approach this situation. What they want is time to find the guilty party, provide proof these people did it, and send them off to the Garou for justice which then satisfies the Garou who then won't try to kill them. This might involve passing along a message to some Garou they know (or via someone they know who do know the Garou) in the hopes the Garou deliberate among themselves and decide to give them some time. But under no means should they actually put themselves under the power of the Garou. Work with the PCs so they figure out an alternate plan more likely to accomplish what they want. If needed, use a Mentor figure to advise them properly IC.

                                                  If the PCs already know a Garou, then that sympathetic Garou could be the one to tell them what a bad idea it is for them to surrender themselves and allow themselves to be put on trial. Declaring their innocence, trying to find someone to mediate who can buy them some time, and then have them go off and find the guilty people or just provide actual evidence is perfectly fine. But not walking in and putting your head on the executioner's block.

                                                  If you want more interaction with the Garou on this, maybe that one mediating Garou arranges a meeting with his pack who contains a Philodox and others, and they can explain why them surrendering themselves is a bad idea. This particular pack may be sympathetic given X, Y, and Z reasons, but the sept as a whole won't be. If the sept finds them guilty, are the PCs really going to meekly accept death? No, they'll fight. So the offer isn't really sincere, is it? It's all based on them assuming Garou justice will find them innocent which is a huge risk.

                                                  Option 3 is my recommendation. If you want some roleplay with Garou, you can do it without having an actual trial which will be very risky for the PCs.

                                                      Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                                                      Garou courts are not like modern court systems. There is no presumption of innocence. There is no excluding of evidence because it is tainted, possibly false, etc. There is no lengthy trial that gives everyone sufficient time to prepare for a defense. There are no appeals. There is not even the benefit of being represented by legal counsel.
                                                      The reason why I asked for more info is that while this is all true, this is all true.

                                                      Garou law does account for an important factor that human law tends to purposefully ignore in a sense: context. Not as in context of the crime, but context of the trial. It isn't ashamed of being biased.

                                                      Are there known downsides to killing those characters? If they have known relations with people the local sept may not want to piss off, or their actions really have helped the sept in such a way that they prefer to not be hasty in losing such an asset, there are many means for the sept to hold their horses and offer a more "fair" trial.

                                                      Here, though, the trick is to understand that this is not about fairness, mercy or trust. It is about convincing the sept that giving a chance is in their best interest and, that is important, low risk.

                                                      Overall just giving yourself to them in the hope that your goodwill will gain their fairness is absolutely stupid. But sometimes stupidity works for the wrong reasons, and this may work as an introduction to the interaction that will give them a real advantage, and then the goodwill may break the tie.

                                                      But then, we need more info. The sept and its circumstances dictate the most probable interests they could have, and knowing more about the Mages we'd know what they can theoretically provide. Is there a Son of Ether that may give some juicy tech to a Glass Walker Theurge? Can the Mages provide relevant information to an Uktena Bane-Tender and offer to get more? Would those Garou give a chance to the group in exchange for a hostage, a treasure or the Mages accepting a certain ritual or Gift without resisting?
