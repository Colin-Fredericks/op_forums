    Making talen versions of fetishes?

    08-06-2021, 10:04 AM
    One of my players just requested the following:

    Can I make a talen that's a tubular chime with a peace spirit bound into it that once activated gives the first garou (or theoretically vampire or other changing breed) that heard the chime -3 successes to their first frenzy roll?
    This seems to be the talen variation on a Harmony Flute:

    Harmony Flute
    Gnosis 5
    Carved from hickory, this small flute has many small songbird feathers decorating it. When activated and played (which requires a Performance roll), the flute emits an enchanting melody, reawakening ancient memories of peace from ages past, when the world was whole. When an aggressive creature hears the song, it must make a successful Rage roll or cease its struggles. Creatures without Rage may not resist the flute’s music. Any being listening to the sound may still defend itself if attacked. One activation lasts for as long as the werewolf continues to play. To create a harmony flute, one must bind a bird-spirit or a spirit of peace, calm, or water into the flute.
    What do you guys think? Is the talen in and of itself a balanced item? Should players be able to make one-off versions of more powerful fetishes?

          The mechanics of the Talen are kind of meh, but there's nothing inherently wrong with the idea. A Talen that restores a point of Willpower could be used to recoup any spent resisting a Frenzy, and be a bit more "one shot effect" appropriate. That's largely getting into matters of personal taste in a very vague part of the rules though.

          I don't think players should be directly copying Fetishes when designing custom Talens, mostly because that's kinda boring and I'd rather see effects oriented around the nature of each kind of item even if they have similar purposes. So yes, some sort of peace talen that helps tamp down on Rage is completely appropriate despite Harmony Flutes being a thing, but they shouldn't just be single use Harmony Flutes either.

          I actually advise players to look at Spirit Charms for possible Talen mechanics simply because it's a good gauge for the ST and they're not "ranked" like Gifts or Fetishes are.

              It's perfectly fine for talens and fetishes to have similar effects, but the end result should always seem to fit the general nature of talens which is not quite the same as a one time use of a fetish. The game in fact has a variety of talens and fetishes which kind of do the same thing, but using slightly different flavor text or mechanics.

              However, I do think there are some issues with the proposed talen as currently described. I would not approve it as an ST as written. Though I do think a good talen could be created that accomplishes what the PC really wants it to do. Your tastes could differ, but below are the reasons why I don't like the talen as currently written. These are not stated in the game, but they are guidelines I personally would use.

              First, the person who uses the talen is generally the person who makes use of its power. This talen does not work like that. Instead someone else – perhaps not even a Changing Breed since a vampire is mentioned, presumably even a fomori with Rage – benefits from it. Since talens must always be activated by a Gnosis roll, the Garou (or someone else with Gnosis) who uses the talen should be the person who benefits from it. Not some other person. The idea that someone else might be able to benefit is bizarre. And what does it mean that the first person who hears the chime gets the benefit? What is there is a group of appopriate candidates around the chime? Who benefits from it? Unless the user/activator of the chime somehow plugs their ears with wax or another means to deafen them, wouldn't it always be them anyway? Furthermore, even though Garou, Fera, and Vampires all have an intrinsic nature for Frenzy, it is possible for others to Frenzy as well as various powers can sometimes do that to even normal people or animals. So why wouldn't they be able to benefit from it if they were the “first” to hear it? As written, this leads to a lot of trouble in my opinion. The person who gains powers from the talen should always be the person who activates it. (This holds true even for things like Gaia's Breath which technically heals another, in fact it is more like the activator getting use of the Mother's Touch Gift – it gives him the power to heal).

              Second, talens take effect when they are used. Some talens are instantaneous and others have prolonged duration, but all begin when the talen is activated. In general they aren't things that can be activated “now”, but not take effect until some future time later. And that is what this talen does. It is “used” when the chime goes off, but the effect doesn't happen until the person has a future frenzy roll which might never happen, or not happen until a long time afterwards. At best, it'd be something like the benefit starts once the chime goes off and lasts for a proscribed amount of time afterwards – say a scene or hour (or some other time period you like whether it is shorter or longer) and then expires. As long as the user has a Frenzy roll within that time period, he gets the benefit (once, this isn't something that helps multiple Frenzy rolls even if they all happen with that hour). If the next Frenzy roll happens 1 second after that time, they should not get any benefit.

              Third, talens are typically consumed after they are used. Their physical vessels no longer exist or are at least not usable. I presume Bane Arrows are broken after they hit. Wyrm Scales combust in flames. Moon Signs have their seals broken. Chiopteran Spires turn to sawdust. Death Dust, Gaia's Breath, and Nightshade are used up or consumed with use. Very often the act of activation itself forces the user to destroy the talen. This isn't a 100% universal law, but it's clearly a motif. And I think it's the right one. I would consider it best practice to emulate that. You don't want the item to remain intact that could theoretically house another spirit and become a talen once again. You want to force the character to create yet another brand new vessel if he wants to create a new talen, not reuse an old one. Something like a flute made out of a fresh reed that ends up splitting after being activated, rendering it useless. Or if you keep with a chime, then something like the strings holding the chimes snapping and the other components tarnishing or cracking. I would prefer something like a flute made out of a reed that splits or crack after use because to me it seems like the most appropriate kind of thing for the Garou. A chime seems much more permanent than something like a reed flute But again, this is really a matter of taste.

              Fourth, what do you want the power to actually do and what is the best mechanic to do so? The basic idea, that it helps prevent someone from frenzying, is fine. But I think you need to figure out the best mechanic to do so. Stockpiling negative three successes to use sometime in the future when a rage roll is triggered is a strange mechanic to me. There actually isn't a “frenzy roll” in the game. There are only “Rage rolls”. Garou can make lots of Rage rolls during a game. It's only when they roll four or more successes is when they frenzy. So what's the intent? To remove 3 successes the first time they make a Rage Roll? But sometimes a player WANTS to make a rage roll, like when remaining active after their Health Levels fall below Incap. So what if you get 3 successes on your Rage roll? Presumably the PC doesn't want to fail because of the activated talen. I assume the PC only wants the talen's power to happen only if he gets 4 successes, which brings a frenzy. That would subtract the successes on the Rage roll down from 4 to 1, allowing the Rage roll to restore one Health level to allow the Garou to remain active, but prevent frenzy. But what if the Garou wants to heal more health levels and would prefer to have 4 successes even if they enter frenzy as a cost? This is why this kind of “contingent on things” mechanic is bad. Or maybe the PC never considered he would actually want to make a rage roll and intended it to always apply. In any case, stockpiled negative successes to be used in the future when conditions are meant is not a typical game mechanic. I don't like it.

              Furthermore, as explained before, the benefit of the talen should only happen to the Garou who activates the fetish. And presumably anything that would provoke a Rage roll does not give the user of the talen enough time to activate the talen.

              I think a better mechanic would be that activating the Talen essentially gives the talen user a power similar to a Gift like Calm (second level Child of Gaia tribal gift) or Calm the Savage Beast (level three homid gift) whichever best works for your purposes. It gives them the power to either cancels the frenzy, eliminates one or more temporary points of Rage, or does something else to someone else who hears him playing the music.

              Now the above only helps in a situation where the user wants to help another person come out of frenzy. It can't be used to protect the talen user himself from entering frenzy. If that is what the PC really wants done, we need to come up with a different mechanic. However, the game doesn't support a talen that turns on only when an event happens. Talens need to be activated by rolling your Gnosis. So what you would want is something like the user activates the talen, and the power of the talen is open for a certain amount of time afterwards – one scene, one hour, or similar. Then if that even happens (like a Rage roll), the power automatically happens (only once). But if that even never happens, the talen is still used even though you got no benefit from it. Depending on what you want done, you may just want the effect to be “it cancels one frenzy” (within the allotted time period after activation). Or maybe you want it it to add a +3 difficulty to Rage rolls (maximum 10). Or some other simple mechanic. But in this case, I don't think a musical instrument would make a good protective talisman. I would choose something more like a protective amulet that needs to be rubbed before activation as opposed to simply playing music and that somehow protects you in the future. But your tastes may differ.

              At this moment in time, I can't think of a common way that a talen should be able to cancel frenzy in another person as well as protect oneself. All the ideas I have suggest either one or the other. But that doesn't mean you and your PC group (or someone else on the forum) can't come up with something good that would do both. If you can come up with something that makes sense thematically and you're happy with, go for it.

              So I wouldn't use the talen as described. I think the basic idea the PC wants to do is fine. But you should follow the above guidelines to help you and the player figure out an acceptable talen to accomplish what he wants.
