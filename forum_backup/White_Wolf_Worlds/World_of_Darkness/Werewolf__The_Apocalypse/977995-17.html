    Not accepting men into the tribe severely cripples reproductive capability. Kinfolk births outnumber garou 10 to 1, but kinfolk-kinfolk offspring are 10 times less likely to breed true than Garou-kinfolk offspring. Therefor, in most tribes, assuming monogamy among males, the ratio of kinfolk born garou to garou-kinfolk born humans is roughly 1:1.

    However, if you eliminate male garou; you're in a pickle. Males can put their seed into a lot of people with little consequence, or they can repeatedly impregnate the same kinfolk. Female garou, on the other hand, can't afford to reproduce like that. A female Garou is out of action for nine months every childbirth, which is objectively a problem if you're running a numbers game. It's significantly easier on female lupus, who take three months (but require the right season) and i suspect black furies have a decent lupus ratio for this reason alone, but we also need to factor in:
    The bred true children that are given away, on account of being male (not a massive problem for the nation, since they'll find other tribes, but not great for the Furies)
    The effect that this decreased birth rate has over time. The majority of the tribe will come from kinfolk or adoptions from other tribes. All things considered, the Furies are most certainly one of the smallest tribes because they lack male garou.

    This, I believe, is a massive flaw, Arguably moreso than being too close to the wyld or hating the weaver. Please consider this when you allow transexuals into the Black Furies. They'd both be invaluable for breeding and yet to breed in the manner of man somewhat denies that you've changed sex. (yes, I'm aware that being a Transexual doesn't mean sexual orientation has changed, but I believe the point still stands, especially with the emphasis on maiden/mother/crone)
    Also. Has this been mentioned yet here? I was under the assumption that it'd be a big thing.

Given that we see related terms being used to describe 'getting old' because it made men less virile and physically powerful, there's more at play too, and we also see a few people who seem quite happy being described by the terms crop up in the sagas.

                                            While it is likely more than a few of the Norse would have found transwomen pretty strange, if they just shrug and go 'yes, I am unmanly. That might be because I am not a man', that's the end of it unless the accuser happens to be an absolute dickhead and keeps pushing. In which case you can, if you like, kick the shit out of him for being a dickhead. It's not a perfect system, but it's not as simple as 'ergi = trans'. A lot of the stuff out there about that comes from modern Asatru and heathenry practitioners applying what they assume to be universal and traditional, but which actually stems from modern Western thinking, back onto the term.
