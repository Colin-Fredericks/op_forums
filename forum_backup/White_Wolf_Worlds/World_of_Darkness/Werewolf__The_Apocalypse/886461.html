              The caerns listed in the books are not meant to be the only caerns in an area. They are just the caern(s) the sourcebook wanted to mention. So yes, there are opportunities for additional caerns. There is a frequent poster (Heinrich) on this forum who is from Germany, and no doubt he will comment with his own ideas.

              Europe has a higher population density than North America so there are likely less caerns remaining in Europe, and that is something several sourcebooks elude to.

              I run a historical chronicle in Germany and identified some areas where I believe caerns could function.

              1) Southern Germany is along the Alps. Somewhere there or in the Alpine foreland, you can probably locate a caern.

              2) West of the Rhine is the Palatinate Forest which becomes the Vosges Mountains in France. This is a good place for a caern somewhere.

              3) The Harz Mountains likely would have a caern. Many real world werewolf legends are based there. It is the highest mountain range in Germany. It is also home to the Brocken, the world famous place of witches sabbath during Walpurgisnacht.

              4) The Thuringian Forest would be another good location.

              5) Along Germany's border with the Czech Republic might be some good places for a caern. The border is based on the Sudeten Mountains and Bohemian Forest.

              6) The Teutoburg Forest in Westphalia was home to one of the most famous defeats of the Roman Army. It makes a far better location for the canonical Get of Fenris caern than the Black Forest. In my chronicle, I moved that there and substituted an entirely different caern in the Black Forest.

              7) I also like the glacial lakes in the Mecklenburg-Vorpormmern area.

              8) If you are looking for urban caerns, Germany would historically have been a good place for them. Because Germany was politically divided, it never had a central metropole that absorbed everything like London and Paris did for Britain and France. So Germany had a lot of more mid tier cities. Historically, Hamburg, Leipzig, Dresden, Bremen, Lubeck, Magdeburg, and Munich all would have been good places. (Note until the 19th century, Berlin wasn't a major city, although it started becoming important in the 18th century under Frederick the Great). Of course, many of these destroys were either destroyed during the Thirty Years War or World War II, so it is questionable how many urban caerns would have survived. In my game, I have Hamburg and Lubeck as centers of major urban caerns, and Magdeburg a historical one that was destroyed during the Thirty Years War during the Sack of Magdeburg.

              There are also lots of smaller places that could be home to a rank one caern. If you count pre-war Germany, you'd have some potential caern sites in Silesia and Prussia as well.

              Plus you have all sorts of local legends you may want to incorporate. At the town of Bingen on the Rhine, there is a legend of the "Mouse Bishop of Bingen" that tells of a greedy Bishop who wouldn't release grain from storage during a local famine. When the peasants complained, he lured them into a barn and burned them down. Later, a swarm of rats and mice appeared and devoured him. I like the legend so much I placed an important Bone Gnawer caern there.

              The 1 Garou per 500,000 is just a general number. It does not mean that the Garou population is tied to the local human population. It probably doesn't in any local area. A wilderness area with very few people probably have a much smaller ratio (way more Garou than the human population would indicate). In more urban areas with high population density, the local ratio is much, much higher (way fewer Garou than expected based on the ratio). The Get of Fenris are probably more heavily populated in northern Scandinavia than Germany. I'd personally make the ratio in Germany as a whole 1 per million or even 1 per 1.5 million. That would place the Garou population between 54-81. If you want more septs, then keep Garou numbers at sept near the average minimum.
