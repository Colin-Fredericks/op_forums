    Houseruling the combat system - thoughts appreciated

    04-06-2018, 01:18 PM
    Hi guys,
    in the process of rewolving (no revamping here!) some systems for a new group where combat should flow a little more fluidly than usual, I've tried my hand at a new combat/initiative system. I would very much appreciate your comments!

    The main purpose is to get rid of two aspects that go together and in my opinion have a tendency to bog down combat: a) Declaring actions and b) the prior need to know how many actions will be used in one round of combat. Removing the declaration phase is simple, but it breaks down when you try to split actions without knowing how many or what actions you will take in the same turn with both splitting mechanisms (take smallest applicable dice pool and distribute among actions versus use individual dice pools and subtract [total number of actions + actions previously taken this turn] dice).

    The houserule goes as follows:

    Initiative is rolled as normal, there is no declaring actions phase.
    A character's turn begins at the start of combat and lasts until the end of their initiative phase, after which their next turn begins.
    Outside of their initiative phase, characters can take as many defensive actions (block, parry, dodge, ...) as they want.
    In their initiative phase, characters can take up to two regular actions (move, attack, use gifts, etc.).
    Every action taken applies a cumulative -2 modifier to all further actions this turn.
    At the end of the initiative phase, all dice penalties are discarded and the character's next turn begins. If they are capable of regenerating, they regenerate now.
    A character can spend Rage for additional actions. These follow the same rules as regular actions (only defensive while out-of-intiative), but neither suffer from dice penalties nor inflict further penalties for later actions. With Rage, a character can take additional regular actions inside their initiative phase up to their rank. The rule about not spanding more than half your Rage in one turn still remains intact. Rage actions are spent in the regular turn, there is no additional "Rage turn".

    Example:
    The Characters Quentin Quick and Sam Slowpoke gang up against Miranda Medium. Q is on at initiative 12, M at 8, S at 4. From M's view, the whole thing would look like this:
    Ini 12: Q attacks once, M defends (full dice pool).
    Ini 8: M attacks once against S (-2 penalty) and once against Q (-4 penalty). Then her turn ends and her penalties are removed.
    Ini 4: S attacks twice. M defends (full dice pool for the first attack, -2 dice for the second).
    Ini 12: Q attacks once, M spends a Rage to defend (full dice pool due to the point of Rage spent)
    Ini 8: M attacks once against S (-4 dice), once against M (-6 dice) and spends a point of Rage to attack M again (full pool). Then her turn ends and her penalties are reset.

    As visual reminders, tokens can be used to represent penalty-inflicting actions taken. Take a token after you (re-)act, count your tokens and multiply by 2 to get your current penalty, discard all tokens at the end of your turn.


    (In addition, I'm inclined to make soak a static value of half your stamina rather than rolling it every time, but that's not what I'd like to discuss here.)

      It's kinda hard to say too much without playing it, but a few things come to mind on some rumination:

      It seems like you're keeping Initiative to rolling once at the start of combat and not each round. Combined with the elimination of declaration phase, this can hamper the value of high initiative. This isn't bad, but at the moment the game values high initiative rather significantly. Not just being the first to go, but staying the first to go is a big deal that requires investment. In this paradigm... well going first has a nice advantage (you can take to actions despite their penalties, because they'll reset before anyone else goes), but it goes away after the first round. After that first round, it's really just going around the table in order, which means the price of boost initiative should probably go down, or having high initiative should have something more going for it. I don't like this solution, but the CofD gives weapons an initiative penalty that generally relates to damage ratings, so you end up with going first but low damage, in the middle with decent damage, or last but a lot of damage as all valid ways to invest your character.

      From there, there's also an alpha-strike incentive issue. Besides the limited amount of Rage available in single fight, this setup makes the optimal choice to attack as much as possible if you go first. There if very little reason for the character that goes first to not try to take out one of the opponents by attacking 3-4 times and either killing them or putting them at enough of a penalty before they can go, that they can't do anything significant. It might be worth looking at how DAV20 gives you a max. number of attacks regardless of multiple actions, with Rage boosting your base max to reduce this.

      How do things like group initiative, holding your action, and similar options function in this? How do you pull off pack tactics?

      Do unrolled instant actions (such as moving a significant distance with a run) suffer penalties, or just add to your cumulative one?

          Thanks for your thoughts! I hadn't thought about some of the less basic ideas like held actions, so this is quite helpful.

          Do unrolled instant actions (such as moving a significant distance with a run) suffer penalties, or just add to your cumulative one?
          Starting with this, since it's the easy one: Diceless actions don't get rolled all of a sudden, but they do apply penalties to further actions. Yes, that means it would be advantageous to always use diceless actions last. I'm still considering if that's an actual problem, given that there are not too many of them and the order of actions is often relevant (can't whack an enemy before you ran to them, can't fire a gun before you've drawn it)..

          It seems like you're keeping Initiative to rolling once at the start of combat and not each round. Combined with the elimination of declaration phase, this can hamper the value of high initiative. This isn't bad, but at the moment the game values high initiative rather significantly. Besides the limited amount of Rage available in single fight, this setup makes the optimal choice to attack as much as possible if you go first. There if very little reason for the character that goes first to not try to take out one of the opponents by attacking 3-4 times and either killing them or putting them at enough of a penalty before they can go, that they can't do anything significant. It might be worth looking at how DAV20 gives you a max. number of attacks regardless of multiple actions, with Rage boosting your base max to reduce this.
          I think those two might actually counteract each other - yes, a high initiative does not confer a constant benefit, but it does very likely provide an opportunity to attack without dice penalties while not neglecting defence. It's not as strong as it could otherwise be since the enemy doesn't have to declare first and can simply use as many reactions as they think appropriate rather than having to guess how much Rage the big bad wolf will throw at a problem, but a strong alpha strike will likely inflict some damage and make the comeback rather tame due to penalties.

          How do things like group initiative, holding your action, and similar options function in this? How do you pull off pack tactics?
          Though I don't usually use group initiative in my games, doing so would presumably pose no problem - everyone's turn simply ends at the same point rather than at a different one. As long as the group doesn't start attacking each other, it is irrelevant whether A's turn ends exactly before B's or the other way around.
          Holding your action might be somewhat messy since it would best be accomplished by permanently moving your initiative phase, leading to more paperwork and confusion depending on how well your group keeps on top of things. If you want to avoid that, it might be possible to allow held actions, but mandate that any use of reactions immediately ends your turn and begins the next one, forfeiting your held action. Thus, chaining actions is possible only if noone pays attention to you or you're that much hardier than anyone around that you don't need to use defensive actions.
          Pack Tactics don't require simultaneous actions, as far as I know. Thus, they would remain unchanged.
