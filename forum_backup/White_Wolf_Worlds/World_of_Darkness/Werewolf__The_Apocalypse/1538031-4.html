        Like this situation:
        Originally posted by Knightingale View Post
        Speaking of nationalities... Famously they took away those connections for the Tribes in W5 but the funny thing is that this broad allowance of what you could theoretically do with that hasn't shown up so far in any of the adventures. There's all this talk in most of them about how the settings are these generic towns but when a specific region is mentioned it's always North America and when it isn't mentioned, it's still clear that the default-assumption is that the described town is a North American one. They stripped away all that lore and background information to just end up overly focusing on North America again.

        And Red Tundra is also an example of what else has been stripped away: The connection to Native Americans. I was thinking that this adventure could've saved itself a lot of trouble by removing all this needlessly convoluted stuff with the high school, the coach and going to the big city and then coming back... if instead you'd center the story around the Dene people in that area. It makes sense why they would have a problem with this hunting-company. You don't even need the fantasy-wolves. That way you could organically tie a lot of stuff together: Garou lore/spirituality and how it ties First Nation beliefs and traditions, the fight against the Wyrm and how that ties to the struggles of First Nation people, the importance of taking care of the land etc.

        But instead, the Dene people are just a footnote in this adventure...
        Is exactly what the WtE team wants to avoid.

                Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
                You cannot Second Inquisition/Sabbat/Stargazers/Cult of Fenris/Hunter orgs, but you CAN play a Tzimisce that fleshcrafts their ghouls. You can't play an eco-terrorist, but you CAN play a garou that eats humans or poisons towns. The difference between the man-eaters and Cult of Fenris seem to be less morality (aside from the constant push for CoF to be neo-nazi) and more which 'side' one is on. As long as you are Gaian, you can pretty much do anything within the limits of Chronicle Tenets.
                I think the design intent is simply about blocking off certain groups from being played by players. So the designers start the design process off first by saying, "We don't want people playing Sabbat, or Get of Fenris (or whatever)" and the explanation as to why you can't play that comes later. So with Vampire they walled off the Sabbat because they want to focus on the Anarchs and Camarilla and allowing players to be Sabbat doesn't work within that concept. With W5, the lead designer had a personal dislike for the Get of Fenris and wanted to make them NPC villains, and then the explanation for why you can't play them came about later in the design process.

                Noteably in none of those cases (or in the Stargazers, Second Inquisition, Hunter orgs, etc) does morality play any sort of role. You can play a monstrous flesh-crafting Tzimisce, you just can't play one that's part of the Sabbat. You can play some asshole man-eating werewolf, you just can't play someone that's part of the Cult of Fenris. It's not because those groups are evil, it's simply that the game designer doesn't want you playing members of those groups.
