    PENTEX and Organized Crime

    12-24-2017, 11:48 AM
    I might buy Werewolf 20th ED tomorrow and i am planning on setting my first campaign in New Orleans. In the campaign, one of the more prominent antagonists is probably gonna be the New Orleans criminal underworld, specifically, The Dixie Mafia, ( https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixie_Mafia) and The Russian Mafia. I am planning on having these two factions, along with other criminal gangs, have hidden ties with PENTEX. Would Pentex have ties with organized crime and if so, how would they do business with them?

      I think it's sort of a case by case issue for each Subsidiary. Each Subsidiary's business - tobacco, pharmaceuticals, oil, toys, media, RPGs - have their own unique and often weird and fascinating history that includes a long list of potential misdeeds and/or demonizations by opponents. For example, there is a long history of the mafia/organized crime being heavily involved with waste management/garbage disposal (primarily for money laundering, but for other things as well), so Ardus Enterprises is probably pretty heavily mobbed up. Black Dog Games, on the other hand, probably isn't, but does have it's own issues of demon worship and whatnot.

      Anyway, Ardus and the garbage disposal business is probably your best window for a Pentex/Mafia connection (and I'm sure they have the waste disposal contract with all of the city's O'Tolley's restaurants).

      Also, if you've never seen/read John Grisham's The Firm (or the film with Tom Cruise), it's about a Memphis based law firm that works heavily with the mob. I forget the name of Pentex's pet legal firm, but I would presume they'd have something similar going on.

                                      Large scale businesses in the US generally do not have anything to do with organized crime. It generates too much risk for whatever profit it brings. And since PENTEX is already involved in its own criminal activities, this just risks even more attention. Besides for most businesses, when they interact with criminals it is a form of additional expense (the mob is shaking them down), not sales.

                                      Small businesses, which are fronts for criminal gangs, are different. Those exist in order to launder money, commit fraud, and allow corrupt political connections give them money in return for services. The aforementioned waste management companies which are mobbed up are all small companies that operate locally. Waste Management, Inc. is not mobbed up, and is noted for working with law enforcement to drive out the mobbed up companies so they can provide services at a cheaper price to municipalities.

                                      It is possible that some parts of PENTEX might need someone to assist them in their own criminal enterprises and contract out to organized crime, but in reality I don't think they would. PENTEX has at its disposal so many resources in the form of First Teams, vampires, BSDs, Fomori, and others that it staggers my mind why they would need to go outside the company. They already have the infrastructure, organization, and resources to handle their own needs.

                                      I think the only real connection is going to be tangential and personal - someone wants access to prostitutes, illegal drugs for their own use, to kill someone who offends them, etc.

                                      There may be cases where PENTEX needs operational deniability and use buffers in order to pass certain things into the criminal network and evaluate them. They need human testing for some kind of new drug that could create fomori. So after going through some front organizations, the drug ends up in the hands of local drug pushers to go into the community, and PENTEX can observe the results. But in these cases, PENTEX is using the local organized crime gangs, and always try to use buffers to protect them from blowback.

                                                      If you've never seen it, I recommend the movie Innocent Blood (1992), a "vampire vs. mafia" film directed by John Landis of American Werewolf in London fame. (I like to believe the two films are set in the same universe.) Beyond that, I'm hard pressed to think of much in the way of mafia meets supernatural films.

                                                      Also, for the purpose of any Werewolf game set in Louisiana, I highly, highly recommend Fletch Lives (1989). It is a comedy, but in the same vein as Beverly Hills Cop, in which there is a serious mystery going on amidst all the humor. The plot is pretty much tailor made for a Werewolf chronicle, and includes R. Lee Ermey of all people as a crooked televangelist.

                                                      Also, Southern Comfort (1981), Angel Heart (1987) and Skeleton Key (2005). There's also a documentary, Pushcarts & Plantations: Jewish Life in Louisiana, which I've never seen but have been told is pretty good.
