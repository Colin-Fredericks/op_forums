                                    Originally posted by Tazmaniacs View Post
                                    I don't really know any specific sources about wolves' state of mind (apart of Jack London ;-), but Frans de Waal's books would be helpful, for instance Are We Smart Enough to Know How Smart Animals Are? . They are written for a general public, have loads of funny anecdotes, and you could probably use a lot of what he says about monkeys or other animals for wolves.
                                    It's written for the general public, but it also has a long, long list of carefully numbered citations (elaborated on pages 277-318), so it's a bit more robust than a set of anecdotes. I'd say chief among the takeaways is that, given how conservative evolution can be, it's not really reasonable to assume that other animals are completely and totally alien to the human mind. The human mind didn't come from out of a vacuum; it was shaped by the same forces that shaped all the other minds and living spaces. Extrapolating from that, you might want to constantly remind lupus players to think with their mouths and noses over their hands until they get the hang of it.

                                    (There's also a section specifically dealing with how humans perceive the actions of dogs and wolves relative to each other, and it's probably worth getting for W:tA just for that alone.)
