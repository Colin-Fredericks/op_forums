    Originally posted by Caldorian View Post
    I just wanted to let you know that I really appreciate all your great responses so far, but my creative juices are still fermenting at the moment, so to speak.

    I like the she-wolf idea, but you convinced me that having a wolf spirit as the patron spirit seems weird when all the other tribes have other patrons. (I'm fine with Fenris being a patron for the Cult, assuming it's a destructive spirit, keen on wreaking havoc on the cosmos).

    Looking at the different tribe patrons again, we have three types of patrons: mythical beasts (Gorgon, Unicorn, Horned Serpent, Griffin), animals (Rat, Stag, Spider, Owl, Falcon), and natural phenomena (North Wind, Thunder). Among the Glory tribes, we have one of each so far. Maybe we have been looking into the wrong direction? What about a tree for a patron? Oak, for instance, for its association with longevity, strength, fertility, and robustness; also it's importance in Germanic cultures, or it's association with Perkʷūnos...

    Anyway, I'm still thinking, but please continue to brainstorm as well.
    Yeah, I have some trepidations about being too on-the-nose with a Patron-Spirit like the She-Wolf. I think the only way to make it work would be through a properly epic origin-story. You get away from the obviousness that exists on the surface by getting into the specifics of an epic story. By that I mean, turning the Patron-Spirit into more of a character and having an origin-story associated with the Tribe that is an actual story with arcs, protagonists, antagonists etc. You get away from the lame qualities that exist on the surface by having a specific story with specific characters to make it work.

    But that's more of a concept for a Tribebook, of course, than a pitch to make this work as an in-game faction during a game. I think you can overcome it via good stoyrtelling and worldbuilding but it needs work. And of course, what's even cooler is to have worldbuilding that doesn't require big amounts of storytelling and lore to make it work. So that's why the wolf-focus is a bit of an issue.

                            So, after some thinking, I'm partial to choosing Wild Boar (or simply Boar) as the tribe patron for the [Get].
                            1. It's not a wolf patron.
                            2. Boars are a species that can be found on all continents (even if their not native) and it's an invasive species, corresponding with the tribe's tendency to conquer new territory. Suidae can be found all across the globe.
                            3. Across different cultures, wild boars have been associated with warrior virtues, aggressiveness, fierceness, toughness, power, but also avarice and recklessness--all fitting for a warrior and survivalist tribe. I also like that the tribe is a bit "pigheaded" and sees/saw itself as the Garou police ("pigs").
                            4. Boars are social animals, and mothers will aggressively defend their young. Going back to the function/renown matrix, this could represent the community aspect of the tribe.
                            5. In Legacy, Boar was one of the totems associated with the Get of Fenris.
                            I'm not too worried about the association of boar with the Grondr for two reasons: First, it's not even clear whether there ever were Grondrs in W5. Second, if there ever were, they are extinct now.

                            The only thing I don't really like is that it's yet another animal patron.


                            Maybe you have additional ideas how to match the boar patron with the direction suggested by the function/renown matrix? Also, do you have any suggestions for nuances with regard to the tribe's character? Tribe verbs?

                                                    So, I had a long hashing out of this with my writing team, and at the end of the day, we decided there are real life cultural representations of societies that espouse many of their ideals without having to be explicitly 'nordic'. In short, the baltic countries, or slavs, hold to a strong culture of resistance and hospitality. Their origins are rooted in being slaves, and developed a viciousness in having to resist both the russian and germanic tribes that sought to control them, while openly accepting anyone into their numbers that espouse their ideals. They do not seek a senseless death for it's own death, but rather, a cause worthy of dying for. They believe in strong communities, capable leaders, and sacrifices of meaning. And as a big plus, if you include all baltic countries in this demographic, it includes nordic folk as well; no need to change the name or patron. Glyph used with permission from Feral Noesis. Here's a brief writeup we put together in a preview chapter for Werewolf: the Essentials:
                                                    Get of Fenris
                                                    We clawed, we chained, our hearts in vain
                                                    We jumped, never asking why
                                                    It slowly turned, you let me burn
                                                    And now, we're ashes on the ground
                                                    — Miley Cyrus “Wrecking Ball”


                                                    Blood and sacrifice are how the Fenrir measure both their own worth and the worth of others. Bloodshed in the war against their enemies, and the blood ties that join them as a tribe. They battle against the Wyrm and battle to defend their loved ones. The Get give no quarter, and expect none. Their actions speak louder than words, granting leadership to those most capable of leading. No material sacrifice is made without cost, and no prayer or oath is spoken without personal investment in it’s success. The saying, “true warriors never grow old,” is taught to all Fenrir cubs as an ideal of both the immortality of legends, and the dishonor of cowardice.

                                                    They are a practical tribe with a history of resistance and hospitality, never hesitating to speak from the heart. Warriors defeating insurmountable odds that have passed down their traditions through the generations, forged in blood, enduring their baltic origins as former slaves and animistic pagans. In the modern day, Fenrir pay homage to these old heroes as tacticians, commandos, and battle masters.

                                                    The Get do not save their ferocity only for doing battle, however, as they are a tribe that has a fierce reputation of upholding and defending their own kinfolk. Warriors are the most fit to lead in battle, and family the most fit to lead the household. They defer to their kin families as the zedakhs on the homefront — anyone wishing to raise their voice in discussion is afforded respect

                                                            I guess the only role I can see for tying things to ethnicities and cultures is to use it as a reference for worldbuilding and to explain the history of a Tribe. So you're saying "A Garou-Tribe is a homogenous collection of Garou and Kin/Kinfolk that settled down in a specific region and by doing so this Garou-community intermingled with the peoples and cultures of that region. And this has led to humans and Garou influencing each other in what their practices and beliefs are like. And then throughout history, things changed, people moved, cultures shifted and with that, the Tribe spread out as well." And in addition to that, you have the whole Ancestor-aspect. So no matter where your Fenrir was born in the 21st century, their identity ties back to the Norse/Germanic culture through their Ancestor-spirits. Like when an Irish-American claims Ireland as part of their identity even though they were born in the US and have always lived there but through the origins of their ancestors, they now claim "Irishness" as part of their identity.
