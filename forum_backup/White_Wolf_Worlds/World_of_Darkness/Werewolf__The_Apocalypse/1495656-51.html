                                    I think the Impergium and the War of Rage, as well as the War of Shame and War of Tears have their place in WtA.
                                    Any induvial garou can be heroic, virtuous and 'good' (in a D&D alignment sense), but still be part of a society that from an outside perspective did and still does things that are wrong.

                                    And I think, that the WtA authors over the years framed the War of Rage and the Impergium as this horrible things that were born of cruelty and a lust for dominance. At least that is the impression I got from revised and W20 more than from 1st Edition. But these events happened 10.000+ years ago. People and society were a lot different then and a lot of ideas, including the sanctity of life, were not as fundamental as they are today.

                                    And sure, we as modern human beings and readers can condemn the garou, and the modern homid majority garou society can condemn the actions of the ancestors, but then again, they have the Ancestors background and can actually talk to spiritual reflections of eye witnesses. That is as if you could today talk to warriors from before the Roman empire and ask them what their conflicts were about. And I believe you will find, that their conflicts were just as much driven by economic factors, emotions and believes as the conflicts of humans today. And that there isn't a society that is purely 'evil'.

                                    For all its flaws I often praise rev. TB GOG for the statement that the War of Rage was inevitable, because it just wasn't the time for co-operation yet.
                                    And yeah, maybe Gaia, Helios and Luna aren’t all that benevolent. Maybe they created the Fera full well knowing that this would cause a lot of trouble, but also foreseeing that in 10.000+ years they would start to occasionally work together – and that is what makes all the atrocities before meaningful.

                                    The same goes for the Impergium. In revised it is occasionally framed as if the garou hunted humans for sport and that the whole process was driven by a need to rule. But there are also earlier descriptions of the garou "culling" the humans, a practise of removing the old and sick from a group of farm animals, sheep usually. In an ancient time that probably was a necessary thing to do. I don't like the notion that the Delirium or the Curse is divine retribution for the Impergium. I tend to focus on the part that emissaries from all tribes debated and interpreted omens and still could not see if it was better or worse for Gaia to end the Impergium or continue to practice it. A majority seemed to agree that each side had equally valid arguments in their favour and that is why they resorted to Gamecraft to find a decision. It is not that the ancestors were careless, came together, feasted and then decided to flip a coin on the matter.
                                    And, obviously the Impergium had to end, so that the WoD could reach a point that resembles modern earth.

                                    So, I find it totally okay that these aspects exist in the mythology of WtA and would not call them "screw-ups".

                                    ----

                                    Werewolf vs. Vampirs is a common theme in many contemporary fiction and within the mythology of WtA it makes sense that vampires are considered (unwitting/unwilling) minions of the Wyrm.

                                    One fundamental problem between the games is their incompatibility in regards of power levels when it comes to cross over. If you ever have a vampire fan and a garou fan who want to play and it comes to direct conflict, the ability to inflict and soak agg damage on the garous side is just deadly to almost any combination of starting to mid-level vampires. That is a mechanical problem of the combat system that might cause animosity.

                                    But then again, the WoD is not designed for playing Crossover games. It simply isn't.

                                    ----

                                    I think the garou superhero is something you can aim for as an ST, if you want to setup your chronicle like that. The system allows for that motive, the Setting does too. It is only a very small part of the possible themes and motives that WtA has to offer. But, because of that, a lot of material and fluff doesn't match up with the superhero theme.

                                    I, personally, like WtA as the "noir"-ish investigator and rough-cop of supernatural occurrences Setting. Not to high fantasy, low on interaction with spirits and combat, no world-ending doomsday devices and not "Shattered Dreams"-like time-travel-multiverse adventures. But, I see, that those can have their place in the Setting, too.
                                    It also can be about sept management and housing, in a D&D-Birthright kind of way. I mean, that is a bit what WtA LARP in a continuous chronicle is like....
