    Cryptids and the World of Darkness: Things beyond the edge of the map.

    06-10-2016, 09:21 AM
    The world is getting smaller

    Oh sure, its the same size its always been. But the world itself seems far smaller then it did at the start of the Age of Discovery. Google Earth lets you see anywhere in the world, from the most rural areas, to the bustling cities of the world. Dozens, if not hundreds, of map programs can help a traveler get to even the most remote parts of the world in a few days if they have the cash to pay for the tickets and gear required. The highest mountain, the lowest abyss, both have been conquered by humanity.

    And yet, and yet, despite all of this, the world still keeps more secrets to itself then it has divulged. We know next to nothing about the oceanic depths, the inhospitable depths and the deep chill of the dark waters hundreds of feet below the surface. We know little about the earth beneath our feet, every time we dig a little deeper, we find a new fossil, a new set of caves, or even new forms of life entirely! Princeton even found bacteria a full two miles underneath the surface (Source: http://www.princeton.edu/main/news/a...n=newsreleases)

    Though men in Ivory Towers speak of discovery and how science has explained everything that is, there are truly more mysteries then there are stars in the sky, even the Umbral sky! In the dark jungles and forests where satellite cameras cannot see, in the deep oceans where men cannot swim, and the dark caves where none know they yawn towards the sky there are...things, never known to mankind, and only infrequently known to the Fera and the Garou.

    The Mages call some of these things 'bygones', creatures of old who have retreated into the Umbra...But they seem to be based on human legends, and may or may not be spirits. Some of the things in the wild (Or wyld) places of the world are unknown to humanity entirely, others have only been glimpsed briefly, long enough for men and Fera to get entirely wrong ideas about what manner of creature they are. Still others are remnants of lost worlds, Umbral realms that have collapsed, or the world before the raising of the Gauntlet.

    Some are rather benign, others would cause even experienced Theurges to clutch their temples in agony at a mere glance at them. And while its easy to blame every monster out there on Formori, or Black Spiral Dancers with funny looking mutations, the Garou don't know everything, and even the Mokole don't see everything in Mnesis Quests. Even the Black Spiral Dancers, who've dug deeper into Gaia's crust then anybody else are sometimes surprised by what they find down there.


    MOST of these plot hooks are based on Werewolf, and Fera lore. Though I have slipped in elements of other game-lines and hooks for them, to give a little something for everybody.


    Sidebar: The difference between Bygones and Cryptids

    Bygones, those creatures who have been forced across the Gauntlet by a solidifying consensus, are legendary beasts. Dragons, Gryphons, Krakens (Though recent footage from a Japanese Fishing Boat has given a few of these leviathans a little more leeway) and other magical creatures who hold a place in Human mythology. Cryptids, on the other hand, are either natural creatures who, while their existence is contested, are considered plausible, or are some sort of Umbral or otherworldly entity. Either way, if you're using Mage and Bygone rules, the consensus hasn't kicked them to the other side because enough of humanity sees them as plausible. Though if you want to claim, for instance, there are both Cryptid and Bygone examples of Bigfoot, contact between the entities might just cause quite the kerfluffle in the Pacific Northwest...




    The Mongolian Death Worm

    The Stargazers speak of a strange creature in the Gobi Desert, though occasionally sightings have been seen on the Steppes, and some Kindred remember sitting around campfires with the strange eastern Bloodline known as the "Anda" from the same area. A wormlike creature with a caustic venom and the ability to shock its prey and those who threaten the scarlet creature, resembling an ambulatory intestine from a great beast slithering across the ground.

    About a meter in length, the creature is, like most Cryptids, elusive and rather hard to find, Stargazer and Hakken theurges in the area claim that sightings have reduced significantly in recent decades. a few old tales speak of the Death Worms as being material servants of Gaia, lowly creatures, the worms till the soil around the rims of the desert, promoting the growth of new life to prevent the desert from spreading and unbalancing the ecosystem of the area. Mongolian and Chinese Black Spiral Dancers, on the other hand, speak of them as the intestinal parasites of the Wyrm, sometimes disgorging themselves from its intestines when large amounts of balefire spew forth from sphincters deep underground, and can spew Balefire themselves.


    Potential Plot Hooks

    * The Sahara Desert is the largest Hot Desert on earth, and the third largest overall. And yet, despite its immense size, the desert is slowly expanding. The Ahadi, a group of African Fera who have managed to actually work together, which actually seems to be WORKING , are taking steps to mitigate the expansion of the desert out of concerns of losing farmland and contributing to further unrest among African nations. Having heard of "Gaian Worms" a pack of Ahadi has traveled east, to the harsh Gobi Desert to try and find some of these creatures, and transplant a few to the Sahara. Any number of things could happen, from the Beast Courts having them under their protection and not wanting the creatures to leave. To the theory of them being Gaian being entirely wrong, leaving an Ahadi pack hunted by great wyrm-worms spewing Balefire at them, as Spiral Packs close in. They could even run afoul of humans hunting the creatures for notoriety or scientific endeavors (Might even run into a few Void Engineers looking for a new strain of Reality Deviant).

    * China has a nuclear program, though it never was refined to the degree that the neighboring Soviets and distant Americans managed to attain. However, a great number of nuclear testing occurred at Lop Nur, a Salt Lake in northwestern China. Through a combination of fallout blowing north, and a few more clandestine tests in the Gobi Desert (Perhaps backed by Technomancers seeking to understand the Atom), a few of the Mongolian Death Worms where exposed to radiation. While in the real world, radiation doesn't cause spontaneous mutations like so many bad movies would have you think, Wyrm Taint certainly DOES. As a result of the nuclear explosions carrying taint with it, those Death Worms exposed to the radiation grew considerably in size, becoming creatures generally indistinguishable to the Thunderwyrms that harry the American Southwest.

    The Thunderwyrms are hungry, and have their eyes set on the nomadic tribes of the Gobi, a great many of which are Stargazer kinfolk. Its bad enough to have them eaten or crushed, but some of the Thunderwyrms are small enough to wriggle their way into the souls of mortal men, making an unusual, and dangerous, strand of Formori. While they seem to be bound to the desert, the Gobi is growing at an alarming rate, and slowly encroaching south towards China, with 1,390 sq miles of grassland becoming desert every year. And every year brings with it more 'breathing room' for the creatures...

    * The Death Worms have the ability to hibernate for long periods of time, slowing their biological processes in times of famine as they rest under the sand. The now-extinct Anda Bloodline of Kindred where familiar with the creatures, and ghouled a great many of them to protect the Mongols they traveled with. Fortunately enough for the Worms, they have endured the centuries. Unfortunately for any Kindred who brave the desert in an attempt to uncover the secrets of the Anda (Or just trying to make inroads to the territory of the Chinese Kuei-Jin), they remember the taste and scent of Kindred Vitae, and upon smelling it on the desert winds, they will awaken and hunt for the source of it.

    In other words, this is your excuse to have a little Dune or Tremors in your Vampire chronicle! Or just royally mess with Kindred who believe themselves to be alpha predators by having something large, acid spewing, and ghouled hunting them for their blood...

    Stats

    The Mongolian Death Worm can be represented through a number of fashions. If they're benign creatures of Gaia, you can simply use snake statistics, its not the same thing, but they're toxic slithering critters just the same. In the event that they're a variety of Wyrm Creature, the "Quick and Dirty Formori" rules in W20 should suffice, and the Book of the Wyrm has Thunderwyrm stats if you're going that particular route.





    The Mothman

    The Mothman is one of America's best known Cryptids, right next to Bigfoot in the urban legend family tree. First noted in Point Pleasant in West Virginia around 1967, the Mothman takes the form of a large humanoid figure with glowing red eyes, and insectoid wings sticking out the back of it. With legends claiming that it has harassed people by stalking them, chasing them down from above, and a number of people believe the creature responsible for a bridge collapsing, after which it was seen only sporadically.

    Some claim the creature brings disaster, others claim it attempts to warn humans of disaster in its own, mysterious fashion. Some say it’s a mutant, others say its an alien, a few others claim it’s just a barn owl scaring the daylights out of people…but that's far less interesting so we'll ignore that for now!

    A few packs of Garou on the eastern seaboard investigated the creature in the sixties, and only the barest amount of information was forthcoming. One Ahroun claims to of sunk his claws into the creature, but was held fast and dragged into the sky, being dropped from a great height, breaking both of his legs in the process. Splinter-Shins is still alive today, and frequently tells new cubs of his clash with the creature.

    Plot Hooks

    • The Glass Walkers know a great deal more about the Weaver then most tribes of Garou, and the Ananasi know their grandmother quite well. Yet even among them, only a bare handful know the stories of the insect shifters, races of Shapechangers from before the birth of humanity. But to those who dabble in such obscure lore, a legend appears. The Weaver made its own races of Shifters, of which the Ananasi where the only to survive through the ages to the dawn of human history. Of these races, the Ananasi destroyed the majority of them, all but one.

    The Moth Shifters refused to serve the Weaver, and where annihilated to the last…Or so the story goes. But as Weaver lashed out at her children, Wyld took notice, and was outright offended at the audacity of one of his siblings violating their understanding about their places in the universe. With a grin on his face, the Wyld swept up a great number of the Moths in the instant before the Weaver destroyed them, scooping them up into his chaotic embrace and offering them a deal.

    He would protect them from the Weaver, in exchange, he would be free to do with them as he wished. Most agreed, and those who did not where simply thrown into the flux to become something else entirely. They are a secret of the Wyld, and while Uncle Change isn't exactly the greatest planner out there, he occasionally turns loose the Moths to do…Well, something, he might not even be sure. The Moths, by and large, loathe the Weaver entirely, and seek to punish her for her annihilation of their species. Perhaps explaining why one of them destroyed a bridge in the late sixties…

    • One of the earlier attempts by Pentex and the forebears of Project Lycanon, the result of stolen Nazi secrets, chemical research, and good old fashioned unholy Wyrm rituals, the Mothmen where supposed to be a new race of werebeasts loyal to the company. Working on the theory that Humans where harder to control then base beasts, they began implanting banes in some of the larger, hardier species of Moth, along with exposing them to mutagenic chemicals to try to get the results they wanted.

    The creatures, little better then particularly resilient Formori, escaped into the world, trying to find some way to explain just what the company is up to, but lacking the communicative skills and human intelligence to explain themselves. The creatures aren't malevolent, at least not towards humans who don't radiate Wyrm Taint, but they tend to lash out at anything tainted. Those who are free of it usually just get the daylights scared out of them as the Mothmen chase them down, screeching in a vain attempt to explain…SOMETHING to them. After the escape of the Mothmen, Pentex looked to other potential projects. Perhaps a hardier insect would be more useful for their purposes, like a cockroach…



    Final Words

    These are just a couple of the myths and legends out there in the world, if this thread proves popular, I'll do up some more! I tried avoiding Bigfoot and Nessie, as they've been done to death, but there are some things I can do with them if people want to see some of the more 'well trod' cryptids put into a Werewolf context for their games! Hell if enough people ask for it, I might just do a write up for Wyld Moth Shifters! Because everybody loves giant chaotic insects!

          The Kappa

          Most anybody who's spent more then a few hours reading about Japanese folklore on Wikipedia, TVTropes, or shockingly enough, an actual book, has heard of the Kappa. A creature that lives in Rivers and Lakes in Japan, some describe them as Amphibians, others as turtle like creatures, still others as mischievous imps. The common threads between depictions are a love of Cucumbers (A sushi roll featuring Cucumber is called a Kappamaki), and a distinct weakness to having a shallow spot on their heads emptied of water.

          The Hakken and Beast Courts know of the Kappa, at least to an extent. The Creatures are assuredly out there, as are a few other 'mythical' Japanese entities in rural areas where the belief in beings such as Oni and the Kappa are strong. The creatures have also been reported in China and Korea, though to a much less frequent degree that seems to be the result of modern migration. Strangely enough, however, similar creatures have been seen in the Americas, with a frog-like being known as the Loveland Frog seen in Loveland Ohio. Are the creatures a world wide phenomenon?

          Plot Hooks

          * The Kappa have been on-again-off-again friends with the Beast Courts over the years, they keep the Hengeyokai aware of happenings around their rivers and lakes, and they do their damnedest to keep hostile spirits and unpleasant individuals away from their homes, and the odd cucumber thrown their way doesn't hurt either. However, a rural temple in the mountains of Japan has recently...well, captured is the wrong word.

          An individual Kappa has begun living with Monks, and a few more followed him along. Now the temple is full of the creatures, being cared for after their river became entirely too polluted for them to live in. Unfortunately, the creatures are blatantly supernatural (Who's heard of intelligent frog men running around the Japanese Countryside?), and this means that humans knowing about them is a violation of the Veil...But at the same time, the beings are utterly benign and the monks are in no hurry to tell everybody and their brother about their new roommates.

          Some of the local Hengeyokai are furious, and want the monks killed, and the Kappa sent packing. Others, however, think that as material beings, they are 'out of their juristiction', or otherwise simply don't think they're causing any harm. And did not the Emerald Mother tell her children "Respect those beneath ye, all are of Gaia"? Well, she may not of told EVERY breed that, but there's quite a grey area occurring! The Kappa might be material beings, and aren't nearly as strong as even a Garou Cub, but they're not without their tricks, and the Monks have refused to meet the Hengeyokai, even suggesting that an attempt to enter the temple would result in a violent expulsion...What will the Sentai do to solve this dilemma?

          * The Kappa legend has existed for centuries, but unfortunately the Kappa themselves have not. A Pentex Subsidiary in East Asia has lost track of a group of Anurana Mockeries (See Wyrm20 for details) and the Frogs have washed up at a rural fishing village on the Japanese coast. Immediately upon arriving, they began lurking in the waters outside the village, watching, waiting, and seeing who they could approach. In time, they started speaking to some of the youth and even rapping on the windows of disillusioned Hikkomori (Shut-is) in the dead of night, promising them a better life...if they will help the "Kappa"

          Over the span of the last couple years, the Anurana managed to start something of a cult, with the youth feeding the frogs and ensuring they stay secret in exchange for patronage, and slowly revealing what little occult secrets they've managed to scrape together over the years (None of the minions know more then a dot or two of hedge magic, the Werefrogs aren't STUPID, but they can't use gifts, and you'd be amazed what you can talk a few banes into telling some disaffected kids...).

          Its gotten to the point where most of the town knows something is going on, whispering about the Kappa who lurk in the night and off the shores. People who leave the town never make it to their destination, never call back, they simply vanish down some frogs gullet. At the same time, some of the youth are starting to figure out the Kappa aren't what they say they are, some don't care, loving the power they get lording over the rest of the townsfolk with their parlor tricks and amphibious patronage. Others want OUT, this isn't the better life they where promised, they work for FROG MONSTERS for the love of god!

          To make matters worse, a few of the Anurana have...bred with some of the local women. And a few are getting near giving birth. As the village slowly spirals into decay and cult worship, the Wyrm (Or Centipede, if you will) grows stronger. It won't be long now before the town draws the attention of a Sentai, or a traveling pack of Garou, what then, will they do? There are a great many innocents here, not all of which know what's going on, and some of the kids got in way over their head, and want a way out. But at the same time, they know too much, and a few of the youths are now in their 20s and are starting to learn some REAL Wyrm magic...its gonna get bloody one way or another.

          * Loveland Ohio is a rather small town, a nice enough place to live, with around 12.000 people in the city. Its quaint, charming, a lovely place to spend a few nights on your way east, or maybe to settle down to start a family with the missus. Sure, there's the River Demons, the so-called Loveland Frog, but every town has its quirks...Granted most of the time the quirks don't involve the thing eating a pair of lovers out of their pickup truck in the middle of a makeout session that the cops then hurry to cover up.

          The Frog's been out there since the 1950s, and the Garou are starting to suspect that the creature is more then a myth. A perusal of the records indicate that on average, six young men and six young women are taken every six years. The obvious occult significance aside, the Garou aren't going to just stand idly by while some THING eats the people of a small town, and a rather young pack might make a name for themselves locally by charging into the wilderness and gutting the creature.

          The Frog might be a transplanted Kappa, as above, and something ELSE is causing the disappearances. Or perhaps its an Anurana who somehow ended up much further inland then usual in an attempt to escape Pentex. Or, perhaps, its a Formori, or a Formori Family out in the wilderness offering sacrifice to the Wyrm in exchange for power, or perhaps a cure for their wretched condition. Either way, something wicked is out there, and its up to the Pack to get rid of the damn thing.


          Depicting the Kappa

          In the first scenario, Kappa should be treated as basic mortals with the Short flaw. They can swim at twice their running speed, are fully amphibious, and have the Possessed Powers of Blending, Armored Hide, Natural Weaponry (Beak Bite) and are immune to the delirium. They look like a turtle with amphibious features (Arms and legs ending in padded fingers) with a stubby tail, a hollow on the top of their head, and a nasty looking beak. They speak Japanese, and register to Sense Gaia and Sense Wyld.

          In the second, use the Anurana werefrogs from Wyrm 20. If you want a particularly Japanese feel, go ahead and tweak their powers a little, change it up a bit to fit the Hollow Headed Cucumber Eater myth. After all, they did come from a Japanese Pentex subsidiary! For the Werefrog's servants, I suggest minor levels in Sorcerous paths, give them a "Ringu" or other Japanese Horror feel, with ideal paths being Hellfire, Shadowcrafting, and Summoning (This last one prolly won't end very well for the kids, though)

          In the third, you can use either the first or second scenario's Kappa, if you're going for a Fomori Family of Loveland Frogs, however, I suggest the following

          * Family Powers: Bestial Mutation, Amphibious
          * Potential powers: Eyes of the Wyrm, Mask Presence, Poison Tumors (Pick one)
          * Suggested Taints: Derangement (Homicidal), Special Diet (Human Flesh), Worms (There's a type of parasite that replaces the tongue of certain fish!)

          Final Notes

          I encourage you to do a little reading on the Kappa, I can't cover every little story here, but there's a rich melange of lore about the critters all over Japanese mythology! Pick your favorite story, and draw some inspiration from it. Or take the good parts out of a bunch of myths and weave them together! Just remember, as a storyteller its your job to make a compelling story...and its so much easier when you draw on an existing source that's been entertaining enough to be told for centuries!
