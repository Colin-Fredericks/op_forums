                        What Knightgale touches upon here touches upon something I’ve heard a lot of other commentators point out about W5 and it’s something I feel is a valuable point to make, albeit a difficult one in so far as it touches upon some highly sensitive and nuanced real world subjects. Namely the issue of political violence.

                        WtA has always had the criticism of the Garou that they often were over-reliant upon violence and warfare as a means to achieving their ends (or, rather, not achieving their ends). The common refrain was that the Garou suffered because they were trying to fight industrial/post-industrial problems with Neolithic/Iron Age solutions. However this critique was largely about the failures of the Garou to make use of other necessary tactics and to attack the roots of the problem (‘attacking the root’ meaning a need for radical solutions) rather then about the usefulness of violence as such - violence was useful and necessary but it could be harmful or ineffective if used improperly.

                        The issue with W5 is one which is hard to describe succinctly but which I feel has elements of what I’ll call either ‘Historical Nihilism’ or ‘Liberal Nihilism’ (Liberalism here not meaning liberal in the sense of Democrat v. Republican or Labour v. Tory but rather Liberalism in the sense of a post-1789 society governed by a bourgeois dictatorship with focus upon equality under the law, free trade/enterprise, the sanctity of private property, etc.). This school of Liberal Nihilism holds violence as a categorically illegitimate means of action and rejects radicalism (addressing the roots; the causes) in favor of reformism (addressing the fruits; the symptoms) at best. In its contemporary form it is heavily influenced by the historical nihilism of the post-1991 world which considers Liberalism the End of History, the Best of All Possible Worlds, and thus forecloses upon radical interventions of all stripes.

                        The issue with this is that reformism is, I contend, fundamentally incapable of addressing existential crises to the system itself, crises which are not tangential to the foundational elements of the system but crises which derive from the foundational elements of the system itself, which can only be addressed by radical intervention, and which those who are most dependent on the system’s continuation are least able to address. Werewolf has always made this clear by having as fundamental to its narrative the existential crises of ecology & environmentalism (a somewhat flawed conception that can be addressed some other time), namely, that the Energy Regime of Modern Liberalism, dating back to the outbreak of the Industrial Revolution, is dependent upon Carbon Energy/Fossil Fuels (Coal, Oil, Gas, etc.). The continuation of global society as it currently exists is dependent upon the Regime of Oil and those most wedded to the Regime of Oil are the ones least able to address the ensuing crises.

                        The limits of what is acceptable under Liberal Nihilism do not contain the means of addressing these root issues. Reformism, passive resistance, protest, electoralism, etc. would simply not be sufficient to win the War. However, to be fair to W5, neither would the purely adventurist campaigns of violence with the Garou have carried out from 1st edition to W20. Random assassinations, bombings, sabotage, & guerrilla assaults are also not sufficient to win the War. Not even a dialectic combination of the two, imo, would be sufficient to win the war. The defeat of the Regime of Oil, if it does not come from the purely mechanical forces of history which will sweep away the old order with escalating disasters and crises, would require radical solutions. Solutions which not only address the roots of the Regime of Oil but also the current World Order (the order we have inherited from 1790, 1815, 1848, 1870, 1918, 1945, 1968, 1991, etc.).

                        Earlier editions did not always perfectly address this imo either, although they may have come closer. There was already Historical Nihilism in WoD, especially imo, as time went on. However the acceptance of the possibility (and necessity) of radicalism which existed outside the bounds of Liberal Nihilism made those earlier editions closer to the mark than W5.

                        Not everything has to be didactically written in such a fashion as to heighten revolutionary consciousness of course. Not every WtA adventure needs to include pages ripped from ‘What Is To Be Done?’, ‘On Contradiction’, or ‘The Wretched of the Earth’, but even from a purely narrative pov, setting aside real world considerations, imo the consideration of possibilities beyond the limits of Liberal Nihilism, especially for a gameline so rooted in the worlds before said Liberal Epoch, can only make the possibilities for storytelling and play alike more interesting and more engaging.

                                        Exactly. It’s a good ending what’s the best possible outcome we can imagine is the maintenance of the current status quo.

                                        I’m not against cynical stories in principle. It’s certainly possible to tell a compelling story with a message that ‘in conditions as dire as these, the only possible victory is just to survive another day’. That can be done well and in a compelling fashion and it describes the experience of plenty of people in history and now. It can even be done well in a high power setting like werewolf where the exaggeration of Garou capabilities in comparison to an average person simply highlight the extremity of the struggle. There’s nothing inherently wrong with that from a narrative level.

                                        It does however, imo, indicate the sort of ‘Historical Nihilism’ I described in my post above, which is valid to critique. It’s nuanced but I think it’s reasonable to make the distinction between those who live in such conditions which make survival itself a form of victory over the world and those who are consciously aware of the crises of the world but are personally in circumstances of such relative comfort that any possible change to the world order (except perhaps a form of reformism which grants them a greater share of the spoils of the current world order) can only represent an existential threat to their already precarious position. Such a position as described in the latter case, imo, can be soothing to the person who adopts it, by the notion that their resignation is merely a realistic analysis, rather then a self-interest excuse for moral cowardice.

                                        Of course, the thing about such ‘moral cowardice’ is that, it is typical for most people. Most people have some things they view as morally abhorrent and if they truly acted upon those convictions would be compelled to take some time of action against it. However, the majority of such persons, do not take any action which would seriously threaten their material position. The psychic anguish this produces can either be resolved into the acceptance of a state of false consciousness or be ameliorated in any number of ways but for many people, they will never truly overcome their ‘moral cowardice’. This statement is as much a confession as it is an accusation of others.

                                        That’s one reason why, I believe, power fantasy narratives such as what Werewolf (or Batman, or Robin Hood, or what have you), are attractive. They allow one to vicariously act out through fictitious circumstances what it might look like to overcome this ‘moral cowardice’ though action of some type, action which crosses over the threshold which would produce consequences that would render one’s relatively comfortable material circumstances impossible to persist. Werewolf has always worn this on its sleeve. The early storyteller advice sections, iirc, even encourage ones ST to make use of their own and their players angst to generate stories (ex: if you know a player at your table is a former fast food service employee, give them an opportunity to rip apart their shithead ex-manager in the form of an O’Tooley’s goon).

                                        Of course these power fantasies are not inherently virtuous. They do not inherently inspire those who participate in them towards right action (not every peasant small holder who loved Robin Hood stories would actually risk their own hide in attempting to cheat the manor lords or bishops out of their expected share of taxation and tithe, even if they may have wished to). To those who seek to produce right action in others this leads them either to condemn power-fantasy as ‘an opiate of the masses’ by another form or to qualify their support for the subject (for instance, the position that power fantasy can be useful, but it should be wedded with a program of political action, political education, etc.).

                                        In other cases, and I think Werewolf itself experienced this, even prior to W5, the power-fantasy is deemed a form of immaturity and is met with the vulgar-realism of the ‘historical nihilist’ who, whether more or less articulately, argues that a better world is not possible and that to attempt the radical assaults on the status quo that would be necessary to change it (and which would necessarily cause one to lose their position of relative security within the system) is misguided, immature, and irresponsible.

                                        To this point, it’s not uncommon for valid critiques of irresponsible action (adventurism) to be conflated with all attempts at radical action. To Knightengale’s above point, we can see this with the rival pack in this adventure. Adventurism actually exists and so it can certainly be represented and it’s causes understood and expressed; but it can also be conflated with all forms of radical action, creating a false dichotomy between ‘Perpetuate the Status Quo v. Attack the Status Quo in irresponsible & ineffective ways for petty reasons’. Of course, again, there’s no reason one can’t present a scenario where these are the primary perspectives represented, and there are cases where accurately representing the major active forces in a situation might require it, but imo, one can do this in such a way that is aware of the possibility of Right Action rather then one which posits only the false dichotomy described above.

                                        I hope that this has been clear and helpful for those interested in these sorts of metatextual discussions. I’m afraid that parts of this may not be immediately clear to those not familiar with certain political mileaus lol so if anything here is unclear, please ask about it, and I’ll try to clarify my points, to the best of my ability.

                                        Thanks for reading.
