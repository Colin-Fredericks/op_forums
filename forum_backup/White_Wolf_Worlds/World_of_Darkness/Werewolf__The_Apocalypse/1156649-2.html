        My personal suggestions on them:

        Unstoppable Warrior:
        What about it start downgrading every bit of non-silver damage? Agg becomes Lethal, Lethal becomes non-Lethal, and previously non-Lethal damage ceases to cause damage at all. Also, instead of regenerating with Rage, it could withstand with it, that is, the character spend Rage points to not take the unsoaked damage at all.

        One on One:
        It could cost you half your maximum Gnosis points, but no Perma.

        Sleep of the Hero:
        Make it a level 5 ritual, or even a level 4 ritual with specially stingy costs. Then the Gift could be Awaken the Hero to rouse those people from sleep on a time of crisis. So a Legendary Garou is the leader that will unite the heroes of the past when the tribe needs most.

        Storyteller:
        Some clarification on limits and possibilities would be interesting. I don't think it should be able to change past, for instance, but it could "instinctively" know if a given piece of the past is true enough to be used. So, lets say the Galliard wants to tell a tale of King Arthur returning from Avalon, the ST could veto this based on his need for no such thing ever happening at the past, but then the Garou gets to tell another thing, but if the ST don't have reason to not want an Arthur coming from Avalon, then the Garou got it right.

        Call Great Fenris:
        Instead of repeating the tale of Tyr, it could cost permanent Rage, as the Great Fenris remembers the character that the puny Rage of a Garou is nothing compared to his. Alternatively, it could have a requisite of extreme honesty to be used without a cost, and the risk of loosing one's hand could then be pending over the character's head without being a certainty.

        Release from Bondage:
        I see no reason to change it.

        Firebringer:
        At the very least, abdicating from defense seems silly to me. A Legendary hero could go the other way, becoming more resilient to each taken power. On the other hand, a simple way to better this one is to give the character a discount on XP to learn the same Gifts. Maybe even wave completely the cost until it amounts to the same cost of the Rank 6 Gift itself.

        Home in All Lands:
        Keep it as is.

        Shield of Gaia:
        It could be expanded to let in new immunity at a cost. Maybe similar to Firebringer in regard to the first exposition, and still limited to modern sources of damage, or even to only industrialized sources of damage.

        Renew the Cycle:
        I would rewrite this thing entirely. I'll post what I did to it later. The fluff is awesome, but killing vampires simply isn't that great or silverfangish.

        Thought-Form of the Twelve Ministers:
        No change.

        Invoked Presence:
        Could be broken down in specific gifts for the presence of each Totem.

        As In the Beginning:
        Needs to do what is supposed to do. Reduce the local Gauntlet to 0 during a scene, and add more specific rules on the area affected.

        Rebirthing:
        Is a good thing for Grimfang. It don't need to be stated as a Gift, though.

        Becoming Uktena:
        Also something to take out from the list as a Gift. It could be interesting as a ritual, as a special Fetish, or as a special event without hard rules attached.

            Unstoppable Warrior:
            Roll Rage difficulty 6 at the beginning of every turn, you regenerate that much damage, even from silver, at the end of the turn even if you have taken enough damage to take you beyond incapacitated. The effects of the gift are permanent.

            One on One:
            The gift may be used only at night time when the moon is visible in the night sky. The cost to activate it depends on the phase of the moon: 2 for full moon, 3 for gibbous, 4 for half, 5 for crescent, may not be used on the nights of new moon.

            Sleep of the Hero:
            The Hero will always return when Gaia demands it, be it for a caern in danger, to resque an important character, defeat an impossible threat or whatever dramatic reason. The hero will remain until his goal is succeeded or failed. The gift automatically activates once per story when the story teller feels its right to do so.

            Storyteller:
            The Galliard must say the story to an audience and must know firsthand the events, if the story has never been true in the first place may never be altered, if the story is based on true events with real persons he may alter it as he pleases, when the story telling ends the events of the story changed for real as well, how that affects the row of history is something the Galliard has to discover for himself.

            Call Great Fenris:
            The avatar of great Fenris has no stats, does aggravated usoakable damage and may not be damaged by any means except from entities of the same level. If the hero who summoned it does not perform in the combat up to his fullest potential, aka does not satisfy the spirit's expectations, will be devoured.

            Release from Bondage:
            The werewolf may not be bonded by any means, instead of gaining 15 strength for that purpose.

            Firebringer:
            Enemies gain no modifier for a power used on the garou, normal difficulty applies, though the power in question must be used successfully upon the Ragabash and suffer its effects. Then he learns automatically the power of the gift and may use it against his foes, he can also teach these powers as gifts to other garou, also may teach any gift he knows as if he was a spirit.

            Home in All Lands:
            Keep it as is.

            Shield of Gaia:
            Automatically works on any crafted, no natural source of damage, be it bombs, laser beams, bullets etc etc. The gift will not work against natural sources of damage, if you throw a rock it still applies normal damage.

            Renew the Cycle:
            Call it Return to Gaia's Cycle, the gift is the bane of all things that should be dead but are not, be it vampires, mummys, warlocks etc etc. The more ancient the offender of Gaia's cycle the more easy is the roll of the gift activation and more dramatic the effects.

            Invoked Presence:
            The gift invokes the Tribal Totem or a celestine he is allied/in favor with, the effects of the gift are up to the story teller and are even more powerful than full powered Totem Gift (metis). When the gift ends the mighty spirit takes the werewolf with it and demands a chiminage to prove his worth before using the gift again.

            As In the Beginning:
            The Theurge not only reduces gauntlet to 0 but actually merges the spiritual with the material world, spirits unite with their material counterparts and gain their true ancient forms, the union of spirit and flesh gives to living creatures supernatural properties, they add their spirit Rage willpower and Gnosis to their respected stats, they soak aggravated damage and regenerate lethal like garou, they become highly intelligent and may communicate with people. Other spiritual entities like gaia, luna or helios may assist the werewolf depending on circumstances.

            Rebirthing:
            No need to be born again from a mother's womb, the werewolf, dies leaves bones behind and reshapes in the umbra at an age that would resemble a freshly changed cub.

            Becoming Uktena:
            Remove the forever transformation, the werewolf though may be in danger to lose himself to the raw power of the gift and really think that he is uktena for ever. The Uktena stats are added to the unmodified homid attributes.

                            Originally posted by Maris Streck View Post
                            Gift of Power (Rank 6 general gift):
                            Win the game. Congratulation, you've just won at Werewolf!

                            There, I fixed your underpowered gifts. No thanks needed. And now I'm out. :3
                            This actually gave me an idea. A general rule on Rank 6 Gifts.

                            Rank 6 Gift (Unique):
                            At the pinnacle of your journey, you was personally gifted by a Celestine. The entity coalesced your own destiny in the archetypal manifestation of your spiritual power.

                            Give a name and concept to your personal Gift, discussing it with your ST. Once per scene you can declare an effect on the scope of the Gift and what each success will mean in a pure Gnosis roll. A single success can exact the same effect a lesser Gift would at 5 successes, for example, while additional successes multiply variables like range or duration. Costs are halved for each Rank of difference between the original effect and 6 (1 Perma dot becomes 5 temp points, 1 temp point becomes 0). If the effect calls for a resisted roll, it is done with either Willpower, Gnosis or Rage with +5 dice and 1 additional success on your result.

                            The ST can let you improvise effects at higher costs. As a rule of thumb, any effect able to alter the status of a battlefield should cost 1 to 5 points of either Willpower, Rage or Gnosis. An effect that can change the status in a big caern and its territory should cost 1 permanent dot, +1 per other caerns affected. An effect that can change the course of history should cost your character in a way or another, but should be truly epic.

                            You can't call those effects for naught, though. Rank 6 Gift is a personal bless from one of the greatest spirits, and a manifestation of your very essence and destiny. Using it for lesser tasks or without good reason can mess with your being and fate, always to achieve the greatness you deserve. The first symptoms seem like ill luck, making those uses result in a big chain of unintended events. If still abused, fate causes even more trouble, as it leads you to hard situations that justify the use of such gifts. The bad luck goes on until the untimely end of the legendary hero in a way fitting to the great stories of the People. Depending on the Garou in question, the spirits can try to warn of such fate before it's too late.

                                                        I think that the idea of a legend gaining a legendary power that has something to do with the legend it's very much on point. You leave your mark in the spirit world and the spirit world marks you in return.

                                                        Not so the "invent what you want on the fly" thing. It departs from what Gifts are (a power the spirits give to you). Besides, a Legend it's someone who has done great deeds without the lvl 6, they shouldn't need "magick(tm)" to change the story.

                                                        Instead make so Rank 6 it's such a big social boost that you barely need Chriminage with Gaian spirits, everyone understands that if a Legend says something has to be done, it has to be done. Incarna are happy to have you in their courts as advisor and ally. NO spirit it's ever going to deny you a Gift, even if they are of other Tribes.

                                                        Also more lvl 6 powers could be rewarded if the character keeps doing Epic stuff.

                                                        I don't get Maris complaints. I don't like power gamers that want lvl 6 to increase their "ass kicking algorithm", but why should a Legendary power be less than earthshaking?, what does THAT add to the Legend from a ST point of view? .

                                                        In my opinion Lvl 6 powers are suposed to be Epic, that being said Epic isn't always "nice" -

                                                        Becoming Uktena does Epic very well. Sure, it "kills you" at the end of the scene, but it also gives you narrative powers limited by the ST (and they should be massively powerful powers, since "even a Thunderwyrm should fear you") during that scene. I think such epic powers should change the result of said scene, and doing the impossible before dying it's a very cool way to retire your character. In a sense, it IS an "I won werewolf button", but done in a very thematic way. Thus I don't think it sucks, far from it, it's the perfect stuff to give a Legend (a way to "die" with awesomeness).
                                                        The link says it's "frustrating" that they don't give mechanics to this thing. I don't see this as "frustrating" at all. It's not casual either. It's because such an epic moment shouldnt be ruined with stats and checks. It would be like giving stats to Ur-Shulgi, an Antediluvian, or an Incarna-I would rather not do that.

                                                        Others, like the "ash a vampire thing", if not necessarily weak, they surely look underwhelming to me and I wouldn't mind to change them.
