            This stems from the fact that most people who are into roleplaying games to the level that they engage in discussions are more into theory of narrative, and Eco is universally seen as a great writer and thinker. Using Eco as a reference shows that you are well read, enjoy deep cuts s on varied topics and are engaging seriously with the topic. It builds your credibility if you go to a source so universally acclaimed.

            But Eco looks at it through the theory of narrative (which of course is critical to any kind of propaganda a regime needing national fervour and/or fascist regime would key into) and how it gets a life of its own among the population justifying what needs to be done. It's easy to dismiss how these myths are built and how they take a life on your own when you live a life free of conflict and without needing to actually band together (heh) to fight an external enemy. The choices you make and the compromises you need to do reverberate for decades after you actually managed to overcome the crisis that put you into this mode. What he is pointing to are negative societal patterns that he noticed as a child in fascist Italy and how they shaped his childhood and postwar Europe. For this reason I feel Eco is useful, but not the actual litmus test.

            In general I feel the whole Fascist label is bandied around way too easily these days, and that we're obfuscating a lot of truly bad actors in the need to attack people we disagree with to demonstrate how right we are and how wrong they are.

                                                    To come back on the origin of the discussion, is the garou nation fascistic?

                                                    Frankly, no, it lacks too much characteristics for that label.

                                                    Yes, they are a war based group, with strict top down hierarchies, a good dose of despise for the weak, some obsession with racial purity, and a siege mentality.

                                                    But that isn't different from most warrior nations across history, Sparta comes to mind, and spartan were not fascists.

                                                    Fascism imply an autocratic ruler, a party following him with absolute power over the population and the state, a total intolerance for dissent and a "everything in the state, nothing against the state, nothing outside the state" mentality.

                                                    The garou nation miss the mark on all of these points.

                                                    For a start, the nation, despite it's name, isn't a structured organisation. It is more an alliance of 13 tribes with a common goal, shared rules and at least lip respect to the authority of the silver fangs.

                                                    Howether, each tribe is basically it's own thing, he silver fangs has no control on the leadership of, say, the fianna, nor could they demand to participate in the reunions of the shadow lords. In summary, each tribe has it's own hierarchy that doesn't depend on the overall kings of the garou.

                                                    Which makes the garou nation closer to a feudal system, if not for the fact that the authority of the silver fang is barely acknowledged. There's a whole tribe that don't hide it's ambition of taking the throne after all.

                                                    Add to that the fact that despite being the "garou" nation, authority isn't merely held by garou, but also by spirits.

                                                    If the silver king says X and your tribe totem says Y, it's expectable that your tribe as a whole will follow the totem.

                                                    And where any issue in a fascist nation is dealed with by representant of the state, amongs the garous, issues are generally dealt with within a pack, a sept or more rarely, a tribe.

                                                    Hence authority is not really centralised in the garou.





                                                    Add to that the fact that the silver king is not above the litanny or the authority of spirits like Falcon or Luna.


                                                    As for the intolerance for dissent, there's massive difference of opinions between tribes and within tribes, and there's no authority to cull these. Each of the point of the litanny are up to interpretation aand debate, and the tribes don't count on the silver fang to decide on that matter.
                                                    ​​​​​
                                                    ​​
                                                    Even the extreme camps hunted down by their brethren aren't killed for dissent, but because they're dangerous either for garou, their protectorates or the mission of the species.

                                                    As an exemple, the stargazers, which leaved the garou nation in revised, were not hunted down as traitors, at most, they were called cowards by the most vehement tribes.


                                                    Edit: also the garou tribes fought and sometime still fight each others over caerns, old grudges and quiproquo. In a fascist state, such internal fight is not allowed either. Any conflict of interest between various part of the state was dealt with by the party
