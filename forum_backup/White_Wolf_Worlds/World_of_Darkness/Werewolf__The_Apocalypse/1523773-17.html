            And now to late 1994 with Fianna. The book was written by Harry Heckel, and has a mildly notorious flaw of being focused very heavily on Ireland (especially Northern Ireland) and all but ignoring most other places with Celtic heritage. (Seriously, in the geographic section, Boston gets its own section that's the same size as the one for the rest of the US and larger than the ones for England or the rest of Continental Europe.) Overall, the book is very "Oirish" (see the TVTropes website's entry for Oirland). The worst part of this is the heavy focus on the Troubles (the long running religious/ethnic/nationalist conflict from the late 60s to the late 90s), including their Camps largely being presented as being totally focused on which modern nation they derive from (Ireland, Wales, Britain, Continental Europe).

            The art for the templates appears to be by Tony Diterlizzi. Overall, it's good work. However, he ended up being a major artist for Changeling, and you can certainly see the reason for that, as a lot of these have a look that feels more like Fae than Garou. I generally like his work, but I'm not sure it's the best fit for Werewolf.

            And we start off with ... (damn it)

            Roving Ragabash (Homid Ragabash)

            Back to the Camp + Auspice naming convention, as this is a member of the Whispering Rovers Camp. The Rovers were the "Continental" Fianna, meaning mainly French, but at the same time were heavily patterned after Irish Travelers/"Walking People" and essentially presented as Fianna versions of the The Rom (see Clanbook Gangrel).

            The character was orphaned at a young age and bounced around between different guardians until reaching an age where she could access their rather substantial inheritance. She immediately started spending the money on world travel without much of any real goal in life. Then came the First Change and being found by a young man with "bright green eyes" (because sure, why not?) who explained the Fianna, and overnight they changed from "a little girl with too much money to a woman with goals and a reason for living." No idea if the guy was a werewolf or just Kinfolk. Anyway, now she pretty much does the same stuff she was doing before, traveling a lot and enjoying herself, but also using her money to fund her pack. About the only real change seems to be a better appreciation for how short life can be in light of having to fight the Wyrm.

            As far as role playing hints, we're told that she knows a lot of trivia, and usually just enough to get herself in trouble (which can be fun). She cares a lot about her pack mates, has had a lot of acquaintances but few friends, and enjoys spending money on people she cares about.

            Equipment is a cell phone (still essentially for upper class people in the early 90s), yacht, credit cards galore, and lots of clothes.

            The image is ... a man. Yeah, the write up is explicitly a woman, and the art is very much not. It's a generally normal looking guy in fairly upscale clothing. Also with pointy ears (the Changeling thing). On its own, it's a perfectly fine image, just perhaps more fitting for a Sidhe than a Fianna. But I can't help but wonder if it was inadvertently swapped with the Theurge one that follows it.

            As I said, the character is a Whispering Rover. Exactly why is not touched on in any form, save that they like to travel. And I presume are probably meant to be from France or something.

            For Attributes, Physicals are secondary, with 2/3/3. Socials are tertiary with an extra dot from freebies, for 3/2/2. Mental is primary, with 3/3/4.
            This seems backwards. While I can see an argument for Social vs Mental being primary, Social shouldn't be tertiary for this character. Just speaking for myself, I would have gone with 2/2/2 for Physical, 4/3/3 for Social and 3/2/3 for Mental.

            For Abilities, Talents are primary, with a 3 in Alertness, 2 each in Brawl, Dodge, and Streetwise, and 1 each in Athletics, Empathy, Intimidation, and Primal Urge.
            Skills are secondary, with 3 in Stealth, 2 in Survival, and 1 each in Drive, Etiquette, Melee, and Repair.
            Knowledge is tertiary with 3 in Linguistics and 1 each in Enigmas and Rituals.
            These feel a little all over the place, without much focus, and a few feel more like they were picked just to be useful in a standard Werewolf campaign. I would've expected higher ratings in Etiquette, and perhaps Expression as well. Also maybe something like Investigation or Law.

            Backgrounds is a 5 in Resources. This fits with the character and is kind of a nice change from the typical range of mostly broke werewolves we've seen so far. However, I think a few freebies spent on some Contacts or even a dot of Pure Breed wouldn't have been out of the question.

            They have an extra Gift, which eats up seven of sad freebies, all of which I feel could've been better spent on Abilities and Backgrounds.

            Rage is the default 1. Gnosis is raised to 2 and Willpower raised to 4.

            So, generally speaking, I like this character. Globetrotting Wealthy Heiress is a fun and fairly different idea for a Fianna character. The stats could be a bit better, but conceptually, it's pretty good. However, I have no clue why this character is a Ragabash. There's absolutely nothing here that says "Questioner of the Ways" or even "Trickster". I think this would've worked a lot better as a Galliard, someone who expresses "Lover of the Ways" through a love of adventure and various cultures, of discovering new places and things, and of the bonds they build with their traveling companions.

                Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
                And now to late 1994 with Fianna. The book was written by Harry Heckel, and has a mildly notorious flaw of being focused very heavily on Ireland (especially Northern Ireland) and all but ignoring most other places with Celtic heritage.
                It's definitely a big flaw. And besides the issue of limiting the Fianna to Ireland only and leaving out the other Celts, there's also the issue of neglecting Stag as the totem in general and imagining what the tribe would be like for non-Celts like lupus breed, or even homids prior to the emergence of the Celtic peoples. This unfortunately makes the book more about magical Irish people than a group of werewolves in cultic devotion to the Prince of the Forest.

                There's no problem with exploiting certain things about Irish culture. The tribe's capital is based in Ireland after all. And there are interesting things like the historical and folkloric fianna having a lot of wolf/outlaw imagery and poetry and tying that into the Garou. But it's unnecessarily limiting. This is a major problem with all the tribebooks. It's easy to understand why the initial writers heavily explored the human culture of a tribe's homid members - it's an obvious source to steal from when developing the tribes, but it was clearly a mistake. The Revised tribebooks would do a lot to expand away from too heavily specific human cultures, but overall they too neglected the aspect that all tribes would predate known human cultures and in origin would be just as lupus influenced as homid.

                        And, finally, continuing. (Had a lot going on the past week+ with work and Christmas prep.)

                        Fey Struck Garou (Homid Theurge)

                        I'm a little meh on calling a template "Garou". It feels a bit non-descriptive. I feel like there are so many other words you could use here. Poet. Mystic. Dreamer. Just something that actually tells me more about the character. I mean, I know its a werewolf already. Tell me something more.

                        So, apparently as a child this character encountered the Fey. Or at least thinks they did. And became a bit obsessed about the whole thing. Exactly what they did about this obsession isn't exactly clear. Study fairy legends? Constantly investigate strange and mysterious places? No idea. Eventually they stumbled across werewolves, and learned that they were one.

                        They're described as a perpetual dreamer, someone who doesn't really want to grow up, and who notices odd things. But also they don't really pay attention to other people, people wrapped up in their own obsession. And tend to presume "faerie" explanations for things regardless if it's true or not. They've visited the Arcadian Gateway realm and want to go back, and believe that the power of the Fey will give the tribe the power to defeat the Wyrm and Weaver and save Gaia.

                        Equipment is a four leaf clover, a map of local ley lines, and a well worn copy of A Midsummer Night's Dream.

                        The image is, again because of the artist's style being so heavily associated with Changeling, looking more of a character from that game rather than one from Werewolf. But it is a very nice image. A very tall and petite female, in what might be taken as a cross between Victorian-esque clothing and mid-90s club wear. It's a cool look, but again, feels maybe a little more Changeling (or even Mage Hollow One) than Werewolf.

                        The character is part of the Tuatha de Fionn camp. Apparently just because of the fey connection.

                        For Attributes, Physicals are tertiary with one extra dot from freebies, for a 2/3/2 spread. Socials are (apparently) primary, but are missing a dot, for a 3/2/4 spread. That leaves Mental as secondary, with 3/3/2.

                        I've no idea where that missing dot might've meant to go. Maybe Charisma, but there's not much in the description to indicate the character is especially charismatic. Or manipulative. And the high Appearance also feels kind of random. Like they just didn't know what else to do or perhaps that the art came in and they just decided the character was now a super attractive female.
                        I feel like, as is, this character might benefit from having Mental as primary, with a high Perception to reflect the idea that they notice (odd) things that others frequently don't, and maybe high Wits in the tradition of certain types of folklore characters.

                        For Abilities, Talents are secondary with two extra dots from freebies. Expression is 3. Alertness and Empathy are 2 each. And 1 each in Athletics, Brawl, Dodge, and Primal Urge.
                        Skills are tertiary with a 2 in Stealth and 1 each in Animal Ken, Performance, and Survival.
                        Knowledge is primary with one extra dot from freebies. Enigmas is 4, and Linguistics, Occult, and Rituals are 3 each. Then there's a single dot in Investigation.
                        While I can understand the focus on the Knowledges, I feel like the rest is maybe a little unfocused. While this would fit a sort of aimless dreamer (which is what the rather sparse character description leaves the impression of), it feels like something is missing.

                        Backgrounds are 3 in Past Life and 2 in Rituals (with the rituals being written out individually). This seems fine, for the most part. Although I think this is a character that might benefit from a Mentor. Maybe also a Fetish tied to the Arcadia Gateway or some Contacts.

                        Rage is the default 2, Gnosis is raised to 3 with freebies, and Willpower is the default 3.

                        There just really isn't a lot here with this character. I get the idea of a character who (apparently) encountered the Fey at a young age and never really got over it, but beyond that, there just isn't much of anything beyond a sort of vague day dreamer. This just sort of feels like an obligatory Fey related character because the tribe is Celtic (Oirish) and so has to have ties to the Little People. It feels like you could do a lot more with a Fianna Seeker of the Ways. The previous wealthy world traveler would have worked here as someone constantly wandering the world looking for things to help the tribe and the Garou nation.

                        Hopefully have the next one up in the next few days.

                                    Black Rose (Metis Philodox)

                                    So, the black rose tends to be a symbol of mourning, mystery, loss, and elegance, as well as major changes like death and rebirth.

                                    This character is the child of a Fianna mother and a father of some other tribe. Apparently she was teased as a child that her father was a Bone Gnawer, but didn't care because she believed her parents loved each other. Her mother raised her and taught her the Litany and the Silver Record, and how to "sing and howl". Her deformity is blindness, which "haunts her everyday" even though she's learned to compensate.

                                    She has a strong sense of self and where she comes from, as well as powerful Past Lives. (Keep this in mind; it will come up later.) Her blindness "makes a fun joke" as she can't see all the suffering of the world. She travels and sings like all Song Keepers (she's a member of that camp, but nothing is said about how or why she joined, or what else she does among them). She hopes to one day fall in love like her mother did. Her favorite story is Cuchulain and Emer, because naturally the Fianna are all about human Irish mythology and not any of the stories of the Garou. No word on what her favorite food or color is.

                                    Roleplaying hints are that she carries herself with pride to cover for her insecurity about being a blind Metis. She loves being a Song Keeper and finds a beauty poetry and songs that nothing can take away.

                                    We get a paragraph about her blindness and optional rules for using her sense of smell to compensate, which is interesting.

                                    Equipment is a flute and rose colored glasses. I presume the latter is supposed to be a joke of some sort.

                                    The image is, again, a very nice one. A young woman with long hair and little round glasses in a dress holding a musical instrument which I am not knowledgable enough about the properly identify. As I said, it's a very nice image, but much like the previous two, it really feels much more like it is an image of a Changeling character (or possibly even a vampire or a mage) rather than a werewolf.

                                    For Attributes, Physicals are tertiary, with a 1/3/2 spread. I'm not sure why this character should be so physically weak, baring some sort of lithe female musician stereotype. Socials are primary, with 4/3/3. Mental is secondary with an extra dot from freebies, for 3s across the board.

                                    With Abilities, Talents are tertiary with two extra dots from freebies. 3 in Primal Urge, 2 in Expression, and 1 each Brawl and Empathy. I'm just going to presume that actually Brawling requires someone to actually grapple with her. And I'm kind of surprised by the lack of Alertness. That feels like it would be an important part of compensating/coping with blindness. Really, I think the only reason Primal Urge is so high is because the bit about coping with deformity via her sense of smell involves Perception + Primal Urge rolls. Also, given the way the character is described, I'd sort of expect her Expression and Empathy to be a little higher.
                                    Skills are secondary, with a 3 in Performance and a wide spread of 1s in Animal Ken, Etiquette, Firearms (?), Leadership, Stealth, and Survival. OK, I'll ask the obvious question here: Why does a blind character have the Firearms skill? Maybe this is the world's subtlest reference to that scene with Ray Charles in the Blues Brothers movie. And a lot of these skills seem kind of random. I think a higher Etiquette and maybe also Leadership would benefit this character.
                                    This leaves Knowledge as primary. 3 each in Linguistics, Occult, and Rituals, and 2 each in Enigmas and Law. Honestly, this Law bit is about the first thing I've seen that fits the Auspice.

                                    For Backgrounds, she has 2 in Allies and 3 in Mentor (her mother). I do like that we get some info on who the Mentor is. I wish more templates did that. I also wish this template had done the same with Allies.
                                    I mentioned earlier than the character write up says she has a powerful Past Life. Except that she doesn't. None of that Background whatsoever. (Or Pure Breed, or Kinfolk, or anything else that might contribute to that strong sense of self and where she comes from.)

                                    Seven freebies have been spent on a fourth Gift. I really feel that those freebies could've been better spent on Backgrounds or Abilities.

                                    Rage and Gnosis are both the default 3. Willpower has been raised to 5 with freebies.
                                    For those keeping track, that means five freebies for Attributes, four for Abilities, seven for Gifts, and two for WP, for a total of eighteen. So, just from a numerical perspective, this template is broken.

                                    Much like the previous one, there just isn't a lot here. The character is blind and a singer/musician. And a romantic. And that's about it. There's nothing really here that conveys Keeper of the Ways. Not really even the low hanging fruit of a character born from breaking the Litany now ironically tasked with upholding it. Really, this character feels more like a Galliard/Lover of the Ways. Someone who dearly loves music and stories - especially love stories - and her family and wants to share that love with others.
                                    I think there's a lot that could have been done here, but just wasn't. At the very least, I think getting rid of the extra Gift and sorting out the Abilities would go a ways toward helping. Perhaps make Talents secondary or primary so that you have the dots to put in Alertness as well as Primal Urge, Empathy, and Expression (maybe even a little Subterfuge).

                                        The Black Rose is actually my favorite template of the first Fianna tribebook. I have an inordinate fondness for it. She's definitely not a combat character, which is unusual for a Garou, but I feel is very much an early World of Darkness trend to open up roleplaying to other play styles. I don't think she makes a good PC. But she would be a great NPC as the ST could explore a lot of things which a PC likely wouldn't. I think very few players would ever want to roleplay a character who is blind, even given the lupus form's scent abilities.

                                        She's not a standard Philodox, but is very much in the keeping of the Fianna interpretation of "Songkeeper" which unfortunately is not adequately explained or distinguished from the Galliard. "Songkeepers" in fact are mentioned as a "camp" of the Galliards, and I wonder if this character in fact was originally intended for that auspice. I think she can be easily seen as Philodox as someone who PRESERVES the songs/traditions of the past. But the Fianna overall always seems a tribe heavily associated with Galliad concepts. This should be made explicit though in order to distinguish her from a Galliard. She's a very positive depiction of a Metis, and I think the template is stronger for that. So many other examples are just incredibly negative. It's a good distinction.

                                        The description mentions Past Lives (Ancestors), but the character sheet does not mention them. Further proof that these may have been given to two different people.

                                        Overall, I have a much more positive assessment of this template than No One of Consequence . I see a lot of potential here even with all the normal caveats that the mechanics should be improved.
