    Heretical Thoughts on Unicorn

    05-28-2016, 08:27 PM
    As some people who follow my posts know, I have issues with Werewolf. I think too often the game becomes an unintentional parody of Captain Planet forcing left wing politics down people's throat instead of being a game of "savage horror" inspired by actual werewolf lore in mythology, folk tales, literature, and cinema. So in my games, I make alterations that keep the game's bones while developing it a little bit differently. Every now and again, I post these differences for the benefit of the forum. If they can make something out of it for their games, all the better. And I don't mind if people disagree with me or think I'm wrong. De gustibus non est disputandum.

    The Children of Gaia tribe is somewhat problematic. Although the tribe has its fans, it seems like many players have issues with the depiction of the Children of Gaia. Their tribebooks are among the most reviled in fandom (anytime it comes up on this or other forums, many people explain their dislike of them). The major reason is that the tribe seems to be at odds with the concept of the Garou in general, and this tension has not been handled well.

    I think the big reason why is how the totem of unicorn is handled, and how unicorn is depicted in the game setting. Simply put, it is not the unicorn of legend, but the unicorn of Saturday morning cartoons and children's toys – Unicorn as My Little Pony. I think if the legends of unicorn are actually consulted, a different interpretation of the totem and therefore the tribe can be used that would better integrate the Children with the Garou.

    This is not the My Little Pony interpretation of unicorn, but I will attempt to preserve the essentials of the Tribe. I think any valid interpretation must cover the important events and aspects of the Tribe. I consider these to be those elements:
    • They opposed the Impergium.
    • They are the primary foes of the Defiler Wyrm among the Garou.
    • They stress the need for unity and cooperation among the Garou Nation.
    • They have a tendency to seek the leadership of the Garou Nation or otherwise consider themselves the “true” Garou.
    The next few posts lay out some of my thinking. I am breaking them into separate posts because I think it aids readability.

      Here are some points about unicorns in real legend and folklore.
      • Unicorns represent the power and sanctity of the unspoiled wilderness. They are paragons of purity.
      • Unicorns are untamed animals, always deadly and ferocious.
      • The pride and ferocity of the unicorn meant it would not allow itself to be taken alive.
      • Unicorns are considered to be among the most, if not the most, magnificent and beautiful beasts.
      • Unicorns are a protectors of purity and defender of the innocent such as virgin maidens and children. Unicorns pay homage to their purity and gentleness.
      • Karkadanns were the unicorns of Persia and India. They were very dangerous.
      • Legend said that the horse of Alexander the Great, Boucephalous, was a unicorn.
      • The horn of a unicorn has healing properties.
      • Natural history places the unicorn in India (probably because they were tales of the Indian rhinoceros).
      • In older times, the unicorn and lion fought over who would rule the wilderness. There was a seasonal exchange – unicorns' power was greater in spring, but the lion increased in the summer.
      Two important call outs – nowhere are unicorns associated with either “peace” or “love”. These are not intrinsic properties of unicorns. At best they are emergent properties based on their purity and tales of innocence taming them.

      The essentials of Unicorn as a totem are thus:
      • A paragon and protector of purity.
      • A creature that is untamed and ferocious.
      • A healer as well as a warrior.
      • A magnificent beast who is a natural leader of the wilderness.
      “Peace” by itself is not one of its properties, although we can see the reasons why promoting peace became a goal. Originally it was in service of its original totemic goals, but later became a goal in itself – probably as a result of a particularly successful Camp in pushing its political goals.

      Indeed, the association unicorn has with virginity places it directly opposed to many concepts the Tribebook has associated with “love”, particularly physical sex acts.

      The “burned out hippies” image which plagues the Tribe does represent some of its members. Whether this represents a minority of majority of the Tribe is dependent on the ST, but for my own chronicles I make this group those who inadvertently serve the Wyrm – they represent a corruption of Unicorn's children who hide behind a false interpretation of the totem and the tribe while failing to achieve the true qualities of the Totem. It is unlikely such people were able to pass their tribal Rite of Passage if they were like this as cubs, so it must be a phenomenon that happens afterwards. (Not all “hippies” are “burned out”, so even in my chronicle I have stalwart “granola eaters” as upstanding members of the tribe.)

      The role of unicorn as a ruler of the wilderness in rival to lion shows a great insight on why the Children of Gaia have tried to lead the Garou Nation in various forms. The nature of the totem keeps pushing its children – the tribe – into that role.

      Why do the Children of Gaia rage? They rage in defense of purity, particularly when it is threatened by the Defiler Wyrm.

      The Children of Gaia opposed the Impergium not out of a sense of non-violence. They opposed it because 1) they thought the innocent were being killed, and 2) they feared the Garou's heart were being corrupted – instead of bravely fighting the Wyrm in its lair, they were killing helpless people – the Wyrm was both corrupting the courage of the Garou and making them cruel and despicable. This was later twisted – likely quite lately, perhaps as a result of prolonged exposure to Christianity in Europe during the Dark Ages – as a general desire for non-violence, peace, or compassion. Means became ends over the course of centuries. This process would be accelerated later as Humanism, the Enlightenment, and international pacifism took hold.

          Let's look how this impacts the current setting if you want to include some of these ideas in your chronicles.

          First, it provides a basis for Children of Gaia to be more fierce and warlike – something many players seem to want to justify.

          Second, the burned out hippies interpretation isn't seen as the basis fo the tribe, but is a weakness or corruption of it. How extensive that corruption is, is up to you. Of course, the burned out hippies deny that entirely and see themselves as the embodiment of unicorn's desire. Most of them haven't fallen to the extent that they detect as Wyrm tainted. But the worst of them do end up corrupted, but so do members of other tribes, so that doesn't prove anything, does it? They would make interesting foils or even villains (for those who have gone off the deep end).

          Third, there is still a roll for granola eaters in the tribe as defenders of purity and the unspoiled wilderness. These would be the stereotypical good aspects of hippies without those elements seen as negative.

          Fourth, the desire of the Children to lead the Garou Nation gives them a harder edge than is typical for the tribe in canon. They are not ambitious like the Shadow Lords, but they could be seen as one of the tribes most likely to challenge the Silver Fangs for leadership of the Nation. This actually fits with some of the ambitious statements found in the tribebooks which give the Children a leadership or dominant role.

          Fifth, there is a role for fierce, combatant Children of Gaia – the untamed karkadann of the tribe. This is not some anomalous role that requires tortured explanation. It is at the heart of the tribe as the defenders of the unspoiled wilderness, and represented by the “black unicorn” spirit. This camp is probably on fairly good terms with the Red Talons, normally a tribe in opposition to the Children. They don't want to kill humans, but they defend the remaining areas of pristine wilderness. There is probably a very strong lupus presence in this group.

          So that's it. Not very much different from the tribe as is, but with a few different emphases that I think puts the tribe into a better situation.

                      I hadn't given any thought to that to be honest, but this is an important role of the tribe. It probably has to do with all metis are obviously babies when they are born. And babies are innocent, even if their parents sinned. As protectors of purity and innocence, the Children would presumably take them (and any other children) abandoned by the other tribes.

                      In terms of converts - talking about adults here, particularly those who already were accepted by other tribes, but then left - there is not a strong connection. It's probably a result of "means becoming ends" once again (Hey, we take in all these babies including metis, why wouldn't we accept adult converts?) plus the ongoing issue of Unicorn as a ruler of the wilderness. I think the early tribebook had some extreme language that the Children of Gaia had all of Gaia's realm as their territory and really pushed the Children as a tribe that should be dominant. If so, there may be a thinking that since Unicorn is the ruler, then everyone should be his Children and if so, why not take everyone.

                      Of course, that does conflict to a degree with the idea that Unicorn is still selective with their Rites of Passage. Unicorn has to have some standards even if the tribe leaders have lowered theirs. It may be that the tests have been somewhat watered down over the years, or of a nature that isn't as challenging in certain areas as it should be. I would probably make the tests to win Unicorn as a pack totem for the Rite of the Totem much harder than it is to become a tribe member. A subtle clue that the tribe has perhaps become a bit more tolerant than it should be.

                      I'll have to give that some more thought though.
