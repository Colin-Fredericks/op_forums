    Narlthus

    03-23-2022, 04:07 AM
    Narlthus was introduced very early in the game in the 1e sourcebook Rite of Passage. It was heavily tied to North America as the Wendigo Theurge She-Cries-Ice was the Garou that bound the bane (IMO she should be a Legendry Garou and awesome Ancestor spirit). Nevertheless, Narlthus was mentioned as a Talon of the Wyrm that meant it was intrinsically involved with the myths of the entire Garou Nation, not just the Three Brothers. In my own chronicles, I have the Eurasian tribes recognize Narlthus as a major threat, but ignorant of how it was defeated until they finally made contact with the Three Brothers (some 10,000 to 12,000 years later!).

    I am working on creating various "factions" of the Wyrm to provide the game more depth in my chronicles. So I associate corrupt winter spirits with Narltus and the false Wendigo (of Ways of the Wolf sourcebook) and other bane spirits that make sense.

    As inspiration, I use a lot of the Ithaqua mythos of August Derleth (prominent in the Call of Cthulhu RPG) and similar media. I want the spirit to be more mysterious to Eurasian Garou than the Three Brothers, but I make some reference to it to Fimbul Winter and such so that certain Old World tribes understand the Bane in its own way.

    Interested in what other people have done with this Bane in their chronicles, or what potential they think the Bane has. While as presented in Rite of Passage, the Bane/Incarna is a revelation to modern Garou, I prefer to use it as an ongoing threat that just became more relevant with the events of Rite of Passage. What do you think?

      I take the complete lack of new posts to mean that essentially people haven't done anything with Narlthus in their chronicles!

      While not an entirely unexpected outcome, it is surprising in the sense that Rite of Passage was one of the original WW adventures and meant to start PCs from the original setting of the Sept of the Green. The assumption would be that many gaming groups utilized this setting and explored the the game from there. And while Rite of Passage gave us NPCs who thought Narlthus was a Talon of the Wyrm, the book itself stated it could have been any number of things. It was left to the ST. It was primarily meant as a plot hook and plot device that STs could expand into whatever they needed. If players actually haven't done this, it means the New York setting of Werewolf didn't hook players as well as Chicago By Night (and its associated adventrues) did for VtM players.

              Originally posted by werewolf43 View Post

              That's probably the end result of most games. Even though Rite of Passage describes what would happen if it was actually released, I wonder how many times this was actually done in game. Most STs don't want the PCs to fail, or figure out the impact of a big setting changing event. Though I'm sure it's happened a few times.

              I am actually less interested in the use of Narlthus itself as an antagonist against PCs or Garou characters, and more as a "faction". By that I mean, what are other kinds of NPCs (human cultists, occult researchers, Banes, corrupted spirits, fomori, other monsters) I can use to create stories for PCs. I can only do so much with Narlthus itself as an entity. But if there are other characters involved with it, I can do multiple stories. Even with Narlthus being imprisoned, I can get a lot of mileage out of that without even needing to specifically run the Rite of Passage adventure.

              For example, in one of my Vampire games, a player wanted to play a Gangrel heavily inspired by real world wendigo lore. Mechanically, I just told him to use the Curse flaw, and we came up with the specifics of the curse to reflect the wendigo lore he wanted to include. But to explain the curse, I tied it to Narlthus and how some of the first (Sabbat) Gangrel to arrive in the New World encountered the mound (sometime in the late 1500s), sensed occult power there, and was corrupted by it. Those tainted Sabbat Gangrel now pass the curse of the wendigo to their childer. (The PC did not know anything about this as he was an abandoned childe, but if he ever tried to hunt down his origins, he could learn this.)

              Once I did that, I ported it over to my Werewolf chronicles since they're a good foe for the actual Wendigo tribe of Garou. There aren't many of these vampire "wendigo", but now I have a nice little foe tied to Narlthus that allows me to run more plots related to this foe. (Now that I think about this having reread RoP, it would make perfect narrative sense for these to be the five Sabbat Gangrel bodyguards for Carmen, the Tremere anti-tribu helping Azarea excavate Narlthus's prison.)

Unfortunately none of the pregens had Sense Wyrm or Truth of Gaia (which would spoil the BSD plan), only Scent the True Form (which allowed the characters to identify BSD as Garou).
                  Second time we used characters we created. After we were exiled from the sept we did the only reasonable thing we could: go back to NY ASAP (first of all it's typical for Bone Gnawers to send their cubs to a distant sept and see how fast they can return; second there was a murder mystery to solve). He even tried to stop Azaera giving two players from his other group the only characters who knew at least some of her plan (Accolon, homid Ahroun Stargazer, Elder who went there following his vision and Rolling Stone, homid Ragabash Silent Strider, cliath send by Sept of the Green to find out why Wendigo don't respond; Acollon was described in Rite of Passage, Rolling Stone was his creation, mentioned in RoP) and let them played them after our characters left. Acollon died gloriously trying to stop Azaera, Rolling Stone returned with the news (Acollon's order, in case he didn't make it).

First she discredited both Wendigo sept and Sept of the Green using her agents as unable to stop her. After the political turmoil ended she suggested that one of the Talons is hidden in the Amazon (as no Theurge know how many of Talons there are) sending us in a wild goose chase, then to Russia for Koshchei and used the plot of Werewolf: the Apocalypse Heart of Gaia unreleased video game (basing on the cutscenes which he found on Youtube; I later did the same). Unfortunately I can't tell you how it ended because he died before he could do that.                       I never ran or played Rite of Passage, but I was inspired enough a few years ago to try and account for all four (or five) of the Talons of the Wyrm and made a write-up here. Three Talons of the Wyrm have been mentioned by name in the W:tA books: Narlthus (assumed to be the "Thumb" Talon) in Rite of Passage, Koshchei (the "First Finger" or "He-Who-Points") in Rage Across Russia and Thrassus Thrice-Damned (the "Little Finger) in Book of the Weaver.

                      No one knows for certain whether there were four or five Talons of the Wyrm, but assuming there were five, I wrote up two more short blurbs for 1) Galchus (the "Middle Finger" or "He-Who-Scorns") who was imprisoned by the Uktena and Wendigo into an artifact (actually, an artifact within another artifact; see the write-up link for details) that was buried underneath the Arctic Ocean, and for 2) Vothnaak, (the "Ring Finger"/Fourth Talon of the Wyrm), who was imprisoned in an artifact that somehow wound up in the Womb, the vault of forbidden items at the Sept of the Bygone Visions (mentioned in Caerns: Places of Power). My notes are pretty raw, admittedly, but perhaps if you guys are curious about the Talons of the Wyrm, maybe my notes will inspire you.

                          The Talons of the Wyrm was an idea introduced very early on, but was severely neglected afterwards. I wonder if this was something meant to be something much bigger in Werewolf lore and setting, but after Bill Bridges left as Developer, whatever plans he had for them was forgotten or neglected. I don't remember them much in the later work when Ethan Skemp was Developer, not even in the final Apocalypse book. He had other concepts he was interested in. We're certainly given very little information about them other than their release would be terrible, and therefore it's a matter of stopping whatever immediate steps are being done by someone else to release them.

                          Koschei probably has the most information about him as he has several pages about him in Rage Across Russia. Unfortunately it's not very evocative. He's not described well except that once grown to full power he'll free the other Talons and a lot of other imprisoned Banes. He's just a plot device to be defeated once the heroes collect enough plot coupons. There's no personality. No theme. No evocative legends. By comparison, Baba Yaga is a far more interesting character and faction.

                          We have even less about Exhibit 338, but since it's blatantly a rip off from John Carpenter's Prince of Darkness, we can easily get inspiration by watching the movie (unfortunately, while there's some neat things about it, I don't think it's a particularly good movie.)

                                      But anyway, since my last post yesterday, i decided to look into Rites of Passage to see if i could come up with a rationale of sorts for Carmen's belief in a connection of Narlthus to the Caine Mythos and how. Hold my Blood Mary.

                                      The book mentions that Narlthus' release would be felt as "a great evil's liberation" by every Black Spiral Dancer, vampire and bane (in that order) within a 1000 miles radius. Hundreds of banes will be consumed, as well as all the blood pool of any vampires present.

                                      This sentence could be easily chalked up as "OP big bad gimmick" but at the same time there are some quite relevant specifics.

                                      - Black Spiral Dancers, vampires and banes - not Garou, pure or wyrm-tainted (relevant since there is at least one wyrm-tainted Garou in the adventure itself) or other Fera (idem, since werebear is a major NPC), non-wyrm spirits or other supernaturals. Those three particular classes of being apparently have a special rapport/ressonance with it.

                                      But then we get back to the twist: "Hundreds of banes will be consumed, as well as all the blood pool of any vampires present."

                                      For one, that little stunt of Narlthus ressonates a lot with some imagery we have seen from V20 onwards on Antediluvians and their messing with the blood of their childer and/or those bound to them. For another, it makes imprisoned Narlthus into the Stone That Drinks Blood, a most peculiar twisted mirror/opposite to the Stone That Weeps Blood worshipped by the Lost Tribe and that is supposedly what remains of Zillah.

                                      As an aside, the fact this particular capacity of Narlthus is both quite broad "all the blood pool of any vampires" - read, clan makes no difference - and yet limited "of any vampires present" in radius kinds of resonates with a "heir of the 2nd generation" vibe imho.


                                      ​Oh yeah, the possiblity of finding an entity similar and potentially related to the pillar of all Lost Tribe legendry is definitely something for a Black Hand member to go completely bonkers over. Damn, that kind of correlation is potential Gehenna Crusade fuel level..

                                      (Seriously, Polonia's crusade in the turn of the millenium was spurred with much less than this)


                                      But wait, wait, there's more, it gets even better.

                                      If Vitae = Banes, would that mean that feeding DRAWS OUT BANES from within humans and DIGESTS THEM into Vitae?

                                      The Beast is a sort of incipient bane-like urge resulting of all the processed banestuff flowing through undead flesh and decreasing Humanity/Path while not directly related to serving the Wyrm, does reflect a slow ascension to dominance of the coagulated dark urges that compose the Vitae.

                                      That would give a boatload of extra layers of meaning to Garou calling vampires "leeches" - and suddenly fit the kindred (or Wan-Kuei for that matter) within Fera cosmology in a most surprising manner indeed, vampires as the Gaia's detox/sin-eaters, while explaining the propensity to ping on Sense Wyrm and yet having no ties whatsoever to banes, black spirals and other such stuff (or why animals offer so little vitae - almost no bane-attracting behaviour contamination on them).


                                      Going a little further into this slippery slope, Genesis 4:10 And the LORD said, “What have you done? The voice of your brother’s blood is crying to me from the ground" brings whole new levels of meaning (and cosmological crossover) to the equation.

                                      Is the Stone That Weeps Blood truly Zillah, an aspect of Gaia or a bit of both even?

                                      In some takes of Setite myth, the Founder claimed to have never been embraced, but to have drank the water of the river Du'at, that he equated with vitae.
                                      Exodus 17: 6 has Moses striking a rock and water springing from it for all of his community to drink.
