            OK, I'm going to get back to a less loaded comparison.

            Ignore all pre-W5 stuff.

            What's the point of having both the Ghost Council and the Silent Striders?

            They're both Wisdom Tribes.

            They're both tribes that deal with moving around ("Travel" and "Journey"). They both use their knowledge to hit the enemy sideways ("Subvert" and "Sabotage"). They both find hidden things ("Unearth" and "Scout"). The only real difference between them right now is "Solve" vs. "Deliver." So basically the Ghost Council is just the less cooperation friendly version of the Silent Striders? There's nothing else that makes them distinct here.

            Obviously we don't have Favors/Bans to separate them a bit more, but we also don't have Auspices either. From what we know so far it seems like they could easily be one Tribe. The Silent Strider verbs are just the Rabagash/maybe Galliard take (focusing more on getting information out to people), while the Ghost Council verbs are the Theurge/Philodox take (taking more time to intellectually dissect the information).

            While as a general approach to making splat selection approachable the verbs thing is actually a good method, in this case it really feels like the thesaurus was out to try to make the Tribes more varied than they really are. There's a lot of synonyms flying around with only minor connotative differences. If we weren't expecting Auspices to also be a big deal to character creation (and we are, because Justin has said they're going to be significant character aspects), it might not be so bad. But right now it seems way too crowded to help differentiate the Tribes instead of clarifying their narrative roles.

                                                I think part of the problem is that these are rarely used in promotional material. They're usually something you find in things like quick summaries for char-gen in the book. They're not really useful in a vacuum of not having the rest of the book to actually reference what they're talking about.

                                                Also different people are going to find different presentations of material useful. The nice thing about short concise approaches is that they can do a few of them in the hopes of one clicking even if another doesn't. Longer explanations are more of a "putting your eggs in one basket" approach where if the reader doesn't get it, you haven't left yourself as much chances to explain it a different way.

                                                That all said, I don't think the W5 chart is a good example of why this can be effective, because there's just too much overlap in the words picked to really do the job. Even times where there isn't as much overlap as the Ghost Council and Silent Striders, there's lots of examples of verbs where it's entirely unclear what the difference is really supposed to be. What does a Black Fury "demolishing" look like that's different from a Red Talon "ruining" in any sort of informative way? The way the verbs feel like such a stretch to not repeat words despite lots of repeats of the same ideas, is confusing.

                                                        I'm not sure how well you can apply that to people that have real, present, supernatural patrons binding them together. Every Bone Gnawer in W5 is going to have a personal connection to Rat, that comes with both a favor mechanical bonus, and a ban mechanical penalty they live under. There isn't any way to compare that to real world social trends because...no religion on real Earth can prove such entities exist like that.

                                                        I'd also wager you're not taking into account other factors that can keep a community together. The fact that permissive forms of Christianity decline more in active membership than restrictive ones only really applies to societies where Christianity is a dominant pat of cultural norms. Minority religions don't see the same patterns because their status as a minority religion imparts the belonging the binds people together. The impositions of the majority's culture on your life as a minority is already a strong social pressure that replaces the need for the strong reinforcement of social taboos to keep people feeling like they are strongly bonded together.

                                                        The Garou are not a majority belief system, nor are werewolves a "normal" part of society. The word "Tribe" might be extremely inaccurate and only used as a legacy term (that applies to Forsaken fairly strongly as well), but the Garou don't need to be exceedingly cult-like (in the common use of personality cults, as opposed to things like mystery cults) to maintain social bonds at the "Tribe" level. That comes naturally from how isolated they are from most of the world, and how unique their belief structures already are. The most permissive Child of Gaia is still vastly far removed from the daily lives of everyday humans, and thus is going to want to be with Garou that actually understand them.
