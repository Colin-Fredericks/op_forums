            In regards to the Legendary Rites, there are these sidebars where Dominique "Death-In-Word" Omar (the Silver Fang Ragabash) and Little Sister (the Children of Gaia Philodox) talk with each other about these Rites. And it's made clear that Dominique isn't trying to portray historical truths in those Legendary Rites. For example, in the section for "The Quest for the Silver Crown", the sidebar says:

            Queen Morningkill
            Dominique “Death-in-Word” Omar: We’ll need someone for the role of Queen Morningkill. Who wants to get assassinated?
            Little Sister: Wait, ’Queen Morningkill’? I thought it was King Morningkill, Albrecht’s father, who was killed?
            Dominique “Death-in-Word” Omar: Who’s running this Rite? You or me?
            Little Sister: You.
            Dominique “Death-in-Word” Omar: That’s right. If I make her into a queen, she’ll be a queen.
            And you have another one of those for "The Last Battle":

            Riding the Wyrm
            Little Sister: Wow, the Wyrm was there? Like, in person?
            Dominique “Death-in-Word” Omar: Sure, of course. King Albrecht defeated it, and we all lived happily ever after.
            Little Sister: But—
            Dominique “Death-in-Word” Omar: If you ever become the topic of a Legendary Rite, I’ll make sure you’ll be riding a dragon and waving a sword twice your size.
            Little Sister: I don’t think I’d ever be the subject of a story like that.
            Dominique “Death-in-Word” Omar: That’s not typically for you to decide. These Rites are created by those who come after.​
            And there's a discussion about "making changes" on pg. 106 where it says "You can remind the players that their characters change the Rite if they so choose. They can shift the story or adjust their roles, but these are in-game choices. Storyteller characters know the Rite as it’s usually performed and notice adjustments. Whether they like them or not depend on how well the players’ characters pull them off. If the character playing the villain throws the fight and loses on purpose, that’s generally considered inauspicious. What’s the point of winning a fight when there’s no resistance?​" So, changes like genderswapping or changing the location make sense to me. For example, you genderswap a role depending who's performing what role in the Legendary Rite. Or the Legendary Rite is performed at a Sept in Alaska but the location of the story is the Sahara Desert. And so the location gets switched to some arctic ice-wasteland. Or the Legendary Rite tells a story from over two hundred years ago but you decide to modernize the references and tell the story as if it had happened last year.

            I wonder how others interpret this but the sidebar for the "Last Battle"-Legendary Rite made me think, though, that it's implied that the source for this Legendary Rite was Dominique simply making shit up. And I think there should be limits to how much of a Legendary Rite can be just fiction invented by a participant. For example, it should be against Garou society norms to do the "Quest for the Silver Crown" Legendary Rite and add a bit about how Albrecht got rejected by the Silver Crown and so he secretly made a fake-replica of the Silver Crown and proclaimed himself to be king anyway. There should be huge repercussions for doing something like that, both in terms of Renown and how both Garou and Spirits interact with a Garou who did something like that. In the same way, you shouldn't be able to do a Legendary Rite, take a well-known Garou-hero of the past and claim that they actually sided with the Wyrm during a big battle.

            A Legendary Rite should be more than just doing an avant-garde local theater production at a Sept and it shouldn't be considered an opportunity to write fan-fiction about the past. It's a Rite after all.

                                My thoughts on Legendary Rites:
                                • At one point, it talks about Sept-politics in the sense that Garou with one point of view took on the role of the good guys and the Garou who opposed their perspective then chose to take on the role of the villain. So on one level, you're playing out a story but on another level, this is a way to hash out differences in a Sept. Legendary Rites could be interesting in that regard.
                                • It's also mentioned that playing the roles in the Legendary Rite attracts the attention of Spirits. For example, in the "Last Battle"-example, it says "This is a tragedy where we know the hero will fail. Most characters will be playing Wyrm entities or Garou possessed by them, but the magic of the Legendary Rite protects them from actually being possessed by Banes. At least, it should." I think you could go further by having Spirits help Garou play their respective roles. And this includes Bane-Spirits helping the ones who play villainous roles. The book doesn't go too deep into what this exactly means but I think this would also add some nuance to this whole thing. The role a Garou is playing isn't just there for individualistic artistic expression. And when you're playing a Black Spiral Dancer in a Legendary Rite, as far as the Spirits are concerned, you are a Black Spiral Dancer for the duration of this Rite. I think there's lots to explore there when you look at Legendary Rites as these mystical affairs that attract the attention of all sorts of Spirits.
                                Where the book falters a bit:
                                • If we imagine that the average table has an ST and three to four players, it's strange that three of the four example Legendary Rites have five named roles attached to them. And in those three cases, it's also five named characters plus indistinct groups of background-actors that should also be there like "Albrecht's packmates" or "Black Spiral Dancers". That's why I thought of LARP. Once you're juggling those types of numbers, a LARP would be the perfect setting for something like these Legendary Rites. It's just that the book offers little in terms of support for the scenario where it is just the ST and three players. Sure, you could get the players to take on multiple roles, for example, but that's then yet another hurdle to get buy-in from the players to play out these Legendary Rites.
                                • This is also the same book that doubles down on Garou not knowing a lot about their history. So, that kneecaps the whole idea if part of the setting is supposed to be that Garou know very little about their past and the 80s and 90s are already "ancient history" as far as the Garou are concerned.
                                • The examples also lack variety in that they're all about a specific named Garou doing something. They are all retellings of a particular Garou's exploits. There's a lot more variety in what sorts of stories you could tell. Myths, legends... that sort of thing with more archetypical roles rather than specific named Garou, for example.
