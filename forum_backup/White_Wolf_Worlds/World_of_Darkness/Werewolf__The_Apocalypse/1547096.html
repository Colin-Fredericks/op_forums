    [W5 House Rules] Gnosis (constructive feedback appreciated)

    12-17-2024, 03:57 AM
    While revising my house rules in accordance with the information and rules in Shattered Nation, I thought about reintroducing the concept of Gnosis into W5. Since I didn't want to completely revise the Gifts system and didn't need a new resource stat, I decided to take the route of Blood Potency in V5. That is, a meta-stat whose rating influences a number of mechanics. The advantage of that is that it allows me to move some of the other minor tweaks in the house rules into this stat, allowing for a wider range of characters.

    This is my first draft, and I would appreciate any feedback you might have.

    GNOSIS
    Some call Gnosis the essence of the Spirit Wilds. It is the expression of the Garou’s half-spirit nature, enabling them to connect with the spirits that surround them. Without this vital connection, the Garou would be cut off from half of their very being. Characters with weak Gnosis may struggle to travel into the Umbra or communicate with spirits. Conversely, those with exceptionally strong Gnosis may feel more spirit than flesh, blurring the distinction between the physical world and the Spirit Wilds. Such Garou often find themselves drawn to the mysterious and inscrutable logic of the Umbra.

    Gnosis is measured on a scale from • to •••••. New player characters begin with a Gnosis rating of ••.

    A Garou’s Gnosis rating automatically increases by one for every full year they spend in the Spirit Wilds. Conversely, for every year a werewolf spends having lost the wolf, their Gnosis rating decreases by one. Outside of these circumstances, players can increase their character’s Gnosis rating by spending experience points.

    SPIRIT BANS
    As a Garou’s Gnosis grows, they become increasingly spirit-like—idealized and archetypal. This evolution causes their very essence to manifest more strongly in their thoughts and actions, compelling them to follow a personal inner logic. This phenomenon is known as a Spirit Ban. Spirit Bans are deeply tied to a Garou’s Convictions, reflecting the behaviors that become fundamentally intolerable to them. To outsiders, werewolves with Spirit Bans often resemble heroes of ancient epics—larger than life and governed by strange, unwritten rules or whims.

    Once a Garou reaches a Gnosis rating of ••• or higher, they develop one or two Spirit Bans (see the following table). These bans always reflect a character’s Convictions. To create a Spirit Ban, consider what drives the character—what compels their actions—and then define an inverse of those behaviors. For example, a Black Fury activist who tirelessly protects survivors of domestic violence might develop Spirit Bans such as “Allowing perpetrators of domestic violence to go unpunished” or “Refusing help for the cause—regardless of its source.”

    When a character actively transgresses against their Spirit Ban, they suffer a -2 dice penalty to all dice pools. At Gnosis •••••, Spirit Bans become so powerful that the character must spend a Willpower point to knowingly act against them.

    Spirit Bans should be neither so narrow as to rarely apply in gameplay, nor so broad as to permanently impair a character’s agency. Players should work closely with their Storyteller to define Spirit Bans that strike the right balance for meaningful and engaging roleplay.

          While I understand the attachment to the original game, this seems a good time to pick a much better word for what the trait does than "Gnosis." It really isn't a word that helps communicate the concepts of what Gnosis does, and it only makes the game better to use something more indicative of it's function.

          That said, what stands out to me as worth keeping an eye on:

          1) The thing that I first noticed since it could impact character death is that since you can lose Gnosis, what happens if you're too old? If my PC has lived 30 years beyond their natural lifespan thanks to high Gnosis, and they drop to Gnosis 3... do they die? If not, what does happen, and how does that interact with gaining and losing Gnosis later?

          2) When you say they have to spend a full year in the Umbra to gain Gnosis, is that a full year without breaks (something you basically can't survive before Gnosis 5) or cumulative? If cumulative, how do you track it since most play is going to happen in narrative time (aka "a scene")? The same applies to losing Gnosis via Losing the Wolf.

          3) The penalties towards exiting the Umbra and towards perception checks in the material world seem.... iffy for something that takes that long to raise (or XP). The Spirit Bans are already a significant downside to consider before gunning your XP to get Gnosis 5, so it doesn't feel like a few additional penalties add much. They also feel very Forsaken instead of Apocalypse. In Forsaken, that happens when you've become imbalanced towards the spirit half of your nature. Getting more in-touch with your spirit side ala Apocalypse doesn't really make these penalties make much sense. If you still feel some additional penalty is warranted, you could consider a social penalty towards dealing with beings not attuned to the Umbra as the Garou's increased connections gives them a serious "weird vibe" from reacting to things most people can't even being to perceive.

              Thanks, these are excellent questions!

                  Essence is always an option as a different name, even if it's not the most original one ever.

                  1) Let me rephrase the question a bit: Should the PC have a chance to regain sufficient Gnosis to survive? If so, how difficult should it be? Also if so, do that revert the accelerated aging, or simply live longer from their new normal state?

                  2) If PCs start at 2, they're still taking Agg, which makes getting to 3 impossible without going the XP route (or using a specific Loresheet, but I was taking it as a given that one wasn't going to be kept as-is in all this). Beyond that, I feel like you might want to consider a more narrative approach to time. A whole year basically turns into "ST Fiat." Most games don't cover that sort of time frame, or have enough detail in downtime that being in one state the whole time is rarely going to happen unless the players and ST agree that the goal of the downtime is adjusting the rating.

                  If you want to go a more story oriented route, I'd suggest something like you need to have [new rating] scenes that result in a deeper bond between the PC and a powerful spirit that's deeply important to their motivations (thematically this also helps link things like spirit bans to the rest): Tribal Patron, Pack Patron, or another spirit of particular note to the PC (an ancestor turned spirit even if not quite W5's deal as much as pre-W5 had them around, as an example). Most spirit Touchstones probably shouldn't count, but a higher up spirit of the same type might. Losing would of course mean acting opposite towards how said spirits expect and driving a wedge between flesh and spirit.

                  If you want to do something a bit more mechanical, you could repurpose V5's Humanity system, but have it go in either direction. Furthering Convictions gives you a chance to roll to gain, acting against them gives you a chance to lose, doing both gives you a chance of cancelling out.

                          That is certainly an option. Though the aging modifier is the only one that really causes complex problems with the idea of it going up and down over play beyond XP. Fine tuning the timing of how often it goes up and down on its own is just that, fine tuning.

                          The whole aging thing is also... more of a lore and setting consistency things IME (sometimes there's a "Chronicles of Ages" deal where you do large time-skips too though). When playing the CofD games that have increased lifespans at high power stat levels, it's only really come up for NPCs. A vampire that's been around in the US since early colonial days is nothing special. A bunch of CofD character types can live long enough that a character that old would be a last vestige of that era, but a few still around from centuries ago isn't surprising (even if actually interacting with them would be special). Other splats would have alarm bells going on what exactly someone did to gain that kind of longevity as it's well past their kind's limits even for their most potent members.

                          So there's a lot it can add if you want to put in the work to make it count. One faction trying to kill a powerful Garou by stripping them of their wolf and locking them away sources of regaining it while another faction is trying to free them and preserve all that knowledge and power is a neat story hook to add to play. It's just making sure you code in the stakes to this the way you want them to be.

                                  Ousia

                                  Some call Ousia the essence of the Spirit Wilds. It is the expression of the Garou’s half-spirit nature, enabling them to connect with the spirits that surround them. Without this vital connection, the Garou would be cut off from half of their very being. Characters with weak Ousia may struggle to travel into the Umbra or communicate with spirits. Conversely, those with exceptionally strong Ousia may feel more spirit than flesh, blurring the distinction between the physical world and the Spirit Wilds. Such Garou often find themselves drawn to the mysterious and inscrutable logic of the Umbra.

                                  Ousia is measured on a scale from • to •••••. New player characters begin with a Ousia rating of •. Players can increase their character’s Ousia rating by spending experience points.
                                  As a character’s Ousia increases, it begins to influence several aspects of gameplay in significant ways. For Garou, the Umbra increasingly feels like a natural environment, reducing any damage they might otherwise sustain from prolonged stays there. At higher levels of Ousia, Garou not only perceive and access the Spirit Wilds more easily but can enter them without performing the Rite of Shadow Passage. By simply gazing into a reflective surface and reaching out, they can traverse into the Umbra.

                                  As Garou become more spirit-like, spirits recognize them as equals. This opens up new possibilities, including the potential for spirits to serve as viable Touchstones. Additionally, greater Ousia makes the use of spirit-taught Gifts feel more instinctive, allowing Garou to activate these abilities with increased ease.

                                  A strong Ousia brings benefits beyond the Umbra. Garou with higher Ousia become more adept shapechangers, regenerating wounds more rapidly and channeling their Rage more effectively to shift between forms. Mechanically, this means they can regenerate more than one Superficial Health level per Rage check and are allowed to re-roll Rage dice when transitioning between forms.

                                  Finally, increased Ousia extends a Garou’s natural lifespan. Their spiritual nature slows the biological processes of aging, adding anywhere from 20 to 100 years to their life expectancy. Furthermore, werewolves with exceptionally strong Ousia are more likely to persist as ancestor spirits after death. Their potent spirit-half helps them retain their identity, even after the dissolution of their physical body.

                                  Spirit Bans

                                  As a Garou’s Ousia grows, they become increasingly spirit-like—idealized and archetypal. This evolution causes their very essence to manifest more strongly in their thoughts and actions, compelling them to follow a personal inner logic. This phenomenon is known as a Spirit Ban. Spirit Bans are deeply tied to a Garou’s Convictions, reflecting the behaviors that become fundamentally intolerable to them. To outsiders, werewolves with Spirit Bans often resemble heroes of ancient epics—larger than life and governed by strange, unwritten rules or whims.
                                  Once a Garou reaches a Ousia rating of ••• or higher, they develop one or two Spirit Bans (see the following table). These bans always reflect a character’s Convictions. To create a Spirit Ban, consider what drives the character—what compels their actions—and then define an inverse of those behaviors. For example, a Black Fury activist who tirelessly protects survivors of domestic violence might develop Spirit Bans such as “Allowing perpetrators of domestic violence to go unpunished” or “Refusing help for the cause—regardless of its source.”

                                  When a character actively transgresses against their Spirit Ban, they suffer a -2 dice penalty to all dice pools. At Ousia •••••, Spirit Bans become so powerful that the character must spend a Willpower point to knowingly act against them.

                                  Spirit Bans should be neither so narrow as to rarely apply in gameplay, nor so broad as to permanently impair a character’s agency. Players should work closely with their Storyteller to define Spirit Bans that strike the right balance for meaningful and engaging roleplay.

                                      I think it looks good, certainly viable to start testing with players for more direct feedback.

                                      The only last bit I would consider, is that since Ousia only goes up now, it would be a good idea to consider something for characters that have the Rite of Shadow Passage to get for not needing it anymore.                                                   I actually saw it before your edit anyway

                                                  I do get the concerns about XP sinks, I'm not sure it would be so bad to raise the separately? W5 gives out more XP than V5 does, and W5 Renown is more useful than pre-W5 WtA Renown was (even if not quite as useful as Forsaken 2e where people generally do not mind paying XP for Renown and Primal-Urge as separate traits).

                                                  Unfortunately, it's a hard thing to test because it's fairly subjective and you'd need groups to play multiple options for extended periods to really feel the difference between approaches. I can certainly appreciate some players might not like being forced to get such high ratings in every Renown to get to 4 or 5 Ousia. I guess it might depend a bit on how you handle rounding, but normal operations means you'd need 11 Renown to get Ousia 4... which means 5/3/3 would be a spread for a character focused on one Renown and is just taking the other two as they come.

                                                  I feel like you'd run the risk of Ousia coming in so far along the existing power-scaling of the game (unless STs are really generous with earning Renown via RP) that a lot of groups might struggle to hit 3 before a story wraps up.

                                                  You could crib something from Exalted 3e: In Exalted 3e, Essence, the main stat of how powerful Exalted and spirits/gods are, doesn't automatically go up when you hit the thresholds (it uses XP spent generally instead of spent on specific things), your PC is just eligible once they hit that point and still have to do something in-character to achieve it. You could lower the threshold to Total Renown / 2, but retain the need for some sort of spirit quest (which could take a few tries at low Ousia because of the lower connection with spirits, just be up-front about that). That way you don't need to be a near paragon of Garou Renown to hit the higher levels of Ousia, but you retain the potential for characters that are more focused on the immediate needs of the physical world to take time for their spiritual side having low Ousia despite their Renown, and it's easier to explain how you could in theory have characters that lost Renown but kept high Ousia ratings from before their fall from grace (as Renown is acting more of a gatekeeper than the source of Ousia if that makes sense).

                                                  As I said though, I think you're at "get people actually trying these," because there's only so much folks theory-crafting online can do for you. Some rules look perfect on paper but just don't play right in the wild.
