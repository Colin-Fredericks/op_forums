                        Fellah, I like the cut of your jib: I was actually trying to puzzle out what a pack of friendly rivals to the Eagle’s Nest might look like and had hit on almost exactly the same mix of tribes (Mostly because my working theory - based on WEREWOLF THE WILD WEST - is that the Black Furies, Children of Gaia, Fianna and Uktena, with their absence of background limits at character creation, make the best starting point for a given pack of pregen characters, being infinitely flexible).

                        Now since the Eagle’s Nest already has the fiery Lorna McNab, that meant the Fianna would be off the table, so that meant looking elsewhere for the last two members of the team - ticking off the Great Powers of the era already represented by the Eagle’s Nest left me with the USSR, Japan, Germany, Italy and France so I assumed that these friendly rivals would represent the elements in those nations seeking to avoid a Second Great War.

                        Oddly enough, though, my notion was that the resident Silver Fang might have been one of those claimants to the dignities of House Conquering Claw that pop up from time to time (Without winning much recognition): I have to say that your notion of ‘Grand Duchess Anastasia’ going through First Change and forming her own pack is far, far more delightfully Pulp.


                        For the record, I’m assuming the pack breakdown is something like:-

                        - Ragabash (Uktena, Lupus): Born to one of the last litters of Japanese wolves and adopted by Elder Brother under mysterious circumstances (It’s possible they were originally Hakken but shifted tribes for reasons they do not care to share).

                        Owes somebody a favour and is therefore acting as minder to this pack of reckless idealists: also keeps running with this pack because they keep getting into all the most interesting fights.


                        - Theurge: ‘Grand Duchess Anastasia Nikolaevna’ (Silver Fang, Homid), busily trying to avert a Second Great War, secure recognition of her royal dignities by the Tribe and make a clean sweep of the various supernatural jack***** claiming to be Rasputin (Who deserved better).

                        Currently operating out of Paris, given Russia isn’t likely to extend a warm welcome and she doesn’t actually want to trigger the Second Great War.


                        NOTE: A little research suggests that the historic Anastasia (born 18th June 1901 New Style) was born a few days after a new moon (15th June of that year), so it’s possible she could be the Ragabash if I’ve misunderstood how lunar phases work).


                        - Philodox (Get of Fenris, Lupus): An adopted German who pays rather less attention to mortal politics than they should and who remains fairly confident the Tribe can handle these ‘National Socialists’ if they get out hand (and/or fail to deliver on all these impressively progressive notions of wildlife preservation)..

                        Equally overconfident in their Tribe’s ability to handle the lunatic fringe currently growing into a maniac mullet and outright bonkers sideburns..


                        - Galliard (Bone Gnawer, Homid): Almost certainly a part-time spy for the Soviets, certainly a part-time Communist, actually an Italian. Currently having the time of his life pointing out all the holes in the aggressively-noble thinking of their pack (and singing their lungs out in support of No More Great Wars).


                        - Ahroun (Child of Gaia, ????): Struggling for World Peace since finding inner peace is currently beyond them.


                        Please don’t take this as a final version of the Pack (‘New World’ Pack? ‘Luna’s Glory’ Pack? ‘Empty Hand’ Pack?): it’s more of an initial sketch inspired by a shared notion.

                        I’m also not certain what the pack totem should be (Though it occurs to me that Zarok might be an interesting pick - if only because Zarok, with his obsessive interest in Honour, provides a complication as much as a blessing* - one assumes that the Eagle’s Nest pack followed Eagle, so having their friendly rivals follow the Planetary Incarna of Jupiter would be symbolically apt).


                        *Given Zarok’s apparent fondness for Silver Fangs and his association with the planet (and presumably the deity) Jupiter, one sometimes wonders if he might have been the Totem of House Conquering Claw: a suitably larger-than-life patron spirit for the Silver Fangs of the Roman Empire, if nothing else.

                            I will mention that all of the Eagle's Nest pack were based on period Pulp archetypes. Vic Stryker was a mix of hard boiled detective and globe trotting adventurer. Dr. Stratus was both aviator and scientist. McNab was the two-fisted brawler and spitfire. Greta Cole the femme fatal and "gentleman" thief. An Lei Sheng the Wu Shu novel hero. I always presumed the others would follow suite with Anastasia being inspired by the stage play (and the animated film inspired by it). The Black Fury likely a Freebooter archeologist, the Bone Gnawer some sort of Mexican "hero of the common people" revolutionary bandit, the Red Talon a Jack London character, the Uktena heavily based in period African American culture, and so on.

                                            I tend to like those NPCs in the Rage Books that inspire me to include similar concepts in my own chronicles.

                                            Grand Prize goes to Bone Juice in Rage Across New York. He's the teenage runaway Bone Gnawer Ahroun who eats other kid runaways by using his milk carton with picture of a missing child fetish to find them. He's a great low level villain. Perfect for the PCs. And a complete distraction to all the important stuff going on in RANY. He really inspired me to make good use of the Maneaters camp of the Bone Gnawers.

                                            Second Prize is Guttooth, the Shadow Lord lupus theurge who is the Prophet of Gaia in Dark Alliance. I really like how that sourcebook says it is up to the ST to decide if Guttooth is secretly of the Wyrm, the actual Chosen of Gaia, or something else. Anything that forces players to take a stand and define their characters is a good thing. And because so many players are hesitant to do so UNLESS they know what is the "correct" thing to do, by letting the ST make their own choice a PC cannot metagame it. My favorite Garou NPC I created was for LARP that I made Untrustworthy x3 and told everyone up front OOC that he was. Needless to say I fucked over some people, but not everyone. So the PCs quickly split between people who thought he was just a dick and hated him, to those that thought he was doing good things by whatever means available, to those who thought him misguided or flawed, or those who just wanted to avoid him. But everyone had their own opinion.

                                            Third Prize is Darius Winchester, Silver Fang king of Australia. Despite coming out before the Silver Fangs tribebook, that sourcebook completely ignores the Winchester family or their kingship of Australia. That alone would probably make me like him. But even better, he's a great mix of good and bad. He's an inbred stutterer already going bald in his thirties. He's also fallen in love (and possibly blood bond) to a Gangrel vampire he met in the woods before she revealed her true nature to him. But he's also a morally principled man who hopes to restore his tribe, his family, and the Garou back to honor by assuaging the anger of the Bunyip, putting their spirits to rest, and atoning for the wrong the Garou did. So I find him an interesting, complex character. An ST can do many things with him, and I imagine the PCs can react in many ways to him depending on how the ST portrays him.

                                            Runner Up: Ruben Bruckner, the gentle Quaker Pennsylvania Dutch Get of Fenris Ahroun of Rage Across Appalachia. It's a great local concept for a chronicle set in Appalachia. The Quaker Get of Fenris is both totally justified, yet unexpected as a result of too much attention on the game rules instead of real life history. Most games need more of these kinds of concepts.
