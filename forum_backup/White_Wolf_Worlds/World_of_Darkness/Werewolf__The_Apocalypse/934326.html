    Complex Litany problems

    07-20-2016, 03:07 AM
    I am thinking of creating a blind weak willed Philodox Metis to tag along to a PC pack, so they have some indication if what they do is against the litany. So to impress them i came up with several "scenarios" he can cite like important case to show off his prowess.
    Things like the status of "war" is generally declared on sept level and why a pack alpha can not declare it and its implications or who is able to change the litany? What about the Child of Gaia who are not really following "Do Not Suffer Thy People to Tend Thy Sickness".

    So, what where your most complicated problems that you guys encountered with the Litany? When did you guys felt like lawyers and tried to interpret every word that was written?

              Most common problems
              1. It is meant to be a compilation of songs and howls and tales, but it is only a list of some one-liners.
              2. It is meant to be intuitive, but players, and homid characters, tend to get caught up on the words instead of the meaning.
              3. It is meant to be the part of the local laws that are equal to (almost) all septs and tribes, not the only laws there are.


              That said, I would see a lot of Get of Fenris don't give a shit if "war" was declared or not. It depends on the consensus of the society whether or not they are at war. I can see a pack on a mission in a planning phase and in an execution phase of their mission. During one it is okay to challenge the alpha, of suggestions or simply laying out a better play doesn't work, during the latter it is not acceptable. There is no need to declare it. Same goes for septs. There might be some grey area, when the plan is under way, but the fighting hasn't started yet.

              For a Philodox or Galliard, it should be easy to come up with three to five local stories that illustrate each point of the litany. But, since White Wolf didn't provide any, you have to come up with them yourself. That is a lot of work.

              It was I think Genghis Khan who said, that the spirit of a law is important, not the words. Especially with the "mate", "war", "sickness" words, the meaning is different to everyone. Still, this are sentences, that try to convey something a lupus can understand in the English language. And how messy this is, you can see if someone tries to translate the Litany into other human languages. The Litany isn't originally in modern English, is it. So, just because you are the biggest guy at the railway terminal, you shouldn't get the first stock the guy your murdered ever bought.

              --------


              Tend sickness
              • a story could tell of a sept who starved over the winter and was taken over by Get of Fenris, because they spend too much of the resources for their old.
              • another story could tell of a leader who became sick, but was mentally still fit. So he was tended to, and when he died he still hadn't taught his skills to a worthy successor.
              • the most basic aspect would be, that the pack couldn't hunt for the sick one was holding them back.

              War Time
              • A story where there was no plan, as the pack/sept was attacked, and while the leaders commands were reckless they would have been sufficient to throw back the attack, but due to the interference of would-be-leaders the whole thing went south.
              • A story where the commands of the leader were seemingly stupid, for he had compartmentalised the information about backup and such. Arguing or even a challenge leads to the window of opportunity to enact the plan closing.
              • A story where a younger challenger was better at combat and beat the leader in a challenge to become the new leader, but lacked the support of neighbouring septs - or started a whole number of subsequent challenges till the danger was at the gates.


              For each story, any good Ragabash should have a story where defying a tenet of the litany actually brought about a good outcome. Just so he can test the conviction of his peers.
