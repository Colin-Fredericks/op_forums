    Using Sam Haight

    10-17-2017, 10:28 PM
    Alright, so....I'm deep diving old Werewolf books to build a new chronicle from and...I like the basic concept of Sam Haight.

    Can anyone give me some suggestions on using Haight without letting the ridiculous stuff he got into later leak in? Because at some point down the line, I'd like to put these same players through the Skinner SAS and I'd like the players to have some history with Haight

      Haight first appeared in the early supplement Valkenburg Foundation where one of the scenarios was him hunting Lunatics in the woods, specifically lupus Lunatics who couldn't change but were otherwise thinking sane. He killed them because they were easy prey - there were Garou for purposes of the ritual and had Rage and Gnosis, but were otherwise underpowered - no Gifts or Rites and couldn't assume other forms. His escape power was a Black Spiral Dancer fetish which allowed him to reform in the Umbra like a spirit in slumber (in some ares protected by the Wyrm).

      All the weird crossover stuff happened after that.

      If you want to use Haight, I suggest you run some kind of plot similar to that to begin with. That introduces him and his threat. Then after it's done, you can name drop him in whatever other plots you'd like to run. Since he is a kinfolk and has knowledge of the Garou, he'd be a good ally for many other bad guys as he could give them proper intelligence about their enemies (the PCs). That will keep the PCs after him and view him as an ongoing threat. Then you can run the Skinner to end that long story plot.

      Another option is prior to having him slay some Garou (who are for whatever reasons easy prey) is to include him as an element in the PCs or home sept backstory. So they are aware of the name, but only know him as a kinfolk ally or friend. They don't know he is harboring these dark thoughts. They only know he disappeared a while ago (to explain his adventures where he learns Wyrm lore and other things so he can return to perform the Rite of Sacred Rebirth).

          I use him right now in my chronicle. In some way.

          1. I don't like to use the meta plot in any way outside the ground structure of the setting I play. The players mostly don't know the meta plot so I can slightly use it by refering to it, without taking it as a whole storyline.

          3. I think the power level of the character makes it unuseable in any direct way.

          He sent an NPC - which is directly interacting with the PCs - to retrieve a diary H. used to document his research on the Rite of Sacred Rebirth.

                          Height is - or at least was as originally conceived - a sort of boogieman/morality tale for the Garou. "Be nice to your Kin, or they could turn into the Skinner." If used well, he becomes a window into Garou/Kinfolk relations, issues of emotional abuse and ostracization (something that should resonate with almost all Metis as well as a lot of Homids), and the idea of the hunters becoming the hunted. The main reason he's so dangerous is because he knows all of the Garou's weaknesses and is such a skilled hunter that knows how to stalk, trap and kill them. All the shapeshifting and wizardry from later is just window dressing. At the core is this uber-predator who deep down inside just wants to dominate and earn the fear of the people who abused, degraded and bullied him as a child and young man. Specifically, his father, who died in a freak accident when Height was a teenager, thus robbing him of any sort of closure. Essentially a serial killer whose target of choice happens to be werewolves. (He's basically Buffalo Bill from Silence of the Lambs, with magic that will make his "woman suit" really turn him into a woman; Most of the psychological insights Dr. Lector gives about Bill applied to Height as well.)

                              Keep in mind also that while Haight is gone, the Skin Dancers are not. If you don't want the overpowered aspects of Haight in the chronicle, you can just as easily make the antagonist some random Kin who starts to drift down the Skin Dancer path. That was always really the scary part of what Haight represented; The Garou were dependent upon their Kin for all sorts of stuff, but there was always a gulf between them -- and the inherent risk that people who knew so much about them (and their strengths and weaknesses) would turn on them as a result.
