        The Red Talons are perhaps the smallest Tribe of the Garou Nation and the reason they are so often limited in things like Online venues is because of that whole Anti-Human stance and the problem of dealing with the Urban world that doesn't threaten Frenzy tests.

        They should totally be a voice in the Nation when presented in a Tabletop game, but they are a fading failing voice due to the kinfolk dying out etc. But they are not a dead voice and they should really be why places that are Wild are still feared by man in the World of Darkness. The problem is that too many people see RedTalon and go "Rarrrg Kill Monkies". See that's wrong, because the Talon's are intelligent, they're not Human educated by they are Garou educated. They place a greater value on instinct but Wolves are really clever predators and Garou leave wolves standing. Thing is the Talon's HATE humanity and it's justified, but they're not unthinking creatures fuelled just by Rage (well some of the Ahrouns might be, but that's the same across the board). The Talons hold firmly to the line of the Wild and think nothing of slaughtering any human they find outside the safety of their cities, they want Mankind to be afraid of the Wild places, of the dark of night, of the howl on the wind and they can certainly reason as well as any Glasswalker or Shadowlord into making those things happen. The RedTalons are very much all the terrifying parts that fill the human psyche on Monster Stories or classical werewolf tales and movies but amplified by the fact that they are totally sentient creatures.

        For those who find the Wild places "boring" or "lacking focus" I well recommend the Book of the Wyld and the Monster segments of the Book of the Wyrm 2nd edit (I dare say Wyrm20 will also have really handy stuff). Also Rage Across the Heavens and Book of the Umbra, because these are All places where the RedTalons are just as at home as any other Garou and in some places even more so (obviously umbral realms will vary) and with the exception of a few Umbral Realms are all well outside any of the concepts of the City. Also take a peek at the Werewolf Dark Ages books as they deal with a lot of wild too.

                    In all honesty it's a rather Wyrmy world view but entirely applicable in the original sense of the 'Wyrm'. The Impergium went too far but it was only at that point the Celestines stepped in, the early years of the Impergium was fine (infact it's exactly the thing the Ratkin exist for). Also the Tribe view of the RedTalons isn't just "Kill all humans", they are equally deep preservers of the wilderness and champions of the Wyld, they are the most primeval of mystics and deeply in tune with the instinctual rhythms of existence. As a tribe they're also isolationists, not always the best option for a character to massively embrace but can add a ton of mystique as NPCs or a plot point in a campaign. Thing is we make them one dimensional by only focussing on the anti human thing.

                    Something I do when introducing new people to WTA or giving novice players a look on tribes they haven't really looked at is by attaching not one but three "Descriptors" about the generic tribe overview. For example...

                    Black Furies: "Amazon Heroines" "Wise Women & Seers" "Den Mothers, Pack Sisters, Protectors of the Family"
                    Fianna: "Fury of Legend" "Songs & Howls of the Ancients" "Mysterious Magic"
                    Bone Gnawers: "Tenacious Warriors" "Streetwise Infiltrators" "Pragamtic Idealists"

                    For RedTalons I go with
                    "Embittered Survivors" "Defenders of the 'Lost, Forgotten and Unseen' world" "Primeval Mystics"

                                    Originally posted by Moirdryd View Post
                                    Which spurs me to this... What three Descriptors would you (the collective you of the Forum users, not just Heavy Arms) use to describe the RedTalons?
                                    Here goes;

                                    Uncomplicated- To a Red Talon, a lot of things are simple. A garou needs a pack, a garou needs to do their auspice duty, fight the Wyrm and protect the Caern. And they do not complicate things with over-thinking, if you need to pack you will pack regardless of your personal opinion. If you need to protect a Caern, you will do so, even if it means dying.

                                    Voice of the Wilderness- Not Wyld, for Wyld is not nature same way Weaver is not the city. Red Talons are creatures of the animal kingdom, as Black Fox suggested above. This means they often voice not just their own opinions, but the opinions of the prey and the land itself.

                                    Unchanging- I always like to point out the bad side of a tribe, and beyond the human-killing, I think this is the Red Talons' worst aspect. They hate the Weaver and the way cities are with humans. Glass Walkers are their opposite and so they are viewned in a bad light. But, yet, Walkers adapt and change, Talons have stuck to the same way for centuries. Nature is never static, it is never unchanging, yet the Talons stick to their ways none the less. Is this a good thing? Or is a flaw?

                                            Just a reminder that it is the Lodge of the Predator Kings which is the camp who wants to exterminate mankind. That is one camp; and they are obviously meant as antagonists. The majority of the tribe, the Warders of the Land, won't hesitate to kill a human who is a threat, but don't intend to fight a war against humanity or kill everyone. Then of course the so-called Whelp's Compromise actually believe there is some purpose or value to mankind, otherwise why would Gaia have made them? The idea that Red Talons all want to kill humans is no more accurate than stating all Black Furies are lesbian man-killers. Those are very obvious hooks, and it is not a surprise many STs and Players grab onto that first either as a stereotype or how they introduce the first NPC of the tribe, but it doesn't accurately describe the tribe.

                                            If anything, the primary emotion of the Red Talons towards Man is not hatred, but fear. They don't understand humanity, and don't understand how after 10,000 years of small numbers clad in leather using bone weapons, humanity has become this huge threat with metal claws and stings possessing cities and huge numbers in only two to four thousand years.

                                            I think there is a lot to explore with the tribe.

                                                    I disagree that Red Talons have no place in a city game, though it is true that it puts you in a situation of many many rage checks. As an ST - I usually involve a lot of rage checks in my game, which balances it out. I don't like it when my players forget that they are, in fact, biting back at the urge to go full spree killer on their home town. However - to the point:

                                                    Search some of the popular World of Darkness locations on Google Maps. (Los Angeles, Seattle, etc.) Being from the east coast of the US, I'm used to a very crowded human world. Out in the American West, though... once you leave those city borders, it's not very far before you find there's a whole lot of nothing. Even Los Angeles can look like a pretty small bastion of safety in the dark wilderness that borders it. In my games Red Talons are the reason why city people look out into the dark wilderness with a shiver of fear. They are the alpha predators. They howl and bark at the edges of humanity, and still inspire the primordial fear that the delirium represents. If you're looking for a few examples of how you can use them in games without having to fall to the old "kill all humans" stereotype, I can offer a few of mine to ponder as you please.

                                                    First, I often use them in tangent with the Ratkin. As a previous poster mentioned, Gaia specifically created the ratkin to cull humanity. The Garou kind of just helped. In this set up, the Ratkin usually handle the inner city work and inform the Red Talons of high value human targets leaving their domain. If you like to play more hateful with your fera, the corax are usually more than happy to facilitate these sorts of collaborations (for suitable pay).

                                                    My Red Talon NPC, which I left open as a mentor for anyone who felt up to the task of going full Lupus, was named Prowls-the-Starless-Night. He was the Keeper of the Land for the local Sept, and had a habit of mercilessly tearing apart anything that upset his balance... or anyone who questioned his method. Just the same though, we're talking a Warder here. Not interested in the fire and death approach to culling humanity. I don't think a Get can play the big bad wolf like a Red Talon can. Get are warriors and perfect the art of battle. Red Talons are predators, and perfect the art of being more cunning, more stealthy and all-together more terrifying.

                                                    If you're looking at Red Talons from a human perspective, you should be affraid of them just deciding you need to die today. If you want a loyal friend - go find a Bone Gnawer. These are Red Talons, they're only interested in the Darwin-esque laws of nature.

                                                    Some of these thoughts are only half formed. Sorry, I'm in a rush to finish this post and get out the door. I hope some of this was interesting to think about.
