Or is that something that the Ratkin began on their own after the Garou started the Impergium but before the War of Rage started? Not to mention the added benefit of not having to resort to mass-murder to keep the humans' numbers down. So yes, you are entirely correct that the Garou screwed up by interfering with the Ratkin's job.

    Not sure about the other Fera being meant to cull humanity, though. As Gaia's "immune system," the Garou are basically meant by gaia to destroy Her enemies (ideally, that wouldn't include humans since humans, as natural creatures, are Gaia's children too). But aside from the Ratkin, did any of the other Fera have a purpose involving humanity? The Corax and Camazotz are (and/or were) Her messengers, the Bastet are Her intelligence operatives, the Rokea cleanse the seas and endure, the Mokole are Her memory, the Gurahl and Grondr are her healers and cleansers, the Apis were her matchmakers for the rest of the Fera, the Ananasi belong to Queen Ananasa rather than Gaia, the Kitsune are a programming patch to help the rest of the Hengeyokai, the Nagah cull traitors to Gaia among the Fera rather than humans... Sure the other Fera cause the Delirium to an equal or lesser extent as the Garou, but I always interpreted that as the Fera having a similar "primal imprint" to the Garou upon a human seeing a non-Garou's Crinos form. Everything I've read in W:tA suggests to me that the Impergium was a Garou-only thing. And I don't count the Ratkin's eating of human crops as part of the Impergium because that doesn't involve mass murder nor any species-wide psychological damage that would be inflicted by being subjected to 3,000 years of exposure to such.

They question is whether the substance is spiritually silver, which should be answered in terms of rather a reasonable layperson would consider a substance to be silver. A silver based compound (like silver halide film, silver nitrate, colloidal silver, etc.) should never count as true silver.
                                                            It's a better starting point than "I don't really know how much." None of those compounds would likely be admitted anyway, since the reference point in the book was to sterling silver, which is 92.5% silver by mass, determined most often by measuring weight. Silver halide, even if you were trying to squirm around the spiritual nature of things, is far less than 92.5% silver by weight, as are silver halides and most every form of silver colloidal compound. At that point, adding on "no compounds, ions, or the like" is just icing on the cake.

                                                            Size of the object probably also figures into it, but that's an ill-explored level of detail. I'd imagine that it'd be really important for Mokole, since they can get really, really huge, and having a single, comparatively tiny silver or gold bullet ruin your kaijuu rampage would be startlingly anticlimactic for any game. A shell would be more reasonable, but it wouldn't be dealing out that damage to a 98-meter tall monster that it does to a merely 7.6-meter long tank.
