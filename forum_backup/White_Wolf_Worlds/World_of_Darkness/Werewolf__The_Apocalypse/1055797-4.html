                Originally posted by dxanders View Post

                They wouldn't NECESSARILY follow human cultural trends (and if that's not the route you'd want to take, it's perfectly valid), but I see a couple of reasons why it would make sense to take that route, and why it's a direction I personally prefer.

                From a lore perspective, it makes sense. These are creatures who live half in the world of man and half in the spiritual. Who they were before their first change informs who they become. The attitudes of their kinfolk and the other people they see in their daily lives influences their decisions, and the political and social attitudes of the mortal climate creates opportunities.
                Then they get broken down worse than boot camp and are faced with the hard reality that their beliefs have been extra-wrong. And the tribe isn't necessarily going to support them in this internal struggle, because they need to be on the page where they're giving proper chiminage to the caern's spirits and not wasting time tooling around with white nationalists.

                In more meta terms, I like my games to be a reflection of the reality I know, and a way of exploring that reality in interesting ways. Especially in modern games, I look for concepts that reflect my lived reality, warts and all, and I think looking for those kinds of patterns is an inherently human thing, and a core virtue of storytelling in general. If there's a tribe of fiercely female warrior women, I see fertile ground of allegory for the feminist struggles I see in the groups I run in. When I see the Get, I see a reflection of military warrior culture and the reflectively hostile insularity of much of modern far right conservatism (and admittedly, my opinion is probably the outlier here, and I'm not expecting the writers to create a game tailored to my preferences).
                Why would the Get be part of the American right? They're incredibly likely to cultivate their Rage high enough to scare off most humans that approach them. They live for war fought hard with hammers, axes, and claws, and venerate a wolf with jaws large enough to reach the heavens when they open, the enemy of the Northfolk's All-Father. By all rights, they should be recruiting any and all new blood from the steelier members of the SCA or from driven athletes from all walks of life.

                For me, at least, Werewolf is very much a game about the safety of the past: the threat of a mythical history that obfuscates the troubles that past represented, and the inability to achieve that in the face of an uncertain and frightening future. It's not the major theme for me (that would likely be the dual natured romance and dangers of radical ideology and the questions of moral righteousness when faced with an unbeatable threat), but it's prominent; and, as much as I abhor and pity the rising prominence of vaguely fascist nationalism, I think there's a stark metaphor in those people's apocalyptic view of the future, a future where they see their values, or religion, or racial identity, or whatever, threatened by existential genocide.
                And, in turn, the Garou are so old that they, oddly enough, wouldn't buy into the human ideologies on account of their disdain for humanity. They have a mission that matters more.

                In regards to the new writers skipping right over Revised, not gonna argue with you there. That sucks, and I'll be honest, I don't find a lot of these ideas, as presented, as very palatable. That said, it's been over a decade now. If the writers decided they wanted to undertake some rather major retcons for the sake of a bold new setting, I wouldn't be adverse to that. But again, these changes don't indicate an edition that's going to appeal to me.

                I'm curious about your thoughts though. How do you perceive the Get? What's their hook for you? And how would you like to see them develop in 5e? It's been quite a few years, so there's a lot of possibilities for how they might have evolved.
                Well, for starters, I wanted to see them have a multi-branched tribe like the Shadow Lords/Hakken, based on legends of monters that threaten to swallow celestial objects. Hilariously, this would involve taking one name from Forsaken, in a way (Rahu, from India), while there are also legends of such a wolf in China (Tiangou, oddly enough, the namesake of the Japanese Tengu). There are a number of other tribes currently presented as just European that could also deal with the same treatment; we're going Silver Fangs here and getting them spread out over the world, to get them to actually look old and well-traveled over the millennia.

                For the Get, like the Furies, what is important is anti-myth. The Revised tribebook had the Get presented as turning many bits of Northern European legendry on their head. For example, Fenris is not one of the progeny of Loki; instead, he arose more or less on his own and has been on a quest for personal growth and his image was conjured to convince Odin that he was surrounded by threats. Flat-out wrong by established myth, sure, but Werewolf is going to kick most of the sensibilities that humans are right about anything to the curb, so you might as well get myths and legends in on it, too.

                Generally, I think of them as more concerned with some personal endeavor than with a group one. The group isn't going to grow your prowess and might for you; you have to do it on your own. They're also very likely to spend more time on sojourns to accomplish this, with the weakness being that cultivating strength is something they take to be time-consuming and which tends to get them to lose track of subtle goings on more easily.
