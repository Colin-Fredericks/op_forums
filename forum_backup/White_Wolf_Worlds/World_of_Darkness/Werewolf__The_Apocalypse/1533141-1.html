One memorable 'tribal lore' explanation even said that a fostern Talon has HEARD of the Litany.

        Luckily, the tantrum throwing Talons aren't as common now. But that is also because they are rarely allowed in games.
        It's also important to note that the whole alpha/omega thing stems largely from a fundamental misunderstanding of how wolf society operates, and is an understanding that continues to evolve. If one were to consider a tribe of Garou divorced from humanity, it would be a tribe that forms its foundations in the ways wolves relate to one another. If taken literally, a red talon's social structure would be more that of a family unit than a hierarchy (a decidedly human construct.) There's a few other wonky bits that get a double take from me, like the notion of the talons conceding to a singular kingly figure in Europe. If someone regularly saw their own family members regularly slaughtered (and those deaths debased by fellow homids) the response is suddenly a lot more relatable than merely the 'rarg must genocide humanity now' snidely whiplash villains so many cast them as. When a license to kill their family only costs 15 bucks one can hardly blame them for keeping other humans at arms length.

        Even setting aside a more modern understanding of wolf society I see them much less as misanthropes that mindlessly attack humans as servants of the Wyrm, so much as an alien tribe that other humans cant relate to, who blame humanity for most strongly enabling the Wyrm than anything else in the tellurian (and they're not wrong about that.) It's a game of telephone that debases who they are as a tribe more strongly attributed to players' resistance to reading and relying on rumors and oversimplified summaries than actually reading the materials. As you say, a big contributor to that frustration is the way other homid Garou dismiss their losses as being less significant than their own. If they have a vendetta beyond the typical thresholds of ordinary society, then it's more an implication that they're more victims of Griffin, using them as patsys for his own agenda having duped them into belief that this is the natural course of action to save Gaia.

                                        The Red Talons were obviously meant to be a more antagonistic tribe among the Garou, but I do not think they were meant to be villainous overall. They were simply an element that could provide easy drama in the same way the Shadow Lords or even Get of Fenris could be used. Early WtA was much more open to internal conflict within the Garou Nation then later editions. Many players conflate the entire Red Talon tribe with the camp of the Lodge of the Predator Kings. But in the 1e Players Guide, even the Lodge was mainly concerned with making alliances with the animal breeds of the other Fera for the future fight with mankind. Not killing all humans all the time. The Warders were primarily concerned with driving mankind away from the remaining areas of untouched wilderness and not necessarily killing them. While the anti-extinction faction was said to be the "main camp."

                                        Now that stance was often undermined by other sourcebooks. Ways of the Wolf explicitly said that the first goal of the Red Talons was the destruction of humans, though this was limited by the Talons' fear that stupid and premature attacks would cause their small numbers to be wiped out. It also referred to the anti-extinction faction as "small" but growing. So clearly the writers were not on the same page as to which camps were to be dominant. I think the tribebooks and later sourcebooks would make the Warders camp the largest and inbetween politically the two other camps. But all of them did try to present the tribe as being somewhat divided, discussing options as to the best way forward, and being cautious in their attacks against humans.

                                        I think it's simply the case that many players and STs enjoy very extreme characters and presentations as an easy way to generate plot. So they ignore the presentation of a cautious tribe trying to balance their goals with their very limited resources, and openly debating what strategies work best and what opportunities can be seized.

                                        I think a bigger problem is the lack of any real description of Red Talon tribal culture and interests. Early tribes were presented almost exclusively through the dominant human culture of their homid breeds, so there was nothing to easily lift for a tribe that was all lupus. So without discussion of "kill all humans", many players and STs simply did not know what to do with them. So they would keep falling back to that. I think what would have been nice is if in Caerns: Places of Power instead of placing the Red Talon caern just outside a human settlement, they would have had something be complete wilderness with almost no humans, and present a purely wilderness setting with some interesting spirits, monsters, and challenges that would provide conflict for the Red Talons absent from humans.

                                        So it definitely takes more work by STs to present the Red Talons in a way where they are not obvious villains and could add to - rather than detract from or blow up - the setting. But possibilities are there for those ambitious STs willing to work on it. But it's definitely one of the failings by the Developers to not properly address this and provide more tools for STs to incorporate them into chronicles.
