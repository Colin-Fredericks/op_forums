    RANK 1 YOUNGER BROTHER GIFTS

    Wendigo (Younger Brother) Gifts relate to the cold, and also have themes of surviving and killing in nature.

    Beat of the Heart-Drum: This is a peerless tracking ability. A weapon left at a crime scene, some blood under a victim's nails, a torn piece of their clothing, and you have a proximity detector for your foe. In practically any mystery this will help you to overcome a dead end, which means it will come up quite often.

    Note that the target needs to HAVE a heartbeat. This rules out most Vampires, and Spirits, and some Fomori. Even then, "they don't have a heartbeat" is a potentially handy clue.

    A less obvious application of this power is defensive. Taste the blood of your whole Pack, your Kinfolk, a prisoner, and have them in a mental library of heartbeats you can track down when they are missing or lost. This could be good inspiration for a Deed Name in fact. A duration of one day per success, a pretty manageable roll and a cost of 1 Gnosis all add up to the power being pretty well balanced for what it does; it's Green because the core concept is very strong, not because of overpowered execution.

    Another neat application is returning lost things to their owners, which is pretty strong indigenous flavour. "Belonging" goes undefined, but I would personally let a Garou who used this Gift on a traditional Zulu shield they liberated from a museum use it to track down its rightful owner, given the nature of spirit magic.

    The RP potential of this Gift includes a permanent bond between two people, "I shall always find you my love", but could also stray into darker territory; imagine playing a Wendigo Kinfolk who knows that their spouse/sibling/parent/stalker will always be able to find them, having tasted their blood long ago.

    Call the Breeze: Situational, but lacks a cost or a roll, and you can direct this as you wish. Calling a frigid wind into existence at will is pretty neat and magical without being an overt Veil risk, unless used in the most stupid ways possible.

    Debuffing perception and freezing people over time is handy and mean. Exhaling gently from a hiding place to slowly debilitate a group of humans with hypothermia is a neat assassination method in theory, but the mechanics don't go any further than a -1 die penalty from the frigid cold.

    Being able to disperse toxins and insects is neat, and given how many Wyrmish threats are miasmas, swarms, toxic clouds and so on this will come up often enough to be useful.

    The RP potential of the Gift is just great drama.

    Camouflage: +3 difficulty to detect you is great. Naturally this doesn't help your Pack, but it's handy for a scout and would work well on any Ragabash or Ahroun in particular. Ambushes are, as always, incredible. The main issue is that this only works in the wilderness, which means it will apply in good chunks of the world and Umbra, but not when you desperately need it in a city or a stranger Realm.

    It's a solid amber, especially since you don't need to activate it and +3 difficulty is so good, but would be a pretty clear-cut Green if it worked everywhere.

    If you want to use this offensively or defensively, try to prioritize heading into the wilderness when you are able to choose where to go.

    Ice Echo: This Gift is shockingly magical, and feels like it belongs at a higher level. The potential for trickery, defense, setting up ambushes and so on is incredible here.

    Do keep in mind that even if your enemy fails the check to determine which one is the real you, it's still only a 50/50 chance to hit the right target.

    The echo looking exactly like you (but mirrored), and speaking exactly like you, is incredible. The Gift has amazing range and there is no roll to use it, just a Gnosis cost. There's also nothing stopping you from making multiple echoes for next level deceptions; have the 2nd echo hide nearby the 1st to be mistaken for the "real" you by a foe who thinks they've outsmarted you. Or mix it up and wander around in the open while a bunch of Echoes hide nearby, which is bound to confuse smarter enemies.

    This Gift combos incredibly well with Scent of Running Water, so a Lupus Ragabash Younger Brother is a really great combination. Six scentless Garou who can only be told apart through touch, magic senses or heat vision, moving around like a pack of identical sextuplets, all dodging past one another until you have no idea where to strike. Since the difficulty of the check to figure out who the "real" Garou is is based on Gnosis, Lupus combos well with this for an additional reason. Keep in mind that many enemies simply won't HAVE the Enigmas knowledge however.

    100 feet is a good range, and allows you to trigger ambushes from a safe distance as well. This Gift has endless applications and makes me wish I saw more people playing this Tribe!

    Resist Pain: See the Philodox list. Amazing as ever, and suits a Tribe which is stewing in the bitter cold.

    ================================================== ============

    Given how rarely I see this Tribe being played, I was expecting a much weaker starting list! Overall I'm very impressed. If you ever get a chance to learn Ice Echo or Beat of the Heart-Drum, do so. Having two amazing unique Gifts is pretty good going.

        And that's all the level 1 Gifts done! There are a few that are left, offshoots and oddities, but those are all of the level 1 Gifts available to a standard starting PC in WTA. I was pleased by how few Gifts were Red, and I learnt a lot going through the list.

        Sense Wyrm is less rare than you'd initially think, given a number of Tribes get it. Certain Tribes feel like they do an Auspice's job better than the Auspice, and ironically encourage you to pair the Tribe with a different Auspice to cover more ground. There's no rhyme or reason to which Gifts cost Gnosis and which don't in many cases. Far more Gifts are focused on things outside combat than you'd expect. The power level of Level 1 Gifts is, as we know, enormously varied but this was also reinforced.

        Given that each of these powers costs 3 or 5 XP, Garou are pretty well off when it comes to their cheapest powers.

            The section on throwing weapons (before general combat maneuvers) makes it clear that thrown weapons still have close and long ranges, but instead of being determined by the weapon, the ST is just supposed to figure it out based on the aerodynamics and Strength of the character (and allows a greater range of difficulty modifiers). While W20 forgot to actually say how to figure out the ranges for thrown weapons in any way, they're still capable of long ranged attacks like firearms and bows.

            The RAW don't really care what the actual distance is, just that it's beyond the base range of the weapon (yes, I agree it's meant to reduce the penalty for up to double range from 8 to 7). A light pistol with a range of 20 gets the benefit of this Gift shooting from 21m to 40m. Thrown is no different. As well, while I don't think W20 addressed this, the WoD games do generally treat throwing assist mechanisms like slings as spear-throwers as still operating as thrown weapons mechanically, vastly increasing their ranges beyond 50m. There's at least a few legendary figure worthy of Silver Fang emulate from early history that used slings to great effect.
            Nice - and yeah agreed on the RAW. It's a handy Gift to offset a penalty, which is kinda handy for situations where foes are out of reach. Plus while the Fangs are super into melee weapons, there's no reason you can't play a tricksy Fang who has a Fetish Bow or Fetish Rifle with talen arrows/bullets, gleefully outperforming their relatives who are too married to melee.

                            RANK 2 HOMID GIFTS

                            Onto some Level 2 Gifts! Homids are up first as always; as usual their Gifts are a mix of social and technological stuff. There are only 4 Gifts in some of these level 2 lists, so hopefully this should be a bit faster!

                            I should note that while I will be taking the level of a Gift into account, it's hard to keep that in mind all of the time; there are enough incredible level 1 Gifts that it's hard to gauge where that leaves incredible level 2 Gifts. Level will be most likely to be taken into account at 4 and 5, where you can have a perfectly functional Gift... which is just 2 or 3 levels higher than it should be!

                            Jam Technology: A Gnosis point, a Manipulation + Crafts with difficulty of 4-9, and a range of 50 feet. Being able to stop cars, guns, planes, machinery in factories and so on is pretty great! In a way this is a bit like Jam Weapon, however it's less effective at jamming weapons in exchange for being much broader. The lowest difficulty being the most complex machinery is also good; stopping a HIT Mark with a difficulty 4 check ain't bad at all!

                            One fun use of this would be completely knocking your opponents out of a car chase; just keep in mind that the Gift does not discriminate. If you're riding a Hispo Garou, then stopping all cars within 15 meters is a great idea. If you're in a car yourself, you've turned a car chase into a standing fight. Which might be handy, who knows.

                            Used without care this Gift could have unintended consequences. Be sure to have a think about what you're inside, riding on, and what's nearby before you turn off every nearby phone.

                            Given the tricky targeting and relatively small range I'm going to give this an Amber, but it's still a pretty neat Gift which could cause some huge moments if you use it surgically. If your campaign is about fighting Cyberdogs or a high tech Pentex division or Etherites or the Technocracy, or causing havoc in the Digital Web, then this will jump up to Green.

                            Mark of the Wolf: Very variable by its very nature. Let's start by keeping in mind that the most fixed mechanic of The Curse is that humans who have a WP lower than your Rage will be afraid of you and go out of their way to avoid or exclude you as a threat. If you have a Rage of 1 or 2, this Gift does next to nothing. At 3 it's dubious. At 4 and 5 things start to get somewhere. Unfortunately this means that this Gift would have, ultimately, made more sense as a Galliard or Ahroun Gift!

                            Assuming a Rage of 5, this Gift is easy to use, has a pretty big impact, and is poetic justice when applied to someone who mistreats you because of your Rage. The duration is also pretty good. Unfortunately this is another one of these Gifts where someone suffers offscreen, which means it will only help you in niche situations. Do you care whether the Pentex rep's hookup decided he had bad vibes or not? Whether his driver put in a report of workplace bullying? Whether the person at reception thought he was a dick? Short of your ST deciding some butterfly effect takes place, it's unlikely to make that big a difference.

                            Speech of the World: A global translator for humans is pretty amazing, and suits the Breed quite well. Being able to speak to any human NPC you encounter regardless of any language barrier is handy, and even for people who do speak English (or whatever language is the base in your campaign) there's a rapport and ease of communication that comes with speaking to someone in their own language.

                            Use this to show off, make friends, travel, and link up with the indigenous communities who can matter so much in WTA's politics and lore. The only thing keeping this from being a Green is that you have to roll to activate it in addition to it solving a problem that is a little hit and miss in coming up.

                            Notably a Homid Galliard of the Elder Brother Tribe winds up having this Gift in addition to Spirit Speech and Beast Speech for the ultimate translator.

                            Staredown: Targeting your opponent's Rank is pretty neat - against mortals and most supernatural foes this will have a difficulty of 5. One difficulty is that a target does have the option to spend a point of WP to ignore the effect for one turn, which means NPCs can still get up to quite a lot of mischief. Draining the WP of NPCs can be handy, but unless they're going to run out in the course of your interaction with them...

                            It's hard to be certain whether a pacified Garou/Fera can spend WP to ignore the anti-combat effect for one turn; I think they can, but the way the Gift is worded is a little vague.

                            Overall this is a pretty good effect, the base difficulty is low, there's no activation cost, and it can banish an NPC from a scene quite effectively. It has some rough edges and isn't perfect, but it sits at the higher end of Amber. Removing the option to spend WP would make it Green, but that would also make it a very un-fun Gift to play with.

                            ================================================== =

                            Nothing too iconic here - Speech of the World is worth picking up as a Metis or Lupus if travel and isolated communities are key parts of the story. There are other Gifts that do Staredown's job better, such as True Fear (at least in my opinion).

                                RANK 2 METIS GIFTS

                                As always I love Metis, and Metis Gifts. Rank 2 has some very exciting ones.

                                Burrow: This is a weird yet effective travel option. Making huge tunnels through solid rock is just funny, especially since with enough successes you can basically go at a walking pace. No cost, a roll you'll likely ace with softer targets, and overall it's just a very fun Gift. Good for undermining places, making a subterranean base swiftly, evading bad weather, creating a back entrance into a Pit, and generally moving unseen.

                                You do leave a huge tunnel without support beams behind you, if you're in Crinos, and that could spell disaster. It's also a tunnel others can follow, and by its very nature it's a potential Veil risk.

                                Curse of Hatred: Spending Gnosis means this is more of a pre-combat attack than anything else. You only need 1 success, so spend WP on the check. The check itself could be very easy or very difficult depending on the target's WP.

                                Losing 2 temporary WP and 2 Rage is a pretty handy debuff, the Rage especially; softening up a target so they have less Rage to spend on Gifts and Bonus actions will make a fight go more smoothly. Unfortunately this Gift doesn't really do anything to Spirits as it were, since they fuel Charms with Essence, not temporary Rage points.

                                The RP potential of this Gift is kind of fun, given that you're hurling an insult packed with the trauma most Metis suffer every day.

                                A niche effect is removing excess Rage from someone who has gone over their maximum and is risking Frenzy.

                                All in all if you assume that you can get this Gift to go off before a fight (semi-reliable) and your target has Rage which they can spend on extra actions (BSDs, rival Garou, some Fomori), you could picture this Gift as a way to steal 2 actions from an enemy for 1 Gnosis. Overall it's an Amber, but it has good potential.

                                Form Mastery: This one is a flat buff which makes you better at transforming, which will save Rage and help you get cool mixed forms off more easily. Removing the WP cost of partial transformation and giving a difficulty reduction is great. With this Gift feel free to accumulate partial transformations until your form is a mix of all five forms. Why? Because you can!

                                The RP potential of showing the protean, fluid nature of the Garou body is pretty neat. It also speaks to the Metis' nature, being Garou from birth, and thus expert in controlling the natural gifts of Garou. Play that up and have fun showing the weirder side of the wolf.

                                Sense Silver: 100 yard range, pretty achievable roll, and this helps you locate Klaives, werewolf hunters, silver bullets and so on. It'll also help you prospect for silver if you want to invest in mining. I'm sure Gaia won't mind taking one for the team with your mining venture...

                                This is Amber because while the execution is really good, and the power works very well, it's ultimately sensing one very specific thing. An important thing, but still niche. I'd suggest picking this up once you have 6xp spare, then using it intermittently to see what pops up.

                                ================================================

                                Form Mastery is the standout here, and is worth 10xp for Homid and Lupus in my opinion. The others are neat and have their place.

                                    RANK 2 LUPUS GIFTS

                                    Lupus Gifts are a bit of a mixed bag for me at this level.

                                    Axis Mundi: The effect only works in the "Gaia Realm", as in on earth. Being a flawless compass would be kind of cute as a level 1 Gift, with another weak effect tacked on. Having it on its own at level 2 is... weird. Silent Striders also get a better version of this of course.

                                    Short of luring your foes into a magical maze garden, it's impossible to use this offensively. I'm sure it would be very handy in a real war, but it's unlikely to impact many rolls or important checks, especially since your mundane senses are so good and you should have some Survival.

                                    Eye of the Eagle: Eagle and Falcon have likely had litigation over Gift names. This Gift and Eye of the Falcon both existing feels like a joke, but they do quite different things. This Gift adds miles onto your visual perception range, but doesn't ignore obstacles. This means that if you are atop a good vantage you can see for miles and miles. This is handy for scouting and guarding, both of which matter a lot to Garou. It isn't my cup of tea but it's good to have in your Pack.

                                    Name the Spirit: Determining the variety and "power level" of Spirits at a glance is amazing; this information can be very hard to come by otherwise. Theurges also get this Gift, thankfully.

                                    Being able to determine that a Spirit is impersonating another Spirit is useful, and knowing which Spirits could one-shot a typical Garou could be a lifesaver.

                                    Scent of Sight: This Gift makes dark, foggy and otherwise obscured places your best friend. Both for stealth and combat. Being able to passively see perfectly with your scent alone is incredible, and will positive impact a lot of scenarios. This will also be useful underground and underwater, and allow you to defeat a lot of visual illusions. Many Gifts and Powers could be more easily overcome if you keep your eyes closed, along with powers from other supernaturals such as Dominate.

                                    Using this Gift offensively is handy - try using flashbangs, Gifts that generate cover, smoke, cover of night and so on.

                                    Well worth 10xp to buy out-of-list, especially if you are blind.

                                    ================================================== ================

                                    Two of these are very solid, one is pretty good, and one is bad. I don't even mind that - level 2 Gifts are pricey. Having too many good options is a big tax on XP at times.

                                                        RANK 2 RAGABASH GIFTS

                                                        Auspice Gifts get pretty intense at 2nd level in some cases; Ragabash get some interesting ones!

                                                        Blissful Ignorance: Requiring you to stay still for the power to work is quite restrained for a level 2 Gift, but this is pretty neat. Stamina + Stealth is a good combo to roll, especially since it's so heavily buffed in Crinos form. The fact that you are hidden from everything is also pretty great; more successes means better hiding, but 1 success is enough to hide from people who aren't actively searching.

                                                        Comparing this to Brother's Scent... it's better in some ways, worse in others. All in all it's good to have a small variety of stealth Gifts which work in different ways, if you're committed to hiding.

                                                        Pulse of the Prey: As discussed above this is another amazing tracking Gift. The prompt to use it is some kind of idea who you're looking for, a description, a sketch, their signature or initials and so on. This does mean there are different means of tracking which could work better, such as Heart-Drum if you have a sample of blood, or a possession, but no description or signature etc.

                                                        This Gift can be outdone by actively hiding, which also is a step down from Heart-Drum, but it's superior in the sense that it helps you nail an exact location and isn't just a hot/cold. Well worth having both, if you happen to be a Younger Brother.

                                                        Handy for finding packmates, finding spirits, and tracking down targets in an investigation. Sometimes a plot will have to have some mcguffin block this Gift, but that's alright in my book as long as it's done sparingly.

                                                        Spider's Song: This is excellent; intercepting calls and texts from mobiles is very handy, and being able to do so from a good distance as long as you keep your eyes on the target is great. This could provide incredibly good clues.

                                                        RPwise being privy to the texts of PCs and NPCs could be handy. The fact that a Lupus Ragabash with Technology 0 and Computers 0 can have this while a Glass Walker won't is also very funny to me.

                                                        A cost of 1 Gnosis is a little annoying, especially since the power is convo-specific. Being able to graze through a variety of text conversations happening in a single cafe would be pretty neat. As it stands this is a great way to get insight you'd otherwise lack and catch out enemies. It's only Amber because it has a price and you need to suspect foul play before using it; wasting Gnosis to read "love you see you soon" won't feel good.

                                                        Taking the Forgotten: I'm in two minds about this one. Because reading it, you assume you'd succeed.

                                                        Let's break it down. Imagine you have a Wits + Larceny of 9, and even have an applicable specialty. And let's imagine that you're trying to take something from a smart yet sheltered Theurge. Intelligence 5, Streetwise 0, say. 9 dice, with a spec, difficulty 5, and you need 3 successes. With WP spent you'll get the Gift to go off 95% of the time.

                                                        Now let's imagine your foe is a canny Ragabash with Int 4 and Streetwise 3 - difficulty 7. And let's imagine your pool is a more realistic 7 dice, and you don't have a spec, since finding one that would trigger for this power is a little tricky. If you spend WP you "only" have a 72% chance for it to work.

                                                        The takeaway is that if you have a decent dice pool for Wits + Larceny, and you choose the right targets (many Garou won't have good Streetwise, and you'll often be able to gauge roughly who will) you should be fine.

                                                        Make sure that you don't show off what you've stolen, and make sure to pick something that other people won't miss; if you get a Silver Fang Elder's legendary blade off him it won't take long for someone to notice it's missing and jog his memory!

                                                        Think of this as a tool to reduce the risk of getting in trouble when you steal something, rather than eliminating that risk entirely. If you grab something from a vault, make its owner forget they ever owned it and escape clean into the night then the odds of the theft being noticed are reduced, but not 100% eliminated. This is handy for stealing a phone from a pentex rep, getting sensitive data from an enemy, snatching a Fetish you like the look of from a lone wanderer and so on.

                                                        This is Green on the assumption you have a great pool, pick a sensible target and use the power wisely. Otherwise it would be a solid amber.

                                                        =========================================

                                                        Some of these are pretty excellent, and they lean into the thief/rogue/scout aspect of the Auspice quite well.

                                                            RANK 2 THEURGE GIFTS

                                                            Theurge Gifts are key in my opinion, since their main role as knowing more Rites and having more access to Rites is relatively negotiable; there's no reason you can't be an Ahroun with a high Rituals knowledge and convince Rite masters to teach you more Rites with Talen gifts and so on, it'll just depend on the Sept. Their unique Gifts mean that they have a defined role even when Rites get shared around.

                                                            Battle Mandala: This one goes out of its way to be Veil friendly which is nice; only those who have Gnosis can see the mark, which has a stealthy bonus use. Ask Kinfolk to draw a symbol you've made on the ground, and you'll get "what symbol" from 99%; those who have Gnosis may unwittingly reveal themselves to you.

                                                            The primary use of this is an AOE pseudo-attack against spirits. A 100 or 150 foot area where all spirits lose 1 Essence per turn is very handy, since Essence both fuels Charms and acts as Health. If a pack fights a horde of spirits with relatively low health this will be an amazing power, and could destroy some weaker spirits in as few as 3-5 turns... which is still unfortunately a very long time!

                                                            This Gift forces Spirits to fight or flee, which is handy, and gives a marginal debuff to enemy's Essence pool which is cool, and dealing 1 point of guaranteed damage each round to 5+ enemies is pretty great. Better than launching an attack which may well miss, perhaps. Note that allied spirits other than your totem will lose Essence too.

                                                            One application would be to place this in an area a spirit can't leave - say they're guarding something within the Umbra - and then kite, evade and hide from them. Overall it's useful but not incredible.

                                                            Command Spirit: Incredible. Use this on spirits who are your enemies, outright. Using it on neutral and allied spirits outside of the most dire circumstances is more likely to bite you. It isn't clear how long a command lasts unfortunately; if you tell a Scrag to kill its companions, does it keep going until it's done or the Scrag is destroyed?

                                                            Regardless of the specifics, this is a very potent Gift which can do great things, and also get you into a lot of trouble. Keep in mind that can and should are different. You CAN force a city father to tell you where your enemies are hiding, but SHOULD you. If you abuse this power it could create far more grief than it will ever solve. The difference between a Theurge and a Mage, Sorcerer or Vampire Thaumaturge is that you are supposed to be working with spirits, working naturally within a kind of 'natural right' as it were. If you bully spirits as obnoxiously as a Hermetic, Tremere or similar then you will get a bad reputation among spirits, which is awful for a Theurge.

                                                            Name the Spirit: See the Lupus entry above. Key to a Theurge doing their job, and fixes a lot of problems relating to deception and disguises.

                                                            Sight from Beyond: I know a lot of storytellers give Theurges visions 'just because', so this is a tricky one. Having a Gift which entitles you to receive regular Visions could be great, or mediocre, depending on your ST. Ask them for their opinion and explain that you want to be sure you aren't just paying a tax to receive visions the party would have been sent regardless.

                                                            If I were adjusting this Gift I would make it so that it makes it easier to navigate and understand existing visions, perhaps allowing you to change your POV, glimpse the spirit that sent the vision, or change the type of symbolism being used so you can see the vision from a different angle; for instance replacing a weather metaphor with an animal metaphor.

                                                            One approach would be to make it so that you can spend Gnosis while receiving a vision to get more symbolism/details, or perhaps make a roll when you receive a vision to then gain bonus dice when you make checks relating to the warning you were sent; for instance having bonus dice to stake a vampire after you had a vision about a vampiric threat.

                                                            Like I say, mileage may vary. I've yet to see a table where lacking this Gift meant a Theurge receives 0 visions, but obviously that's ST-dependent and people will have different experiences.

                                                            =========================================

                                                            Command and Name are key, and must-haves. The others are good to grab if they seem to fit the Chronicle. Massive spirit battles, if they are going to happen, will be shaped quite well by a Battle Mandala.
