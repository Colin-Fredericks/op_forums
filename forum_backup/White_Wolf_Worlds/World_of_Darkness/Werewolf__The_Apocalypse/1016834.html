    How easy is it to learn gifts

    12-13-2016, 08:39 PM
    I've been playing Werewolf for about 3 years now, the first 2 as a player, the next year as an ST. However, a lot of what I did was based on how our old ST ran things, and as I started reading more and more of the book, I realized there was a lot of things we did wrong. One of them I'm not so sure about though, is learning gifts. There's a small section before the gifts that says:

    "Most often, a werewolf must either petition a particular spirit to teach her its powers or ask an elder to summon that spirit on their behalf. Traditionally, the Garou first travels to a caern with a power level equal to or greater than the desired Gift to petition the spirit personally - to do otherwise is to defy tradition, which risks incurring the wrath of the spirit. But as more caerns fall to the talons of the Wyrm, many Garou are forced to make do with whatever places of power they can find, or even summon spirits outside of caerns completely."

    In the game in which I was a player, we just summoned spirits willy nilly and had them teach us where ever. Our Theurge had the Rite of Spirit Summoning, and we would just summon whatever we needed, and with a good enough roll, the chart always ended up on them coming in "Friendly". However, reading the passage makes it sound like learning Gifts is not that simple. I'm struggling with a few things about it though. It says Garou would travel to a caern to address the spirit personally. Does that mean finding one with a caern totem that matches? Or just finding a caern and asking any spirit of the kind you need that resides there?

    My old ST also made it seem like most Garou had a ton of Gifts. In his mind, if you're not leaving Rank 1 with 9+ Gifts, you're not doing it right. So how does everyone else run learning Gifts in their games, and how many do people tend to end up with?

          I don't think most players and STs make it that difficult. I mentioned on a post here several months ago that learning Gifts required you to summon a spirit at the same the Caern level as the Gift you want to learn (or higher), and everyone else who posted asked, "Where did you read that?" When I pointed out it's been the language in every core book edition, all acted surprised. Most people just want to spend the XP and move on.

          In my chronicles though, I follow that. Proper petitioning of the spirit is important. And at higher Gift levels, it may require you to visit other caerns. I've always been find with that because the early Rage books made it seem like that the default setting of any chronicle needed to include multiple caerns of various levels. and that visiting other caerns were routine. Of course, once you properly propitiate the spirit - the actual learning of the Gift is automatic.

          On the other hand, if you eschew the proper protocols, you can still learn Gifts or even be taught by Garou instead of spirits. Of course if you do that you gain notoriety in the spirit world.

          The question about how many Gifts a Garou should have really depends on each chronicle. Different people are going to have different ideas about what is right. 9 Gifts when turn rank 2 seems way too high to me, but I think a lot of people have fun with those kind of high XP games. I'd expect more between 4-6 Gifts when you reach rank 2. I tend to like games that are more down to level than epic fantasy.

              Have a Spirit show up friendly is great. That doesn't mean that they are going to (or even can) defy tradition. It is understood that the Garou asking for something is going to bargain for that thing. So offerings are prepared in advance to thank the Spirit for arriving, and then the Garou negotiate what services they will provide in payment for the Spirit teaching them the Gift (Chiminage). This has always been my experience in Werewolf. So while the XP cost is relatively low, the in game role playing cost tends to be more in depth. And the higher the Rank Gift, the more involved the Chiminage is going to be.
              And again, having a spirit like you isn't enough to overturn tens of thousands (or more) of years of tradition. In general, Spirits cannot defy their nature. And Pacts are ingrained in their nature.

                  Originally posted by Vulcan7200 View Post
                  There's a small section before the gifts that says:
                  It also says, "Such slighted spirits often demand recompense or service before consenting to share their blessings."

                  So the "have to go to a high Ranked Caern part," can simply replaced by "doing more chiminage for the spirit to get the Gift," instead.

                  I don't think your group was doing it wrong necessarily. The point for the books is that learning Gifts has a significant in-character opportunity cost involved with it. Traveling to other Caerns is the "simple" method, but that means leaving your home territory less protected, and dealing with the other Caern's Garou (or others) to get access. If you want to stay focused on a single location, you can summon away, but then there's the cost of needing to pay much higher chiminage each time. If you can't summon, then you have to Umbral quest on top of the increased chiminage for not going to a Caern.

                  The books add a lot of exceptions to this, probably to avoid locking the game into either happening at Rank 5 Caerns, or being a high travel game. You pick the option that works best for the kind of story you want to go with.

                  It says Garou would travel to a caern to address the spirit personally. Does that mean finding one with a caern totem that matches? Or just finding a caern and asking any spirit of the kind you need that resides there?
                  It doesn't really specify. I've always gone with the later option though, simply because the first makes learning Gifts obnoxiously hard simply by the shear number of different Caerns you'd need to have to visit to cover your bases (and various spirits that generally don't act as Caern Totems like Ancestor spirits).

                  My old ST also made it seem like most Garou had a ton of Gifts. In his mind, if you're not leaving Rank 1 with 9+ Gifts, you're not doing it right. So how does everyone else run learning Gifts in their games, and how many do people tend to end up with?
                  It depends a lot on the ST. Part of the problem is that there's a big disconnect in the books. The books tend to present Gifts as relatively rare. Most high level canon NPCs with stats have between 9 and 12 Gifts total across all the Ranks they have access too, and almost never have out-of-splat Gifts. Meanwhile Gifts are extremely cheap for the XP, and are generally better than most other XP investments you could make for the same XP. So if you have access to a Gift that suits your character, there's almost never an in-character reason or and out-of-character reason to not take it. So IME, PC Garou tend to have way, way more Gifts than the books seem to think they're going to. Even in game where the ST has been fairly restrictive with learning Gifts, having 6-9 Rank 1 Gifts was not uncommon.

                  The only time I see Gift selections that look more like the characters in the books are games that tend to ramp up Renown and Rank fairly quickly (because you'll take higher Rank Gifts if you can most of the time); but then the other traits don't tend to line up instead.
