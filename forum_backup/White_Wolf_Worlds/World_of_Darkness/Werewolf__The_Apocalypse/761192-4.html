                The whole thing is a non-issue most of the time thanks to the fact that Sorcerors are quite rare and need to find a source that will teach them. Since kinfolk tend to hang with their own, their exposure to groups that could teach them is quite rare. Worse yet, most of the printed Paths are quite dubious as picks and as something for the Garou to digest.

                However, I've always had the opinion that the most common and acceptable Path among the Kinfolk is the one of Enchantment (that's the crafting one, right?) - to the point that I actually used them as a npc support group for crafters and mystics. Mind you, I do not use Mage terms or contrivances - they are simply a group of kinfolk dedicated to crafting and using ancestral techniques given from father to son for the past thousands of years. Their style is deeply influenced and patterned on the practices of the Theurges.

                The Path of Enchantment allows for specialised skills and knowledges that are perfect for the craftsman, since actually making weapons of silver and all the goods that will pass the muster in the eyes of a spirit is a great endeavour, as i deeply doubt that most Theurges are masters of both the spirits and craftsmanships.

                The other form of Kinfolk magic that i use is giving them low level rituals and gifts. A lot can be achieved with them, allowing you to fulfil most narrative roles you could need from them. This is something that works within the confines of Werewolf and that is supported by the setting.

                Classic sorcery and true magic brings with it way too much baggage. The former is pretty much married to a long standing organisation and mentors who would intrude into the Kinfolk's lives enough to be a threat to the Garou, and the latter makes a disservice for the Mage to be in service of the Garou and their goals rather then themselves.

                    Depending a bit on the Gift and the successes rolled:

                    1) The level of detailed offered is left at the supernatural "type" level. A vampire or blood magics are detected differently from a wraith and Arcanoi, and so on. The books leave mages and sorcerers in something of a undifferentiated state without ever clearly addressing how to separate a Hermetic dynamic mage from a Hermetic linear mage. Most of these Gifts directly identify the thing in question though. Scent of the True Form says, "that's a leech, that's a wizard, that's a fae." without prerequisite knowledge of those groups specific natures.

                    2) Sense Magic of the baseline Gifts for this is only one that seems to convey this sort of information in a way that could be taught outside of knowing the Gift, since it operates more like Auspex 2 or other aura scanning powers (though it isn't dealing with auras) in terms of delivery a suite of sensory information that indicates specific aspects of the magic in question. The text leaves enough leeway that particularly rare kinds of magic could lead to the user not being sure what they sensed, and thus to a more experienced Garou informing them that the feeling they had means X; but that seems to mostly be for things like Mummies or Kuei-jin that Western Garou never really see.

                    Also, what makes "human magic" human?
                    From a Garou's perspective? It doesn't come from spirits/Gnosis/ghosts, vampires, fae, or other lesser known but clearly inhuman (from a Garou point of view) entities.

                    Changelings are essentially humans, kinain even more so. And psionics, are they magic human or another breed entirely. Human-form Kami, and other possessed (well, most are bad because of what they do) ?
                    Mummies are often allied with garou, Gypsies ?
                    The fae aren't tolerated well by the Garou either (though like the Uktena are more tolerant of magic users, the Fianna are more tolerant of the fae). Kin fae face the same 4/7 freebie issue to be tolerated/have anyone actually like them. The fae are taught too be to unstable to trust and form full alliances with.

                    Psychics are numina users, just like hedge wizards and true faith weilders.

                    Kami are more like kinfolk with Gnosis. They're blessed directly by Gaia, and some Garou might not like that, but aren't going to argue with their primary goddess on it either.

                    Pre-Revised mummies are presented as too rare and too reclusive to have any solid stereotypes about. It's hinted that the Garou can easily mistake them for things they like less due to that unfamiliarity but hard to say more. Post-Revised mummies are really only allied with the Silent Striders against their mutual enemies; this new breed of mummy is too new to know how things would play out in the long term for Garou/Mummy relations.

                    And... lets not touch WOD: Gypsies? Ok?

                                        The language parallel is a bit more nuanced in a way that I think matters. While it is generally true that most people do better with languages when they're exposed at a younger age, that is more of an "average" than a universal truth. Some people are always going to suck at language acquisition (my sister tested for a learning disability in this, I tested on the edge) even for languages they were raised around. Other people (my brother-in-law's family is full of people like this) has brains that never turn off the early acquisition stuff, and pick up new languages with annoying ease well into old age.

                                        Magical aptitude should probably be seen the same way. Some people are just never really going to have the talent. Some people might get the talent if they're raised in the right environment, and others are just naturals with it who will pick it up whenever they finally get exposed to it.
