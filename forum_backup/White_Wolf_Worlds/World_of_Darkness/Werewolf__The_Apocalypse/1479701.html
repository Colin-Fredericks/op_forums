    Other werewolf option

    04-29-2022, 12:01 PM
    One of the most curious things I realised WtA is missing, is an alternative to the garou.

    Not the Changing Breeds. but another type of werewolf. Vampire, Mage and Changeling run the gamut of different types of vampires, mages and fae. But werewolf instead is stuck with only the garou as werewolves.

    And, personally, I think that is a loss on WtA's part. I am a big fan of the NWoD way of not tying everything into one mythology and I feel having a more 'traditional' werewolf would do good as a contrast to the garou. On top of everything, it would stop the recent push to make the garou fit a peg they were never meant to fit.

    So what do I mean by 'traditional' werewolf?

    The person starts as a human and is then turned into a werewolf through infection, curse or wearing a wolf-skin.

    The ability to change forms is tied to the method they became werewolves. I.e. Those wearing wolf-skin can shift whenever, the infected are bound to change without control,etc.

    The forms are limited to one or two.

    The morality of the werewolves is not tied to Gaia/Wyrm but instead various per person.

    They cannot access the umbra or gain gifts from the spirits, instead, the powers would be mastering their nature better.

    This is very rough, but I do feel it'd give people not into the Ecoterrorist Furry Captain Planet something.

      There are numerous other ways that werewolves could be done in an RPG that are different from Garou in W:tA. The issue is, I think, is that if you're playing some other sort of shape shifter who isn't a Garou, then that character isn't really going to have any place in a W:tA chronicle and the splat isn't going to have much overlap with Garou even if they have some similar abilities. So I think it would be interesting to have different kinds of werewolves as NPC's in a Mage or Vampire game, for example, but in a standard W:tA game they might be out of place.

      If I were doing a very different sort of werewolf, I think I would have them essentially be mystics of sorts, maybe even shamans with a tie in to some of the spirit stuff in W:tA, but not a separate race. A shaman with a spiritual connection to wolves who learns a spell that allows for transformation into a wolf. If we go with one of the myths, the ritual to transform could involve a wolf skin or this could be done with other animals as well.

              I think you're overstating the case of the rest of the WoD a bit here.

              Core book VtM vampires don't deal with "other kinds of vampires." Until Kindred of the East was published it was assumed all vampires worked on the same principles even with local variations (remember there were two retconned Japanese Clans before KotE). There being other "vampires" out in the WoD is something that comes from supplements, other game-lines, and so on.

              The nWoD/CofD isn't any different here. The Uratha of Forsaken don't have Skinchangers as a default antagonist... those show up in a general supplement about how to model the "wear an animal skin to change into that animal" types.

              The problem here isn't WtA, it's that the cWoD never did all of the "monster building books for mortal/minor supernaturals to face off against," that the nWoD/CofD has. And at this point.. why change the cWoD? It's a nostalgia brand for people that want to play the games it popularized in the 90s. There are lots of a horror RPGs, and plenty of them that let you play the monsters, and lots of them do their own spin on how their versions of those monsters work (including the CofD). There's competition now that just didn't exist in the past, and "we rough fit this other idea into our already popular game," isn't a selling point for anything that's not D&D.

              How many people is this really for at this point? If it was thirty years ago, your points would have a lot more weight. Now, it's a lot smaller potential population of people that are going to care. You have the old school WoD fans that for some reason want this but haven't already made it happen using the scattered tools for doing so (Just giving core book powers of other splats to mortals via a metaphysical process, the various supernatural object building systems, Numina, the various splat-oriented monster building systems for custom non-core supernatural critters) and refuse to move onto different systems that have this more built in, and... potentially some portion of new players that would gladly pick the cWoD over all the other options if only it had better defined "other" werewolves?

                      I've done a lot of work in my chronicles to introduce the feel of more traditional werewolves among Garou. I made a thread about traditional werewolves and original folklore as one of my first posts on this forum. I also supplemented it with later threads such as this. My work aren't the only methods available, but I found it to have worked well for me.

                      Want to include wolf straps and such in your chronicle? Make it a Fetish that improves transformation speed and acts like the rite of talisman dedication.

                      Want to include wolf salves and other ointments that witches used to transform into wolves? Treat it like a Talen or the end product of the Rite of Spirit Brew.

                      Want werewolves biting people to allow transformation? Say that it's a way that Garou have found useful to activate the First Change for a lot of Lost Cubs or Lunatics who are having trouble changing.

                      Want the drinking of lycanthropic streams or eating of lycanthropic flowers to activate? Say those are examples of Wyld Fetishes that help provoke the First Change in the same way.

                      Some of these methods might result in Ronin who are technically Garou, but some of them form societies with very different ideas about their condition than what the Garou Nation has, and these can emulate some traditional stories of werewolves. Instead of talking about "Kinfolk" they talk about "cursed bloodlines" where the curse is passed down through the blood, able to erupt at any time.

                      I also make Lunatics more common.

                      I also make in my chronicles that Skin Changers are far older and far more common than canon. Sam Haight was not the inventor of the method. At best he was a rediscoverer of a secret the Garou try to repress. But there are plenty of very old wolfskins handed down from generation to generation of lost kinfolk who act as counterparts to the official Garou Nation.

                      Then there are Fomori and Kami that transform ordinary humans into monsters who can shapechange into wolves. If it is a Fomori, then obviously it's an antagonist that they'll kill. But a Kami would be an ally.

                      And small groups like the Fenians of the original WoD: Sorcerer who know the Path of Shapechanging, but are limited to being wolves and often requiring focuses like actual wolf pelts. They're not Garou, but they're what ordinary people would still consider to be werewolves. Garou hold them in disdain, but also see them as useful allies at the same time.

                      I'm also big on using the existing Merits and Flaws that mirror traditional and cinematic werewolves. I openly encourage players to use them for their PCs, and I often give them out to NPCs especially those of Pure Breed. If players see them as relatively common (I try for a 25-33% ration of NPCs having at least one of them), they begin to see those Merits/Flaws as normal.

                      This keeps things in the chronicle heavily based on existing Werewolf: the Apocalypse concepts, but creates the feel of more traditional werewolves. It's mainly a matter of some creativity and how the ST presents information to the players.
