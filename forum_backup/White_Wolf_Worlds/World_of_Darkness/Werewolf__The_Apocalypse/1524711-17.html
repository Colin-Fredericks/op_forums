                Originally posted by Damian May View Post

                100% and as we've seen so far from Parawolf/Renegade the only ones doing anything about said puppymill or campers are the CoF.

                I mean the corebook says that the Garous main problem is that they are too quick to act with violence but then every single IC and OOC depiction of non-CoF Garou shows them doing absolutely nothing of the sort and instead just kinda camping at their Caerns while the world burns.
                Honestly that seems to track with the mechanics. Mechanically, for any Garou there's basically only 4 endings. 1) They die, 2), they give up the wolf, 3) they fall into Harano, 4) they fall into Haglusk. Points gained in Harano or Haglusk are permanent and can't be reduced, so over the course of years or decades, it's only a matter of time before a werewolf collects enough of those points (and it's only 5) to permanently fall into one of those two states.

                In the setting, this is reflected in the fact that most of the Garou Nation has fallen into Harano. They've given up, they've retreated and they're not interested in fighting anymore. The remainder has fallen into Haglusk and are out to fight against the Wyrm in any way possible. There's no third path or third option because that simply doesn't exist.

                PCs, of course, haven't fallen to one or the other, yet, but eventually they'll fall too (unless they die first or willingly give up the wolf). This is one of those cases where the setting actually reflects the rules as written (and that's honestly pretty rare in RPGs and I have to give Parawolf/Renegade recognition for that) but unfortunately in this case, the rules suck and and that's reflected onto the setting.

                Ultimately this makes W5 a profoundly pessimistic game because you can't win, you can't change things, you were born too late to save the world or make any major, lasting difference, and there's a legitimate argument that having your character die or give up on being a werewolf is better than the only other two long-term alternatives - falling into Harano or Haglusk.

                        Except the W5 corebook takes a firm anti-Litany-stance. The section on the Litany ends like this: "For all of the self-glorifying tales that old Garou tell around the fires, younger werewolves note that the Garou Nation didn’t collapse because the Litany was so goddamn perfect." So a tenet like "Combat the Wyrm Wherever It Dwells and Whenever It Thrives​" is thrown under the bus by the W5 corebook along with the other tenets. Of course, it's precisely because of tenets like the Wyrm-one that the conversation about the Litany deserves more nuance than what was offered by the W5 corebook.

                        And the sidebar "Changing Ways" as mentioned before floats the idea that the Garou abandoning their mission is one valid way to solve the issues according to W5. Additionally, the section "The Real Problem With The Garou" ends like this: "In a world that is dead and dying, they are the ones with the best chance to right some part of a worldwide wrong…
                        … they just need to understand the limitations of the tools at their disposal.​" The "not doing anything"-part isn't just about the Garou literally not doing anything but also about how Garou can't do anything to make things better. The book heavily questions whether there's anything Garou can meaningfully contribute to improving things in the Age of Apocalypse.

                            See, I think this is where a lot of the frustration is coming from:

                            To use another game that I play. imagine if Paizo started publishing a lot of level 1-10 Adventure Paths, but starting at level 3 your characters start running into more and more setbacks, taking on more and more inherent debuffs whenever they finish encounters that you can't get rid of, and at level 5 you are hit with an unkillable brick wall of a boss that makes it impossible to progress, and the DM is then ordered to lecture that you, the player, is the only one able to stop slavery or evil cults or whatever the big bad was doing in real life, and that by trying to do so in a game you're being just as evil as the villains.

                            And then, when Pathfinder fans are understandably upset about this, I go onto Pathfinder subreddits and forums and start arguing that hey, I haven't played Pathfinder in years but I do play a lot of Call of Cthulhu and 40k so every single TTRPG ever is supposed to be an exercise in futility and nihilism, regardless of what the game's name implies about what a player can expect, so all the criticisms of this new direction are overblown and wrong.

                            Why should WtA be held to a different standard? Why should a game called Werewolf: the Apocalypse, a game whose main theme for over 30 years been about raging to your last breath against the dying of the light, be any different? Why should its fans have to just accept that its newest iteration is about spinelessly hiding in a hole until you die or go crazy just because uhhh that's what other games that aren't even in the same gameline, or even the same medium do?

                            If you like more fatalistic games and stories, that's absolutely fine, and I mean that. There's definitely a time and place for those. But it's not within WtA and it never has been. And trying to cram WtA into that framework just creates the ugly, confused tonal trainwreck that is W5
