    Teaching the Garou / vignettes and tasks

    07-16-2017, 11:27 PM
    I am looking for some good ideas and brainstorming on how to show in game about teaching Garou, especially Garou cubs, the essentials of being Garou. What kind of things would a Mentor or Den Father do.

    I am not asking about the specifics of what should be learned. The game mechanics (the Attributes, Abilities, Advantages) and basic Garou lore (Litany, Moots, Songs of the Garou) are obvious.

    What I am looking for are specific actions, events, or quests a Mentor/Den Father would have the cub perform in order to learn them. Lectures are one thing, but at some point the Garou cub has to go out and learn them himself or simply train. I am thinking of scenes like in The Karate Kid when Mr. Miyagi made Daniel paint the fence and wash the car which Daniel thinks is meaningless, rote work, but which in fact actually prepares him to learn since he is a) building his stamina, b) creating muscle memory, and c) teaching him basic movies. Later on he learns that the work he is done also provides spiritual lessons in addition to practical ones. We also see similar things in movies and TV in basic training (many of the things Captain Sobel did to train Easy Company in the Currahee episode of Band of Brothers was brilliant even though he was a terrible officer in other ways - when he knows Winter is promoted to Lieutenant and assigns him the task of mess officer for two weeks to show his leadership abilities, or when he orders the men to drain their canteens after the Currahee run to see if everyone obeyed orders to not drink from them).

    These kinds of tasks often seem like BS at the time and are resented during training, but usually afterwards the student or soldier is very thankful for them. For story purposes, they are also great vignettes which get the point across while not using up too much time.

    These are the kind of things I am looking for. They are very good things to cover in a general backstory/prelude of the character, or if a PC has taken Mentor it can help give the NPC more personality if there are actual stories the player has about them. Here are some specific things I think tasks can be divided into.

    So we have general physical conditioning and skill learning.

    How to understand, handle, and master Rage - from its first use to fuel a Garou's power to understanding the pain of Gaia.

    How to understand and use Gnosis - how it starts as this is how you fuel Gifts to understanding a Garou's connection to Gaia

    How to step sideways for the first time.

    How to understand the other forms (lupus/primal-urge for Homids, humanity and human based skills for lupus and metis) and control shapechange

    What it means to be in the company of Pure Breed and recognize it

    How to channel and use Ancestors

    Understand the basic concepts of chiminage and interaction with spirits

    Learn about each of the Auspice roles and how it affects a Garou's individual behavior, their role in a pack, their role in a sept


    So what kind of things have people included in their background, seen happen in game, or can think up right now?

      Learning the basics of the litany, and learning to perform and understand common howls, such as the howl of introduction, are important. Learning some tribal history would be important to most, if not all, of the tribes, and probably interesting to any Galliard.

      For lupus, learning how humans measure time has been an interesting challenge in a game I'm playing in now: days and seasons are intuitive, but the division of days into hours and minutes has had to be explained. The same would go for measuring distance in any way other than how many days it would take to get somewhere on foot. Human concepts of time and distance may or may not be important, depending on the setting.

              Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
              How to understand and use Gnosis - how it starts as this is how you fuel Gifts to understanding a Garou's connection to Gaia

              How to step sideways for the first time.
              I'm mastering a Rite of Passage right now, and because of that the players started without having put a single foot in the Umbra yet, let alone having gifts. Because of the increased difficulty of stepping sideways after months spent in the material world, I assumed that for a werewolf was near-to-impossible reach the Umbra alone the first time, especially if we consider an inexperience malus (it would be, what, difficulty 12 or so?). I pushed my players in the Umbra through a moon bridge, but you can also use one of those gifts meant to push something corporeal through the Gauntlet (they were rank 3, i believe?).
              It was kinda funny, since as they step through the silver portal they would find themselves with no clothes, no money and headed to a far, far away place that would put them in an unfamiliar setting with no resources beside their own companions. I described the stepping into the Umbra as a physical sensation of awakening, like that tingle that your arms sometimes do if you slept on them. It was also written (somewhere) that the Umbra feels more real than the real world to the Garou, with stronger smells and deeper colours.

              I took the use of the Gifts as something natural. The spirit transfers it in your body, and at that point you're suddenly able to do it. Like grasping a bottle, now you can create fire with about the same ease. Once learnt, the gift become a natural part of the Garou, and since their spirit side kinda awakens the first time they step in the Umbra I don't think that learning how to use it should feel too intellectual. It should be more like an instinct: once you've been there and back, you can try to attune yourself again to that place and that sensation. And suddenly, you're there again.

But since Gifts are that instinctive, simply by learning them they may understand that a particular sensation weakens them on a spiritual level, and that it's exactly the thing that fuels whatever effect they now know.
