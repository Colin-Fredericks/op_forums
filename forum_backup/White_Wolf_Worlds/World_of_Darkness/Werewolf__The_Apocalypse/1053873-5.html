                                        Originally, there is no such thing as the Christian Hell (at least not beyond the idea of Tartarus for the fallen angels). If you look at the original texts of the Old Testament and the New Testament in the Ancient Greek, you will see that the first Christians did not worry about Hell because Hell did not exist for mortals. The idea of Hell for mortals came as pagan beliefs were incorporated into Christian doctrines and as late Roman Church leaders, who restricted access to the Bible to the elites and the priesthood, decided to use the threat of Hell and Damnation as a motivation to increase conversion to Christianity (and to gain wealth and political power). The Medieval and Modern Church simply followed the lead of the late Roman Church because they found it was easier to promote conversion (and to gain wealth and political power) as long as they threatened people with Hell and Damnation.

                                        Erebus is a wonderful resource for the Garou. If they feel that they have committed a sin, they can subject themselves to the silver of Erebus in order to cleanse the taint from their souls and to gain forgiveness. On a more personal level, what mortal would not desire a place like Erebus, where they could find true cleansing and true forgiveness through their suffering while they are alive and could return to the world of living? I would not hesitate for a second to enter Erebus if I was guaranteed the cleansing and the forgiveness that the Garou receive from their suffering (and the guarantee that I would return to the world of the living).
