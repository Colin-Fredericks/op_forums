                            For me the constricting part of Harano/Hauglosk is that you have to figure out what having dots in both means. I mentioned in the other thread my personal solution which is to redefine Hauglosk to be more about giving up fighting for a cause. In that paradigm, both Harano and Hauglosk are paths to giving up on being a Garou. But this also includes redefining how Chronicle Tenets work because as others have mentioned, that implicit link between Hauglosk and Chronicle Tenets gets no explanation whatsoever in the W5 corebook. The ST-section's bit on Chronicle Tenets is wholly unconnected to Hauglosk - even though they are mechanically closely tied to each other.

                            If you were to just dump the whole framework of W5 there are a lot more options to figure out what a Humanity-equivalent from V5 could look like in W5. I think the only good thing to say about that is that it could've been worse: At least, W5 isn't as bad as H5.

                            Now there's a system that's just bad: H5's Danger-Track, for example, is just an utter mess. A fine idea but the H5 corebook forgot the game-part and didn't give it any mechanics. I'm not joking about this. H5 has no mechanics for the Danger-Track - it's just vibes. The Danger-Track is whatever your ST tells you it is.

                            That's the thing with H5 and W5: There isn't just this disdain for the lore/worldbuilding of previous editions but also this aversion to work with the system (as introduced by V5). Like, if you think the interaction between Hauglosk and Chronicle Tenets is awkwardly handled in W5... guess how H5 handled Chronicle Tenets...? Of course, it didn't. Chronicle Tenets are a waste of space in the H5 corebook with no relevance. Basic Session Zero stuff already covers the Chronicle Tenets stuff. The point of Chronicle Tenets was to mechanically integrate aspects of Session Zero into the game. The moment you remove that, the concept of Chronicle Tenets is pointless. But that's why I talk about disdain for the system... Both with H5 and W5 the creative move was often to just remove bits and pieces and replace them with nothing. Not change it or replace it but just remove it.

                                    Accelerationism is a good point of reference for that. The term comes up two times in the book (and it's interesting when it does):
                                    • The Black Furies Tribe section (pg 61): "At their most extreme, Black Furies can even become accelerationists, consumed by infinite campaigns of bloody vengeance. Why wait for the crypto farm to waste a small nation’s GDP in energy costs when you can wreck the mining operation before a bunch of techbro choads can buy new Teslas? Fuck Tesla anyway, since most of the power for their “green” electrical cars comes from fossil fuels. And fuck you for doubting."
                                    • The Black Spiral Dancers section (pg 277): "What Do They Want: From the Black Spiral Dancer perspective, the Apocalypse is over, Gaia is dead, the Garou lost, and they are now simply nursing the corpse of their spirit-mother. The Wyrm has done its duty, and all that’s left is for the remains to decay. In this respect, Black Spiral Dancers are perverse accelerationists trying to hasten the End of It All, so that the cosmological cycle can renew. Those Garou still loyal to Gaia are obstacles preventing the wheel from turning — and thus must those Garou be destroyed.​"
                                    The basic idea of accelerationism is that things getting worse is a necessary stepstone to things getting better. Stuff like "the night is darkest before the dawn". There are many variants of accelerationism because what does "getting worse" mean exactly ("getting worse" for who and how, to be more specific) and what is this "better world" that is envisioned here... For example, the Christchurch-mass-shooter considered himself an "accelerationist.

                                    The irony is that by not delving too deep and trying to stay neutral somewhat, they made things more complicated actually. There is a broad spectrum of things like "accelerationism". What W5 does is ignore the substance of the issue. They were so concerned about people thinking that capitalism is bad (despite invoking "capitalocene era" at the very beginning) that they had a whole sidebar trying to make clear that capitalist companies aren't inherently bad. But apparently, there was no concern for carelessly throwing around a term like "accelerationism".

                                    And let's not forget that section at the end that invites the player to play around with Hauglosk by playing "ecofasicst characters".

                                    What's also relevant here is how W5 is deemphasizing the Umbra. Where before you could take a lot of issues related to WtA into the realm of fantasy and allegory, now the game is pushing werewolf-PCs to be warriors for Gaia mainly in the real world. Also, the removal of Wyrm-Sense invites the idea of wrongful killings - in addition to the general lack of knowledge of PCs when it comes to the setting.

                                    W5 would've been better off choosing a particular political take on the WtA-setting... I don't think anybody on the political spectrum likes to see such political ideas being invoked while feigning such a surface-level political understanding. At the end of the day, politics is about communication and what this W5 corebook is doing is an example of bad communication. Terms are thrown around in a way that neither adds anything interesting nor reflects an interesting perspective from the writers on the subject.

                                    If anything, the W5 corebook is sure about one thing: What it isn't about. The book has made very clear what the writers disliked about the Garou - but the positive pitch for why you would want to play them is missing.

                                        Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post

                                        Pretty much yeah, the obvious question is why arnt the garou blowing up pipelines over first nation territory or launching campaigns of extermination against the spirals when the cult almost certainly are.

                                        it has some worrying implications irl where i find it increasingly difficult not to justing not engaging in at least industrial sabotage to a degree.
                                        The issue is that in W5 wants the PCs in a very specific narrative and wants to position them as "punks" who are trying to fix or oppose "the Establishment." So there was an intentional design choice to make the Tribes and their leaderships fuckups so that the PCs can oppose them. Note that setting up Garou society and its leaders up as "the Establishment" (or at least part of it) that PCs are fighting against is a huge change from the earlier editions where "the Establishment" the PCs were opposing was the concept of modern industrial society in general (with more specific groups like Pentex serving as something the PCs could oppose directly).

                                        In W5, the Tribes got together, held a big moot, and decided not to go off crusading and fighting the Wyrm. The only group that did, the Fenrir, are presented as clear cut bad guys, even moreso than the Black Spirals. At the same time however, the game also reinforces this idea that the leadership of the Tribes is out of touch and wrong for allowing the Apocalypse to happen as well as for not fighting hard enough. So the PCs are in this weird narrative situation where, unlike the Tribes, you're supposed to fight the Wyrm, but also only on a part-time basis, rather than dedicating yourself to the fight like the Cult of Fenris.

                                        So yeah, it's problematic that the Garou Tribes aren't doing the things you mention, but that was an intentional design choice. The Tribes and their leadership is supposed to come off as out-of-touch screwups, so that the punk-PCs get to rage at them for screwing everything up so badly while the Cult of Fenris serves as a warning for what happens if you take the fight too far in the other direction.

                                        I think it's a bizarre design choice, but it is what it is.

                                                This is part of that "grant theory" Justin Achilli had about WOD5E. For example, to him, V5 is about playing young Anarch Neonates who oppose the corrupt Camarilla and the evil Sabbat while they're mostly just busy surviving each night. For him it was about that - and only that. Gothic punk, street-level-only... he had a very specific way of playing WOD5E in mind and then he applied that same "You play a young Anarch"-idea to all the splats. If they follow his directions on M5, for example, it will be all about playing rebellious young Tradition-mages who oppose the older mages (who are losers) and battling the super-evil Technocracy.

                                                But how do the Fenrir fit into this...? They don't actually. If you compare it to the general formula I mentioned before: Are the Fenrir against the status quo? Check. Do they think the Garou-Nation has failed? Check. Do they think Garou-Elders can't be trusted? Check. Are they opposed to the evil forces in the setting (in this case, the Wyrm)? Check. Without the heavy-handed descriptions of the Fenrirs' particular evil they would fit very neatly into the pattern of the "young Anarch"-model for the PCs of WOD5E. But the whole Fenrir-thing is only there because the other main-developer for the book, Karim Muammar, seems to have been obsessed with the idea of "Get of Fenris=Nazis" and making that a central element of W5. In the end, it was toned down from the way he talked about when they were working on the Hunters Entertainment version but you can still see the traces of that idea in the book (and in the way Hauglosk is described).

                                                And now it's up to the Renegade-writing-team to make sense of this. If the Deepest of Wounds adventure with its torture/brainwashing-stuff is anything to go by, this is gonna be a wild ride...
