                        Vampire is a game whose setting has lots of secrets, and a big part of people's interest in the activities of NPCs is that we learn about those secrets. We're better understanding the Jyhad. When done right, that provides us information STs can use in the game. Unfortunately all often it just becomes a form of ersatz fiction that isn't playable. But at least it explains why people are so interested in "lore".

                        Werewolf is not a game which has many secrets. There is not some hidden Werewolf Jyhad going on. When Garou have conflicts, it's for fairly clear and obvious reasons involving issues of resources, prestige, personal animosity, and so on. Learning about NPC Garou isn't providing us information we can use in the game. In so far that the game has any secrets, its the individual plots of their enemies that the PCs have to find out and thwart.

                        Furthermore, while Vampires are static creatures that do not age, the Garou do. We can talk about Kevin Jackson becoming Prince because Jackson and all the other vampires in Chicago By Night are essentially still the same. He's still the same Kevin Jackson we remember. The setting slightly updates, like a comic book superhero series.

                        The Garou move on. The 16 year old kid that Evan Heals the Past was in 1992 is now 44 years old. He's unrecognizable as that small, scrawny kid. Albrecht and Mari are in their fifties - still vigorous and active as Garou, but in very different roles. An update of Rage Across New York would make it almost unrecognizable from it's 1992 origins. The Legendary Wendigo Ahroun, Cries-in-the-Wind, is probably dead. So would Mother Larissa who was Garou back in the Great Depression. And Alani Astarte. As is Accolon (although he was mentioned in Rite of Passage, not RANY) The intriguing conspiracy to seize control from the Bone Gnawers by Spotlight and Zachary Ellison? Way past resolved by now. It either succeeded or failed. The "mystery" behind Aeschylus, Arn Guth Stormbright, and Bladetooth also resolved. As would be the attempt of the Shadow Lords to seize the Caern of the Sentinel. Even a fair number of Garou who wouldn't be dead of old age would now be fallen in battle, or moved to different septs. You wouldn't be updating the setting so much as creating an entirely brand new one, making the occasional reference to the few NPCs still around. Unless you are someone with the skill of Alexandre Dumas, you can't pull off a Twenty Years After, Ten Years Later approach. And that's if you are treating it as fiction instead of an RPG (which you shouldn't). RANY had a very large number of hooks buried within it. Despite its flaws, an ST can do a lot with it. Any update would just erase all of these hooks, but not effectively replace them.

                        Now I won't say there can't be interesting "fan fiction" done with it. It'd be interesting to read that Spotlight killed Mother Larissa, and Zachary Ellison became sept leader; or that before he died Cries-in-the-Wind lead the Wendigo, Uktena, and Red Talons taking over the Sept of the Hand of Gaia and subjugating or expelling the Children of Gaia and Black Furies there. Or that Aeschylus survived, came back and slew his father in a Challenge, and took control of the Sept of the Sentinel and successfully defeated the Shadow Lord attempt to invade that caern. That could be interesting and lead to fun hooks. But the problem is no matter what you do, it's going to conflict with other people's "fan fiction" - what they established in their own chronicle continuities. Or it might just not be interesting to them. It's also highly unlikely for it to be interesting because the most interesting results are probably the ones the writers could never do in published material either because it creates too much disconnect from setting expectations or for OOC reasons. There is no chance Aeschylus is ever going to be used as a character again. Wendigo and Uktena aren't going to depicted as oppressive tyrants. You're not going to show one of the iconic urban Garou, the Bone Gnawers, not be in charge of the Sept of the Green.

                        I don't think updating the Garou is going to work. If such a book is going to succeed, I think it'll have to be about talking about the villains and other concepts mentioned, but never properly developed in the game. It won't be fan fiction. It'll give us something to use in games. It'll be playable.
                        • Show us how the Seventh Generation survived their defeat in NYC, and continues to struggle against the Garou. Ditch the ridiculous political commentary that marred it in RANY, and turn it into an interesting occult conspiracy worthy of a Delta Green sourcebook.
                        • Show us how Van Gelding's financial empire survived, and has mutated into some other enemy still fighting the Garou. The game needs another corporate antagonist outside of PENTEX with different aims and goals.
                        • Finally develop the Wagnerians and give us insight into how we can create a new kind of chronicle dealing with them. What entities did they find in their journeys that can populate a Deep Umbra chronicle, and what did they find and inadvertently bring back with them that now imperils Gaia?
                        • Give an interesting depiction of the Man Eaters that can be used as a significant antagonist. Show how Bonejuice lead the secret survivors of the Dandelion Hill Sept and elsewhere so that the camp survived, and regrew in strength, and how they can be used as both obvious antagonists and secret conspirators within septs. After all, if half of the Bone Gnawers are Rabble, how hard it could be not to spread there and remain secret?
                        • Detail one of the Fomori Families in the Appalachia, and how they created a vast network of kin and gained power so they are a significant local threat to the Garou now.
                        • What about the cult of Hakaken and the Shadow Lords that worship him? How do they do that? What benefits do they get? How can they hide and escape notice?
                        • Have the Typhon spirit succeed in its quest to free itself from Grandfather Thunder, establish a second Wyrm tribe, and one of its leading members rally the survivors of Baba Yaga's armies so there is a new and powerful Wyrm threat in Russia. Tie in the other mentions of the Typhon spirit and its fomori mentioned in Freak Legion into a single coherent faction.
                        • The Fianna Ossian from Kindred Most Wanted was said to be actively hunted by other Garou. He had a travelling companion, Blanca, who was a BSD disguised as a Glass Walker, and she was supposed to have a metis child with him for unknown reasons, but important to the Wyrm. Have Blanca succeed and bear that metis. What has that metis been doing? What things has he done, or could do in the future?
                        • Create a group of Ronin who succeed in creating some kind of community distinct from the Garou. Make it large enough so that it can't be destroyed, but make it clear there's still lots of Ronin outside of it. Make it a complex and interesting faction that isn't an outright enemy, but still a threat to the Garou by its mere existence as an alternate group for new cubs to join.
                        • Revisit the Gamor from Ways of the Wolf, and make them a credible antagonist faction for the game.
                        • It doesn't need to all be bad guys. Revisit the Valkenburg Institute and reintroduce the concept of Lunatics back into the game and how they can be an interesting part of the setting. And not just have Lunatics be humans who have not changed. Go into Lon Chaney Wolfman territory - have them go into Glabro or Lupus, but not be in control, or driven by their Beast. Give us various tools and options and how PCs can possibly help them, or permanently fail, or as a long term story device.
                        • Have DNA make some kind of spectacular breakthrough, and how that can change the setting while not making it gonzo. Return it to its roots of a modern hunter instead of as part of the Weaver.
                        • Do something interesting with the Chimera monster (not the spirit) which was in the original corebooks anf first Storyteller's Handbook, and then just dropped.
                        • Build some specific and interesting spirit faction for use in the Cyberrealm that would make that realm interesting to visit, or how it is expanding into the real world (or any other Near Realm; though I think the Cyberrealm has the most potential). That would add another interesting faction.
                        • Describe some of the monstrous banes kept imprisoned by the Bane Tenders; some that escaped and built cults and fomori around it; are examples of those banes in the Old World that were not imprisoned but would have been ones the Uktena would have done so had they been in the Old World.
                        • Detail one of the other Wyrm cults into a permanent and interesting faction. Build an interesting cult and story around the Pretanic Keys and Pretanic Order.
                        • Not seeing enough of "X" in the Werewolf setting, and think there should be? Create a new and interesting faction of Garou (in one tribe, multi-tribal movement) to represent that, or provide details to existing groups that could fill that role. Give them reasons to create conflicts within the Garou Nation so that it can be used to create plot. Many interesting potential factions were mentioned, but never properly developed because they were just an idea - as opposed to real characters with real organizations behind them. Too much was left to the STs to do, so these groups are actually lackluster and probably underutilized.
                        • Actually take the time and effort to create NPCs of the Judges of Doom camp of Shadow Lord who go around holding star chamber trials and execute "criminals" of the nation; and the other Garou of other tribes who oppose them. How does the Order of Our Merciful Mother work both as a camp and as part of the Catholic Church? A lot of camps are basically political factions and not true organizations, so there's not a whole lot that is needed outside of personalities. Those aren't needed in a published sourcebook unless they are driving a particular new story that has lots of potential and uses. But there are a lot of camps which are organizations and have very specific agendas, are secret conspiracies, or obviously intended as villains, and these are the camps that need more development - Loki's Smile, Bringers of Eternal Peace, Mechanical Awakening, Bitter Hex, Grey Raptors, Society of Bitter Frost, etc. If given more details about them, STs are more likely to use them in game, and be inspired to do more now that they have actual examples.
                        Anyone can determine the fate of King Albrecht, Mari Cabrah, Darius Winchester, Guttooth, Atahualpa "Blood of the Incas", or any other canonical or signature characters. It's easy to do. Having official answers is not only lazy, it is disruptive to existing chronicles that answered those questions already are now "invalidated" by the new material. Going back to existing concepts that were lost in the shuffle, or never properly given bones and muscles is actually helpful, and will expand the setting. It provides new mysteries and challenges to be uncovered, and resurrects old ones that were forgotten or ignored for more immediate threats in the setting.

                        There's a lot of interesting concepts, hooks, and seeds mentioned in various books, especially the early ones, and then dropped and never developed. They are unlikely to have been used by many people, and most are just forgotten. In many cases, you can even assume the PCs defeated whatever antagonist mentioned in a Rage book, but resurrect that concept somehow without invalidating the actions of the PCs. Go back to those and bring them back to the game.

                                It sounds that you like RPGs to be used as literature. You want serialized stories that tell you what happens next to your favorite characters. No problem with that - I like comic books and other forms of serial fiction too - but that's not an RPG or an RPG sourcebook.

                                I want my RPG sourcebooks to be something I can use in game, not an inferior format of presenting fiction. If I want fiction about vampires, werewolves, or what not, I already have really good options for less cost.

                                I want toolkits that inspire me with ideas and provide me with tools I can use in game to create the kind of stories I want. I don't want to read what some other ST has decided to do with their chronicle, and then be told that is now the official version. That kind of material is what pushes me away from buying sourcebooks. Why would I care about Evan's quest if I never used that NPC in my chronicles, or what the official line about Albrecht's fate is if I already determined that in my own chronicles or if a PC in one of my chronicles became the king instead?

                                Obviously "RPGs as literature" works well to boost sales as people rush out to buy to learn what happens in their game... for a while. But it drives out other players who decide they only need the books they already bought. And with each new "RPG as literature" book is pushed, more and more players drop out. In the medium and long term, I don't think it's a formula for success. Do you want an expanding fan base, or do you want a declining one that buys whatever you push out (for a while)? There were a lot of RPGs in the nineties who thought the latter strategy was a gold mine, and it didn't end up well for them. Maybe I'm wrong and that is a profitable path forward. But even if it is, it won't be something I'll be spending my money on. On the other hand, I think if a sourcebook came out that explore new ideas and presented new NPCs, new organizations, new details, new plots that STs can use, then that'd be something both of us would buy.
