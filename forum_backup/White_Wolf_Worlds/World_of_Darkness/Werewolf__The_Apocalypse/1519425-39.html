                Originally posted by Knightingale View Post
                H5 just continues to double down on that perspective from the Second Inquisition book mentioned previously with its fundamentally negative outlook on hunter-orgs. You get this from the Lexicon right at the start: "Org: A formal organization that exists to hunt monsters as a means to an an end rather than for its own sake; orgs often support a status quo situation or preserve the power of elites (or themselves), and are generally larger and better resourced than individual cells​" The bit about hunter-orgs actually just reaffirming the status-quo is particularly interesting if you think about it. After all, if that cynical conception of hunter-orgs were true and hunter-orgs actually don't make a difference then that makes one aspect of WOD5E very peculiar: There's a thing called a Second Inquisition - so what about the first one? According to H5's conception there's no way it could've had an impact and yet obviously it did have an impact on vampire-society. So hunter-orgs can make a difference. And even in V5, you have the whole thing with Vienna. But according to H5 they are bad and ineffective and only local Hunter-cells of Hunters with Drive can be good but similar to how the young Garou-PCs in W5 aren't positioned to make changes beyond their street-level setting, the only-hunter-cells-are-good-idea of H5 similarly restricts what it allows mortal hunters to do.

                Also, in describing the hunter-org-types it also just doubles down on the fundamentally bad nature of hunter-org-hunters. Like this description for religious hunter-orgs: "Religious orgs tend to have small but dedicated — even fanatical — memberships with rigid, fundamental outlooks. As with Governmental orgs, they attract support from those drawn to authoritarian figureheads and those who would inflict violence on others because an influential leader told them to do so; it’s simply a religious authority as opposed to a secular one, and originates with a divine purpose instead of a temporal one… even if the results end up looking very similar.​" Rigid outlooks, drawn to authoritarian figureheads... Wait, where have I heard that before... Oh, right, the Cult of Fenris... I mean, if we're going by H5 and W5, the WOD5E-setting seems to be just chock full of lovers of authoritarianism. I can already picture someone making a pitch for M5: "And of course, the big antagonist are the Technocracy who are very bad and..." I interrupt "Stop right there. Let me guess: They love authoritarianism."

                There's another striking similarity between H5 and W5: the vague history. Reckoning is even an actual concept in H5. But similarly to the vagueries in W5 with the Garou-Nation and Age of Apocalypse, Reckoning has the same whiff of "Who knows". There's a sidebar called "What is the Reckoning?" and it says this: "Ask a dozen Hunters, get a dozen answers. “The Reckoning” has entered Hunter parlance as of late as a shorthand that conveys the same sort of awareness as many modern social-justice movements.​ [...] Ultimately, the Reckoning is the struggle against supernatural abuse and domination, and it is now.​" It's never explained how or why Hunters with Drive is a thing. This sidebar just makes it even weirder because this Reckoning-concept gets this nebulous time-window of "as of late" and "it is now". It just happened. Like the Age of Apocalypse or the fall of the Garou-Nation. It just happened. Makes you wonder what the lore/worldbuilding for V5 would've been like if the same creative team that worked on W5 and H5 would've led development for V5...
                I mean it's a punk game. In what punk game is the federal government meant to be the defender of the weak and protector of the innocent?

                Mind you, I've known people who say, "Werewolf isn't Gothic Punk!"

                And I'm like, "Yeah, it really is."

                Honestly, this is why the Second Inquisition book denying any chance to play the other side bothers me. Humanity has, after EONS, gotten an upper hand. Humanity finally can be a bit safer. And then the writing treats this as a purely bad thing.​
                I mean, humanity is behind Pentex.

                The Wyrm is the people providing the ingredients but the supernatural isn't what makes the World of Darkness a hellhole. It's humans who gain supernatural powers misuse them the same way humans misuse capitalism, guns, and corruption in the real world. The Second Inquisition is a vile organization because it's committing inhuman atrocities on people because they're different, not because they have any interest in protecting humanity.

                Because, again, punk.

                It's great satire on the War on Drugs and War on Terror (which barely get mentioned despite obvious inspiration). "Yes, there are bad people here but we'll spend 50 billion dollars trying to wipe out drugs rather than care for dying children. Because wars are sexy."

Certainly doesn't sound very punk to me...

                            Originally posted by Psicose View Post
                            Someone know how are the Bone Gnawers in W5?
                            They are essentially the "Nosferatu" of the Garou now. It's all about information-gathering for them. There's still a bit about poverty/low social status but you get this vague paragraph about that:
                            It’s a classic chicken-and-egg story. Were the Bone Gnawers always at the bottom, so they adapted to the tricks and trades of the bottom? Or were they so good at skullduggery and dirt-finding that they were pushed out of “polite society” by colluding pipe-smokers and​ rhyme-saying furhead “nobles”? Who cares? Because when it comes down to it, who’s got the goods? The rangy, shadow-slinking wolf who knows how to creep where creeping nets the most information, or some bespectacled ponytail with elbow patches on his tweed jacket?​
                            Their Patron Spirit is still Rat and their Favor is one of the vaguer ones with "A Bone Gnawer can add a die to a dice pool used to find something lost or overlooked.​". The 'overlooked' bit is weird because if it's overlooked you wouldn't know it's there so how can you find something you don't know it's there. If it's overlooked by somebody else, does that mean the bonus only applies if somebody else has actually overlooked it before? Their Ban is similarly broad and vague: "If a Bone Gnawer passes up a significant opportunity to give the disadvantaged a leg up, they regain only a single point of Willpower at the beginning of the next session, instead of their Composure or Resolve rating.​" There again, "give the disadvantaged a leg up" and "significant opportunity"... certainly stuff the ST and the player should clear up beforehand what this exactly means.

It's street-level-only - and don't leave your local environment.

                                                And W5 carries over similar sentiments as it wants the good kind of Garou to just settle down in a caern and just stay there. On one side you have the Garou-Nation as a symbol of failure and defeat and on the other, you have the Cult of Fenris as a symbol of how anger and adventurism are fundamentally bad. You sit in your caern and you stay there. And occasionally you solve local street-level-only problems.

                                                I mentioned adventurism because that's the thing about WtA... What Garou aspire to be is heroes and those don't just stay in a local environment. Quests, adventures, missions... all sorts of heroic endeavors to go beyond the local setting. But W5 isn't a fan of that perspective. In a way, the Garou of W5 might as well team up with the H5-hunter-cells since they're just two types of neighborhood-watch. The world sucks but they can get self-righteous about what happens in their block...

                                                    On top of everything else here, I think all this shows the massive problems with trying to force Hunter and Werewolf to fit that "rage against authority within your group" mentality that V5 has, or even just the "your own group are your main antagonists" of Vampire in general.

                                                    Vampires have to deal with hunters sometimes, but most of the conflict has always come from other vampires. Vampires of a rival sect, competing for resources, politicking for power, that kind of thing. Also having to cope with how your character is a predator now, and how fast they fall into being a monster. It's a game with a very particular structure to it that isn't really found anywhere else in WoD. V5 turning into "Camarilla Bad, don't even think about playing Sabbat, Anarchs only" is overly simplistic and limiting but it at least has some kind of basis in how Vampire is usually played.

                                                    Werewolf and Hunter are games where the threat is mostly external. Werewolves have the Wyrm and Hunters have monsters that threaten humanity. Their brand of punk is about raging against other societal issues (the destruction of the planet and against those with powers above normal people that seek to prey on the downtrodden) instead of just the general "Man". So when you give lip service to the original points but then just go on about how the real bad guy is organization within these groups, things just get... really, really unfortunate. Not only because it makes these games really confusing and boring by saying the only good thing the PCs can do is sit in one place and hope problems come to them, but also because even if they're flawed, the organizations here are a lot closer to activist groups than the government, so it (accidentally? That "Not All Corporations" sidebar makes me wonder) gives off the impression that organizing for an actual cause is bad. Not just bad, but organizing for a cause basically makes you a Nazi.

                                                    EDIT: This isn't even getting into how both H5 and W5 treat "caring about your cause" as a fast-track to authoritarianism and fascism...
