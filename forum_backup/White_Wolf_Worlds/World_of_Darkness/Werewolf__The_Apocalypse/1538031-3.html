    Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post

    ....And you know what? For the exact same reason,too.

    The reason you have the cartoony villains in Captain Planet, is because the creators did not want kids to look at their parents working in polluting fields as monsters.

    W5 moves so much of its guilt away from companies and into bad apples, rather than do what previous editions did and just say the industry itself was the problem.
    I think the lack of spiritualism and Garou lore/community robs the game of a lot of opportunities to add more depth to these adventures. And this is part of the bad writing in this adventure particularly. Scent of Decay, the one adventure from the Storytellers Kit with the security guy (who's a Fomor, of course), the crazy Garou from Reclamation, the Fomor from the adventure in the corebook... in all these cases the antagonists have a backstory at least. Here, though, in Red Tundra you got this CEO of the FOMO Hunting company named Olivia Monreau with no personality whatsoever. Just a blank slate Fomor who's doing bad stuff. And really, once you get down to it, that's what's happening in all of these adventures. You have a bad guy doing bad stuff, the PCs become aware of it - and they kill the guy(s). That's all there is to it when you break it down.

    It's not just the way the adventures are set up but that Garou aren't set up in W5 to do anything else. The Umbra is entirely optional and between the old Kinfolk-definition and the Wolf-Blooded in WtF, the Kin-concept in W5 is the weakest and blandest one of those. So when Kin appear in these adventures, it just feels random. "Oh and by the way, that character is a Kin that you can recruit to your Pack.". The concept is just too broad and vague to be impactful. On one hand, it feels unearned and random who gets to be a Kin and on the other hand, it lacks the impact on the character's life of them either being Kinfolk or a Wolf-Blooded. And that in turn weakens the Garou-PCs connection to humanity. The Touchstone is just gonna be some guy the PC knows/cares about. With V5 it makes sense because it's about remaining human and with H5 it makes sense because the game's about local street-level hunter-cells. But this doesn't work in W5. We see that with the Coach-character in Red Tundra. Because his role in this adventure is to just be the damsel-in-distress who has to be kept out of trouble. And you can't get these humans involved in the combat directly because everything has to be scaled up to be a challenge for a Garou in Crinos-Form.

    Additionally, there are constant references to Garou being a danger to humans around them during their First Change and this fear of Garou going overboard in their violent attacks (resulting in collateral damage). But if you were to take that fear-mongering seriously, human allies are discouraged because then the idea would be instead to treat all humans with connections to Garou like the coach in this adventure. They would all be considered fragile things that need to be protected and kept safe. But that approach seriously limits to what degree human allies and connections can be involved in the adventures of the Garou PCs.

    Going back to the Captain Planet reference, the themes of the game become more toothless as it maneuvered its storytelling into these shallow waters. Both the first adventure in Scent of Decay and this adventure are setting up these straightforward storylines where you have a bad company with bad Fomori at the top and then the Garou-PCs come in, kill the bunch and the company gets shut down. The small towns in question are in even more trouble economically and the Bane-Spirits fled into the Umbra. The ST allows PCs to buy more Renown with XP but it's not like there are big Septs or Moots mentioned in these adventures. At most, a handful of other Garou are even aware of what the PC Pack has done.

    Non-Garou are there to be kept out of trouble and in the dark, Spirits are just a sideshow and all the PCs do is kill bad guys without any other valid alternatives being offered by the game. The game can talk about stuff like it's about "fighting capitalism" and the Age of Apocalypse is the "capitalocene era" but if all that's happening in W5 is just have an ST put bad guys in front of the PCs they then have to kill in the course of the game then there's honestly not a lot going on thematically in W5. Sure, you can dress it up and make it look better than Red Tundra by, for example, giving the antagonist some kind of backstory but from a storytelling perspective it's all about telling, not showing. Because the essential formula doesn't change. You put a bad guy in front of the PCs and expect them to kill the bad guy. You can't maintain interest in the game like that. I think in that context it makes sense to do something like what the Book of Hungry Names has done with its endings. You should be absolutely willing to break the setting and status-quo of W5 to get the story/setting into a better place. Because the core formula/status quo of W5 is boring and bland. You have your group of Garou-PC, you put a bad guy in front of them, they kill the bastard, then you tell them the world is still shit because hey, look, here's another bad guy they gotta kill. And anything that would've interrupted that cycle and would've made things more interesting has been stripped away from the game.

    To end on a more hopeful note, maybe the release of the Shattered Nation supplement will lead to them reintroducing Garou-politics in a bigger fashion since that book focuses on Moots in parts. Maybe they could release adventures again where Garou-politics matter. But right now W5 has a very narrow horizon in what is considered a standard W5 adventure.

                As we've discussed in other threads. For a game that starts with the declaration of "re-imagination, not continuation", there's way too little re-imagining happening here to make this work. You don't want the players to go into the Umbra, you don't want them to endanger humans but rather keep them out of trouble and on the sidelines and you don't want them to have epic confrontations with their enemies. Well, there's not a lot left to do if you're gonna stick to the WtA-framework that closely.

                And the anti-capitalist take also doesn't work because if you were to take that idea to its logical conclusion and with so many other avenues taken off the table, you'd have to play the Garou in W5 as outright eco-terrorists. But then of course you got the whole Hauglosk-thing and the Cult of Fenris as a refutation of that idea. So, the core-concept of W5 kinda goes nowhere. It's actually a very similar problem to what you have in H5. That corebook is way too invested in explaining why hunter-orgs and everybody who works for them are bad. But then you follow that core-concept to its logical conclusion and you got a local hunter-cell taking out a monster and... now what? If you stick to the corebook premise of H5, nothing is going on beyond that.

                Both H5 and W5 would be improved by just going out of your way to destroy the setting/status-quo as laid out by their corebooks. Another idea of Book of Hungry Names: Because it's the Age of Apocalypse, lift the Veil and work more closely together with humans. That's not necessarily what I would do with a WtA-games but it's at least something that injects a bit of energy back into the premise.

                Also, as an aside... remember that sidebar about animal Touchstones in the W5 corebook? Whoever wrote Red Tundra did do a little setting-breaking themselves by ignoring the corebook (that, for example, feels the need to mention repeatedly that wolves don't live as long as humans) and turning wolves into these human-like characters .
