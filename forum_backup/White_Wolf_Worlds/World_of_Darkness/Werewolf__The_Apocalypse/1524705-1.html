    Originally posted by Gryffon15 View Post
    The thing about low-Wyrm lore Garou not being able to fully comprehend the nuances of Wyrm-glyphs even if they can more or less recognize the Black Spiral glyph and could theoretically reproduce it (even if this might be looked askance at by other Garou) is actually interesting imo and offers some compelling ideas for things which might be further developed upon, regardless of how one feels about the new W5 glyphs.

    The notion of fleshing out how the Garou can use their glyphs/language, both for practical purposes, but its social and political implications is very interesting imo. It might be something worthwhile to look for inspiration from irl beliefs about the spiritual/magical powers of writing systems. Perhaps the reason why it would be looked at with suspicion to know the Black Spiral rune is that merely being able to read it, much less reproduce it, really does provide an extra route by which a Garou might become the subject of Wyrm influence or molestation. One could further flesh this out as part of what Older Brother/Ghost Council and the Shadow Lords do which make other Garou somewhat uneasy; they learn the fallen tongues and do expose themselves to serious risks in exchange for the knowledge and power which that brings.

    Leaving the glyphs themselves aside, I do think there’s room there for a development of that concept for in-setting uses.
    A very similar situation came up when there were some Gaians interacting with a Wyrm-glyph in the W20 novel, The Poison Tree, which also illustrates one of my favorite parts of glyphs — their spiritual resonance:

    Originally posted by The Poison Tree, page 59

    “Look at this.”

    She tapped a claw on the polished surface of the table. Symbols had been carved there, where Eleanor could see them. There were a several more strange glyphs, similar to the garou’s written language but there was something about the odd curves and jagged lines that both intrigued Ingrid and repelled her. They surrounded a larger symbol: a crude set of nested circles, like a target. Or perhaps an attempt at a spiral?

    “There is a message here,” the Theurge mused. “But what it means is beyond me.”

    “Do you recognize the symbols?” Ingrid asked.

    Anna sniffed. “Spirit glyphs,” she observed. “Symbols representing potent mystical truths.”

    “Such as?”

    The garou shaman’s expression turned guarded, as all Theurges do when prodded with questions about the spirit realm. “I don’t know. And I’m not sure I want to know.” She shook her head. “I don’t like the way they…call to me.”

    Ingrid scowled at the glyphs. She reached down and, one by one, scratched through each symbol. She was surprised – and more than a little unsettled – at how hard it was to bring herself to do it, but the last thing she wanted was to let some fool of a mortal get a good look at them.​

        A thing with W5 is very often with lore is that things have been removed without any care for what that does to the lore/worldbuilding of the game. Any good idea you might get from what W5 is doing is 90% the reader adding their own ideas. Like the idea of forbidden glyphs as mentioned by Yakecen-Sings : It wouldn't actually quite work with the tone the Loresheet is striking in the W5 corebook. First, this is the reason where this argument arises that the W5 corebook allows you to redeem Black Spiral Dancers but not Cult of Fenris members. There's a strangely collegial tone in how Wyrm-followers are talked about on that Loresheet. For example, the two-dot-bit talks about your PC being friends with a "remarkably stable Fomor", a Black Spiral Dancer or a vampire (I guess the writer there forgot there wasn't supposed to be any inherent connection between vampires and the Wyrm anymore). From there you learn about Wyrm-follower-specific hideouts you can use and other stuff. The Loresheet is about your character being close to Wyrm-followers with the five-dot being the ability to redeem a Black Spiral Dancer by convincing them to "emancipate themselves" (that's how the Loresheet phrases it). And the drawback is always that other Garou will like you less for using Wyrm-affiliated resources. But that's all. After all, Wyrm-taint isn't a thing in W5.

                                            There's a huge irony in going down that road, I think. Let's assume you can use the W5-setting to justify it: The Garou-Nation has fallen, the Litany is a joke (either it's Elders exploiting it or young Garou ignoring/disrespecting it), it's the age of Apocalypse and Gaia's dead/dying. And in all that turmoil the lines between friend and enemy aren't so clear anymore. And there's no more "Kill the minions of the Wyrm on sight!"-policy anymore for Garou - and that's what gets you that kind of Loresheet.

                                            If all of that is true, the huge irony is that the Cult of Fenris are the good guys then. They are absolutely right to look down on the other Garou after all. The Cult of Fenris are the only Garou still invested in being the warriors of Gaia. At least, they're trying to take the fight to their enemies. Even ignoring the torture/brainwashing-scene in the Deepest of Wounds adventure, the Cult of Fenris venturing out to fight the Wyrm with other volunteers from other Tribes is portrayed as misguided and pointless... but on the other hand, what exactly did the Garou who stayed behind accomplish in contrast? They sat in their caern, protected it while the world is getting worse and worse and... that's about it.

                                            Like you said, W5 is setting up a scenario where survival is the highest thing to aspire to. And I guess in light of that, maybe it isn't so hard to imagine that your Garou-characters are just gonna start hanging out with BSD-characters because... who cares.
