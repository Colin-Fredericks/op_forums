    Ok, the forum ate my long post so here is the short version:
    1. DW Yalden in History of British Mammals (1999) states that black rats were in Britain by the Roman period with the earliest finds in London dating to the 3rd century CE.
    2. Kim Aaris-Sørensen in Fossils and Strata, Diversity and Dynamics of the Mammalian Fauna in Denmark (2009) states that the earliest finds of black rats in Denmark date to the Medieval period (p.16) but that they should be expected to be seen during the Roman period as well. Brown rats are found at even later dates, but appear in the record in Schleswig-Holstein around 800 CE.
    3. McCormick, in Rats, Communications, and Plague: Toward an Ecological History, Journal of Interdisciplinary History (2003) states that mt-DNA testing of black rat remains shows that they followed the spread of Rome across Europe (p.1)
    4. Kovacs, in Dispersal History of an Invasive Rodent in Hungary, Acta Zoologica Academiae Scientiarum Hungaricae 58 (4), pp. 379–394, 2012, states that the black rat followed the Romans through Europe and spread beyond the limes, with the earliest dates of black rat remains in Slovenia from 1100-800 BCE. He also cites other authors that discuss this spread: (Ervynck 2002, Audoin-Rouzeau & Vigne 1994, Armitage et al. 1984, Armitage 1994).

    So that's a few things I found trying to keep to scholarly sources.
