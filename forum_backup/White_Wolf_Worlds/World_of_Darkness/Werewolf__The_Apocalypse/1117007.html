    Cyanide's Werewolf: The Apocalypse video game

    07-16-2017, 07:32 PM
    Some more information which is disappointing in some places and good elsewhere.

    E3 2017 Interview â€” Werewolf: The Apocalypse
    http://aggrogamer.com/article/5841/E3-2017-Interview--Werewolf-The-Apocalypse
    At E3 I was able to sit down and gather more information on Werewolf: The Apocalypse from Julien Desourteaux. Here's what I was able to get on Werewolf: The Apocalypse.


    * You will be Ronin
    * You will be able to join different factions but that won't change the story
    * Pentex is the primary antagonist
    * A major theme is controlling your rage as you'll be able to kill friend and foe alike in frenzies.

      Cutting Glabro and Hispo from the game probably merits a mention as a major thing to consider. It's a mixed one to me. On one hand, it's hard to imagine WtA without all five forms being a thing but I can see from a game-play perspective trying to find a role for all five could be tough (it's not like Forsaken where they did more to ensure that each form has a specific built in-situation they're best for).

      The Ronin vs. Pentex highly linear story seems like it's going to be an issue no matter how good the game play ends up. The Rage mechanics could be really good, but that doesn't seem like something to sell a whole video game on. The idea that they made sure to pull in dialog options as things that can stoke your Rage is good, but with a highly linear story Frenzies are either going to end the game, or the impact dies because no matter how many friends and innocents you kill, the plot will get pushed forward.

                          I don't mind linear stories in video games in general. But it's a problem if they present things that imply consequences of choice, because those choices don't end up having much effect. Bloodlines was very linear, but plenty of player choices matter. How you deal with the Gargoyle makes a difference towards your future relationship with the Tremere, even if it doesn't effect the endings.

                          And again, the point is the combination. Something like the Rage mechanical ideas are lost on a highly linear game. It's not that linear games are bad, but that they're bad for specific mechanical elements to be added to them.

                                                  One of the best supplements of all time.

                                                  Ronin are great characters because they're the mercenaries of the Garou Nation that have to live off the grid and make private backroom deals with packs and septs to take the worst jobs. You're an exile and a deniable asset in the complicated games of the Garou politics. It's a good premise for a single-player game as you never have to be caught up in pack or local politics but can go wherever the ST wants you. When you're a Ronin pack, though, you have a LOT more interesting potential missions too as suddenly you're a threat.

                                                  It's also great for incorporating the greater WOD as Ronin can end up working for vampires, Pentex, Mages, and so on.

                                                  Your goal isn't protecting Gaia, it's survival.

                                                  Ronin games also have the benefit of allowing you to potentially have a more "human" view of the world like if you reject Gaian beliefs or want to just protect the local human population versus engage in a eternal war against Satan.

                                                  The fact I often play Ronin games may be why I often forget the Skindancers are villains in my games since I've often used them in allied or friendly roles despite how intrinsically horrifying they are.
