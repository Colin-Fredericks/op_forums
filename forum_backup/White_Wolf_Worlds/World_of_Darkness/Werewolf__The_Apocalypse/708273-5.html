    I think the Time of Kings is interesting*, but I don't think it makes sense for the Mokole to be non-crocodiles. First of all, AFAIK plesiosaurs were all huge, so having one as an animal form means your Archid form is crazy giant... also they lose continuity with the Mokole if they don't have any crocodile or turtle forms. How did they survive for long enough to give them the time to change every one of them to breeding with new species of reptiles? I always thought the Mokole back then were crocodiles and sea crocodiles (and turtles and sea turtles, I guess) and their archid forms were dinosaurs and flying pterosaurs and (non-crocodile) sea reptiles and bird-dinos. My suggestion is to replace the plesiosaurs with crocodiles and sea-crocodiles, and ... I don't know. Maybe the origin of the Ao is tied to the change from Mnesis-as-a-place to Mnesis-as-a-curse? Or else just include the Ao... in other words the "varni" would be big categories instead of individual species: ao (turtle), suchid (crocodilian), pterosaur, and (relatively small) dinosaur.

    *I should be stoked and excited because this aspect of the Mokole is awesome. I honestly don't know why I'm so blah... maybe it's all this danged rain. Please pretend that I'm excited and jumping up and down. It's great that this is going in the book.


    One other thing... I hope the Drachid are portrayed as therapod dinosaurs with tails and horizontal posture, not human-shaped. Dinosaur skeletons make a human posture physically impossible, and I think reptoids look... silly.


    Edit: Forgot to add: The names El-Kaataar, Alpeseak, and Tkash might be copywrite infringement. I don't know where El-Kaataar and Tkash came from, but Alpeasak (correct spelling IIRC), "yilane", and most of the continent names in the Mokole Breedbook were taken from the Eden trilogy by Harry Harrison. Some other names and words (Sal-Afsan the Farseer, "heesh", "whileelin", "quintaglio") in the Breedbook were taken from other Scifi books. I don't know if they were taken with permission in the first place.

                        Clearer? Probably not. Mokole are to Dragon Kings as Bunyip are to Garou. Still werewolves, but they've lost some things, and gained others. Edit ended.

                        Thirdly, in my (biased) opinion, it makes more sense for Dragon Kings to be dinosaurs (and for this post, I'm using 'dinosaur' to include pterosaur and plesiosaur) than crocodilians and turtles. Dinosaurs existed from early in the Mesozoic and endured throughout. Turtles appeared about half way through the Mesozoic, and Crocs only appeared in the last 20 million years or so. By the time these animals appeared, Dragon Kings were well and truly established. They didn't need to be crocs etc. Also, what Dragon King would want to be a puny crocodilian? Being that small would be some kind of punishment, compared to their glorious dinosaur forms.

                        They didn't need time. The change in animal breeds was Gaia practically wiping the slate clean and starting again millions of years later with smaller creatures. Ananasi and Rokea endured through the ages (Rokea bred themselves into downsizing as years turned ever onwards). Gaia tinkered with the basic design of the surviving shapeshifters, adding additional forms, and opening up the potential for them to have a common breeding partner in the Drachid-like creatures she envisioned in her dreams. She also elevated a whole new slew of shapeshifters, all of which were smaller than the Dragon Kings.

                        And then she had the fallen Dragon Kings, which she had cursed to ever remember what their folly had wrought. They no longer had dinosaurs to breed with, so she changed them as they slept. They had new forms (and had lost some old ones), and bred with new creatures, but they endured, and the memory endured with them. After all, a curse to remember for all time isn't very curse-y if the cursed ones just sleep through it and then die off. The Mother's anger wasn't going to subside in a few dozen million years.

                                                Yeah, to echo CeltSPZ I like most of what I read, but the crocodilians are just as old as dinosaurs, and got large at an earlier time. I like the idea of including dinosaurs and pterosaurs, please don't get me wrong. I understand not including turtles. I'm excited about this regardless, and my concern about the saurid varnas is relatively minor. But crocodyliformes, pseudosuchians, and sea crocodiles were big and really cool back then too. (Plesiosaurs with their small heads don't impress me as much as pliosaurs and sea-crocodiles).

                                                Mesozoic crocodylomorphs evolved in the Triassic, rapidly got rather large, and some even ate medium-sized dinosaurs. They included fully terrestrial ziphodont forms (with tyrannosaur-like teeth and relatively long legs for running fast) and fully-marine forms of pliosaur size, in addition to amphibious forms resembling to modern crocodilians. If you cast the net a little wider (and why not when dinosaurs and pterosaurs are included) to look at other crurotarsans, there were rauisuchids, postosuchids, phytosaurs, ornithosuchids, and poposuchids of large size (some as large as modern crocodiles, and larger than contemporary dinosaurs and pterosaurs) in the Triassic, such as the 7-meter-long Karamuru (from the mid-Triassic, while the 3-6 m Herrerasaurus didn't evolve until the late Triassic, contemporary with 6-9 m Saurosuchus and the possibly 8-10 m Fasolasuchus). Some of these things were bipedal and resembled therapod dinosaurs, with jaws and teeth like carnosaurs.

                                                Triassic true crocodylomorphs were small, but so were most Triassic dinosaurs. (I had always thought the Kings didn't appear until the early Jurassic.) The exceptions I know of were Herrerasaurus and some prosauropods, but they lived alongside the equally large pseudosuchians. Large therapods didn't get top-predator niches until the Triassic extinctions wiped out the crurotarsan competition.

                                                Fully-shelled turtles evolved in late Triassic, and they also grew to quite large sizes like Archelon during the Mesozoic. Though to be fair CeltSPZ, the horned turtle and giant sea-turtle fossils only go back to the Cretaceous.

                                                AFAIK Pterosaurs were all quite small and lightweight (up to turkey-weight in the Cretaceous, despite their wingspans) until the Cretaceous Neoazhdarchians evolved: more "puny" than 5-10 m crocodiles.




                                                As for the "reptoids"... please at least give them tails, for the love of Gaia. I beg. Please. I absolutely cannot stand reptoid 'aliens' from bad scif, they don't in any way resemble dinosaurs. And the mortal Drachids were explicitly not shapeshifters, so making them mammal-dinosaur hybrids... please just don't. Please. The Dinosaur Kings are so awesome because they're a dinosaur civilization, not for being lizard aliens.



                                                I have presently forgotten what I knew about Mesozoic sharks. My guess is that, although smaller sharks were available, Rokea weren't interested in them. Since they can breed with any shark "varnas" without any need for a rite, they have less reason to follow specific evolutionary lineages. That said, I don't know of larger-than-modern sharks from in Mesozoic... Megalodon were post-Mesozoic.
