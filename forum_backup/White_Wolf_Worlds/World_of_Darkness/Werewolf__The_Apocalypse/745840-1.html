            Originally posted by Sergeant Brother View Post
            I've always thought that the way Gifts work is clunky. Kind of like D&D spells. I'm not sure how to go about fixing them though. I think that a lot of Gifts aren't well balanced, but fixing those is different from revamping how Gifts work. Do you have any specific ideas?
            I do, but I'm not sure I want to clog the thread too much with this. I'll do an outline, and if people are interested I can start a different thread about it.

            My basic concept is somewhat like Forsaken 2e in that there's a core Gift you buy, and then you add expansions to it.

            The basic structure of it is the following (though an important note, if I fully develop this XP costs will be increased drastically):

            There are two classifications of Gifts. Birthright Gifts are the "baseline" Gifts of the current WtA rules of the 1-2ish Rank in power. This are Gifts that are inherently part of the Garou nature (though some splats have affinities for specific ones, though this only effects chargen and Aspects, all Brightright Gifts have the same XP cost) that the spirits help unlock from within them. Deed Gifts are rewards from the spirits for dedicated service in times of peril, or for long quests, and the like (and do not cost XP, the effort of getting them should be enough).

            Mechanically, all Gifts in this structure are never "activated" any more than an arm is activated to throw a punch and do not have level ratings. These Gifts should focus on either passive supplements to standard rolls (bonus dice, diff bonuses, expansions of normal derived things like lifting, movement distances, etc.), or the acquisition of new "standard" rolls for those characters (ex: Sense [Triat] becomes just an additional part of your sensory suite as natural as smell in use). Brighright Gifts focus on core fundamental powers with an eye towards utility and splat roles in the group. Deed Gifts are generally more plot/narrative focused, and should be varied slightly based on the spirit that Gifted them. For example, a Deed Gift of Prophecy from a jaggling Lune vs. one earned from Luna herself will follow the same basic rules, but the Lune's visions are likely to be more immediate in nature (which is beneficial in that it's likely to be more useful for pushing local plot forward, but limited in larger scale plots), while Luna's visions are likely to be immense in importance and be prophecies that could span generations in importance (which is beneficial for more grander scale games, and renown, but less useful if you're hoping to divine some guidance about what's going on in your Sept).

            Deed Gifts are a key factor in allowing Birthright Gifts (and the expansions of them to be details below) to be easier to balance mechanically, because it segregates Gifts with functions that are inherently less mechanically rigorous into a category that's immediately off the table for PCs to just buy, as well as explicitly about plot device powers.

            In addition to Gifts, Birthright Gifts have Aspects. Aspects are the more overt "powers" of the game in which your characters are clearly doing something supernatural to Gifts being more easy to disguise as "just being really good" at things. Aspects are also where affinities matter the most. Selecting/buying an affinity Gift nets you a free Aspect, and additional affinity aspects (if you have overlapping ones) cost less to unlock than unlocking non-affinity Aspects. This covers the intended niche protection in RAW Gifts having affinity vs. non-affinity XP costs, as well as making overlap in affinity something that isn't an overtly sub-optimal effect.

            Each Aspect is meant to be a distillation of the broader nature of a Gift. Homids, Glasswalkers, and Bone Gnawers might all have an Affinity for a Gift of "the city" or some such, but it expresses itself differently based on the Aspect. The Homid based Aspect might be more about the cultural aspects of city life, the GW Aspect about control over the city environment, and the BG Aspect about adapting to city living.

            All Aspects are rating 1-5. These might be a suite of powers, or one power that gets consistently stronger as thematically needed. Levels in Aspects, however, are never purchased directly. They are derived from other Traits. This means Aspects can wane in power for a Garou. It also means that starting level Garou aren't locked solely to 1-dot level powers. Most starting PCs will have at least one Aspects in the 2-3 range.

            Breed based Aspects grow with Rage/Gnosis/WP as the Gift thematically dictates. Breed Aspects are generally the most straight forward with more focus on "this just gets better as it gets more powerful" like Potence in VtM, or perhaps much more tightly controlled Spheres from MtA. These are also the ones starting PCs are going to have above a level 1 rating; as it makes sense that they've spent far more time with being human/werewolf/wolf than realizing their Auspice, or joining a Tribe.

            Auspice based Aspects grow with Renown (I understand some people aren't going to like this, but this presumes a change to how Renown is handled as well). This means a very wise Theurge that hasn't had major accomplishments in battle for the Glory needed for higher rank, might still have access to surprisingly potent powers. This also gives Ragabash a bit of a bonus for their harsher Renown issues, since they can choose their starting Renown; which gives them greater flexibility.

            Tribe based Aspects grow with Rank (making them more like the current RAW in application). This is meant to help emphasize the hierarchy inherent to Garou. A Cliath Silver Fang might be a strong leader with some oomph to back that up, but an Elder Silver Fang can issue supernaturally empowered edicts that cannot be disobeyed lightly. These are going to be more dedicated to powers-per-level style structures, and their higher levels are generally going to start to outstrip the Aspects of the other groups for it (just like Potence 5 is powerful, but doesn't really hold a candle all you can do with Dominate 5). This is a purposeful part of the balancing act. Rather than balancing just on level value alone (since they aren't purchased differently anyway) some Aspects give you more upfront but less total growth, while others are gated off until later level play, but get you better stuff for it when you get there.

            While I haven't entirely decided on this, mostly because the books make no attempt to be mechanically consistent with it, Camps might provide an additional Deed Gift, an additional affinity (and I need to decide, if that's the case, if this is retroactive), or an additional Aspect. I think those could be roughly equal, and it would be interesting if all Camps(/other sub-groups) had something. It also gives something for cross-Tribal camps to orient around mechanically.

                I am very, very leery of any setting reboot, mountain of retcons, total rewrite a-la W: the Forsaken, post-Apocalypse setting, or any other wholesale rewrite. Werewolf: the Apocalypse has warts and problems, but for the most part I love the setting as it is and I would be very sad if supplements for the game and setting I love would no longer be written.


                There are minor changes I would like to see, that I think would benefit the game if done well.

                1. Clarify some things, like other posters have said, such as whether you enter breed form to sleep, or a way to make our own Totems.

                2. Pure Breed is one thing I would rewrite, because as it is it doesn't make sense IMO. Does it represent your resemblance to specific ancestors, or to some hypothetical ideal? What happens to your Pure Breed if you join a tribe you weren't born to? What happens when kin or werewolves from different tribes, who both have Pure Breed, have kids together? Why do Fianna and Silent Striders have Pure Breed that makes them resemble bizarre imaginary animals instead of the actual real wolves in Europe and Africa that they supposedly breed with? If somebody born to the Red Talons joins some other tribe, or a Lupus born to another tribe joins the Red Talons, do they have the smear of red fur or not?

                3. Rename the Metis. I hadn't thought of this idea before, but I would welcome it as a minor change. Otherwise don't change them, though.

                4. Mechancs! I would hope that any mechanical changes would be small and come with a built-in (or free online) conversion guide for previous editions so older books don't become useless, but... there are indeed problems with the original WoD mechanics that could be fixed without a big overhaul. I would like Dexterity divorced from how fast a character can run, and the form stats for shapeshifters tweaked to reflect that more realistically. It's silly for cheetahs to have +4 Dex because they need that to run fast. Dexerity (jumping, aiming a gun, stabbing with a klaive, controlling a speeding car, crafting a fetish) should not determine how fast somebody can run. If somebody is both fast and dextrous, then those stats can reflect those traits separately. But some animals (and people) are fast and not particularly dextrous, or agile but slow.

                5. Some tribes (*cough* Children of Gaia *cough*) could receive some additional material or small changes to make them more sensible, or reduce the use of dumb ethnic stereotypes or hippy dumbness. I would not want to see major changes, but small improvements and wider diversity within each tribe would be cool. I will say that Red Talons don't get to be the "Wyld tribe" because the Black Furies are already that. Hengeyokai could receive small changes or additions to reduce the ethnic stereotypes as well, but they already have the same Breeds and tribes as the rest of the world, they just have different names which... frankly, shapeshifters in every region of the world probably have their own translations of their names and the words for things, variant customs, and so forth. Beast Courts are mostly just politically distinct.

                6. Sure, throw in some weird ronin groups that hang out with Fera or vampires or mages, or want to "cure lycanthropy," or have lived in the Umbra until they turned into spirits, or have some odd philosophy that nobody else follows. Have entire camps or mini-tribes like the Siberakh and Skin-Dancers who do their own thing without joining the Garou Nation, Beast Courts, or Ahadi. Have weird rumors of mysterious groups that might be Garou, but might be something else. Have strange cults of shifters and/or kinfolk who worship weird shit in strange creepy rural towns or isolated communes. We could have a really cooky, misguided group who think that the way to save Gaia is to interbreed all the Changing-Breeds together until they "merge."

                7. Same with some more diversity in other Changing-Breeds -- it could be cool to have Kitsune who have spread out beyond East Asia, Mokole living in the Pacific Islands, Bagheera who join the Beast Courts, little splinters and sub-tribes of the Bastet and Ratkin and Rokea, Nagah renegades who refuse the authority of the Sesha, Ananasi who ally with other shapeshifters, etc. I don't mean entire tribes that would only make sense with large retcons of the setting history, but small or isolated groups, similar to the Siberakh.

                8. Throw in some ominous prophecies and weird portents of the Apocalypse that hint at things that could happen, sort of like Rage Across the Heavens did, but instead of expanding on them and telling us where the metaplot goes, leave each as an open plot hook for STs to interpret however they want.

                9. I have heard complaints from people who dislike W:tA because it depicts technology and science as evil. I can see the validity of these complaints -- as written, it kinda does. I would not want the Weaver to stop being a major antagonist, but I think there could be a subtle shift in depiction, as a force of control and stasis and smothering that uses science and tech a lot, instead of being science and tech -- much as the Wyrm abuses a lot of industrial tech but doesn't personify it. Or maybe in the way that death is technically "Wyrm" but is of the Balance Wyrm, not the crazy Triatic Wyrm; so science and advanced technology could be "Weaver" but not of the Mad Weaver. Most Garou can still be prejudiced as Hell, but the setting could move a bit away from that fear being entirely correct.

                10. Positive incentives for Kinfolk to help the Garou. Sure, this is a good idea.




                There are some of things (radical changes) that I would not want to see, though:

                1. Major setting rewrite, invalidating previous books because the setting, history, and metaplot are too different -- such as getting rid of the Hengeyokai or Black Tooth or the Wars of Rage.

                2. Significant mechanics changes without easy access to a conversion guide. Any really major mechanics changes.

                3. Making the tribes pan-ethnic (I think this is how it works in NWoD?)

                4. Getting rid of the Striders' curse

                5. Replacing W:tA's unique mythology with real-world wolfman legends. I like the current blend of taking inspiration from legends and myth, while building a mythology that's more and also distinctly different.

                6. Dropping the politics. I don't want to see W:tA's discussion of real world environmental, historical, and social problems reduced to Fox News "give equal time to both sides" BS, or outright ignored to make it a sanitized children's game. I always liked that this game explores real world problems, the history of humans' wars against each other, and the human condition, and takes a hard look at problems in the real world that we humans need to actually fix before they kill us. That's no different from philosophical questions that Mage: the Ascension asks. That doesn't mean there has to be an objective "correct" question for every political controversy on the planet. Look at how the Black Furies Revised Tribebook handled abortion: different Black Furies have different opinions and some care enough to fight each other over it (because they're Garou, they just can't manage to get along or discuss their differences peacefully.) Nowhere does the book support any one side of the argument. Personally I'm not sure that particular controversy should be depicted as a big deal to the Garou, who have much bigger problems to deal with.

                7. Changing how many forms each Changing-Breed has. I know that may look like a minor change to some, but it would really bug me.

                8. Making a 4th edition that's post-Apocalypse. My understanding is that V:tM 4E will be post-Gehenna. I absolutely do not want to see Werewolf taken in that direction. Not at all. I am not interested in that setting except as a one-time supplement like the old Apocalypse book -- not as a definitive, one-canon-version of what happened, replacing the pre-Apocalypse books and gameline that OPP is writing currently.

                9. Removing an entire Changing-Breed from the setting. I don't think any writer would do this, but I feel like mentioning it, apparently just 'cause I'm brainstorming IDK. On the other hand I might be alright with adding one, if it made sense -- I've always been confused why Kitsune are shapeshifters, but the almost identical Tanuki legends were used for Hsien instead, though my personal preference for that is to just make Tanuki a breed/tribe of Kitsune, since their animal is similar enough.
