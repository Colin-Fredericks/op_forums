        Originally posted by Erinys View Post
        If the territory belonged to another tribe, or even another sept, I don't think punishment is likely. Garou attack each other so often to steal septs, it clearly isn't viewed as a crime by most tribes.
        There's definitely an element that things that happen within a sept, or between friendly septs, are "domestic" politics and violations are handled as crimes; and things that happen between unfriendly septs that are "foreign" politics and are treated as acts of war or other provocations.

        So trespassing a "foreign" sept might not lead to a rite of punishment, but it could be interpreted as an act of war. The Garou of the "invaded" sept might still try to punish the intruder (assuming he was captured), but the rite of Ostracism wouldn't make much sense in that case. The sept may decide something like the Voice of the Jackal would be good. I don't think that would be a standard punishment. A good beating is more likely to be it in most cases. But a particularly egregious trespass could be escalated into the rite.

        [I actually have had trouble thinking of scenarios where the Voice of the Jackal would be used (it might be used in cases of Garou mating with Garou in those septs that punish it severely, but don't kill the Garou involved). So its possible use by a sept against interlopers (or perhaps visiting Garou) is an intriguing option when the rite of Ostracism isn't appropriate as the Garou's home isn't at that sept. It's a serve punishment, but one likely to keep Garou at their best behavior at other septs.]

        Of course, that only happens if the invaded sept captures the Garou. Assuming the Garou escapes, it becomes a political issue between septs. That might just lead to bad relations. It might have no impact, or it might lead to war. Or the sept might agree to punish the trespassing Garou themselves or even turn the Garou over to the other sept for punishment. It all depends on so many unique details on what could happen - the tribes involved, the types of caerns at each sept, the nature of the trespass, the reputation of the Garou involved, if there are other outstanding issues between the septs, etc.

        This is why I originally asked for specific examples of crimes because the details matter. But it also works by exploring different scenarios if we talk about specific crimes in a general matter.

                            Here's something I'm sure will be controversial - what to do with Garou who have dealings with vampires?

                            I know there is a very large constituency that believes this would never happen. Vampires are of the Wyrm, and they' be immediately attacked! However, that's like saying no human would ever break the law, or the moral commandments of their religion - which happens all the time in real life. I think this is mainly a reaction of the fanbase to early crossover attempts which presented this as seeming far too common. And I think among players whose PC tribes are the most likely suspects to tolerate this, it's an overreaction to redeem their favorite tribe's reputation (just look how much the Bone Gnawers cleaned up from first edition to Revised).

                            Personally, I believe the Urrah tribes are forced to tolerate and make deals with vampires just to survive, and their are either foolhardy, ambitious, or criminal Garou of the other tribes who do so. But everyone will have their own opinions on where this divide comes down. For our purposes, we don't need to consider them to be common. We just need to examine each one on its own merit.

                            I am including here as our examples those actually mentioned in canon! Whether you think these are stupid or not is beside the point. It's best we add enough details in our heads to make them plausible rather than simply reject them. Let's assume each of these have been exposed. How do the Garou get punished? What exactly is the crime - merely hanging out with a Vampire? (What if that vampire did not stink of the Wyrm?) Or is something other law at issue? Consider that different septs will react differently in some of these examples. The Sept of the Green will be acting differently than the Sept of the Hand of Gaia or the Sept of the Sentinel (all examples from Rage Across New York). So it's OK to mention multiple decisions.

                            I will exclude some things - any mention of a "treaty" or "deal" or likewise which I take to simply be an informal arrangement on division of territory especially when its either designed to protect the caern or allow the Garou to concentrate on more urgent matters. Same thing with temporary alliances to go after a common foe. That just must be too common an issue. I don't think these are crimes, but "foreign policy" issues that many septs debate and can come into being or end as each side sees fit.

                            Rage Across New York, pg 91 Younger Fianna formed nonaggression pacts with Toreador of the NYC art world. Early Werewolf really pushed the idea of the Fianna as creative, artistic types so this kind of interaction makes sense. I don't think it makes so much sense now. But if we assume there is some kind of local tribal protectorate there, this may seem less dubious. I didn't exclude this one as the mention of "younger" Fianna indicates this was not done with some kind of approval, but an initiative of this small group.

                            Shadow Lords (I think this is Caleson Ruah NPC in the book) have special alliance with the Giovanni dealing with the underworld, though not “completely” sanctioned by the tribal leadership. This obviously was approved by senior Shadow Lords, but they have obviously given themselves plausible deniability in case this arrangement blows up in their face. In this scenario it has as Carleson has been exposed. Carleson is also mentioned to have dealings with Sabbat and is working to fight the Wyrm from within. This sounds very similar to the Bringers of Light camp.

                            Bone Gnawers secretly exchange information with the Nosferatu – they appreciate the food the vampires serve them. I think this is likely an euphemism for man eating of dead drained of blood. So this is more than just some Rat Finks collecting information from an informant.

                            Rage Across Australia, pg 123
                            Darius Winchester, Silver Fang king of Australia, has a lover in Diana Wentworth, 12th generation Gangrel vampire. When he began his affair, he didn't know she was a vampire, but does now. The book strongly hints that there is a possible blood bond. So it could be argued Darius is not in his right mind.

                            Storyteller's Guide to the Sabbat
                            In the section on Sabbat allies this is mentioned. Quoting it here on full since I think it's important.

                            Bone Gnawers: This tribe of Lupines has been known to aid certain packs within the Sabbat. The Lupines work with the packs to drive out and destroy both vampire and Lupine enemies of the Sabbat in some areas. Little else is known about the Sabbat's connection to the Bone Gnawers.

                            I know many people will object to a Vampire supplement being canon, but at this stage in the WoD, crossover was just common. For the purposes of early second edition, this is just true. Now it is written from a Sabbat perspective, so I don't think this reflects any tribal policy. It mentions "in some areas." But there had to have been enough Bone Gnawers septs, packs, or groups that it was noticeable. For our purpose, we don't need to examine the problem anymore than one specific group of Bone Gnawers (say a pack) did this.

                            Gangrel Clanbook
                            In the templates section, there is a Lupine Impersonator. The werewolf pack temporarily adopts a “lost cub” / first change who they later discover turns out to be a vampire. Let's assume typical PC interactions of PC Garou and this PC Gangrel in an early crossover play chronicle. Everyone needed to get along to be in the same game and all characters made the necessary adjustments even if they roleplayed as not liking it. So these Garou are not typical and may not belong to a typical sept. We could also say the impersonator is in fact a lost, unknowing kinfolk. Something plausible that might explain initial acceptance and a decision to not destroy her immediately once they find out.

                            Werewolf: the Apocalypse, 1st edition corebook pg 245
                            There's a section on ten ways Garou and Vampires can interact. This was obviously done to assist people in wanting to play a crossover chronicle, possibly allowing people to keep playing their Vampire character(s) while others play Garou in this new Werewolf life. I won't mention all ten, but some are worthwhile to repeat here.

                            Gangrel vampire and Garou fall in love, and only reveal their identities to their packmates. Again, we need to assume typical PC behavior here that this settles into the realm of possibility. Will a pack of Get of Fenris allow this? No. But a pack comprised of Children of Gaia, Bone Gnawers, and other squishes who are prone to romanticism?

                            Shadow Lord or pack of Shadow Lords work as assassin for vampire elder in exchange for power, works extensively with a vampire coterie. This seems like a quid pro quo arrangement done simply to gain power.

                            Galliard hangs out with Toreador and start some kind of art troupe with them. This is just the slimmest basis of interaction so we have to use our imagination here that the Galliard and Toreador are really engaged in a true work of art, and that initially the Galliard may not have known. But by this time, it's a long term association and the Galliard knows some of his associates are (high humanity) vampires.

                            I have my own ideas on all of these. But rather than possibly prejudice responses, I'd like to hear others first.

                                    I’m also of the mind that Garou/Changing Breed-Vampire interactions definitely happen in more contexts then mutual murdering, but I get the sense you’re looking more towards the legal aspect of this then the social, so I’ll limit myself to saying that it happens and I would also imagine that informal treaties are as often as not found among urban tribes and that even other tribes will have some members participating in minor, small-scale, if not permitted then tolerably ignored interactions.

                                    All this said though, there’s an obvious issue here. Vampires are tainted by the Wyrm and Weaver and unless they’re of particularly high humanity they read as such mystically. To the perspective of many Garou, particularly traditionalists, befriending or tolerating a vampire makes as much sense as befriending of tolerating a fomori - they’re both agents of the Wyrm that will inevitably further the Wyrm’s ends and undermine Gaia’s health and vitality. However as we all know their are many practical differences between fomori and vampires and their would be Changing Breeds which discover this as well.

                                    The most obvious issue is that of the second tenet which is very clear ‘Hunt the Wyrm wherever it dwells and breeds’. This gives the Garou an obvious prerogative. Find vampire, kill vampire, maintain veil, leave. However, as urban tribes are also quick to point out, the Garou cannot kill everything wyrm-tainted indiscriminately (anymore). There’s far too much taint and it would take away from efforts to destroy greater taints. From a consequentialist perspective, as much as it stings, they must prioritize greater threats.

                                    However, what happens if a Garou is discovered to be doing more then just prioritizing greater threats? Obviously this will vary between tribe, sept, and pack. However what follows is what I imagine would be the general Garou response (which does not necessarily compare well to the Bone Gnawer and Glass Walker response).

                                    The first thing to do is to isolate the Garou, put them through immediate spiritual inspection, enact as many rites of cleaning and contrition as the ritemaster deems necessary and then have philodox grill the Garou for EXACTLY how far things went, what the Garou did, and how much of a threat this is.

                                    Afterwards I imagine there’s going to be some deliberations to follow. The Galliards will have plenty of stories regarding the dangers of vampires and accounts from the ancestors regarding trusted vampires who caused septs to fall. The Philodox know the law, consider precedent, and judge the case. The Theurges figure out how bad the spiritual consequences will be and how much will be needed to appease any insulted/upset spirits. I imagine from this point you enter into layers of response compared to severity of harm done. Early Garou may have slain the culprit and been done but the Nation wants for numbers too much to do this anymore. Regularly at least.

                                    At the very least there will be a rite of punishment conducted to demonstrate such behavior is wrong and to offer the Garou a chance to atone. Depending on how bad things got this is going to get gradually more and more severe. There could be reprisals, spirit quests, ostracism, confiscation of fetishes. There’s certainly going to be some loss of renown and perhaps even loss of rank or exile if things got bad enough.

                                    However I think the ultimate matter is whether or not the caern was ever threatened by the garou’s actions, even indirectly or in the long term. If not then I imagine much can be forgiven with proper rites and shows of contrition and taking punishment honorably. If the caern was ever so much as slightly threatened I thing that changes everything. The garou should receive exile at least, might get a redemption quest, but will probably be executed within a rite of punishment. Then the question is if the Nation can destroy the vampire without creating a greater threat to their sacred places.

                                                Please remember to tie verdicts into actual Rites of Punishment. Even something simple like "kill" has multiple Rites - the Great Hunt, Gaia's Vengeful Teeth, and even Rite of the Shattered Soul. Banishment has the Rite of Ostracism, but we still have the need to determine the length - one month, a year, permanent, or something else? A lot of the judgments are too vague.

                                                And I'm seeing some punishments that don't involve Rites. That's not necessarily incorrect, but they're not in the source material. So I think we need to carefully consider these cases. We don't have example of corporal punishments, nor the taking away of fetishes.

                                                I could see a sept deciding to take away fetish, but it's not a standard punishment. Certain fetishes - like a klaive - seem to have a reputational or leadership function, and I can see a council of sept elders deciding to remove it. But other fetishes don't. Is the Sept Council really going to strip away Magpie's Swag or Nyx's Bangle? What about other possessions? Issuing fines is fairly typical for legal codes. And although we don't have any examples of such, I could see it being done especially with talens since that is often a form of currency. We do know Garou sometimes challenge Garou for control of fetishes so maybe this is something that could be an informal punishment - everyone knows they're free to challenge that Garou and win the fetish from them. But that's something that still needs to be adjudicated separately in each instance as it's possible the guilty Garou wins the challenge.

                                                I do heavily question the idea of corporal punishments. Garou either seem to want to reduce public reputation or even mock and humiliate Garou, but the only physical punishment seems to be death. Similar human legal codes of primitive societies also rarely have corporal punishments. I think this is because letting someone physically abuse you is considered to be crossing an important boundary between theoretical equals. We only start to see this with the rise of states and subjugation of individuals to it. And the Garou aren't at that level yet. So I'm hesitant to see this as a way Garou deal with each other.

                                                    You are right, and what I meant by corporal punishment wasn't necessarily getting whipped and certainly not getting a hand/claw/pawn cut off. It was more on the lines of either being send to "pack your stuff, say your good byes and leave", compared to "get a beating and run and never come back".

If anything, an offender should be barred from performing certain sept duties and therefore loose one way to mitigate the renown loss...

                                                    While looking up the Ostracism Rite, I found this on corporal punishment, though: Garou have been known to injure ostracized werewolves “accidentally” (W20 page 214).

                                                    With the Rite is is not clear to me, although I believe it should be that way, that when enacting to be permanent it can be chosen whether to exile from sept or sept and tribe. While when enacting the Rite to last only a month or a year it makes sense that every one treats the garou as a non-wolf. But in this case the garou isn't expected to re-settle somewhere new. It would make the punishment pointless if one was to move from Germany to Wisconsin where no garou knew about the punishment Rite, one would form new friendships only to return when the year of ostracism is over...

                                                    When the Rite is permanent, however, I'd assume the garou is expected to move away from the protectorate of the sept a considerable distance. Since W20 has, as far as I see, no Rite to remove a garou from his tribe, I believe this Rite might also be used, when performed by "tribe leaders". The Rite explicitly mentions "sept or tribal leaders", but what does that mean, in practice? If a mixed sept wants to ostracise a traitor, and the remaining tribe members in the sept also want to ostracism him from the tribe, does that work? Even if the other tribe members is just one cliath and the offender is Adren? Probably not. One would send for a representative amount of garou of that tribe to be effective, I'd guess.

Also, some variance in the length of the sentence should be applicable.
