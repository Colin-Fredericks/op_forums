    LARP Style Guide for Werewolf: the Apocalypse?

    01-14-2016, 12:51 AM
    I had a conversation with some folks about possibly playing in the By Night Studios​ "Rage and Retribution Remix" event at Midwinter Gaming Convention​ this weekend. But they had never heard of Werewolf: The Apocalypse​ before and wanted to know what sort of costume to wear.
    At fist they were thinking fantasty-LARP (being from that genre of LARP), but then they thought it was post-apocalyptic. Finally, I did my best to try to explain it... but it still wasn't that great.

    Modern/Victorian Goth Vampire is pretty easy to come up with. Just look at "Interview with a Vampire", "Underworld", "Blade", "The Matrix". But coming up with "Punk Gothic Neo-Spiritual Werewolf Tribal Warrior" is a bit more difficult.

    So, I'm wonder if anyone has ever considered putting together a sort of "Visual Style Guide" for Werewolf: the Apocalypse LARP.
    Sure, each Tribe, each Breed, each Region/Location/Nationality/Culture is going to have variation, but when you're trying to encourage new players about what sort of costuming helps them get into the character of their Garou, I'm wondering if some sort of style-guide might at least help.

    When it comes to LARP garb for parlor LARP, I think there's two ways of looking at it.

    1) What you see is what you get. Your character is literally wearing what you are wearing. Your Garou is wearing that tie and top hat, or those furs and vest, or that BDSM gimp mask and tape over your nipples. You're walking around in the IC public places, or in the secret locations, or fighting for their lives in that outfit.

    2) The outfit represents the concept, not what they're actually wearing. Your character isn't actually dressed to the 9s, or in ragged clothes, or in mundane attire, but that's the general concept/impression others get from you. This is the concept most of these PCs would know you as. It may be why each time you show up to a game, you wear the same costume--your persona/identity hasn't changed, so your representation-attire doesn't either,and changing it means there's a change in your character that others should be aware of.

    Both of these ideas are valid, and both of these are used in games. (I'm ignoring the straight-up "I didn't garb at all for this. I just brought my dues and sheet for this, since there can be many good and bad reasons for not doing even minimal costuming; no time to grab it, no cash/time to buy/make something, new character, etc..)

    But if a new player wants to come in with some sort of costume/garb, how do you describe that attire that is "setting appropriate" to them for Garou, especially if they haven't really learned the "feel" of the game?

    Going off of Joshua Timbrook and Ron Spencer pieces aren't necessarily the best, IMO (as cool as they may be.) And I'm not necessarily thinking full-blown Crinos-in-combat illustration either. More like, well, like I said, a "Style Guide" for costuming--clothing, jewelry, armor, fetishes, scars, markings, etc., both the small stuff like necklaces and small do-dads, or large costume pieces like body-armor, or whole wardrobe ensembles.

    What are your thoughts? What gives YOU the inspiration for your pieces? What do you think DOESN'T fit, what do you think is NECESSARY?

          This is actually a really interesting and tough question. While a Vampire might change their clothing from time to time (and thus a Vampire player their costuming), Werewolves have to be prepared to shift forms and so they are typically always wearing the same Dedicated outfit. It's usually simple and provides freedom of movement. Here are some of my Werewolf costumes from over the years:

          Jason Saroff, Homid Shadow Lord Galliard: jeans and long-sleeved shirt.
          Sohcahtoa, Homid Glass Walker Philodox: Red Camouflage
          Andreiko Eferiev, Homid Silver Fang Galliard: tuxedo shirt, slacks
          Anklebiter, Lupus Wendigo Ahroun: Grey track pants, grey Boba Fett T-shirt [mighty hunter], grey fleece jacket
          Jiao River-Dragon, Homid Sai Chau of the Zhong Lung: Farscape T-shirt ["Share the wonders I've seen..."], slacks, silk robe
          Lars Nielsen, Homid Get of Fenris Ragabash: combat boots, jeans, custom Minnesota Vikings T-Shirt ["#13, Fenris"]
