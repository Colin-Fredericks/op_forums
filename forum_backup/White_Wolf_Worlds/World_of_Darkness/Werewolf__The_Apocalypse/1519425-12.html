                                                08-06-2023, 02:49 AM
                                                Another issue that is running through W5 is that while it's mentioning multiple ways to play W5 from time to time, the writers clearly structured the book around one assumed right way to play this game.

                                                It starts on pg 45 with the sidebar called "Packs In A Modern World" where multiple Pack-types are mentioned. But W5 hasn't taken Coterie-Types from V5 and reproduced them for W5, so that's the first hint of how hollow this effort has been to follow up on what's presented here. And you get the first hint of what the writers think you're supposed to do instead at the end of the sidebar:

                                                The most effective kinds of packs for chronicles are proactive — troublemakers, if you will, but overall the sorts of Garou who investigate where conflict thrives, rather than waiting for it to come to them. Docile werewolves don’t turn back the tide of Apocalypse.

                                                Packs may also find that their initial concept changes over time, or when they acquire resources to protect, such as a caern that ties them to a particular location. That’s great! Not only will the characters themselves change as the chronicle progresses, new dynamics of how those characters work together as a pack can emerge, as well.
                                                (Emphasis mine)

                                                If you didn't already get the message there, the moment you get to the section on Caerns, the message intensifies and now in a sidebar called "The Players' Pack" (pg 191) this happens:
                                                As a game, Werewolf: The Apocalypse assumes that the players’ characters will have their own caern, or that their pack will belong to the sept of a specific caern. The assumption exists for multiple reasons.
                                                […]
                                                This assumption doesn’t have to be true — the chronicle may well be about a pack that ranges far and wide, across an extensive territory — but many troupes enjoy having a bit of territory that can show the outcomes of their actions (and the consequences of their choices).
                                                (Emphasis mine)

                                                In the first sidebar, being tied to a caern was described as something that may happen but now it's already called an "assumption" on the game's part. And even when the last paragraph mentions "The assumption doesn't have to be true", it's immediately followed up with a "But...!". And if you're under any illusions whether there's one right way to play W5, the section called "Local Conflicts, Global Implications" (pg 206) disabuses you of that notion:
                                                Once the pack has a purpose, establish relevant conflicts so the players’ characters act locally. Where you can, plan for the characters to acquire territory that gives them ties to the local community, such as a caern or participation in a sept.

                                                Local antagonists (both active and passive) provide conflicts in which Garou characters can secure real and meaningful victories, and where their resources can make a difference against the environmental apocalypse. Just because the stakes aren’t epic save-the-world stories, that doesn’t mean they’re not important. Werewolf is very much a game of personal crisis and meaning in the framework of a worldwide catastrophe. And it has all the more meaning if it happens in the context of, say, retaking and then rebuilding a caern, or protecting caern territory as a responsibility to the sept.

                                                Straight up, the pack won’t defeat the Wyrm (and is unlikely even to confront it directly). However, the packmates can challenge local antagonists who advance the Wyrm’s agenda of greed, selfishness, and destruction, especially when those antagonists threaten what the werewolves have fought hard to attain or recover.
                                                (Emphasis mine)

                                                Now the Storyteller is just straight-up told to make PCs take care of a caern and limit the game to a local conflict. And it's ultimately pointed out that this game isn't even about saving the world in the first place...
