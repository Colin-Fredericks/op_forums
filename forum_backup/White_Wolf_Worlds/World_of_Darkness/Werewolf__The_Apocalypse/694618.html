      Part of the problem is that the books don't really have a solid in-game explanation for what "causes" favoring in Gifts to occur. I've been in games where the ST had no story difference between favored and not; story difficulty was based solely on the Rank of the Gift and how the spirit in question felt about the Garou in question. The difference in XP cost was considered an extra "tax" on the process Garou go through to learn Gifts rather than anything about what spirits might think about an Ahroun Glasswalker trying to learn Spirit Speech. I've also been in games where the ST increase the story cost of Gifts under the assumption that Gaian aligned spirits enforced the favored/not thing as a real part of spirit politics.

      Both extremes work, and don't really contrast with the setting material. Though it lends very different tones to play (and the first option sees a lot more cherry picking of specific Gifts; which is why I think so many STs favor the second).

      So, what I think is the best approach is to discuss the hows and whys as a group to decide what the in-game explanation of this is, and from there it should be more clear how to proceed.

          This is how we have done it in the past. As Heavy Arms has stated, each game can be a little different and the storyteller has a lot of play in this area.

          If your character doesn't know someone with that gift or have seen the gift being used during the course of the game, I feel the most important part of this is explaining how a Garou knows that a gift exists. Some tribes are very stingy with knowledge of their gifts (Silver Fang/Shadowlord) while others are willing to share in order to strengthen ties (Bone Gnawer/Children of Gaia). All gifts are not common knowledge.

          I don't know if the W20 books says this but in one of the older books it said that Garou learning gifts outside their Auspice was often frowned upon by Elders because the Garou was trying to be something that he isn't. Other then Tribal/Sept politics I don't think this is often hard to overcome as long as its not the common occurrence. When going up in rank the first gift bought from the new rank should always be from the Garou's Auspice, again because of tradition.

          Learning a Gift from another tribe usually requires the Garou to be on good terms with at least one member of the tribe (generally a packmate) who can summon the appropriate spirit. Even then, the Garou must convince the spirit she is worthy of its blessings, and that she won't turn them against its tribal allies.

          Breed gifts are probably the most ambiguous in this regards, they seem to be based around the nature of the Garou in question. I think its simply this nature that makes them unfavorable. I think a Homid who has a "back to nature mentality" would be more likely to pick up Lupus gifts then one who spent his entire life in a skyscraper. I normally don't impose many road blocks.

                              Favored gifts are part of the Pact with the appropriate spirit brood. Every Garou is inheriting a lot of earlier work done to make the Tribal, Auspice, and Breed spirits favor you. It's like asking family members for help. Those spirits either know you, or know your family.

                              Going to other spirits is like asking that guy down the street who doesn't know you. Whether he does or not depends on the specific person, what mood he's in when you ask, and the reasonableness of the request. Usually it is best to already be doing things that cause those spirits to become familiar with you and develop a favorable opinion.

                              I am more inclined to allow out of bounds Gifts be taught if its appropriate the pack Totem or its brood can do so. If you are a Fianna and belong to a pack with Owl as its totem, then I'd be more willing for that Fianna to learn some Silent Strider Gifts than I would for Wendigo Gifts. Of course, Bone Gnawer Tribal Gifts may be less likely since those spirits may not like Owl. Of course, this is not entirely perfect for the reasons Heavy Arms laid out (Tribal Gifts and appropriate spirits who teach them sometimes seem to have little logic in terms of spirits and the actual Spirit Brood of the Tribal totem). The Garou though should be in good stead with their pack totem, and if there are particularly "signature" Tribal Gifts, they are more likely not to be taught. Ubqiuitous Gifts found in many places are more prone to be taught if asked.

                              Auspice Gifts are less likely to be taught since that is not so much based on a Tribal totem spirit brood, but the favor of Luna. If the Garou in question is particularly exemplar in their auspice, then it is much easier for spirits to teach them the odd Gift belonging to another auspice as it won't interfere with their duties. If the Garou isn't doing their auspice roll well, then the spirits are less likely to teach them less it encourages them to continue to stray.

                              The higher rank of the Garou, the more likely spirits will teach them out of bounds Gifts. Cliaths never learn out of bounds gifts. Starting at Fostern, their pack totem may being considering to aid them in learning Gifts associated with their brood. At Adren and above, out of scope breed or auspice Gifts become possible.

                              In the games I run, I just don't let players just spend XP and get the Gifts. They have to roleplay it out because I want to encourage spirits being part of the world, and to emphasize these are gifts, not something to be taken for granted. Usually I won't require too hard a standard. If they ever ask for something out of bounds, they better really sell it to me, otherwise the spirit will just say no.

                              One thing I avoid is the idea that spirits should feel compelled to teach certain Gifts because the Garou in question "need" them or "should" have them. Spirits don't care about that. They've been around a very long time, and seen a lot of Garou come and go and seen Wyrm threats come and go. Spirits are not the supplicants, the individual Garou in question always are. No Spirit should ever think "I need to give these Garou Sense Wyrm or Spirit Speech because what they'll do with it should be of benefit to me in some way." Garou always need to earn the Spirit's favor, any presumptions on the Garou's part is not giving the spirits their proper respect. Spirits do not act like humans or Garou and live in their world. They have their own natures, agendas, realities, and values. Proper Chiminage is always needed, and must be paid in advance - no spirit performs a favor on "credit" or future promise.

Usually these spirits are not Gaian: ghosts, Weaver-slaves, dream-entities or other stranger things are all capable of sharing some of their secrets to the Garou (the rank 3 'Ghost Touched' Silent Strider gift is one example). Of course, since these beings don't hold to the same old pacts the totems do, such deals are often fraught with risks.

                                  I tend to be a bit of a hardass when it comes to allowing out of tribe gifts to be learned- I don't use tribal weaknesses and I feel like the mechanical differentiation of the gifts helps give a more concrete feel to the tribes. But I do very similar things to what others here have suggested- getting in good with a particular totem's brood will open up access to gifts that would otherwise be off-limits. Roleplaying out such things lets you impress on the players what a big deal gifts are, and it gives them a sense of achievement when they successfully engender the respect of an out-of-tribe totem.

                                      This is very much a question of what your storyeller feels is right. The concept behind favoured gifts is that certain spirits are closer to you and therefore make it easier for you to learn them. While you can learn most "foreign" gifts up to level 3, beyond that is usually quite a big beef to get the biggest secrets of the Fera groups.

                                      I usually make it more of a story to learn gifts. Everyone needs to find, persuade and study under a spirit to get a Gift. It makes for fun stories. The key difference is that the favoured spirits will be more inclined to help you and treat you better once you get to them.
