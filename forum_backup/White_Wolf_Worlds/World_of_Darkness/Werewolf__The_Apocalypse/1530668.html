    What makes a good ‘rage across…’ setting?

    02-12-2024, 05:05 PM
    Recently I have been rereading those books from the eponymous series that are available to me and also trying to puzzle out my own ideas for one or two locations.

    Well, maybe a few more than that (Given a persistent habit of ‘On the one hand … but on the other’ thinking) but that’s by the way: as the thread title makes clear, I would love to hear the assembled fans’ thoughts on features are most crucial when it comes to identifying the setting for a WEREWOLF: THE APOCALYPSE location.


    The only ideas for that subject which have occurred to me so far are:-

    - The location should ideally have a name that works well with RAGE ACROSS… (I have a suspicion that a name which favours a natural region over a human polity would be ideal, but the problem is that, for example, RAGE ACROSS THE BERING doesn’t work quite as well as RAGE ACROSS ALASKA).


    - The location should have a decent balance of rural against urban areas (But the urban areas in question should not distract too much from the countryside and wilderness).


    - Ideally, there should be at least some wolves in the vicinity other than those who go around on two legs as the spirit moves them.


    - There should be enough diversity in local populations to support at least one or two Garou from each of the tribes (No matter if one or more tribes dominate the area).


    Please share your own ideas and comments as they occur - also, please share your thoughts on where the likeliest locations for new RAGE ACROSS sourcebooks might be (Also, on whether there ought to be a noticeable difference between the requirements for a good Fifth Edition chronicle location and those for the classic editions).

      Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
      - Ideally, there should be at least some wolves in the vicinity other than those who go around on two legs as the spirit moves them.
      I have a gripe with this, because it undercuts the global nature of the Garou's mission. Rage Across the Amazon might have had issues, but the Amazon if very far from wolves. It's still a pivot book is setting up what the larger war against the Wyrm looks like, how reconciliation with the Fera can look like, and so on. While I am personally all for a take on WtA with a much looser idea of what counts as a "wolf," there's still places the Garou need to be putting a lot of work into helping to further their cause where even a very broad idea of "wolf" wouldn't cover this.

      I think it's ideal to cover at least some places without wolves for the "Rage Across..." set because that's is a reality for the Garou even if places that used to have natural wolf populations.

      ------------

      Beyond this, for me the key thing to making a good "Rage Across..." book? The book needs to think about territory. They can't just be focused on a single location like a city (or a large Sept in the wilderness). Wolves are animals that take large territories to see to their needs. It adds to making the "Rage Across..." books feel real to the core of WtA when the territory described feels naturally contiguous. This covers a lot of the specific points you made like mixing wild, rural, and urban areas, or population diversity.

                                  Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                                  I’m familiar with the classic 1:50,000 metric when it comes to estimating a werewolf population, but is this intended to give us an idea of how many Garou would be resident in an area or how many Garou an area is likely to produce in a generation?

                                  I ask because applying this metric to (for example) Alaska, by almost any definition prime werewolf country, suggests that the local population of 700,000+ would be kept an eye on by no more than two Garou.
                                  That ratio is actually useless in practical gameplay. It wasn't used in the setting books. And it doesn't make sense because it leads to conclusions that are at odds with the setting. So I ignore it. I only keep the idea that each tribe probably only numbers of the hundreds or low thousands.

                                  First, I assume you mean 1:500,000, not 50,000. That figure first appears (to my knowledge) in the Revised Werewolf Storytellers Handbook (page 33). They do give justifications for it, but in my opinion that is post hoc justification. They gave a number because people were asking for one, and they wanted something less than vampires. They thought it would be helpful, but it's a meaningless ratio for multiple reasons.

                                  Second, the early sourcebooks clearly did not use this ratio. The Rage books are all vastly overpopulated by that figure. Rage Across New York lists about 320 Garou in the state. The population of New York was around 18 million in 1990. That comes to around 1:56,000. Rage Across Australia also lists about 320 Garou. Australia's population in 1990 was around 17 million. So the ratio is 1:53,000. Dark Alliance: Vancouver lists almost 370 Garou for British Columbia. Its population in 1990 was around 3 million. That ratio is about 1:8150! But for that matter, the septs listed in these books also do not correspond to the recommended caern population per rank level also shown in later books. Instead, the writers just seemed to want 300+ Garou divided over a half dozen to a dozen septs and across all the tribes when writing the Rage books.

                                  Third, using a ratio for Garou per human population makes no sense. It would lead to Garou being found more often in large metro areas than rural areas. Yet other than the Glass Walkers and many Bone Gnawers, the Garou are overwhelmingly supposed to be based in the wilderness and rural areas. To your point, Alaska should have more than two Garou. In fact, it should likely be a very large center of Garou population, much larger than say the Eastern Seaboard of the USA.

                                  Not only does a ratio based on human population not make sense for lupus and metis, it doesn't even make sense for homids. Because homid Garou are not born from humans, but kinfolk. And the population of kinfolk does not need to be based on regular human population distribution. There might be very few or zero kinfolk in a large metropolitan city while in some small isolated town of 5,000 people in sparsely populated mountain or plain states could be 100% kinfolk.

                                  It's also a bad approach because such a ratio implies the Garou population should be increasing with the human population when we know according to the setting lore that Garou population has a trend of slow decline that has lead to a Garou demographic collapse. At 10,000 BC the human population is probably around 1 million. During the Impergium it's only around 5 million. During the Bronze Age maybe 30 million. During the Classical era it's about 200 million. Yet I imagine during all this time the total Garou population is probably stable and much greater than what it is today.

                                  I think a better approach is to figure out how many caerns/septs you want in your chronicle, determine their rank ratings, and populate them according to the caern rank:sept population guide. Then figure out what kind of kinfolk communities must exist in the same area in order to support it. Make adjustments as how it makes sense to you. If you want to set your game in or near a big metropolitan area with a large Glass Walker caern, that is OK. If you want to set your game in Montana or Alaska and make everything wilderness based, that works too.

                                  Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                                  Is there any generally accepted method for estimating how many Caerns might be found in an area and how potent those places of power are likely to be?

                                  I ask this because, when estimating a likely Garou population for Alaska and getting the grand total of TWO (Rounding up) it then occurred to me that the number of local caerns at least gave me a ‘basement figure’ for local population numbers (‘Up to ten members’ per caern, at minimum).
                                  There isn't any such guide and there shouldn't be. Because such places of power aren't defined by so many square miles should have so many caerns. Instead, caerns are sacred places that are not evenly distributed around the world.

                                  This is how I have approached this issue. Other STs will do it differently and will offer other suggestions, but this how I do it. If it helps you, great. If not, maybe someone else's ideas will help you.

                                  I first begin by determining how many "protectorates" exist, as defined in Rage Across New York:

                                  "These are areas are not established like homid territories, which tend to surround cities. Rather, these areas tend to extend around open spaces, with cities along the borders. Since the Garou often dislike water travel, rivers and lakes also form boundaries. They are usually named for natural formations, like mountains, valleys, rivers, lakes, and plains."

                                  So I find a good physical map of the region I intend to set my chronicle in (usually found online), print it out, take out a marker or highlighter, and figure out the rough boundary for each of the protectorates. Then I research the area and figure what places might make good caern locations. Folklore, legends, pop culture, natural places of beauty, history, etc. can all give you inspiration for good caern types and their approximate rank. I typically try to put one caern in each protectorate, but there is nothing that prevents you from having multiple caerns in a single protectorate or even zero caerns in a protectorate. It all depends on what you want in the game, although that should affect Garou politics within that protectorate.

                                  I rule that almost anything can be justified to be a rank one caern, so I keep that separate from the above. Rank one caerns are usually the possession of small groups of Garou - perhaps even a single extended family of Garou from one tribe. So I use the above method only for rank 2-4 caerns. I don't use them for rank 5 caerns because in my chronicles, I have decided there are only around ten or so rank 5 caerns in the entire world, and I already know where those are. Typically I only put in rank one caerns as needed in order to fill out the chronicle as needed

                                  Then I figure out what tribe or tribes should make up each sept. If it is an area of low human population and actual large wolf population, I make sure to boost up the number of lupus as necessary. If you are setting the game in a place like Alaska or northern Canada, lupus could very well make up half the Garou or even more.

                                  I also like to have at least one pack of Anruth Garou that travel from sept to sept. Sometimes I have multiple packs of Anruth. These might be local young Garou simply getting to know their neighborhood, to more adventurous Anruth packs who enter the area from far away because they seek adventure and renown. Likewise if you want a Stargazer hermit or a Silent Strider traveler. This is a good way of introducing more Garou into the setting than would normally be justified, but still have it reflect the setting.

                                  And in addition to this, I usually add some Ronin, Lost Cubs, and Lunatics who are in the region, yet not affiliated with any sept.

                                  The actual number of caerns and Garou depends on what you want in your game. I actually find the 300+ Garou described in the Rage books to be far too much as an ST. Vampire chronicles often only have 30-70 vampires. So I find detailing maybe 3-4 caerns to be more than sufficient. Of course, like with any worldbuilding you can start small with several local caerns, and then as the PCs increase in power and ambition fill out additional caerns that are farther away.

                                  Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                                  DIGRESSION: I must admit that one of my notions for working out where and at what power level to place Caerns was to use maps showing ley lines to estimate those regions where places of power are likely to cluster most thickly.

                                  Do you feel this is practical - for gaming purposes - or is the nature of these lines more ‘Weaver’ than ‘Gaia’?
                                  If you have such maps, I would use them only as inspiration. Rather than use ley line maps, I would much rather use my method above and use actual folklore, history, legend, etc. to help me place caerns. But all of us will gravitate to different methods that work best for us.

                                      Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                                      The only ideas for that subject which have occurred to me so far are:-

                                      - The location should ideally have a name that works well with RAGE ACROSS… (I have a suspicion that a name which favours a natural region over a human polity would be ideal, but the problem is that, for example, RAGE ACROSS THE BERING doesn’t work quite as well as RAGE ACROSS ALASKA).


                                      - The location should have a decent balance of rural against urban areas (But the urban areas in question should not distract too much from the countryside and wilderness).


                                      - Ideally, there should be at least some wolves in the vicinity other than those who go around on two legs as the spirit moves them.


                                      - There should be enough diversity in local populations to support at least one or two Garou from each of the tribes (No matter if one or more tribes dominate the area).
                                      There are no wrong or right answers. It just depends on what and your gaming group find fun. I don't use any of your guidelines for my chronicles. If I was designing a sourcebook to be sold to the general public, I don't think those are bad guidelines. But they simply have no meaning for my own games. I prefer tight, focused games that can produce an interesting experience.

                                      I generally set my chronicles in a time and place that I find interesting. It could be 1970s New York City with high crime, disco clubs, urban blight, the Son of Sam, and Billy Martin's Yankees. It could also the huge sprawling wilderness of northern Canada with the largest city being Yellowknife and its 30,000 or so people. Or it could be an intimate local wilderness area in the Southwest. Or Weimar era Germany around Berlin. As long as it is distinctive with a good hook that interests me, I'll go with it.

                                      That being the case, I often limit the tribes available to the PCs and heavily constrain what tribes appear as local NPCs. The tribes chosen are the ones that make sense for that time and place. That game set in 1970s NYC is probably limited to Glass Walkers and Bone Gnawers, with all other tribes staying away from the Anruth. That Canada chronicle is likely limited to Red Talons and Wendigo plus some other tribes that can be justified like Uktena, Stargazers, Children of Gaia, and if my players really wanted, maybe one or two other tribes I could justify by developing a local spot. The Weimar Germany Berlin game would be having Get of Fenris, Silver Fangs, Bone Gnawers, and Glass Walkers. There may be some tribes with low population in the area as well, but I usually reserve those for NPCs. If I ever want to introduce Garou of other tribes, I can always justify it by having a Concolation someplace and the PCs meeting other tribes that way.

                                      Yeah, such things constrain player options. But I generally feel that is often for the best. Your players are more likely to create characters that fit the setting. Rather than generic characters whose concepts and motivations don't make sense for the time and place. Some gaming groups won't like that, but my group is OK with it.

                                      The things I do make sure to include are:

                                      I define multiple geographic protectorates in the area.

                                      I have multiple caerns that are intended to be visited at some point by the PCs even if they are based out of only one of them.

                                      I establish concrete kinfolk communities for each sept. Those kinfolk might be isolated from normal humanity, living with them but segregate themselves, be integrated with them, or mixed with some losing their kinfolk identities and connections with the Garou. I do a lot of thinking about kinfolk and like using them in game.

                                      I figure out if there are any non-traditional Garou like Ronin, Lost Cubs, and Lunatics - where they are and what they are doing. They often make interesting antagonists, good foils, or potential allies when the PCs finally bump into them.

                                      I populate the area with distinctive jaggling spirits. I try to have spirits be just as important personalities as other Garou and kinfolk.

                                      I determine where some Penumbral domains are - especially common ones like Glens or areas where the PCs will fight in like the various Wyrm domains.

                                      I like putting in multiple minor Wyrm factions appropriate to the chronicle setting. I know what they are doing, but don't have real plans for them. They grow in power, or fall and be destroyed depending on the actions of the PCs. I think they are good for sanbox style play.

                                      I have one tentpole mystery or plot that drives the general chronicle for the PCs to discover and figure out what to do.

                                                  Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                                                  Out of curiosity, may I please ask what would be your own ‘Dream Project’ in terms of being handed a RAGE ACROSS … sourcebook?
                                                  I'm not sure I have a "dream project", but if I was doing a commercial product (either for the Storyteller Vault or if the IP holders gave me money for an official setting sourcebook), I'd probably use some of your guidelines. Particularly about needing to have all thirteen tribes in it. I would probably set it in northern Montana/North Dakota and southern Alberta/Saskatchewan. It would have a name like "Rage Under a Big Sky". I would default to a 1e/2e/W20 type setting. I think many players would prefer that. If anyone wanted to make changes to accommodate W5 metaplot/continuity changes, they could do that on their own.

                                                  It would be set north of the Missouri River, south of the North Saskatchewan River, have its western border be along the Rockies, and its eastern border at Lake Winnipeg and the Red River of the North. It's a very wide area perfect for a wilderness chronicle. It has lots of different geographical features and climates. It has the prairie for ranching, the Aspen Parkland for farming, lots of resource extraction, many national parks, mountains, and major metropolitan areas in Alberta and Edmonton on its fringes. Lots of wilderness and plenty of animals so opportunities for native Changing Breeds. You can create a lot of interesting protectorates and caerns there.

                                                  It would be my attempt to show how to do a good wilderness based sourcebook. I find, for the most part, that many STs default to a setting that is either city based or just outside a major city. They just don't know what to do in the wilderness. There's still plenty of traditional city based foes since resource extraction is a big part of the economy and the two Canadian cities are there, but for the most part I'd concentrate on the wilderness. Lots of potential to create Wyrm monsters and Banes from Indian legends. Use Narlthus in northern Saskatechewan as a malevolent influence in the area. Use concepts mentioned in books like Ways of the Wolf that have been mostly abandoned like the Gamor and False Wendigo. A wide variety of Wyrm menaces and factions. I'd reference the Valkenburg Foundation as a setting element even though its located south in Wyoming. It's an important enough element that its presence should be noted.

                                                  In my home chronicles I have Triple Divide Peak be home to one of the few rank 5 caerns because it is a hydrological apex with its waters flowing into three different oceans (Pacific, Arctic, and Atlantic). Along with Snow Dome, there are the only two hydrological apexes in the entire world. I thought it fitting for a rank 5 caern. Caern totem would probably be some kind of rain spirit who could be "Father to Three Oceans". So the Silver Fang king of House Unbreakable Hearth uses it as his capital to reign (if not rule) over much of North America. So the sept would be stocked with the other tribes that support its rule. Of course there are plenty of tribes that are discontented with the Silver Fangs. And the Wendigo and Uktena would be major presence, quite resentful that such a powerful caern was taken from them. So plenty of scope for Garou politics and how that affects the rest of the septs/caerns in the region.

                                                  That would not be the only caern described because we need plenty of others, but I'd make sure the various caerns are connected with their relationships similar to old school vampire coterie charts. The area would be considered one of the strongholds of the Garou which even the Gangrel fear to travel, but that doesn't mean there aren't threats and menaces capable of challenging them.

                                                  It's not quite in present-day wolf territory, but there are native wild wolves in the region so I'd beef up the population of lupus Garou. Maybe a third of all Garou in the region. Some tribes would be more wolf heavy than others (the Red Talons of course, but the Wendigo should be about 50/50). Tribal Garou "control" over lupus kinfolk breeding stock would be a major issue. I'd have the Red Talons be prominent and much more involved in Garou politics and try to portray them as being more productive and positive than as obvious antagonists they were usually relegated as in other sourcebooks.

                                                  I'd play up the fact the geography of this area has greatly changed over the years. When the Three Brothers first arrived this would all be covered by glacier. Then when it melted, there'd be Lake Agassiz which would experience many changes as the ice sheets receded before the geography we know today appears. And I'd have this documented through Wendigo oral history and legends with various old spirits of a land that no longer exists, and the bizarre Wyrm creatures that roamed here at different times. Something that is neglected is the idea that when the Pure Ones first arrived, the Americas were under the influence of the Wyrm and the Three Brothers purified it. So I would want to incorporate that. There's much ancient evil here - dormant, but slowly waking as the Apocalypse comes near. The Wendigo would have a lot of secret knowledge that the Eurasian tribes lack, and this would be a plot point.

Stargazer hermits living on isolated mountain peaks. Packs under Bear that are unconsidered untrustworthy simply because their pack totem is Bear. Ancient Gamor lairs hidden underneath Lake Winnipeg. Dissident Garou of the tribes working to overthrow the Silver Fangs - all the rival groups mentioned in the 1e Storyteller's Handbook: the Shadow Lord Revolutionary Guard, Loki's Smile, the Avenging Mother, Crest of the Horn, and Fang Breakers while being countered by Silver Fangs Renewalists and Royalists. Various Garou man eating cults uniting under the False Wendigo spirits that serve Narlthus. Packs under Raven living off the land working with local Corax. Cannibal dwarves of Indian legend that kidnap and torture. Taghairms of Qualmi with their own agenda. Angered bison spirits that must be appeased. There's just so much that can be done.
