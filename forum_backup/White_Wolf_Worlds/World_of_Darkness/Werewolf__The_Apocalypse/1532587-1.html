                I’ve said it before and will say it again - a lot of WEREWOLF 5th Edition’s basic assumptions make so much more sense of you assume that the Weaver is the Big Bad in that version of the setting.

                The difficulties piercing the veil, the emphasis on trying new & different approaches to being a Werewolf, Cockroach being kicked to the curb by the Glass Walkers in favour of Spider (I’m not saying Roach spirits HAVE to be all passive aggressive to the Walkers, but it’s a perfectly valid excuse for a running joke), the running theme that Evil is more like gravity than a wildlife …


                A thallus, I really like the idea of there being a cycle over the course of which the various members of the Triat wax & wane in terms of Power & Dangerousness (With EXALTED showing what happens when the Wyld gets a bit too potent).


                I’m a little torn over Black Fox’s suggested cosmology - it’s a perfectly plausible, indeed intriguing, vision of things that I can imagine using: on the other hand I’m a bit of a purist at heart and enjoy trying to work out what the best balance between the individual Game line’s cosmology and the sense of a World of Darkness greater than a single line might be.

                    Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                    I hold that the Triat is just Garou mythology that anthropomorphizes the metaphysic trinity of Mage. Dynamism becomes the Wyld. Stasis becomes the Weaver. Entropy becomes the Wyrm. It's just the physics of the World of Darkness. It's akin to making up gods based on gravity, electromagnetism, the strong force, and the weak force. They're neither good or evil. But in addition to "Wyrm" being Entropy (the Balance Wyrm), "Wyrm" is also used to denote the force of supernatural evil in the world. Call it Satan, Melkor/Morgoth, Leviathan, Nidhoggr, Ahriman, Typhon, Apep, Wyrm, Oblivion, or whatever.

                    The Garou just join the two and then come up with a reason why things "changed". It's their answer to the philosophical Problem of Evil. It's also why they further subdivided the Wyrm into the Beast of War, Eater of Souls, and the Defiler Wyrm.

                    The Garou are very knowledgeable, but also have their own biases, ignorance, and superstition. I don't believe in silo-ing the original games of the World of Darkness. They were clearly intended to work together and be linked. So this is how I do it. Others may do it a different way, or just prefer each game does its own thing and has all the answers.
                    That is an interesting idea, though of course it runs into issues that the Wyrm has magical servants who worship and follow it and also most of them make the same sort of claims that Garou do. And many non-Garou also acknowledge the Wyrm-as-corrupted (like the werespiders), so it's definitely not just a Garou thing. Plus with the ability to "Sense Wyrm" in older editions, it would ping on both corrupted and older non-corrupted Wyrm entities. So it's seems fairly certain that in Werewolf, at some point the metaphysical concept of entropy was thrown off kilter somehow and is now fundamentally broken.

                    Of course, you tie that back to Mage if you want. I recall various theories from Mage fans that Entropy/Necromancy has been fundamentally broken and twisted (possibly by Voormas or other Barrabi), such that any powerful Necromancer inevitably seeks to tear down the wall between the living and the dead. If we go with the idea that Entropy (the Wyrm) itself was twisted or corrupted at some point, you can use that as a basic concept within both Mage and Werewolf.

                        Plus with the ability to "Sense Wyrm" in older editions, it would ping on both corrupted and older non-corrupted Wyrm entities.
                        The Gift Sense Wyrm obviously detects something objectively. But many STs struggle how to depict it without it becoming a "Detect Evil" spell. There's lot of interpretations. The one I use in my chronicles is that it acts like a Geiger counter. It detects some form of spiritual pollution that is anathema/harmful/alien to Gaia. But it is not infallible, and it doesn't always mean what people may initially think it means. Lots of Wyrm creatures don't detect as the Wyrm. Things like ghosts aren't necessarily evil, but they are also not part of the Gaian cycle, and when their presence affects the Skinlands it detects as Wyrm. And I hold that things like a miner getting black lung or someone dying of cancer also detects as "Wyrm". Not to mention mundane things like industrial pollution. I think Sense Wyrm should be used as the starting point of the PCs investigating something, not a reason to kill everything they find. And of course one reason PENTEX and others like to spread around Wyrm tainted products is that it pretty much makes everything detect as Wyrm, rendering it useless.

                        This is just another restatement of the Problem of Evil. Why do bad things happen to good people? Why is their death, misery, and suffering in the world? Philosophers and theologians have debated this for thousands of years. Why was paradise lost? Is it because Pandora opened the box? Is it because Eve ate the apple? Is it because the world is an illusion? Is it because the Mandate of Heaven has been lost? Is it that Evil actually rules the world and good is wrong? Is it because Melkor created discord in the music of creation? Is it because the Weaver trapped the Wyrm? It's just that in the World of Darkness, supernatural forces are an outright fact and are a part of the equation. So besides the natural evils we experience in the real world, we also have active supernatural forces and creatures inflicting evil on the world.

                                                Okay, I'm a little late in this conversation, but here's my two cents. I believe so, the Wyrm could be good, after all the triad seems to have a function that at least in theory would work, after all the Wyrm should devour life so that it doesn't become overwhelmed, besides I believe that the Weaver and the Wyld itself They could also get out of control and would try to take advantage if given the opportunity.

                                                In Weaver's case I believe there wouldn't be much mystery, it seems very methodical, cold and bound by order, so if it were in control would try one of two options. The first would be to control all life and make the animals, plants and humans follow their functions in the food chain without much differentiation, new species wouldn't be created unnecessarily and there would be strict population control in order to facilitate the administration, human beings would have lives focused on work and the proliferation of the species and the jobs would be decided by their ability alone, and anyone who deviated from the norm would be erased, whether due to opinion or physical/psychological problem, they live basically like drones in this scenario.
