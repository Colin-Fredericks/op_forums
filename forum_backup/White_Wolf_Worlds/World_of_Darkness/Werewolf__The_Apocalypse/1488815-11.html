                        Ultimately, it's not whether or not there is an audience for WoD5. Paradox didn't abandon Martin's grand scheme of trying to turn the WoD back into a cultural fixture, so they need more than being a decent - by TT RPG standards - selling set of books.

                        Back in the 90s, WoD and VtM more specifically, was the second TT RPG people could name after D&D if they weren't RPG people. It was the dark goth younger sibling who didn't give a shit about being like their more popular older sibling, and that attitude had a social potency to it. There was a TV show (and it being produced by Aaron Spelling was a huge deal). There was strange licensing that doesn't seem logical but still worked like the (now) WWE paying oWW to have a wrestler called Gangrel. There were video games. While D&D was the TT RPG giant, the WoD was actually cool wider audiences.

                        It just feels too much like Paradox was hoping the giant gap between CCP buying WW but never making the WoD MMO, and them finally getting V5 out wasn't going to matter to that bigger audience... but clearly that's been miscalculated. Fans of Bloodlines 1 are not flocking to new VtM games; even though BL 1 was flawed as hell on release. People that didn't play the games, but read the fiction books and comics aren't clamoring for more VNs/IFs or just plain old prose fiction.

                        This is, ultimately, where a lot of the things like this thread's title come in. The way WoD5 pulls from the CofD makes it feel like the closest Paradox is going to acknowledge that they picked the WoD over the CofD for brand power, without actually properly assessing what that brand was really worth. They've always known that the CofD was better designed for their large goals, but it was never the cultural phenomenon than the WoD was. So every time Paradox dips into the CofD well, it feels like they're just patching over the flaws introduced by massively overestimating how much the WoD would matter to new people/general audiences without putting in the work that made the original what it was.

                                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                Wizards of the Coast has over 1K employees. Even if most of them are not working on D&D specifically, they are by far larger than any other RPG company past or present.

                                And they have Hasbro backstopping them. As long as Hasbro is happy with it, WotC could sell D&D book at a loss as long as it act as a loss-leader for other products.

                                Paradox does not have Hasbro money. Like everyone else in the TT RPG industry that isn't WotC, their books need to turn some level of profit (even if not their biggest profit) to justify itself.
                                WotC isn't an RPG company. They're a games company, focused on board games and card games. Both are far more profitable than RPGs, specially Magic the Gathering. They have plenty of money to waste if D&D goes bad even before counting on Hasbro. But while not to the same degree, Paradox isn't much different. They're a video game company and are doing very well as such. They can also waste plenty of money with WoD even if it is an utter failure. Not even the production costs of those games are enough to make those companies worry too much, TT RPGs are relatively low cost for their native markets.

                                Yet, no company can just accept a failed project forever for no reason. Sooner or later, unless it is a really adored and low cost pet project for some higher-up (and then, people die), sooner or later it needs to present some benefit for the company. Not all benefits are directly as revenue, but they need to be identifiable and in some extent quantifiable (and companies have means to quantify things most people wouldn't ever imagine being thought in numbers).

                                Sincerely, I see no big difference in their circumstances, although I certainly see in their methods and results. WotC has enough success with D&D to keep it running. They had it since 3rd edition. It is, as far as can be said of an RPG, a success, generating more revenue than it costs, and more importantly, more than the money spent on it would generate elsewhere. For now. But it isn't as comfortable as it could be, and they have been struggling with this conundrum for decades now.

                                For Wizards, though, D&D is an asset on itself. It is still a powerful brand in the general fantasy and fantasy games marketing, it is a cultural behemoth even if a market kitty, and they're well positioned to turn it into advantage for them in other products. D&D helps them selling Medieval Fantasy board games, card games and minis, so they're gold anyway. That is what Paradox can't say about WoD: it is completely out there compared to their core business.

                                Anyway, my point was just that not even WotC is exempt from the maxim that the RPG market is shit. It is shit for them, too, they're just not solely dependent on it.

                                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                Even today, video game consoles - despite massive costs - don't make money on their own. They make money because the lock users into an ecosystem and game sales (even more so as games have become digital licenses ran than an item you own) sell more than enough to make it work since once someone buys your box they have to buy their games for it.
                                By the way, that's kind of what WotC tried with D&D 3rd edition. Their design strategy made supplements an important aspect of the game, even if not strictly necessary, and then lock players in a similar system where you want to buy supplements for the system you already have, also attracting other developers to do the same so they could keep their own books relevant even if your interest wasn't on them properly.

                                It didn't worked as well as they wanted, but it was the first big experiment on expanding the RPG market and we own a lot to the results of this experiment.

                                Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                                I think one problem with this kinda thinking is just how absurdly profitable digital distribution is.
                                That's not how it works.

                                However cheap it is (and it is cheap, indeed), it still has costs. You still need to pay writers, servers and management. And the big question is: could this money be better used elsewhere?

                                Digital distribution is cheap, but it isn't unlimited. You have no guarantee that it will make you any particularly large sum, just that it has little loss to recoup. That's just one aspect of an investment.

                                Paradox had a net income last year of almost half a billion in USD. In comparison, they paid some millions to buy WW and the whole package. So WoD has to make millions just for them to recoup their expenses, and at the same time they're making money with other projects in such a pace they could just buy a whole new White Wolf every quarter. OPP also has "low cost" for digital distribution and definitely aren't making that much money, despite selling several lines that are astounding successes in the RPG market in their own right.

                                Certainly digital distribution reduced costs greatly. But in the big picture of business this is just one aspect, specially if you have a wider plan. If there's one thing I don't think they did wrong was to put some money into making the physical books. They're an investment in the IP, not a main distribution method.

                                                        Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                                        That's a bit hair splitting.
                                                        No, it isn't.

                                                        WotC didn't grew only after being purchased by Hasbro, it was purchased by Hasbro because it grew with card and board games. At 1993 they were just a minor publishing house when they published MtG. They rejected RoboRally because they couldn't afford basic production. Two years later they were profiting almost three times the amount they would later spend on buying TSR plus FRPG, the owners of L5R card games. In 1997 the Pokemon Card Game was launched and profits skyrocketed again. They announced earlier already that they would buy TSR.

                                                        Since then, while they always had a close proximity with RPGs and published many, their main revenue comes by far from card and board games. Including the same RoboRally they initially couldn't make. They were far bigger than any RPG company, and only in 1999 Hasbro bought them. This financial success didn't came from Hasbro, and it didn't came from RPGs either. It came from other games.

                                                        Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                                        The degree is enough money to buy Paradox ten times over. That's a very big degree.
                                                        Not in the context of their "meddling" with RPGs. For both those projects are dear, but in the end cost a fraction of their revenue and, unfortunately, doesn't profit much better.

                                                        Yet, I do agree that they're heading in very distinct directions, and WotC has both more to gain from its ties to D&D than Paradox from its ties to WoD, and generally speaking they had enough success with D&D to justify at least to keep trying, it is not a complete failure or even anything near it. Don't worry, as little as it is, Hasbro wouldn't let them keep such a pet project so long if it weren't profitable at least enough to earn its stay.

                                                        Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                                        They're profitable, but by video game industry standards they're still small potatoes. They're doing well by the standards of a relatively small studio that has found a niche in some popular RTS titles.
                                                        More or less. They had quite some success in the last two years and are expanding, so they're already being seen as a name to pay attention for investors.

                                                        But regardless of that, my point again is about their RPG projects. However small Paradox is among video game companies, they're still orders of magnitude above RPG publishers.

                                                        It is like comparing Jeff Bezos to someone with a few millions in total assets, but then asking if they can afford to eat only at Taco Bell. It doesn't matter how many times Bezos is richer, because it won't put a dent on either's fortunes.

                                                        The difference between WotC and Paradox in their success with their RPGs is that WotC had a decent strategy and actually good development. Not just ok, but award-winning good.
