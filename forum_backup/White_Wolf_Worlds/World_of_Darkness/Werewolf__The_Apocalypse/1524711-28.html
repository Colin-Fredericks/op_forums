                                                    On one hand the book does seem to indicate that Garou are now more tied into human society by mentioning so many jobs as archetypes but on the other hand the "Werewolves are terrible at living a normal life because of Rage."-bit is still there as well. And I think the latter as the chosen perspective is more convincing because mechanically the game still sets it up that way. In the Advantages-section you have one Merit and the description for the Resources-Background to convey that idea:
                                                    • First you got the Merit "Day Job" which is described like this: "Despite the fact that you’re a goddamn werewolf, you somehow manage to hold down steady employment, or at least the appearance of it. This post can provide everything from an alibi for your whereabouts to the protection of your hairy secret. It doesn’t necessarily provide a noteworthy income — that’s Resources, though a combination of a Day Job and significant Resources can make for a curious werewolf indeed." (BTW I hate these Merits that are like a Background+ where instead of just buying extra dots in the Background you're buying a modifier, with safehouses or control over territory, I kinda get it but this Day Job Merit is just a needless complication; dots in Resources and Allies just do the job of the Day Job Merit just fine)
                                                    • Then you get this description for Resources: "Being Garou often clashes with traditional employment or even business hours. Dots of Resources provide an income for you to maintain your standard of living, but you must detail the source of your income and the form this Background takes."
                                                    If you portray it as presented every PC without the Day Job Merit doesn't have steady employment (thereby making it clear that it's a special/unusual thing for a Garou to have that) although I don't really like the idea of making players jump through a hoop like that. But anyway, I think if W5 wanted to have Garou with closer ties to humanity, I don't think they did a really good job of that.

                                                    And as an aside... It's noticeable that there's one particular writer who has this acerbic tone. Like here "Despite the fact you're a goddamn werewolf" or "For all of the self-glorifying tales that old Garou tell around the fires, younger werewolves note that the Garou Nation didn’t collapse because the Litany was so goddamn perfect.​"... whoever that is just loves the word "goddamn". Probably the same person who also wrote that Ahroun-bit.

                                                            Originally posted by Knightingale View Post
                                                            • First you got the Merit "Day Job" which is described like this: "Despite the fact that you’re a goddamn werewolf, you somehow manage to hold down steady employment, or at least the appearance of it. This post can provide everything from an alibi for your whereabouts to the protection of your hairy secret. It doesn’t necessarily provide a noteworthy income — that’s Resources, though a combination of a Day Job and significant Resources can make for a curious werewolf indeed." (BTW I hate these Merits that are like a Background+ where instead of just buying extra dots in the Background you're buying a modifier, with safehouses or control over territory, I kinda get it but this Day Job Merit is just a needless complication; dots in Resources and Allies just do the job of the Day Job Merit just fine)
                                                            This Merit is very much an "Air-Breathing Mermaids" situation. By creating the Merit, the designers have extracted what is presumably meant to be a default Garou ability - gainful employment - and charged you extra for it. Like paid DLC in a video game where the material was obviously designed to be a part of the core experience.

                                                            It's a tax on character creation, for players who stupidly wanted to run their PCs the way the example characters were presented.


                                                            It's clear W5 was written with the intent that PCs should no longer be hyper-divorced from normal humanity. To open up the kinds of characters that can be run or stories that can be told. (One of the few ways W5 hypothetically increases options rather than strips them away). Absence of kinfolk reinforces this arrangement, making it more necessary for Garou to get real jobs.

                                                            Evidently, no one informed the rules writers of this intent. Between the "Day Job" Merit and how Rage infects every action, the actual effect in practice is deeply against Garou ever maintaining employment. Either the rules writers had very different ideas than the fluff writers, or no one considered/playtested the implications of the Rules As Written.

                                                            Rules tell stories of their own. Even more readily than any flavor text or authorial intent.

                                                            At least in older editions, Garou had a built-in mechanism for justifying their income, if any. Without Kinfolk, and with the rules coming down firmly against Garou employment, players are left to tie themselves into knots rationalizing how their characters survive in a market economy. Once again, W5 abdicates responsibility for helping gaming groups tell stories, making their customers do all the work instead.

                                                            What are people buying when they pick up this book again?
