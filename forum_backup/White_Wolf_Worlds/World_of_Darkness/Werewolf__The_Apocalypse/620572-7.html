                                                    Originally posted by Lyrics Of War View Post
                                                    How different is it currently, between games? I've only done apocalypse for Cwod, but for nwod I've done vamp and werewolf and noticed they had similar systems all around. Never occurred to me that classic world would be radically different.
                                                    You know how in nWoD they have that neat blue book called World of Darkness, with all the core rules in it? How even with Second Edition copying those rules (updated by GMC), they just copy the main rules? And how they even have some common rules covering all the powers, like the rule on Clash of Wills?

                                                    So, how to put this? In cWoD we don't have NOTHING like it whatsoever.

                                                    Each book basically have its own rules. And by that I mean, its own rules, really. They only share most (not all) of the sheet and the premise of rolling Att+Hab against set dif. Every other thing in the book will be different in each core. They change initiative systems, grapple rules, everything.

                                                    They are proud of traits that don't exist in the other books. You know how a lot of Gifts use the target Permanent Rage as dif? well, no other splat have Rage whatsoever. Actually, most don't have ANYTHING mechanically resembling Rage. Heck, not even the rules to create a mortal sheet are the same. What if a mortal runs into something scary? In Vampire the mortal rolls Courage. In Mage Courage isn't even a thing.

                                                    Hell, the inconsistencies from one system to the other are, seriously, everywhere. Simply because in the end they are distinct systems. There are no cWoD Storyteller System. There is a system for each and every game line, none of them bound to follow the others. They simply never wanted to cross-compatible. Even sharing the same "base" system and, arguably, the same setting.
