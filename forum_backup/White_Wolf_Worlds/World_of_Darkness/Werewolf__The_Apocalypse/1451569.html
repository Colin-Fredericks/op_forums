    What Makes a Great Werewolf?

    06-10-2021, 04:40 PM


    I agree with the above video. It's why I've never liked that garou can spend a rage for a painless, instant transformation, or that frenzy was so rare, or that a cheap, common rite exists so that garou can keep their clothes when transforming. I never feel like a werewolf when I'm playing a garou. I do occasionally get in the mood to play WTA just the same, but only when I am in the mood to play a superhero game. WTA fills the same occasional itch that Aberrant and other superhero games do. It has never filled the itch to play an actual werewolf.

      I agree that it is too easy for a WtA chronicle to turn into something that doesn't seem like werewolves at all, but either high fantasy or some other unique supernatural creature. It's something I routinely struggle with whenever I run a Werewolf chronicle. Unless the ST makes a real effort to respect the source material, it can quickly become something that almost seems unrelated to the genre. All the World of Darkness games are afflicted by this, but I think WtA is harmed the most by it.

      One issue is that we have wildly divergent source material, and the game provides us little tools to emulate them. Or when they did provide tools, it quickly forgot to incorporate them into the setting (very little was done with the concept of Lunatics outside the Valkenburg sourcebook; the Curse is routinely ignored in character templates and NPC descriptions; despite having fetishes, talens, and rites little of them seem inspired by actual legend or source material; and iconic werewolf identity markers are sometimes included in Flaws, but they rarely show up in NPCs or are reflected in Pure Breed).

      The werewolf of folklore and legend isn't some monstrous creature. It's a person who uses evil magic to turn themselves into a large predator animal and either kills people or eats their livestock.

      The "werewolf" of history appears to people we would now call psychopaths or serial killers. This is obvious from a lot of the werewolf trials Europe held in the early modern era (though some "werewolves" are other cases).

      The werewolf of cinema shows werewolves as bipdeal human-wolf hybrids because filmmakers need to use human actors to portray the monster. Perhaps to avoid depictions of Satanism which was a touchy subject under the Hays Code, werewolves also stopped by a kind of witch or black magician and were turned into a tragic figure dealing with a curse. This would later become the iconic depiction in future media (books, movies, roleplaying games).

      So you can pick or the other, or try to blend them all. I prefer to try for a blend of them, and the game actually supports it though it never talks about how to do it.

      There have been various books and short stories about werewolves, but nothing iconic like Dracula. The best literary story seems to be Guy Endore's The Werewolf of Paris, but very few people have ever read it. I personally like Steven King's Cycle of the Werewolf which may be the werewolf book that has had the most readers, but it's mainly about the townsfolk and not the monster.

      Whatever the source material, this isn't something that seems natural for a multiplayer roleplaying game. I do have to give it credit for creating a society that explains why multiple werewolves would form a "party" so they can do something. And since one criteria of the Lon Chaney. Jr. movies is that werewolves are sympathetic monsters (since the human doesn't control their horrific actions) who fight other monsters and therefore indirectly protect humanity, the general gist of the game is OK.

      However, when the Rage regional sourcebooks were produced, they did not do an adequate job of depicting the NPCs or their society as sufficiently werewolf-like. Some good bits of old fashioned werewolfery arise here and there, but they are easily overwhelmed by other elements foreign to the genre.

      Worse, as the game developed, it seemed to quickly become more like a political platform to push a specific kind of left wing politics than depicting werewolves. And once Revised took over, it really enforced the idea that the game was primarily about Lawful Good versus Chaotic Evil, and they purged almost everything that did not project Garou into a good light. The man eating camps were eliminated; the skeevy thing the Bone Gnawers did to survive were eliminated; the massive failures of the Silver Fangs were overlooked; the terrible and sometimes treasonous activities of the Shadow Lords were ignored, and so on. Good triumphed everywhere so it made the Garou even less like monsters. Some people really wanted them to be unequivocable good guys instead of monsters of horror from legend.

      But I think most of the elements we need are in the game. Unfortunately, they're mostly buried, forgotten, or neglected.

      This is how I try to handle it in my Werewolf chronicles so it seems more like "werewolf" and less like "captain planet with furries."

      1) Keep the home sept/caern small and preferably in the wilderness/rural. This allows for more personal stories dealing with Rage and the Curse.

      2) To introduce more Garou for politics, conflict, etc. have the game include other septs that can be visited for multiple reasons. Sept based politics are important as it keep things local.

      3) To accomplish this, you really need a wide geographic area for the game to take place in. Don't place it in a large city. Your setting should be a large geographic area. This is something the Rage books get right, although it seems most readers neglect that lesson. Make extensive use of the anruth concept so your PCs can visit multiple septs and learn the regional politics.

      4) Make extensive use of Lunatics. Lost Cubs, and Ronin in your setting and these typically are the concepts that best match up to popular culture tropes of werewolves. It's just that these aren't the PCs or the majority of werewolves. They are the exceptions, though they can be a large minority. The PCs can then play out these stories as they deal with these NPCs. Or encourage their use as part of PC backstories.

      5) I encourage PCs to take the Flaws that best emulate traditional werewolf stories if they are simply looking for build points (Mark of the Predator, Banned or Forced Transformation, Cursed, Sign of the Wolf). I also use them extensively for NPCs especially those with high Pure Breed. Some Garou lineages are known by the same Flaws appearing time and time again in that line.

      6) I include much more appropriate antagonists for them to fight, extensively using monsters from folklore, legend, and the horror genre. The game has some good concepts, but it lacks diversity of opponents, and those that are good are often overused. And there's a lot of useless crap in the Book of the Wyrm.

      It's gotten things to a point I can accept it as a real game about werewolves. But I am constantly trying to refine it.

      A major issue is that an iconic feature of werewolf movies is what we would call frenzy, but frenzy in game almost always disrupts the game and story and lead to complications no one wants to deal with.

      As for things like the Rite of Talisman Dedication, I am constantly of two minds. As an ST, I think it subverts the feel of a "proper" Werewolf game. As a player, it's a real pain in the ass as it constantly interrupts the game and prevents me from getting things done which is just frustrating and not fun. So it's something I've learned to live with. Usually a good balance is that there is a limited number of clothes that are a Garou PC's "work clothes" which they use on mission, but their other clothes are not dedicated. And when I want to run a traditional werewolf scene, I pick these other times to do so.

      The key cinematic "werewolf" inspirations for me when running the game are The Wolfman (1941), The Howling (1981), Wolfen (1981), An American Werewolf in London (1981), Company of Wolves (1984), Silver Bullet (1985), Wolf (1994), and Ginger Snaps (2000). None of them are perfect in my opinion, but each of them contains something I want to show up in my game.

              Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post
              I never feel like a werewolf when I'm playing a garou.
              I want to talk about this separately because my first post was mainly about the setting and how an ST can set things up to better emulate the genre. But certainly it is very easy to ignore that you are a werewolf as a PC. Many times as an observer looking in, other PCs don't seem like werewolves either. That is less the case for Vampire because there are certain inbred mechanics to vampire PCs that force a certain level of emulation like Blood Pool and the need to hunt/feed (and that the Disciplines of VtM are really suited to emulate traditional vampires while the Gifts of WtA are obvious add-ons unique to the game). So what can be done on the player's end to create that feeling?

              Unfortunately, if you go by the cinematic stereotypes, all the things you might add to the PCs would be aspects that would take away the agency of a character, and players don't like that. So I'm not sure what could be added to the PC mechanics to better emulate the feel of werewolves, but which would be welcomed by the players. That's very different from vampire films when most of what a vampire does is a willing choice except for things like fleeing from crosses or being compelled to feed when hungry. Most cinematic werewolf stuff would seem to take agency away, unless you somehow make it a Jekyll and Hyde scenario where you have two faces to the same PC depending on whether they are homid or one of the other forms, and I don't think that's really viable as a standard for characters (though an interesting twist for individual characters).

              There are certain elements embedded in the game that harken to this. The Curse, Frenzy, certain ways you gain Rage, various Flaws, but they're not the focus of the game so either get ignored or elided. It takes a commitment from the player to keep the focus on their predatory instincts and animalism in homid form and elsewhen.

              This is why I think other inspirations for werewolves besides cinematic examples need to be included to get a proper feel. This is a big reason why I've made several threads over the years on including more folklore, legend, and literary ideas into the game because at least it's something that establishes more of a lore. Having a wolf strap as a fetish, making wolf salve as a kind of talen or rite, or incorporating more ways to trigger the Change in a pre-Change Garou than just waiting for it to happen naturally (being bitten, eating certain plants, eating wolf meat or bloody raw meat, etc.) at least adds more elements of the werewolf mythos to the game. These are things players can at least decide to become part of their character sheet or backstory.

              In terms of making your PC feel more like a werewolf, I think first you need to determine what iconic elements you feel are missing, and then figure out how they could be emulated.

              To a certain degree, if you want to make enough changes it may be easier to blow up the setting and just start over. That's fine, but not very helpful. I do think there are enough elements either buried in WtA or can be easily added to it without changing it into something else, that the game is salvageable.
