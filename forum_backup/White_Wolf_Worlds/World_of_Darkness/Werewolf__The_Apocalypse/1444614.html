                  Garou often have different perspectives than humans on creatures of legend. It's not just the Wendigo. Look at Fenris. Humans see Fenris as an evil figure related to Jormungandr who will lead to the destruction of the world. To the Garou, Fenris is a noble, though ferocious, spirit who is an enemy to Jormungandr. There are other spirits that are similar. You reconcile real world myth and legend to the Garou's version by saying that the human perspective is incomplete and ignorant while the Garou's version is more correct, and here are the reasons why the human version is "incorrect".

                  For my games, this is how I describe the Wendigo spirit. It's a spirit of survival in a harsh winter environment. So while it teaches survival, it is also very harsh itself. Harsh teachers provide harsh lessons so that their children learn what they need in order to avoid calamity when it really matters. But the spirit has certain taboos it punishes, and one of those is cannibalism, even survival cannibalism. It punishes people who engage in it. (In my chronicles, while other tribes often believe the Wendigo violate the Litany against man eating because of their misconceptions of the Wendigo spirit, the tribe actually has the least problems with man eating of all the tribes precisely because their totem is completely against it).

                  The game Wendigo itself is not actually a spirit of cannibalism. This is actually made explicit in Ways of the Wolf which introduced the False Wendigo spirit. The False Wendigo was once part of Wendigo's brood, but was corrupted by the Wyrm, and it is this False Wendigo spirit that humans identify with the wendigo myths. Unfortunately, this was not built on in the actual tribebooks which instead attempted a different means to reconcile game lore and human legend, and I think it wasn't very successful. The entire story about Sasquatch and Wendigo was unneccesarily complicated and muddied the waters rather than clarifying things.

                  In other words, the creature of legend you identify as a spirit of greed is not Wendigo, it is one of the False Wendigo.

                  The actual Wendigo tribal totem does not correspond with the real Native American legends, though there is of course a lot of overlaps. That Garou tribe took Wendigo as their tribal totem because it is a valuable spirit ally to have in a place like northern North America.

                  So why did the developers name this tribe after the wendigo? For the same reasons they used Fenris for one of the tribes. It was a known creature of legend and folklore, identifiable with a geographic area, that provided an easy hook to base a Garou tribe around. Simply by association, you know any werewolf tribe associated with the name "Wendigo" has ties to harsh winters, is located around Canada, and is probably a group of fierce fighters. And that is how the tribe is presented. For whatever problems you may have with Wendigo, at least it is a lot more coherent than the Chimera spirit and its tribe the Stargazers, as that spirit has practically nothing in common with the actual Greek legend.

                              Something I've gone on about at length before, is that beyond changing the terminology, there's not a whole lot you can do without overhauling WtA with a full on reboot. Even then you face a tough choice: You can make the Tribes more global to reduce a lot of the more problematic issues with linking some of them so tightly to specific (and frequently very pop-culture versions of) real world cultures, but then you lose a lot of the representational aspect of having prominent groups within the game that are focused on indigenous cultures. If you want to make sure all the Tribes are doing more to positively center the plights of indigenous and marginalized peoples, 13 Tribes is never going to be enough to do so without tossing groups together that comes off as insensitive.

                              You can try to split the difference like Forsaken did (Tribes are global spiritual philosophies which strains the word tribe pretty hard, with Lodges being subgroups within the Tribes that can be customized towards tackling whatever issues you want to focus on), but it's hard to avoid that really falling to one side or the other in how people end up playing the game.

                              And you have to deal with the issue of the well-intentioned move to use the Fera to add representation to groups the Garou don't cover only creates an air of exoticism where wanting to play an indigenous person from huge swathes of the world means you have to play a special character type that isn't designed to play nicely with the main PC types.
