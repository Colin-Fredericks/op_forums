                                Originally posted by heinrich View Post
                                And sure, there are no rules on what throwing dirt into a spirits eyes would do. Penalising all Gnosis seems inappropriate. Penalising all rolls made for perception purposes might be better. Reducing the range of actions a spirit can take might also work.
                                This is kind of why I lean into pack tactics here. If you use any of the existing normal moves for trying to blind a target, it doesn't mesh well with the level of abstraction, while a pack tactic can be better customized to this function. And being powered by the supernatural bonds of a pack, you can handwave some of the fine details that disappear (perhaps a better option for now than remove?) when you get into spirit combat.

                                Does a spirit need eyes for seeing stuff or not is an interesting question. I can't remember anything on that in the books.
                                As I mentioned, Realm Sense is a all-spirits-get-it Charm. It's a simple Gnosis roll to "sense" anything going on in any Realm that matches the spirit's Domain. They don't even have to be there, or care about the Gauntlet if they're sense the physical reflection of something (high level Glass elementals can just roll Gnosis diff 6 to sense what's going on in any major city with enough glass clad buildings, Gauntlet be damned). The only real way around Realm Sense is using Summoning or Binding to get the spirit out of a Realm that matches their nature.

                                Realm Sens written kind of insanely broadly, but it's not exactly a new to W20 issue. But if a spirit doesn't need to "physically" look to sense what's happening in any Realm that matches their domain... they just have to think about it... it implies that any sensory organs they have are supplemental at best.

                                Therefore it might make sense that an animal spirit is relying on the sense the material animal is also relying on.
                                But that tends to run counter to how spirits are constructed. A falcon spirit should have a Charm that massively boosts the power of their eyesight to represent how important sight is to their nature. That's how the books handle that. So said falcon spirit might be really used to some big boost to Gnosis rolls around eye-sight, and clawing their eyes weakens them to "just" normal spirit senses, but just forcing them to be sight based and subject to eye gouging and blindness doesn't really match how the books address things.

                                It also runs into a thematic issue where different spirits of the same broad strokes types can represent very different aspects. A falcon spirit that's focused on the falcon's noble symbolism, or incredible speed, or grasping talons, might be too "niche" to warrant their eyes being a weakness to exploit. We have examples of Totems of the same type of spirit (ex: Sphinx) that are still divergent enough to be different types of Totem (there's three different versions of Sphinx, Cunning, Respect, and War, in the books, and Cunning/Respect overlap a lot otherwise but are quite distinct from War)., and have different pack Bans. Merlin (the bird, and those close to Falcon) and Salmon both have two different versions as pack Totems to further illustrate how different individual spirits under the same broad umbrella and differ. All of which is a very long winded way of saying the default idea of building spirits by adding Charms rather than adding unique weakness is not out of step with how the game works.

                                Sure, some spirits have Domain sense or realm sense, but it has it's own issues, like activation versus permanent power, accuracy, etc.
                                Just to reiterate, Realm Sense is a Charm every spirit has, and while rolled to use, it doesn't have any specifications about things like accuracy as a concern,

                                        Originally posted by heinrich View Post
                                        I mean, the Glass elemental would still need to specify the area to look at first before rolling, right.
                                        Yes, sorry if that was unclear. I meant "any" in the sense of, "can target any city to extend their senses to," not, "can be sensing all cities with one roll."

                                        I can't recall reading about such bonuses in the books, but that is how I would handle it.
                                        Well, they're pretty much always Charms. Most of the supplements that detail 10+ spirits end up introducing new Charms, many of which are about giving a spirit some unique niche power to make them stand out and more able to suit their narrative purposes mechanically that the extant Charms would allow. They also frequently copy Gifts or Rites in effect rather than reinvent the wheel.

                                        How much a given attack on the eyes would harm the total eyesight of a falcon spirit, I'd leave open to a lot of factors, like successes on the attacking player's part but certainly also narrative needs of the given scene.
                                        It feels like the simplicity of spirit abstraction is encouraging over doing it in the other direction. Blinding someone in normal combat isn't all that complicated; largely because there's a default status of being blinded with rules and the maneuver(s) to inflict it only need to do things like adjust the difficulty, etc. In normal combat, if one character tries to blind another character, you just use the maneuvers for doing so and move on. "Narrative needs," aren't really going to weigh heavily into adjudicating these things. Even if the player's come up with something not directly covered by the rules (though WtA has a lot of maneuvers at this point), most STs are going to just best fit in an extant move instead of thinking too hard about the narrative needs of the action.

                                        Most of the narrative impact of such things are going to really matter after-the-fact anyway. How other characters react to a character using a dirty move isn't really a big deal to resolving the move in the first place (at least not in how the WoD rules are constructed).

                                        If I describe a spirit and a scene I want the players to interact with it and the players be given the opportunity to affect the situation in a non-standard way.
                                        Outside of house rules for more interesting spirit interactions (which I'd enjoy seeing more STs do since it doesn't seem like WtA is getting official rules for it), I think the problem is that once we're talking about spirit combat, we're solidly entrenched in the "standard way," of dealing with the situation. Even more so for the already quite fight-happy Garou. And even while normal combat has more tactical depth to it... WtA's rules don't really encourage engaging much with it unless your ST puts in a lot of work to make it worthwhile.

                                        IME players that are invested in dealing with spirits in non-standard ways are going to be looking for non-combat solutions in the first place. CofD's codification of every spirit having a Ban that it can't do, as a tool the PCs can use to exploit against it, is what I feel players want to engage with while trying to be cunning/smart/etc. rather making spirits more subject to normal combat maneuvers (not that more unity there would be bad if executed well just for not needing different rules for different fights).
