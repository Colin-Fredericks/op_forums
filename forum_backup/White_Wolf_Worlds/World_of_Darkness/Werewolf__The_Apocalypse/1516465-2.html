    Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post
    But why not just make your own Werewolf property? If W5's devs plan to make bank off of licensing, I'm guessing they plan for the license to cost $$$, but I can't see anyone paying anything significant to license WTA right now. W20 featured a broad, "CW-able" setting which you could turn into a TV show akin to Vampire Diaries, which I thankfully know way too much about because of an excellent Jenny Nicholson video. CW shows are full of bombastic, stupid, over the top supernatural and superhero settings with way too much going on, way too much angst and drama, and Fera + a huge spirit world + endless weird monsters which look like humans + prosthetics or some light CG is great for them.

    Instead we have a much smaller, much less ambitious, much more anaemic and *sad* version of Werewolf. One where the writers and characters seem to be embarrassed to be Werewolves, even. How do you market that to consumers? More importantly, how do you market it to a TV, film or game company?
    Crinos, spirit worlds, enemies with deformed forms, all of these are expensive, it's much more attractive for investors if you make something cheaper that doesn't require a lot of resources or prowess to make, Crinos and Hipos would require a lot of CG to work and it's very easy to to get rough, so limit to Glabro which only requires makeup; complex spirit worlds would also require CG so the idea is to limit their use so when necessary you can invest in scenes with good figuration in key parts of the story, it can look cool and it's not as expensive as creating an effect; another option would be to make the scenes in the umbra in animation; as for the enemies and other shapeshifters, they must want something like supernatural, put teeth, contact lenses, CG in the eyes, and bigger nails, nothing much big and the biggest differentiation would be in mental powers that don't require special effects or if they do it's a small thing. As a fan this may seem off-putting, but from an investor's point of view this seems more attractive than a more expensive series. In the case of games, WOD already had games, but none expressive. So... these elements could be an attempt at that, but it could also be something else.

            I have to disagree on a few things here; First of all both Heart of the Forest and Earthblood used some version of the W5 setting. What with Earthblood crinos being a battle ender and Heart of the Forest treating the Fenrir as semi-bad guys.

            But more than that, if they wanted to make W5 into shows or games (especially GAMES since it is paradox we are talking about), they would have dropped the 'protect the Earth' aspect entirely.

            See, the reason Bloodlines and most VtM games are easy to get into for non-fans is that the core of the vampire stays mostly the same. You are an undead creature that feeds on blood, the rest can be learned later.

            But in terms of successful werewolf games, being a werewolf is something you acquire on your own if you want to and often the werewolf isn't even the main point. Bloodborne is mostly about werewolves at the start but turns into a cosmic horror story. Skyrim and World of Warcraft have a werewolf plot IF you want to engage with it.

            (Also, you'll notice only one of those games is even fully about werewolves, the rest just have an additional werewolf flavour.)

            Most WtA games start with the state of the planet and so far that has clearly been a deterrent because it takes away from the cursed fantasy of being a werewolf. Vampire can have its primogens and camarillas, because the core of a vampire has not changed. But even W5 starts with 'the Earth is doomed, the apocalypse is here, you are the chosen warrior of Gaia', which is not a cursed being trying to find balance.

            Another thing, from my experience, most people wanting to play werewolves in videogames generally want the werewolf-form to last LONGER. Not be a short burst.

                        On a bit of an unrelated note, another curious thing about the latest update is that on the first page you get rules for "Grappling". Now this is weird because last October this document called "Expanded Mechanics" was released with this goal:
                        (If you have certain printings of Vampire: The Masquerade, you have a lot of this already, but we want to both make it available to everyone and to free up 20-ish pages of core book space for things that are specific to each of the games themselves. We can also update it as a living document, as new material or recurring edge cases merit it.)
                        And in this Expanded-Mechanics-document you have a section on "Additional Conflict Options" and one of them is "Grappling". So originally the idea with the Expanded Mechanics document was that this corebook wouldn't have to include things like rules for "Grappling" because you're supposed to use this free pdf for that stuff instead. But clearly, it's still in the W5 corebook (and it is also still a copy-paste from the same bit in the V5 corebook - minus the rules for vampire-biting, of course) which, I guess, means that they abandoned the idea of this Expanded Mechanics pdf...

                                        Can I say, a little off topic, but tangentially related to the idea of making $$$ by licensing, rather than through book sales - I hate "IP".

                                        Not a setting, not a world, not a lore, not a story, nor a game nor a book. It's just... IP.

                                        Intellectual Property.

                                        A possession. Something you can leverage and finance and sell and rent out and try to bank on. An investment.

                                        Obviously we live in a society where any successful piece of art, outside of fringe stuff like street art and hobbyists (and sometimes not even then!) is a commercial endeavor. That's a given. But when it becomes apparent that something beloved like Werewolf, Vampire, Mage etc. has been reduced to an "IP", a problem child which needs to be poked and prodded until it can be profitable and 'Make It On The Big Screen' or 'Get A Vidya Game!' is just... sad. I make my living working in a hospital, I'm not an artist, and I write in my spare time as an amateur, a hobbyist. Still, were I a creative lead on a new edition of a beloved tabletop game, I'd want to make it as good as I possibly could. Make it a fun, balanced, interesting game, one which sells copies of its corebook. Merch, licensing, supplements, that all comes later.

                                        Unfortunately when a company buys an "IP" it wants to squeeze blood from the stone, and WoD has never been... that profitable. It's about as famous as TTRPG settings get without literally being D&D, but I don't think anyone has ever gotten rich off of VTM, even at its height. It isn't a good investment. It's just a good game. I wish that were enough!
