    Rank 4 Silver Fang Gifts

    Mastery: Suits the Silver Fangs to a tee, though it should probably include Garou Kinfolk. This Gift lets you force Garou to obey you, including BSDs and Ronin etc.

    Charisma + Leadership, difficulty of Wits +3, so 4-8 typically. On a success you get the target to fulfil a single non-suicidal command lasting for one turn per success. Your Pure Breed probably adds to your pool here.

    Did you notice? There's no cost. No Gnosis, no WP, you just roll.

    At Rank 4 you can completely change your role in combat and just start going "BSD #1, help us attack your Alpha!", "BSD #2, help us attack your Alpha!", "BSD #3, help us attack your Alpha!" Gradually you can completely shift the action economy to suit your party. Auto-take for any Rank 4 Silver Fang. It's also important to note that being Rank 5 or 6 grants no protection against this Gift, RAW.

    Oh, and for what it's worth, based on what we're told about the Silver Fangs and how they see the other Tribes, this Gift has probably done more harm to Gaia and the Garou Nation than most others.

    Mindblock: This, with Mastery, feels like a bit of a kid in the playground situation. "I can control other Werewolves! Nuh-uh you can't control me back, I'm immune!"

    Attempts to control your mind are difficulty 10. Lots of enemies in the WOD will struggle to possess, control and command you as a result. It's worth noting that this grants no immunity to powers which are based on emotion; you can be terrified into freezing, Presence powers will still work on you, and so on. There's no reason not to take this; it's passive and will likely come up often enough to be worth the investment. It also lets you confront Vampires and other Silver Fangs / Shadow Lords with more confidence.

    Sidestep Death: Flavour-wise this might seem a bit off, but assassination attempts on rulers are very prominent throughout history. Personally I would also have this on the Bone Gnawer list, taught by Rat instead of Lion.

    This Gift lets you spend 3 WP, once a scene, to completely evade an attack which would put you below Crippled (before Soak). It could be a nuke, or a gunshot, or a claw strike. Some teleportation is baked in; chances are you'll shift a few inches away for 99% of attacks, but very rarely you could be thrown a huge distance away.

    Ideally you don't want to need this Gift, but I can't see any reason not to take it. You could use it for non-suicidal suicide-bombing, though Stepping Sideways already has that covered. Much like Mindblock this isn't exciting, and it feels a little bit... boring? Like a power baked up to remove all potential weaknesses from your character, a "ha- did you think that would work on me? ¬‿¬" situation.

        Rank 4 Stargazer Gifts

        Preternatural Awareness: This Gift makes you harder to hit for a whole scene, and handily subtracts dice instead of manipulating difficulty. This can be better or worse than manipulating difficulty.

        Spending Gnosis and rolling Perception + Athletics (difficulty 7) activates the Gift. For every success, all attackers subtract 1 die from all their attacks to hit the Stargazer. At the upper end you can expect 3-4 successes. Subtracting 3-4 dice from all incoming attacks can make it so that weaker enemies have no chance to hit you (remember that many spirits have a WP on the lower end!), and removing 3-4 dice from the attack rolls of powerful attackers can reduce their hit chance or reduce their overall damage output by reducing successes. Since it lasts for a Scene and applies to all incoming attacks, there's really no reason not to take this or not to use this!

        Mindblock: See the Silver Fang list.

        Strike the Air: See the Children of Gaia list. Arguably you should either take Preternatural Awareness or Strike the Air, since they have overlaps in their utility, but arguably it's worth having both.

            Speed Beyond Thought always reminds me of how much the WtA books seem to forget how distances work. Like, I'm find with "distance" being a funky concept when it comes to spirit magic so some Gifts work variably because spirit magic doesn't care about precise human measurements. But these sorts of Gifts just modify your mundane speed. Speed Beyond Thought lets a Dex 5 (in homid) character travel about 2,200 miles. While is impressive in a vacuum, when is that actually practical? Like, you can run straight across the US from one coast to the other without having to fly... but you could also just fly on a Silver Fang's private jet. Or use a Moon Bridge and get there a few orders of magnitude faster (even if the Moon Bridge isn't going to land you exactly where you want to go, a few hundred miles is still easy enough to deal with for a Garou). The normal long running rules lets any Garou travel that kind of distance on foot in 2-3 days depending on their Stamina without any powers beyond basic shifting for extra Stamina.

            There's just zero use case for it because the distance is way too far to be useful for shorter trips, and ends up in a category of way better options (most of which don't cost XP to get), and it doesn't do anything if that massive distance runs into things like crossing large bodies of water/etc.

                    Rank 4 Elder Brother Gifts

                    Call Elemental: It would be very easy, and a mistake, to simply view this as a "combat summon power". It can be that, but it can also achieve so much more.

                    This Gift costs one Gnosis, you have to roll Gnosis at a difficulty of the local Gauntlet, and then you have to roll a Manipulation + Occult (difficulty 7) to convince the Elemental to help you, which is a little annoying but flavourful. If you convince them to help you (convincing the water elemental to be your guide, convincing the air elemental to calm the storm threatening your city, convincing the fire elemental to incinerate your foes, convincing the earth elemental to guard and block a path while you escape) they will, RAW, remain under the control of the ST. They're an NPC, not a simple extension of your character sheet, and they'll behave in a manner that is helpful from their elemental and sometimes alien POV.

                    That means the Earth Elemental won't likely be convinced to let your ally who you thought was dead but was just KO'd pass (you convinced them to block the way!), and the Fire Elemental you convince to help you incinerate your foes may well incinerate an enemy who you had thoughts of trying to redeem who you weren't expecting to show up, or indeed blow up someone who was being mind controlled. So while this power is amazing, it should be used with caution.

                    The Elemental statblocks we are given in W20 are for minor elementals, but they are all we have. Simply reading the Gift you might think this one would be unrated (black) or perhaps Amber, but in truth it's a solid Green due to its versatility.

                    All spirits have Airt Sense, Materialize, Realm Sense and Re-form, in addition to the Charms listed.

                    Earth
                    W9, R4, G5, E20
                    Charms: Armour, Umbraquake

                    Earth Elementals are hard to hit, soak well, hit well, and their damage is middling (though a Willpower of 9 will carry over for more damage via successes on the hit). Armour lets it spend 2 Essence to gain +5 soak for a scene. Umbraquake lets it knock everyone present to the ground, inflicting 2 dice of bashing damage on all present.

                    Arguably the best option for a combat, depending on whether you value Dexterity/Stamina (Willpower) or Strength (Rage). This spirit could very easily act as the tank of your party, holding a choke point. With a soak of 9 or 14 and 20ish points of 'health' to work with, an Earth Elemental could be hugely influential in protecting your pack or guarding some vulnerable NPCs.

                    Air
                    W3, R8, G7, E18
                    Charms: Create Wind, Updraft

                    There isn't any reason you'd want to use this, since Fire Elementals get +2 Willpower and +2 Rage. If you want mental or spiritual assistance, you'd go for Water. This also has the least Essence. Create Wind lets you start or stop wind patterns, including starting or calming a tornado, which is great, but specialist. So if you want to inflict or avert powerful weather patterns, Wind Elemental is a great choice. Updraft allows the Elemental to throw a man-sized target up into the air... by rolling its Willpower. Which is 3.

                    Red unless you need to start or stop a storm for some reason.

                    Fire
                    W5, R10, G5, E20
                    Charms: Blast (Flame), Create Fires

                    Blast is, to be frank, broken. You don't roll to hit, there is no chance to dodge, and you just skip to rolling damage. By spending 2 Essence the Fire Elemental can automatically inflict a ranged attack which deals 10 dice of Aggravated damage. If you can summon a Fire Elemental and instruct it to destroy your enemy, it may well oneshot them. This is a great ranged support option, with the Pack working to stop the Fire Elemental from being surrounded and ripped apart.

                    With a Dexterity/Stamina of 5 and a Strength of 10, this is a handy ally to have around. A Rage of 10 makes them highly aggressive and brutal, likely setting fire to things for the hell of it, and making the situation much more caustic and aggravated! Keep in mind that you have convinced an NPC to help you. The ST should keep control of that NPC and have them do things they see as helpful, unless you take direct control with a Rite or Gift.

                    Water
                    W6, R4, G10, E20
                    Charms: Cleanse the Blight, Flood, Healing

                    Gnosis is Charisma, Manipulation, Appearance, Intelligence, Wits and Perception. Even before you get to powers, a Water Elemental is a great source of advice, wisdom, lore, social support with spirits, a graceful companion and so on. While this spirit is handy enough in a fight with a Willpower of 6 and a Rage of 4, you'd generally be better off using Earth or Fire for combat. That said, this spirit can be very helpful in combat if you keep it protected.

                    By spending 2 Essence it can use the Healing Charm, rolling its Gnosis (10) against a difficulty of 6 or 8 (depending on damage type). On a success it heals 10 points of damage to the target! This will fully restore an unconscious Huge Size PC to full health in a single go. This Charm can only be used on a single target once per scene.

                    Cleanse the Blight allows a Water Elemental to purify water (a stream, a pond, a lake, a river), removing Wyrmish corruption and pollution. Rolling Gnosis against a difficulty reflecting the intensity of the corruption, this is a great "clean-up" power to trigger after you've killed the most powerful Banes and put a stop to the source of the pollution.

                    Flood is a tad niche but it's pretty impressive; by spending a single point of Essence the spirit can flood a small or large area depending on its power; at the lower end it can flood a city block! This alone could massively influence a session, though it's worth noting that the flood is natural; it must relate to a body of water which already exists, and it can't flood a skyscraper or a mansion at the top of a dry hill.

                    Water Elementals are the best at Airt Sense, making them exceptional guides; with a Gnosis of 10 they will travel through the Umbra and find obscure locations with relative ease.

                    All in all the damage, healing and overall support potential of this power is incredible. While you could easily summon and command an Elemental using Rites, these will tend to be slower and require the right setup and equipment; this Gift allows you to pull off a lot of spiritual shenanigans in a very short period.

                    Durance*: See Shadow Lords list. Arguably a stronger Gift for the Uktena who specialize in such matters.

                    Hands of the Earth Lords: This power grants some very strong-ish telepathy. Spending a Gnosis and making a difficulty 7 Dexterity + Occult check lets the Garou move an object weighing up to 900kg for one turn per success. This is equivalent to lifting with a Strength of 11, so a Garou with a base Strength of 5 (9 in Crinos) would need to roll Willpower or use a Gift to shift it. This lifting can be done with a gesture of a hand and requires no direct contact. The object moving at 20mph ain't bad!

                    What you're looking at is, unfortunately, a slightly light and slightly slow wrecking ball. 8.9 meters per second just isn't fast enough, at this mass, to get the momentum you need for a serious collision. You can't pick up and throw a car (a random Volvo will be 2000ish kg), and I feel like if you can't throw cars around what's the point?

                    If this power didn't cost a Gnosis, or came at a lower rank, I might like it. But all in all I just find this boring and weirdly conservative with its numbers. The effect is meh, it requires a roll, it costs Gnosis, and it's Rank 4. All of this just tips it over into Red, for me.

                    ============================

                    Call Elemental was a real revelation for me, and a rare example of a Rank 4 power I'd go out of my way to purchase out-of-Tribe. I think most Theurges would love to have such an ability!

                        Rank 4 Younger Brother Gifts

                        Call of the Cannibal Spirit: Interesting call to have a summon power with no stats for the summon. Wendigo is in W20 Corebook, but purely as a Totem Spirit, we get no idea of the stat-line for his Avatar.

                        Searching around online gets us this from an older edition:

                        W8 R10 G6 E32

                        Blast (Ice), Create Wind, Freeze, Tracking

                        Assuming you're ready to use those stats, or find a version of Wendigo in one of the books, or the ST is happy to invent some stats for him, it could work well. You have to have a piece of the target, and you have to dance for three turns before spending 1 Gnosis and 1 Rage. The Gift does demand a Charisma + Occult test, difficulty 8, and on a botch you will be devoured by Wendigo (though presumably he has to beat you in combat, rather than just instantly swallowing you whole).

                        Similarly, if the spirit fails to devour your target, it will come back to eat you.

                        All in all my takeaway with this Gift is that it's silly. You are a Rank 4 Garou, likely in a pack of Rank 3+ Garou. You are a pack of murderous Garou killing machines. Why are you sending a spirit on a side quest to devour one of your foes? If you can get past that, and assuming the stats are decent, this Gift is pretty good. There is no max duration mentioned, though the Gift does say that it can be 'prevented' from getting its target which suggests a time limit of some kind, or Wendigo simply giving up eventually if it can't find the target or the target is too well protected. Given how few details we have, and how the basic formula of things being resolved on-screen is challenged by this Gift, this winds up feeling more like a poorly defined Rite or NPC Gift, and thus I'll leave it unrated.

                        The either/or of an enemy being killed outright offscreen, or Wendigo crashing back into your Caern to devour you, is silly. Don't bother with this one.

                        If you wanted to rework it to be more interesting, simply use the stats listed here, and have this as a power where you entreat Great Wendigo to help you with a hunt. You pay upfront with some of your Rage and Gnosis, along with a piece of the target, and no roll is required. You have entered into a bargain with Wendigo where the two of you will travel together and hunt tirelessly until the target you have fed to his Avatar is devoured by the spirit. Your pack can follow, sure, but it's a bond between you and the Avatar. If you either refuse to keep going or try to divert from the hunt, or otherwise offend the spirit, he will attack you mercilessly, and when one of you is dead the effect of the Gift is finished. If the hunt does succeed, Wendigo disappears when he devours the target's heart. Now it's a player-driven power where you can find yourself hunting down a single BSD for weeks on end to the exclusion of all other targets because you entered into this bargain and you aren't sure you'd win the fight if you backed out now. It's risky but in a frenetic, interesting way where your role in the hunt matters; after all you might be able to track the target in a way that the spirit can't, like by checking their social media or phone signals...

                        For what it's worth, I would give my version Amber. Solid, not broken.

                        Chill of Early Frost: This lets you drive a large area to a temperature below freezing. Intelligence + Occult at a difficulty depending on season (4/6/9), lasting for one day per success, and impacts a 8km radius. Creatures without a natural coat of fur lose 2 dice from all their actions, likely due to the mystical nature of the cold. If it was already freezing, this Gift can make it even colder. The narrative impacts are potentially impactful, including stopping construction as people are stuck indoors, blocking roads with ice, bursting pipes and so on.

                        Against most non-Garou enemies this Gift's -2 to dice pools is very handy for fights and other challenges. If you're being chased by a Pentex team or by a gang of Mages or perhaps some Fomori, they'll be at -2 dice to all their attempts to follow your tracks. Human hunters going after your wolf-kin will struggle to find them, struggle to hit them, struggle to chase them, and be more easily found by Garou and Kinfolk. All in all this is a great conservation power and could do a lot to help the Garou tilt the odds in favour of wolves and the Garou Nation. Given that it could influence many battles and challenges and scenes playing out at once, I give this Gift a tentative Green. If you're dealing with complex events which span a whole area, rather than problems which boil down to "go to place, solve problem which is located purely in place", Gifts like this can help a lot.

                        Hero's Stand: See the Get of Fenris list.

                        Scream of Gaia: See the Lupus list.

                                A note on Rank 5 Gifts; I expect them to be character-defining. Expect a lot of Reds, and I'll be giving suggestions on how to fix Gifts that fall short.

                                Rank 5 Homid Gifts

                                Assimilation: Cool in concept, bad in execution. The Gift is meant to make you assimilate into a foreign culture, loving that. You can haggle in a Soukh or debate in a Mosque despite being an extraordinarily white New Yorker, love it. The problem is the execution. Assimilating into a culture which is 'foreign' to you is difficulty 9. That is, using the Gift in the only situation where it would meaningfully help you is almost impossible.

                                More to the point, this Gift isn't mind control. It doesn't make you seem like you fit into the BSD Hive, it just makes you behave and talk and understand in a way which would blend in. So you still need a disguise, or some kind of cover story ("my visions led me here, I am a Theurge of the Festering Pit, you might have heard of us").

                                I would fix this Gift by making it 100% passive and requiring no roll. You embody the Cosmopolitan Human and the Cosmopolitan Garou. You are at home in every city, every temple, every Caern, every office, every caravan. While you don't magically blend in, you will always know the cultural expectations and lingo, the language, the customs. This would come with an innate -3 difficulty to all Etiquette rolls as well.

                                Beyond Human: This feels like a very neat capstone to the Homid Garou, conceptually. That Curse which has kept you apart from the world of man from the day of your First Change? It becomes flipped on its head. Now you are a feral creature of intimidating magnetism. That on its own, turning the Curse from a weakness into a strength, would make a pretty neat Gift and probably be Amber. The ability to spend Rage or Gnosis to boost your social stats, including above 5, for a whole scene really pushes the Gift into something spectacular. The ability to fortify your Manipulation for Rites, your Charisma/Manipulation/Appearance for Gifts, and simply to ace simple Social rolls, is great. If you have a good way to generate Rage then this Gift becomes a very easily fueled means of mastering social situations.

                                I have no suggested changes here; the Gift works perfectly.

                                Part the Veil: Boring and pointless. This Gift allows you to grant immunity to Delirium to a single human for the duration of a scene. If they later encounter some cause of Delirium they will forget much of what they learned.

                                Not to be mean to regular mortals, since they absolutely are important in the WOD, immunity to Delirium is already pretty common. Kinfolk, Fomori, Kami, Mages, Vampires, and so on. If for some reason you need to prove you are a supernatural to a trustworthy regular human, such as a romantic partner, chances are you need to do that before you hit Rank 5. But say you do - you'd turn into a wolf. Or show off some obviously supernatural ability. There are real uses for this power, like stopping a friendly cowboy from running off in a panic when you go Crinos, but honestly there is already a form that solves this problem; Hispo.

                                Parting the Veil should be a Rite, and it should grant immunity to Delirium lasting a whole lunar month. Meanwhile I would replace this Gift with an totally new one which allows you to turn a willing human into Kinfolk. A permanent cost or limit would need to be baked in, such as you only being able to bring a human into the fold with the approval of a revered Ancestor Spirit, or perhaps having to spend a permanent dot of Gnosis.

                                                    Rank 5 Metis Gifts

                                                    Madness*: This is the capstone of Metis as an oppressed and hated group, festering with internalized resentment. It captures that theme quite well.

                                                    Spending a Gnosis and passing a Manipulation + Intimidation roll (difficulty of the target's Willpower) allows you to inflict a Derangement for one day per success. You get to control the intensity of the insanity, making it worse or better at will. The exact Derangement appears to be the ST's call, since it varies by individual.

                                                    How good is this? Fugue, Hysteria, Multiple Personalities, OCD, Paranoia, Schizophrenia... it depends on how debilitating they wind up being for the NPC in question, whether they matter immediately or are more of a slow burn, how the ST implements the idea of you "intensifying" the madness at will, and whether your Chronicle includes NPCs who you want to oppose but can't kill.

                                                    This is a bit of a lesson-teaching power, if nothing else. It isn't incredible, it isn't bad, it's suitably broad and story-driving for a Rank 5 power. I wish it were more powerful, less restrictive, and had a few more guidelines. All in all this is okay enough to be Amber.

                                                    Protean Form*: This is the capstone of Metis as born Garou, fluid by nature. Partial Transformation becomes enhanced to allow you to apply +2 dice to a type of attack (e.g. grappling, clawing, biting) or +2 dice to the damage inflicted by a certain kind of attack. It also allows you to increase your speed.

                                                    Note that there is no limit on the number of partial transformations you can undertake! So, in essence, this Gift grants you the ability to add +2 dice to all your attacks and +2 to all your damage rolls, with enough preparation, and the Form Mastery Gift.

                                                    Beyond the obvious and RAW applications of this Gift, it's quite obvious that there are many situations where suckers on your palms would help, or where a mouth in your hand might be useful, or where a false gullet which diverts things you don't want to drink into an internal reservoir could save your life. If your ST is chill with these adaptations helping you the ways you'd expect them to help you, great. Given that it's a Rank 5 Gift you expect that kind of thing.

                                                    But I need to judge the Gift based on the bare bones, and I'm left to consider whether +2 to hit and +2 to damage is enough. There are a lot of Gifts which can do these sorts of bonuses, but with Form Mastery this comes without cost and is basically 'free' if you have even a little prep time. If you're attacking several times in one round this sort of bonus is... pretty great! It might not be revolutionary in a given turn, but across a whole battle it will certainly add up. That, on its own, would make the Gift a dry yet functional Rank 5. The flavour and storytelling opportunities baked into the variety of transformations you can do with this Gift does bump it up a tad.

                                                    Totem Gift*: Divine Intervention from the Totem Spirit of your Tribe. What you can expect from this Gift will depend on your Tribe (I'd rather be able to invoke Unicorn than Griffon, but that's just me!), but ultimately you are only limited with this Gift by your Gnosis and your dice pool. Rolling Charisma + Rituals, difficulty 7, with 1 success giving minor results and 10 successes giving results which might effect a whole country or completely change a destiny, opens a route to things which you likely couldn't achieve otherwise.

                                                    This Gift could, for example, cause a major scientific breakthrough for environmentalism. Or bring a Pack of dead Garou back to life. Or create a new thriving Caern from nothing, where a Caern of legend stood 1000 years ago. Or produce a Klaive befitting the nature of your Totem.

                                                    Personally I would, if I were adjusting the Gifts, broaden it to apply to both the Totem of your Tribe and the Totem of your Pack, making the Gift cover more ground for a single Garou. For example a Child of Gaia who was in a Pack bound to Eagle would have many 'domains' to call upon, from life and defense to travel and pride.

                                                    This gets a Green, but the results are so ST-dependent that it has to take an asterisk.

                                                            Rank 5 Lupus Gifts

                                                            Many Lupus Gifts suffer from being Gifts which would be useful to a wolf, rather than being useful to a Garou who was born a wolf. Hopefully we can shake that off for their final set!

                                                            Elemental Gift: Spend a Gnosis, roll Gnosis (difficulty 8). With a Gnosis of 5, which is the Lupus base, this gives a ~64% chance of passing the test, with an average of 1.8 successes.

                                                            If you are successful you summon an Elemental ally which has power roughly equivalent to that of a Nexus Crawler. I won't bother getting into how strong that is beyond the fact that you can expect an ally who is a wise perceptive socially capable genius (Gnosis 10) with immense Strength (Rage 10). Naturally them being an Elemental of immense power will interfere with their problem-solving abilities somewhat!

                                                            For each success you roll, for the duration of the 'summon', you can control a larger and larger volume of the element in question. So you could help to shield your pack from a storm, direct a flood of water, shape a wildfire, or carve a path through a mountain.

                                                            All in all the ability to call upon a spirit on the upper end of 'normal' power levels by spending a single point of Gnosis is great. The roll being difficulty 8 is a little tricky; your odds of passing are decent but far from guaranteed, and even if you have a Gnosis of 9 you will still fail 25% of the time. Simply trying the Gift again does work, and strictly speaking there's nothing stopping you from summoning more than one elemental with this Gift either!

                                                            This takes a Green much for the same reason that Call Elemental takes a Green; it's an excellent ability which makes you stronger and is certainly worth 15xp, even if it has some flaws. If this Gift's difficulty for the roll were the local Gauntlet, or were reduced significantly by how powerful the element of the Elemental you are summoning is in the area (easy to call upon a Fire Elemental in a firestorm, easy to call upon an Earth Elemental in the tunnels below a mighty mountain etc.) it would also be much better.

                                                            Song of the Great Beast: I have very rarely spoken about the learning process for Gifts, but it's noteworthy here that the spirits which are capable of teaching Garou this Gift are secret, and possibly near-extinct. That doesn't take points off, but it makes the Gift less relevant even than most other Rank 5 Gifts.

                                                            This Gift lets you spend 2 Gnosis and roll Charisma + Primal-Urge, difficulty 8, to try and summon an extinct hyperbeast of some kind. Personally I'd go for one of those giant slots the size of an elephant. The beast will solve a problem for you in a way which suits its nature, which will likely be a very blunt and savage approach for most animals, though not all. This power is flexible, expensive, difficult to use, and hard to define. That said we are told that the results should always be 'impressive'. More successes simply make the beast more friendly towards you.

                                                            Oh and you have to howl deep in the deepest wilderness to use the Gift at all.

                                                            Really, really hard to judge this one. Whether you have means of reducing the difficulty of the test with a Merit or Fetish or Gift, whether you have a boost to your Charisma, whether you have plentiful Gnosis... this is without even getting into what your ST produces when you tell them you want a sabretooth! The results may well be impressive, but compare with Totem Gift, for example, which is similarly loose in its definition, costs 1 Gnosis instead of 2, and can potentially do ANYTHING rather than the narrow set of things which might be achieved with a Really Big Animal.

                                                            Ultimately I'd call this one narrow enough that it's Amber. Perfectly worth grabbing, it could do something huge and impactful, but between the cost and the difficulty and how narrow the potential outcomes are (big animal shows up and helps you with a problem a big animal would be able to help solve), along with the GIANT veil breach and the really nasty restriction of needing to be 'deep in the wilderness' to use it... it's not quite what I'd hope for.
