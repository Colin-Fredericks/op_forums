                    Again, the Setites weren't ever tied to real, actual Egypt, but to a pulp Egypt inspired by someone who wasn't all that familiar with real Egypt. Like where did the corruptive, deceptive evil snake come from? Snakes in Egypt never had that sort of symbolisms (in Egypt snakes were the symbols of divine protection and wisdom). The idea of the corruptive, deceptive evil snake comes from the Bible and that was the snake symbology that was used in Howards setting (which was loosely inspired by Egypt) and which then served as the basis for the Followers of Set. It's very important to note that since day one the Setites never represented or embodied an authentic Egypt.

                    As such, moving the Setites away from those pulp roots isn't engaging in "whitewashing" or a removal of a racial or cultural minority, because the Setites were never authentic ancient Egyptians in the first place, and indeed, were never meant to be authentic ancient Egyptians when they were first created. Revised attempted to try to come up with explanations for why the Setites didn't resemble in the least actual Egyptian mythology, but no matter how many knots the writers twisted themselves into, it just never worked. At the foundation the Setites simply weren't and never were a good or authentic representation of real world ancient Egypt and, short of a complete rewrite could never be authentic.

                            In the lore, Set ate Apophis or somehow gained Apophis' power, and that's where the snake/evil stuff came from. Would have made more sense if the had been written as the Children of Apophis from the start, but that was how they tried to explain why Set had evil snake powers. Also it was the Bubasti, not Celican.

                            So back in ancient Egypt, Set murdered his brother Osiris and tried to take over Egypt. Osiris' wife Isis (a powerful mage) and Osiris son Horus (who eventually became a mummy) declared war on Set. The Silent Striders, the Mokole, the Nagah and the Bubasti all ended up getting involved on Isis' side as well. The Setites basically ended up winning the conflict. They maintained control of Egypt for centuries. Set cursed the Silent Striders and forced them out of Egypt. They basically wiped out the Bubasti kinfolk and the tribe teeted on the edge of extinction ever since. Osiris (now a wraith) and Horus made more mummies to keep fighting against Set. Later the Setites and Assamites ended up entering into a conflict. Both were powerful vampire clans in the Middle East. The Assamites were mostly judges and demon hunters, and the Setites ticked enough boxes that the two clans were enemies. They also ended up at war with the Tal'mahe'Ra, a faction of immensely powerful vampires.

                            The Setites were sort of the generic "evil" clan and were basically in conflict with groups from every other line - VtM, WtA, Wraith, Mage. I think Changeling was the only line in which the Setites didn't have some sort of beef or conflict with an established power.
