    Revamping Werewolf: the Apocalypse - What would you do?

    11-01-2015, 01:47 AM
    WtA has, over the years, had some legitimate criticism leveled at it. Like a lot of cWoD games it has the flaws of a 90's game before research became easily available. There are elements, which in this day and age, looks a little close-minded. However, this thread isn't exactly about that. If you had creative control over the future of WtA, what would you do? What would you fix, changes or add to the line? This assumes that it is a reboot of the line, updated for the modern age. How would you change the tribes. Would you change the breeds? Does Metis serve a purpose enough to keep, or should it have a name change? Should the Garou language be expanded on (much like the First Tongue of WtF). Would you change the mechanics? And in which way?

    For me, there is a lot I would change. I was attached to Werewolf since it was my first WoD game. (A Bastet lore-keeper of sorts), but looking back at it and through my copy of W20, it seems to have aged. These days I have preferred the Forsaken, but with the possibility of a Werewolf game being made by our new Overlords, I'm looking back at my old WtA books. Since Apocalypse will, most likely, the world that is used for the media projects, I'm catching up on Apocalypse, and since we will most likely get an updated world, I'm wondering what people would want.

    I haven't gotten through and digested all of W20, but I'm sure thoughts will come to me.

                                  Things I would change:

                                  Mechanics (beyond just cleaning up the cWOD system in general) -

                                  1) Gifts. The current Gift structure is extremely clunky. While I don't want it moved to something like Disciplines, I think there are much better ways to express their diversity that can be much easier on bookkeeping, and also help some of the weirder mechanic-to-fluff disconnects go away.

                                  2) Rites. While the Rites themselves are rarely a problem, getting them is a pain. A lot of the rites are highly narrative/plot driven in function, but eat up the same amount of resources (freebies or time) as the ones that actually matter to vital mechanics. The simple fix for this is just to divide them up that way explicitly. Have some rites that anyone that meets the general requirements is assumed to know, or can try without a massive penalty, and other rights that need to be formally taught.

                                  3) Dump the rather inane value disparity in the starting Rage/Gnosis/Willpower assignments. I've posted my personal fix for this in the past. Possibly also have each Breed have a weakness.

                                  4) Totem creation rules. Large lists of Totems are nice, but I see so few of them get used because the pricing on others is just too good, and bans can make some Totems useless no matter how flavorful, while others are easy to turn into passes just by living in specific places. Just have the tools to make the Totem you want, that can also be adjusted more easily to the ST's setting (aka following a Totem that demands you protect its physical relatives is worth more if the game is set where they are under significant threat, than if the game is set somewhere those animals don't even exist.

                                  5) Tribal Bans over Tribal Weaknesses/Background Restrictions (unless they're part of the Ban); though keep the discouraged Backgrounds from W20.

                                  6) Scrap the current Renown award structure, so Renown can actually be a better gauge of how Renowned your werewolves are. Have Renown awarded at Moots based on retelling the pack's deeds (with plenty of boasting and embellishments); which avoids annoying bookkeeping and gives you actual things to use as what other Garou have heard about your characters.

                                  -----

                                  Setting/Lore -

                                  1) I don't need the Garou Tongue to be an invented language like Forsaken, but lets change it to something that makes sense as a linguistic whole. Words borrowed from dozens of languages willy-nilly gets really silly.

                                  2) Something else I've gone on at length about before: Fix the Red Talons to give them something to do besides hate humans more than everyone else. My personal favored approach is to have them be the "Wyld" Garou to the GW's "Weaver" Garou. The Red Talons aren't just the big bad wolves lurking in the woods, but they're actively trying to break humanity's dominance over nature by bringing back more Wyld and letting it lose on the world.

                                  3) More alternatives to the Garou Nation, and less Tribal exclusivity to those groups. The Garou Nation can still be dominant in the setting thanks to European colonialism (much like the Camarilla), but have a Pure Ones Alliance in the Americans that includes more than just the Uktena and Wendigo, but also the Red Talons, Bone Gnawers, and an much longer in the New World strain of the Silver Fangs

                                  4) Give the kin more to do than be breeding stock. I'm not looking to change the overall power dynamic, but give the kin more of a place in the setting besides "family you care about/people you bone." The current situation is kind of silly with the kin being far too numerous, and necessary, for the Garou to really use large scale violence to keep them in line, but they have no real incentive to become third class citizens to the Garou when they could just go off and live their own lives. It doesn't have to be much - it doesn't even have to be mechanical - just something that clearly says, "being a kin that's active with the Garou provides benefits other options don't."

                                              It's less about stuff in the corebook, but how certain things were developed. Some things that were developed are good and works for me. Some things really don't add value, but they don't distract either. Some things go against what I want to do in the game, and I ignore them and do my own thing. No matter how a game develops, that is always going to be true. However, I would definitely present some of this information differently than how the game does it.

                                              1) The game's materials should always be a toolkit that allows the ST to run the game he wants to run. Too often in White Wolf history, supplements present someone's campaign he thinks is cool and wants to force down everyone else's throat as metaplot. Revised was particularly bad that way. The 20th Edition books are much better and in the spirit of early 2nd Edition.

                                              2) Too often the setting strays far away from what I think are werewolves. Instead of being based on popular culture and folklore, Garou seem to be their own fantasy race. While you can't fault players from taking things and running with it, I think those traditional elements should be emphasized in official products. In theory, the game already has concepts and mechanics to do this (Lost Cubs, Lunatics, Ronin, certain Merits and Flaws, the Curse, certain Fetishes), but in practice it does not come off that way. Instead of being prominent elements the setting has to deal with, they are things mostly ignored in my experience.

                                              3) A major problem is overuse of Crinos form. Whenever I see some Crinos holding pistols, wearing specialized made clothing, or other things that make it "normal" I want to puke. It doesn't look "cool" to me, I think it looks lame (I may be in the minority here). It makes the Garou much more like creatures that should be in a fantasy game than in a horror game. In my game, I hold that Crinos don't have precision grip or even a true opposable thumb. People can use Homid or Glabro if they want to do that. I'd like to have the game utilize the other forms much more.

                                              4) The game needs expanded antagonists. Industrial pollution fomori certainly have their place, but it should be as one kind of antagonist. I use a lot of Lovecraftian concepts to introduce new kinds of fomori, monsters, and banes. It does a good job of really making things horrofic, and introduces a lot of mystery. The game needs multiple kinds of "large target" groups like Pentex - large scale Wyrm cults, big evil books that grant mortals infernalist magics that disturb the earth, deep umbral horrors with their own conspiracies, etc.

                                              5) Introduce more antagonists not of the Wyrm, and do more with existing ones that are like that (such as DNA). It's just too easy to kill something if it is of the Wyrm. With other kinds of antagonists, it introduces some element of moral doubt for the PCs. Presenting problems that require solutions outside of violence would be beneficial.

                                              6) The game needs to stay away from the viewpoint that left wing politics is "Gaian" and if you hold right wing views, it's of the Wyrm. This was particularly prominent in the early days, but it's still been retained. It is far more pronounced than in any of the other WoD games. The game needs to take a more sober attitude and acknowledge that either political view can serve Gaia or the Wyrm. Not wanting to be lectured in a roleplay product is one reason I avoided the game for over a decade.

                                              7) Eliminate the Beast Courts and other strange ideas the WoD had about Asian supernatural creatures being different from the rest of the world. At some point, White Wolf bit hard on multiculturalism and it didn't work out well for the game. Instead of treating other cultures respectfully, it was racist in the way only well meaning liberals can be. The Chinese have Glass Walkers, Red Talons, Shadow Lords, Silver Fangs, and other tribes just like anyone else, even if the tribal names would be translated into Chinese. I have no problem with Stargazers having high Pure Breed if they come from Tibet, but that tribe is not limited to there either. A white Stargazer is no more exceptional than an Asian Silver Fang.

                                              8) Emphasize the differences between septs, and alliances of septs, within the Garou nation. The game almost, but not quite, captures the feeling of ancient warrior aristocracies and their feuding petty kingdoms. The game gives us conflict between the breeds and tribes, but ultimately those are too big concepts to drive major conflicts towards. Both are too large and unwieldy for politics. Septs should be the primary drivers of most politics in the game. Tribal moots and inter-Tribal concolations are rare enough that they have a limited impact, but sept politics is at a level stuff can happen. Sept alliances should be driving things in game. It would be a way to introduce some factions/sects within the Garou, but keeping the overall setting intact. Some of the tribal camps can probably be reintroduced this way. Of course, the best way to do this would be through regional sourcebooks like Rage Across the World and others.

                                              9)
                                              Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                              Dump the rather inane value disparity in the starting Rage/Gnosis/Willpower assignments.
                                              Agreed. This is the easiest way to abuse the system in character creation so certain PCs are more powerful than others.

                                              10) Add a bit more Totem options in the corebook. I was disappointed we didn't have ten to twelve in each category.

                                              Most of the problems that I have are with individual Tribebooks or Rage Across supplements.

                                                      - Make most of the tribes range historically over wider territory. The Fianna and Bunyip can stay being primarily European and entirely Australian, but we've had Asian Uktena and Wendigo since first edition (Rage Across Russia), and there are other menacing wolf figures like Fenris in surprising places (like the Tiangou in China, or Rahu in India), and the obsession with triptych goddesses that the Black Furies had seems like it would fit in quite well with the Tridevi, which got imported all across Asia with the spread of Buddhism.

                                                      - Name metis something else, much as how Nagah and Kitsune don't labor under having a breed named, presumably, after a Greek titaness that can so easily be mistaken for an ethnic slur. Also, remove disfigurements in favor of a flaw in keeping with the breed, like how ahi can't take defiled water.

                                                      - Rename Thrall of the Wyrm so that there is actual ambiguity about where Rage comes from. Also, get rid of the correspondence between triatic Wyrms and breeds, so that there is no "rape the body if you botch this roll" to worry over. Note that there would still be a mechanical drawback to rolling too many successes with Rage, like not being able to stop it, but drawbacks other than that and the potential for cannibalism would be changed.

                                                      - Simplify animal attraction down to adding Primal-Urge to seduction and carousing rolls.

                                                      - Separate refinements to shapeshifting from Gifts, give everyone five basic forms. Eventually, you can practice and get more; these come with a minimum Primal-Urge rating coupled with an Attribute requirement, like Charms.

                                                      - Level out the power of most Gifts so that they're almost entirely about spiritual ties; let none make the mistake that beginning Gifts must necessarily be equal to level one powers in other games, because seriously, Create Element is equal to several two-dot Spheres, or potentially all five dots of Lure of the Flames, and that's just for one part of the Gift.

                                                      - Clarify how Rage goes above its normal rating, say by having each point gained after the max count for half.

                                                      - Maybe have important personality facets a la Passions from Wraith, or Intimacies from Exalted 3rd edition, to help clarify how much Rage one might get from being thwarted, loss, betrayal, endangerment, etc. To further help determine when the Rage 1 Ragabash stops being so relatively calm and collected.
