      Well it seems only right to start with WEREWOLF: THE WILD WEST (The sourcebook) and THE WYLD WEST as a setting, since this was the hook that really pulled me into the World of Darkness (VAMPIRE: THE MASQUERADE - REDEMPTION made the original introduction, but I’ve always leaned much more to Van Helsing than Count Dracula).

      Also, there’s something inherently gleeful in the High Concept of ‘Werewolf Cowboys Vs Manifest Destiny’.


      In terms of favourite characters, just about every signature character commands a share of my affections (Yes, that includes Evan Heals-the-Past, I am a soft-hearted fan) but Vic Stryker, with or without the Eagle’s Nest Pack, is a special favourite: he’s the perfect mixture of Pulp Werewolf Fantasy with pure pathos and personal tragedy.

      I like to think that he and King Jonas Albrecht (Another favourite) made at least one joke about finally having the perfect pair of eyes if they ever met (and it’s easy to imagine a hard-charging King with a spymaster grandfather and trouble with in-house personnel having plenty of uses for a trustworthy PI).


      In terms of Garou Tribes I have to say “All of them” but one must admit to loving some more than others: reading through the BY NIGHT version of WtA led me to an interesting revelation - almost all of my particular favourites were with the Sanctum of Gaia (Black Furies, Fianna, Silver Fangs, W******), though one must admit to having a real soft spot for the Bone Gnawers and U***** over on the other side.

      Oh, and mustn’t miss the Silent Striders, steering a risky course somewhere in the middle.


      As for favourite Pentex subsidiary, it’s definitely the Black Dog gaming factory: ask me about my takes on their various game lines (How they differ from and resemble the ‘real’ World of Darkness) only if you have free time and patience with rambling enthusiasm.

          The redemption of Bat has always been my favourite plot event. It is so rare to see actual progress made in the game, plus it showcases where the Shadow Lords differ from the Silver Fangs.

          The Shadow Lords work to make up for their mistakes. The tribe killed the Camazotz, and centuries later a pack of Lords with heritage to those who did the deed go on an epic umbral quest and redeem a part of Bat back to Gaia.

          It is also one of my favourite crossover events, as Lords and Tzimisce have been enemies for easily millennia if not more. And Tzimisce have their giant bat monster form. AND Europe has no vampire bats. It doesn't justify how the Lords reacted but it gives a very good reason WHY.

          The second crossover I love is the Silent Strider curse. They lost Egypt and if they stay long in one place ghosts are attracted to them. Hence they travel all around. Everything is set up so it is not a chore to play but ADDS to the play and in a lot of ways demystifies the tribe.

          My gallery.

              For me it's a three way tie between Iona Kinslayer, Gretchen Valkenburg, and Sept of the Fanum. I often name-drop them if not find a way to work them directly into stories.                               To me, the two biggest draws of Werewolf are Family and Culture. Fundamentally it's a game about communities that share history, laws, culture and family.

                              Family is crucial to Werewolf - in the sense of blood relations, in the sense of found family and ultimatley in the sense of belonging to the World and being responsible to it.

                              Ancestors, kinfolk, traditions, local folkore and landmarks and ultimatley lived legends all make up who you are and where you came from. You belong somewhere and you have a duty to protect it. You fight for you own and what the people that came before you have fought.

                              The pack is the ultimate found family - to the point that they are a close bound intimate union. One where there may be conflict but ultimatley you live and feel their lives with them and your totem. It makes you more whole then you could ever be and allows you to brave the fires of hell.

                              This unity is also representd through the Tribes and the Nation. They all have clear history and identity that is defined by a marriage between the culture of the Garou and the people that they live and sleep with. Afterall 9 out of 10 werewolf community members are kin. The views, experiences,languages and cultures of the kin reshape the Garou into their own variant of the Tribe that is still united in praxis, faith and methodology.

                              This allows us to bring in the wealth of human experience,myth and faith into the game. This ultimatley reflects the duty of the Garou that is to all of the World.

                              What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.

                                  It's hard to say what I love about W:TA the most. It's my second favorite gameline in the setting and always been neck and neck with Mage but as I grow older, I grow less and less enchanted with the kind of apologia that M:TA had toward the Technocracy and more interested in the larger environmental themes of W:TA. I also feel like a lot of what I initially thought was silly in W:TA had been proven to be sadly all too accurate.

                                  Basically, Pentex is the most realistic and chilling satire in the setting as the real life evil doers aren't tortured antiheroes or ignorant but deliberately horrifically malicious toward the world as well as its people--often for religious accelerationist reasons mixed with their greed. In 2001, I never would have believed a man would burn a forest if he could make as much money growing one. In 2024, I know they'd go out of their way to spend money to burn the forest down.

                                  I found the Subsidiaries most useful as a sourcebook but my favorite work probably was Rage across Russia.

                                  Part of what I love is the sense of doomed heroism that comes from the Garou. I love the characters of the game and wish we'd see more novels about them as well as comics. Sadly, they seem to have been left behind for 5th Edition.

                                          On a slightly less high-handed note, there does seem to be two Signature Characters who made the transition from the classic editions to 5th edition: Mari Cabrah, who is mentioned by name, and King Jonas Albrecht (Whose family is alluded to and whose presence may be inferred through allusions in the Silver Crown’s wrote-up).
