    Originally posted by Kharnov View Post
    I must admit, I haven't really looked at the Talen, and I wasn't reading too closely when I read about Moon Paths; going back to read the section in W20's Umbra - tVS, it emphasizes that where they appear and where they lead can be unpredictable. I imagine knowledge of Moon Paths is mostly a local, or perhaps regional, thing that has to update over time as the Moon Paths appear, disappear, and change. I would read that as indicating that a Moon Path is as likely to lead to another part of the Penumbra as it is deeper into the Umbra. It just depends on the particular Moon Path.
    It seems each of the editions give slightly different details to Moon Paths. That may be because each edition's writer is reinterpreting them.

    I have not played Werewolf as much as many people on this forum, but I can say I don't remember Moon Paths ever being a prominent part of gameplay. I don't remember an ST ever using one. Though it is possible an ST simply handwaved a Moon Path into existence so the PCs could move onto their next destination in the Umbra, and I simply forgot a Moon Path was involved. I'm thinking maybe that is the case for most chronicles. Though certainly not all as we know Heinrich's LARP makes extensive use of them. As such, maybe their lack of use means everyone interprets them on their own and gets slightly different practical facts.

    There's a lot like that in Werewolf and the other WoD games. There's some great flavor text and some specifics of what something does, but when you go to run it in game you suddenly realize there are a lot of gaps in terms of how to apply it.

    I'm not a fan of the idea of where a Moon Path leads is unpredictable (at least outside using one during the Crescent Moon because that's the peril of using it during that time) because I think that makes them much less useful in game. If a Moon Path has been explored and we know it goes to X (or even that it can be used to go to X,Y and Z), then that becomes useful knowledge the PCs may want to exploit. Then if they decide they have to use one right now during the time of a crescent moon, then that is a risk they willingly take in order to get to X immediately. On the other hand, if the destination is always unpredictable, most PCs will simply choose to never use Moon Paths because it doesn't help them achieve their goals and will likely only delay them longer.

    I'm definitely glad the thread has lead to this discussion because having multiple people work things through helps me to decide of how this can be actually used in game.

            Yes, there are lots of similarities between Moon Paths and Moon Bridges. It's mostly a matter of how they are created. Paths seem to be natural phenomenon - "gifts of Luna" - while Bridges are artificial creations at caerns by the Garou.

            I do think it's perfectly fine for a mix of Moon Paths. Some appear solely to help a spirit quest of a Garou especially devoted to Luna and then disappear. Others are permanent or at least long term and stable. Others seem stable, but only appear at some kind of frequency or interval like you suggested. I think a mix is fine, but for gaming purposes there needs to be some kind of stability for them to be useful to the Garou. The others just become flavor text, or perhaps an excuse for a random adventure.

            I think a harder question is to determine how many (stable/useful) Moon Paths might be "typical" within the range of a sept's protectorate. The books given us no direction at all. That both empowers the individual ST, but can also make it overwhelming to decide how many is a good number for their chronicle. There's a lot of things that require the ST to decide on. And I, at least, can sometimes shut down and entirely ignore something if I feel like I don't have any guidance at all.

            You may just want to start with just one in your setting, and as you get comfortable add more as needed, and then stop once you feel there is enough.

            But I tend to have a lot of pain-in-the-ass players who want to know a lot of things up front for RP purposes. And if I decide to introduce a new Moon Path (that was always supposed to be there), sometimes I get responses of PCs feeling angry at the NPCs for "not telling them that before." Of course they couldn't because it didn't exist until I just decided. Of course I could just tell this is completely new, but that tends to hurt the idea that there's a history to the setting. And I can tell them that this is new because I just created it as a retcon element, but that always takes us out of the game.

            So my preference is to figure out what is a good amount to include up front that may or may not come into play. So that comes into things like is 4 a good number? 6? What about 9?

            Maybe 3 should be the magic number. Use one that connects points in the Penumbra. Have another that connects to one or more Near Realms that are the typical destinations for Garou like the Legendary Realm or Scar. And one that connects to a useful Domain - whether it be a good ally to the caern, or a frequent enemy. Maybe one of them has something special about them like the examples you gave of only appearing at a certain time or condition. And anything else make up on the fly or known to be random or recent as background flavor. Not too many, but enough to cement the concept in the minds of the players, and allows the ST to play around with the details. And they don't need to be readily available. Some of them might be hard to get to from the caern. It's fine for the ST to want less or more, but as a ground rule maybe 3 is a good way to go.

            = = =

            In terms of other spirit menaces, I've given some thought of other things to steal. The first edition Werewolf Storyteller's Handbook had a bane called Unakas I think could be used to haunt Moon Paths. They appear like undead skeletons and seek to devour spiritual energy. Sounds like something that would set up ambushes at Paths. And I think there are a lot of flavor text that can be stolen from other authors. William Hope Hodgson wrote some cool horror stories of creatures that might be useful - Pneumavores of the Nightland, the various Outer Monstrosities of Carnacki the Ghost Finder, Swine Things in the House at the Borderlands. You might just use the statistics for Scrags, but by stealing descriptions they can seem more interesting and appropriate. Lovecraft is another good source to steal from - Nightgaunts are very cool looking, and the astral terrors in From Beyond also fit. As well as other creatures in the Call of Cthulhu RPG - I think Desh and Dimensional Shamblers could be reskinned. I even think some monsters from D&D could be useful like the Devourers or the various kinds of Nightshades (Nightcrawler, Nightwing, Nightwalker).

            Maybe there are Scryers serving minor Bane lords that keep watch on known Moon Paths, and they minute they see Garou enter them, they report back and whatever servants of that Bane jaggling lord show up like sharks following chum thrown in the ocean.

            Some of these could easily be used elsewhere. Maybe on the nights of the new moon or during eclipses they escape the intermediate mists and can enter the Penumbra. Or certain ones can be summoned by Wyrm cultists. Or they are just present in the skies of the Penumbral night in limited numbers to begin with.

            This would give the ST some tools to decide if they want to use some of these spirits randomly if the PCs travel the Penumbra on nights when the full moon is not out, in order to create conditions where a Moon Glow might become more useful.

            During the daytime, it's know that Weaver spirits are very numerous and active, so PCs should be avoiding the Penumbra during the day unless they use a Moon Glow.

                                Now that I have a better understanding of likely hazards in the Umbra that a Moon Glow can avoid, I think this will become a more important Talen. And one that PCs may want to create themselves. So the next question is what kind of chiminage would be appropriate to convince a Lune to be bound to this Talen.

                                Talens require less chiminage than Fetishes, but the spirit is still performing one defined service to the Talen's user/creator. And typically, one performs an equivalent like minded service to a spirit in exchange for what it does for you. It's not restricted to this, but a general guideline to evaluate chiminage. We also know that Lunes are well disposed to Garou, so they are likely to be friendly and receptive to helping them, reducing the price. They're also very mercurial and chaotic, and their mood swings with the moon's cycles.

                                So what does the Talen actually do? It protects the Garou, but not by fighting for them. It's more like hiding them from their enemies, scaring them off, or otherwise deceiving them for a specified trip. So what should a Lune want in exchange?

                                Axis Mundi doesn't include Lunes per se, but it does include a variety of moon spirits and their descriptions include a general chiminage section showing what each one likes. They provide the following examples of chiminage:
                                • bloody sacrifices of Luna's and Gaia's enemies
                                • helping the harvest moon prepare people for the upcoming winter
                                • set right offenses done against Luna/Gaia
                                • allowing the spirit to possess the Garou for a period of time
                                They're all very different given that each was for a different aspect of Luna's brood. But we can see they are somewhat generic in the sense they're not very tied to the moon, and could work with other kinds of spirits. We might also decide that this means what a Lune asks for may be very different depending on the cycle of the moon when it is done. A Lune during the full moon might demand something very warlike or bloody, while during a crescent or new moon might be more spiritually symbolic, meditative, or random.

                                Any ideas would be welcomed.

                                    In our LARP talen creation is rarely done at the scene, but mostly done in the computer-aided character management tool. We do not require specific chiminage, but an appropriate raw material to inhabit. The garou are required to put time and effort into collecting of crafting this raw talen, which is the show of their dedication. With Moon Glows it is the harvesting of appropriate crystal pieces. It would be possible to trade for the raw pieces, or be gifted them as price for a competition or a well done job, though. Basically, every interaction that does underline or add to the worth of the raw talens, rather than diminishing it.

                                    And, of course the Rite must be performed satisfactory. We estimate the successes a ritemaster might have and see this as the appropriate number of raw talens he should present during the Rite. If he chooses to present more, we raise the difficulty. We also raise it, when the spirit is more powerful, but grant free successes if at least one regular one was scored.

                                    Besides that, we work under the assumption that tales don't loose power, until activated. But the spirit ceases to exist, while his essence is spread in possibly multiple talens. Only to reform if a sufficient amount of talens is used. In this regard the differ from fetishes which inhabit the complete spirit and the spirit can be communicated with through meditation.

                                    EDIT: Oh, we also assume that the number of successful, failed and botched attempts at the Rite of Binding factor into the difficulty. As well as the time elapsed since. So, trying and failing, trying and failing is something that offends or annoys the spirits, raising the difficulty further.
