        Originally posted by Damian May View Post
        Something the writer said in the Q&A session stood out as being the opposite of what the W5 Core states. The W5 Core intimates that the Nation failed because it treated every problem as a nail and the Garou acted as hammers meaning that they used brute force and were quick to violence in situations where that only made things worse. Whereas the writer stated that the nation failed because they were too wishy-washy and could not come to any decisions about what should be done which is why things only got worse.
        The W5 core states that but what that writer is referring to is also there. From the introduction (pg 9): "Almost all werewolves agree on what’s going wrong, but they often disagree violently on how best to make the necessary changes (or even what those changes should be).​" I assume that's what the writer is referring to.

        But I always found that idea a bit confounding. If that's a problem inherent to Garou-society, you would never had something like the Garou-Nation to begin with. And explaining the change from when this wasn't a problem (so that the Garou-Nation could be formed) to the time when these differences turned into an issue (which is when the Garou-Nation fell) isn't covered by the W5 corebook, of course. Also, if you emphasize that idea as a fundamental problem of Garou-society, how come the idea of multi-Tribal Septs and Packs is still endorsed...? The logical follow-up would be more something like the faction-building the BNS-version engaged in. And why would the Fenrir disagreeing with the other Tribes be a big deal then if these disagreements are the norm...? "Who knows" can only do so much in covering up bad worldbuilding...

        The issue of how much of the W5 setting is ambiguous or just a blank slate will always come into conflict with all these supplements that aren't written with the idea that these ambiguities and blank spaces stay that way. The current WOD5E-approach may despise old lore/worldbuilding but it's not like they're writing these books with the idea that worldbuilding is completely left to the people at the table so that's what gets supported. Street-level-only, the Garou-Nation has fallen, the Cult of Fenris, it's the Age of Apocalypse... W5 still draws many lines in the sand where the setting gets confined and limiting.

                            I think it's just that the way the W5 corebook did its worldbuilding is unsustainable. For example, I don't think the W5 corebook's take on the Garou-Nation ("How long did it last? A century? A decade? Who knows. And it fell... but when? Who knows.") is sustainable either. Like, you have a Garou-elder NPC that says "I remember the fall of the Garou-Nation." Bam! And now you have a timeline. Because the only way you could keep the approach of the corebook intact is if either no one ever talks about the Garou-Nation or when they talk about it, all they can say is that it once existed and now doesn't (which isn't really that much of a contribution in terms of worldbuilding). And if you keep to doing either of these things then really what was even the point of making it a part of the setting in the first place...? And so instead at some point, a Renegade-supplement will fill out this part to some degree.
