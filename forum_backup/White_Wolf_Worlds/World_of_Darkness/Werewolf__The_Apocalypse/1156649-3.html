    Originally posted by Aleph View Post
    I think that the idea of a legend gaining a legendary power that has something to do with the legend it's very much on point. You leave your mark in the spirit world and the spirit world marks you in return.

    Not so the "invent what you want on the fly" thing. It departs from what Gifts are (a power the spirits give to you). Besides, a Legend it's someone who has done great deeds without the lvl 6, they shouldn't need "magick(tm)" to change the story.

    Instead make so Rank 6 it's such a big social boost that you barely need Chriminage with Gaian spirits, everyone understands that if a Legend says something has to be done, it has to be done. Incarna are happy to have you in their courts as advisor and ally. NO spirit it's ever going to deny you a Gift, even if they are of other Tribes.

    Also more lvl 6 powers could be rewarded if the character keeps doing Epic stuff.
    My goal is to have a guideline for creation of custom Gifts instead of a list of examples that will be used much like other Ranks lists. I'll try again, and implement your considerations, I agree with them on a second look (it was just a first draft), and I liked the social bonus idea.


    Originally posted by Aleph View Post
    I don't get Maris complaints. I don't like power gamers that want lvl 6 to increase their "ass kicking algorithm", but why should a Legendary power be less than earthshaking?, what does THAT add to the Legend from a ST point of view? .

    In my opinion Lvl 6 powers are suposed to be Epic, that being said Epic isn't always "nice" -

    Becoming Uktena does Epic very well. Sure, it "kills you" at the end of the scene, but it also gives you narrative powers limited by the ST (and they should be massively powerful powers, since "even a Thunderwyrm should fear you") during that scene. I think such epic powers should change the result of said scene, and doing the impossible before dying it's a very cool way to retire your character. In a sense, it IS an "I won werewolf button", but done in a very thematic way. Thus I don't think it sucks, far from it, it's the perfect stuff to give a Legend (a way to "die" with awesomeness).
    The link says it's "frustrating" that they don't give mechanics to this thing. I don't see this as "frustrating" at all. It's not casual either. It's because such an epic moment shouldnt be ruined with stats and checks. It would be like giving stats to Ur-Shulgi, an Antediluvian, or an Incarna-I would rather not do that.

    Others, like the "ash a vampire thing", if not necessarily weak, they surely look underwhelming to me and I wouldn't mind to change them.
    Well, my complaint with Becoming Uktena is more on line with it being mechanically a Gift than the idea itself. Though it did gave me another idea for the next iteration of Rank 6.

    Originally posted by Story Letter View Post


    The 2nd and the 3rd paragraph remind me Mage rather than Werewolf.

    Appart from that a rank 6 gift may, or may not, have anything to do with destiny, gifts that call upon the favor of celestines may indeed force their wrath upon a bad user of their blessings, like calling Helios to bake the bacon and eggs you have just put on your car's bonnet. On the other hand there might be a use of a rank 6 gift for lesser tasks that will actually end up to a great benefit for Gaia, that of course might make the celestine invoked to question the werewolve's reasons for his power's abuse and that actually might be a great story theme.
    As much as that works fine for lesser Gifts, a Rank 6 should be more attuned to the specific character than anything else, hence my take on it being linked to personal destiny. Until Rank 5, Gifts are "just" the favor of spirits upon the Changer. But now this guy is a Legend by all means, so it should be as much the Celestine giving favor as guiding the legend itself to settle. Also, you already have for "lesser" Changers the watching eyes of the spirits, a Legend shouldn't be someone who needs a babysitter. So, instead of imposing limits through the wrath of gods (something this Garou is already powerful enough to face), I wanted to dial up the idea of your actions having consequences. A Legend is so powerful that even the simplest actions can ripple into world-shaking events if done without the proper consideration, specially if implementing such signature powers.


    Ok, so lemme do it again:
    Rank 6 Gift (Unique):
    Reaching the pinnacle of your journey, you was personally gifted by a Celestine. The entity coalesced your own destiny in the archetypal manifestation of your spiritual power.

    Give a name and concept to your personal Gift, discussing it with your ST. If it evolves from a lesser Gift, a single success can exact the same effect the lesser Gift would at 5 successes, for example, while additional successes each double variables like range or duration. Costs are halved for each Rank of difference between the original effect and 6 (1 Perma dot becomes 5 temp points, 1 temp point becomes 0). If the effect calls for a resisted roll, it is done with either Willpower, Gnosis or Rage with +5 dice and 1 additional success on your result.

    You can create unique higher effects. As a rule of thumb, any effect able to alter the status of a battlefield should cost 1 to 5 points of either Willpower, Rage or Gnosis. An effect that can change the status in a big caern and its territory should cost 1 permanent dot, +1 per other caerns affected. You roll either Gnosis, Rage or Willpower for non-resisted effects, and with +5 for resisted. It is a rough guideline, and you should work with your ST the details.

    Along with those stated effects, every Rank 6 Gift also have a constant effect for interaction with spirits and a single-use effect called a Call to Destiny. The effect on spirits rouses from you being recognized both as a favored of a Celestine and a powerful Legend by yourself. Most spirits recognize you on spot, and any below Incarna will defer to you out of respect or fear if not coerced by other powerful beings. You never have to roll against those spirits, but you can have to roll against their master's influence (you get a -2 dif. in the roll if the master isn't felt present by the spirit in question). Greater spirits still respect you as equal to the lesser among the Incarnae, so even the greatest Tribal Totems will at least pay attention to you and heed you advice. Some can go as far as to challenge others for the right to have you as a counselor.

    Call to Destiny is the ultimate effect a Werewolf can expect to achieve in life. It can come in two flavors: Sacrificial Destiny or Guarded for Destiny. A Sacrificial Destiny lets you access the full power of a deity for a brief time. You can call Great Fenris in its full glory to the Battlefield, or become the very Uktena, mingle permanently with the mind of Queen Ananasa, or curse a single place with the very presence of Wendigo forever. Whatever you do, your character is gone for the player. The deed can have lasting effects, but no hard rules are necessary on the creation of the Gift, just the way the character will leave play.

    If you choose a Guarded Destiny, instead you state a way for the Gift to save you from an untimely death. It can be used only once, and can't save you from a Sacrificial Destiny, but can save you from threats you couldn't overcome with all your Legendary might. Most Legends have a single Guarded Destiny, if any, to strike back in the same battle. You also choose a Legendary Scar to mark you once the Guarded Destiny was used.

    Only one Call to Destiny can be used in the entire character's life, but new Gifts can expand on the descriptive options when the time has come. When you trigger the Call to Destiny, you can mix all your Rank 6 Gifts in the final effect as you want, only keeping the limit of not causing the damage of a Sacrificial Destiny at the same time that you use a Guarded Destiny (tough you can use other effects of the Gift that grants the Sacrificial Destiny and mix its effects in the narrative of the return).

    Rank 6 Gift is a personal bless from one of the greatest spirits, and a manifestation of your very essence and destiny. Using it for lesser tasks or without good reason can mess with your being and fate, always to achieve the greatness you deserve. The first symptoms seem like ill luck, making those uses result in a big chain of unintended events. If still abused, fate causes even more trouble, as it leads you to hard situations that justify the use of such gifts. The bad luck goes on until the untimely end of the legendary hero in a way fitting to the great stories of the People. Depending on the Garou in question, the spirits can try to warn of such fate before it's too late.

                            A) Guide to Technocracy already worked on the above lvl 5 BG, and I'm not very fond of those rules, though they were for pooling BG. They mostly take the BG to another level of OP in narrative without adding nothing really of interest to the game. Instead, we could do the middle ground: new BGs based on the "natural progression" of common BGs, but with distinct rules to fine-tune how it really affects the game. I'll take a look in a WoD BGs list later to start organizing some ideas, or someone could do that.

                            E) Need some streamlined ruled on this, though. I'll take a look at Mage's Destiny later.

                            F) I'm thinking on wrapping the other elements around this one. You personal concept or epic calling defines what you get as a Legend.

I think so since this BG is already described as a spiritual resemblance to heroes of old. Pure Breed could attain an entirely new level of meaning for a Legend. And maybe the old Pure Breed should be replaced by such characters, since it doesn't matter anymore to whom in the past they have a semblance. They are the Great Heroes themselves, now.

Maybe instead of normal Frenzy rules, you should roll to keep yourself from loosing control of the consequences of your actions.                             I) Fetishes of such level should be legendary by themselves, so as much as it is interesting, it is something to make with some caution. At least they should be as unique as the Gifts, or even more.

                            Black Spiral Dancers and other minions of the Wyrm, if not the Hydra itself, should be as affected as any other by the Legendary status. I did thought it through when making my little rule. Spirits of the Wyrm would still listen and obey a Legend out of sheer terror and respect for such prowess, unless directed by the greater horrors of their masters.
