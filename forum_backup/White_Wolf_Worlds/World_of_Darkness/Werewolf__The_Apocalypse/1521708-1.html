    Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
    I am often wary of including too many fantastic elements in the World of Darkness because ultimately it’s too easy for the setting to become obviously fantastic and impossible for ordinary people (the vast majority of NPCs in the game world) to ignore it, and thus the WoD is no longer our world through a mirror darkly, but some fantasy world where people need to account for all these fantastic monsters and creatures. But I think you sold me on physical swine things being OK.
    And of course, these swine things must have a Delerium of their own. But maybe in some people, their Delerium eventually helps breaks down their mind so they now become susceptible to the dream-pull of Thrassus whereas before they were not.
    My thinking with the physical pig Formori was that it wouldn't be all that strange, given the normal ground state of Fomori. They're deformed body horror monsters, the likes our Furry Blender protagonists mow down by the dozens. They're already operating on the razor's edge of blowing the "Masquerade" wide open, just by existing. As such, they either need to be very careful, or have powerful figures (Pentex, the Technocracy, the Camarilla, even just corrupt local cops working for the cult) working in the background to cover them up. It makes sense for the more splatterpunk mode Werewolf often occupies, more than other game lines.

    (Moreover, the secrecy angle gets more easy to handwave if pig Formori are encountered in pre-modern times. When things like monsters are easier to hide in the dark corners of the earth, or even in the wilderness outside villages. These cults, I think, make for great antagonists in these time periods, where the big conspiracies are absent but not needed, because everyone is more superstitious and information travels slower.)

    I sort of tried giving them cover, by saying some pig Fomori might have a Bane power that lets them escape notice or appear less fantastic. But just giving them native Delirium is also a very elegant solution. Befitting of some of the "special" Fomori associated with a Talon of the Wyrm. They don't enforce Delirium through deep-seated psychic trauma written in humanity's DNA, as is the case with Garou, but rather because they are somehow especially wrong on a spiritual level.

    That same wrongness is Lovecraftian in texture. Causing mortals to reject them from their conscious minds, or eroding their sanity. Or perhaps decaying it, to go along with the Orkus theme.

    I like the idea of severed swine heads, infested with flies and insects, somehow providing oracular guidance due to black magic. It’s evocative. Maybe such heads do not act only as oracles, but as warning beacons and sentries wherever they are placed, so whatever it is they observe (like Garou packs coming to get them) they automatically and telepathically warn the high priest of the cult.
    Another angle I just now thought of is for the severed pig heads to be able to mutter incantations. These could either be used to "cast spells", or the words themselves act as a memetic hazard, causing psychic harm or madness. Again, that erosion/decay of sanity. As if the head is acting as an emissary of Thrassus (or close enough), uttering foul words in a blasphemous tongue from the dawn of human history. Fraying thoughts, spoiling dreams, and putrefying emotions.

    These heads could be mounted on stationary locations, like walls or posts, to act as further defense mechanisms. These would be more interior heads, farther in the bowels of cult territory, while the "more mundane" severed pig heads mounted on periphery locations act as sentries and beacons, as described above. Would-be Garou invaders might become so used to seeing random pig heads hanging around, they don't think to see the ones farther in as tangible, direct threats.


    I really like that blog post on “Orkus”. Its interpretation of the classic D&D villain has lots of things we could steal for Thrassus.
    ...
    I'm glad I shared it. The Bottomless Sarcophagus is a great blog, and I'm sad the author has largely moved on to other projects. Still, great body of work to pull from. (I still kind of want to run a Thawing Lands campaign some time.)

    I also like the idea of some cults building deep underground pits where they keep them in pickling jars/containers so that Thrassus will have some kind of army whent he Apocalypse agains. These might be honeycombed all over the earth. Finding these pickling stores and destroying them thus becomes a game objective. And individual swine things can still be encountered as guardians or sentries at places, but not necessarily be overused.
    One thing I kind of wanted to emphasize with these "pig-stitcher" cults is that they're kind of designed to break down. It's thematically appropriate, given our talk of "The Orkus". But importantly, the effects of the cult's activities outlive their founders.

    Sure, random people with ready access to swine parts and of susceptible mindset can be made into cultists through their dreams. They can pop up anywhere. But the pig Formori can often outlive the ones who created them, or even keep being made generation on generation. The idea that cult founders might be reanimated in pig-man bodies gives the cult's essential malice a perverse longevity. Even, of course, as those old wizards slowly degrade - decay - in sanity as the years go by. The nuances of their personality being worn away, until only undying contempt for life and a lust for power remains.

    This process of making pig-slaves need not be limited to human participants. I can't imagine pig Fomori can't, in turn, stitch their own swine progeny. They need to source more pigs, or have dupes to provide them, but they could keep doing it. To paraphrase the lore on the Necromancers from Darkest Dungeon: "the pigs reanimating the pigs, forever".

    (I'm also reminded of the Thrulls from Magic: The Gathering, from the plane of Dominaria. In MtG, Thrulls are constructs made from dead flesh. But while on the plane of Ravnica the creators of Thrulls, the Orzhov Syndicate, continue to make such servants as they've done for 10,000 years uninterrupted, on Dominaria the Thrulls overtook their masters. On the Dominarian continent of Sarpadia, the Order of the Ebon Hand created Thrulls as slaves and sacrifice fodder. But gradually started using them as battle constructs, and even as assistants for rituals. Once the Thrulls learned how to make more Thrulls, it was over for the Ebon Hand. They came to eradicate almost all other people on the continent, and rule it to this day. It's said to be a blessing for Dominaria that the Thrulls aren't aware anywhere else but Sarpadia exists. Even Phyrexians failed to conquer it.)

            Other things that the swine aspect of the cult of Thrassus, is that we could tie in some mythological and folkloric references.

            Myth has all sorts of dangerous boar (or boar like creatures) that the legendary heroes had to fight. Theseus slew the Crommyonian Sow. Hercules had to capture the Erymanthian Boar as one of this Labors. A group of heroes including Atalanta and Meleager, hunted the Calydonian boar. The knights of King Arthur hunted Twrch Trwyth and Ysgtithyrwyn. Were some of these dangerous fomori associated with Thrassus? Even if not, we can place similar monstrous boars as such.

            Then there are 17th century legends of Pig-faced women. Are they the result of a certain Thrassus cult becoming noteworthy enough that it impacted folklore of western Europe during this time? Or the appearances of "learned pigs" in the 18th century that could answer people's questions by picking out the appropriate word cards laid before it. Might be ways for certain swine things to go out in public, and introduces the possibility of carnivals and freak shows being ways the cult moves around. Garou PCs might be surprised the source of some local evil is the "pig" held by the travelling carnival that appeared in the area recently.

            I don't want to associate Thrauss too much with the swine theme. There should be other, stronger references. But combined with others and used appropriately, I think it can be useful.

                I do think there's room in the game for both "stitched" and "fed" swine Fomori. And there's advantages to both, under different situations.

                "Fed" swine things are arguably easier and more primitive. Mix the right (or wrong) concoction, feed it to a pig, along with all manner of human parts. It's something that can be done on a farm, with one's own livestock. Indeed, it might be done accidently. Maybe the farmers feed them a concoction, they thought of in a dream, thinking it will help the pig. Or maybe a murder happens, and they try to dispose of the body by feeding it to the pigs. The weight of the transgression attracts the Bane. What's worse, if the farmers butcher the pigs and eat them, they get tainted too. And not just by Prion Disease that will make swiss cheese of their brains in thirty years. Wyrm taint, that makes them susceptible to Thrassus. A kind of self-reinforcing feedback loop of corruption that makes monsters of livestock and farmer both.

                The advantage of "stitched" swine Fomori is you don't need as much access to live pigs, only pig parts. For modern times, this makes it a favored "project" by meat-packers, who have access to all manner of bits. (Even human bits. In the sort of Upton Sinclair "The Jungle" days of early industrial meat-packing, safety standards are all a mess. Workers may randomly find a human hand lopped off with one of their colleagues had an accident. Does the person need to be sacrificed to make a pig Fomori? Or is merely the transgression of mixing pig and "long pig" together enough? Something to consider.)

                In pre-modern times, "stitched" pig Fomori are rarer, though could still be found among butchers or aspiring surgeons. (Can't always dissect a human to learn Anatomy. Might be forced to dissect pigs to learn how the body works. No one said when you were done taking it apart, you couldn't put it back together in new shapes.) It requires a greater degree of sewing skill, and a basic grasp of how meat fits together, so it's generally out of the reach of most pre-modern cultures. Aside from the rare rural enthusiast, I'd imagine. The dreams of Thrassus might provide the "spackle" needed to fill in gaps in a layman's knowledge of anatomy. (Isn't that a horrifying mental image? A random villager is beset by dreams of meat, until they start seeing how they connect. Then they start getting ideas...)

                One place you might find pig-stitchers is among military surgeons. An army on campaign needs men to saw off gangrenous limbs or pull out arrows/crossbow bolts/bullets. And those same armies need a plurality of livestock on hand to feed the men. If a few parts end up in the surgeon's tent instead of the cooking pot, would anyone notice? Moreover, the horrors of war can leave folk in such a mental state as to be susceptible to supernatural influence. Especially if they're the ones sawing off limbs or trying to sew men's guts back into their bellies. Seeing the rot set in on a soldier's leg. Pits filled with filth and blood and bodies.

                Truly, it's enough to make a man sick. Sick in the head, too. Make a whole company sick. Thrassus cults might form under such conditions. Imagine Garou fighting mercenaries during the Napoleonic wars, having lopped off limbs in a vain attempt to stop the rot, only for it to settle in their bones and minds. When the ammunition runs out, they call on the surgeon to bring forth the pig-thing. Or lead the Garou into an open pit of putrescent black ichor, the remains of a former mass grave where Thrassus's influence was permitted to accumulate and fester.

                    I think it is important to emphasize horror in any faction of the Wyrm. This is not a game of heroic fantasy, but savage horror. It is very easy for STs and PCs to reduce the bad guys into disposable goons so as ST I try to remember to portray the minions of the Wyrms in a creepy or dreadful way. That doesn’t mean it needs to be a tough combat fight, or that the Garou PCs themselves are scared. After all, Garou PCs must be able to handle an ongoing war with such creatures. But if you can creep the players out, it’d worth it.

                    Something I find very similar to the body horror of beings like Knygathin Zhaum is Junji Ito’s horror character Tomie. For those who don’t know, Tomie is somethin akin to a succubus, enciting deep lust and attraction in men and even able to control them. She leads them into some kind of psychological breakdown and acts of violence. Now that aspect has nothing to do with Thrassus. However, the tie into this kind of fomori is that whenever she is killed, she is able to regenerate (or even replicate) herself, effectively making her immortal. Furthermore, she is even able to replicate herself by taking over other victim’s bodies. People who receive an organ transplant or blood donation from her transform into her. Locks of her hair can burrow into people’s brains possessing them, later killing them as the hair grows wildly within their bodies.


                    That kind of plasticity and body horror ties in well with our themes, and the bizarre and horrific survivability is perfect for this type of fomori (it might be one of the less powerful kind, but probably more likely one of the Ferectoi if our intention is to develop a particular NPC as opposed to just having something weird happen). The corruption of the flesh as a result of the green liquid infects as well as destroys. In WtA, these fomori don’t need to be unkillable or always return. Good players should quickly figure out permanent ways to dispose of them. But the bizarre ways they might survive is a great way to play up actual horror and freak out players. We don’t need to duplicate this perfectly, and certainly not for the same fomori NPC. But we can steal various bits of it for a variety of fomori NPCs (perhaps limited to Ferectoi).

                    I find it is rarely possible for an ST to scare players when ther PCs are in direct conflict with the monsters. That doesn’t see heroic to them, and they’ll downplay anything the ST does. During other scenes however - when fights aren’t happening - players are more willing to acknowledge any unease or terror they are feeling, and the kind of events above are examples of things that creep people out. After they kill a Ferectoi of Thrassus, having them see someone - perhaps some NPC they already know - slowly turn into that same Ferectoi because they had a blood transfusion or kidney donation that came from the Ferectoi can be very unsettling.

                    Thrassus and its minions are just an entropic force in the universe leading to everything breaking down - social relationships, individual psyches, society in general, controlling the natural forces of decomposition in general, and eventually to the breakdown of the laws of biology and physics itself.

                    In general, I think the bizarre and unsettling horror works of Junji Ito is perfect for Thrassus with how they showcase a breakdown of the natural world. Of course, things found in stories like Uzumaki, Smashed, Remina, and Gyo can’t be introduced into the game without fundamentally changing the world from one that is supposed to be ignorant of the supernatural. But I think it’s exactly the kind of thing we might see when the Apocalypse begins and the great forces of supernatural evil rise. So you can steal imagery for use with prophetic Gifts like Sight from Beyond, and you might be able to steal some limited imagery and ideas in some encounters.

                        Hollow Men I imagine are strong contenders for "endlessly survivable". Even if you tear the skin suit to ribbons and crunch the mass of vermin to paste, so long as a single tainted member of the swarm survives, they can slink away and begin again.

                        They might assemble a new swarm, then stitch together a new "container" from materials like burlap. (Scarecrows or cloth manikins are useful for this, as they're already human-shaped assemblages of flexible material. Just hop in, eat away the stuffing, and the Hollow Man is good to go). Real Oogie Boogie vibes.

                        Alternatively, the swarm finds a wounded person (or someone who can be MADE wounded), and pours into the opening to hollow them out. A dark mirror of the process whereby the Hollow Man was originally created. It certainly matches that scene from Prince of Darkness, where the murdered guy is puppeted by the insects until his body falls apart and is "recycled". This kind of consumptive possession by swarm furthers the theme of Thrassus's minions being a creeping force of decay. Every vessel is of finite usefulness, before the entropic principle devours them from the inside out.

                        If a Hollow Man takes over new bodies, it's likely they take on more and more memories. W20 Book of the Wyrm frames the Fomor almost like the vestiges of the person whose skin the swarm wears. That the swarm is a separate but vital party to their horrific shared existence. If the skin is destroyed and a new body is claimed, whatever personhood that remains might be discarded along with it.

                        Or that personhood could follow along with the swarm (hence the framing as a creature that survives from one encounter to another). With the caveat that each new body overlaps the memories and psychological quirks over the chittering, verminous center. The nuances of the Hollow Man's personality becoming drowned out by many vessels, until they slowly lose track of who they are or were. That is, unless there's some powerful personality that dominates.

                        (I'm reminded of Hellsing, where the vampire Alucard is not merely Dracula, but every person he ever consumed over the centuries. It's actually a late series plot point that any single soul devoured into Alucard has no sense of self. Their personhood drowned in a sea of other souls, unable to identify a division between them. A Hollow Man using this framework may experience radical character shifts every time they "die" and come back.)

                                                The concept of the Talons of the Wyrm were introduced in the early 1992 sourcebook Rite of Passage which only gave us Narlthus (and made it clear that the actual status of Narlthus was up to the ST). The next mention of the Talons was in 1993’s 1e Players Guide which mentions in the Black Fury tribal section that they are actively seeking three of the Talons. The Sept of Artemis and Sept of the Black Pegasus each guard one. The third presumably was being searched for. That same year in Caerns of Power, we’re introduced to the Sept of Bygone Visions but no mention of any Talon is made.

                                                1993 is also the year of Rage Across Russia, and once again the Talons are mentioned. Koschei is introduced, and thus between Koschei and Narlthus, we know the names of the Talons that the Black Furies are not looking for. But we are given new information - now we are told that “another Talon, whose name has been forgotten, is carefully guarded by the Sept of Bygone Visions, a Black Fury caern in the Mediterreanean Sea. Almost no Garou in the world today knows that it is there, and no one knows which object within the vast horde kept by the sept controls the Talon.” It makes no mentions of the Talons supposedly at the other two Black Fury septs, but implies they may have been forgotten by the world.

                                                This is very strange since at face value, the information in the Players Guide implies the two Talons guarded by the Septs of Artemis and Black Pegasus are the ones that should be best known. But now it seems they’ve been completely forgotten. Since all three sourcebooks date from 1993, I don’t think there was any retcon, but this (contradictory) information reveal was coordinated. If nothing else, this was something then developer of the line, Bill Bridges, was interested in establishing. But the contradiction between the three sourcebooks is hard to reconcile. The easiest would be too assume that the Furies at those septs know what they are guarding, but everyone has forgotten about them. At the same time, the Furies also captured the third Talon, but subsequently forgot they had it. That works, but I find this a weird situation.

                                                I don’t think any Talons are mentioned again. Bill Bridges steps down as developer of Werewolf, and Ethan Skemp takes over. It isn’t until 1998 that the Book of the Weaver introduces Exhibit # 338 There we are told “a few, pointing to same-time similarities between this item’s discovery and the recent struggles at the Sept of Bygone Visions, whisper that it serves as prison to no less an entity than Thrassus the Thrice Damned, Smallest Talon of the Wyrm itself.”

                                                Assuming we don’t simply ignore all this as misinformation, there is an implied backstory here. This Talon at the Sept of Bygone Visions is somehow forgotten. This might be a result of the Black Fury Iona Kinslayer (falsely) telling Garou that all artifacts hidden at the caern had been destroyed back after WWII. But there could be something more nefarious going on.

                                                We also know there is some dissension at the sept. Other Black Furies seem to be angered by this sept’s leadership. Iona violated ancient bans and opened the sept to outsiders. Another leader, Kyra Firefoot, has a known friendship with a Leech (the Gangrel Brunhilde of all people). There is perhaps an implied backstory that Kyra somehow utilized Brundhilde to stop the Get of Fenris attack at the caern in 1967 although this is never stated and the origin of their “old friendship” is not given. But it makes sense to me that when Kyra was a “vagabond” she somehow befriended Brunhilde after she rose from torpor and Brunhilde has helped her against their common enemy - the Get. There is also a male Metis at the sept, Tireseias. And we know Kyra and Iona squabble. So we have lots of potential reasons why this sept might undergo turmoil from within the sept, between the Black Furies of this sept and other Black Fury septs, and between the Black Furies and outsiders like the Get of Fenris and Shadow Lords.

                                                Presumably some turmoil did come to a head, at which point the fetish prison of Thrassus was removed by parties unknown. I assume Exhbitit # 338 is now in some vault of the Technocracy in North America, although again an ST can assign this to some other group. The most likely explanation IMO is that Thrassus somehow arranged his escape to a move favorable location. I just cannot imagine a situation where outsiders invaded the sept secretly and took Exhibit # 338 at random. If they took it, it was intentionally. And since the description in Book of the Weaver does not indicate that the people at the storage location know what it is, then it implies any intention was from Exhibit # 338 itself.

                                                This implies that proximity to Thrassus could result in memory loss and amnesia, as well as ratcheting up social tensions among the people around him. Possibly attracting outsiders (other Garou tribes, the Technocracy, Wyrm minions) to attack the sept overtly or clandestinely via some kind of dream summons or subtle influence. Such memory loss and amnesia can easily be associated with a Delerium effect which we already discussed in regards to Thrassus.
