                    So like, with V5, it's a direct continuation of the previous Vampire games. It's built on the same setting, it uses the same metaplot. There are differences, like the Banu Haqim joining the Camarilla, or the the Ministry/Setite split, but this is all just a matter of the metaplot moving forward. With W5, it's essentially a new game that reuses a lot of the ideas from older editions, but also removes a lot of important foundational concepts. You can't even really call it a retcon, since much of the old continuity doesn't exist. For all intents and purposes, it's a new game with a new setting.

                    Previously, the central theme of WtA was hope. Even if the world was dying, it wasn't dead just yet, and every action the Garou took helped to stave off Gaia's death by another day. Yes, corporations were killing Gaia out of greed and humanity out of apathy, but things weren't completely hopeless. In contracts, in W5, it's a game about playing in a hopeless world. Gaia is already dead. It's too late to turn back the clock and save her. The only faction that hasn't completely given up hope and is raging and trying to do something are biggest villains in the setting. At it's core, W5 is about trying to find some meaning in a world without hope. The story is much more personal - its' not about saving the world, it's about trying to save the people close to you.

                    But thematically it's also kind of a mess. It's still a punk game, but it's in an odd spot. Prior editions "the Man" was the capitalist, consumerist world. In the new edition "the Man" is sort of the Garou Nation that failed and allowed Gaia to die. But the Garou nation doesn't really exist anymore. So the Man is sort of the tribal elders, but the Tribes aren't as big of a thing either. So you're kind of being a punk against the elder Garou at your local Cairn and it doesn't really carry the same weight.

                    In the end there's a lot of mechanical changes from prior editions, there's a lot of setting changes, there's a lot of other changes. All of this combines to make W5 extremely different from prior Werewolf games. It's not quite as extreme as the change between the old Hunter and the new one, but it's still much more significant than the change Vampire got, and a lot of people aren't terribly thrilled about it.

                                It's a bit more complicated than that and a lot of it comes through the metatext.

                                The only faction that's out there crusading against the Wyrm are literal Nazis. You, as a player, aren't supposed to want to emulate Nazis. So doing thar same hing the Cult if Fenris is doing is bad.

                                This goes back to a central idea in the game, that you can't beat the Wyrm by being a raging ball of violence and rage.                                 And that's the thing. The Garou are balls of violence and rage. There's no escaping that. This is the "buy in" for players in W5. They're playing a monster of rage and violence in a game where rage and violence can't save the world (Gaia is dead and the world is doomed).

                                Where prior editions of Werewolf were more about never giving up because there's always a sliver of hope, W5 is more about accepting that even if everything is doomed, you can still find things to fight for and protect. It's a pretty massive change in mood and theme.

                                This is also why the game is more "street level." You're not out there crusading to save the world (that's what the Nazis are doing and it's a lost cause). Instead you're buying your loved ones and community as much time as possible before the inevitable end.

                                                While it isn't directly a part of W5's take on the WtA-setting, I think the mechanics also give an impression of the designers' pessimistic attitude towards the Garou. One of the most relevant elements in this regard are the Harano/Hauglost-tracks. These are two tracks that trigger checks in various story-related situations and if you fail those, you get a dot in either Harano or Hauglosk. Additionally, you can add a point to either for some immediate "healing" (a full WP-regeneration or immediately filling up your Rage-track to 5). But Touchstones only allow you to switch a dot from one track to the other and besides that, the only way to actually reduce the number of dots in those tracks is to get a specific usable Talisman (in other words, it's up to the ST's generosity whether you have access to that or not). And the moment you hit 5 dots in either the Harano- or the Hauglosk-track, your character is immediately rendered unplayable. So, most of the time, every W5-Garou-PC is automatically engaged in this kind of doomspiral. And that isn't just a harsh mechanic compared to previous WtA-editions, it's harsh compared to the other two WOD5E-games.
