        Originally posted by Voytek View Post
        Except it ISN'T the end of everything in existence, just like how Climate Change ISN'T the end of the world despite the fact that it's already happening.

        The Apocalypse is allegorical for Climate Change in 5th edition. I don't think anyone can argue that. And in the real world, Climate Change is already a thing. You can't stop it because it's already happening. There's already rising sea levels and massive ecological disasters. You as a person aren't going to do dogshit in the face of that. What you can do and should do is work to help your local community and whatever charities you can devote the energy and resources to make the world a better place. And this is reflected in 5th. The Apocalypse is the destruction of everything you knew before, but the world's still there. You're still alive, you're still able to have fun, fall in love, laugh and have fun, help people, and make the world a better place. You can make your local environment better, be an activist that helps that mountain range or saves that endangered species. And the more people who do that, who make their world better, the more people can work to make their place better and so on to make the world better.

        That's the vibe of W5 now. You aren't going to save the world because no individual person can. No big furry Superman is going to come out of the blue and beat up the Wyrm and call it done. You're going to have to look at what's around you and make it better because you should. I personally find that a lot more engaging of a thesis especially with the removal of all of Werewolf's Omniprevelant Eugenics Lore making it much more of a willing fight.
        Environmental destruction to some extent was already a foregone conclusion in Werewolf, and contrary to how it's often thought of, in older editions 'big furry Superman beating up the Wyrm and that being the end of it' wasn't in the cards. What was possible was saving Gaia and preventing the Wyrm and Weaver from killing or strangling everything. The real world analogue to that isn't "climate change doesn't happen" it's "there is not a sixth mass extinction." And yes, that's still possible--we're in the opening stages of the sixth mass extinction now, but if environmental destruction was to cease, the extinctions that have already happened are not nearly severe enough to qualify what we've done for mass extinction status. To say that Gaia is already dead and all we can do is try to save a few shards is really fucking convenient for the people that don't want to avert the sixth mass extinction. There are plenty of those people--there's a whole cottage industry of people who state that nature/the wilds/Earth is dead, so we may as well scale back our efforts and only try to focus on small improvements to try and mitigate and adapt to a changing world.

        But they are wrong, and while the fight is dire it is not over yet. Resigning ourselves to it locks in the worst path. Not trying mass action and rejecting militancy--as W5's advocates frequently tell me it does--lock us into the worst path forwards too. And this line of thinking tends to be cowardly and self-centered--there are a lot of people who focus on global warming to the exclusion of all else because it can threaten them or their lifeways while habitat destruction or overexploitation doesn't. Many of these people are advocates of the "mitigation and adaptation" path and it's not uncommon for them to just breezily declare biodiversity loss a done deal. As even the social ecologists note on occasion, worrying about polar bears has become passe.
