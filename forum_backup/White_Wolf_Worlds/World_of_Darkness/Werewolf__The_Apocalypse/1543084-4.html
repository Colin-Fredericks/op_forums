    Just like with the descriptions of Garou-history, the Umbra-descriptions are way too vague. Like what are you even supposed to get from a paragraph like this one:

    In considering the animistic role of the Umbra, it may help to use a metaphor. If the physical world is an object, and the Umbra is its shadow, what’s causing the illumination that results in the shadow? The truth is that no one knows.
    "To better understand this thing, let me offer you a metaphor. * presents metaphor * Is this thing like this metaphor? The truth is that no one knows." What the hell! Then why did you present the metaphor in the first place?!

    Also, on the count of "animism" in W5, I forgot about the sidebar in the Storytelling-chapter which... lacks a bit of self-awareness. Here's what it says:

    Context Matters

    Remember that animism is a real-world belief system practiced by millions of people in one form or another both throughout history and today. Be appropriately respectful at your table.

    Werewolf does not suppose the Garou’s specific animistic lens is the “truth” of how animism works in the game world — people’s faiths and perspectives are not to be reduced to game mechanics. The relationship between the Garou and the Umbra is but a single, fictional expression of animism.​
    So, "be appropriately respectful towards this real-world belief system - that we've determined should be constantly questioned and doubted in our game-setting". Oh, yeah, that's how you pay respect to a real-world belief...

    Another thing is that W5 keeps talking a big game about all this regional variety that supposedly exists in the setting. On pg 225: "Differences that arise help paint a World of Darkness in which the Garou aren’t a monoculture, and in which the greatest mysteries of the universe cannot be untangled, despite all of the fury and insight werewolves bring to bear.​" or in the Spirit Veracity sidebar on pg. 232 "So, for example, a Ghost Council Garou with a Native American background may well perceive Horned Serpent in a different way than fellow Ghost Council Garou living amid Scandinavian, Iranian, or North African human cultures.​" or in the recent Shattered Nation supplement on pg. 7 "Modern communications technology means that Garou can talk to each other easier than ever before, and so have realized that their immutable traditions and culture are often actually entirely local. Other Garou in other places do things differently.​" W5 keeps going on and on about all these differences and never gives any examples for this. You just get "And of course, the Glass Walker in Norway has different traditions and views than the Glass Walker in Iran." But that's all you ever get! W5 in all the writing that has been presented thus far never actually has supported that idea once with any concrete examples.

    Of course, it doesn't help as well that all the locations for the adventures are exclusively located in North America and Europe...

    But the cherry on top for the Umbra chapter is "Gaia's Howl". This is that central event in the setting which explains why the Umbra is in so much chaos, how there's such a high awareness of it being the Age of Apocalypse and this is why Spirits don't like Garou. So you get this section in this chapter:

    Whether experienced as a literal howl, a painful frisson physically felt while traversing the Gauntlet, intruding visions of Gaia in pain, or an alarming burst of taste or smell, Gaia lets her champions know that they have little time left. (See p. 224 for more information on Gaia’s Howl.)​
    But here's the thing: This section is on pg. 224. And this section is the only time Gaia's Howl gets mentioned on that page. And the Index also tells you to go to pg 224 for the section on Gaia's Howl. In fact, there's no section to be found about Gaia's Howl in the Umbra chapter. It's simply missing!

                                The Quick Characters Chapter (Antagonists and Allies)

                                One of the few places where it actually feels like a modern game, this chapter condenses stats heavily into broad categories and a few exceptional specifics. Regrettably, this means that Spirits were, like Garou, flattened utterly down to having fewer stats. Where the Garou lost Gnosis, the Spirits lost everything except... Power. Yeah, they're not really beating allegations that they didn't keep current on Werewolf stuff (we used Essence since Revised, thankyouverymuch).

                                Spirits are the first on the docket for the chapter, which makes it seem like the Umbra chapter was longer, but they just cut the part detailing spirits out of it at some point and put it here. I suppose that works well enough.

                                What isn't well enough is the book stopping to kick you, yet again, with some heavily Mage stuff about Garou having an "imperfect understanding" of the Umbra and possibly not knowing a bane from a non-corrupt spirit. While describing what a character perceives in sensory details is a general goal for running games a storyteller - showing rather than telling - the sidebar sounds like it's encouraging you to be a willfully malicious ass about it.

                                Spirits only have Power as a stat, and some modifiers for Abilities or other actions. Spirits are also no longer capable of materializing; in a particularly "BOO!"-worthy passage, it poo-poos the idea of a Nexus Crawler emerging from the Umbra like a kaiju, and mentions that it instead temporarily possesses whatever is on hand - floors, tables, industrial equipment, etc. Banes are noted as being more capable in this arena, with long term possession resulting in fomori. I supposed it's doubtful that we'll be seeing gorgons or drones anytime soon.

                                Everything that a spirit changes about its host disappears after it leaves. This is supposed to preserve the veil in the case of spirits not having physical bodies, but those faded from existence after no longer needed or when a spirit was physically defeated, so this isn't really a new thing, nor is it necessary to preserve the veil in the case of spirits actually building up physical bodies to act on the physical side of thing. Also spirits only reliably take agg from fire. Truly, this is the Promethean edition of Werewolf.

                                Also, compare these two passages.

                                [quote=Werewolf 20th Anniversary Edition]Banes are Wyrm-spirits, a collective name for the vast menagerie of spirit-minions at the Wyrm’s command. Their forms are legion, each reflecting something of the particular aspect of the Wyrm that they serve. Most are foul of form, embodying the Wyrm’s taint, but even this is no certainty. Destruction, deception, and corruption take
                                many forms throughout the world, and if inhuman beauty and seemingly sweet seduction serves their Master’s needs, some bane will surely reflect those “virtues.”

                                Originally posted by W5
                                According to the Garou, Banes are part of the vast menagerie of spirits that serve the Wyrm. Their forms are legion, each reflecting an aspect of that cosmological force. Most are grotesque, but many are subtler. Destruction, deception, and corruption take many forms, and if inhuman beauty and lustful seduction serve the Wyrm’s needs, such is the face Banes present.
                                That "According to the Garou" addendum is so fucking smarmy. ​I want to punch it. The book also seems to turn around and basically just copies the rest of what W20 had to say about them being wholly corrupt and malevolent. Does anyone else believe it's a reboot still? The only real difference is a fearful need to backpedal on every statement where it might be construed as standing for something, and also to dunk on animals that aren't humans - repeatedly, throughout this chapter, it regards them as "lower order animals". I think only Freak Legion did that.

                                Most of the banes are familiar, but I'm blanking on whether I've seen the Asklepian before under that name (it's a disease bane that spawns more of its kind of by infected other spirits and Garou).

                                Bitter Rages are no longer in rare company in only temporarily possessing others, but they can make frenzies last as long as they want. Since you have to roll a potentially diminished temporary Willpower roll against half the bane's power, you'll probably need to get someone to help you kick one out unless you roll two 10s.

                                Dissonant Gestalt is the only other new bane, basically one of organizational coverups and denial. The others are all quite familiar to anyone who's been playing for a while or who just got on board with W20. Some changes exist in odd places; humans possessed by scrags using guns, for example, or Wyrmholes not being able to possess hosts or throw down with their enemies. Nexus Crawlers got an awkward translation where they just decided that they should throw lots of dice at the characters instead of making the crawler weird and horrifying in what it does, as what it does can be resisted with Stamina + Occult now.

                                Meanwhile, the Wyld-spirits known as the Nameless have been drastically de-powered, and of course, there's no mention of their capability to cause a giant explosion by stripping something's name from it. Wouldn't be very street, I guess.

                                Lunes still exist, and technically someone managed to use them to sneak moon bridges back in! It isn't quite instant, but being able to fast travel anywhere in a single night, provided you've placated the spirit, is a close second.

                                Englings are still there, even though they no longer act as ways to regain Gnosis, because you don't have that Trait anymore. Now they just cause small miracles to happen, like fortuitous power outages or roots that your attacker trips on.

                                Fomori powers changed a lot compared to past editions. Stomach Pumper is still there under a different name, they can still do toxic suicide attacks, but tentacles, instead of just being extra appendages, is now similar to said suicide attack; instead of chemicals, it erupts in an area effect grappling attack... which doesn't do the fomor much good if they can't grapple anymore on account of being dead.

                                Pentex is there, as the Pentex Group. While I don't always agree with Justin Achilli, I do feel like it's not really necessary to keep it, but apparently in addition to all the classic banes this "reboot" needs it for some reason. I feel that giving it a Patron at all that wasn't a Corporate version of the City Father is a bad take, and selecting Fly for that role strikes me as something along the line of Gary Gygax hating hyenas and making gnolls to constantly harp on about how much he hates those filthy, ugly, disgusting hyenas while also writing for the game on the side, I guess. Seriously, unlike corporate executives, flies have an important role in supporting ecological webs. Fuck this game.

                                Back to characters, the game starts to creep back into assuming you'll structure your chronicle into three acts, and gives an ascending threat level of operatives and employees that you'll meet early to late into the campaign. First Teams are also mentioned here, but srsly, you guys, it's totally a reboot. I feel that following a four act structure with reversals and upsets might better allow you to incorporate the equally twisting and labyrinthine politics of Pentex, or any other Wyrm-Influenced Corporation (WIC) into the mix, since rival board members might start supporting the pack with deniable assets. But then, I did technically come here from Shadowrun so many years ago, so...

                                Using Garou as antagonists: the text box suggests not fully using the number of rolls that would be required to play a Garou as a PC. Gee, y'think? I don't even want to use all those rolls for PCs.

                                The motivations given for the BSDs are far, far too charitable. Also, they're compared to accelerationists.

                                The Cult of Fenris! I'd ask WTF the rest of the tribe of Wolf joined up with the Cult just for screaming louder, but I already know the answer. Also, this last part...

                                Originally posted by VV5
                                Some among Wolf’s pledged, rather than follow their fellows, abandoned their Patron Spirit and pledged to other tribes. Trusting in the Patron Spirits to perceive any evidence of poor faith, the other tribes have accepted them, but being a former Wolf-pledged often earns Garou suspicion in their packs. Which, it must be admitted, is a very strange state in which werewolves find themselves: unable to trust Wolf.​
                                It's not working, dudes. I'm not joining in one your angst parade. Also, the motivations ascribed to them are seeking a form of Valhalla. Let's face it; this isn't a reboot when shit like that slips out, setting aside that by Revised the Get would want to invade Valhalla to set it on fire and kill the Einherjar. Again.

                                Some of the character details are also... oddly, perversely amusing. Like, the Ragabash is literally said to find joy in others' failures (schadenfraude!), or how the Ahroun is "festooned with a variety of abhorrent symbols". I'm betting that the symbols are of a type that aren't considered abhorrent in India or East Asia, where they're associated with Buddha or the dead, but that's just going off of the general path from "this is a continuation of the game!" W5 from back in the early 2020s to the present "this is a reboot, nay, a reimagining!"

                                Also, the theurge from the CoF has a rite that allows them to stop a character from regenerating. Having read through the system fully now, that seems crazy powerful, because Garou have very few good damage reduction powers and are absolutely dependent on regenerating damage constantly to not die.

                                The Stargazers are the new Fianna, in that they are akin to Spinal Tap's take on the druids. No one knows where they came from, or what they were doing! BUY MY BOOK to find out more whenever we decide to get on that!

                                Stolen Moons - presented without credit to J. F. Sembrano! They constitute not just werewolves that have stolen skins to become werewolves, but also the indeterminately cursed and those who... (sigh deeper than in Track of the Moon Beast describing stew) made a deal with the devil.

                                The Starving Remnant hints that disconnection and becoming a spirit in the Umbra when you die is a thing. But it takes a giant shit on the prospect of ancestor-spirits by basically just making it into a Garou spirit pulling a Ju-On to attack other werewolves.

                                Raven-shifters won't shut up and have an eye for shinies. No fucking way is this not supposed to be the W:tA of old.

                                Spider-shifters get a break on being able to use Sacred Territory; they can just shot web. No need to unzip or cut yourself (or... both?) when you don't have sacred caern water to spill for a temporary boost.

                                The section on vampires opens up with a Garou talking about how they're an infestation in cities. Doesn't seem like anyone who was claiming that Garou and Kindred could be friends were really reading this book, honestly. Two vampire antagonists, one of each of the other games from the Big Five, with the fae being presented as some kind of super-edgy fishmalk.

                                We get normal threats. Breaking a WoD tradition, cops aren't given borderline superhuman stats. Well, maybe the IAO or SAD. I'm not quite sure where the IAO is going to place spy devices where a being who can arbitrarily control technology isn't going to find them, though. And for Glass Walkers? Fuhgeddaboutit!

                                Animals as antagonists - I'll at least give the text that it's good to consult groups before using them, since fighting off beasts in the forests like some kind of early level hack RPG grindfest is not a thing that most probably take well. Wolves are mentioned first, and... have exactly the same stats as great cats. Do... do the people working on this game know that tigers are closer to bear size than wolf size? Also, someone forgot to give them good Survival stats; animals all get 1 for social and mental actions that don't involve intimidation, which is right out. No one's going to be rolling Academics, but there's often a wide variety of social effectiveness in groups of social animals (such as wolves), and spotting traps for an animal that has experience with humans is a common capability (just ask anyone with very stubborn community cats). We also get swarm mechanics, for treating larger groups of small animals or other beings as a single target with a single health level track.

                                Oh, since bears have higher Physical pools than great cats or wolves, they also get a higher sneaking dice pool. This is actually closer to real life than you might think; Golden Kamuy could rattle on about bears being tricky all day, but at the very least, they are among the animals that will backtrack if you're following them.

                                        Loresheets! They're supposed to connect the character to the greater WoD. They work like Backgrounds, so they kind of fall through at certain levels. What do we get?

                                        Monkeywrenchers: Go from swaying crowds to setting fires to throwing parties to finding scapegoats to making another monkeywrencher a scapegoat once per story in exchange for pinning something definitively on Pentex. I think they lost the plot somewhere, since most presentations of monkeywrenching that I recall were a wee but subtler, involving deep cover infiltration and information drops. This is definitely a pessimistic take on the act and practice of being a monkeywrencher, in keeping with its "nothing matters, you can't win" attempts to wrench angst from the reader.

                                        Project Twilight: What the Monkeywrencher was trying to be, but with the FBI's monster-hunting SAD. You're like Marsha Crowe from the original book, but instead of Kinfolk posted to monitor it, you're a full-on werewolf. You know, if the Garou could do this, how did they lose? This allows you to cover up shit, distort the truth, and potentially sic the agency on your enemies. Inconsistent with many of the presentations of the world, to be sure, but haven't you wanted to sit back and laugh while SAD cooperates with the SI to take down a vampire?

                                        Umbral Traveler: This shouldn't be a Background. This should be the default for how you interact with the Umbra. This is like Day 1 DLC fixing a broken Umbra to allow you to, you know, survive it and actually use it in a chronicle.

                                        Renunciate of Fenris: You were caught in the editor's temper tantrum between editions. Much like Umbral Traveler, this is Day 1 DLC in some cases, like being the only system that allows you to avoid being counted out from accumulating too much Hauglosk. The first dot, though, is just insulting, as it describes your character as liking the taste of leather.

                                        The Black Spiral: The infamous "I guess those Spirals aren't so bad, after all!" Loresheet, as the ceiling level allows you to deprogram BSDs. I can only assume that Mage will allow you to redeem Nephandi like Jodi Blake after just three days. The other levels are like Wyrm Lore from older editions, only instead of risking insanity, you're risking a hit to your reputation.

                                        So, what's my problem with these as Loresheets? Well, in Weapons of the Gods, where Ken Hite got this concept from for V5, Loresheets had a wildly variable cost and effect, or set of them. As such, I'm of the opinion that Backgrounds were the wrong way to go, and that they should have been merits, as long as you're using both by default. Secondly, as noted, some of these are just restoring prior functionality instead of actually building up to something, especially with the mountain of lore that W:tA has. If it seems overwhelming to others, you can slow drip it to people in the form of Loresheets, kind of like how WotG manages to ease you into its setting (as well as cultural background information, for those unfamiliar with Chinese history and culture or the genres of wuxia and xianxia).

                                        This would have been the perfect place to cram in the various prominent ancestor-spirits featured in Rage, for example. Or talismans, you remember those? They have legends associated with them; they aren't just equipment with better plusses. By making them Merits and detailing various facets of the setting, you could use Loresheets to get engagement with any portion of the setting, zero in on one corner of the lore as your Chronicle's focus, set up conflicts for your character that are narratively interesting... and they use it as fucking Day 1 DLC. Well, I suppose that's a classic PDX move, but still, what a waste.

                                        How I would make it work:

                                        Project Twilight (*)
                                        Many among the Garou Nation attempt to place moles in human agencies and other places of power, but you have actually managed it, passing all background checks and avoiding too much suspicion to sit right within the belly of the beast as a SAD agent. You will need to keep up with typical work expected of a federal investigator, but if you have a supernatural method of sharing thoughts and information, you can keep your pack or handler abreast of SAD operations and potentially steer them away from any incidences involving werewolves, at least before anyone can clean up, without any the wiser.

                                        *-**** Rank: The default assumption is that your ranking in SAD is as a junior agent. By paying more merit dots, you can increase your seniority, up to the point of being its director. Talk with your ST to determine how far placed your chronicle can see you.

                                        *+: Armory: Equivalent to your standing, you can requisition potentially valuable items from the SAD armory, potentially including devices that are effective against vampires or other threats in the dark. Normally, these must reside in and be returned to the holding location, or else the character risks scrutiny.

                                        *** Raid: After spending several sessions during the story researching a threat, the character can arrange for the target of this scrutiny to suffer a raid by the agency's primary combatants. Such agents will be armed and armored, with whatever countermeasures SAD possesses against it if the party is known to be a "strange threat" off the books. Note that while this can be an incredible weapon against some threats, its usefulness is limited against very old vampires, archmages and other figures of power, or large concentrations of beings that can easily take on an agent one on one, such as Black Spiral hives. It is also ill-advised to try it against corrupting threats like the Chulorviah, who can infect agents participating in the raid and turn them into the Enfolded.

                                        ****: Top Secret: You have security clearance to know whatever US federal law enforcement knows about the supernatural; if you are below an appropriately senior rank, you must come up with a good reason for this alongside your ST, such as through a "Deep Throat" or "Mr. X" figure. This allows you to roll again to discern something about another monster if you fail your roll.

                                            I would just like to add, that in my own extensive review of W5 that I posted here earlier this year, I mentioned how a few other problems: the lack of crediting the creators of earlier editions on the credit page; a sidebar in the Umbra chapter on how only the Garou can visit the Umbra, other splats have their own sections of the Otherworlds, and if any of them could get to the Spirit Wilds, they wouldn't experience the same things; and an unusual typo in the index of where "Gaia" is capitalized in one instance, but not another, showing how each entry was probably entered in at different times. I could go on, but it would just be easier to find the closed thread yourselves. (I am not good at adding links. Sorry.)

                                            I will add that many of the 'problems' with W5 seem stem from the lack of time to finish writing and editing it before the self-imposed deadline. The designers seemed to have particular ideas in mind in 'correcting' the problems with previous editions. While they did manage to make those changes, other portions were left incomplete. It feels like they were following a video game model, where important additions would come in later forms, for the about the same amount of money; instead of the traditional TTRPG model where such material would be more 'optional' in nature.

                                            What gets me is that when the information about W5 was first released about two years ago, I was able to come up with metaplot explanations for many of the changes that afternoon, without the help of looking at any of the previous material. Yet, after many years, the designers felt that they had to start over from scratch to get to the same points. I could give a lengthy rundown, but one example should be enough.

                                            For instance, the current generation of Garou don't trust their history because it is mostly oral, and 'everyone knows' how untrustworthy that can be. Many W5 fans defend this saying it allows them to make up their own histories, something that fans have always been allowed to do. These fans also gloss over the fact that it could be hiding that the design team ran out of time to figure out what to keep and how to interpret it. Short, simple, and very quick.

                                            My take: During the lead-up events to the Last Battle, the Garou Nation found out that the Silver Record was a reproduction, the original destroyed by the White Howlers when they emerged as the BSD. A critical prophecy was needed, but had been lost due to this, and it needed to be recreated. In the distant past, the Garou asked each Tribe to send in some of their own records to recreate the Record. However, this was incomplete, as each Tribe redacted portions that they felt shown their Tribe in a bad light, leaving a widely biased form of most of the histories. Even then, the Record is barely 20% the size it was then, even with the modern additions included in the centuries since. A entire cross-Tribal camp was created to prevent anyone finding out that the Record was destroyed, save for a few Elders every generation. Those kept out of the loop included the Keeper. When the Stargazers joined, they revealed that a complete version still existed, in the High Umbra. Thus began a campaign to discourage Garou to enter there. The entire story comes out when one of the Elders reveals it when the need for the missing prophecy becomes too great. While that aided in the Last Battle, the current generation of Garou distrusts anything about the histories, even those events that occurred later, especially after a rouge member of the camp does something to prevent anyone in finding the Record in the High Umbra.

                                            Yes, my way is much longer, but it easily connects to existing lore, even in this shortened version presented here. It still leads to a suspicion of the past, but in a way that just barely stretches the imagination, in a way that W5 does not. I did this for many of the changes found in W5, even with changes to past lore. Now, if I could do that without looking at any of the older material, with the majority outline being made in a few hours, someone with access to every book ever made and many years could have done the same.
