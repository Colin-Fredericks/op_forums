                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                My general statement was that the book doesn't utterly trash the idea of doing anything to fight against the Wyrm but suggests that it is going for the idea that any action might be pointless but that provides the player character context for doing their actions anyway. Which is an interesting mood to explore for a roleplayer, IMHO.

                It's not for everyone but it's not even something that wasn't present in other installments.
                But one of the biggest issues W5 has is a lack of context. Reading the W5 corebook one learns a lot about what the game is against and what parts of Garou-culture are bad - but there's no counter-point to that. Moreso, there's no perspective for gameplay in the book.

                Take Rage. It's decried as a bad thing and one of the main-reasons why Garou are the wrong tool to fix the world. They've removed Gnosis and folded it into Rage. You sit down with this game and every PC has to use Rage. So while it's one thing to have that pessimistic take in the book, it will be rendered completely meaningless at the table because mechanically everything has already been decided. It doesn't matter what any player decides to do, their PC is going to rely on Rage and use Rage as a resource. So if you want to take that pessimistic take of "Garou can't fix anything because of Rage", W5 sets up a scenario where PCs are forced to use Rage and then STs are supposed to punish them for using Rage and show them that that's the wrong tool to fix the world. But there's no struggle here or any agency given to the players. There's nothing interesting happening here, it's outside of the control of the player, it's terrible for roleplaying and it ultimately just becomes a feel-bad-mechanic. If any ST actually goes through with the very pessimistic take on Rage, then players are forced to get punished by the ST for simply playing the game. There's nothing interesting about making players go through that.

                Or take Renown. The book makes a big deal about how bad Garou-society is (as shown by the fall of the Garou-Nation) and how Garou-Elders are assholes as well that exploit tradition. And so young Garou are disillusioned and have lost faith in things like the Litany. Okay, that might be interesting... except the Renown-mechanics don't reflect that one bit. In fact, what is going on instead is that is another bit where context has been removed and instead it's now all put on the shoulder of the ST. It's not as bad as H5 which is even more reliant on ST-fiat for its mechanics but Renown has been turned into a mechanic completely reliant on ST-fiat. No longer do you get lists of deeds that raise or lower Renown, no longer is Renown tied to any social event... now it's all just about what the ST thinks of what a Garou-PC does. A PC does something and the ST can immediately dole out a "You're in chagrin!" and then it's also up to the ST to define how the PC can redeem themselves. Say you want to play one of those rebellious young Garou that wants to ignore Garou-traditions... well, the ST can just dole out Honor-chagrins all day. There's no play happening here. It doesn't matter what any player wants to do with their Garou-PC. In the end, it's entirely dependent on the STs definitions of the three types of Renown. You wouldn't set this up this way if the game were truly about having young Garou try to reform Garou-norms and -traditions. There's no path here for players to have an impact on how Renown works. It's all entirely dependent on the ST.
