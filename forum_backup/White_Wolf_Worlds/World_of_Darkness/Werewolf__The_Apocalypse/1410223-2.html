    Owl is described as having a relatively small brood. He finds few spirits worthy of his protection. But he is a totem of wisdom, secret knowledge, and knowledge beyond the ken of others as well as secret spirits he hides from others. It's a potent description – tight and focused.

    Only three spirits are given, but all very evocative. They all have memorable names and concepts. Probably my favorite spirit in all of Axis Mundi is the Twice Born – mouse spirits eaten by Owl that dwelled in death and thus learned its secrets, and then regurgitated alive in rebirth. Common before the Gauntlet, they are quite rare now. Death's Breaths are jagglings created when spirits themselves are destroyed and aren't returned to Gaia and are the liminal spirits of memories and knowledge lost upon death. Darklings are spirits that Owl created to help teach the wisdom associated with grief, death, pain, and anguish so these negative things don't overwhelm and destroy them.

    This is a great and creative imagination of Owl's brood. And I can see these showing up in many chronicles at some point. I am not that creative and would just recite a bunch of owls and other nocturnal animals. I want to be able to create spirits that are appropriate, creative, even poetic. The problem is that with just three of them, it's insufficient to see everything that could comprise that brood.

    I would say that Owl is he who sees in darkness. Everything else springs from there. He is the lord of all nocturnal birds. The overlord of all nocturnal predators, and overseer of all nocturnal animals. He is at home with silence and darkness, and the lord of the woods at night. He teaches the unknowable mysteries unseen by others. And Owl has superb hearing and sight. So there should be spirits of darkness, silence, vigilance, bringers of unwanted but necessary knowledge, and a scattering of other nocturnal animals that have arguable ties to Owl. If we got five more spirits at the same level of the three we have, the brood would be awesome.

    This is much better than the totems in the Silent Striders tribebook which is just a list of animals associated with Egypt plus the celestial objects North Star and Sirius which are important because of their aide to determine place and time, important to travelers. Unlike most tribebook totems and Axis Mundi spirits, there is very little that gels together. Most of those totems seem better suited for other broods. And that's fine since not all friendly spirits need to belong to the same brood. The spirits here soar above the depiction of spirits in the tribebooks.

        Pegasus is next. We're not really given a description of the totem or what Pegasus represents. Just a complaint that man treats woman terribly, and then an attempt to reconcile the Greek myth of Pegasus with Garou lore. But it doesn't really work. Since the Furies are said to be one of the oldest tribes, Pegasus must predate the Greek myth of Bellerophon by thousands of years. This is always something the game has struggled with. The obvious links at face value - Black Furies = Amazons = Greek Myths = Greeks - falls apart upon inspection. The Amazons were not Greeks, but barbarians to the Greeks. And there is nothing particularly feminine about the Greek myth of Pegasus. And the chimera monster of Greek myth does not fit the actual Chimera of Garou totems. Various attempts to explain and reconcile this has never been satisfactory in my opinion.

        However, the spirits described are better and form a unity we're not given in the description. Again we only have four spirits. The chimera of legend is now the Chimaera gaffling, created by Pegasus to represent man's divided nature and allow him to slay it, becoming a better person and thus capable of treating women better. It's a very psychological spirit. But it is rarely summoned because one needs a dragon's blood, hair, or excrement to summon it! Needless to say, it's almost never summoned. It's a Gaffling, but only one exists. It's a noble attempt to incorporate the real myth in a meaningful way for Garou, but doesn't quite work. What purpose does an almost impossible to summon unique Gaffling have in the game? This came so close just to fail. I think a second or third rewrite may turn it into something useful in game.

        We have a Pegasus gaffling, which is obvious but great to have as we now have details on what Pegasus is supposed to be like. They only exist to perform brief missions for Pegasus – to save someone from danger or provide warning or wisdom, or announce omen of sorrow. Then we build on earlier game concepts and it states Pegasus will never appear in from of a Get of Fenris except to attack one! It's solid.

        We also get the obvious neopagan concept of the Triple Goddess as the Triptych spirit. And then the Haima, the Red Mothers, which is essentially the spirit of menstruation. However, the Haima is done very well, and it's the best spirit out of the bunch. Not sure how much practical use it'll get, but very fitting for a game containing a Black Fury.

        The common theme of these spirits, of course, is “woman” and “Pegasus as a protector of women”. Build on that for the totem's brood. The four spirits here (except maybe Chimaera because it ultimately doesn't do the job it's supposed to do) are actually a good foundation. It just needs to be expanded upon, touching on the sacred feminine, women as an embodiment of it, and the need to protect it.

        I think the issue is to get past the Greek myths and figure out a purely Garou interpretation of Pegasus that still reconciles Greek Myths. I think the first step is to stop thinking of the totem as “Pegasus” since it is so loaded as a term, and think how the same symbol might be depicted earlier.

        Pegasus basically is a white winged horse so let's think of it as that. White, like silver, is the lunar color. As a winged creature, Pegasus is an intermediary from the heavens to the earth, representing a divine messenger. She brings the wisdom of the moon, and the moon is a potent symbol of women because its cycle mirrors the menstrual cycle. In the time when the Black Furies formed, the horse was an untamed animal. It would not become domesticated until around 5000-3000 BC which is probably after the Impergium ends. So at the time, it might have been a symbol of the Wyld. And white horses in general show up in many different mythologies representing an entity of exceptional powers, divinity, or excellence. So we can say the White Winged Horse (aka Pegasus) represents a carrier or bearer of the power of the sacred feminine from Luna to earth. The Furies wanted Artemis as a totem, but she sent Pegasus instead. I think it is for the same reason Father Wolf is not the totem of any tribe either – Luna does not pick favorites, all Garou and Changing Breeds are her nieces and nephews. Pegasus was the consolation prize.

        (That still doesn't explain why Pegasus hates Fenris and his Get so much. Metagame reasons is that originally the Get were positioned as the tribe that discriminated against women. When that was eventually jettisoned, the ban of Pegasus stayed the same. Interestingly, this was always one way. Pegasus would never accept a Get of Fenris as a pack member, but Fenris had no problem with a Black Fury being one of his children We likely need to invent a reason why Fenris as a totem angered Pegasus as a totem. Maybe he tried to eat him.)

        So we need spirits that represent or confer the spiritual gifts of femininity, especially the ability to give birth to life. We also need spirits that protect those gifts and the bearers of them. And certain spirits associated with the moon. The four spirits of Axis Mundi are a perfect foundation for this – we just need more examples. The Tribebook again is disappointing – relying mainly on Greek myths instead of universalizing the theme of the totem. I think things associated with traditional, universal women's roles would be a good start – weaving, sewing, and other work that can be done by mothers who need to stay close to toddlers. It's stereotypical, but I think that's only bad if that is all you do. Those archetypes should come into play. We just should not be limited to them and also look at non-traditional roles, and other archetypes that center on women.

        Amazons of course are an important inspiration. So I would assign Aray from the Players Guide to Garou here. Aray – obviously derived from Ares – is supposed to be an early war spirit. I'm still not happy about spirits derived from human culture for Garou, but unlike other war gods, Ares was always more a manifestation of brutality, bloodlust, and the savagery of combat than a true personality. It's a very primordial concept, and Aray should be more like that. More like a force of the Wyld similar to the frenzying maenads. And of course, the legendary Amazons are said to be the daughters of Ares which creates a tie if we believe the Furies had Amazons as their kinfolk (or were the legendary Amazons themselves).

        Ares was supposed to have mated with the naiad Harmonia. Naiads were nymphs of springs, brooks, wells, and fountains. So there's a possibility we might place Sulis – a spirit of thermal springs from the Players Guide to Garou – here. A freshwater nymph represents the giving of life, and therefore embodies a feminine concept. I think Sulis could be in several broods (I mentioned earlier it could be in Bear's), but a strong argument could be made here. Or if "Sulis" is in Bear then "Harmonia" a similar spirit could be here.

        Likewise you could place various equine spirits here. Not only are they tied to Pegasus, but the legendary Amazons were masters of horse riding. That conflicts with an earlier idea that feral horses are under Griffin. Most horses now are domesticated horses which conflicts with the Furies as being heavily tied to the Wyld. So this may only be a historical association than does not exist in modern games except for the very few wild horses reintroduced in Mongolia (the last tarpan died in 1909). There's also zebras, an equine animal that has never been domesticated. Few Garou have connections to sub-Saharan Africa, and it'd be a distinguishing mark. Besides, outside of the Asia, the Greeks identified other Amazon tribes in Africa (albeit north of the Sahara as they had very limited information farther south). There's also a role for some kind of vengeful punishment spirit. Pegasus's tribe is named after the Furies after all. And in many myths Artemis punishes lots of men for defiling her temples, sacred places, or attempting to violate her priestesses. So some kind of Nemesis spirit would be good. And as an essentially lunar spirit, perhaps some variant of a Lune.

            Rat is defined as the wild incarnate in a survive or die environment, and specifically towards the “urban wylderness” and that rat's power comes from his great numbers. This is a good theme for a brood, but I'm a disappointed no thought has been given to Rat before the rise of modern cities or human civilization. It's quick reproduction, large litter size, and role as small scavenger would have had some merit for the omega tribe when civilization was fairly small.

            We're given 7 spirits here which makes Rat one of the better defined broods in the book. These are mainly gafflings of animal and other spirits one would expect in the city. The only real surprise is a Wanderlust spirit for those Bone Gnawers on the move. There's rat and raccoon gafflings. While I understand raccoons are now a common animal in some cities, I don't think it really fits within Rat's brood. Heap Gafflings represent trash spirits, but were originally the spirits that represented things that could be scavenged. It's a neat little spirit. We also learn that these are "deserters" of the Great Trash Heap which is now an Incarna in its own right. I like this a lot. Urban Englings are just englings in a more appropriate form for the urban penumbra. Then there are Feral Psychagonea and Lost Dogs (the spirits of abused animals and feral pets).

            Overall this is a solid, but unspectacular brood. It has some interesting new spirits, but nothing incredible like we've seen in some of the past broods. It covers the basics and has lots of spirits any urban based chronicle will run into again and again. Bone Gnawers are seen as a mostly urban tribe, but I would have liked to see examples of spirits from an earlier time when their defining trait would the “omega tribe” in a wilderness setting. Since both Cockroach and Rat covers "cities" it would have been nice to see a clear distinction between what kind of spirits fall into either, but there is a loose division between the more Weaver spirits go into Cockroach and the more Wyld spirits go here. Still "Scab Birds" could have gone here just as well as Cockroach. Still this is a good brood which will get lots of use in any urban chronicle. I think this is a good example of what some of the other broods might have been liked if they added another four spirits to their list of 3-4.

                Stag is said to have a large and unified brood, protecting it well as befits a totem of respect and honor. He wants his children to try to solve their own problems before calling him, but he will do it when needed. My perceptions of Stag is heavily influenced by Bambi so I expect him to be the Prince of the Forest and represent the bounty of the land. That's not mentioned in the description, so let's see how the spirits compare to my ideal.

                We just hit the motherlode with no less than 12 spirits for this brood. The variety and scale makes this the best brood in the book hands down despite none of them being at the creative level of, say, the Twice-Born of Owl. I wish the other broods had been done this well. Good job to Harry Heckel, the writer per the credits. First, we get a wide variety of spirits representing dawn, grain alcohol, antelopes, rabbits, brooks, squirrels, kelpies. Second, we get different power levels with lots of gafflings, then jagglings like Black Stags and White Harts, and either unique jagglings or lesser incarna such as Dawn, Hind, and Yale.

                We don't get anything really imaginative, but the number and variety make up for it. (I use Stag's brood as my goal for my own attempts at defining broods.) In an ideal world, all of those broods would have development like this plus some imaginative spirits at level where we find in Owl and Chimera or even Pegasus. The spirits of Stag's brood should get a lot of play in most wilderness based chronicles. Their large numbers and unity/loyalty to Stag are a good way to distinguish them from other broods.

                    Next is Uktena which is linked to water, search for hidden knowledge, and lots of snakelike creatures, including those from other cultures. It's fairly basic, but it is a strong, resonant theme.

                    We get six spirits. Four of which are large serpent monsters from different cultures, and two being fish and snake gafflings given silly names (Dakwa's Children and Inadu). Somewhat boring, but it gets the job done. The writer defined his totem brood and then produced spirits that fit it. We can do lots of variations of a theme, but the main things are covered. We could do one or two truly creative spirits, but the spirts here will get lots of play – unlike say Chimera's or Falcon's. It's a solid C+.

                    It fits a needed role though compared to broods I've mentioned earlier. Uktena is basically a spirit of the fresh waters and legendary serpents representing hidden knowledge and the obstacles to overcome them. That separates it from more saltwater creatures or other reptiles found elsewhere. Not all spirits will easily fall into Uktena versus Dragon Kings or Turtle or Old Man Sea, but most will and you wing the rest.

                    The Werewolf totem Uktena is actually a combination of two separate spirits/creatures of Indian myth and legend. There are various horned serpents (known by many names, but the Cherokee calls it Uktena) and the underwater panther of the Great Lakes peoples, usually called Mishipeshu or some variant. In fact while the name Uktena is used, most visual depictions of the spirit in the game better matches Mishipeshu. Both have similar features. Both seem to represent the dangers of the waters – especially rapids and the like. Both can cause storms, and both are opponents of Thunderbird. Sometimes they are viewed as helpful and protective creatures, but more often they are seen as dangerous and malevolent. For Werewolf, we can consider that to be the difference from human perspective versus that of the Garou. I'll just call this combined creature Uktena for ease.

                    So we might add spirits of whitewater rapids, flash floods, whirlpools, and other freshwater spirits that could be considered to be dangerous as well as any water that originate from below the ground (as opposed to falling from the heavens) so it can be a spirit of wells, geysers, and reservoirs. Besides the fish who live here, we might add some freshwater aquatic birds as well. Some of these bird spirits might be associated in other broods, but this could be another place to those that are not obviously part of

                    I think it's also appropriate to add some sort of Healing or Medicine spirits. That is also something that fits legend, and it ties in well to various healing snakes in the Old World. We could call them Asclepians. This might be controversial to some people who want Uktena and Wendigo to be merely North American based. But I've come to the conclusion that these spirits and their broods should have been known to the Old World Garou – though perhaps known by other names. Likewise, spirits of poisons and venoms are also located here.

                    These kind of water spirits were said to live under the earth, so we could extend correspondences to Uktena like caves and subterranean passages. Since these are often created by underwater water erosion, it makes sense. So any spirits associated with cave networks could potentially fit here.

                    These water spirits often had special attendants that served as lookouts for their enemy Thunderbird. They would appear to let Uktena know whether there were clouds about. There were three main sorts - beaver, elk, and deer. Beaver is obviously associated with water, and the last two had horns like Uktena. Theoretically any animal that had attributes linking it to Uktena could be used. In Werewolf, some of these animals are already claimed by other totems, but we could still create a spirit type, “Uktena's Attendants” or "Uktena's Scouts" to represent the spirit that does this role.

                    The importance of Uktena's conflict with Thunderbird may have direct relevance for us. Thunderbird is clearly a spirit and lord of thunder and storms. So what is the connection between Thunderbird and Grandfather Thunder? Personally, I would say that while there is a separate Thunderbird spirits and pack totems, that ultimately “the” Thunderbird is just a different name for Grandfather Thunder. There is no need for two separate spirits that control the skies, weather, storms, and thunder. It also fits the theme that the thunder god is opposed to a serpent figure which is found throughout the Old World. So we can use this legend to explain a conflict between broods of Thunder and Uktena. This actually fits well with the stories of the Shadow Lords being very hostile to the tribe. It could also explain why we see Thunderbird mentioned among Wendigo and even Pumonca, but not with Uktena.

                    I don't think Onkwe has posted on this forum recently which is too bad. He'd probably have a lot of insight to add here.

                        Last we have Wendigo. He is a greatly feared totem with bitter rage born from a cold heart. I take that to mean he has no mercy and is unmoved by tears. We're given a story about how Wendigo was once Sasquatch until bad things happened. I never liked that element to the totem and ignore it myself, but it's a burden I have to deal with. And that's it. It's not a good description. It's a partial narrative that is incomplete, and does not bode well. The best totems and broods have strong thematic elements. We'd need something like “Wendigo is the spirit of terrible winters that lead to deprivation and possible starvation,” and then build on that strong theme. Obviously there is some of this here, but it is more incidental than thought through.

                        I believe that all the totems should have been known to the Garou. So although Wendigo is very culturally specific, I think the Old World Garou just would have known him by a different name. Maybe Fimbulwinter, or the Great Winter, or Boreal Wind as I mentioned before.

                        One nice bit is that all members of Wendigo brood can be talked with anyone who knows the Gift Spirit Speech OR the level two Wendigo tribal Gift Speak with the Wind Spirits. This is a great idea. Not only does it improve a rather lackluster Gift, it implies a much greater bond between the Wendigo tribe and Wendigo's brood as potentially all tribe members can speak with their allied spirits, not just theurges. This is an interesting take on the tribe, and a great use of the spirit brood idea. It's very distinctive. The writer gets extra points for this. Incidentally, this is an example of so many great things about the setting that are buried in various sourcebooks, but never get repeated elsewhere. (At least, I don't remember it. Does anyone know if this is mentioned in any of the Wendigo tribebooks?)

                        We have five spirits which isn't many, but certainly is better than some of broods. We start with Wolverine which is great. He's known for his ferocious appetite. However, the description describes him as more like a trickster, and emphasizes he is not the pack totem in the Players Guide. This is technically Kwakwadjec (an alternative spelling of Carcajou, a trickster figure of Innu lore) and not "Wolverine", so he's like a specialized wolverine spirit. It's good to be expanding the brood, but there's a general tendency by the sourcebooks to make the Wendigo less about a fearsome winter spirit and more about generic American Indian humans. And this is not a good sign.

                        Next are Atcen which are “ice cannibals” created when humans succumb to cannibalism in winter. They fit thematically. Although they are not compared to “False Wendigo” spirits mentioned in Ways of the Wolf, I imagine those might be Wyrm corrupted versions of the Atcen. There are also Ice Elementals which are obvious brood members. Windtooth is a kind of wind spirit whose presence is noticed as frostbite. A little more creative treatment of an elemental/weather spirit. I like it a lot. Then there are mosquito spirits, appropriate as they are found throughout the north – especially on the tundra – and “eat” people like cannibals do. Great choice in my opinion. I would separate “these” mosquito spirits from their tropical members.

                        I like these spirits, but feel it was a mistake to not make Wolverine an actual Wolverine. It comes from a desire to incorporate more Indian myth and perpetuates the problem that the Wendigo are best defined by human identities as opposed to the totem. It's fine to include some elements as a trickster and perhaps a companion jaggling, but the brood would be strengthened by simply assigning Wolverine here.

                        It's a good start, but we have a very narrow brood and thus quickly boring. We need some variety and increased scope, but appropriate to its theme. Usually at this point I turn to the tribebooks for inspiration. Unfortunately, I have low opinion on the Wendigo books. They are less about a cult devoted to a fierce winter spirit and more about generic Indian stuff. First edition tribebook has Salmon, Otter, Jaybird, Peyote, and Thunderbird. What do they have to do with Wendigo or winter or desperate hunger? There are the Winter Manitous, which are obvious, but still limiting. Second edition is not much better giving us Dolphin, Moose, Caribou, Sturgeon, Rabbit, Goose, Porcupine, and Beaver. Caribou is nice, and I can see a Garou myth where Caribou defects from Stag in order to be better protected by Wendigo so it can survive the harsh northern climes. But the rest have little to do with Wendigo's brood, although all are fine pack totems by themselves that Wendigo or other Garou might want to use. We could take the North Wind from the Wind Incarnas and place him here, but it's probably best to keep all the Winds together.

                        The Wendigo have a very strong concept, but it's not been properly developed in my opinion. The spirits here are good, but insufficient. We need to do some thinking. I've never had a problem with the idea of Wendigo being a cannibal spirit, but the Garou being against man-eaters. There's a strong concept of the game of spirit taboos, and that Garou adopt certain taboos in exchange for power. Garou vulnerability to silver, the lunar metal, is best explained as a taboo they accepted in exchange for Luna's blessings. That's just how spirits work. Likewise, as a beneficiaries of Wendigo, a cannibal spirit, the tribe does not partake of meat. I see Wendigo as embodying both cannibalism as a result of a starvation winter AND a punisher of said cannibals for violating natural laws. He doesn't transform those people into monsters – the False Wendigo spirits from Ways of the Wolf does that.

                        In a certain sense, he represents a spirit of judgment against transgressors who put their own hunger, consumption, or greed ahead of their community. We could have a spirit that represents not consuming any more than is needed, which in today's world could act against our entire consumer culture and represent an ethical, simple life. There is probably also a starvation spirit, as well as spirits of “starvation foods” (foods not typically eaten, but only in times of famine. Those could include plants like cattails, certain wild nuts, dandelion roots, chickweed, certain mushrooms, and other forageable plants. I don't know enough myself to name specifics, but there could be a generic spirit type that represents those plants. One or more evergreen trees could also be one, representing survival in a harsh environment. Spruce might be a good tree to use. Lichens and mosses might go here. Possibly spirits of permafrost, peats, and bogs. Strangely, we may wantr to raid Rage Across Russia and find appropriate spirits there since Russia is obviously associated with a lot of the same climate as Wendigo. We'd just need to rename some spirits to make them more universal than using the "give stats to anything mentioned in Russian folklore" approach there. The aurora borealis would also be a good spirit as it is iconic of the arctic circle. It's even listed in the Wendigo tribebook under Allies, and is the only other spirit listed there besides Great Wendigo himself! We may want to include seals and walruses as iconic arctic animals that northern peoples hunt to survive.

                        Both Fenris and Wendigo are harsh masters, but if Fenris is about courage, then Wendigo should be about endurance and survival. I also prefer the idea that Wendigo is not human culturally specific and should be known in some form in the Old World (though not by the Wendigo name, at least not until the Old World and New World Garou met and the old tribes learned that spirit was the patron of the Wendigo tribe).

                        If I want to do this totem justice, I think I may have to read a lot more Jack London and study more on taiga and tundra ecosystems. It would be easy to pick every animal that lives in the far north and stick it in Wendigo's brood, but I don't like the idea. Every clime should have spirits of many broods in order to make potential chronicles set there interesting and diverse. And Wendigo's brood may be less likely to appear the more south you go, but as this winter's weather shows, Windtooth might even show up in Texas sometime. I just want something more than just winter weather. Of all the tribal totems, I need to think the most on this one.

                                            Large Herd Animals Part I

                                            I do think there is still a major gap for the spirit broods. That would be large animals that move in herds. Things like buffalo, bison, cattle, elephants, and the like. At one point I was thinking of two separate broods – one for herd animals (like bison), and one for large animals (like elephants), but the more I studied them I decided these overlapped and are best just one brood. These are generally prey animals.

                                            Many prey animals fall under Stag. And we also have Boar. But I felt these animals were thematically different. Stag's prey animals, while some are herd animals, stay safe by running away. Boar's prey animals are dangerous and fight back individually (some of these are small enough to be prey for larger animals, but are also predators themselves to smaller animals). These animals though employ a herd defense – weaker animals inside while the stronger ones defend them – and those that are taken are the stragglers who don't form up in time. They stand their ground and do not run away. It's not a perfect definition, but a division of “those who run”, “those who fight”, and “those who stand” is good enough to work. STs can easily slot random prey animal there if a spirit comes into play and its brood matters.

                                            There are several totem spirits that fall into this category. Buffalo in Wild West, and the Players Guides have Bison and Bull. Elephant is mentioned in Uktena Tribebook, probably more because it is “exotic” and an early feature of Uktena was joining with people around the world. Mammoth would fit, but we know it now belongs to Griffin's brood. Before they died out, it might have been here. In fact, it might have even been the major incarna that lead it!

                                            Although not mentioned as totems, animals like hippos, giraffes, wildebeests, wild yak, and muskox fit here. I also think we should the marine mammals that also fit the idea of large herd animals that eat grass – manatees and dugongs. It both diversifies this brood as well as adds another layer to the ocean diversity.

                                            One thing we should discuss is the nature of Bull. Is Bull actually the spirit of domesticated (male) cattle, or is it more like Aurochs, its now extinct wild ancestor? A case can be made for both, but the answer may have consequences. If Bull is actually Aurochs, how does it continue to survive as a spirit? Is it still too soon for it to fade? Might Aurochs be better off under Griffin? Is there a reason Griffin might not accept him (perhaps allowing himself to be “domesticated” offends Griffin.) If instead Bull is the domesticated animal, should it be elsewhere like some theoretical brood of domesticated animals (this issue will come up again as the game has several domestic animal totems, and I will try to address it in a later post)? Or is “Bull” more like the essence of a strong, aggressive animal that transcends its domesticated nature? STs will need to decide this.

                                            Of course Bull and Aurochs brings up the topic of the Apis, the werebulls. It is probable that Aurochs/Bull might even have been the original leader of the brood as having a Changing Breed would provide it with a lot of spiritual support and power. Apis supposedly oversaw the the cycle of birth, reproduction, and death. These would have been more important to the brood when Aurochs was present, but I believe Aurochs lost rulership of the brood sometime after the destruction of the Apis. When I'm not sure, but after the Aurochs became extinct around 1627 it certainly could not have remained the leader. An import time in the history of this brood might have been when Aurochs lost power and another spirit became the incarna who lead the brood.

                                            Lastly, Apis are supposed to be the source of the Minotaur legend, and of course the Minotaur spirit is behind the Skindancers “tribe”. Now I don't believe there was any intention at the time to link the Minotaur spirit of the Skindancers to the Apis and Aurochs at all. But this could be a nice synchronicity. Some kind of surviving spirit could have been angry at the Garou and agreed to strike back at them. It's even similar to Bat falling to the Wyrm after the destruction of the Camazotz. More interesting is Minotaur's anger towards Pegasus. I'm not sure of the reason why unless the original writer thought two legendary creatures of Greek myth should be enemies of each other. We might invent a reason why such as the Black Furies were heavily responsible for the destruction of the Apis. While Minotaur would not be a member of this brood, it could indicate a degree of resentment, disappointment or anger of the brood to the Garou.

                                                Large Herd Animals Part II

                                                At this point, we need to figure out who the major Incarna of this brood is. I think either Elephant or Bison/Buffalo are our two best candidates. Both are endangered animals, though the Bison's long term survival is probably better. I am willing to be convinced of another candidate should someone make an argument. Simply for geographic diversity, having an African animal like Elephant (though they exist in Asia too) as a brood leader would be nice. But Bison has its own advantages. As a fellow bovine, it might have been easier for it to “inherit” the position. This is important because obviously more correspondences will be created once we have the major incarna. Elephant would have correspondences with memory, and ivory which might expand the brood to includes other tusked animals like walruses. Bison might emphasize the importance of being a food source or source of economic benefit.

                                                One interesting aspect (especially if you consider Mammoth to originally be the brood's leader) is that as endangered animals, the brood is particularly vulnerable as a result of failing leadership. The entire brood seems under assault with so many key animals/spirits being lost over the years. This could have interesting consequences in the Umbra. And it could lead to a very distinctive roleplay among these spirits – very wary, nervous, untrusting, paranoid, etc. With many spirits who once belonged to this brood falling to slumber, or fading entirely away. This might mean this is one of the weaker and smaller broods. Maybe there is a concerted effort by the Wyrm to destroy this brood entirely. That could lead to some interesting chronicles.

                                                Interestingly, these are also the animals that mankind has attempted in the past century to retrieve from extinction. There have been historical and current attempts to bring back both Mammoths and Aurochs from extinction, as well as concerted preservation efforts for bison/buffalo, and the African megafauna. Perhaps this could in part be attributed to the work of the packs of Mammoth following its ban.

                                                So what other kind of spirits could make up this brood? They are known for their size and strength though I can't think of spirits based on those properties along. There is a safety in numbers theme so perhaps there is some kind of protective spirit that gathers/watches the herd much like a shepherd dog. And these animals provide a lot of sustenance (and other economic goods – hides, ivory, leather, etc.) to nearby people who either domesticated them or actively hunted them. So there may be spirits of bounty and wealth.

                                                I also think they impact the soil a lot – their manure and urine fertilize it, their hoofs help bury seeds, and the both dig up the soil and impact it. And these species impact the environment. Their large size and need to browse and graze prevents woodlands from getting too dense. They knock down trees, trample vegetation, eat shrubs and small trees, and open up dense stands of bushes and trees so they can graze the meadows and grasslands. Without them, dense forests can spring up. So they could be seen as a kind of “lords of the grasslands” - animals who not just dominate their landscape, but also created it. So perhaps some sort of fertility spirits, some grasses, or even a landscape spirit of the steppe/savanna could be here. There might also be some kind of “big sky” spirit to reflect the sun able to send light to illuminate the entire land, unlike forests whose canopies can block out of the sun.

                                                Their large piles of dungs attract a lot of insects, so certain flies and beetles could go here. Large herbivores like these often have inefficient digestion, so their waste provides a lot of nutrients back to the environment. I think it would be easy to simply dump most insects into a small group of Insect totems, so putting coprophagous insects here would provided welcome diversity.

                                                And these insects in turn provide meals for a lot of grassland insectivores like bats, birds, and smaller ground animals. Meadowlarks, crested larks, and pipits are examples. This might be a good place for some of them to nest here, grateful to the larger animals as the “founders of the feast”. If Stag's brood includes lots of woodland animals, than this brood would include a lot of similar animals that live in the grasslands instead of forests (for example, maybe ground squirrels belong in this brood, but tree squirrels belong to Stag). Baboons typically live in savannas as well, but raises the question about primates in general, especially the more humanlike monkeys and great apes. Should they belong to their own brood?

                                                This could be a very diverse and rich brood, but of great importance to Gaia. A distinctive quality that it has a lot of prominent species that have gone extinct under the Garou's watch, and there could be an element of the Garou failing this brood time and again. And it's own that mostly been ignored in Werewolf since the animals that make up its brood aren't typical or preferred totems of players. But I think it could play an important role in a lot of games, and the spirit brood could have a very distinctive attitude in terms of roleplay.

                                                    As I borrow friends' old copies of the breedbooks, I'm revisiting some of their patrons as major incarna. But this post actually isn't about them, but to discuss a third totem often associated with tricksters – Raven.

Although a trickster figure, I think the game has given Raven enough distinctive qualities that Raven is more than just a trickster, although that is still a role. The Corax have a very different function than tricksters – they are the messengers, scouts, and informants of Gaia. And they have lots of other distinguishing features. Ravens are carrion birds and Corax therefore have ties to death and the underworld. And in the setting the Corax are tied to Helios. In human mythology and culture, while foxes and coyotes are universally seen as cunning and tricky animals, ravens have a more varied interpretation. So at least for my chronicles, I am putting Raven with its own spirit brood. If you want to put it under a single Trickster brood where all these animals, that can work too.

                                                    I start where I normally do and look at the published pack totems to look at those I think share common themes. As a pack totem, Raven was first published in Ways of the Wolf as a totem of wisdom, and must have been popular enough that it was included in several tibebooks and finally the Revised corebook. There he's associated with wealth (Raven will always provide though the pack must not carry wealth with them) and is interestingly described as the hungriest of totems as well as described as being a trickster. As a totem, he is clearly oriented towards wilderness Garou living off the land, but there's a role for him in urban based packs too. (Interestingly Raven is described as being similar to Coyote, but “not as powerful”.)

Very similar to Raven, so we have an obvious candidate for his brood already. Now the book then states Hare is also known as Mahtigwess of Great Rabbit, this is derived from Native American legend. Of course, while very similar hares and rabbits are not the same, but Werewolf is a game about myth not science, so we'll let it pass. It could be a different spirit, or just that the Wendigo have good relations with the brood of Raven. I really don't like the inclusion of human cultural concepts for Garou (which is a too frequent issue in sourcebooks), but the name Bacchus is clearly a placeholder.                                                             Australian Spirits Part 2

                                                            In Rage Across Australia, we are given four spirits specifically mentioned as hostile to the Garou. Rainbow Serpent and Bunyip are listed as lost totems. Ghost Bunyip (a kind of Ancestor spirit, I suppose) are found in the Penumbra and represent the “ghosts” of the Bunyip. Then there is Kurpanga, a kind of Wyld spirit which happens to be hostile now. I think we can safely include all of these in Bunyip's brood.

                                                            So what else can we include? Before we do that, I think a discussion is needed about Australian spirits in general in the game, as we know some of these are friendly to Garou and should not be included,

                                                            Australia separated from the other continents around 40 million years ago. And as a result of it being tectonically stable, it lacks events like earthquakes and volcano eruptions that recycle soil rock and soil. Due to erosion, its soil is generally poor which forced its flora and fauna to coexist. It lacked dominant species and encouraged great diversity. As a result, there are many unique species there. 95% Fungi, 85% Land Marsupials, 89% Land Reptiles, 94% Frogs, and 57% Mangroves are endemic there – they are not found anywhere else. Many of this wildlife, though not all, should be found among Bunyip and Rainbow Serpent.

                                                            We know that many iconic Australian animals remain friendly to Garou, even serving as totems, so they can't be members of Bunyip's brood. Or they might have originally been, but decided to leave and join other broods after the destruction of the tribe. This is something STs can decide for themselves based on their own chronicle needs. We know Kangaroo, Owl, Kookaburra, Lyrebird, Possum, the Boobook Owl, Echidna, Crocodile, and Wedge-Tailed Eagle all act as totems for Garou. Some of these have obvious associations with existing brood. Kangaroo could be in Stag's brood as an obivious herbivore prey animal, but he would also be a good candidate as his own Incarna representing native wildlife that remains friendly or at least approachable to Garou. Given that I don't think it is possible to fit all remaining wildlife with the existing Major Incarna, I think it can be a good default. If someone has another suggestion as this Major Incarna, or even suggestions for additional broods of Australia, that would be awesome to hear. I have limited information concerning Australia, so any contributors from Australia are particularly welcomed.

                                                            RAAus mentions that in the special conditions of the Australian Penumbra (due to “spirit wards” established by the Bunyip in the Penumbra that caused it to show not the spirit world of Australia currently, but as it was at the time of the Bunyip's original arrival), that the spirits of Pleistocene Megafauna, some of which qualify as Great Beasts, still exist in the Penumbra. So there are still spirits of giant kangaroos, diprotodons, huge wombats, marsupial lions, and even thylacines here! I would place all of these in Bunyip's brood, which makes Bunyip a special figure for Australian extinct animals similar to Griffin's role elsewhere. (This could be used to explain the Red Talon's interest in Australia – make contact with these spirits and extend Griffin's protection should Bunyip be failing in this since the lost of the tribe.)

                                                            I think the relatively few marsupial carnivores should be part of Bunyip's brood. The Tasmanian Devil and Tiger Quoll slot in here easily. Most venomous and constricting snakes in Australia also fit, as these are connected to Rainbow Serpent. After that it becomes more difficult for me.

                                                            Large flightless birds like emu and cassowary might fit here. They don't obviously belong to another spirit brood, and we need more spirits to make Australian Penumbra hostile to Garou. But I also understand why someone would place it in the more friendly native wildlife brood of Kangaroo.

                                                            Hammer & Klaive gave an example that the Bunyip used a specific bird spirit to create their boomerang fetishes, and since their destruction no Garou has been able to recreate that fetish. The books says bird spirits are still willing to enter other fetishes, so it's just some lore that was lost. But it might also be that some specific bird spirit was part of Bunyip's brood, and it specifically what was entered into that fetish and now it just refuses to cooperate.

                                                            I would place other iconic Australian wildlife like bandicoots and wombats in other broods, perhaps Kangaroo, but existing broods might also make sense.

                                                            Interestingly, the Dingo should not be part of Bunyip's brood. It arrived quite late to Australia, and despite Rage Across Australia allowing dingoes to be lupus breedstock, it explicitly mentions the Bunyip deliberately choose not to breed with dingoes, even though the arrival of dingoes coincided with the extirpation the thylacine from all of Australia except the island of Tasmania (and perhaps some isolated spots in SE Australia). So that meant the Bunyip accepted the destruction of the vast majority of their lupus members thousands of years before the arrival of Europeans. There can be many reasons for this, but it's a notable conclusion. If Dingo belongs anywhere, it best goes to Father Wolf's brood. At least there it would explain why lupus were-dingoes can exist in your chronicle.
