                                      Sure, if there was a clear answer here, it wouldn't get debated so frequently.

                                      I think we both agree that the W20 designers wanted there to be more uncertainty around detection. That's obvious in plenty of the text, not just things like Scent of the True Form being changed. But they didn't remove detection completely, nor did they remove things that rely on a successful detection (or at least a presumed to be one).

                                      The idea that Test Vials are "the only way," flies in the face of the all of the Gift, Rite, Fetish, and Talen lists explicitly being examples/common powers, not exhaustive lists. If a Talen can do something, there's no reason presented in the books why there can't be a Fetish or Rite that can't do something similar enough. There's no reason why there can't be a Gift that can't.

                                      What we have, from the stuff that's in the books rather than conjecture about intent, is that the Garou have to actively check on an individual basis. You can't just have a Cliath Philodox walk down the street and automatically notice every pre-Change Garou, so their Sept can then rather easily gather up Lost Cubs. You have to have a reason to use a Test Vial, which is only good on one person before you have to make another, which makes it OK for the known children of Garou and kin deeply involved with them, but leaves lots of more peripheral kin and callow kin completely unable to access things like Test Vials to know if their kids are Garou or not.

                                      That's the big change that was made indisputably.

                                      But at long as detection is possible, then pre-Change Garou are not the same as kinfolk on a metaphysical level. In game characters might not be able to tell the difference until something happens, but out of character we can know things like two pre-Change teens having sex can result in a messy situation.

                                              What someone is if they're not automatically detected as a Garou or kin.

                                              The book doesn't specify. I would assume a failure would be recognized as such and a retry would be possible, but not rolling enough successes for the target true's supernatural nature would have the Garou conclude they're a normal human/animal/etc. One success on a mage would ping "human," and the PC would need cause to roll again. This remains useful if you consider that means they could at least tell a vampire in the form of a wolf isn't actually a normal wolf (even if they don't know what it really is) as "wolf" isn't their natural state.

                                              But the subtext of W20 Kinfolk,...
                                              I think it's important to judge either Kinfolk book by the fact that both say that they're written from the perspective of playable kinfolk, not necessarily from the perspective of how WtA works as a whole. That is, to kinfolk, any other kin could First Change at any moment because they have no way of knowing for themselves who's kin and who's a pre-Change since Scent of the True Form pings them as the same. There are far more kin than Garou, not everyone is going to get tested, not every test is going to work correctly, etc. There's always a chance someone they think of as kin is really a Garou on the verge of popping without any warning. That's there to tell you what it's like to live as a kin, not necessarily to comment on the metaphysics of the setting.

                                              Trying to be respectful of recent US news...

                                              The vast majority of students in the USA will never experience someone coming into their school and shooting people. For as horrific as the rate of it happening here is, it's still someone most people will never directly experience. However, the rate at which it happens, and the danger of it happening anywhere, has also left deep traumatic scars in multiple generations of younger US citizens. I'm too old, you're from a country without this (and maybe too old, I don't know how old you are and it's not really important to my point), so it's hard to put yourself in their shoes. But for younger US citizens they have never known a life without a childhood of constantly worrying that today is the day when their school gets shot up. That constant anxiety and stress has measurable psychological effects.

                                              Kinfolk have been dealing with that for thousands of years. Even if most kinfolk will never experience a person they thought was kin but was actually pre-Change, and the damage that can cause, they've lived their whole life constantly worrying that today might be the day they're the unlucky one(s) that get clawed and mauled before anyone can help. That's burned into the minds of kinfolk over generations of no way to avoid that some number of kinfolk are going to suffer like that.

                                              To me, that's what Unsung Heroes and A Breed Apart are really trying to get at. What it's like to be powerless and uncertain in the fact of potential sudden violent and brutal death. The subtext is about understanding that psychology, not about Test Vials and Scent of the True Form.

                                              I feel this whole affair is messy and was not discussed thoroughly before it was written into the book.

                                              Possibly, but my whole point was that we have books, we have what they say. We can figure out the most reasonable way to interpret them as they are without trying to speculate on the authors and their process. Once we acknowledge how things are, we can either accept them, or change them, as our individual games would benefit.

I mean the garou parent, if one is present, would surely boast about a true born child more than about a kinfolk baby, so the community usually knows.

                                                          You're ignoring the logistics of it.

                                                          The thing is, even among Kinfolk in-the-know, an actual Werewolf still has just a 10% chance of having a Garou child with a Kinfolk. IIRC Two Kinfolk together has something between 1% and 0.1% chance. And all things considered, you can expect that most children in a Kinfolk community are the child of two Kinfolk.

                                                          It means that in the best scenario the children of a perfectly Garou-friendly community will be Kinfolk 98.89% of the time, about eleven in each one thousand children are Garou. In the worst case a community should expect a Garou only once per thousand births.

                                                          Now lets reverse the perspective. Where do Talens come from? Fetish-making Kinfolk are rare, most Talens are coming from Garou, and from specialized Garou on top of that. They have a lot of other concerns, and can't dedicate themselves solely to produce this one Talen. While far easier to produce in bulk than Fetishes, Talens are still handmade by what amounts to some of the rarer and most specialized craftsmen in the world, that are not dedicated to make Test Vials exclusively, or even particularly.

                                                          Which proportion of Garou actually create Test Vials? How much time they dedicate to it, that is, how many Test Vials do they really create? How many Kinfolk children are out there to be tested? With this numbers alone we can expect at least 100 Kinfolk for each Garou, out there and having children. If you run the numbers for a time and account for the far lower life expectancy of Garou due to violence (most won't dedicate a lot of time to have children nor live much beyond 30 yo), probably the number of Kinfolk even in a in-the-know community is far greater than that.

                                                          Getting Test Vials for every single birth means producing large quantities of it and delivering to every community, then an extra to compensate for Vials lost or destroyed in the way, because you can't expect perfect deliveries all the time at such a scale. And then you just have the risk of the Vial failing for whatever reason, like not being administered correctly. What's the cost of it in terms not only of money, but other valuable resources and time and attention of Garou that need to also focus on other things? What when anything disrupts the supply chain? You ask Kinfolk to stop having babies?

                                                          All things considered, the most likely scenario is simply that most Kinfolk children won't ever be tested. They'll get the Vials they have and decide how to use it to maximize efficacy as they can, but with a resource that is essentially scarce for the task at hand.
