                                        RPGs are, in many ways, easier to solve than most media that hasn't aged well. RPGs are designed with customization and interactivity in-mind. I can decide what to call a group at my table far more easily than I can alter the coding of a video game, or redub a film, to try to not have these words diminish the fun of the rest of the game.

                                        But this falls into the same issue with all older media: acknowledge the problems as they are, self-reflect on your own personal boundaries, discuss the boundaries of those that will be taking part of the media in question with you if any, do not defend the problems as not being problems when talking about the media in public. Some people's personal boundaries mean they cannot enjoy WtA, and that's valid, other people can find ways to deal with the problematic stuff but still play, and that's also valid.

                                            I am not happy with the idea of just cutting the Garou-born out, and I'm not happy with completely cutting out everything that qualifies one's birth instead of just the mechanics that would justify the prejudice.

                                            Garou society also has its problems, and it was one of the best lines to explore the problems related to those themes. Things like Pure-Breed giving actual benefits was certainly a bad choice, but just eliminating the whole concept does reeks of sweeping problems under the rug.

                                            Now, if they make something to deal with such representations, great. It may come out better and maybe not come across as erasure. If they simply retcon the issues and the new stuff doesn't come out really good, the stench of erasure won't be so easy to ignore. If they do not even try to create new representation besides just making those character concepts "possible", but without any exploration, then I'll be seriously pissed.

                                            The old representation did got a lot of things right. Namely, the actual kind of prejudice we face. People are deemed as failures for being disabled, parents, specially mothers, are frequently judged for the disabilities of their children, disabled children, specially babies, are killed to this day even in the richest countries, sometimes right after the birth and reported as stillborn, and those that live are treated as liabilities and weight for most of society for the rest of our lives, with varying degrees of subtlety (or none at all). Heck, disabled people that commit suicide are frequently depicted by media as tragic heroes that did the right thing by taking their own lives instead of their desperation getting any serious examination.

                                            Werewolf did a very good job in showcasing how some common beliefs of our society are wrong just by having the Garou being blatant about them, and the Garou-born were a big part of this. I still don't feel confident about what we'll see in the end.

                                                            Part of the problem is the whole WtA lexicon doesn't make any sort of sense. Words are picked from many time periods and many areas of the world with minimal eye towards consistency, original usage, modern usage, or how well it reflects why the Garou would use a term.

                                                            While it isn't going to solve everything wrong with the game, there's a lot of low intensity work that can be done reevaluating the whole game's lexicon using a simple basis: The Garou all speak the Garou tongue, and that is the default "word" for any given concept. But since humans can't comprehend said language, the lexicon is a common translation for the given time and area assume in the text.
