        RITE OF PASSAGE

        More information on the Sept of the Green is found in the Rite of Passage adventure book.

        The most important thing we learn is that the Sept has an agreement with a Wendigo sept in the Northwest Territories of Canada (!) to exchange cubs for the rite of passage. In fact, it seems this Wendigo sept are the ones who actually enact the rite of the Rite of Passage, not anyone at the Sept of the Green. The Sept of the Green does the same for any Wendigo cubs sent to them. (Note the exact location of the sept is not clear by the adventure as it becomes quickly apparent the writer lacked some basic understanding of distance in Canada. Toronto is supposed to be fairly close by to the Wendigo sept, but the book specifically mentions that the Wendigo sept is in the Northwest Territories and a nearby village is in northern Saskatchewan, so not only is Ontario far away, the province of Manitoba must be inbetween them as well. The distance between the two septs must be almost 3000 miles.)

        This is fascinating as it makes no sense on the surface. Why would the very different tribes of Wendigo and Bone Gnawers have such an arrangement? I have to imagine this is a legacy of the original, extremely rare “Imperial Moot” in 1855 when the Silver Fangs created the caern and lead the sept and the Wendigo, being traditional and therefore wanting to keep custom, accepted that their “sister sept” was now run by Bone Gnawers after the 1930s. In truth, I don't think the writer gave this any thought. But I grew up reading Marvel Comics and work hard to get my No-Prize.

        One wonders if the reason for the only organized Sept moot in the winter happens (per the corebook) because that is the likely time for Wendigo cubs to appear for their rite of passage in New York City?

        We learn that Mother Larissa has a BG Ahroun bodyguard named Timmy. This is very smart of her as the sept she leads is a boiling rebellion about to happen.

        We also learn that the symbol of the Sept of the Green is a subway token. Do all septs have symbols? Why does this sept have one? This is an idea that initially seems cool, but then I wonder if there's anything that can be done with it?

        The beginning of the rite of passage (as a cultural event, not the mystic rite) is for Mother Larissa to show up where the cubs are stationed and insult them Fight Club style that they aren't worthy/ready of the Rite, and then have the other Garou of the sept to harass and harry them towards the Ramble so the moon bridge can take them to the Wendigo sept. If the cubs fall out of line or don't go, they get physically attacked – by Garou in crinos using the silver claws Gift. While not with the intent to kill, the Bone Gnawers seem fine if they do end up culling the unworthy since attacks with unsoakable aggravated damage can easily kill. This seems much more hardcore than I expected from this tribe. I would anticipate this from a Get of Fenris rite of passage, not a Bone Gnawer one. From my experience, violence against cubs in their rites of passage are far more played down.

        The entire nature of the rite of passage in the Sept of the Green seems a mix of the various “tribal” rites of passage explained earlier in the supplement. I suppose that again this is a legacy of the original Imperial Moot which brought many tribes together, and this was done by a tribal committee so that each tribe would feel the sept's rite of passage was sufficient to work for this tribe. If so, the harrying of the cubs with silver claws might indeed represent a concession to the Get of Fenris by the Silver Fangs which the Bone Gnawers are maintaining out of local tradition (not only are physical attacks a Get theme, but the Silver Claws gift is a tribal gift of the Silver Fangs meaning a large number of the tribe could participate in that, not just Ahrouns). I wonder what other Bone Gnawer septs think of this.

        We are also introduced into another NPC of the Sept of the Green. A very cool one in my opinion, but he marks the third Stargazer in New York (the other two are Antonine Teardrop in the Catskills and Howard Koar also in the city). He is Accolon “the Dragon of 86th Street”. He is a grizzled old man who lives in a penthouse on top of a very high skycraper in Manhattan. His penthouse spirit ward blocks the light pollution so that he can see the night sky for what it is. (This is a very cool image). Although an Ahroun, he has mystic visions and interprets them correctly with his Enigmas 5. He has been around forever, predicting the Silver Fangs would abandon the Central Park caern during the Great Depression. His advanced age saves him from being a “cooler than the PCs” kind of NPC, and it is possible he will die in the adventure so the PCs can live. He'd make an excellent Mentor if he survives.

        Although not needed for this story, I would have liked to known what the rite of passage for the Wendigo cubs would have been liked in NYC.

        This sourcebook contains a lot of important information, and the adventure itself isn't too bad and introduces some important information to the setting as a whole, but that's out of scope.

                There is also mention in the book of a Council of Peace which is held to prevent war between the Sept of the Green and the Wendigo sept. Its held in neutral ground outside Chicago (this might be a reference to the Shadow Lord controlled Sept of the Raccoon in the Lake Geneva Protectorate described in the Vampire/Werewolf adventure Under a Blood Red Moon). Interestingly, the Council (which I assume is technically a Concolation moot) is sponsored by the Children of Gaia (presumably from the Hand of Gaia sept) AND the Silver Fangs (presumably on behalf of King Morningkill). Here peace will be restored as the moot questions the PCs and learn what happened to them.

                Concolations are supposed to held on important matters. I don't think it makes sense for outsiders of both septs to quickly step in and make the peace between two relatively unimportant septs thousands of miles away. However, if the septs are regional, and one is lead by a Legendary Wendigo Ahroun, then it makes a lot more sense. This moot would be a good opportunity to include a lot of regional politics as the young PCs (still technically cubs) witness this.

                The inclusion of the Silver Fangs might also be slight support for the idea that the sept's exchanging cubs for their rites of passage was something established under their aegis. However, it was probably included just to demonstrate their leadership of the Garou Nation over all.

                Incidentally, it would also establish ties to the Chicago region and introduce some local Garou there that would allow an ST to run the Under a Blood Red Moon adventure at a later date.

                    VALKENBURG FOUNDATION

                    The last sourcebook is Valkenburg Foundation. Once again, I'll only comment on the things we learn about New York and avoid most of the adventure. However, with this and Rite of Passage, it emphasizes the idea that a werewolf game does not take place in one sept, but is wide ranging with PCs visiting many septs over a large geographical area.

                    One thing I did like about early WoD is that in both Vampire and Werewolf first edition, there was a concerted effort to build up one setting for the players early on. There is a snippet in the corebook itself, a fairly good setting sourcebook (excellent in Vampire's case, and reasonably good for Werewolf), and multiple adventures which make use of the setting and provide more information. The other WoD game lines did not have this support for their initial chronicles which is a shame.

                    Unlike with the Wendigo sept in Rite of Passage, the PCs would need to arrive at the Foundation's location in Wyoming by car so the sept obviously does not have any connections with nearby septs there that would allow a Moonbridge or perhaps because it is too far away.

                    One of the Foundation's patients was a Bone Gnawer Theurge named Graveseeker originally from New York City. In age, rank, and auspice, he is very close to Mother Larissa.

                    We learn New York City mostly has a Gauntlet of 8 (expected), but that Wall Street is 9 (equivalent to a scientific laboratory).

                    We're introduced to the Darkrunner pack of Cliaths which gets obliterated before the story begins. They were a pack interested in corporate espionage and employed hacking. There is a Shadowpad, a Child of Gaia Philodox, and Hacktalon, a Glass Walker metis ahroun. Deceased packmates include Whitemane and Scheckly. Shadowpad originally grew up in upstate New York, which I take as a reference to the Hand of Gaia sept.

                    It is mentioned the metis was raised in a Catholic orphanage. I can't imagine how such a thing was possible as metis don't look human, and humans wouldn't have the strength to raise a metis safely. However, early Werewolf sometimes allowed for strange things and it is possible Hacktalon shifted into homid form early on (Fengy, the Bone Gnawer metis in RANY, shifted into his lupus form soon after birth). This isn't something maintained in future sourcebooks, but it does give an ST another arrow for his quiver in order to explain a similar situation.

                    The main antagonist of this section is Harold Masters, a former Lunatic “cured” at the Valkenburg Foundation who returns to his home in New York City. A Wall Street trader, he is now a tribeless Garou – technically a lost cub of the Galliard auspice. He teams up with a street gang that would fit in the movie The Warriors, but not in real life – their gang “theme” is Freddy Krueger from the Nightmare on Elm Street movies. A better story is needed here – reskin the gang and change things so a middle aged Wall Street trader's ties to a street gang seems more plausible. Perhaps he had earlier ties with them from childhood friend or he used them in some schemes.

                    The rest of the adventure does not take place in New York, so I won't comment more. The information here is limited, not important, and can be easily ignored. But we do have a low rank NPC pack that could be included previously as background to the PC's activities in the city before the events of this adventure take place. It is good, in my opinion, to have Cliaths or other Garou that fail/die during the course of the game so that PCs can see that the war against the Wyrm has casualties.

                        So what do we have if we combine everything known for a chronicle?

                        In terms of caern/sept politics, the most important ones are the Get of Fenris controlled Sept of the Sentinel, the relatively open Hand of Gaia which is nevertheless run by the Black Furies and is rank 5 (but which most be available to the rural Garou of its protectorate, the caernless protectorate of the Catskills, and those who live in the North Country Protectorate but cannot access the caern there since they are not Get), and the Sept of the Green where Garou from the different tribes can meet up in order to negotiate on neutral ground. The rank 1 Niagara caern is simply not important enough. There is also the dynamic of the mad Silver Fang king in Vermont, but I imagine he is ignored by almost everyone except in honorifics and platitudes.

                        The Sept of the Green thus has regional importance because it is the one place people can go to be diplomats. (Although not stated, I imagine there is a Get of Fenris from the North Country who goes to Central Park to talk about various things – why aren't you controlling this skinhead pack, why not allow others access to your caern, etc. - with other tribes. These meetings must take place in New York because a Get of Fenris going to a Black Furies controlled sept to do this is bad negotiation tactics – you want a real neutral site). But the sept itself is a huge mess.

                        Mother Larissa is an ineffective leader at least by the standards outside her tribe. She's obviously good at holding onto power, but her way of running the sept angers the other tribes. There is an anti-Bone Gnawer conspiracy growing underneath her under the leadership of the Shadow Lord exile Zachary Ellison. It existed before he arrived, but he has become its leader. He has an ally in the Red Talon Spotlight who is Caern Warder. I imagine that the inciting incidence of the coup will be Spotlight stating the caern is in danger, and this is used as the pretext for the conspiracy to move against Mother Larissa.

                        The thing holding back the conspiracy at this point is the actual power that Mother Larissa and her allies have. She is an Elder Theurge, has an Ahroun bodyguard, and is assisted by several other competent Bone Gnawers. The conspiracy likely would prefer to not kill them, just remove them from power. They are probably looking for a way to avoid outright killing Mother Larissa if for no other reason than to keep the fighting contained. However, coups rarely succeed if you let the deposed leader live, and I can't see the conspirators not killing her. Likely they are waiting for a good pretext so it seems justifiable, or an incident where they can claim her death was done by others or by accident.

                        The Glass Walkers are a wildcard as they are divided while this is going on. Two factions with figureheads in Kleon Winston and Harold Hunt are fighting for control of the tribe. It would be easy to say that one of these would support Mother Larissa and the other Zachary Ellison, but I don't think that should be the case. Any change in leadership likely benefits them as a tribe although I could see Harold Hunt be more willing to kill than Kleon. Likely, the conspiracy is waiting for the Glass Walkers to settle their differences so they can move in full against the Bone Gnawers. The other tribes simply don't have the numbers by themselves. They need all those Glass Walkers to quell the numbers of Bone Gnawers. In this case, if Mother Larissa is not an idiot – and she's not – her best bet is to keep the two sides of this tribe divided helping first one, then the other (preferably by different proxies). In my opinion, this is what she should be doing which would make her very Machiavellian.

                        Complicating matters is the rise of the Man Eaters camp represented by the “leadership” of Bonejuice. This is something that could be quickly squashed by the PCs if they become aware of it and take action themselves. By doing so, they may anger a lot of the Bone Gnawers if it becomes known they killed Bonejuice before proper evidence is assembled. If the PCs do nothing, then the man-eaters will grow over time and eventually be supported by other Wyrm minions. Whenever this does become known, it's entirely possible this would be the pretext which will set off the coup attempt.

                        There is also a gang of Get of Fenris skinheads in the city just screwing stuff up. Not a long term threat, this is exactly the kind of obvious distraction which would allow the Man Eaters to grow unmolested at a critical time. Of course, acting directly against them might lead to trouble as the Get of Fenris in the North Country may not like these skinheads, but they are THEIR skinheads.

                        Other threats to the sept leadership is the potential, but not in process, interference by the Silver Fangs and Silent Striders. We can assume that the conspiracy against Mother Larissa is supported in part by these two tribes, but at a distance. Their tribes are not directly involved, but individuals of each tribe show up again and talk with the major conspirators to keep tabs on things. They might also be alternatively working with and against Zachary Ellison in order to manipulate the timing of the revolt so their tribes can send in packs and take control. I imagine preventing these other tribes showing up and snatching the leadership of the sept from him is one of the main complications Zachary faces.

                        Outside of Garou politics, there is a lot of intrigue involving the vampires of New York City. You have the non-aggression pact with the Sabbat which must be negotiated/maintained by Bone Gnawer Rat Finks and Nosferatu antitribu in the sewers, hidden safely away from the other tribes. You have a Shadow Lord Ragabash making deals with the Giovanni. Fianna making deals with Manhattan Toreador of the Camarilla in the city's art scene. And a Camarilla archon coterie trying to break the peace between the Sept of the Green and the Sabbat. Garou politics is heavily embedded in vampire politics – any change in that status quo could drag them into a fight. It also implies a very unsavory expediency of the Garou in New York City compared to Garou upstate in rural areas. Since the Garou involved are very high rank, the PCs better have lots of evidence besides their own words if they accuse any of these Garou.

                        In many ways, this is an absurdly complicated political backstory for PCs to be dropped into. None of this is exactly hidden in the way that Helena versus Menele conflict is in Vampire's Chicago By Night, but little of it would be obvious to PCs beginning a chronicle. And because it is multiple factions with different goals, it is much harder to figure out. It'll take PCs some work to become aware of the first onion layer, and a lot of work to uncover the other layers of the onion. Not only is there the conflict over Bone Gnawer control of the sept, there is the conflict over the Get of Fenris control of the North Country sept and BOTH conflicts impinge on each other. If the Shadow Lords gain control of the North Country, then the next obvious move would be to push the conspiracy in Central Park so Zachary Ellison can take control. If Ellison wins, he can change the dynamics of the diplomacy involving the North Country caern so that the Shadow Lords gain control of that.

                        Should the Shadow Lords move to seize control of both the Sept of the Sentinel and Sept of the Green and thereby destabilize the political situation, it is entirely possible that the three lupus ahroun warmongers in the Uktena, Wendigo, and Red Talons lead an attack on the other tribes. Taking control of the Sept of the Sentinel is one possible objective, but if the Hand of Gaia sept gets sucked in, that rank 5 caern would obviously be the most prime real estate for conquering. Since the potential lupus alliance on the fringe of the setting is likely to be the thing most PCs would be less interested about, any attack will probably come out of the blue. Therefore it is probably important the ST gives some kind of clue early on for PCs to follow up or at least be aware of the dissension among the Uktena, the legendary Wendigo in Canada, and the pack of eleven Red Talons in northern New York. Good use of the Hand of the Gaia sept is probably essential to give some kind of rumors so that clues of what is going on along the Canadian border reaches the PCs.

                        This implies that PCs, whatever the home sept, should make good use of the Anruth concept and travel a lot to the different septs. If they only stay at the Sept of the Green or even the Hand of Gaia, they will miss out on a lot of important regional politics. If the PCs don't care about politics and will simply react to whatever happens, the ST will need to have other NPCs mention some things in passing so that the PCs are at least aware of the coming crescendo of war.

                        In the Revised era, there is no way this would have allowed to exist, and Mother Larissa's leadership would have been white washed of all the interesting things happening at her sept which creates PLOT! The presentation of matters here, is much more interesting to me than the superheroics that happen in the Revised era when tensions between the tribes, and bad factions within each one, was toned down.

                        Now that I've read through everything, much of this is a surprise. The actual plot of RANY isn't defeating the Seventh Generation. It's preventing this massive Garou civil war in the region developing in the background.

                        The key to this, I think, is the Legendary Wendigo. If the PCs can persuade him to not attack, then they can thwart the lupus Ahroun alliance. As a Garou of Legendary rank, he should be known to the PCs and they need to work to convince him to keep the peace. If we follow the original plot of RANY, no doubt an important part of that is foiling a Seventh Generation operation in Canada, perhaps involving the evil Mountie in the Warrior Caste. In certain ways, it's amazing how all of this comes together.

                        Yet I have the feeling that the above has never happened even once in all the games played using the setting over several decades. Oh bother. It's likely unintentional in any case. But this is how I would do it.

                                The early depiction of the game was rife with inter-tribal, inter-sept, and inter-pack conflicts. Which is great because conflict creates PLOT for the PCs. When Revised put the entire setting on a railroad, that was heavily eliminated and the game focused entirely on outside antagonists. That's a shame because RANY shows there is actually a whole lot for the PCs to do.

                                Do they support Mother Larissa's rule, or do they support the conspiracy against her? In either case, what is their price or reward for that help?

                                Do they go after the obvious, but unimportant, menace of the Fenrir skinhead gang? Or do they notice the rise of the Man Eaters in the city?

                                Do they disrupt the peace with the vampires in New York City, or do they join the collaborators?

                                Do they become involved in diplomacy to defeat the malcontent lupus ahrouns? Or do they ignore it?

                                How do they handle the intrigue of the Shadow Lords to takeover two of the caerns?

                                Do they help either side of the divided Glass Walkers?

                                In each of these cases, the actions of the PC can heavily impact the chronicle. One failing of RANY is that while these plots is obvious in hindsight, most were not upon a first reading. One must really read the text critically to notice this and go, "Hey, the actions of the PCs are important here." If this was spelled more clearly in the story hooks portion as "which side do they take", it would give more direction to the ST.

                                Also one issue is that sometimes the sourcebook takes obvious sides here - you're supposed to root for Kleon Winston instead of Harold Hunt. I think it would be better if the book did not take sides so that by helping Harold, it's not so obvious you'd be aiding the Wyrm. I think a good tactic by the ST would be that in the beginning, the PCs be approached by Harold (or have them come upon some other plot) and be given the option of helping him first and thus becoming allied with his faction inadvertently, and only afterwards present Kleon's side. Do the PCs switch at that point, or have they already built up too much of a relationship with Harold to go against that?

                                Likewise for the issue of Mother Larissa's negligence (per how most Garou tribes handle it) of her sept duties and moots. She is clearly the more sympathetic character and already in charge. But most PCs, as long as they are not Bone Gnawers, SHOULD be against her. The ST really needs to build up some righteous indignation among the other Garou, especially those outside the Sept of the Green, to alert the PCs that she's failing at some important responsibilities.

                                    APOCRYPHA: THE LUPINES OF DARK COLONY

                                    We get a description of the Garou of New England in the Vampire sourcebook Dark Colony also published in 1993 when the above Werewolf sourcebooks were published. It gives us some details on the Lupines there despite the emphasis naturally being about vampires. Since at this point, the various game line developers were not having pissing contests about interpretation of the setting, but embracing crossover, we can take this as canon for Werewolf.

                                    In Vermont, the Garou fight Sabbat packs. Garou are concentrated in Vermont's Northwest Kingdom and extend southwest towards the Taconic Mountains. This creates, in essence, a protectorate around the Green Mountains which the book specifically mentions is controlled by the Silver Fangs. On that basis, it could make good sense to just say that the Morningkill royal family is based in the Green Mountain Protectorate rather than being in the North Country. Or we could believe there is a much smaller second protectorate which the Silver Fangs control in addition to their domain in the North County.

                                    New Hampshire has fierce packs of Native American Lupines in the north. That must mean the Wendigo control a caern in the White Mountains which straddle northern New Hampshire and Maine.

                                    Massachusetts has Garou in “rural” Massachusetts, particularly around the Quabbin Reservoir in central Massachusetts and Webster Lake on the border of Connecticut. We're told that that the Get of Fenris and Shadow Lords were among the European Garou who fought the Uktena and Wendigo. Webster Lake is sacred to the Lupines as it has a portal to the world of the Fey. This isn't a wilderness area. So let's assign a low ranking caern here. Get of Fenris are probably Anglo-Saxon Garou from England. Instead of Shadow Lords, it makes more sense for British Fianna to be here, especially since fey are involved. A major trod must lie here that leads to a freehold in the Near Dreaming.

                                    Last we have Maine. In the south, we have Wendigo along the Mahosucs Mountains, a part of the White Mountains already mentioned. That leaves the North Maine Woods as a protectorate for the rest of the state. Maine is known to be a hotbed of Sabbat, so the wilderness is actually a battleground between nomad Sabbat and the Garou. Tribes are not mentioned, but I like to believe it is a mix of various warlike tribes forced to work together because of the prevalence of Sabbat. A sept of uneasy Wendigo, Get of Fenris, Fianna, and Shadow Lords would be a good mix. They present a major obstacle between the Sabbat in Montreal and Maine.

                                    This expands the setting of New York, and I think these four septs and protectorates are easily added to the ones in New York state. For STs who want more Garou, this is a place to easily put more while not overpopulating New York proper.

                                        Rage Across the Eastern Seaboard

                                        At this point, we've covered the protectorates and septs of New England and New York. We also have some Appalachian protectorates in the Upper South in another published sourcebook. There's only some mid-Atlantic states where this is a gap between the two.

                                        The next step would be to detail some protectorates and septs south in Pennsylvania and New Jersey. If we did that, we'd be able to link up to those described in Rage Across Appalachia, but alas there are no such canonical supplements. But it would be easy to create one Protectorate in the Allegheny National Forest in northeastern Pennsylvania, another in the Valley & Ridge Mountains, and a third in the Pine Barrens of New Jersey. The idea of Pennsylvania Dutch Get of Fenris and their kinfolk hiding out as Amish settlements while Children of Gaia descended from Quaker settlers oppose them is pretty cool. We'd just need to decide whether it makes sense to include an additional protectorate in the Tidewater of Virginia.

                                        Rage Across Appalachia covers five protectorates/septs in Tenneesee, North Carolina, West Virginia and Kentucky. Tribebook: Bone Gnawers Revised mentions the Sept of the Moon in Kentucky and Sept of Dandelion Hill in “southern Appalachia”. (In canon, the discovery of the man-eaters camp in the Sept of Dandelion Hill is the pretext used to eliminate the man-eaters camp during the Revised era's transition from savage horror to Epic Fantasy. Similar “evil” camps in other tribes were likewise eliminated during this time. For our purposes here, this corrupted sept serves as potential allies for Bonejuice if he can live that long.) So we have a good southern anchor.

                                        Combined with the Sept of the Awakening in Washington, DC as described in Caerns: Places of Power, an ST would now have almost the entire American Eastern Seaboard to the Appalachian Mountains covering from the Mid South to Canada. That would give us around twenty protectorates for an extended supercampaign built mainly from published sources.

                                        There would be a lot of extra work by the ST to run such a large campaign, but at the very least it would present the PCs in New York with an expanded background. If one wanted to build up the Rank 5 caern at the Hand of Gaia, or the importance of the Sept of the Green as a neutral meeting ground for diplomacy between septs, there would be more to pull from now. It would also allow a PC pack (or even NPC packs run by the ST) to wander sept to sept looking for work to do and renown to earn. For those STs who like lots of NPCs and locales, a wider geographical range helps while keeping local populations manageable.

                                        These additional septs don't need to be described in much detail as the focus will still be on New York state, but now there's an easy way to let the PCs wander at the edges without the concern that there isn't material out for them.
