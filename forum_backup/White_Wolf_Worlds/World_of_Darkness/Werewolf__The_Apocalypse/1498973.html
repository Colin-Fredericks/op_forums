    W20: Pulse of the Prey

    12-06-2022, 02:07 PM
    Hi guys!

    I am running a campaign in which a player has Pulse of the Prey (Fostern Ragabash Gnawer) and they are using it to track certain targets throughout the game. I was curious, how do you guys tend to utilize this gift? Below is the description of the gift. When it says, if he knows 'anything' about the prey, how do you tend to portray that? Cinematically, I've seen this as a primal gift, in which you can follow the 'pulse' of the target. Their heartbeat, etc.

    I'm sure that you can track a target if you know their name. But what about a common description?

    "The guy is about 5'10, black hair, weighs 150, athletic build, speaks with a Boston accent." Does that count as 'anything'?

    What if the target is like a Gangrel and can shape shift? If the Garou saw only the vampire in it's wolf form, are they able to track it that way? Can they track Vampires who are slumbering in hard to find places such as under the Earth in a coffin?

    I would just like to know how some of you super experienced guys have used it in the past and what limits you may put on it?

    If he knows anything about his prey, the character can track it down as rapidly as he can travel. This unerring sense of direction operates anywhere, and it is useful for tracking spirits through the Umbra as well as finding beings on Earth. A wolf- or dog-spirit teaches this Gift.

    System: No roll is required unless the target is hiding actively (intent alone is not enough), in which case a Perception + Enigmas roll is made against a difficulty of the target’s Wits + Stealth. If the target is a spirit, the difficulty is the spirit’s Gnosis.

      It's a powerful gift and should be useful, but it is not all powerful. So it does require some bit of handling by the ST to adjudicate.

      The original versions of the Gift called it "Sense of the Prey", but W20 changed it to "Pulse of the Prey." For some reason you are quoting its earlier description, as W20 adds "even a nickname, initials, or crude description." So if having a person's initials allow you to track that specific individual, and ignore everyone else who has the same initials, the problem is not in zeroing in on that person. Eventually the Garou will find that quarry.

      But bear in mind it still requires some knowledge of the person himself. As an ST, I would not allow someone to use "I want to find the person who killed my friend Bob" because that is not a description of a person. If he detected an unknown scent at the crime scene, and wanted to find that unknown person (who may or may not have actually been the murderer), I would allow that because a scent is still something. The Gift should not impart any new knowledge to the Garou about that person. You shouldn't be able to use the gift to solve a murder mystery, or learn anything additional about that person. I also wouldn't allow things that don't actually describe the person like the type of clothes they wear because anybody could wear such a thing. You need something specific like a crude description, something intrinsic about that person that doesn't change from day to day or minute by minute. Names, nicknames, and initials still work not because it makes sense physically, but because magically a person's name is part of them.

      But what about something they left behind? Does leaving a generic balllpoint pen behind allow you to "identify" the person and thus track them? I would say "no". But I would be open for the Gift to work if that artifact was somehow unique or otherwise identified that person. An expensive custom-made pen he received for his birthday? A monogrammed handkerchief? I'd probably allow that provided I could justify it as "Yeah, people could know the guy if you showed them that thing." A fancy pen a businessman carries could do that in a way a standard Bic pen would not. Likewise it tracks "people" (humans, animals, spirits, vampires, fomori, etc.), not objects. Knowing the license plate of a car doesn't allow you to track the car. (It could allow you to track the owner, but not necessarily a passenger or the person actually driving the vehicle if it was not the owner.) I might change my mind on these things, but this is how I feel now.

      = = =

      The real issue is how long it should take the Garou to find that person, and how the ST should describe the hunt? We know the person does not need to take any time to follow the "trail". The person can walk, jog, run, or even drive a car and still be able to follow it. The Garou will inevitably find his prey, but he determines how quickly he will go about finding him.

      But it should still be a "trail". It is not a straight line. The Garou should not know the actual location of his prey or the distance from him. He won't know that until he gets there. He just begins following the trail. I would also rule that the the Gift does not work like the quarry is putting out a signal beam that the Garou is locked on, and he zeroes in on the prey automatically. That is boring and a bit too powerful in my opinion for a second level Gift.

      Instead I would treat it more like there was an actual trail to follow. What would be the nearest place to the Garou that the person was once at? The Garou goes there first and then follows the "trail" from there. If the person has gone back and forth from that place, the Garou can pick up the trail that is more recent, go there, and then follow that trail. And so on as he inevitably gets closer and finally finds "C.C." or "Fat Moe" or "The One Armed Man" or whatever he has to go by. The player might be surprised to learn the "C.C." may not be the person he thought him to be,

      As a storytelling device I like that more than someone going on a straight line. 1) It's more flavorful. It seems more like following a trail. 2) The ST can use it to provide more information, details, and clues about the person if he wants. 3) It can build suspense if used properly. 4) It allows for things to change like the prey changing direction or moving. If the target is at the airport and then takes off while the Garou is on the hunt, the Garou might not know that was done until he arrives at the airport and suddenly realizes the trail has changed (NOT gone cold). If for some reason the target has begun to actively hide (for whatever reason), only then could the trail suddenly go cold. 5) While the tracking is going on, the ST has more opportunity to throw obstacles, events, or encounters along the way. If the trail to find Fat More means the PC runs into a street gang who will harass them, that might make things interesting.

      In other words, it provides more tools for the ST to craft the story while still giving the PC what he wants.

      What is important is that 1) the Garou should always be getting closer to his prey. He should not be going on a wild goose chase or feel like he is being delayed from finding his prey. The player should always feel like he is getting closer, not farther. 2) Don't waste the players time. Don't prolong the tracking just to do it. Do it because it adds to the game somehow. 3) Keep in mind different prey/scenarios should mean they are handled differently. Some times you just want the PC to find that person so the game can go on. Other times you want to build up the encounter. Understand what your players want and will be open for.

      Those are my initial thoughts. But they are not set in stone.

                      The thing is... the Garou aren't the most dangerous creatures in the World of Darkness; except maybe to themselves. That's part of the core dramatic tension of the game. The Garou are very good at what they do, but what they do isn't actually all that effective. The best thing to do with the Antedeluvians? Keep them asleep and hidden. The Garou weren't even responsible for the Week of Nightmares, and it cost them hundreds of warriors, and for what? One (probably) dead ancient vampire demigod with enough collateral damage to feed the Wyrm's forces to grow far stronger than they were with [Ravnos] safely sleeping away eternity. All those nukes, all the magical insanity, all the death, all the environmental destruction... there's no way that helped the Gaian cause in the end. While lower stakes, the same thing happened with Mithras. A whole Sept lost to weaken one vampire enough so another vampire could kill him (and the Garou do not know about how ancient vampires can end up possessing those that eat them, since the vast majority of vampires don't know that's really a thing).

                      You can find and kill Pentex executives? So what? For every one of them, there's a dozen that were trying to kill their boss and take their place anyway. Pentex will keep going on as it has. Corporations like it don't just halt everything their doing because of a staffing change.

                      The Garou are in a war where they need to stop trying to win everything by killing it harder than last time. The enemy has learned to mass produce disposable bodies for the Garou to waste their efforts on. Finding and killing those disposable pawns over and over is how the Garou are nullified, not how they're actually dangerous.

                              Well, the specific fight between Mithras and the London Sept was largely an accidental encounter. Mithras had been in torpor due to events during WWII, and the Garou weren't trying to wake him up when they came across where he'd been buried by German bombs. So the Garou didn't really have time to prep for a fight with a 3250 year old 4th Gen Ventrue with Dominate 9 at his disposal on top of impressive (if not as maxed out as possible) combat stats that meant he could easily take down low and mid-range Garou in single hits, and throw a few of those a turn. He even had Quietus (he and Haqim used to be friends in ancient times) which meant he could use his plentiful 4th Gen blood even more effectively in combat.

                              Mithras's main sources of Herds was starting cults to himself as a war deity, and he was embraced specifically for his tactical and combat skills which were exceptional even before his Embrace.

                              We also have no details on the Garou he got into a fight with. They had numbers, but we don't know what Tribes, how many higher Rank ones there were, what spirits were involved, etc. Lots of urban Septs aren't really focused on the raw combat heavy numbers needed to take on really old and powerful vampires, and aren't the most powerful Septs around since it's hard to maintain them.
