    Taking Out-of-Tribe Gifts

    08-19-2016, 07:35 PM
    A common house rule (I'm assuming it's an house rule?) I've seen in games I've played is a loss of temporary renown for taking gifts that are outside your tribe, breed, or auspice. For those of you who use this rule, what's your reasoning behind it?
    '
    I can see the logic in some NPCs looking down on those who step outside their 'roles' (say, an Ahroun learning Command Spirit), but why would it be discouraged to learn something like Mother's Touch, which could mean the difference between life and death in a fight?

      It's kind of on the border of "house rule," and "loose application" of the rules. The expanded Revised/W20 Renown chart has a Renown penalty for Auspice inappropriate behavior, and a few things that are similar for Breed and Tribe (though not quite so broad).

      I personally never use it, because if a player is after a Gift, it rarely is a Gift that doesn't make sense. If the pack is follow a Totem of a specific type, it makes sense to seek out Gifts that support the Totem's focus. If you're in a war pack, picking up a few out-of-splat Gifts that focus on fighting is hard to view as inappropriate. Sept is also something that can impact this. If a specific Tribe/small selection of Tribes dominate the Sept, being the odd one out without certain Gifts can be seen as a problem vis-a-vis lack of conformity, as well the Sept as a Caern Totem that sets goals and tone.

      Sometimes there's just plain old out-of-character things. There's a Revised Bone Gnawer Gift that I really like, but it's a "meta-Gift" that only works by expanding the function of three specific other Gifts; which aren't Gnawer specific Gifts. So letting people buy up the Gifts boosted even if out-of-splat is a bit necessary if you're going to let them have that Gift in the first place.

      If I want to push back, I focus on the process of getting the Gift. Have either the elders summoning spirits to instruct, or the spirits themselves, demand higher chiminage and justification for Gifts that are out-of-splat.

                                              In terms of Garou society, the question is are they properly fulfilling their auspice role and acting like home a member of the tribe should? If so, there is not social pressure in regard to out of scope Gifts. If however, the Ahroun doesn't seem to want to be an Ahroun and wants to act more like a Philodox by getting Philodox gifts and neglecting his Ahroun role, then there could be social pressure involved. But ultimately that is about not acting like they should for their auspice, not that they are learning out of auspice Gifts by themselves.

                                              The real issue with out of tribe, auspice, or breed gifts is everything that is needed to learn them. You need to find spirits willing to teach you, then convince them to do so (the character is worthy of it based on their past roleplay), then pay a lot more chiminage (and XP of course). It is much more of a hassle than the ones available to you naturally. Or at least should be.

                                              However, based on their pack totem, I do make certain Gifts more available to them as an ST. If they have Stag as their pack totem, then some of the Fianna gifts come into play (especially those not unique to the Fianna but found in other lists), and their pack totem can assist them with a lot of the Roleplay work although chiminage and XP costs ares till higher. For those totems that aren't Incarna who have tribes, it'll mean some extra work (for example if the PCs have Boar as their pack totem), but I can cobble together a list of Gifts that would make sense. Boar would have Stamina and Fighting feats, plus things related to eating and Grondr role inspired gifts. Raven would have a lot of Ragabash style gifts, and I'd look at some Silver Fang (Falcon) and Silent Strider (Owl) Gifts to see if anything fits generically with birds and flight.

                                              If the Players do not have one of each auspice, I am sympathetic to them trying to cover major holes in the Pack's abilities (if there is no Theurge, then someone probably is going to learn Spirit Speech). In character, the NPCs likely tell them just to recruit a new member from somewhere else, but the real issue is that there is not a player available so that this NPC grumbling isn't going to be anything more than flavor. Still, their major emphasis should still be their actual auspice, not any "back up auspice" role they are trying to fill.

                                              Out of breed gifts are probably the most difficult to learn, at least those not found on other lists. The key here, I think, is Ancestors. A Red Talon lupus is going to find it very heard to learn Master of Fire (and this would probably be one of the few times that roleplaying NPCs could involve real impact on relationships or renown - I imagine homid breed Gifts would be taboo for the Red Talons, but less so for other tribes) simply because there are no homid Ancestors for them. Silent Striders, Glass Walkers and Bone Gnawers will have problems since Ancestors are restricted. However, given people's ancestral lineages can go back thousands and thousands of years, most Garou can probably find an ancestor of either homid or lupus breed somewhere.
