    I'm probably the absolute worst person in the world to do a review of W5 because everyone knows that my W:TA experience is heavily V:TM inspired. It leaks over between my fandom and my takes aren't what most people think of when they talk about the game. I emphasize the personal horror element much more that was still a thing when I bought my first Werewolf supplement that emphasized creepy houses and werewolves investigating evil toys. It also strongly played up the Garou Nation being divided and probably unable to successfully complete their mission due to the sins of the past and old grudges.



    When I heard about the premise of W5, I should share that I DIDNT HATE IT. I actually think ending the Apocalypse was a possibly bold and interesting direction to take the line. So much of the game is basically, "The End of the World is Nigh! Get those Banes!" that the idea of what Garou would have to do when the war was over was an intriguing possibility. I admit the Forsaken influence was expected but I was hoping they'd go with the idea of existentialism.

    "The Apocalypse is over and now we have to figure out what to do with our lives."

    Instead, the game just feels CONFUSED about what it's trying to achieve. I understand the changing terminology (Metis, Wendigo, and so on), I understand the getting rid of Lupus, and I understand trying to get rid of the racial superiority angle. W:TA has always had a problem with Nazi fans and this was told to me by Ethan Skemp way back when. It's why they purged the Swords and got rid of Pure Breed as a concept. Even though the joke was that the most Pure Bred werewolves were the most inbred and insane.

    But it all feels like half-measures and the thing still goes, "It's no longer the way it used to be....except we're going to allude to being EXACTLY the same way it was."

    Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

        It was already pretty easy to imagine from the history of V5’s development that a significant contingent of the legacy fan base was going to take umbrage with what W5 put out if it was designed along similar principles.

        That said, I do think I would have preferred, if one was going to put out a W5 product with similar design principles as V5 or H5, I would have liked to see the designers fully commit to a redesign of the setting to fit their principles.

        I’m not saying that I would have preferred that product over legacy or that there wouldn’t still be controversy and debate, but at least in that case there would be a way to contrast the old and new materials against one another and see what one prefers. There are plenty of problems with the old material, problems that a lot of people acknowledge. Even just seeing ways that one might go about resolving the old material’s issues would have been interesting and in some cases might have seen certain ideas back-ported into legacy. For instance I know that if this hypothetical W5 had fully committed to a redesign of the setting and had included elements such as the Sambrano rework of Older Brother, Middle Brother, & Younger Brother; I probably would have back ported those things into my legacy settings, even if nothing else, and I don’t think I would have been the only one either.
        I think it's probably fair to say that the vast majority of WtA players and storytellers had already fixed most if not all of the issues in their own games, almost every ST I know online and in RL has, for example, been using Warborn or Crinosborn for literally decades and Younger Brothers Patron has been swapped out for Angklut or Glooskap or, more often, Amaroq.

        I think its also often forgotten that a whole bunch of other relatively minor issues were fixed in Revised but then those fixes were rolled back in 20th or others introduced in an effort to make it Metaplot agnostic, I think this was a major mistake as it punted a lot of lore back to early 2nd Ed which was pretty rough even at the time.....glares at Thrall of the Wyrm and resurrecting the SoH.

                                                        PHASE THREE PART FIVE

                                                        Character Generation Part Two

                                                        One Background that has returned is Totem, now renamed Spirit Pact. So far, there is not much that goes into this BG. Since there are no rules for communal traits, all such Pacts are with one Garou, not an entire pack, thus limiting the strength of this BG. Basically, this allows a Garou to have spirit around to help out. It doesn't provide any Boons, like those of the Tribe Patrons, but they don't have Bans either, unless one purchases a Flaw to do so. The Fetish BG has also returned, now renamed Talisman, which was already a Mage term for something slightly different that isn't harboring a spirit. No example was provided of how to rank Talismans, although a later section offers suggestions on how to do so. Seriously, there should have been more text given to this. How one ranks a BG if there aren't any examples nearby?

                                                        Finally, Renown is still around, but in an altered form. Instead of being tied to Auspice and Rank, each type of Renown is tied to a Tribe. Rank "technically" no longer exists, even though the game is built around the disparity between elders and the current generations. How does one define these things if Rank is no longer a thing? While there are no longer lengthy charts detailing how almost every type of act can affect Renown, there is no replacement to determine it. It is just guess-work, and circular logic could lead one down multiple routes in determine what new Renown will be. Now, Renown is directly tied to Gifts, without the interference of Rank in the way. I'll explain this in more detail when I get to Gifts, but the process is a lot more complicated than what it used to be. (The Rules chapter also adds that Renown can be used to determine social rankings, but doesn't provide full context on how and why.)

                                                        The remaining points of Character Generation in W5 are unique to the new edition, and they will not be covered here. Check earlier Phases of this Review for information.

                                                        Rules and Systems

                                                        Most of the rules for rolls are close enough to the old rules , save for the lack of numbers on dice. I need to mention that there is a homage to the classic Jonas vs. Mari fight from the older editions sprawled across the first part of the chapter on rules. Nothing is provided about the original influence, which is the fair thing to do when an artist is doing an homage. It also doesn't help that one of the pieces of art appears to be out of order, as well as some images may have been 'flipped' from their original orientation.

                                                        Some portions have been cut out, with many others included in a supplement that I mentioned previously that had an incorrect web address. There are no longer specific rules for dealing with certain types of damage, such as poisons, drugs, or more exotic sources such as radiation. As biological beings, such sources have repercussions, and they needed to be mentioned.

                                                        The main type of roll is the Rage roll. All other types of rolls from previous editions have pretty much been condensed into this type, save those dealing with WP. Depending upon how much Rage a Garou has, that many dice replace regular dice in a roll. This can lead to what is known as 'Brutal' results upon failure. Garou also use Rage to regenerate, shapeshift, or to use Gifts. While using just one power source for multiple things makes the game easier, the economy does not. Even if not spent, Rage can be lost so fast, that the game is almost rigged to suggest that playing as a normal human might be the best option.

                                                        The five forms of a Garou are still here, but with major changes. Homid and Lupus can handle silver without damage, but they cannot regenerate. Only the three 'supernatural' forms can do that. The Glabro is still just slightly bigger, but most people are still afraid of it. Crinos is still big, but one can only maintain this form for a short length of time, unless they try to kill something. Upon leaving this form, Rage drops to 1, or 0 if it ends in a frenzy. Hispo is misdescribed as a really, really, big dog, instead of what the form used to be, but then Lupus is presumed to dog-sized, neither of which is entirely accurate.

                                                        The final rules are mostly about the new Harano and Hauglosk mechanics. While they are extremes, one can suffer from both at the same time. To be honest, I really can't see how to incorporate both into the same setting in a single character. Maybe one or the other, but not both. In fact, Hauglosk almost seems to be beneficial in certain regards. Delirium is also brought up, but without the fine delegation of effects that is used to have, even though Willpower is a more direct track of comparison than it used to be. What is't included are other systems for things such as Aging or Battle Scars (which doesn't seem to be a problem with regenerating any more). Since this is a new game, I guess they didn't think anyone needed such time-progressive systems. Or maybe the developers didn't think anyone would stay with the game long enough for this to become important.
