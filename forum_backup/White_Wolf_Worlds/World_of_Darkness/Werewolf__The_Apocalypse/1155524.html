    What is sept life like?

    11-03-2017, 05:02 PM
    I've read up on septs in a few online sources (I have none of the books), but I'm not sure what life in a sept is like or even how a sept is structured. What I especially want to know though is how a sept goes about teaching new cubs and what a metis Garou would be doing all their life in a sept (aside from being treated like trash, of course) prior to their First Change. If I could get some answers regarding the above, that would be great!

          The sept is the basic political structure of the Garou. The sept is a social unit dedicated to the use and defense of caerns, the holy sites of the Garou. Most politics in the Garou Nation is actually politics between septs. Think of each sept as its own city state, although everyone is part of the same cultural Garou Nation. I always use the analogy of the ancient Greek city states especially in times of the Iliad. They have a common Greek identity against outsiders, but otherwise the various city states were rivals to each other although there are alliances and associations between cities as well.

          A sept's political structure has been outlined in multiple sourcebooks. A sept is ruled by a council of elders, the oldest and wisest of the Garou of the sept. They may not be Elder in Rank, but they are probably Elders and Athros. Some Adren might be included as well if a sept lacks enough Garou of high rank, but this would only happen at less powerful caerns. The size of the council varies depending on the rank of the caern. A rank one caern may only have 1-2 Garou who qualify while a Rank 5 caern might have a dozen or more members. One of those members is the Sept Elder. He is the spokesman of the council. The council of elders generally do not go out and adventure, but spend their entire time at the sept and in the umbra dealing with sept issues.

          There are also specific positions that need to be filled at a sept. Some of these will be filled by members of the council of elders, but others are not. The Gatekeeper is the sept's main diplomat who negotiates with other septs and therefore controls the caern's moon bridges and keeps the pathstone. Think of him as the Foreign Minister. The Warder is responsible for the sept's defenses and leads the Guardians, who are Garou who assist him in that defense (many Guardians are rotated packs or other Garou). Think of him as the Defense Minister. The Master of Challenge oversees all challenges that happen at the sept, whether for rank or to settle disputes. He is there to insure fairness and keep the peace so he is like the Minister of Justice. The Master of Rite oversees all rites done at the caern. Anyone wishing to perform any kind of rite needs permission of the Master of Rite. Think of him as the sept's Archbishop or other chief religious functionary. Finally there is the Keeper of the Land who is responsible for keeping the spirits around the caern happy. These people are the government of the sept so to speak, but they answer to the Council of Elders.

          Then there are the so-called "lesser positions", but I prefer to think of them as the Moot Positions. While the other positions manage the caern, the Moot positions obviously oversee the Moot. The Moot is important, because it is here that the entire Garou of the sept assemble to discuss business. It is Sunday church attendance plus New England town meeting all in one. Moots happen once a month, and all official activity of the sept actually takes place here. Individual Garou ask for their achievements to be recognized in terms of Renown, packs share stories and news with the sept, grievances are handles, individuals are praised, and any trials and justice take place. Many of the more important Moot Positions might be held by those who have sept positions, but many of the lesser moot positions are given to younger Garou in order to give them experience, tie them closer to the sept's leadership, and so the elders can evaluate how they handle responsibility.

          Many games have a position called Den Father, but I can't find it mentioned in any of the sourcebooks. The Den Father would be responsible for overseeing the education of the cubs and looking after any pre-Change metis in lieu of their parents or others doing so. I imagine there is a several year period after one's First Change where the Den Father, assisted by other Garou of the sept, educates new cubs into Garou society and their roles in it. Of course, Metis would start with a much earlier education (as would some homids who come from knowing kinfolk who imparted some basics of the Garou). How they're taught isn't described, but I imagine each sept has its own "syllabus" established by tradition where they go about teaching each cub roughly the same skills and knowledge at roughly the same time after their arrival. This is adjusted as needed. I also assume that younger cubs have longer preparation times while older cubs undergo an accelerated schedule.

          Now the specific details of how this works out depends on the sept's membership, especially what tribe or tribes make up the sept. A monotribal sept would handle things very differently if it were Get of Fenris or Children of Gaia, and multi-tribal septs would need to make various compromises.

          Outside of the ordinary sept structure, a sept also deals with visitors. There are packs of Anruth, wandering Garou who visit sept to sept in order to perform tasks so they can gain renown and get further experience of Garou society. I imagine they are treated much like legal aliens - they are allowed to be at the caern, they do tasks for the council of elders, and ask for renown. But they aren't actual members of the sept. They'll stay there for a certain amount of time, but will be leaving at some point. Then there are other Garou who are not members of the sept, but who show up every now and again and pay chiminage to the sept to access the caern, have their renown recognized, etc. They're like tourists who show up and each time have to get a new visa.
