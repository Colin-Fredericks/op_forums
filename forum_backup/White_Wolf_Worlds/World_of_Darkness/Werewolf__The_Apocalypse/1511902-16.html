    Hakken Gifts

    If you're a Japanese Shadow Lord you add these to your list. Again, there is no cost to doing this, beyond the buy-in of your character being from Japan and people perhaps mistrusting them as a foreigner to the Garou Nation.

    Dream of a Thousand Cranes (1): You have to fold an origami crane to activate this Gift of good fortune, which takes a turn. When you do so, spend a Gnosis and roll Charisma + Occult, difficulty 6. Every success you get translates to a single die which you can add to other checks in the same scene.

    Immediately this makes you a far better ritualist, crafter, and in fact anything where you roll for a project. In a PBP game, or a game with Downtime, this Gift would be a nightmare to balance. Yeah there's that one Philodox from the Hakken whose katana Fetishes are incredible, yeah his planning and crafting and Ritual rolls to make the sword and turn it into a Fetish get loads of bonus dice from folding cranes-

    This Gift is great, but I wouldn't suggest using it in your games.
    The Hakken explicitly never bind spirits into katana (or wakizashi, but those don't have quite the same... reputation of use in gaming circles). It's considered a violation of the principle of benevolence, as the ordinary mortal steel is used to dispatch mortal beings and mockeries as a kind of kindness and checking against excessive force, since Garou claws are substantially more likely to wreck someone than a katana.

    Of course, this social principle got really weird when it practically became the Beast Court's stance on all fetish weapons in Hammer & Klaive. Which is rather awkward, because there were a number of fetish weapons in Hengeyokai.

    Fair Path (1)*: This Gift lets you wash yourself from battle by touching clean water, and on a successful activation with no cost you add +1 to your Appearance. You can think of this as a free (3xp) way to boost your Appearance by 1, with an activation. If your Appearance is 4, this is much cheaper than buying Appearance 5. Is it worth it? Well does Appearance matter in your games?

    If you have a Gift or Ritual where you roll Appearance, or you're dealing with Garou/Supernaturals/Spirits where beauty and fashion are important... who knows. Let's call this Amber with a *, assuming that Appearance is at least somewhat useful (which is the RAW!) and you care enough about Appearance to pay 3xp to boost it.
    I think this may be less about the Appearance boost and more about not appearing in disarray around spirits that wouldn't like that. It also potentially gives you an alibi if you aren't spattered in evidence.

    RE: Fu Xi's Honor, Rage of the Nest Mother for Mokole actually uses the same system. So, hurray for the Warding in taking the importance of Honor international, I suppose?

    Honor as a Garou and Fera Renown category also has a few connotations that humans would find rather seedy. For example, while you get Honor from protecting the helpless, you also get it from making sure that normal humans don't learn too much, which means misdirecting investigations, destroying evidence, disappearing witnesses, and other general work in making sure that the Veil Shall Not Be Lifted. How ruthless any given changer gets at this is up to them, but I doubt that they have witness to otherworldly horror and gore protection programs, and not everyone has memory-tampering Gifts; a fair number of disappearances are probably going to involve quietly killing otherwise innocent folks.

            Bunyip Gifts

            The Bunyip Gifts are primarily here, I assume, for games set during their genocide, and also for Garou who are interested in Lost Tribes to try and recover their Gifts and Rites, and also for a possible Bunyip Recovery situation. I like that they put effort into them! Ultimately, the also show what was lost, even if you ignore the ethical atrocities. On a purely tactical level the Garou Nation really shot itself in the foot destroying a Tribe with Mother's Touch and a bunch of unique Gifts.

            Bunyip's Spell (1): A cost-free paralysis power where you roll Willpower, resisted by Willpower. On a success the target is frozen in place for one turn per success. If the target is an ordinary mortal you even get to prolong the duration for a whole scene by spending Willpower.

            A big downside is that this Gift doesn't work on undead or on shapeshifters, but it does work on Fomori, so long as they are 'mostly mortal'. Presumably it would work on Mages, Sorcerers and so on, but possibly not on Changelings who are technically 'shapeshifters' by default given their Fae Mien and the ability to go Wyrd.

            The best thing about this Gift is that a mortal who is stunned by this power will forget what happened to them. They'll remember being paralyzed but not anything else, so this is a great way to deal with Litany Breaches and mortals wandering where they shouldn't be. Amazing Gift with many uses.

            Mother's Touch (1): See the Rank 1 Theurge entry. Wiping out a Tribe with a Gift like this is a huge own goal.

            Resist Toxin (1): See the Rank 1 Bone Gnawer entry.

            Sense Prey (1): See the Rank 1 Lupus entry.

            Coils of the Serpent (2): See the Rank 2 Elder Brother entry.

            Crocodile's Cunning (2): Spend a Gnosis, breathe underwater for an hour, and gain +2 to stealth tests when underwater or submerged in mud.

            This... isn't great. This Gift could be 100% passive and still work great. I'd call it Amber.

            Lonesome Voice of the Bunyip (3)*: Spend one Gnosis. Roll a Charisma + Performance (difficulty 7). Everyone within earshot who isn't a Bunyip loses 1 temporary Willpower point per success and can't regain Willpower until they leave the area where the Gift was used.

            This Gift is a weapon of a Tribe at the brink of extinction, and it lets a single survivor scream at people coming to their home to leave them alone. This Gift is very powerful, but it has problems for a non-Bunyip to use. You, the user of the Gift, are not explicitly immune to the power. Bunyip are immune. If you aren't a Bunyip, you'll suffer the effects, RAW. Even if that isn't the case, all of your allies will get hit.

            This is a Green with a big asterisk. It's amazing in the intended use case (a Bunyip using it) but can be pretty bad otherwise.

            Pulse of the Invisible (3): See the Rank 3 Theurge entry.

            Dance of the Lightning Snakes (4)*: A Gift which can only be used in the Umbra is a great idea. A bunch of outlandish "video gamey" Gifts should be Umbra only. It would really help the setting feel more cohesive, like when you have a mobile shield of earth following you around, which is much easier to visualize in our world.

            Spend a Gnosis, roll Willpower, difficulty 5-9 (5 if a storm is already brewing in the Penumbral skies, 9 if there is no weather at all or the skies are clear). The difficulty is 1 lower in the native lands of the Bunyip, which is something I'd love to see more often in Gifts, not super often, but at Level 4+ I'd love to see more "this Elder Brother Gift is at -1 difficulty in North America" type Gifts.

            The storm gathers in 3 turns and covers an equivalent to 5 miles per success. The difficulty or Essence cost of fire, travel or perception Charms increases by 1. A sympathetic storm can also appear in the mortal world.

            While the storm exists the Garou can attack using the lightning snakes, Charisma + Occult, difficulty 7, 10 dice of Aggravated damage. A ranged attack spanning miles is pretty amazing!

            All in all... I'd call this Gift pretty cool. The Umbra-only restriction is annoying but it's pretty amazing for huge-scale Umbra battles. Assuming that you can use it at great range and that you are able to fight enemies in the Umbra, I'll call this Amber as you can strike from stealth (the attack comes from the sky directly above, I would imagine).

            Quicksand (4): See the Rank 4 Red Talon entry.

            Billabong Bridge (5): This Gift lets you enter any body of fresh water and exit any other body of fresh water, so long as you are familiar with your destination. With enough travel in your history you could teleport anywhere on earth with this Gift, though if the place you arrive is high Gauntlet you may need to step sideways. The cost is 2 Gnosis and there is usually no roll.

            This is pretty great; by the time you're an Elder you will know a lot of valid targets for this Gift. That said, as an Elder you probably have access to moon bridges. This is very neat but it's expensive in terms of Gnosis and very late in the game too. Definitely worth buying, could help you escape a lot of terrible situations, but the cost is bad.

            Invoke the Spirits of the Storm (5): See the Rank 5 Younger Brother entry.

                Predator's Many Eyes (3): This Gift is like Territory, but instead of urinating to mark a territory, you mark an animal (Fera, and presumably Garou, are valid targets, with their consent) simply by looking at them. Which is supposed to be the case really impacts how it compares to Territory. So you can get to far more places than where you can urinate (toss a small stone into an enemy encampment and freely spy on them from a distance), and stones don't have lives of their own to go attend to like the animals you mark with Predator's Many Eyes.

                                    Middle Brother Gifts

                                    Middle Brother are meant to fall between the other Tribes of North America. So while Elder Brother might be scheming of some plan many moons away with spirits and rituals, and Younger Brother might be demanding violent action tonight, Middle Brother mediates. Stoicism, endurance and balance are meant to be key to their Gifts.

                                    Master of Fire (1): See the Rank 1 Homid list.

                                    Mindspeak (1): See the Rank 1 Galliard list.

                                    Turtle Body (1): This Gift lets a Garou enter a torpor-like trance for hours at a time, ignoring poisons, bleeding wounds, needing to breathe, extremes of heat and cold, and so on. Spending a Gnosis and rolling Gnosis (difficulty 6) lets you enter this trance for one hour per success. It's perfectly... fine? It's a handy way to play dead, hardly a bad Gift at all, but the Tribe would be much better off with Resist Toxin or similar. The Gift also really doesn't need a Gnosis cost.

                                    My ultimate problem with this, beyond the cost, the fact you roll Gnosis (a relatively weak pool), is that you have to go inactive. Not needing to breathe for 3 hours is great, but if you just lay there immobile for 3 hours, then return to the land of the living to suffocate as normal, that's pretty terrible. You absolutely can make this work, but there are much better Gifts for the same job, like Cocoon. At any other level than 1 this would be Red, but it scrapes an Amber since it only costs 3xp. That said, it's taking up a slot which a superior survival Gift could be used for!

                                    Wyld Resurgence (1): See the Rank 1 Black Fury list.

                                    Strength of Purpose (2): See the Rank 2 Philodox list. Infinite Willpower! Annoyingly, for a Tribe about balance, this does mean you have a weird incentive to play a different Auspice. An Ahroun or Theurge with Strength of Purpose would be pretty incredible for instance.

                                    Turtle Shell (2): With this Gift you can create a defensive shell with variable size and power depending on your successes. Annoyingly we don't get any clues as to whether the shell is meant to move, but I'm happy to say that the intent is almost certainly for it to be immobile, based on my reading.

                                    2 soak per success, 2 health levels per success, covers 2 yards per success, and it costs 1 Gnosis. The roll is Stamina + Survival, which is pretty easy to boost by taking Crinos. If you can get 12 dice, this will consistently get ~5 successes, granting an average of 10 soak, 10 health levels, and being big enough to shelter 10 Crinos Garou. Having a protective barrier you and your whole pack, plus allies, can hide behind is a pretty great Gift! Many foes simply won't be able to harm you, and of those that can, wasting some of their time is handy.

                                    Call Earth Spirit (3): Costing a Gnosis for a damaging attack is a bad start, and this Gift is difficulty 8 to invoke. Furthermore if you botch it hurts you instead. Basically this Gift lets you send an earth spirit 30 yards in a direction to hit everyone in its path for 10 dice of lethal. But Manipulation + Occult difficulty 8? Not great!

                                    If you can manage the difficulty somehow, great, but I don't know of a way to make this Gift easier to use. Wasting a Gnosis on a difficulty 8 test isn't fun, and the result is a straight line AOE attack which inflicts lethal. Even if you luck out and inflict 10 lethal on every enemy in this straight line, chances are that enemies worth fighting at Rank 3 will soak a good amount of it. If this were difficulty 7 it would be far better, the probability curve is much more steep than the writers seemed to realize.

                                    Shell (3): See the Rank 3 Metis list.

                                    Hands of the Earth Lords (4): See the Rank 4 Elder Brother list. It would be interesting, given that the EB Tribe are said to have 'recently uncovered' a bunch of their Gifts, if this Gift was originally a MB Gift which has been recovered by the EB.

                                    Stronger on Stone (4): Spending a Gnosis and a Rage, a Garou using this Gift can gain a bonus to Strength and/or Stamina based on where they're standing. This is 1-4 points to distribute between the two Attributes, so for example while standing on naked stone they might get +4 Strength, or +4 Stamina, or anywhere in between.

                                    +4 Stamina is neat, don't get me wrong, but the situational nature of the Gift is a little annoying. It would be easy to fix the Gift by making it give a bonus to Strength and Stamina, rather than a bonus you split between them, but no matter. The cost of G+R is also a bit much.

                                    Katanka-Sonnak's Spear (5): This Gift is shockingly amazing. Spend a Gnosis, target anyone you can see (including miles away), and roll Dexterity + Occult (difficulty 6). If the strike hits, a sunbeam from the sky, the target takes 10 dice of Aggravated damage every single turn. The fire burns for 10 turns, can't be doused by normal water, and will very likely kill most targets. That said, the effect is canceled if the Gift is "dispelled", if a water spirit puts out the fire, or if the fire does no damage on a specific turn.

                                    For example, 10 dice of Aggravated, you roll 5 successes. Rank 1 Garou gets hit, they only soak 3, so they take 2 Aggravated. Next turn they are in Crinos form, you roll 4 damage, they soak all 4, and the fire dies down.

                                    Still, the Gift only costs 1 Gnosis, the range is incredible, and with any Gift which helps you to see much further it'll be even better. Solid Green and a fitting Rank 5.

                                    Survivor (5): See the Rank 5 Bone Gnawer list.

                                    The list winds up pretty middling, sadly!

                                                            White Howler Gifts

                                                            The White Howlers were Pictish, and their Gifts are intended to have heavy themes of morbidity, darkness and passion. It's said that the Howlers were extremely passionate for hunting and battles, and that their rites and mystical Gifts have eluded Theurges attempting to recreate them. While the best White Howler stuff is in their Rites, let's see what their Gift list is like. Note that I am including all of the Gifts in their Tribebook, which is currently the only Tribebook I own for W20, so this list will be VERY beefy.

                                                            Blood Scent (1): Passive buff to tracking individuals whose blood you have tasted. This buff of +2 dice applies to mundane tracking and also to supernatural means such as those given by Gifts, and presumably by Fetishes. With this Gift it's a good idea to have tasted the blood of your packmates, with their permission, to help track them down if they escape in a Fox Frenzy or become subject to mind control. An interesting thing to consider with this Gift is whether it helps you to recognize people whose blood you have tasted. You have +2 dice to track them, but them as in the person whose blood you have tasted. Would this Gift give you a +2 bonus to notice that the person you are looking at isn't your packmate? Though the senses provided by Lupus mostly cover this issue, since an impostor will often smell wrong.

                                                            Handy buff, well worth 3xp, but not good enough to be a Green.

                                                            Death Sight (1): This is equivalent to Visions of Duat, see the Rank 1 Silent Strider list.

                                                            Desperate Strength (1): See the Rank 1 Bone Gnawer list.

                                                            Haunting Howl (1): This looks horrible and pretty worthless, on a quick examination. By rolling Charisma + Intimidation, difficulty 7, you can make all enemies within earshot suffer +1 to Willpower tests for 3 turns. 3 turns per success, so with a few successes it lasts longer. No impact on spending Willpower, just the difficulty of the rolls.

                                                            Anyone figured out why it's good yet? No, it isn't because it messes with Tremere. It definitely isn't because it interferes with Feats of Strength.

                                                            It's because it works on Spirits.

                                                            Willpower is Dexterity and Stamina. Spirits roll Willpower to hit, and they roll Willpower for soak, plus they roll Willpower for some Charms. They roll Willpower to sneak around, they roll Willpower to dodge, they roll Willpower to parry. Now, given that this Gift costs 3xp to purchase and it has no cost at all to use, just a difficulty 7 roll, and given that it has a generous duration (most likely 3, 6 or 9 turns), and given that it adds +1 difficulty to the attacks, dodges, and soak attempts made by all enemy spirits, without harming allied spirits... yeah this is actually pretty great! It's niche, but it's hard to overstate how much impact +1 difficulty can have across a whole battle. If you're not convinced, ask yourself how you react to a Flaw which gives your character +1 difficulty to all of their attacks.

                                                            Against enemies other than Spirits? Well it'll mess with some Gifts. It'll interfere with blood magic, and with all the things that might call for Willpower tests depending on the ST, like whether enemies flee or hold the line.

                                                            The key question is how important is rolling Willpower in Werewolf, for Garou, Fera, Fomori, and various other enemies. Cause Insanity, a Fomori power, is a WP roll. Warp Reality is a WP roll (difficulty 7), and that is arguably the most fearsome ability of the Nexus Crawler. Garou roll Willpower to avoid Fox Frenzy.

                                                            Primal Anger (1): See the Rank 1 Metis list.

                                                            Sense Wyrm (1): See the Rank 1 Metis list.

                                                            Shining Sigils (1)*: This Gift lets you present your Renown ratings as shining symbols appearing all over your body, like woad spirals. There is no cost, and it grants you a pool of bonus dice equal to your Rank which you can benefit from in a given scene.

                                                            So, as a Rank 2 White Howler you might have 2 dice to call upon, and you could split those between two rolls or use them all in one go. We are told they work on "any Social roll (including Intimidation)", so presumably they would work with a number of social Gifts. While this Gift isn't as good as Persuasion, for example, it's still pretty great. In many scenes there will only be a single social roll, and it may be a totally pivotal one. Being able to add 1-5 dice to that test, combined with any difficulty reductions you've gained from Merits, Gifts or similar, can have a very big impact. All in all this is a pretty solid 3xp investment, and it may well work out as Green if you have the right setup and you choose the social rolls you use it on carefully.

                                                            In an absolutely ideal setup, where you're maximizing every possible input (Pure Breed, Ancestors, all the right Merits, the right Fetish, maxed out social stats, this Gift, Persuasion, and a bunch of other stuff including the right Totem) everything compounds on itself. When you're rolling 20 dice at a difficulty of 3, you naturally get results like 14 successes. This Gift is well worth taking for flavour alone, and for the non-mechanical impact of being able to literally prove your Rank and Renown to strangers through a Gift. I just wish there was a Level 2 White Howler Gift which allows you to see the invisible markings of Rank & Renown on others, for example immediately recognizing that a stranger is Rank 4 with a certain combo of Honour, Glory and Wisdom, due to the invisible spirit-markings trailing across their form.

                                                            Howl of the Banshee (2): See the Rank 2 Fianna list. This was added in the WH Book, and its absence was pretty conspicuous. This is a Gift which combos well with Haunting Howl. This Gift remains far too powerful for a level 2, due to the ability to trigger a panic across a huge part of a city.

                                                            Howls in the Night (2): See the Rank 2 Galliard list. Funny to imagine a BSD using this to piss off or punish all of their allies.

                                                            Lion's Bite (2)*: This is an interesting one. Instead of doing a Dexterity + Brawl attack to bite, you do a Strength + Brawl. You inflict your 'normal' Bite damage and the target becomes 'grappled' in your maw as you try to crush their throat or equivalent. Every round your victim can try a resisted test of Strength to escape (this is where a Gift like Pain-Strength would theoretically be handy, but that doesn't save it from being terrible), which is their action for the turn, or they can try and do something else at a +2 difficulty.

                                                            How good is this in practice? Well, I'm not a Bite Scientist, but gaining access to a means of attacking which is Strength-based is favourable for Garou, with their +4 to Strength in Crinos. An automatic grapple into the bargain ain't bad either. After a number of rounds equal to 2x the target's stamina they fall unconscious, at which point they start taking Lethal damage every round, which can't be regenerated. It isn't explicitly stated, but presumably this damage ignores Soak. This does grant a means of harming a target who otherwise might be impossible to harm.

                                                            For me, personally, as a person who isn't a huge fan of bites, this is decent enough. A Strength-based combat action with damage and a grapple all baked in together is neat. Normally to grapple someone you need to devote an action to a non-damaging grapple, and start damaging the next turn. With this you can get started a turn early. I'd call this an objective upgrade well worth 6xp, but mileage will vary depending on playstyle and what attacks you favour. Combining with Clenched Jaw (Ahroun, Rank 4) is probably tempting as it will grant you a bonus to the Strength test to keep the target grappled, and reduce the difficulty of bite attacks while grappling the foe down to 3. All pretty good. Calling this Amber, but it could easily be Green if you prefer biting and made the right 'build'. Much like with Clenched Jaw this feels like a Gift covering something which should be in the combat rules chapter; ordinary wolves do this!

                                                            Pain-Strength (2): When wounded, you can spend a Rage point to activate Pain-Strength. While the Gift is active, wound penalties give a boost to your Strength rating, and to your damage pools, essentially reversing the penalty.

                                                            So, for example, if you would normally suffer a -5 to all dice pools, you suffer a -5 to your to-hit but you get a +5 to your damage pool. Notably you still suffer the penalty to Strength + Ability pools, which means if you are grappling someone you'll have neither a bonus nor a debuff, since you get +5 strength and -5 wound penalty.

                                                            Is this good? Well, is it good to be wounded? No. Is it good to suffer dice penalties to your attack rolls, your dodges, and so on? No. In fact, a lot of effort goes into avoiding wound penalties. Spending WP, getting Resist Pain, and so on. And this Gift is explicitly not compatible with any Gift which lets you ignore your wound modifiers. Furthermore, being at -5 means you can only crawl; you're horribly wounded.

                                                            The Gift is flavourful and pretty cool, but it's just horrible to use. Resist Pain is better, any day. Spending WP to ignore wound penalties is better, and getting healed is better. If this Gift were 100% passive you could make an argument for "well you want to avoid wound penalties, of course, but if you ARE wounded this kicks in and gives you an upside, even if you want to get healed ASAP". Unfortunately the Rage cost messes with that! All in all, not a fan of this one, especially since it doesn't fully apply to Strength + Brawl and Strength + Athletics tests, which would give this Gift a great niche for Challenges!

                                                            Spectral Authority (2)*: This is equivalent to Call to Duty (Philodox Rank 2), but it works on Ghosts. You must know the full name of the Ghost in question, and the Gift may only summon a single Wraith at a time, there is no urgent call for all allied ghosts as you find with Call to Duty. This works well with the ghostly theme of the White Howlers, but it's worth remembering that Wraiths are inherently less relevant to Werewolf as a game. The lack of a Gnosis cost is handy, and a network of Wraith spies with this Gift is totally doable. It's Amber, but it could easily be Green with the right ST, story, and setup time. A Werewolf ST who is intimately familiar with Wraith would be a godsend with this Gift.

                                                            Blooding Fury (3)*: Once per scene you may take a level of Aggravated damage and recover ALL of your Rage. This Gift is somewhat redundant in terms of game design when sat next to Primal Anger. There isn't much point writing both. That doesn't mean the Gift is mechanically redundant, however. Primal Anger works once per scene and grants you 3 temporary Rage regardless of your normal limits. If you are at 0/5 you go to 3/5, if you are at 1/1 you go to 4/1. Blooding Fury is very different, it can take you from 0/1 to 1/1 or from 0/10 to 10/10, or from 4/6 to 6/6, and so on.

                                                            With that in mind, a combination of Blooding Fury and Primal Anger lets you go from 0/5 to 8/5 by taking two points of Aggravated damage. If you have a means of healing that damage easily (such as a Water Spirit, which you'll be able to summon from next rank) then this combination could be very good; it's basically a 100% recharge of your Rage between fights. Yes, some Werewolf players like to say "you get rage for stubbing your toe", but that isn't the RAW. The intended mechanics include Rage being a resource which is sometimes a struggle to recover when you really need it.

                                                            Given that this is Rank 3, and there is a bit of overlap with another Gift which is only Rank 1, and this Gift comes with the inherent downside of needing to hurt yourself... Amber is just fine. If healing the damage is utterly trivial to you then the Gift is even better, but I'd say that Green is a push unless your Rage rating is very high, for example 7. 0/7 to 10/7 in a quick combo with your Water Spirit or Theurge healing the damage? Sure. Green.

                                                            Sense of the Deep (3)*: A bunch of really neat passive benefits. While underground you always know which way is North, you can always retrace your steps, and you can always see normally, ignoring penalties from darkness. Used aggressively, this Gift could be exploited in any fight below ground level. Sabotage the lighting, or even have a Challenge in a subway or some other underground chamber. The flavour of this Gift is also pretty great, as it highlights the role of the WHs and ties into how they were lost.

                                                            This is Amber, mostly because it's a bit niche. It could very easily be Green if you are constantly fighting underground, invading Wyrmish pits, chasing spirits into their tunnels and so on.

                                                            Ley Lines (3): See the Rank 3 Fianna list.

                                                            Territory (3): Mechanically identical to the Red Talon Gift, but Homid and Metis White Howlers may mark stones instead. Already a great Gift, arguably improved here.

                                                            Waking the Dead (3): This Gift is very Dungeons and Dragons, though in the case of this Gift I mean that as a compliment. A corpse, any corpse, can be made to answer one question per point of Gnosis spent on activating the Gift. The body of a Kinfolk ally, for example, can be made to answer 5 questions by spending 5 Gnosis. There is no roll, but the Gift can only be used on a body once. After this it is impossible to use the Gift again.

                                                            It's interesting to note that this is a phantasm conjured from the body, it isn't a true Wraith, or any kind of spirit, and the fact that you can use it on a Vampire is very funny to me. The answers to a problem being in the mind of an ancient Kinfolk who was embraced by a Leech in the 1100s, for example, is a possible plot thread you could pull using this Gift.

                                                            Call Elemental (4): See the Rank 4 Elder Brother list. Losing the only European Tribe with this Gift probably wasn't ideal.

                                                            Collapse the Barrow (4)*: This Gift lets you collapse an underground structure, ideally after the Wyrmish inhabitants have been killed and the area cleansed. Strength + Occult, difficulty can be as low as 4 or as high as 9 depending on the integrity of the structure, but 6 is the default. There is no cost. For every success a 5-foot area collapses, which is... not huge, all things considered.

                                                            Using this Gift to block off means of escape, with yourself on the inside with the enemy, or with the enemy stuck inside while you lay siege outside, is interesting. For example you could block every tunnel leading into a Wyrmish Pit, forcing all of your enemies to come in and out through a single tunnel.

                                                            Having the cost-free option of using Strength + Occult to demolish stone, earthen and underground structures is neat. But "other Fomori will reuse this underground cave system" feels like a niche problem to have, especially given that you can introduce animals (even wolves!) into the space after it is purified. At Rank 4 I struggle to imagine "A Cave Exists" is a huge problem for you, given that there will be many Garou and Kinfolk under your command. Red, but could be Amber in the right setting.

                                                            Hero's Stand (4): See the Rank 4 Get of Fenris list.

                                                            Maddening Howl (4)*: Spend a Gnosis and a Rage, roll Charisma + Performance (difficulty 7), and all non-White Howlers who can hear the howl have to roll Willpower difficulty 7. Worth noting that the Willpower roll will almost certainly be difficulty 8 thanks to Haunting Howl! If any victim gets fewer successes than you they fall into a state of madness, picking one of five random results from the Delirium table. The ST chooses, but the idea is that the result is more random than characteristic to the target. So a Get of Fenris Elder may well fall into a state of complete panic while a peaceful Child of Gaia could go into a berserk killing spree.

                                                            This has the potential to be very, very good. You can hit everyone who hears it, everyone... including you and your pack, if you aren't White Howlers. Even if we assume the user of this Gift is immune (and we can't really!), your pack all being hit is pretty bad. You could have them protect their hearing before you use the Gift, and remove that protection after. But still. Heavy asterisk on this one; it's absolutely incredible for a White Howler who is either solo or who has a pack of White Howlers. The resulting chaos from this Gift is probably much what you'd expect, which could be highly beneficial to the PCs. A Caern or Pit full of powerful enemies is impossible to escape, meanwhile a Caern or Pit which is full of people fleeing, screaming, fighting eachother, going into complete denial about their surroundings is much more manageable, even if it's still a horror show.

                                                            Visions of Slaughter (4): This Gift allows you to haunt someone, mortal, animal, supernatural, with visions of any animals or people they have caused to die through action or inaction. After smearing the target with your bodily fluids (saliva, please) you make an Intelligence + Occult, difficulty of the target's WP. The visions last for one day per success.

                                                            While 'haunted' by the visions of those they have killed the victim must roll Willpower once a day (Haunting Howl synergy!) against a difficulty determined by how many victims they have had. 4 is the minimum (having killed the odd mouse or hit something with their car) while 9 is the maximum (centuries of slaughter). Even if you don't care about all the mortals you've murdered over the years, seeing them all following you around is still pretty distracting! If you're effected by the Gift you can't recover Willpower by any means, and once your Willpower falls below half your maximum you suffer a +1 difficulty to all rolls (besides soak and damage).

                                                            So, how good is this? Well, I could easily see this as a Punishment Rite rather than a Gift. But since it is a Gift... the requirement of getting some of your saliva, blood or some other bodily fluid onto the target is hard to do without starting an altercation. The roll against Willpower means that this Gift is harder to pull off against an enemy you actually care to curse. But only one success is really needed in a proper battle, since you want them dead! With that one success they will hopefully become effected, since even with a Willpower of , a difficulty 7-9 test (what one of your enemies will typically need to pass) could be challenging. The lack of a Gnosis cost on this Gift is also a bit odd.

                                                            This might do a lot, or it might do nothing. It's a bit of a slow burn, but it's a unique and interesting concept. Just gonna call it Amber.

                                                            Fog on the Moor (5): See the Rank 5 Fianna list.

                                                            Howl of Healing (5)*: Intelligence + Expression, difficulty 7, all White Howlers within earshot recover 1 point of damage (any kind) per success. This is pretty great! It costs 1 Gnosis, and it's extremely powerful as a form of healing. Compare with Mother's Touch, which is single-target, typically a lower difficulty, but can effect anyone. Howl of Healing could effect 100s of White Howlers... if 100s exist. If you are the Elder of a WH Sept and there is a thriving WH Tribe, this is Green. Otherwise it's likely Red. If a Child of Gaia were able to salvage this Gift and have it reformed by Unicorn it could be a great boon; if I were the ST in such a game I'd be open to this Gift being picked up by a dedicated Garou of a different tribe, with them being able to work to convert it to apply to their tribe instead.

                                                            Mad Strength (5): This is a passive boon, granting you +2 Strength and +1 Stamina while in a Berserk Frenzy or Thrall of the Wyrm. Great flavour for a pre-fall Tribe, suits the lore of the Howlers, and matches their Totem too. How good is it? Well, it's niche, and the bonus is decent. Having 11 Strength instead of 9, and 9 Stamina instead of 8, would be pretty good. But I'd much prefer a Gift like this if it were at a lower level, like 3 or even 2.

                                                            Making the most of this Gift is like trying to make the most of Pain-Strength. A lot of Elder powers I find myself thinking "well, let it apply to the whole pack", but "everyone in my pack has +2 Strength while frenzying" is a very mixed blessing. The potential for this Gift to backfire and help you just barely do enough damage to kill an ally shouldn't be ignored. If this Gift were stronger, and followed on from an earlier Gift where you voluntarily enter a Frenzy with some specific benefits and limitations (e.g. the Frenzy ends when a specific foe is dead) then the rating would be very different.

                                                            Gaia's Vengeance (5): See the Rank 5 Red Talons list. The Gift is basically unwritten, but I can see how it would be helpful in underground battles, if it had mechanics.

                                                            White Fire (5): The bone-fire of the White Howlers, later corrupted to the balefire of the BSDs, has purifying as well as damaging properties. This Gift allows you to fling it at people! There is a cool benefit that those killed with this power won't become ghosts or undead, and most likely a Fomori destroyed with the Gift won't have its Bane return to Malfeas, instead being destroyed.

                                                            The attack costs one Gnosis, which is expensive for a single-shot attack, but the attack is a Dexterity + Occult (difficulty 6) which deals 6 dice of Aggravated, with bonus successes carrying over. Ranged Aggravated is always great, and a damage of 6 + Successes is also pretty great, though a typical claw attack will do more. What's important, however, is that if you get 3+ successes on the attack you will also blind your target for the whole scene. If you have a difficulty break on the Dexterity + Occult attack, for example from an ambush or the enemy being stunned, it's very likely you'll get 3+ successes.

                                                            How good is a solid attack which comes with a blinding effect, plus a lot of handy narrative benefits relating to purifying the targets you kill? Well, the fact that you can create bone fire is pretty neat. The blinding is neat. The attack is neat. That said, this could be a lower level Gift, given that every attack has a Gnosis cost. At Rank 5 this Gift could easily have a Rage cost. We'll call it Amber.
