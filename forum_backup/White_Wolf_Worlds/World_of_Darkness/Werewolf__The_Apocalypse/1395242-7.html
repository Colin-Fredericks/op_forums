            I think many rural kinfolk communities in the US will "heavily" armed because that is often the norm. They'll have sidearms for self defense and rifles for hunting and sportsmanship. They may even have heavier arms because some people find it fun to set up a target range and shoot them. It's simply part of the outdoor culture of the United States. Many rural kinfolk are going to be hikers, campers, hunters, and fishermen. That could be different in other countries which have a different outdoor culture.

            I don't think they'll deviate much from the non-kinfolk population in this. And I don't see why the Garou would object. They would want their kinfolk to defend themselves if needed. Some, like the Get of Fenris, will probably insist on it.

            What I think the Garou would object to is the presence of silver in their communities especially any silver somehow not known or controlled. (There could always be some kind of justification for a known and monitored stockpile of silver, such as protection in case Black Spiral Dancers attack).

            I don't think Garou only view kinfolk as fit for some kind of menial labor. Kinfolk are too great an asset to be used like that. Highly skilled kinfolk would not only be preferred as mates, but they would want their kinfolk communities to be self-supporting and able to productively contribute to Garou in terms of providing finances, equipment, and skills. The Kinfolk background is primarily for routine or mundane help, but many of the Allies and Contacts a Garou will have will also be Kinfolk but using that mechanic instead. Garou would want their kinfolk to be skilled enough to be physicians, sheriff officers, enlist in the armed forces, lawyers & judges, and many other skilled or prestigious professions because that will protect those communities and help the Garou.

            And while I think most Garou will use other Garou as their teachers. if there is truly an excellent local expert in whatever ability in a kinfolk, I don't think they'd refuse to make use of it. And before the First Change happens, a Garou's early preparation will often be from adult kinfolk instructing them how to do things. One of the benefits of truly having a kinfolk community of several hundred people is that kinfolk adults can adequately prepare pre-Change cubs in skills they will need after they change. Those kids won't have to wait until their First Change to pick up skills in Survival, Firearms, Stealth, and other skills. They can learn them in school, in Boy Scout troops, in family camping trips, on the farm and family owned businesses, and extracurricular activities. They'll have an advantage over cubs who don't come from those backgrounds, and thus will more quickly become Cliaths and be useful to the Nation.

            I see sometimes in online games that Kinfolk PCs want to be ersatz Garou, and I don't see Garou wanting that. They're not packmates and they don't replace Garou. When supernatural threats arise, the Garou want the Kinfolk out of the way and in a safe place just like adults want children to be safe inside the home if something dangerous is happening outside.

                                                            I definitely think that the question of how the Garou Nation deals with those kinfolk who do not have the benefit of a "garou ambassador" of sorts, those who have either suffered the tragedy of losing their garou kin to battle or (even worse) have suffered the tragedy of not bearing any garou this generation.

                                                            While I do imagine that such groups would often try to reach out to their nearest living garou relative, it's possible that no such figure exists or that they would be beyond the group's reach, all too possible as the war rages and the kinfolk population continues to outstrip the garou minority by leaps and bounds.

                                                            So from there what I imagine happening is that Kenning Kinfolk are often forced to make a choice of two options and from there to balance their obligations to *at least* two-three different areas. They must first choose to stay within the Nation as a group, which I imagine is a regular choice for those raised within the Nation, yet without a direct garou tie I can see some deciding to up-sticks.

                                                            Assuming however that they remain within the Garou Nation as kenning kinfolk; I see them tied between their loyalties to each other, their sept, their tribe, and potentially any remaining packmates of their final garou who may feel obligated to offer stewardship.

                                                            Naturally different tribal cultures will lead to different reactions and qualities but let me break down my logic.

                                                            First the kinfolk must determine how they function as a sub-culture within the Nation. They must determine the needs of their membership and how their spiritual, martial, and managerial needs are to be addressed. The latter is likely the easiest as most kinfolk probably already had matriarchs, patriarchs, or elders who worked alongside the family garou. These are now likely to be the clan/band leaders. They must then determine how to achieve their other needs. Some might begin to create their own martial and spiritual practices if cut off from the Nation, but most will likely seek out Garou and here begins the balancing act. Who to go to? They're likely going to be inclined to find a suitable member of their own tribe, yet they are somewhat restricted by the dangers of leaving their local sept territory. They might represent an important resource bloc for the local garou, who may not feel comfortable simply letting them go. Yet they aren't likely to feel comfortable dictating to them either...

                                                            Kinfolk families make up unspeakably important resources to the Garou. They provide material, logistics, and support as well as being absolutely vital for creating the next generation. This creates a strong incentive for septs to want to keep them close. I imagine that at the best of times the sept will put forward respected and honorable stewards to serve as regents for the family until they bear a garou of their own, or perhaps a quick marriage is made between a garou of the kinfolk's tribe.

                                                            Yet what happens if the sept does not have a suitable member of the garou's own tribe or the kinfolk won't recognize a strange garou's authority over them? Trouble. At this point the kinfolk may attempt to leave or remain as a difficult to control subculture which the sept leadership likely will not tolerate. They will need to toe the line or leave and sometimes leaving is not an option, either to the Garou or the kinfolk.

                                                            These cases I see as being one of the most prime sources of recruitment for tribes such as the Bone Gnawers, Glass Walkers, and *especially* the Children of Gaia. The former tribes have lost their pure breed and as such can welcome any newcomer while the latter tribe I imagine as having a significant population for a few key reasons.

                                                            Their willingness to tolerate all gaian tribes, their relatively liberal style of government, and their absolute devotion to the ideals of purity and unity within the Nation. They may be an isolated kinfolk family's greatest chance for fair representation amongst Garou Society and as such I can see many kinfolk who cannot return to the territories of Garou of their own tribe flocking to them.

                                                            That all said, I also think that the matter of tribe is extremely important as generally, a kinfolk group can expect to seek out another family of the same tribe and be welcomed as brothers and sisters under the protection of the latter group's living garou. The tribes are vast extended families that provide commonalities of culture for their members to seek out and be comforted by.

                                                            Tribes also provide a safety net for uncertain kinfolk groups. Worst case scenario, if news reached the tribe in foreign septs that their kinfolk are at risk of becoming domineered or forcibly assimilated by other garou, the tribe may well come calling to claim their kinfolk, as many of them will see as their Gaia given rite.

                                                            What I mean to say by all of this is that in most instances I imagine that the most common reaction of an isolated kinfolk group as to welcome a garou of the same tribe and sept into their number. Failing that they may seek out the safety of the Children of Gaia or call out to the tribemates further afield.

                                                            However in these dark times where lives are perhaps the most important resource of all, there is much potential for conflict to be had simply by one garou deciding that their claim to or need of a kinfolk is superior to another's...
