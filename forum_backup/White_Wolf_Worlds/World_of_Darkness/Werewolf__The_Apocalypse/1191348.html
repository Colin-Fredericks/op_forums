              As a concept, PENTEX doesn't work if it is possible to beat it in a legal way. If it could be, then the game wouldn't need supernaturally powerful werewolves to defeat them. We could just assume normal humans and kinfolk could use the legal system to do so. Garou would not be needed.

              Legal and political tactics are probably tried and have some limited form of success, but any real defeats would not to be done by Garou. Any legal successes would have to be relatively minor (in terms of what PENTEX really wants done), or irrelevant. For example, court orders and protests finally stop some resource extraction operations done in a sensitive area. It appears to be a big victory, but PENTEX had enough time to free the Bane trapped there while the justice system handled it. So even though on the surface it appears PENTEX was defeated, in reality they accomplished their mission.

              So while I think there is a place where legal measures can be taken, in anything that is really relevant the Garou still need to take direct action against the supernatural component. Of course, legal means can be PART of the strategy necessary to stop them. But if that could work only be itself, I think it undermines what the Garou are there to do. Of course, if you only want there to be background setting, then you can have any number of lawsuits, political lobbying, etc. happening done by whoever. That can make good hooks for PCs to enter the story whether it is a Kinfolk legal firm engaged in a lawsuit, a normal human reporter uncovering a story of business corruption, or a news story alerting the PCs of activity.

                      Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                      As a concept, PENTEX doesn't work if it is possible to beat it in a legal way. If it could be, then the game wouldn't need supernaturally powerful werewolves to defeat them.
                      You know, I'm going to disagree to an extent. The issue in my opinion is not can Pentex or its subsidiaries be beaten in court or public opinion, but rather that any such victories are either pyrrhic, unsatisfying to the Garou, or both. The Exxon Valdez disaster is a good illustration of this in the real world. Exxon was defeated in court -- but it took over a decade, and in the end the corporation was able to out-litigate most of the really vulnerable people who were hurt in the disaster. What was left was an award that they managed to whittle down to almost nothing. In the end, they simply had to admit wrong-doing to get a slap on the wrist. Now, that's not exactly a defeat from their point of view (although the PR disaster it became has haunted them to an extent) since such an admission is cheap. Nonetheless, there were very real consequences for the industry as a whole that were difficult to quantify on a quarterly statement. Exploratory drilling (particularly in coastal areas) have become a bureaucratic nightmare precisely because neither the public nor lawmakers have any trust of the industry in general left due to the way Exxon so flagrantly avoided responsibility for their actions almost three decades ago.

                      ​The Garou on the other hand would have little patience for so unsatisfying an outcome. Violent retribution is far more immediate and fulfilling to them.
