    Yeah WtA really neglected its outcasts. It was never on the level of Mage, nor would it need to, but even Vampire got more love with the Thinblood and far flung bloodlines. It would be nice to have an updated look at ronin, more on the Curs and Skindancers (even the little tribes not part of the Nation would be nice too), and for them to really delve into Lost Cubs and Lunatics.

    There's lots I love in what Revised and W20 did for the game, but it really lost that sense of social disintegration that just oozed from the older books I've read (despite early installment weirdness).

    At the same time, I'm also tired of the calls to make the Garou more monstress. They can be pretty bad already raw by most standards, and it's something I've found myself having to defend the game against. Making everyone into asshole and everything darker does not make a setting deeper.

            Originally posted by Onkwe View Post
            Yeah WtA really neglected its outcasts. It was never on the level of Mage, nor would it need to, but even Vampire got more love with the Thinblood and far flung bloodlines. It would be nice to have an updated look at ronin, more on the Curs and Skindancers (even the little tribes not part of the Nation would be nice too), and for them to really delve into Lost Cubs and Lunatics.
            I think one reason for that is unlike any outcasts in the other games (like Caitiffs or Anarchs in Vampires, or various Orphans in Mage), there was a mechanical factor that prevented werewolves from gaining power if they were outsiders - the need for renown to get rank so you can get more powerful Gifts. A PC who went down that route was giving up a lot of cool stuff in a way that Caitiffs never did.

            Also, one could imagine a group of anarchs or other loner vampires avoiding the Prince and his court and Camarilla society, yet those PCs could still have lots of fun doing stuff and still interacting with other vampires. It was much harder for a group of ronin to do so - the ostracism was too strong. Or the attraction to join the Nation and accept everything about it was too strong so the concept wouldn't last. It'd take a lot more work for the ST to integrate those PCs into a setting in the way a Vampire or Mage ST did not have to do with a similar group of PCs. I think it's entirely possible to do that, but some adjustments are needed. Having a good setting book, which takes some NPC ronin or Lost Cubs or Lunatics (not that these last two would be models for PCs, unless you're talking strictly backstory inspiration) and set them up in that situation for a model for the readers, would have helped that.

                                        The contradictory system for ronin doesn't help in having full filling outcast games, along with what exactly is renown.

                                        Starting with ronin themselves, it says in 'Pariahs: Guide to Outcasts' and implies in the core books of 1st, 2nd, and Revised that ronin have no rank. That's fine, but it doesn't present a system in how they earn further gifts, even though they can. Does this mean ronin can learn pretty much any gift then, regardless of rank, if they can convince a Garou or spirit that has access to them to teach/grant it? Then it says in Rite of the Lone Wolf that they simply lose tribal membership and only temporary renown they've accumulated up to that point, but they can in theory still advance themselves mechanically as any Garou. However in W20 it says ronin are now rank 0, which puts us back in the nebulous system mentioned before. On top of these systems, the ronin section in Pariahs makes a distinction between criminal outcasts and those who willingly leave for various reasons (penance, harano, etc.); such as the later can keep their name and tend to be more pitied and somewhat acknowledged unlike the exiled convict variety.

                                        This brings us back to the issues of renown. In WtA it's never made clear if renown is purely a social system established by the Nation or if its something more related to the Garou's relationship to the Umbra (unless W20 or some sourcebook I've never read clears this up). At times it seems like it could be either. I figured it has to do with their connection to their tribal totems, which backs up of their status in relationship to Gaian spirits and why they teach certain levels of gifts based off rank. Otherwise I don't see why spirits would bother acknowledging something so rigid that was purely a social constructed system by the Garou. Being outside this relationship then makes sense why outcasts would be on the outs and not trusted by spirits, along with how how the minor tribes outside of the Nation can function more or less as the big tribes (or BSDs in relationship to the Wyrm). It's why I like Forsaken being very upfront about what exactly renown is in relationship to Uratha society and the Spirit world.

                                        This is why I think a system for outcasts needs to have an update. Certainly most of the perks should go to those being a part of the Nation or at least part of a tribe or some kind of entity aligned with a totem spirit, but there should be a greater exploration of a system for those operating outside them. If renown is a part of being attached to a greater spirit, maybe a ronin who has a personal totem spirit can be vouched for to a lesser degree in dealing with spirits. Maybe those who voluntarily leave, but are still more or less loyal to the Nation, are more like Forsaken's Ghost Wolves. This could also help with those who maybe want to play a Lost Club that never joined Garou society, opening the game up to more varied style of play without fundamentally altering the core of what WtA is about.

                                        And yeah I think the term 'ronin' itself should not be its official designation. It works fine as a slang term, but I do prefer something like Ghost Wolf or the older canon term of Lone Wolf.
