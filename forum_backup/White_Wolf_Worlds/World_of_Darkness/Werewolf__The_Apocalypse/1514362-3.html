    In pre-W5 WtA, hispo is actually better for combat than crinos before you apply powers/etc. The higher Dex is better than the higher Str, and they get a damage bonus to bite attacks anyway to make up for the lower Str... while bites are -1 diff to hit.

    Crinos has a bigger advantage in flexibility because you get hands (or more importantly thumbs). Crinos can use Fetish weapons or guns meant to be mounted on military vehicles. There are way more Gifts that boost claw attacks than bite attacks. Crinos has better communication abilities (at least with other Garou). Basically, crinos embodied the concept of "war form" while hispo embodied the concept of "killing form," in the old books. Crinos was the favored form because warfare is about more than pouncing on opponents and ripping their throat out, and the Garou were at war, not just killing things.

    Yes, that did leave glabro out in the cold, but that was more because it was originally meant to be more of the classic "Wolf Man" of Lon Chaney Jr. that's obviously not-human. Editions vary a bit here, but never really made sufficient use of glabro as a functional combat form for when normal folks were around to give it a more specific niche. The love/hate relationship the fandom has for the Fair Glabro Merit probably says a lot here: people love it because it make glabro really useful, but hate it because everyone wants it making it feel more obligatory than a fun unique quirk.

    W5 is very clearly moving to WtF's concept of what each form is supposed to be for, and this is fairly evidence in the form bonuses/penalties. However, Forsaken also changed the themes werewolves deal with, and how the forms work in WtF (esp. 2e) are made to reinforce those different themes. Themes we haven't seen expressed as W5's themes.

                                        Originally posted by Psicose View Post

                                        Sure. So let me rephrase...

                                        In a mechanical sense, the forms are a bunch of bonuses and drawbacks. And so you want to create a balance between the number of bonuses and drawbacks but also not have bonuses that break every encounter with a foe in favor of the player or drawbacks that make something so unfun and/or punishing as to essentially be a motivation to avoid a particular mechanic/form. Creating balance in that sense is relevant.

                                        (Although, as an aside... I think one thing about W5 that will be interesting to see as well is the separation between "simplified rules" and "expanded rules". For that reason, they released this pdf called "Expanded Mechanics" a year ago. The goal was to take these "expanded mechanics" out of the corebooks and put them into this splat-neutral "Expanded Mechanics" pdf. And one part of this divide is combat. Like, take this rule for the Crinos-form and spending Willpower per turn, for example. The simplified version of combat is never longer than three turns. The expanded version goes for a more tactical approach of different kinds of actions you can take and the combat lasts turn after turn after turn... until things get resolved. Will the W5 corebook address this divide? I guess, we’ll see.)

                                        But the other side to these mechanical considerations of balance is the setting. And these oWOD-games have a lot going on in that department. Even with all the changes and "re-imagination, not continuation", you still have five Auspices, five Forms, two Breeds (human-born and wolf-born) and eleven Tribes. And the question is, from a story-perspective, why are there five Forms? How do five Forms fit into this setting? As mentioned, WtF 2E had "The wolf must hunt" and so each form is a stage of the hunt which then in turn informs how you mechanically depict the forms. But the Garou of WtA are not the Uratha of WtF. The focus of WtA isn't on "The wolf must hunt" but on the war against the Wyrm. And a Garou struggling with Rage isn't the same as a vampire trying to hold onto Humanity while struggling with their Hunger/Beast. The reason Garou are losing the war is that it isn't enough to just shift into Crinos and say "Fuck you, Wyrm!" (to quote the Ahroun-preview). So, making the Crinos-form an accessible physical-prowess-powerhouse shouldn’t be that big of a deal. The physical/martial prowess of Garou isn't the thematic focus of the game. With WtF all the hunting-prowess of Uratha is balanced with a great rogues-gallery of antagonists that challenge that. With WtA the challenge is that the project is about saving the world and things aren't looking so good. And how do you change that within this setting? Physical prowess alone certainly isn’t the solution. And part of the old setting were tragedies where said prowess was directed at the wrong targets even.

                                        With W5 it feels like the game is stepping away from being part of this epic world-saving project that's in crisis. You have things like "The Garou-Nation has fallen" and "mid-Apocalypse" but the mechanics also center the individual instead of worrying about the world. A lot of Rage-checks are mentioned on that char-sheet, then there are the WP-points for the Crinos-form and the Harano/Hauglosk-trackers are suggesting each Garou is in a constant struggle to either give up in despair or lose himself to Rage.

                                        And that brings us back to the Forms. I think the risk/benefit-analysis these Forms want to evoke is between the physical prowess of these forms - and the individual Garou's struggle with Rage. Not only in how it can make you lose control but in how it could advance your Harano- or Hauglosk-tracker. And that's very different from being a warrior for Gaia worrying about what needs to be done/how things in the Garou Nation have to change in order to save the world.
