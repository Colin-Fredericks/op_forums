              Accumulated lore has it that it's spiritual heritage, not genetics. The genetic component is basically just a marker; kin and changer (Fera is a word that Garou use to refer to non-Garou changers) will have the same markers, and be genetically indistinguishable, because there is no protein or group of proteins that can warp physics and cross over into the Umbra, even if Developmental Neogenetics Amalgamated insists otherwise and continues to bang its head against a brick wall trying to prove it (spoilers: they're bound for failure, forever, as long as they cling to their scifi fringe hypotheses).

              Lore also has it that if you clone a Garou or Fera, you get either kinfolk (if homid or born of an animal) or a stillborn corpse (if born of two of the same kind of changer, where applicable). Bringing to term the result of a Garou-Garou union or what have you requires that the mother has Rage and is capable of shapeshifting. This information, incidentally, is in the Revised Storyteller's Handbook.

                      The game is very explicit that Garou (and other Changing Breeds) are half spirit and half flesh. The game is also clear that it is not genes that determine if someone is Garou. In other words, it is the spirit side of Garou that determines things. Therefore no amount of genetic manipulation can create Garou. It is whether the person inherits the spirit component (which is not detectable or manipulable by science).

                      Whatever the laws are of "spirit inheritance" is, they are likely completely alien to genetic science.

                      To a lot lesser degree, this is probably true of kinfolk as well. While they are not made of spirit, there probably is some kind of spirit force that clings to them that marks them as different and allows them to pass on the spirit ancestry to full fledged Garou (and which sometimes allows kinfolk to have slight supernatural powers themselves).

                      Is there wiggle room? Of course there can be. The spirit side, while not part of real world science, could be affected by some kind of super science. The company DNA, whether it is the original version of Garou hunters being secretly supported by the Progenitors or the revised version of some kind of Weaver controlled company, should have some of that. And there are still certain genetic markers in Garou because they are still half flesh, though how that is tied to any supernatural powers is unclear. So if an ST wanted to run a plot along these lines, I think it's OK as long as it is shown that there are special reasons for it that would not be available to the greater public.

                                                      On genetic tampering with adults:

                                                      Genetic expression is an extremely complex phenomenon. The expression of a gene isn't just the result of its presence in your body, but the interaction of the gene with the current make-up of the cell it's in, plus external factors and other genes.

                                                      So while you can have a big impact by genetically tampering with gametes or a zygotic cell, the further development goes the less you can affect the organism through genetic tampering, even if you actually manage to alter it in every cell in the same way.

                                                      In short, even if it was just about genetics, tampering with the genes that make a person Kinfolk or Changer doesn't necessarily have an effect on a grown up, or even on a child. Depending on how the expression of the gene causes the state it can take time or be outright impossible to alter, and be so from any particular point in life starting at early cell differentiation.

                                                      Altering an adult's genetic make-up to resemble a wolf wouldn't result in a werewolf, a wolf hybrid or a wolf transformation. It would result in metabolic collapse. It doesn't have to be this drastic, either, completely changing a small percentage of an adult genetic code to resemble that of another person may also result in catastrophic metabolic collapse. Gene editing is, by all means, rocket science.

                                                      ====================

                                                      On the matter of genetic compatibility for generating offspring.

                                                      The ability of generating offspring derives primarily from chromosomal compatibility. Two gametes can only fuse if they have enough compatibility to have a hard maximum of 1 unpaired chromosome in the resultant cell. This depends on both number of chromosomes and form, as too distinct chromosomes won't pair at all.

                                                      What this means is that only following the laws of genetics it is impossible to have an organism that can breed true with two species too distinct. There is a caveat I'll talk about later. Humans have 23 chromosome pairs, wolves have 39. Even before comparing format it is mathematically impossible to viably pair them.

                                                      Not without "anomalous" help, where comes the caveat. In theory maybe you could have a species that can game this system to force the cross. But how would you do it without magic? And what's the role of the Kinfolk in this situation? This species need to have a bigger genetic code than any of the other two as it must house the distinct genes, and a way to produce viable gametes for both. Since Kinfolk are capable of producing Changers, they all must be able to pass down the whole anomalous code, so Kinfolk couldn't be genetically human, they'd have to be genetically Changer, which brings a huge amount of assumptions and consequences of their own.

                                                      Most importantly, though, it would be noticeable. In a world where Garou exist and follow strictly the laws of genetics, Kinfolk DNA wouldn't pass as anything remotely similar to human. The first time a Lost Kinfolk gets to have not even DNA per se, just their chromosomes under a microscope, it would be enough to blow up the veil royally. That thing would need natural properties that don't exist in any vertebrate, at the very least.

                                                      Since WtA isn't that world, and even the D.N.A. group barely managed to find a couple suspected genetic markers after specifically studying Garou and Kinfolk genetics, we can only conclude that the magical aspects are of considerable importance and maybe completely divorced from genetics (since although closely related, genetics isn't the same thing as hereditary or congenital).
