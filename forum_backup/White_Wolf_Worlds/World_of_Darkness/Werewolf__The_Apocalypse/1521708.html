    Thrassus/Exhibit #338 as an enemy faction in Werewolf

    08-30-2023, 09:02 PM
    Time to move on from vampires.

    I like creating distinct “factions” of the Wyrm because it introduces much needed variety in terms of antagonists. The way banes and fomori are usually presented, it is easy to think of them as generic and therefore lacking in interesting details. Building a faction around a great tentpole and building on that tentpole’s theme is important to how I run chronicles. Exhibit # 338 the Anti-Christ Serum is a Weaver fetish described in the Book of the Weaver. It is a fetish prison that might be holding one of the Talons of the Wyrm, Thrassus the Thrice Damned. I also want to point out that while we, the readers, are told of the (possible) association of Exhibit # 338 and Thrassus, this does not seem to be known to anyone IC.

    The Talons of the Wyrm were described in apocalyptic imagery when they were mentioned, yet somehow never became a major focus of the game. These should have been the basis of major Wyrm factions, not trivia or afterthoughts. So we have to do better. This thread is about using Exhibit # 338 to develop a full blown Wyrm faction STs can use in their chronicles.

    I think the Talons are a vastly underutilized enemy in Werewolf. While their names get mentioned (especially in early edition), the Talons don’t seem to ever drive anything itself. Instead, they are just plot coupons for some NPCs to look for, these NPCs don’t have strong connections to the Talon, and the forces of the Talon are just an individual NPC. You could easily switch out the Talon for some other plot coupon of the ST’s choice. To me, if you have a concept like the Talons of the Wyrm, there should be recognizable factions around them. Rage Across Russia gave the most details of any Talon, but there Koschei was clearly subservient to Baba Yaga.

    I think a key element is that any Wyrm faction needs to be somehow relevant in multiple eras and locations. Pentex is fine, but is limited by it only able to exist in the 20th century. Something like the Talons of the Wyrm should be found throughout historical eras and multiple human cultures. So we should keep this in mind when developing our ideas.​

    Exhibit # 338 is clearly based on John Carpenter’s 1987 film Prince of Darkness. It even gets referenced in-character as “The Anti-Christ Serum” is presumably the title of a similar movie by “Wes Carpenter” (a portmanteau of Wes Craven and John Carpenter). So we can start there as inspiration.

    And I mean just inspiration. We need to start somewhere, but we should not be limited to what is just in Prince of Darkness. The needs of an RPG is different from any movie, and adapting something to a new setting requires more. But I think it is a good beginning so let’s move on. Also, if you have ideas on a different direction be sure to post too.

      I do not find Prince of Darkness to be a very good movie, but it does have a cult following. Like most John Carpenter movies, it has a lot of evocative, creative, and intriguing ideas in it. For those that do not know, despite some religious trappings in the movie, this is not religious horror. It is cosmic horror based very much on the fringes of science and the unknown of a materialist, reductionist universe. But if you have not seen the movie, there will be spoilers, so decide whether you want to continue.

      These videos provide some important information for those who have not seen it.




      What are the elements of Carpenter’s Prince of Darkness that are most relevant? I would pick the following:
      • a terrifying and powerful entity is trapped within a device
      • the device, the liquid inside it, and its effects on the outside world are in defiance of physics
      • the entity is the essence of ultimate evil
      • dreams an important medium to communicate to characters about the entity (albeit these dreams are not from the entity, but other people in the future attempting to warn others)
      • the device appears to be not so much a prison as a timebomb that will go off based on certain celestial events
      • once the celestial event happens, there is a noticeable, but undescribable changes in earth and sky
      • mirrors can be used as a gateway to another dimension where even more dangerous entity is imprisoned
      • the entity, once it is partially released, is able to possess or take over the mind of people with weak willpower (in the movie this was nearby homeless people)
      • the entity seems to be able to control the natural forces of decay and degeneration in this world such as insects
      • the entity is able to seize outright control of people that become in physical contact with the tainted fluid that leaks out from the device
      • the entity inside the device (“the anti-christ”) will free another, more powerful entity trapped in the other dimension (“satan”)
      • this other entity has destroyed other worlds before arriving on this one
      • the other entity’s arrival on Earth will herald the end of humanity
      You might come up with a different list than me, and that's fine. But this is what I'll go off of.

          There’s obviously a lot relevant to Werewolf in general and the Talons of the Wyrm in specific. And while we do not need to directly translate these elements into Werewolf, there is a lot of inspiration to steal.

          The Weaver device/fetish is obviously a prison. Similar to how the meteorite in Rite of Passage is a prison for Narlthus (and all the Talons are somehow imprisoned). So instead of two entities, there’s just one - Thrassus.

          While Prince of Darkness gives it own explanation for the obvious technological prison (it was built by space aliens), that is not really an option for Exhibit # 338. So the Weaver device is much more anomalous. (Though to be honest despite calling it Weaver technology, it just appears to be a large glass container whose glass appears indestructible and does not react to the outside environment. That’s not what I normally think of when something is called a Weaver device. It might simply be considered a “Weaver device” because it seems to be in the hands of some organization which is either science based or possibly the actual Technocracy). An ST can either decide to keep that a mystery (which can be effective to players, though I personally dislike mysteries the ST herself doesn’t even know) or come up with some explanation that somehow makes sense to them (regardless of whether the PCs ever discover that).

          The dreams of Thrassus contained inside the prison shoudl obviously communicate its desires to the world around it. Narlthus’ prison was pretty much the same way in corrupting spirits, animals, and people around it in order to free it, and this included the Black Spiral Dancer Azarea in Rite of Passage who had ongoing dreams that propelled her to find Narlthus. So that matches well.

          However, instead of it being a time bomb meant to go off at a certain time, perhaps there are certain celestial events (that will happen at the time of the Apocalypse - something similar to the Red Star) which will allow Thrassus to be free. Unlike Narlthus’s prison, it is not possible to free the Talon earlier. Unfortunately, that also means it is inevitable that Thrassus will be released (at the worst time possible) and the Garou cannot prevent it.

          It is foretold that Thrassus will herald the end of humanity. But it isn’t quite clear when this happen or whether that can be prevented. It might just be an event that will only happen in the late stages of the Apocalypse after lots of other things have gone wrong. Or that the Garou (or others) might still be able to prevent this should they win the final battle. Or that this is just somnething that will happen on its own in a long distant future of geological time. Maybe humanity will survive, but in a different form; or that it won’t be limited to humanity? This shouldn’t be something relevant to most chronicles, just a terrifying flavor to the faction. In various Lovecraft stories, there are sometimes mentions that the earth is to be cleared off or similar. I like the idea that one of Thrassus’ (presumed) roles will be to be clear humanity from Gaia, perhaps in preparation of some event or arrival.

          Originally posted by Dunwich Horror

          ​

          We have some direct correspondences to concepts like fomori created by Thrassus. So that is an easy and obvious match. We’ll talk more about this in a later post.

          I will use the above as the basis for Thrassus and its fetish prison. So how do we go about building an actual faction around this, since the Talon itself (as a character) will likely never appear in game?

          The one thing I know is that I want any cult of Thrassus to be a terrifying opponent. I don’t mean from a combat perspective necessarily. I mean from a lore perspective and how Garou should perceive the threat of Thrassus and its thralls. As a Talon of the Wyrm, it should be seen with a healthy bit of trepidation, concern, and alarm. It should have some perceived role in the Apocalypse and an accompanying dread as a result. Its minions may not necessarily be any more difficult to fight or disgusting to witness as other Wyrm minions, but they should be distinguishable from other factions and have an identifable theme. While this faction in the pre-apocalypse may not be too terrible, there should be a sense that it will become very powerful once Thrassus escapes.

Not just for the changes themselves, but the realization by Garou that it means the powers of Thrassus is active among them. But we will need to come up with some imagery and flavor text to create this new bane.

                          I want to describe now the Ferectoi that could be associated with this. Ferectoi are more like a concept than a fully described fomori. As written, Ferectoi are mentioned to be very individual with unique powers given to each one. They are not one size fits all, but supposed to be very customizable.

                          So the Ferectoi of Thrassus won’t be like Damien from the Omen movies. I think they should be more like the Spawn of Nyogtha in the Call of Cthulhu RPG. Those are offspring between humans and the Great Old One Nyogtha. They are born looking human, but sometime between their thirties and sixties they degenerate into shapeless abominations much like Nyogtha itself. While initially just like human children (except that they have viscuous black ichor instead of blood), around puberty Nyogtha fills their dreams with his thoughts and visions, and these hybrids eventually succumb to Nyogtha’s will. They then seduce others into their dark god’s cult.

                          They’ve only appeared in a few scenarios, but are mentioned in any of the big monster compilations for CoC. There is a good summary of everything Nyogtha in the CoC RPG on this blog post.

                          https://mdcls.blogspot.com/2015/07/t...ld-not-be.html

                          I think that fits perfectly for these Ferectoi. They’ll have several decades of appearing human after they fall to Thrassus’s will, maybe some years afterwards where they degenerate but an still disguise themself, and then afterwards they are just horrid, inhuman looking monsters that can hide under the earth, waiting for the Apocalypse and perhaps ruling some of the Thrassus cults behind the scenes.

                          This does not mean all Thrassus Ferectoi have the exact same powers. But there should be a variety of powers that overlap between them (providing a common theme), and then some level of specific customization.

                              Now that we have some idea of who or what makes up the faction, we can figure out what that faction may be like. The movie doesn’t show any long standing cult. But the idea of an ancient evil, slumbering until certain celestial events happen, and its cult being drawn to it by dreams have some obvious antecedents. The most prominent in my mind is the cult of Cthulhu as described in HP Lovecraft’s story The Call of Cthulhu, but it's an old trope by now and plenty of other examples.

                              This youtube video does a good quick overview of the main points. But like with the movie of Prince of Darkness, we shouldn't be looking to duplicate things exactly, just draw from it for inspiration. We should always try to put our own spin on things.


                              The cult is formed mostly by those minds weak and sensitive enough to receive the dreams of Thrassus and be influenced by them. The cult, therefore, is probably mostly made up of dregs. That’s fine because Ferectoi can provide any leadership needed for the cult. This doesn’t mean ordinary cultists are imbeciles or too insane to do anything, but they probably aren’t the kind of people others are going to choose to follow. They are not on top of the social hierarchy. This does not mean that only dregs receive such dreams, just that those of stronger will, character, or intent won’t be influenced by them. Individual reactions to these dream sendings are varied. Neither does it mean that anyone actually understands these dream sendings or what Thrassus may actually want. They just experience them and come to their own conclusions.

                              The cult is ancient, going all the way back shortly after the Talons were defeated and imprisoned. These cultists have existed since the Stone Age before the Impergium was ever instituted. So the Garou have had long experience fighting them, just as they have experience fighting the Garou. Even if human cultists by themselves cannot pass on such knowledge to the next generation of the cult, the banes associated with the cult will remember.

                              The cult is worldwide because the dreams of Thrassus can reach around the world. Thus despite the cult appearing in a diverse variety of human cultures, there is remarkable uniformity to the cult in its practices, imagery, and rites because they all get it first hand from Thrassus. So the outer appearance of the cult might be different in China versus Europe versus Peru versus Arabia, but pierce the surface and the signs are there.

                              Although the cult was contacted by Thrassus through dreams, he isn’t actually commanding them to do anything. He’s in slumber within the fetish prison. Cultists are influenced by Thrassus, but he is not actively instructing them. So while they attempt to please him, it isn’t clear what does please Thrassus although after some twelve thousand years or more, individual cult groups have their own ideas. It is actually the Ferectoi that provide any kind of instructions (although this too are probably just guesses than true knowledge).

                              The cult’s practices are loathsome and aberrant, and thus relegated to the fringes of society where it is easier for cultists to hide their crimes and not attract attention. Between PENTEX, vampires, Seventh Generation, and many other groups that operate in the socio-economic heights, I think it’d be interesting to approach things from a different angle. The threat of Thrassus comes from places where you expect powerlessness.

                              The cult looks forward to the day when Thrassus awakens once again because he can then formally instruct his disciples in all manner of things so mere men could become like Thrassus. Then they’ll learn secret knowledge, arcane power, new ways to shout, kill, rape, and pillage - to be beyond good and evil like Thrassus itself. Then Thrassus would rule the earth, or whatever is left of it.

                                  We need something more. This is Werewolf after all, and we need some kind of physical threat to the Garou. So this is an area where we have to look beyond Prince of Darkness while hoping what we’ve gleaned can point to a productive direction.

                                  One area where I want to look are the works of William Hope Hodgson. His works display the kind of hostile universe that Thrassus implies. It also has a setting where the normal laws of physics seem to degrade and breakdown in the presence of those hostile forces in works like The Nightland, The House on the Borderland, and the cases of Carnacki: Ghost Finder. That kind of entropy breaking down physical, spiritual, and human laws fits the theme of Thrassus.


                                  A common element in Carnack’s horror stories are terrifying pig-like creatures. They show up in both House on the Borderland and the Carnack story The Hog. I mention it because it also shows up here, and I don’t think there was no inspiration of one on the other.


                                  This is something I think resonates with the savage horror of Werewolf: the Apocalypse. But they can’t be like orcs in a fantasy world. The World of Darkness still needs to be similar in appearance to our own. Pig monsters can’t be showing up in downtown Chicago or besieging a small town. So I would say the Swine Things are not inhabitants of our world. But extra-dimensional alien invaders from outside reality are good. They are attracted to the “psychic energy” of humanity. They feed on that, not material flesh and blood.

                                  So if they are not from our world, where do the Garou encounter them? I think Chimares are a good place. In the Hodgson story the Hog, it was in a victim’s dreams where he was being psychically devoured. Perhaps we extend this to them devouring other forms of psychic energy like Gnosis as well so they are a threat to other spirits. But they have a particular taste for the human psyche. While we may want to keep them reminiscent of pigs, they are obviously not pigs or pig spirits. Just piglike. So when naming them, we my want to avoid including anything like pig or swine, just to make clear to the players that this is something unnatural and not native to earth. These are not corrupted Gaian spirits. Of course, if you don’t like swine imagery yourself, you can change their appearance to something else. While I like that imagery myself, I would not want that to be the identifiable thing about them, so I’ll use a more netural name for them like Gnostic Marauders.

                                      Going with the idea that Thrassus is such an abomination its presence can distort laws of physics and the laws of Gaia, maybe there are parts of the earth that are so impacted. And that these areas can produce aberrations that should not exist - thus forms certain kinds of penumbral Wyrm domains, although I don’t think they should be wandering far from their domains in the Penumbra itself. Just at special extra-dimensional fault lines that lead to cracks in reality, perhaps partially dependent on certain celestial events so the threat comes and goes. Garou just can’t dogpile on known Thrassus domains because these domains never stay long. They arrive, cause havoc, and then suddenly disappear. I think that goes a long way at creating the sense the Garou are not in control ofthe situation. Yeah, they can congratulate themselves that the local threat is “gone”, everyone knows the Garou had nothing to do with it leaving.

                                      Somewhere out there in the Dream Zone is the so-called Dream House of Thrassus. That is not where Thrassus is from, but it’s the terrifying dreamscape that embodies the collective madness of Thrassus’s outreach to the sleeping minds of its earthly minions and victims. This is where the Chimares and Domains connect to. Tribal legends of the Stargazers and Uktena have the names of brave Garou whose packs voyaged to the Dream House of Thrassus in the hopes of learning information on their foe. None have come back. Because such Dream Realms can border and intersect the Far Dreaming of Changeling, on rare occasions the Gnostic Marauders manage to invade the Dreaming itself. When this happens, the Fae always call packs of Stag to help.

                                      As for the Penumbral Domains themselves, some kind of ruins or underground tunnel imagery works well as that calls back to the Hodgson stories and Darkest Dungeon. But again, you may want something more imaginative.

                                          We also need some more opponents in the physical world, especially some thing that can provide some kind of physical challenge to the Garou. They don’t need to be as physically tough as Garou, just enough that players can enjoy the fight. We do have the Hollow Men fomori already, but I think we need something else as well as those fomori are not noteworthy fighters.

                                          My inspirations here go back to the viscuous green liquid contained in the device in Prince of Darkness. One aspect of Prince of Darkness is the green liquid inside leaving its prison and infecting others. It’s a strong image, and I’d like to use it. That first brings to mind the fomori power Viscuous Form, which is a power I’ve never used before in game. Viscuous Form then brings to mind gelatinous horror monsters found in Call of Cthulhu like the Formless Spawn of Tsathoggua. And mention of Tsathoggua reminds me of a story Clark Ashton Smith wrote that included the figure Knygathin Zhaum. He was a human looking monster which would come back from the dead as strange liquid pools from his body would reform into more monstrous versions that look less and less human. That’s a good horror image and would be a good basis for a fomori. That story can be found here:

                                          http://www.eldritchdark.com/writings...t-of-athammaus

                                          These would be very similar to the Ferectoi based on the Spawn of Nyotha, but perhaps a bit more physically powerful, less intelligent, and not as long lived. Good disposable henchmen types for the Ferectoi. So what kind of powers would be good to give this kind of fomori? Going with the disgusting liquid theme, here are the fomori powers that seem most evocative to me. We may want to only use some of them and not all of them.
                                          • Viscuous Form
                                          • Noxious Miasma
                                          • Slobbersnot
                                          • Tar Skin
                                          • Toxic Secretions
                                          I also like the idea that perhaps the fomori degenerates over time into a more liquid, plastic form as it takes damage or perhaps is “killed”. Maybe that its own unique power or some variant of the Regeneration power. These are not very offensive minded powers with the possible exception of Toxic Secretions (but perhaps combined with Tar Skin, it’d be an evocative way to deal damage to Garou). So we may either want to give the fomori additional powers (like Mega Attributes or Unnatural Strength, or do the traditional thing and just outfit them with weapons (probably also including silver).

                                          I don’t want these fomori to go one on one with a Garou. Just be an interesting opponent that could provide some trouble. Bad dice rolls can make many fights perilous, so even weaker fomori can still be dangerous to Garou. But you may have other ideas.

                                          So how does someone become this kind of fomori? It obviously cannot be limited to the time of Thrassus’s release in the Apocalypse or being physically near Exhibit # 338 (like how the liquid in Prince of Darkness does). We want a fomori that can be found and fought in our chronicles. Instead, I think small portions of the green liquid must somehow materialize far away and at any time. Why? Because the laws of physics don’t apply in either space or time for Thrassus. That Thrassus will one day be released in the future at some place (or was on Earth after it first arrived long ago) means its liquid form CAN appear anytime and anyplace, even in the past (or future). Maybe this is done through the Warp Reality charm by its special spirit servitors if the STs want something that makes sense to them or explained to the players, and perhaps it is done only under certain rites by the cult.

                                          Anyway, human becomes in contact with the Thrassus liquid, and it taints them leading to bane possession and transforming into this fomori. This is probably not random, but part of cultist activities. Perform certain rites and the green liquid of Thrassus manifests. They would make good protectors of the local cults once the Garou do discover them and send in a pack to wipe them out. Do we need a new bane to be possessing these fomori? Or just use an existing one - like the new bane with the Warp Reality charm?

                                          I've come pretty far laying out some ideas for a faction based around Thrassus. So if people would like the thread to continue, it'd be good to hear some commentary and new ideas.

They feed on that, not material flesh and blood.
                                              Personally, I kind of like the idea of fighting actual, physical pig monsters. It not only reminds me of Darkest Dungeon, as stated before, but also of Weird West. In that game, pigmen are creatures made from adding the brains and hearts of humans to a body of stitched up pig parts, and then animated in a dark ritual. (The feeding of souls to a tree is also involved).

                                              I'm imagining Wyrmish ritual practices engaged in by low-class Thrassus cults among slughterhouse/meat-packing workers, pig farmers, or butchers. Folks with plenty of access to whole or partial pig carcasses, and have frequent contact with all manner of blood and death, on an industrial scale. At least in modern times, of course. You could easily have this kind of "make pig monster" cults popping up at any point in history where hogs are butchered in abundance.

                                              The cult stitches together the form of a creature out of pig bits. Often humanoid, though it's hardly limited to that. (You could go WILD with the swine horrors, and it would be appropriate for the more splatterpunk aspects of WtA.). These are then implanted with lesser Banes associated with Thrassus, as an animating force. A kind of primitive form of artificial Fomori, developed ad hoc long before the advent of genetic engineering. Pentex in modern times considers such handiwork terribly crude.

                                              But the pig-stitcher cult doesn't care. "It's the way grand-daddy did it, and that's good enough for me".

                                              What do you do with frankenstein pig monsters? I mean aside from brute labor and defense, of course. I have a few thoughts.

                                              One aspect of the pigmen from Weird West is that they're hollow shells tied to people ritually sacrificed. In game, it's possible to free the souls put in the tree (as one of the playable PCs had done, being an intelligent pigman), so they come to inhabit the bodies. For our cult, we can perhaps suppose the Bane spirits, through dreams, promise a way to cheat death, or to bring one's loved ones back to life. Craft a surrogate body, and through ritual draw the soul of the departed to inhabit it. Is it really the soul of the deceased being brought back, or just a Bane masquerading as such? It doesn't matter that much, for our purposes. Although I imagine a person who returns in a Bane-riddled pig-Fomori body may go insane before long, assuming they weren't insane to begin with. I do like the idea of a cult harboring half-mad swine-men, the elders of the cult, persisting for centuries. (Indeed, that's VERY Lovecraft).

                                              (Also imagine them returning the souls of dangerous individuals, like serial killers, for use as agents. Maybe the Bane grants the pig Fomori powers to walk unseen among mortals, or appear less like swine stitched into the rough shape of men. Allowing them to do the cult's work out in the world.)

                                              Another reason you might create a Fomori pig construct is as an oracular tool. I'm imagining Lord of the Flies, where the rotting pig head stuck on a stake seems to speak to characters. Imagine the cult having one of their own, a swine head oracle granting predictions or advice. Claiming to speak wisdom or the will of Thrassus.

                                              (I'm reminded that the pig head from Lord of the Flies was visibly attracting, well, flies. Another insect parallel to add to the Thrassus pile. Come to think of it, it also reminds me a bit of the "Ribcage of Vrghhrh", a "magic item" from this blog post.)

                                              A more messed up reason for making pig-men is, of course, as depraved sex slaves. Thrassus invades the dreams of those with a particularly depraved mind, and teaches them to make a creature they can then have sex with. We need not dwell overmuch on this one, save that's its very in keeping with the Call of Cthulhu idea of cultists waiting for their eldritch god to return and teach them new ways to sin.

                                              And naturally, making pig monsters can be fairly useful if you think the apocalypse is nigh, and you need troops to fight the Final Battle. It doesn't matter if the apocalypse isn't nigh (yet), like in earlier historical periods. All that matters is the cult believes they need tons of monsters for what they view as an imminent fight. Perhaps amassing so many pig Fomori is a requirement for releasing Thrassus (or they think so).

                                              Alternatively, the cult may be playing the long game. Creating pig mutants and then pickling them in large jars. Amassing them slowly over years or generations, so they'll have an army to release when the Final Battle comes. Imagine a series of tunnels beneath a pig farm or in a barn, full of shelves of grotesque pig creatures suspended in chemicals. Break one open, and the creature might awaken and attack whatever's nearest. If the Garou start smashing jars, deliberately or accidentally, they may find themselves overwhelmed by a press of squealing flesh.

                                                  One thing to add about the pig Fomori: what, exactly, do the Fomori/Banes WANT?

                                                  We've already gone over what the cultists want and use them for, or at least what they think they're getting out of the deal of crafting them. But what's the Bane's motivation here, aside from vague guesses as to Thrassus's will? What urges propel them to action?

                                                  I'm think three: Gluttony, Sloth, and Waste.

                                                  Gluttony because of the classic association of pigs with eating. To eat to excess is to make a pig of yourself. To eat more than one's allotted share is to hog it all. Like the pigmen from Weird West, bereft of human intelligence, the pig Fomori eat whatever they can get their hands on. Whatever they are allowed to eat. Pigs are clever, so the cults they serve must pay close attention, lest they sneak food they aren't allowed. Venturing from the compound to steal crops, livestock, and people for their private larders. The cleverest may even drag living people back and force them into cages, feeding them enough to fatten them for the slaughter. They learned from the best, it seems.

                                                  Pig Fomori rarely share their meals. They're greedy bastards. Wholesome qualities like sharing or a sense of proportionality are decidedly unWyrmish. They're qualities of law, of morality. When Thrassus returns, they'll be unburdened of such concepts, and taught new ways to consume.

                                                  Sloth because a domesticated pig is the manifestation of a post-hunter-gatherer lifestyle. Humans didn't want to go hunting, like a normal predator, so they invented animal husbandry. Turned the feral boar into livestock, so they can have their bacon at home. This descent into indolence is reciprocal, as the pigs grew fat and lazy from having their meals handed to them in a trough. Just as a pig is fattened for the slaughter, humanity and their world of soft edges and secure systems is being fattened for the slaughter of Thrassus.

                                                  Those pig Fomori used for labor must be whipped into action. Left to their own devices, they would be indolent, save in the act of procuring food. They're just clever enough to cheat, and find ways of achieving goals with minimal effort. But they're ultimately sloppy. Being careful takes effort. They'd rather take what they want, and leave others to clean up the mess. This is often what attracts Garou attention to the cult.

                                                  The virtue of laziness is a gift they relish spreading to everyone else. They want others to wallow and let themselves, and their world, go to seed. The Weaver-artifice that binds their master, like the Weaver systems that bind this modern world, break down when there is no longer the will to maintain them. Perhaps the prerequisite to releasing Thrassus lies in getting the world to stop caring. Stop caring that the seas boil and the skies burn. Stop caring that carefully maintained infrastructure crumbles, and carefully shepherded environments are choked by ecological collapse and evasive species. (Invasive species like the Old World Pig is to North America).

                                                  Waste, because a pig Fomori is nothing but a waste. Waste of a pig life (and probably a human life sacrificed to make it?). Waste of perfectly good food. A waste of time, because who goes to the trouble of sewing together parts of a pig carcass? Waste of a world, that is having the Talons released on it, bit by bit. They love to accumulate waste - garbage, feces, etc. - and horde it like treasure. They'll wallow in their own or others' filth. They'll break things, or people, simply because the destruction of perfectly good things amuses them. If they can't eat it, they'll throw it out, seemingly out of spite. There is no conservation here, no sustainability. They will use up everything - even their own would-be cultist masters - then leave it a ruin, of no use to anyone.

                                                  Only the decomposers will feast, when the pig Fomori are done. (That insect connection again). They have powers related to summoning pests, so they can break down organic materials into rotten sludge and have them eaten by flies. On some level the pig Fomori are alive. But on another level, they're dead meat brought back into animation. They might not be currently rotting, but they carry the memory of being rotting meat, attracting flies. (If the layer of filth on their skins didn't do it). Many are living hives, puking or shitting swarms of flies or maggots that do their bidding.

                                                  The cults that make them are so often the lower classes. As the Powers That Be (whether Capital, Monarchy, or Fascism) hollow out the world, those below become more obsessed with breaking the machine down. (A machine for pigs, you might say). But while others may try to dismantle the machine so a more equitable and sustainable society might be created in its place, the Banes, Thrassus, and the Wyrm just want it all brought down and left to rot. To take the edifices, of society, nature, and humanity, and lay it to waste.
