    My experience with this pack (because nothing points out that templates are from one pack except those from RoP).

    As for fetishes I assumed that Whisperer's Spirit Tracer was a gift from Uktena sept for the Sept of the Green to accept him but Mother Larissa decided that cubs shouldn't pay for sept membership so she let him keep it. Curtis Harmony Flute was in form of a referee whistle to better accent his Glass Walker heritage.

    First me and my friend run this story for a group of new players whose only experience was Talisman board game. We didn't modify those templates but expanding their background (such as the name of Janet's band). After being cast out they didn't believe that Sept of the Green was responsible for murdering Wendigo cubs so they choose to stay close to the Winter Wolf sept. Later, because of the encounter with the tainted bear and a bane that haunted the waiter's son, they were sure that something is wrong. After a few days, when Azaera found out that they weren't killed by members of the sept, she send a lone BSD Gaillard (played by my friend, then First Officer and one of the best STs that I met; because of that none of the players expected treachery) who told the PCs that something is indeed wrong and that he and his buddies wants to correct it. None of the characters has Sense Wyrm gift so they believe him (after all the dialogue was run so well that Linda couldn't use Truth of Gaia gift) and ended in the mine. Of course the showed some concerns about it but as the BSD said "who could expect defenders of Gaia in a place like that". Ultimately they all ended among Dancers.

    The second time I run this characters it was for a group of experienced players, who played CP2020 and Vampire mostly. They also modified the characters bio (some of them to the extremes; one of the players assumed that, as Curtis has an earring in his right ear, he surely is homosexual which later led to a situation when, during the moot, Curtis was accused of mating with a female garou he shouted in front of an entire sept and a large pack of Fenris visitors "but I'm gay"; this was one of the best roleplayed scene in my life). After being cast out they assumed that it will be a Bone Gnawer style Rite of Passage (you know, far away from home, without any resources, and you have to return). With Billy's help they survived the snowstorm and returned to NY. Later a Silent Strider send for them returned with the news of Accolon's death and Azarea's treasure. After that they had a new Rite of Passage and went on. But the pack basically splited: Snarl at Fleas went to the Sentinel Sept in the wild, Janet founded a new band with other garou and a kinfolk, Linda returned to college, Whisperer made his home in Central Park and Curtis tried to live as far from garou affairs as he could. It was also because the players were older than the first time, they had normal life, work and families. Of course sometimes, when Gaia called, they reformed the pack, but basically from then on it was mostly Janet who run the show.

                    And so, back to 1994, right about the same time the 2nd ed core book was being released. Bone Gnawers was written by Sam Chupp, and once again Joshua Timbrook is doing the art for the templates.

                    Ragabash Rat Fink (Homid Ragabash)

                    ::Sigh:: So, this begins what will be a recurring thing, both in this book and later Tribebooks. Namely, just calling a template's concept by it's Auspice and/or Camp. Or in this case, both of those and nothing else. For some reason, it's the Ragabash who keep getting this in the future Tribebooks.
                    Just personally, I find this rather annoying, mainly from a creative standpoint. Besides being a bit lazy, it doesn't really say much about the character in question. It's just what you picked from column A and column B on the takeout menu. Nothing at all about how those particular character traits manifest themselves nor how the character might view themselves. A good concept name is, along with the image, the first thing that draws you to a template. This should have been called "The Invisible Man" or "Trash Collector".

                    Anyway ....

                    So this guy has always been a very nosy individual. And even a little bit of a creep, not above blackmail. It's not specified, but he's apparently from a small town or the suburbs. After his First Change, he was taken to the city. It doesn't say anything about how or why he's a Bone Gnawer. Just that he was put to work, somewhat unwillingly, as a janitor for a Pentex company. (This was back when the entire Garou Nation apparently knew about Pentex.) And now they're trying to kill his family. That or brainwash them.
                    I'm a bit confused about how that's supposed to work. Surely he didn't sign up using his real name and SSN. His Equipment even lists multiple fake IDs. So how would they know who his family even is? And if they're being attacked right now, as this write up seems to indicate, why wouldn't he try to drop everything and return home?

                    Once you get past that, the write up is a fairly solid concept. He works as a janitor, and in the process collects assorted keys, phone numbers, floppy discs, print outs, and other detritus. He's effectively invisible, both in the sense of "the help" being totally ignored by those in power, and literally with the Blur of the Milky Eye gift.

                    Equipment is a disposable camera and a well used pocket tape recorder (I really do miss the 90s sometimes), as well as the previously mentioned fake IDs.

                    The image is of a somewhat scrawny and disheveled young man with glasses wearing janitor coveralls, with a broom/mop and trash can. It looks fine and represents what is described.

                    For Attributes, Physicals are tertiary. And are 1/4/1.

                    Damn it.

                    Socials are secondary, with 3/4/1.

                    ...

                    Mental is primary, with 5/3/2.

                    This just feels all kinds of broken. I mean, I get the idea of a character being physically unimpressive, but having a pair of ones feels excessive. (Also, I've done custodial work in my lifetime; it requires a certain degree of stamina.) 1/3/2 would seem more reasonable.
                    With Socials, there's nothing about him that feels like it would indicate above average Charisma. And if you're wanting to blend into the background, being completely average and unremarkable seems better than being notably unattractive ("tend to attract the hostility of others" as the core book puts it). 2/4/2 would probably work fine here.
                    With Mental, if you want to keep that 5 in Perception, I think having Intelligence 2 and Wits 3 would be more suitable.

                    For Abilities, Talents are primary with one extra dot from freebies. 4 in Subterfuge, 3 each in Alertness and Streetwise, 2 in Dodge, and 1 each in Empathy and Primal Urge.
                    Skills are tertiary with four extra dots from freebies. 4 in Stealth, 2 each in Firearms and Repair, and 1 in Drive.
                    Knowledge is secondary with two extra dots from freebies. 4 in Investigation, 3 in Computer, and 1 each in Linguistics, Medicine, Politics, and Science.

                    Overall, these seem fine. It's a decent spread of abilities that fit what he does, as well as giving him some combat effectiveness.

                    Backgrounds are 3 each in Allies and Contacts. Contacts seems especially fitting here. I confess that I miss characters having Pack Totem as a background from Rite of Passage. Also, I sort of expected him to have a Mentor of some sort, just from the fact that he was apparently forced into his current role, and you'd think he'd have some sort of handler or something.

                    Rage, Gnosis, and Willpower are all at their default values (1, 1, and 4, respectively).

                    Overall, this is a fairly solid concept. I wish it had actually be given a name instead of just stringing together two traits, yes. And the Attributes feel really broken, yes. And the family thing doesn't make a lot of sense, yes. But I think all of those can be easily overlooked, fixed, or ignored.
                    I also wish I knew more about how this guy ended up in the Tribe he did, as well as how he ended up in the Rat Fink camp. (I've never been really wild about that name; I think "Muck Rakers" or some variation on that might've been better.)

                        I think the Rat Fink is a fairly solid template, but I understand your dislike of the name being "Auspice Camp". Your suggestion of "The Invisible Man" would be better. Tribebooks were expected to fill out a lot of details first mentioned in the original Players Guide so it makes sense to use them to explore the various camps. But it could lead to a lot of unoriginal templates. As you mentioned before, showing 10 templates instead of 5 could have showcased more originality. I don't think the "camps" collectively comprised the majority of each tribe so I would have liked templates that made no mention of them as well.

                        Character sheet wise, I think it is fairly solid, but agree with all of your improvements.

                        Of all the Bone Gnawer templates, this is my favorite. One thing I really like about it is that it is not necessary for this character to change form unless absolutely needed in combat. As an ST, I see players make all sorts of unnecessary changes to form simply because they can. They change form not only when it is not necessary to do so, but when it could endanger the Veil. I find it extremely annoying. But as written, this character likely rarely changes unless it makes sense to do so. I understand the appeal of playing a werewolf is to change form. I would just like it to make sense when players decide to do so. This is a solid concept given the parameters of the setting and this character idea.

                        I think this would be a great character concept for an urban based chronicle.

                        My one big complaint of the tribebook is that it seems to discount rural Bone Gnawers despite introducing the Hill Folk camp. Too often the tribe seems to be "unemployed bums in the city" as opposed to "runts of the Garou Nation" which I think is more appropriate. Historically the tribe's kinfolk is probably more "oppressed peasants" than "urban homeless", but that is not what the tribebook evokes.

                                            Rust Shaman (Homid Theurge)

                                            This one is fairly interesting. Someone who was apparently touched by the spirits of the city almost from birth and instinctively aware of the animist nature of the World of Darkness. Their First Change started while in a subway station and they fled deeper into the city's underground, eventually to the sewers. There they apparently met Rat herself (or at least an avatar) and was shown secret keys to the city, and now serves as her prophet. Now they wander the city, telling others what the streets of destiny hold for them.

                                            I use they because, even thought the artwork is of a male, there's not really anything in the write up to indicate gender and this could just as easily work as a female (or any sort of non-binary, as that does have traditional associations with shamanism in some cultures).

                                            The character is described as seeming somewhat out of it to others, but this is because they are constantly aware of the spiritual aspect of the world. They also engage, in a seemingly OCD manner, in constant drumming and other rituals directed toward the various city spirits of taxi cabs, fire hydrants, pay phones (again, the 90s), and the like. They also regularly talk to said spirits, something that probably looks like schizophrenia to humans but is probably fairly normal to most Garou by the time they hit Rank 2.

                                            Equipment is a massive key ring full of random keys that may or may not be important, and a handful of random paper detritus. I feel like maybe they should have a staff of some sort. Having it be a Fetish would be optional, but I just think it would be ascetically cool for a modern urban prophet.

                                            The image is of an almost stereotypical early 90s homeless man. He appears fairly older than normal for a starting PC, being partially bald and having a long beard that feels like it's probably grey. He has an interesting necklace with some assorted junk hanging from it. As with a noticeable amount of Timbrook's artwork, it's a little different to guess his race, and arguments could be made for multiple directions. Overall, it's a pretty decent image. Though I can't help but wonder what this might've looked like if presented as a 20-something female.

                                            He's not part of any Camp. That actually makes him the only one of these five who isn't associated with one. Again, we come back to the whole "I wish there'd been 10 instead of 5" thing.

                                            For Attributes, Physicals are secondary, with 1/3/4. Sam Chupp seems to have some sort of weird fixation on giving Bone Gnawers attribute ratings of 1. (And you haven't seen anything yet; just wait for the Ahroun.) Socials are tertiary with 3/2/1. Against, I don't know what the deal is with Bone Gnawers having 1s in Appearance. I think Knightingale may be correct and it's some kind of Nosferatu-lite thing. Mental is primary with 4/2/4.
                                            The Mental spread is fine. I think the other two would be better being flipped. Have Physicals as all 2s, and Socials as either 3/3/2 or 4/2/2. This keeps his Charisma high for his prophet role and but doesn't conflate his disheveled clothing and possible lack of regular hygiene for actual physical ugliness.

                                            For Abilities, Talents are primary, with 3 each in Alertness and Primal Urge, 2 each in Dodge, Empathy, and Streetwise, and 1 in Expression.
                                            Skills are tertiary, with 3 in Stealth and 2 in Survival.
                                            Knowledge is secondary with one extra dot from freebies, for a 4 in Rituals, and 3 each in Enigmas and Occult.
                                            For the most part, this is fine. However, I question the lack of Performance given how the write up specifically mentions his drumming. I'd also sort of expect his Streetwise to be a little higher.

                                            Backgrounds are 4 in Rites and 1 in Allies. It's not specified who the Ally is. It could be some sort of spirit, maybe.
                                            I sort of feel like he could benefit from some Contacts or else more dots in Allies. I think this would fit in with the mention of his equipment including a bunch of business cards, notes, and other stuff, where they could represent people he's actually assisted through his role as a prophet and now help him on occasion with information or other favors.
                                            He has one unspent freebie point, and I wonder if it was supposed to be spent on Fetish. The book has a new level one Fetish, The Lost Keyring, that matches the description of the keyring in his equipment, and would seem appropriate for this character.

                                            With Gifts, I really think that Persuasion would be more useful for this character than Smell of Man.

                                            His Rage is the default 2. Gnosis is a whopping 7. This is where the vast bulk of his freebies went. Willpower is the default 4.
                                            I'm sort of torn here. On the one hand, having such a massive level of spiritual attunement certainly fits the character. But on the other, this seems perhaps a little excessive. I really do feel that at least some of those points could've been better spent on addition Ability or Background dots.

                                            Overall, this is a really interesting character. I enjoy how it plays into the animistic nature of the setting, as well as how certain behaviors which make total sense in one context (urban shamanism) seem nonsensical or off-putting in another (modern rational materialism). I like the idea of a Homid who has been aware of the spiritual aspect of the world for their entire life long before the Change.

                                            I will however point out the obvious: This is also essentially a Dreamspeaker Mage reskinned as a werewolf. Not that this is necessarily bad. I think it's also an interesting potential Mage character. (I'm also almost 100% certain that it, as well as the Trashheap totem in this book, are primarily responsible for the inspiration of the narrator of the revised Dreamspeakers book.)

                                            So, I like this one. It's a slightly offbeat idea that actually makes a lot of sense in context of the game and the Tribe, and certainly not something I would have thought of on my own back in 1994.

                                                        Overall I got an idea why Bone Gnawers templates have Appearance 1. During my games this stat not only describe how your character looks like but also the general "aura" which surrounds it. So:

                                                        As for Ragabash: he is a janitor. Just think of that: could you call Mike Myers or Rick Moranis totally unattractive? And now think about Brian Thompson. He has a very brutal, almost primitive, face.
