      For the most part I think Black Dog was put in the game as a kind of meta joke (as were the fomori their game books create). I'm sure it's roughly as popular (or, in a sense, unpopular) as the World of Darkness series is in our reality. Of course you can adjust as needed... I can see arguments for it being even smaller and more niche inside the WoD since many elements of society are more of a caricature of themselves in-game (and because White Wolf really enjoyed poking fun at themselves... just look at the authors' self-deprecating parodies)... or you might rule that Black Dog sort of covers and represents all RPGs inside the WoD, in which case it might be a bit more prevalent.

      Either way, do try to avoid characters running down to their local game shop to get the latest Revenant core book to learn all Kindred's secrets and weaknesses...

                                          Hm, a potential plot hook. Adolescent Kinfolk are starting to get into a new RPG, Succubi: the Submission, which actively encourages a very involved form of LARP. The characters are drawn into the subculture of Succubi: the Submission, which celebrates sexual exploration and sexual liberation.

                                          The goal of Black Dog Games is to produce a reactionary backlash by concerned parents (to encourage alienation and rebellious behavior among adolescents). They offer free PDF versions of Succubi: The Submission with the PG-13 rated artwork (the R rated artwork can only be found in the softcover print vetsions while the NC-17 rated artwork can only be found in the hardcover versions). Within the PDF versions of the corebook are sufficient instructions for sorcerers to develop the Mythic Numina of Fascination 1-2, the softcover versions Fascination 1-4, and the hardcover versions Fascination 1-6. Each additional publication contains instructions for a different Mythic Numina, with the greater secrets being found in the more explicit versions of the same book.
