                                                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                                The new terms introduced around packs are certainly part of the problem with perception about things being too "Forsaken" because they looks like words from Forsaken's fictional First Tongue language (which is based on Sumerian and run through a linguistic heuristic to give it a very primitive feel) rather than WtA's approach of a pidgin of terms from multiple human cultures.
                                                That is a pretty obvious thing, yeah. Garou would not have their own terms, more likely terms they loaned from various cultures. Another factor is that the book goes into portraying these packs as having issues if they step up into the territory of another pack and of the control of their totem (which, mind, I do like). Garou packs, unless anruth, are satellites of their septs. Even if packs poke into another's territory, there usually is the idea that they still share a caern and packs just take chunks of the sept bawn. Forsaken packs ARE their septs (especially in 2nd ed), so if you poke into another pack's territory, it IS a big deal.

                                                And, yet again, the blessed pack. I am in no way implying anyone stealing anything here, but I own Signs of the Moon and it states on p.190-191 that a blessed pack is one with all five auspices. The exact same thing applies to the blessed pack in Changing Ways p.110. What is more, it is written as somewhat unusual as it states that with one member always under their auspice moon, there is no breathing room. But generally, a pack is almost expected to have all five auspices in WtA, while in WtF it is treated as a luck of a draw how the pack's auspice situation goes.

                                                Not to give the book few praises, though, I do like most of the first chapter, minus the Crinos being treated more like Gauru and the aforementioned issues with future and trans-garou. The chapter details really well how a garou might think and act AND the auspice sections are solid on different motives. The pack section is also solid, especially the example packs.


                                                Also, the use of alpha in the Lupus chapter could have been replaced with the word dominant, since its used in that context.

They divvy up the Protectorate, but the Protectorate can be huge depending on the Sept, and encouraging on another's territory is a breach of the Litany. Between members of the Sept, it really shouldn't a regular or major deal, and this book over plays it to me, but it's not a new thing, or a Forsaken thing, it's a WtA thing being stressed oddly.

                                                    And, yet again, the blessed pack. The exact same thing applies to the blessed pack in Changing Ways p.110. What is more, it is written as somewhat unusual as it states that with one member always under their auspice moon, there is no breathing room. WtA has always said that one of each of the auspices is ideal, but rare because of practicality. Many packs have more or less than five members, the Sept might not have members of certain Auspices around for a new pack to take in to have all of them covered, etc. The game doesn't present one of each as standard in the Garou Nation, even if people usually play it that way because it's the ideal.

                                                    So again, it comes down to questionable ways of stressing elements already in WtA, not stuff from another game.

                                                            Considering that Rich and Brucato recently gave a pretty detailed breakdown of the process of how making a RPG book is handled by Onyx Path, seeing the chapter draft a writer submits to the line developer only means you know that changes were made.
