    My Rebuttal: The Cult of Fenris is fine and logical in-universe

    I know, popular opinion.

    Here's the thing, this is going to have an even more unpopular caveat. Specifically, the politics and out of universe reasons for the Get of Fenris being turned into the Cult of Fenris don't matter. If your opinion of the change is influenced by them, you should probably drop it because it won't help and isn't really relevant. What matters is the in-universe handling of the subject.

    Part of this is because is the telephone game and leaks have created the out of universe perception that the Get of Fenris have become Nazis or white supremacists. Which is objectively lying about what happened. It didn't make it to the cutting room floor and is just an idea someone had. The Swords of Heimdall? Not anywhere in the book. Racism? Not anywhere in the book.

    Fascism?

    Yes.

    Comparison to Nazis?

    Yes.

    All of it justified in lore? Absolutely.

    The Get of Fenris have always struggled with their rage. The Get of Fenris has been battling the Beast of War for their entire career. In the end, the Beast of War won. Other clans have tetered on the edge as well with the Glass Walkers, Red Talons, and other tribes all potentially getting corrupted by the Wyrm. The original editions emphasized just how flawed and struggle orientated the Garou Nation was. These included abuse of kinfolk, honor killings, mass terrorism, and rule by strength/dueling.

    Because it's a horror game.

    REVISED had the Garou Nation start making some genuine inroads to saving its soul. They were far from perfect but getting better. It just might have been too late.

    The Get falling happened because they got to be the one tribe who failed to make with the times and decided to go suicidal in Ragnarok while everyone else hung back.

    Now they're the baddies and at war with the rest of the Garou Nation. It's a metaplot change but these things have happened before, see the Stargazers. They're bigoted evil fascists and you know what, ALL OF THEIR BIGOTRY IS FICTIONAL.

    And that's fine.

    Because that sort of thing happens in the game and there's rule for playing Get in the back of the book who have not lost their damned minds.

    Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

            "Telephone game"? We have direct notes from inside development that making them Actual Nazis was the intention, and they just awkwardly sanded off the white supremacist aspect off at the last second because writers like Sambrano basically had to beg for it, and even then there's hints back towards it everywhere through way of dogwhistling (why are they all Nordic and have constant references to Norse culture when all the other tribes had their cultural aspects removed? Hmmm.). You can't just pretend that the out-of-universe factors that went into this decision just don't matter when they muddy the game's tone and make the overall product worse. If anything, they give the ways that this game doesn't work a whole lot of context, and hell, make the decision-making behind other failed products context as well (looking at you, H5.)

It being a horror game, and there being a precedent for the Garou dealing with the baggage of their own flaws doesn't mean the writers get a pass to not put things together in a way that makes sense (for example, if external factors don't matter, why have the Get fall and not the much more unstable Red Talons who are now openly cannibals? How is that not "too far" but the Cult is?). It doesn't really matter if the game doesn't explicitly say "THE CULT ARE NAZIS AND THEY ARE THE BIG BADS OF THIS GAME FOR REAL." if the entire point of being a Garou is dismissed and more attention is given to fighting this fallen tribe than, you know. The Wyrm and the destruction of the planet, or the Umbra, or Gaia, or doing anything at all. If this wants to be a game primarily about werewolf vs. werewolf to the exclusion of actually saving the planet, it needs to not be called Werewolf: the Apocalypse anymore and drop all the metaplot baggage from its namesake. They need to just flat-out make a new game at this point. As it is now, it just pleases no one. Doesn't make sense to new players because there's way too much metaplot baggage from the old game, doesn't please old players because they spend most of their wordcount insulting their game of choice.

            The external factors matter because game's director really, really, really wanted this to be a game about sticking it to neo-nazis and nothing else, and has active disdain for the legacy the game shares a name with, and it shows. And it can't even do that former thing right.

                If anything, that's a good argument for why the lore for the Cult makes no sense in W5: This edition's guiding principle is "re-imagination, not continuation". This sort of logic only shows how the lore obviously does not work in this case. This context for the Fenrir and other Tribes established in previous editions does not exist in W5. The Beast of War isn't a thing in W5. The Ghost Council and Galestalker Tribe don't work like that as well. You can't just point to the Native American Tribes and conveniently say "Well, that lore is all gone now. Hope you weren't a fan of those Tribes." and then point to the Fenrir and be like "Considering what the Fenrir used to be like in previous editions..." (with an additional "And if you were a fan of those guys in the past... Sucks to be you..."). The only reason the Fenrir are bad guys in W5 is because of the idea of "Get of Fenris=Nazis". Otherwise, it would have made more sense to bring them in line with the other Tribes by renaming them to remove any ties to human culture or ethnicity. There's no lore-reason for why the Fenrir above any other Tribe turn into the one Tribe falling to Hauglosk. Also, it completely goes against W5's idea of Tribes being these global, diverse entities with no ties to any particular human identity. How can any Tribe really "fall" in that conception...? Tribes have even less of an united identity in W5.

                But also: Why does W5 even need any Tribe to fall to Hauglosk in the first place? Take the Fenrir out of the equation and put it more generally: What exactly does the game even gain from having another fallen Tribe...? Because that's the thing: The extremities of Garou-behavior turned on itself, turning Garou against Garou... you already have all that with the BSD! And with no Garou-Nation, it's even less relevant who's doing what when it comes to the Tribes. I mean, consider this: If the Fenrir are supposedly these extremist, bad Garou... how did them leaving contribute to the fall of the Garou Nation? If they were truly bad, wouldn't the Garou-Nation want to get rid of them and be more united by them being gone? There was never anything like "The White Howlers fell and turned into the BSD and then the Garou-Nation fell." or "The Croatans sacrifices themselves, disappeared - and then the Garou-Nation fell." With the fall of the Garou Nation you just get another vaguely defined event that nobody knows about apparently. The lore just doesn't make sense.

                Unless, of course, none of this matters because we're not talking about what makes sense in the past lore or the changes W5 has made... Instead what this is really about is one of the main-developer's personal investment in turning one particular Tribe from the previous editions into an antagonist-faction. Then of course it would make sense why erasure (like with the Native American Tribes) or reimagining (like with the Black Furies) weren't considered. Instead, this one particular Tribe got transplanted into W5 no matter how little sense it made lore-wise only so that it could be turned into an antagonist-faction.

                And the adventure Deepest Of Wounds shows how much the Cult of Fenris doesn't fit into W5. The notion of being worse than other Garou but not as bad as the BSD is an awkward place for the faction and the adventure ends up failing to do that balancing-act by starting with the Cult of Fenris as allies (!) of the Garou and then introduces them doing BSD-like stuff during the course of the adventure. Also, the CoF leaving the Garou Nation has been rendered meaningless by the W5 corebook because its status quo already has a fallen Garou-Nation: At the starting-point of W5, practically nobody is in the Garou Nation because there is no more Garou Nation.

                Any argument based on "But the Fenrir used to be like this and that" just misses what W5 is trying to do. Pointing at past lore to make an argument for why something is the way it is in W5 is just an indication of how the lore in the W5 corebook doesn't work. Same with the Stargazers, the lore on that front doesn't make sense either.

                And the text in the W5 corebook directly links the Fenrir to Nazis as well, after all. Not seeing that link just means you ignore the W5 corebook.

                                                Development details matter when the product itself doesn't give an answer.

The problem arises when you consider what these invisible Chronicle Tenets must be because neither Red Talons poisoning entire villages nor BSDs seem to have broken any. Yet, Red Talons start with 0 Hauglosk and the BSD write-up doesn't mention them suffering from it.

                                                The only thing that the Talons and BSDs lack is the very on-the-nose Nordicness of the CoF. Which then directly leads us to the development of W5 where the Brand Manager told a person working on a positive version of the tribe that the very idea of them being Nordic was the issue.

                                                I am not trying to woobify Nordic people. But given the material we are given, this is the best conclusion I can come to.
