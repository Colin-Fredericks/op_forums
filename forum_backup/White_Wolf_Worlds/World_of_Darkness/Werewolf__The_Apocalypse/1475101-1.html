                            Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                            The problem is that the Garou are the Worfs of the WoD. They get talked about how dangerous and badass they are, but we never get to see it in the crossover fiction of the WoD, where they usually get their butts handed to them to show how powerful something else is (generally an ancient vampire, maybe the Technocracy, at least once both).
                            Yeah, as I said it is cheap storytelling, the Worf Effect is in full blown with them, sometimes with some Conservation of Ninjutsu applied, which makes no sense for Werewolves at all. This isn't even exclusive to other lines, as W:tA does this with other Fera more frequently than it should.

                            In the end things are a lot more complex. Everyone but Vampires tend to talk from a perspective of superiority to everything else, even though vampires aren't actually as weak in comparison, but as a rule of thumb everyone recognizes Werewolves as dangerous in combat. Other splats are just confident in being able to either avoid or outsmart them.

                            And the final problem is that the same way the authors in other lines have a tendency to greatly overestimate their strength in battle (until Worf Effect kicks in), they also have the same tendency to greatly underestimate their ability to be a complete and complex splat with other resources, strengths and strategies to deal with things. They are not just glorified chainsaws.

                            Originally posted by heinrich View Post
                            In Vampire: Bloodlines the werewolf is basically a killing machine and unstoppable, except for a trick...
                            Which is how many players think it is or should be, but is one of the many moments in that game that doesn't really convey how things actually work in the game.

                            In the end that battle works as a storytelling device for the game, but it is still completely off in the greater context of the setting.

                            Originally posted by Lashet View Post
                            So Onyx Pathe essentually assert gamelines at WtA expense. And demote Garou to biggest loosers of WoD, who only exist to be humilated.
                            Yeah, you did meant something similar to what I thought.

                            And this isn't quite like that in many ways. First that it wasn't Onyx Path most of the time, I think most fiction with this characteristic is from WW's time. WoD isn't an OPP product, they just work on X20 lines, and most fiction that I know of is older than that. Other can correct me if I'm wrong, I'm not really taking notes of everything released and rarely bother with novels.

                            Second, as we already said here, saying that they only exist to be humiliated or that other lines are asserted at their expense is greatly, hugely overstating this.

                            Characters are asserted with this. Lines not. Each and every splat in the WoD knows that facing Werewolves head on is a dumb idea, period. This is repeated in variable, but no uncertain terms every time. Most have reason to not be too bothered by this, but that's because they don't expect conflict to be unavoidable for one reason or another. Not fearing the fighter because you're confident in avoiding the fight is not the same as thinking you're stronger. Some also can be formidable foes on their own, but then they're recognizing that they need their best to be able to take on a Werewolf. Again, by default.

                            I do agree that they receive a fairly bad treatment in crossovers, but we're talking a setting were crossovers are a niche thing to begin with, and where that happens precisely because they're the most meaningful foe to show how much a given character is powerful beyond the measure of their kind, while you can hardly show that for Werewolf characters pitting them against other splats.

                            Remember that pitting important NPCs against one another in a live-or-die scenario is a no-go, and strong characters are usually also important even to be recognized as such. Mithras killing a whole Sept shows he is extremely powerful. Albretch killing a whole Elysium shows it is Thursday. Albretch facing Mithras won't happen, because they won't sacrifice one for the sake of showing of the other.
