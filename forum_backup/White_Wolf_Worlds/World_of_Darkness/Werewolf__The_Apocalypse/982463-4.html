    ​We know that the Ritual of Sacred Rebirth can be enacted by Kinfolk. So why not others? I hate to revive this flame-war, but I'm genuinely not getting what the issue is here.

            Originally posted by Uniform Two Six View Post
            I think you don't like this because you've gotten so wrapped up in the idea that spirits do not, will not, cannot empower Rites for anyone other than a Garou, ever, that this is violating your worldview of WoD. As long as the storyteller is competent, and approaches it intelligently this shouldn't be an issue. And besides, if you're the storyteller, you get to say "yeah, but not in my game".
            I don't like it, because it was stated before that a kin person could learn or teach a Rite but not enact it. It was a hard rule, with an exception. Not to retcon, that there have been, all along, ways to do Rites, if garou are participation, or maybe even without - for normal kinfolk is a Retcon and I hate those.
            For me Retcons and Reboots destroyed Ultimate Marvel and the DC Universe - I hate it happening in Werewolf, too.

            We are all adults here. It would be common decency to have a sidebar and give an honest out-of-character statement to world changes. Something like:
            "In early Werewolf sidestepping was limited to places where reflecting or shiny surfaces were available. Different authors weren't always on the same page on how the process works, though. In Valkenburg Foundation the surface was in the umbra, while in Rite of Passage a blinking earring would have allowed ambushing an inexperienced Wendigo pack's campsite by sidestepping out of the Umbra right between them. By revised edition it was possible for garou to attempt to sidestep anywhere, the reflecting surface merely reducing difficulty by one. In our opinion, this made the Penumbra too easy to reach and reduced the gravitas of the question on which side of the Gauntlet a character was. That's why for W20 we changed the system again, stating that it is normal to use stationary mirrors or reflecting surfaces as means to cross the Gauntlet. Portable mirrors also work, but at increased difficulty. Sidestepping without those means is not permitted.
            You can either assume the world always worked like presented here, or regard this change as something that happened in the last few years. You can also play W20 using the sidestepping rules from revised edition, although this might require further adjusting in regards to gifts etc.
            If you allow gifts from previous editions, we suggest to raise the Stargazer cliath gift Inner Light, that allows to sidestepp without a mirror, to Fostern Rank and possibly make it available to all Theurges, if you find the W20 rules to restricting, but don't want to use the revised rules, either."

            Something like that - comment from game designer to player/storyteller on what a given mechanic is about and how it came to be and how it is changed from other editions of the game would make the whole W20 stuff much more accessible.
            Also, the whole "Can't detect pre-change garou anymore, and never could"-aspect would be a lot more thought through if someone had bothered to explain, why the change felt necessary for the development team, how it was handled before, how one could ease the transition and so on...

            So, no, it is not that I don't like the Rites for Kin people because I am stuck in my metaphysical belief of how spirits should handle Rites in this fictional Setting. I would have allowed Kin rites anyhow, if they were the tools (Foci) to perform True Magic (which is canon) or Hedge Magic (which is kind of canon, because Sorcerers need a craft/tradition like basis for their sorcery and while not explicitly listed garou shamanism falls within the scope of what players/ST are encouraged to design for their characters).
            The way the book handles Rites, and gitfts for that matter, is just kind of unsatifactory for me.

                                Morning Song is very similar to Greet the Sun. It's a Rite in which the participant literally sings the praises of a spirit (in this case Helios). I don't think that a Solar-Gaffling nearby deciding to reward the Kinfolk for the song of praise is too out-there. Kin being able to use Rites in the same way that the Garou do (any Rite just whenever they need them) does fundamentally alter the relationship. I'm not in favor of that.

                                Now, most Rites could (theoretically) be enacted by a Kin, but it would be dependent upon the spirit in question. If it's like the Garou, where some werewolf does a Rite to cleanse Wyrm-Taint, and any spirit floating along is required to enact it (as opposed to responding to a direct appeal of praise directed at that very spirit), I don't see that happening. The Great Spirit Pact should still apply. If the Rite is going to work for a Kinfolk, there should be a reason -- and that reason should be that the spirit wants to (for whatever justification).
