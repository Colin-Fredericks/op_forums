                                            I've seen that definition before (Racism = Power + Prejudice) but it's not one I agree with because it ends up being arbitrary.

                                            Let's take the example of Joe, a white guy in the United States. He hates Chinese people and says horrible things about Chinese people. Joe is very clearly bigoted and is racist because he's a white guy in the US and in the US white people have had a ton of institutional power to oppress Chinese people. Joe gets on an airplane and moves to China. Joe says the same stuff about Chinese people there. Joe is still bigoted, he still says the exact same things he did a a few hours ago in the US, but he's no longer racist. As a white person in China he's not part of the group that maintains or holds institutional power.

                                            This means that whether someone can be considered racist isn't determined by what they say or what they do, but rather it's determined by the geographical location in which they say or do those things and then only if they're part of the racial/ethnic group that holds power in that geographical location. Someone screaming the n-word could stop being racist in the middle of their screeching simply because they stepped across a national or geographic border in the middle of their tirade and went from being part of an ethnic group with institutional power in one country to an ethnic group with no institutional power in another.

                                            Personally I believe that trying to define racism in that way is garbage. IMO that definition of racism was simply created so that minorities could say or do things that would be considered racist if white people said or did them and then they could say, "No, no, I'm not racist. It's okay for me to say that because I'm not a white person (and therefore don't have political power and nothing I do or say can be considered racist)."
