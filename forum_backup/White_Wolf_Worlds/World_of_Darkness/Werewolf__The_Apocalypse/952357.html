    Plant spirits as a familiar

    08-17-2016, 02:50 AM
    I posted this in the Mage forum, but I haven't received any responses yet so I figured I'd try here. I'm playing in a mixed campaign, including werewolves. We use bits and pieces of the cosmology from all of the games, but when dealing with the Umbra we default to Werewolf. This is why I posted here, because I'm guessing the regulars here would have some helpful information. My character is a mage with a decent amount of Spirit and Life. He has recently covered a very large building in ivy, intending on awakening the spirit of the ivy and using it as a sentient defense system. The storyteller half-jokingly mentioned using the ivy spirit as a familiar, and I actually thought that might be a cool idea. The problem is I don't know too much about the types of spirits in Werewolf so I don't really know how plant spirits act and whether or not they'd be useful familiars. So I was hoping someone with more familiarity could tell me what plants spirits are like in W:tA or tell me what book to look in to find examples.

      Check out Axis Mundi (I prefer the older edition), it has some examples of plant spirits. Generally I portray plant spirits not so different from other spirits associated with terrain- they are not as gregarious as animal spirits and what they care about is usually more staid and predictable. I like the idea of an ivy spirit creeping and slinking along, maybe even getting a little handsy without realizing it.

      As for whether it is 'useful' or not, that is up to you. There is the standard 'tangles up your enemies' route, but it could have other charms and abilities depending on what you want it to do. Using tumble-down stone and jagged shards of mortar as bludgeoning weapons might be fun.

                                  Originally posted by Aya Tari View Post
                                  Sorcerer: The Hedge Wizard's Handbook, p. 70.
                                  That's an early edition book, with all of the silliness that comes with that. The same Merit was reprinted in Sorcerer Revised with the part about the Gift learning removed. There is a reason for that. In any case, even if you did allow a Sorcerer to learn Gifts, I don't see how you could interpret that to mean that they can learn any rank Gifts. They have no method of gaining rank.

                                  You can obviously (and will) run it however you want, but interpreting the books that way, and cherry picking which edition's wording to use, strikes me as incorrect.

                                  On the Corn Maiden Totem specifically, I would also argue those Traits and Ban are specifically tailored for Garou. A human follower would have different Traits (there are examples the same Totem giving different traits to different creatures). Giving +3 dice to all social rolls (since 99% of the time Sorcerers are interacting with people) comes off as pure min/maxing.
                                  Maize (Corn) appears as a Totem for the Nunnehi, and the stats there are much more fitting for a Sorcerer to use (Empathy 2, Cooking 2, +1 Wits). Similar-ish Ban. I don't see you being open to the suggestion though, since you would prefer to use the one that increases every social roll ever for a human.
