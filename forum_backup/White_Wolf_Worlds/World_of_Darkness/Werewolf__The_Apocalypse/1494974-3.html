    So, in regards to Justin Achilli's attitude... He had this interaction with Bryan CP Steele on Twitter recently. Bryan CP Steele is a game-developer/author who worked with Renegade Games recently as a writer for their Power Rangers RPG. This is their interaction:
    Justin Achilli:
    See, now, I’d argue that Werewolf is a game about wrong answers only, but they’re extremely righteously performed.
    Bryan CP Steele:
    *puts on his gurahl voice*
    "Now, pup... that's because you and your friends have been asking the wrong questions. Grab a mug, the good stuff, and I'll tell you a tale..."
    Justin:
    What’s a gurahl
    Bryan:
    Just an echo from the past that I hope to get the chance to roar back into existence someday.
    #Werewolf5 #gamedesigner #careergoals
    Obviously, Justin was joking there not knowing what Gurahl are but it's interesting that Bryan assumes Gurahl are something he wants to "bring back in the future". Maybe I'm reading too much into this but in the context of all the other reveals so far, I could easily picture that the W5 corebook won't acknowledge the existence of any Fera.

    And with that attitude in mind, I think that further supports the notion that Justin probably was never a huge fan of the old WtA lore.

        For now I really like everything I saw from Patron Spirits to tribes and tribal verbs, also the renown associated to each tribe, to me makes sense and I don't want to see these things changed. Indeed, without the old tribal concepts about legacy and blood, the garou seems to be less ''problematic'' and therefore some pathos is lost, but I honestly always storytelled WtA in a dirty, street, horror way ( I still use the Swords or the terrorist conncetion of the Fianna), it's not difficult to me to create terrific single tribe packs that breed young werewolves in order to prepare them for being chosen by their own tribal totem. Actually, the fact that a Patron Spirit choses you because of your attitude, to me sounds like some packs/tribe, could be even more extreme and hardcore than in revised or 20th, since they will try to push some kind of attitudes in their cubs, in ...interesting ways.

        And even if it will not be canon something like that, anyway I will use this idea for my chronicles.

        Yes I know, many things are extremely different from the other editions, and It's right to be negative or skeptical, bu my thoughts are positive, I like this reboot idea, and I like the fact it cuts many ties with older edition, I prefer two feet outside or inside, than one in an one out.
        These are my thoughts, but I still think we have few things to talk about. I understand why by the book Players who loves the general fantasy and more epic vibe of werewolf are worried.

            Originally posted by Helur View Post
            Yes I know, many things are extremely different from the other editions, and It's right to be negative or skeptical, bu my thoughts are positive, I like this reboot idea, and I like the fact it cuts many ties with older edition, I prefer two feet outside or inside, than one in an one out.
            On that part I disagree with you. W5 is the definition of one foot inside and one foot outside. That's the issue at hand here. It doesn't go as far as WtF and it doesn't want to be a continuation. So we get this version of WtA that is full of references to old WtA but with like 70 to 80% of the lore cut out with a forced focus on street-level storytelling.

            "Tribe is what you do" isn't some new, interesting take on how Tribes work. The idea is that a player thinks "I want to play a character that is good at doing x." and then the Tribe is chosen accordingly. Of course, any esoteric stuff like an all-female Tribe or a Lupus-only Tribe or any Tribe that has prerequisites (or any historical, geographical and (problematically, of course) ethnic ties) complicates this simple conception of what role Tribes play in the character-creation-process so that, I imagine, is why all the deeper stuff (tribal traditions, history and character) gets cut out.

            So rather than coming up with a new conception of Tribes and the Garou-Nation like WtF did or trying to deal with all the history, background-detail, flaws and problems of previous editions, W5's take on Tribes is to streamline them and make them a simple choice and leave it up to the players to figure out the details. But the names of most Tribes are kept because W5 still wants to be called Werewolf: The Apocalypse. That alone prevents the game from really doing something new and different. The take W5 has on certain concepts can't be too different. Sure, Gaia's dead or dying but W5 won't say that it's setting is our world but a thousand times worse because we're in the middle of the apocalypse. It will still be the same old WOD from V5 and H5. What it can do, though, is cut out a lot of the lore and downsize the scope and detail of its worldbuilding. That, it seems, how W5 has decided to differentiate itself from previous WtA editions.
