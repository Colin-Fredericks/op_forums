    Fluctuations of the Gauntlet Rating

    07-25-2015, 08:12 PM
    The rules as written seem to imply the Gauntlet is mostly "fixed". Of course, changes in the environment eventually are reflected in the Gauntlet, but this usually slow and long term. However, the Gauntler for any said area is usually the same. In Wraith, the Shroud operates similarly in mechanics, but the books of that line talk about various times when the rating of the Shroud lowers or increases (like on Halloween). I think Werewolf should be similar.

    Foklore and magic often mention appropriate times or places when magic is performed, or when beings from the Other Realms cross over. These are liminal times and places - not one thing or the other, but a mix, and this breakdown in order allows... things to happen. I think this would be a good time for the Gauntlet to decrease, and to compensate other times that are much "stronger" in one way, the Gauntlet might even rise.

    For example, the changes in the seasons are good time for when the Gauntlet could be lowered.
    • The solstices and equinoxes and their relevant seasonal celebrations (Imbolc/Candlemas, Walpurgis Night/Beltane, Lammas/Lugnasa, and Samhain/All Soul's Day) could have a Gauntlet rating of minus one than normal.
    • Certains hours of the day would also be good. Twilight and dawn are appropriate. We also have times like Midnight, "the witching hour", or "hour of the wolf" when the Gauntlet might be lowered - but only for Wyrm creatures.
    • There are also liminal places like crossroads, caverns, bridges, springs, and coastlines. All could lower the Gauntlet by one.

    I like this because it would drive behavior. Garou and other beings of magic would want to hold important rituals and such that work better with a lowered gauntlet at these special times and places. This is what happens in folklore and stories. This isn't what I usually see at the gaming table though. I think it would improve the game, but making it seem more like how people believe the occult to be in the real world. It doesn't penalize anyone in their normal roleplay, but gives them options if they want the benefit. It also is a way to increase tension when fighting the bad guys as the ST could use the date of the next seasonal equinox or whatever as the obvious time for the bad guys to perform their plot device ritual, and the PCs know they are under the gun to find and stop them before that.

    Has anyone does this in their own games?

                      I'd argue that the Shroud and various magical effects are somewhat tied to human perception, and sure, the Umbra itself reflects emotions in some cases, but the Gauntlet is much more mechanical than that. Is there a reason that pattern spiders stay away from this particular cave or lake? They're not superstitious. Is there some powerful spiritual effect that's fighting them off (maybe counterproductive), or fooling them, or actively working its own chaos? Then sure, maybe. Otherwise no.

                      I think of the Gauntlet as expanding like sand...if you have some very high gauntlet area like a research lab in a city, you're pouring a lot of sand on that spot, and it's spreading out around it. If you try to make a low gauntlet area, you're digging out sand, and more keeps pouring in.

                          One concept -- and a very critical one, in my opinion -- from early on (up to 1994/5) that got lost pretty quickly is that the barriers of the Gauntlet (Middle-Penumbral), the Shroud (Low Umbral) and the Astral Gauntlet (to the High Umbra) were similar-but-separate things, metaphysically. That is, for example, the Gauntlet to the Middle Umbra in the city may be an 8 or 9, depending on location; however, the Shroud rating at the city cemetery might be a 6, even though it's located in the same geographical area. On the flip, the Gauntlet might be a 5 to enter the MU along a bright, secluded island cove that is largely untouched by civilization (a few huts, simple boats, but not luxury hotel, etc.) -- especially if the character attempts to enter the MU by crossing into the cove or sailing out towards the horizon of the sea; the Shroud there, though, might be an 8 or even 9 due to the ambient forces of life and light that the place happens to embody. That city example might include a very high rating to enter the High Umbra as with the other layers of the Umbrae... save that, in the old college building -- and particularly the centuries-old, intact library that has stood for centuries -- the Gauntlet to the High Umbra might only be a 6 vs. the 9 one might expect for the MU or LU in possibly the same area.

                          This idea, which is metaphysically much more sound than a more "streamlined" Gauntlet/Shroud, reflects the differences between the various Umbrae, as well as takes into account *concept* as well as locality when it comes to metaphysical influences. It's a shame it got mostly simplified. My friends and I have stuck with the original alluded idea, and it really has given things a more "legit" feeling over the years by allowing the individual aspects of the various "penumbral connections" to make more sense relative to their expressed concepts.

                          Food for thought.
