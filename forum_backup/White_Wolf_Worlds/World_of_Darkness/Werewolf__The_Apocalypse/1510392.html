      19the Century, European Garou come to Australia and find it protected by small tribe known as the Bunyip. There are tensions, problems and violence, but they claim they'll not do the North American thing.Then they proceed to do the North American thing and mess up their kinfolk and take their Caerns. Yet, there may be a chance to fix things, even with the Ananasi manipulating both humans and the Garou into getting rid of the Bunyip.

      This is when a group of Black Spiral Dancers uses the tensions and the insecure Garou to manipulate them into believing the Bunyip attacked first and went for the European Garou and their kin. At the same time the BSD's convince the Bunyip that all Garou are untrustworthy. The Garou flip out and murder the Bunyip pursuing them deep into Australia.

      By the time the Garou stop to think about it and figure out they were misled, they have already murdered the last Bunyip and pissed of the spirits that the Bunyip were allied with. The BSD's laugh. The Ananasi don't, because one of the spirits the Bunyip awoke now hunts them whenever they plague their lands.


      It's more complicated than that, but it's the summary.

      What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.

          The War of Tears refers to when non-indigenous Garou exterminated the indigenous Bunyip Garou tribe of Australia. This was due to the Garou having been tricked by the Black Spiral Dancers.
          Mara the Scream was a Black Spiral Dancer who tricked the European Garou of Australia into exterminating the Bunyip
          But it was actually a bit more than that
          The Bunyip were living on borrowed time from the start
          Shortly after the War of Rage the Garou who became the Bunyip came to Australia but were lacking wolf kin (bit similar to the Red Talon of africa, who have made a pact with the Mokolé that allowed them to breed with the native African wild dogs becoming the Kucha Ekundu)
          The Australian Camazotz were sympathetic to the Bunyip, and indeed were the ones who introduced them to the Mokolé who taught them how to adapt themselves that they might breed with the thylacine.
          However, the Bunyip population, never very large, gradually dwindled over time. Australia's Ananasi, dissatisfied that the decline wasn't faster, nudged the Aborigines into hunting the thylacines to near-extinction, until the only surviving population was on the island of Tasmania, too few to be viable. - The Yahwie is a creature that began hunting and slaying Ananasi in Australia around 1900. No one knows what the Yahwie is. It appeared one day and slaughtered the Ananasi population, without warning and without hints at its motives. There are few things that frighten the Ananasi, but the Yahwie has become one of them. Even its name is drawn from Aboriginal legends, describing a death-spirit from the beginning of the world. There are Ananasi that claim to have faced and defeated the Yahwie, but it always returns and it always succeeds. Even fleeing Australia proves to be no protection, as the werespiders later vanish and appear again on Australian soil, dead. Some Ananasi, however, suspect that the Yahwie seeks vengeance against the were-spiders for their part in the fall of the Bunyip. Evidence for this is that the corpses are usually found at desolate Bunyip Caerns. Also, the Yahwie never targets anything else but the werespiders...​
          The Bunyip were isolated from the other tribes for centuries until European Garou arrived with the white settlers in the late eighteenth century. The invaders sought to control the caerns of the Outback and fought with the Bunyip
          ​
          The War of Shame occurred basically as the rest of the Garou Nation found their way to Australia.
          The Bunyip’s thylacine form already made the incoming Garou suspicious, since at this point they had hostile relations with most other werecreatures. Still, unlike in the Pure Lands, the two parties managed to maintain a truce.
          The Garou would keep to the colonies while the Bunyip would hold most of their caerns. In reality, this was a cold war, constantly in danger of heating up.

          The trigger for the Bunyip genocide was when the Garou found a desecrated Red Talon corpse in near their encampment. Enraged, the Red Talons swore revenge against the Bunyip. Fighting broke out between the Talons and the Bunyip. - contrasting to amiable relations to the Pure Tribes, the Red Talons regarded the Bunyip as usurpers and thieves. Since they did not even breed with wolves, but with thylacines, many suspected that the Bunyip had killed all native wolves in Australia. The Talons that came to Australia bred with dingoes and similar diluted creatures, and thus were susceptible to the Wyrm. Their prejudice lead the Talons to starting the genocidal War of Tears, a war that most Talons feel little regret over.
          Still, the Silver Fang in charge of the Australian Garou was willing to see reason. He called a moot of all the tribes residing in Australia, including the Bunyip. The Bunyip never made it, and so the Garou declared war against the Bunyip. Every tribe participated, even the Uktena and Wendigo, somehow. Only when the last Bunyip died did the Black Spiral Dancers behind the corpse reveal themselves. The Bunyip had sent an emissary party, but they were waylaid by the Dancers...
          ​

                              Great, so Garou could've reasonably came to Australia between 4000 and 8000 years ago.



                              Seriously. There's no good rational for Werewolves to come to Australia before that. Is there a need for Garou to protect Australia at that time? Well, aboriginals certainly caused a good bit of damage before Europeans arrived: they slowly drove many species to extinction and were overly fond of causing forest fires (as are the birds), but this damage was done over thousands of years. Plus I'm not really sure it's the Garou's place to get in the way of a fairly natural process: If it was a civilization deliberately trying to control the natural environment it might be cause to step up, but hunter gatherers with a few interesting life-hacks is more in line with natural selection and shouldn't warrant an impergium. There are local Fera better suited for hunting serious arsonists and given their track record the Garou probably would've only hastened the destruction of the megafauna..

                              Does it benefit Garou to become Thylacines? Absolutely not. Oversimplifying but Marsupials are generally speaking less intelligent than placentals (Most other mammals) and arguably less competitive than other kinds of mammals: Australia's ecosystem is fragile and anything brought from outside tends to outcompete the locals*, which is why border patrol/customs is so famously strict and all Australians are morally obligated to kill toads humanely . There are many prominent invasive species in Australia: indeed dingoes were a big part of thylacine decline (modern scholars will contest this and it has more to do with humans, but they certainly had an effect. I could also argue the domestication of Dingos was great in helping humans multiply) . Becoming a thylacine should be considered a downgrade, so unless the spirit of the Thylacines was an absolute genius in con-artistry or otherwise gave some real heavy bribe I don't see why proud Gaian Garou would take the deal.



                              Lastly, close relatives of the dingo exist in SE Asia, but grey wolves don't. Did the bunyip travel all the way to Australia from the Eurasian mainland tens of thousands of years ago, refusing to settle in the pacific, until finally they got to Australia and immediately went bunyip. I mean, they might've been on some weird spirit quest, but I think moving along with the people and merging with the local canines first would make a lot more sense.


                              Essentially for Bunyip to be a thing, the entire tribe needs to have skipped over what is now Indonesia on a quest taking them all the way to Australia, and then they need to have decided to stay there and not go back to Indonesia which has plenty of wolf approximates.

                              To just be more frustrating: The Bunyip is not a legend that lends itself particularly well to Garou or Thylacines. It's a water monster.

                              Like, I'm not all that great at articulating things, but the idea of the bunyip is built on massive stretches. It feels like someone with an axe to grind just wanted to throw shade on white Australians and didn't mind dragging the aboriginals in the mud to do so.

                                              Shattered Dreams does its best to explain the why's and wherefores of Garou arrival in Australia in the word count allotted. The people whp first arrived on the continent included Garou kinfolk. All their wolf-kin had been killed by the time they made the crossing.
                                              Sometimes, kinfolk give birth to a Garou. Thus, the Garou were in Australia, but without wolf kin they couldn't sustain their kind. Faced with extinction, they cast around for options and the Mokole helped them try on many other animals as kin until finally the thylacine proved the most compatible of a bad bunch. Unfortunately the change to thylacine came with a drawback unnoticed until dingos arrived. The Ananasi were the first to realize the dingo caused the Bunyip "allergy", and encouraged dingos to spread. Not much encouragement was needed, but they helped pave the way and foiled plans to kill off the dingos etc.

                                              So successful were the dingos that thylacines were eventually eradicated on the mainland. Bunyip very unhappy about this and kinda had good reason to dislike the Ananasi even more. But, at least some thylacines survived, and so remained as Bunyip kin. It was just a bit of a hike from the far north to the far south.

                                              When the European Garou arrived, Bunyip Garou suffered even more severe 'allergic" reactions in their presence. Alas, the allergy caused their bodies to warp and change to reflect all the tribe's previous animal kin attempts. To the Europeans, a rapidly mutating animal-man was obviously a mockery of all that was good under Gaia. Slaughters began. The BSD also fooled the Red Talons to drum up a rage-enhanced war against the Bunyip. Little provocation was needed, so mad props to them for pushing the Europeans Garou into full on no holds barred genocide against a foe they didn't consider Garou - at best just a weird Fera, at worst a wyrm-twisted mockery.

                                              The Bunyip fought back, of course, but constantly hampered by their allergy and more Ananasi betrayal and European Garou willing to use silver weaponry and Europeans willing to wholesale slaughter indigenous tribes and then slaughtering thylacines too. Eventually the last Bunyip got together to curse the Ananasi with the Yawie, and take off to the Umbra/Dreamtime/Everywhen.

                                              Those who fled may or may not still be extant, and in the 1980s Australian Garou were made to realize the Bunyip were actually for reals a legitimate Garou Tribe. The recognition hit hard. Disagreements erupted into fights. Quarrels and arguments and denials within and between Tribes. However, consensus settled and the whole conflict was named the War of Tears. One would hope something had been learned by all this, but Garou (and humans) are kinda slow on that sort of thing. I should also note, that Bunyip is what the Europeans called them. No one knows what the "Bunyip" named themselves - I dare says something more appropriate.

                                              Some Garou think the Bunyip who fled could still be contacted, or brought back to undo the War of Tears. Others disagree. Players can use it or not as a plot hook for the Apocalypse.

                                              So that's all in Shattered Dreams. It's somewhat expanded upon in minor ways within the Apocalyptic Record, and in Icons of Rage.

                                              Key point is: I tried my darnedest to not invalidate anything that was in the WW WoD Wiki at the time - so the wiki info should be accurate despite the massive differences between Shattered Dreams and Rage Across Australia (which I was told explicitly to ignore as trash, albeit, I gave it a read a couple of years ago and found a lot within that was pretty decent, despite the face-palming oh-god-nos also there).
