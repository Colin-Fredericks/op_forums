                    Questions:
                    1. Are the Dragons' insanely gigantic archid forms basically Gozilla-sized or what?
                    2. I'm guessing this won't be addressed in Shattered Dreams, but is the intent here for every single Dragon/Mokole, without exception, to sleep the entire time from Wonder-Work until after humans evolved? I liked the idea of some of them being awake occasionally during the Age of Sleep (which has its own pretty neat crocodilian varnas), and their arrogant-and-probably-exaggerated claim to have created humanity raises potential and questions about just what they were up to in the Pliocene. Also the wholesale evacuation of Antarctica has more potential than "They all froze in their sleep 'cause Gaia hated them." (Although "Some Dragons weren't evacuated and are still down there in magic coccoons, the scientists drilling there are in for a surprise" has potential too...)




                    Now, since I've been critical of the few things that actually bugged me in this 1st draft I want to comment also on what I liked.
                    • Just having the War of Dragons at all is awesome and making me want to buy this book when it comes out, more than the Wars of Rage material did. I was and remain a big fan of 2nd Ed. Mokole (yes, I know that breedbook has major flaws, but the parts that succeed are awesome and the Revised/CB20 depiction made me unhappy). Just having some details sketched out about history, politics, and society can makes the Age of Kings much more playable for interested STs (which I probably won't ever find... sigh).
                    • And entire Age of Dragons book!? Sweeeeeet.
                    • The concept of Mnesis is way cooler and more interesting/unique than what I was imagining in the Age of Kings and really contributes (along with DINOSAURS and not-insane Wyrm) to the sense of a radially different world on a cosmological level.
                    • Justification for why metis/innocents didn't exist for hundreds of millions of years.
                    • The depiction of the Bunyip here is pretty good. The authors clearly are doing research into the cultures of Australia and coming up with clever solutions for some of the problems in past depictions of these guys, and justification for the Bunyip name, totem, and appearance. They've got their own distinct purpose and projects and explanation of what they were up to and who they did and didn't get along with and everything. (Am I right in interpreting that their uprovoked attack on the Ananasi was because of all their past fights with the Kumo, and just assuming all werespiders were Kumo/hating all werespiders indiscriminately?)
                    • The Bunyip tribal weakness doesn't look fun to play at first glance (and I think additional justification is needed for including any animal parts other than from Thylacoleo, Sarcophilus, and Indonesian canids) but the basic concept really helps deal with the "peaceful" depiction, their retreat from the mainland/the dingos, the million-and-one chimeric descriptions of bunyips, etc. It's clever.
                    • Australian Corax. Yes they only get one line, but I love that their existence is included at last. And their disappearance, like with the Ao, isn't the same oft-repeated "Some asshole genocidal shapeshifters murderized them all." Or at least that's only one possibility, along with the much more interesting possibility of an entire tribe of Sun-Lost who flew off because the Australian Umbra was just too weird and shiny to resist. "Helped" by the Ananasi, of course... I wonder if the Ananasi manipulated them not by open warfare which doesn't seem to be their way, but by sociocultural manipulation to encourage them to abandon their duties and allies and get more obsessed with Dreaming mysteries. It's plausible given the context, and they could still be out there somewhere in some form...


                    In my headcanon Australian Corax are named Wahn and Australian Camazotz are named Garkain, but I admit I only did minimal research to find those names.

                            I've been going back through my design notes when I was designing the Age of Dragons, the Mnesis etc., and I've reminded myself why the Dragon Kings are dinosaurs and not crocs, turtles etc. (Besides any debate about when various animals appeared and disappeared.)

                            Simply put, when Mokole dream, they dream of being dragons. They have nightmares of their Archid forms that appear in flesh in the first change. These dreams and nightmares (and eventual physical forms) show the Mokole as dinosaurs and great prehistoric creatures. Modern Mokole are bound to modern reptile forms, their Archid legacy reminds them of when they were something more, something wonderful. When they were Dragon Kings.

                            And they've lost that glory forever, cast out into these smaller, less glorious forms. They survive, but are much reduced in stature.

                            If this wasn't true for the Dragon Kings, if they had ancient reptilian varna (regardless of size), it minimises the tragedy of their fall. They can't retain what they lost and still call it lost. They can't dream of former glory if they retain the link to that glory.

                            Thematically, that's what I had in mind for something in the World of Darkness, in Werewolf the Apocalypse. The world is doomed, you played a part in that with your poor choices. Welcome to the end.
