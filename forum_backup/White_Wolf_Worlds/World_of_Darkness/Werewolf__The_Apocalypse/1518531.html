    [WiR] The Apocalyptic Record

    07-22-2023, 12:22 PM
    So, life has been a bit complicated and that meant that my read of the Apocalyptic Record has been going on for way longer than its page count would suggest. I'm sorry to say that I'm still not done, but I'm getting there!

    As an introduction, I feel that I need to say that when the Apocalyptic Record came out I was filled with trepidation and worry over how Changing Ways ended up. Not having the money for a big bad prestige edition was not helpful either. As time went on I first heard some very nice stuff, then a friend shared the draft with me and pointed out some cool sections. This hooked me and I got on the backerkit and got the Record to complete my collection and because I wanted to endorse a broad and colorful look at what's going on in the World of Darkness. I skipped going into the draft deeply looking forward to the finished piece. I would have probably only skimmed it until my physical copy arrived, were it not for the authors asking questions about what we thought of the book. So I did!

    My plan is to give a quick outline of the book and then give a single reply for each chapter to discuss its contents.


    So. What is the Apocalyptic Record?
    It's a 239 page sourceboook about what is going on in the Werewolf setting. It introduces a number of new viewpoint characters and reintroduces a number of classic faces. The topics are broad and are explorations of Garou life and new threats and mysteries as well as the return of some of the old. It is also intended as a capstone and sendoff for the 20th Anniversary. There are two more books coming that are spinoffs to the Record:
    • Icons of Rage - A Who's Who of the Garou nation, including the old signature characters and new characters from the Apocalyptic Record
    • Howls of the Apocalypse - Three Werwolf chronicles inspired by the Apocalyptic Record

    It's organised into 5 chapters - one for each auspice, and an overview of the lore and metaplot of Werewolf as Chapter 6.

    Each set of chapters consists of a set of stories from different members of the Chapter Auspice or important to the members of the Chapter Auspice. There are a lot of different voices here and it's nice to see the different characters coming to life in their own words, retellings of their tales and even texts and recordings relevant to them and their stories.

    The chapter ends with an out of character section which summarises the main topics of each Auspice and storyhooks and ideas how to use them in your own games.​
    I'll follow up with a summary, but please keep in mind that this is an incredibly rough summary as there is s million little stories, opinions, sidebars and documents all building up tangents that won't be obvious from the outline. Also there is a lot of callbacks between the chapters and it's obvious the authors wanted us to go through the whole thing with a fine comb.


    Chapter I - Ahroun Tales
    • The Amazon War stories
    • Consequences and views on the War of Rage
    • How deep is the Weaver's influence and how to fight it
    • Albrecht and the quest for an ancient source of Gifts and Rage
    Chapter II - Galilard Tales
    • The troubles of the First Change
    • An epic quest to create as many Caerns as possible
    • Ghosts of the Impergium
    • How to bring back the Bunyip, and has it already gone horribly, horribly wrong
    • Kinfolk relations, Skin Dancer recruitment and horror stories
    Chapter III - Theurge Tales
    • Garouroboros and the Rise of the Abominations
    • The Troubles of New York Revisited
    • Changes and mysteries in the world of spirits - including rites to cure fomori, more active spirits, umbral quests and strange prophecies
    Chapter IV - Ragabash Tales
    • FreeX - Pentex X-Men created from kinfolk
    • Pentex Operations and Monkeywrenching (featuring a Kerasi!)
    • Redemption - personal and otherwise
    • House Blood Crest rises again
    • Return to Shigalu monastery
    Chapter V - Philodox Tales
    • The Weaver - Enemy or Friend?
    • Redemption of the Black Spirals
    • Dealing with extremes
    • Return of Turtle and it's children?
    • Visions of the End
    Chapter VI - On metaplot
    • History of Werewolf the Apocalypse
    • How to deal with the history and metaplot of Werewolf
    • Referenced guide to the events of Werewolf

    What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.

          Chapter I - Ahroun Tales​

          The Amazon
          This opens with some very strong art a pack of Werewolves facing a gigantic monstrous Crinos taller than any three other Crinoses with multiple heads and arms. I don't think the piece is referred to specifically in the text, but it does feel like the monstrous incarnation of past garou actions coming to haunt them. It also screams Vozhd and Hydra Form to me, and I wonder if it might be tied to a later chapter, the one speaking of a horrifying Abomination that is to come, The Garouboros.

          The first tale is from Nina Freeman, and it's interestingly framed because Nina is actually retelling the tale of her grandmother Astrid Broken Claw. Astrid seems to have been a veteran of the Amazon War. If we assume 2018 is when Apocalyptic record happens, she spent most of the 90s down there. And what follows is a classic Get tale of pride, honor, glory and getting into trouble because you didn't think two steps in advance. Astrid is really stereotypical in her worldview and conviction that they were doing the right things. However, her story is also one of loss and having to deal with watching all your packmates die horribly, while your glorious Victory is snatched from your hands and all you have left is loss and bitterness. It's a great snapshot of the War. What elevates the text are Nina's notes which give context to the situation and generally challenge her grandma's ideas - especially the outdated ones. I really took a liking to Nina throughout the text.

          There are two cool letters inserted into the tale, a communication between two Balam that are looking to handle the fallout of the Garou battles and how they might just try to trick new and younger Garou into doing it by seeding the right stories and conflicts for them. It also implies that Balam had pretty much taken over all the local Kinfolk. These letters actually bugged me quite a bit, not because they are poorly written or because they don't give cool plothooks, but because they are symptom of something else.

          W20s is married to the idea of being timeline neutral, which means that mostly only very old sourcebooks have actually happened and most of the events of Revised have not. Now there are plot developments that are either divergent or parallel to old Werewolf in a number of books, and especially Apocalyptic Record has decided to forge ahead with new ideas and new interpretations of where W20 started and where it may go. I loved what happened in the Amazon in Revised - how Golgol learned a lesson and showed humility and asked for forgiveness. I love how they forged a way forward and how the tone of the conflict changed. Apocalyptic Record's presentation of the Amazon events doesn't give a very empathetic portrayal of Garou actions nor does it give any slivers of how these things could start to happen or how we could make the PC's pivotal in these events (just like what was done with the Ahadi). Worse yet, it refers to W20 Rage Accross the World if anyone wants to learn more of these events - and there you will get another tale of blood and conflict with little hope for the future of the Fera in the Amazon. I wish they actually referenced "A World of Rage" from Revised instead. And while I feel we must recognize the problems and idiocies of the Garou, I would have loved to see more about these efforts.

          This topic is later in the chapter continued with a messy exchange between a Balam and a Garou pack that is partially provoked by the location making Rage even worse (Ragers?) and partially by both sides being assholes. They agree to both leave warnings to anyone else passing by and leave each other alone. There are notes that they spoke more and exchanged notes, and I would have love to have seen this exchange, maybe as something building towards what Revised gave us.The text does speak of the Wyrmcomers and implies that there was a deeper connection between the Middle Brother and their totem and the Balam. This part also introduces "The Geomid Network" a global Garou intiative to record lore and share it amongst themselves. A cool chronicle frame if there ever was one.

          Outside that, there is some really cool stuff in the section, including the freakishness of some of the things Pentex deployed in the Amazon, the spiritual and ecological consequences of the battles for the Amazon and horrible ways to die. Also, I especially loved the bog monsters that keep the spirits of the dead Garou within them still living in the old Garou compounds, a very cool development of a monster from the original. In many ways it evokes parts of the original Rage Across the Amazon with the feeling of going off to a foreign place to fight and things going poorly.

          War of Rage
          Next is a section is in New Zeland where three (!?) Ahroun members of Pack are discussing the changing breeds and the War of Rage and is intended for the education of the local cubs. It offers three views:
          • The Garou fucked up and need to make amends and take responsibility for their actions
          • The Garou messed up, but there's nothing to be done now because the Fera are too different and not willing to talk
          • The Fera are unnecessary and didn't listen to the Garou and they deserved everything they got
          I.. loved this bit. It's not really common to see a pack with this much tension and active dislike of each other, and I wonder if parts of their views are colored by the fact that they want to stick it to the people that oppose their views and provoke them into conflict. Also a Bone Gnawer progressivist as a pack leader to a Silver Fang War of Rage apologist is a special kind of fun. The conversation literally ends with them ready to rip each other's face off and the moderate dispersing the kids and trying to calm the two down. My only nitpick is that I would have preferred to have this transcript in one long section instead of split into three chunks throughout the Ahroun chapter.

          Way of the Form Breaking Fist
          What follows is Antonine Teardrop showing up, which made me really happy. We don't get to see a lot about him as the focus is him talking about meeting and studying a Stargazer Ahroun that invented the Way of the Form Breaking Fist. He will be back later, though!. This whole section is intriguing because it posits that the Weaver is so inherit to the world that it shapes the way people think - including the shifters themselves! Master Pure Mountain continues to offer koans and teaching about how he came to the path of Body. Essentially, the idea is that the mind is so constricted by the concepts of man, beast and shifter that it misses the point that it's exactly as free as the Weaver wanted it to be. Gonji Pure Mountain seeks to break this hold over the Garou by instructing them how to live through instinct and knowing the intent that the Garou were created for, rather than what they think they must do. This of course challenges a lot of staples of Stargazer thought, but it's an interesting way to go about it.

          The instructions how to gain understanding of the Fist sound like an epic quest worthy of a xianxia tale, it's also a list of gifts from Rank 1 to Rank 4 that you need to master to be able to lean the Form. There is a inference that the Form itself might allow for great things to the Ahroun that masters it - including using Physical attributes for Mental tasks and Rage for Gnosis.

          Albrecht's Quest for the Source of Rage
          The penultimate section of stories is all about another familiar figure - Albrecht. Albrecht seems to have a chronicler these days that follows him around to see what legendry he gets up to and seems to have a very Quirky Uncle energy to him. He's meeting with a Fury legend Kula Wiseblood, an elder and (former) sept leader from Rage Across New York. He wants her to join him on a legendary quest for a special kind of Lune that can teach Garou out of Auspice gifts. It seems that his grandfather, the old bastard Jacob Morningkill, had found this spirit and imprisoned it to claim its power. Of course what follows is some even stranger things - this spirit, Rimefleece may have been a direct servant of Gaia/Luna whose milk was drank by the Garou to gave them Rage. And this milk - belonging to either Gaia or Luna may still be around! The two of them set off to find and free the spirit and find this milk, but the end of the Chapter points to the two of them disappearing while on this quest.

          Now, I do find the whole thing about learning out of Auspice gifts a bit strange. It's not that hard for lower level gifts and it was never prohibited, even if I never allowed higher rank gifts to be obtained. However, the book makes a lot of kerffufle about this, even for rank 1 and 3 gifts. I'm not really sold on the way that was presented, but the rest of it is gloriously crazy. Jacob being an epic asshole, the quest for the Raindeer/Goat's milk of Rage and how horribly good or bad it could end up. I loved it. It begs to be used and the whole uncertain narrator/alternate interpretations/trap angle is excellently implemented.

          A Metises view
          The final section of stories comes from a Metis in a Sept somewhere in "Central Europe" (conveniently, name of the place is blacked out). It recounts how they were asked to intercede between their mother and father and stop unnecessary bloodshed. Yet the character refuses to do so, for they believe that conversation is weakness and action is strength. They even blame their parents stopping their fighting for the characters conception. It also speaks of a new entity known as "De Profundis" involved in fracking and geological surveys. What is interesting that while they destroyed their facility and killed everyone there, there is no direct involvement of the Wyrm implied anywhere in the text (thought the name is a bit suspect), implying that they may have just went for normal humans. The character embraces action and power and sees this as a revitalization of their sept and Garou.

          I didn't enjoy this bit.This is not the fault of the authors as I feel it's on theme and well crafted, carrying an important message about the Garou. It felt like the narrator was abused to the point that they accepted some of the Garou's biggest faults as their biggest strengths. I realise that this is a scenario that can happen, but in my opinion it's a failstate and I wish that any of the framing characters pointed this out. This one also doesn't have any specifc plothooks linked to it.

          The Ahroun's Tale
          Each chapter's stories end with a character of the Auspice meeting an old man by the fire. The man challenges them and their fault and advises them to find inner strength despite their failings. I'm not sure who this is supposed to be, my first impulse was Old Man Manyskins, but I wonder if it might be Wolf itself talking to the Garou at the end of days.

          Plot Hooks
          Each of the chapter ends with a section called Plot Hooks. This is an out of character section that looks more like a classic Werewolf book. It goes into each and every one of the topics that the chapter went into and offers thoughts, impressions and ideas how to make them into playable elements. These are well done and complement the stories as presented in the preceding chapter. I'll just single out some of the ones I really enjoyed, but there's great inspiration here.
          • I love the crazed ancestors trapped in bog spirits, even if I never understood Ancestors to work like this before this book
          • there is a scenario where a Fera seeks remediation for the Garou claiming one of their own heroes as a Garou hero
          • Jacob Morningkill and Rimefleece plothooks are great and offer a lot of potential storylines, I also feel it could easily be mapped elsewhere by say, exchanging Morningkill for Blacktooth for instance

                  Chapter II - Galliard Tales

                  Stories of the First Change
                  Delivered by Nils Son-Of-Iron, an old Get that has done it all, the first set of tales is essentially a warning tale to all the gathered Garou how problematic the question of First Change is and how much they need to adapt to their wards and the world they come into.

                  He goes through a couple of examples he taught. The firs one is already a mess - two cubs, one riot - one alt-right, the other socially concious. The protesters and counterprotesters fight, and the two of them go through the first Change in the middle of the riot. This is a plothook screaming for a PC pack to do the Veil cleanup. But that's just the start, because one is too fixated on fighting authority and tradition and the other is a vicious racist. Nils proceeds to talk of his struggles with both of them and how hard it was to deal with the first and how hard he had to knock some sense into the other.

                  What follows is a tale of a young Lupus and how she went into Harano not 9 months after her First Change. Not only is it a great representation of depression, but its also an excellent insight into how messed up the Garou life is for a Lupus. How strange the transition is and how impossible the scale shift is to their lives. How simpler it was, and how hard the burden can be on them. This is something that could apply to homids as well, but at least they were not new to the concepts that the Lupus have to accept right here and now.

                  The final section is of course about the metis, and is a transcript of the final conversation between Nils and his student Xolo. It speaks of his cloistered life, of the stigma and seeing the world without living in it. How the first change was a liberation from the strength and fury of the Crinos form and how it was finally a way to be free and experience the world. I feel so sorry for him and that he didn't get a chance to do more and see more. Really well done.

                  The first section in this chapter is a a very strong start. The message of taking care of the new generation and making sure that they are acknowledged and supported is a good takeaway.


                  Stories of the Caern Builders
                  This is a tale of Water-that-is-Born a Galilard who interprets tales through traditional Mexica dances. They are a collector of tales and lore, and with their pack have gone on a quest to Build as many Caerns as they can. This is an epic undertaking in and of itself, and one of high risk considering how the Rite always culminates with every servant of the Wyrm within miles crashing upon the site.

                  The tale goes through the rites and dances of the Caern Building in Alameda Central (the grand park in Mexico City). Water-that-is-Born sings of the Tribes and their trials and how Older Brother kept a watchful eye, they sing of the past and the future. The rite keeps going as a battle is joined around the Sept, and is successful.

                  It concludes with two interviews - one with Water-that-is-Born - which is all about accepting those willing to bleed and die for the land, even if they may not be of the tribes that once owned it. A strong statement from a member of Older Brother. One that most likely ties into the conclusion where mysterious shadows of the old defenders of what is under Almeda assist the rite defenders!

                  And the second with Madre de Pantalones Holgados (Mother of Baggy Pants), the Sept leader of the Almeda Garou and a very no-nonsense Bone Gnawer. Practical and to the point she is proud of how far Mexico City has come and how the largest Sept in Mexico has its Caern and a hearth for their protector Quetzalcoatl.

                  This section is framed by the Pattern Recognition pack (named for their totem) which is collecting tales over the world to contribute to the Geomid Network. Geomid Network is a global Garou lore reference that even uses spirits to convey deeper meanings of concepts, such as claw mark symbols or even smells. They popped up in the first chapter, and will be popping up throughout to book.

                  It's evocative, deeply immersed into things I want to research more about and filled with interesting tidbits about the Rite, the people performing it and the people it's performed for. However, it suffers from the fact that the symbolic song is broken up in a font that's hard to read and the tale has multiple points of perspective each interrupting each other. This is usually done much better in the book, and here just feels messy and hard to track. The interludes and insertions detract from the piece and unnecessarily shift focus from what it should be on. I did not need the intrusions and the background on the Geomid Network in this section and it would have been much more welcome later on.


                  Legacy of the Impergium
                  From the perspective of a Red Talon who went deep into the Umbra and found mysterious places where the Impergium still exists, this section is a strange one as it's written through the impressions of the emotions and scents the Red Talon lived through, and conveyed through the spirits. It's a novel and evocative technique, playing up the usual storytelling tip of giving descriptions of unexpected dimensions in your storytelling.

                  The section has some interesting ideas about the oldest of days - with the Red Talon moniker being given to them by the Homids, and how Unicorn itself may have been a vastly different spirit. Unicorn is presented as a treacherous spirit of war that struck at Garou hunting men. And how some of these Garou decided to follow the Unicorn, while Griffin protected the ones that remained true to the cause. About a village that fought back and which knew how to kill Garou without Silver (maybe with the help of a magic potion!).

                  The story ends with an unexpected conclusion for a Red Talon, while the Impergium was a way to protect Gaia it had to stop, for it was destroying the Garou from within. Something I heartily agree with.


                  The Fate of the Bunyip and the Dreamtime
                  The key figure in this section is Amber-Dreaming. We hear of her exploits from people carrying her message and people involved with her before we finally hear from her towards the end. This leads into a common theme in the book - of finding and bringing back the lost tribes. Amber-Dreaming wanted to know more of the Bunyip and questioned any and all sources, even following up with the Fera. This earned her a lot of grief, but also allowed her to look into the Mnesis with the Gumagan. She and her pack travelled the breadth of Australia, seeking to tell all the Garou that they need to accept their responsibility for what they did to their cousins. And that only then could they find the lost Bunyip that have moved into the songlines to avoid the depredations of the Garou.

                  This led the pack through outrage, violence, pain and suffering, ultimately splintering them. But the message has been taught and the Garou seem to have taken it to heart. Now a new generation is sought, one willing to track the stories and the songlines, and find where the Bunyip's spirits are, and bring them back through reconcilation and peace.

                  Alas, things are not that simple, for one of her packmates tried to bring back the Bunyip kin, but he brought back something wrong. Someone seems to be out for Amber-Dreaming and she has dissapeared. The Bunyip spirits themselves are furious at all European Garou. Time seems to work strangely in the Songtime and Amber-Dreaming has experienced many things that were and will be. We learn more theories of the Bunyip - how they were more likely multiple tribes than one, how they may have performed their own War of Rage and how they took their forms through the Rite of Spirit-Skin-Blessing from the Gumagan.

                  It is a chapter about the folly and pride of the Garou and how violence is easy. How tradition is comforting, but how they need to accept their errors and the errors of their forebearers and step up. It's a sentiment I agree with and one I feel is key to Werewolf.


                  Kinfolk troubles
                  This section starts with a lot of drama from a Bone Gnawer how troubling things are happening to the Kinfolk and how the Garou need to be super careful. My first impression was rolling my eyes, then she shows the reader what she is worried about. And oh, hell it is bad. The Skin Dancers have found glorious new ways to recruit unhappy and uncertain Kinfolk and have strong new allies, some of which allow them to hide their digital traces. They present themselves as a support group for Kinfolk rights, but they're recruiting for their own purposes and their own war.

                  The next tale is even worse - it's a story about families and how once a Garou falls in battle their family is next. It's a grisly tale of a husband expecting his wide and figuring out that her pack has fallen and they are coming for him and the kids. And that they are better of dead than caught. A tale conveyed by ghosts.

                  Thankfully after the two gut punches, we end on a happier note, about a Kinfolk named Aimee Collins infiltrating a Rainbow facility, identifying the trap set up by Pentex stooges and enabling the Rainbringers pack to destroy the operation while keeping the local people safe. A win if there ever was one, and a clear demonstration of how much a kinfolk that can enter places the Garou cannot can help out.

                  The Garou do not appreciate their Kinfolk enough and need to accept that they are a part of their lives and their culture, they are shutting out allies and families in a war that everyone is a part of. I've loved the tone here as it shows off both the horrors of the life as a Kinfolk and how easy it is to prey on those that have been treated unfairly. Yet, still it ends by saying that the Garou need to be better and embrace their kin if they want to make a difference.


                  The Galliard's Tale
                  Again the old man awaits at the flame, rummaging about how hard it is to inspire those people that act before they think, and how many dark things stay unspoken to keep the fires burning. Yet, the future is in their hands.


                  Plot Hooks
                  This section offers more on each of the topics, but again I'll single out the ones that were most interesting to me
                  • Veil clean-up after a public First Change
                  • How to help and participate in the Great Caern Building Effort
                  • The Geomid Network under assault and data being corrupted
                  • Spirits of the Wolf Slayers of the Impergium - and their tools
                  • Dealing with the dead Kinfolk after a horrible assault of the Wyrmspawn​

Water-that-is-Born sings of the Tribes and their trials and how Older Brother kept a watchful eye, they sing of the past and the future. The rite keeps going as a battle is joined around the Sept, and is successful.

                      It concludes with two interviews - one with Water-that-is-Born - which is all about accepting those willing to bleed and die for the land, even if they may not be of the tribes that once owned it. A strong statement from a member of Older Brother. One that most likely ties into the conclusion where mysterious shadows of the old defenders of what is under Almeda assist the rite defenders!

                      And the second with Madre de Pantalones Holgados (Mother of Baggy Pants), the Sept leader of the Almeda Garou and a very no-nonsense Bone Gnawer. Practical and to the point she is proud of how far Mexico City has come and how the largest Sept in Mexico has its Caern and a hearth for their protector Quetzalcoatl.

                      This section is framed by the Pattern Recognition pack (named for their totem) which is collecting tales over the world to contribute to the Geomid Network. Geomid Network is a global Garou lore reference that even uses spirits to convey deeper meanings of concepts, such as claw mark symbols or even smells. They popped up in the first chapter, and will be popping up throughout to book.

                      It's evocative, deeply immersed into things I want to research more about and filled with interesting tidbits about the Rite, the people performing it and the people it's performed for. However, it suffers from the fact that the symbolic song is broken up in a font that's hard to read and the tale has multiple points of perspective each interrupting each other. This is usually done much better in the book, and here just feels messy and hard to track. The interludes and insertions detract from the piece and unnecessarily shift focus from what it should be on. I did not need the intrusions and the background on the Geomid Network in this section and it would have been much more welcome later on.


                      Legacy of the Impergium
                      From the perspective of a Red Talon who went deep into the Umbra and found mysterious places where the Impergium still exists, this section is a strange one as it's written through the impressions of the emotions and scents the Red Talon lived through, and conveyed through the spirits. It's a novel and evocative technique, playing up the usual storytelling tip of giving descriptions of unexpected dimensions in your storytelling.

                      The section has some interesting ideas about the oldest of days - with the Red Talon moniker being given to them by the Homids, and how Unicorn itself may have been a vastly different spirit. Unicorn is presented as a treacherous spirit of war that struck at Garou hunting men. And how some of these Garou decided to follow the Unicorn, while Griffin protected the ones that remained true to the cause. About a village that fought back and which knew how to kill Garou without Silver (maybe with the help of a magic potion!).

                      The story ends with an unexpected conclusion for a Red Talon, while the Impergium was a way to protect Gaia it had to stop, for it was destroying the Garou from within. Something I heartily agree with.


                      The Fate of the Bunyip and the Dreamtime
                      The key figure in this section is Amber-Dreaming. We hear of her exploits from people carrying her message and people involved with her before we finally hear from her towards the end. This leads into a common theme in the book - of finding and bringing back the lost tribes. Amber-Dreaming wanted to know more of the Bunyip and questioned any and all sources, even following up with the Fera. This earned her a lot of grief, but also allowed her to look into the Mnesis with the Gumagan. She and her pack travelled the breadth of Australia, seeking to tell all the Garou that they need to accept their responsibility for what they did to their cousins. And that only then could they find the lost Bunyip that have moved into the songlines to avoid the depredations of the Garou.

                      This led the pack through outrage, violence, pain and suffering, ultimately splintering them. But the message has been taught and the Garou seem to have taken it to heart. Now a new generation is sought, one willing to track the stories and the songlines, and find where the Bunyip's spirits are, and bring them back through reconcilation and peace.

                      Alas, things are not that simple, for one of her packmates tried to bring back the Bunyip kin, but he brought back something wrong. Someone seems to be out for Amber-Dreaming and she has dissapeared. The Bunyip spirits themselves are furious at all European Garou. Time seems to work strangely in the Songtime and Amber-Dreaming has experienced many things that were and will be. We learn more theories of the Bunyip - how they were more likely multiple tribes than one, how they may have performed their own War of Rage and how they took their forms through the Rite of Spirit-Skin-Blessing from the Gumagan.

                      It is a chapter about the folly and pride of the Garou and how violence is easy. How tradition is comforting, but how they need to accept their errors and the errors of their forebearers and step up. It's a sentiment I agree with and one I feel is key to Werewolf.


                      Kinfolk troubles
                      This section starts with a lot of drama from a Bone Gnawer how troubling things are happening to the Kinfolk and how the Garou need to be super careful. My first impression was rolling my eyes, then she shows the reader what she is worried about. And oh, hell it is bad. The Skin Dancers have found glorious new ways to recruit unhappy and uncertain Kinfolk and have strong new allies, some of which allow them to hide their digital traces. They present themselves as a support group for Kinfolk rights, but they're recruiting for their own purposes and their own war.

                      The next tale is even worse - it's a story about families and how once a Garou falls in battle their family is next. It's a grisly tale of a husband expecting his wide and figuring out that her pack has fallen and they are coming for him and the kids. And that they are better of dead than caught. A tale conveyed by ghosts.

                      Thankfully after the two gut punches, we end on a happier note, about a Kinfolk named Aimee Collins infiltrating a Rainbow facility, identifying the trap set up by Pentex stooges and enabling the Rainbringers pack to destroy the operation while keeping the local people safe. A win if there ever was one, and a clear demonstration of how much a kinfolk that can enter places the Garou cannot can help out.

                      The Garou do not appreciate their Kinfolk enough and need to accept that they are a part of their lives and their culture, they are shutting out allies and families in a war that everyone is a part of. I've loved the tone here as it shows off both the horrors of the life as a Kinfolk and how easy it is to prey on those that have been treated unfairly. Yet, still it ends by saying that the Garou need to be better and embrace their kin if they want to make a difference.


                      The Galliard's Tale
                      Again the old man awaits at the flame, rummaging about how hard it is to inspire those people that act before they think, and how many dark things stay unspoken to keep the fires burning. Yet, the future is in their hands.


                      Plot Hooks
                      This section offers more on each of the topics, but again I'll single out the ones that were most interesting to me
                      • Veil clean-up after a public First Change
                      • How to help and participate in the Great Caern Building Effort
                      • The Geomid Network under assault and data being corrupted
                      • Spirits of the Wolf Slayers of the Impergium - and their tools
                      • Dealing with the dead Kinfolk after a horrible assault of the Wyrmspawn​

                      ​

                      What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.

                                          So I've still not managed to read the Philodox chapter, and the Theurge and Ragabash ones got delayed by work. So let's get on with it.



                                          Chapter III - Theurge Tales

                                          The Garouroboros, or Abominations everywhere
                                          The Theurge chapter starts with an old friend, Mari Cabarah, speaking to us of her dreams. Dreams that imply a horrible new threat is emerging, one that may take her away with it - a new and important Abomination. She starts asking about it and proceeds to dissapear. This seems to be a general thing for the old cast in this book, they show up only to disappear under mysterious circumstances. And while I was intrigued about the whole concept and what it really means, the section decides to give us two POV's on relationships between vampires and the Garou.

                                          One is an old Galilard known as Grandmother that gives us yet another origin for the Vampires (as parts of the Wyrm that turned into serpents and slithered into the world) and tells the young Garou how they are dangerous and should be avoided for their risk for the Garou is greater than the need to destroy them. The other is a Bone Gnawer Theurge in Chicago who is presented under a pseudonym. Why? Because the guy in question has a solid working relationship with a local vampire known as "Tyrus". The two meet so that they could discuss the strange rumors and dreams, and we learn that Vampires don't want abominations any more than the Garou do, and that while Garou have been having troubling dreams, Vampires have been obsessively scribbling a "Garouroboros". Both sides seem disturbed by the situation and the vampire even seems to care for the Garou's safety. Of course the Theurge proceeds to dissapear.

                                          The final section of this is about a Silent Strider called Yasmine that hunts the spirit Raven to find out more of the Garouboros. I'm not enamored of the presentation of Raven in this story, but it works in the context of a quest for knowledge. We learn two things - the person that the visions are about wanted to become an Abomination and they have a plan. There may be an implication that there is more than one, but it's unclear. I'd definitely go with more than one if I was using it in a tale. A kind of a secret society of Abominations?

                                          This whole section is a very mixed bag. Again it's not poorly written, but it spends so much time discussing the relations between vampires and Garou and what the abominations are that it really gives us very few hooks for the central mystery. The out of character section is not much better there. And why I find the topic intriguing, I'd have to do the heavy lifting for any storyline based on this. Oh, and I hate the "Garouroboros" as a name. It's in this strange crossroads of a dadjoke and WoD mysticism that just makes me roll my eyes.


                                          New York, New York - Or, Why do We Fight?
                                          The narrator of the next section is Weasel the totem of a Garou pack that had been wrecked by walking into a nest of Banes unprepared. It opens its narraton by stating "I am Weasel". I giggled at that way more than I should have. Rowena, the last survivor wracked with guilt and the horrors that happend is taken in by Antonine Teardrop. Antonine proves to be an excellent listener and takes her into Central Park where they get dropped on by Mother Larissa to wax about how the Wyrm is most keen on exploiting those who have the least to rely on. However, the reason Antonine is taking Rowena here is to meet with Loba Carcasone. Now Loba is a veteran of the war against the 7th Generation - the wicked pedophiliac club of influential Wyrm cultists. She shares her story of how the Garou excised this foul tumor, and yet how there are still things to fight and how the war is never over.

                                          This is the core theme of the whole tale. Rowena is distraught and broken. She has lost her pack, she was even responsible for the death of one of her packmates. Her totem has abandoned her. She has seen how easily the Wyrm can roost next door to the defenders of Gaia and she has lost her will to fight. But all of the people she meets are here to impart a lesson - just because the war keeps going and may look hopeless, does not mean that you cannot win worthwhile victories. Loba's lesson is that there is one evil less in the world and that many children have been saved and will be protected due to the actions of the Garou. This is a leson Rowena takes to heart, performs the Rite of Cleansing on herself with Antonine and is welcomed back by Weasel.

                                          I enjoyed this one quite a bit. I wish there were some more NY hooks explored, but the story itself is a great teaching opportunity.

                                          nb. I think there is a layout error here, as there is a letter from Jacob Morningkill that I believe belongs more in the next two sections so I will address it there.


                                          Ch... ch.. changes in the Umbra
                                          This one is a bit of a catchall section, as it comes with a lot of short stories which all seem to be inspecting the current actions of a number of people and the interesting new developments that have been popping up, challenging some of the Garou ideas about the Umbra.

                                          Coming up from the last chapter is an illustration of a horrifying lighting storm in the Wild West, and the last words of Isaiah Morningkill to his son Jacob. His letter details how Isaiah valued the native Garou and blamed the settling Garou for messing things up and waking up the Storm Eater. How he has to admit the blame of his people and do what has to be done with the heroes of the other tribes. It's a farewell, a coming with terms and a powerful message for the Garou. It's a shame his son grew up to be an asshole.

                                          The first tale is one of a Garou family wracked with the horror of having their child possessed by a Bane. Their grief and rage such that when an ally shows up offering them help they are sooner to drive them away at clawpoint than concede that they need help. Yet the Older Brother chosen of the Thunderbird persist and are able to finally persuade them into demonstrating a new tool they have recovered from the Umbra - The Mockery Curing Way. This arduous and dangerous Rite can cure some of those possessed, and the pack is willing to teach others how to do this.

                                          The second one is a discussion about different totems and what they mean to their tribes. The person conducting this interview seems to have picked three specific totems well known for being slightly askew for Gaian totems - Fenris, Griffon and Great Wendigo. And while the first is pretty much what you expect to hear about Fenris, the story about Great Wendigo is the really intriguing one, because it posits that the names that we know for the Native American tribes are the names that we know them under are names the Wyrmcomers assumed and they kept using them because it was simpler than to teach them the right words. Not just that but they have other names for their Totem, and prefer to have it named as "Great Wendigo". The Red Talon doesn't want to play because Griffon has no mysteries for those that are of man, and he knows what and why the person conducting the interview is doing. That person being Miguel Gutierrez who is collecting his bravery and secrets of the lost Totems for what he must do.

                                          The Return of Father Gutierrez - Or How the Bat was won. This is one of the things I really regretted W20 dropped, because this character and his quest for redemption of the Shadow Lords and the reclamation of Bat has always been one of the big hooks in Revised and I felt it was the right direction for the game and for the Garou as a people. Not only does talk of his quest, but says that he has succeeded and now his pack's totem is Bat. Good job, Father, glad to have you back. We also learn that Bat has multiple facets and that at least one of them wants to stop with the Wyrmish nonsense, while another is fully committed to the Wyrm's cause.

                                          I'll also deal with two other bits that are not in this section of the chapter but I feel it belongs here more then the final chapter. There is spirit recording of a torture session where the Margrave has caught a vampire and is enumerating the horrible things that he will do to it, while the Vampire's terrified thoughts are recorded. It's mostly used to deliver how the Margrave could be morally ambiguous and some thoughts from the narrator on how while the Margrave is brutal he is still one of the greatest hopes for the Garou.

                                          The reason he is torturing the Vampire is because he wants to know what it knows of Albrecht. My pet peeve regarding the Margrave that keeps popping up in the books. On a number of covers and pieces of art he is lurking, menacing and threatening Albrecht and positioned as his great rival. However, that's utter gibberish. It's about as silly as Albrecht on the cover of TB: Silver Fangs flanked by the Kremlin. The two of them have most likely never met. They operate in completely different spheres and have little reason to engage with each other. Albrecht may be the King, but he's the kind of king that does his own thing and doesn't lord it over anyone, he's spending more time drinking and adventuring, than being the bearer of the Silver Crown. Yuri is building his alliance all over Europe and most of his allies are steadfastly loyal to him. Even in Apocalypse the two of them operate in different Theaters. Of course Albrecht could be a threat to Margrave's plans if he started pushing, and I'm fine with him getting all the info he can, but the whole big "conflict" between them is just a load of poppycock.

                                          The chapter ends with a sidebar called Troubled Reconciliation, which is something I'd recommend for anyone looking to bring back lost tribes and lost breeds. It speaks of plans how you could educate a new generation in the Umbra, making sure that you ease them into Garou society and make sure they are ready to deal with the challenges. Considering that this is something that is sponsored by the Margrave and a pack called Lost Light, it could have more sinister undertones. Especially considering the line about how "if it fails, noone needs know".



                                          Mysteries of the Cosmos, or Rage Across the Heavens: Electric Boogalloo
                                          This is a section all about the Celestines of the Planets and we get to hear a set of tales from different people and how they interact with the Celestial Incarna.
                                          ​
                                          First is of Desmond-Dances-On-Silver lying on his deathbed, surrounded by his family and telling them the glories of Luna's Garden and the glorious Moonstone he brought from her abode. A unique Pathstone that can open moon bridges to any place with a Pathstone. It was charming to see this exchange nad reinforces the fact that family is key to most Garou relationships. Desmond somehow becomes Delores by the plot hook section, though.

                                          Then we hear of a Garou who fought the guardians of Helios and when he died fighting them was brought back by Katanka Sonnak itself. Makes for a great bar story, and a unique thing to look for if you really, really need someone back mayhaps. We hear of Abdel who went into the traps and challenges of Nerigal the Hunter to claim him as a totem to save his people. The stories end with Samantha, a Theurge that engaged Shantar of Neptune and solved her puzzle to gain a great artifact to guide the Garou in the future.

                                          All in all, it's short and sweet, and refersto the different challenges and facts of the realms you can visit in Rage Accross The Heavens. Wish we saw some Rorg, but this was great, especially Samantha's and Desmond/Delores tale.

                                          Again. We end with the old man who challenges the Theurge for the things it wishes it didn't know and having to guide the clueless and violent. Yet for all the darkness, maybe the Theurges can see the light as well. All in all, a solid chapter, but feels slightly more disjointed than the first two, with a less concentrated set of topics. Still, a lot of fun to read.



                                          Plot Hooks
                                          This section offers more on each of the topics, but again I'll single out the ones that were most interesting to me
                                          • I do feel the lost Elder trope is a bit overused, by I really want to explore what happened to Mari
                                          • The Maeljin competition to corrupt New York and manifest one of them within it
                                          • The chase for Bat
                                          • The Troubled Reconciliation sidebar does more for me than the hook itself

                                          What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.
