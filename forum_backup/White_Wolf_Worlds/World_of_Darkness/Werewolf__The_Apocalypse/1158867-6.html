                        I'm going to commit the grievous twofold sin of enacting necromancy on a thread on a game I've never played, but if my perspective as an outsider means anything:

                        I don't have an issue with the idea that the game has environmental themes or an environmentalist message. I think the idea of a game where you help to curb corporate excess and pollution with animistic superpowers is cool and fine.

                        That's why I could never really enjoy Werewolf. In this world, one very specific version of anti-humanistic, luddite philosophy is the objective cosmic truth and everyone who disagrees is the pawn of an eldritch abomination.

                        A major source of these issues was the early developer's efforts at trying so hard to be the polar opposite of Vampire. Out in the wilds, being more about living in tune with the planet's natural cycles, and addressing a lot of environmental and Native activism, and post-Cold War emergence of nationalism in the early 90s*. Despite my love for all things science, I can say that the modern world has not benefited my people in many ways. We joke a lot that being Native in the 21st century is like living in the post-apocalypse for us. That part really resonated with me, along with making Native tribes a full part of the setting. Beyond that, you consider nuclear warfare, global warming, and all that jazz, it can look very apocalyptic. Unfortunately the presentation of often came off as very black and white. The Wyrm being linked with solely modern industrial pollution, and the Weaver with technology and science instead of something more nuanced, such as leaving its former mentioned attachments being more byproducts of corruption and stasis, it becomes problematic. Then there's the rather poor presentation of many tribes as 2D ethnic stereotypes, and making a pop-culture noble savage view of Native American spirituality the default didn't help either. I can see why it pushes away many potential players.

                        There's a lot of potential with the themes, which is why I still really like WtA and the rest of the WoD.

                        * - I spoke with a lot of authority in my wording on this. Didn't mean too. It was meant as my personal assessment on why WtA went the way it did in relation to the previous game and the RL political/social circumstances around the time.

Like I said in my previous post, there were a lot of problems incorporating Native spirituality into the game.

                                                        As for the Weaver, she's about predictability, order at any cost, and turning everything into a tool. Also, going with the game's metaphysics, with the soul and flesh completely severed, both will die and fall into oblivion. You also need to look at the price of technology, such as the exploitation and colonialism that fueled the creation of our modern world to present day manifestations of this in the form of actual and virtual slavery that goes on in Africa and Asia so we can have our smart phones. It feeds into both the Weaver and the Wyrm. This is quite different than the research that goes into understanding how all life is part of a chain, and finding sustainable ways for us all to live here, and so on and so forth; the actual science that is fed by wonder, creativity, and understanding.
