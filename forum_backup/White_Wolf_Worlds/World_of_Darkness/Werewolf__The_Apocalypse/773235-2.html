        The reason the core books are written so lupines use disciplines or leeches use gifts is because they draw upon that corebook's rules for running those things. If you look at each line as a holistic entity, however, you will see that they are written more faithfully. For example, Pariah in Children of the Night is written using rules from a Werewolf supplement along with the actual Werewolf rules and with the relevant rules from Vampire. If the games were truly written as you claim they are, then Pariah would have been written entirely with Vampire rules and no reference back to abomination rules in Werewolf. For that matter, the abomination rules themselves contradict your claims.

        So the core books write up the various supernaturals in terms of the rules in that core book, but that doesn't mean that the different supernaturals are genuinely being treated as something different from their home lines. And the entire idea that each cWoD game is in its own little pocket universe is a terrible explanation for what amounted to poorly thought out game design (writing the games so that crossovers would be difficult to impossible) that was not supported in any given line's supplements.

The fact is if the WoD was unified as you have stated then Hunter the Reckoning instead of Foe books would have went with hey buy Vampire the Masquerade to make vampires. You want Werewolves pick up Apocalypse. At anytime after the 1st edition of Vampire was printed White Wolf could have said hey let's put Vampires from Masquerade into Werewolf and listed a brief summary of powers. The fact is they didn't. I can play Werewolf the Apocalypse and only Werewolf the Apocalypse without buying a single other line in the WoD.

I don't think the writers were lazy, I think that fans wanted to be able to do crossovers and so every time that a writer tried it bit the game lines involved.
