    It's been so long since I read the original Get of Fenris tribebook, I forgot they ever mentioned the binding of Fenris.

    In my own chronicles, I also wanted to reference the legend, but didn't want to make the Fenris spirit seem like some kind of chump. So in my version, after some offense Fenris intended to materialize in the Realm and really go after his enemies. And all the spirits were really alarmed at what this might do. So one spirit - I think I made it an Incarna of the Mars Celestine because Tyr/Tiwaz is a variant of the war god - offered Fenris great chiminage - his own hand - if he would refrain his fury until the Apocalypse so that the Realm would be spared devastation. And Fenrir then went into a kind of quasi-slumber - still awake enough to be contacted and do things, but much less active than other Incarna.

    I thought that was a good enough explanation. Players could understand how such a story would be re-interpreted, but it would not be so offensive to the Garou of the tribe.

                Originally posted by Shadeprowler View Post
                About Tyr... a little funny quesy-historical tidbit

                Snorri Sturluson, a 13th century Icelandic historian, believes Odin was a famous warrior who led his people out of Troy and into Scandinavia.
                His greatness was such that he ascended to the status of a god, and became worshiped as one.
                His myth continued to grow, especially among Germanic peoples, and he eventually usurped Tyr as the chief god, both in myth and in religious practice and worship.
                I do know this, and in my chronicles this is actually true. Except that Odin did not come from Troy (around 1200 BC), but was part of the original Indo-European peoples as they were leaving the steppe and embark on the Indo-European invasions (Odin was likely part of the Yamnaya Culture peoples around 3000 BC, and he followed what would become the Germanic peoples into northern Europe). And that, of course, he was a vampire instead of just being a sorcerer.

                At that time, all the progenitors of the Indo-European cultures we know today had one mythology that eventually became all the different mythologies of the Greeks, Celts, Germans, Slavs, Indians, Persians, etc. that we know today. Little is known about it, but linguists and comparative mythologists have reconstructed some elements of it.

                Since Odin was just a neonate when the migrations began, it took him a long time before he could plausibly portray himself as an actual "god". In my chronicles, this process began some time before the Romans made contact with the Germanic peoples, and was accomplished sometime around the 6th century AD. This process slowly transformed the (fictionalized) Germanic pagan religion from a more "Gaian" centered faith heavily influenced by the Garou (and their kinfolk) and turned into the human sacrificing religion to Odin we know from history. This "corruption" is one reason the Get of Fenris really hate Odin.

                This is just part of the background, and the details aren't very relevant to Garou PCs in any kind of modern chronicle. But this is the kind of archaeology and fitting actual myth and history to setting lore that I often do.

                    Since there are fire spirits named the Surtur that are part of Fenris's brood in Axis Mundi, this makes a lot of sense. We would expect that Surtur - one of the great enemies of the gods (just like Fenris) - should be part of the Wyrm's forces, but the spirits named after him are Gaian. It just makes reconciling the mythology to Garou lore really difficult. Everyone is going to have their own interpretation, which is why I think taking "inspiration" from myth rather than stating clearly what is going on is a better approach. There's been some great ideas on this thread of how people have done that in their own chronicles, but they are all mutually contradictory. Which is fine, everyone will approach it in the way they like best.

                    In terms of the outcome of the Apocalypse, I prefer the idea that the outcome is essentially unknown (which is why it is important to fight). In the early days when Garou numbers were more plentiful, the Wyld was flourishing, and the great Garou heroes lived, I like the idea that the Garou easily assumed they were going to win. The next age was going to be great and cleansed Gaia of evil. But in the modern day with their numbers reduced, various ecological damage, vampires flourishing, and Garou culture, ideals, and identity in decline, many Garou assume they are either going to lose or that the odds are stacked against them. Those that remain optimistic are seen as delusion. They fear they are going to lose and the next world to come will be everything they are struggling against. It's a cultural collapse.

                        One thing I like in Werewolf are ancient artifacts and relics and the like. Since the Garou have fetishes and sometimes name them and have legendary reputations for them (primarily for klaives, but not exclusively), I think it's appropriate if their enemies do as well. And we have a rich trove of mythology to mine.

                        While not from mythology, we already know about the Blood Horn from the Wolves of the Sea sourcebook. There's already been some discussion of that on this thread.

                        In an earlier Vampire thread, I discussed the Eye of Odin as a vampire relic inspired by similar "relics" mentioned in the original Storytellers Guide to the Sabbat, so I won't detail it here. But here is the link to the thread for those interested. Instead, I'll talk about some other artifacts that the Host should have, once had, or are searching for.

                        Another vampire relic is the Head of Mimir. In my chronicles it is the decapitated, mummified head of an ancient Malkavian vampire who once accompanied the All High before he was destroyed. The fae members of the Host believe Mimir willingly allowed himself to be sacrificed by the fae in a magical ritual in order to seal the alliance between them and the All High. That is how important Mimir viewed the alliance between the All High and House Aesin in preventing Ragnarok. The head is contained in a sack and would be easily confused with the preserved head of any bog mummy.

                        Anyone who consults the head can evoke the memories of Mimir. The head may provide five additional dice like the Ancestors Background (difficulty 6) for any skill or language the ST sees fit for an ancient Malkavian vampire destroyed thousands of years ago to know. Because consulting the Head takes time, it is best that these additional dice are best used for Knowledges, as combat skills and the like are not a practical use of consulting the Head. The head of Mimir can also provide advice from the perspective of a Malkavian vampire who was destroyed during the Nordic Bronze Age. Occasionally, the audience will hear advice and memories from the Head so alien and bizarre that he descends into crazed fits. If a Storyteller does not want to decide what information it contains, she can have the character roll Intelligence (difficulty 9). A botch means the wearer has called up knowledge and memories he would rather not have.

                        The Head of Mimir is currently in possession of the National Museum of Denmark where the head is simply identified as “Ulfeldt Man”, an extremely well preserved head of a bog mummy. At some point, the vampire Brynhild will discover its location and attempt to retrieve it.

                                    Black Fox I didn't want to go into too much detail just in case Shadowrun was not going to make the cut here. The extra time also allowed me to dig out the book and refresh myself. Essentially Threats is one of the best books for Shadowrun (and one of my favourite RPG books in general). It presents a number of antagonists and well, threats that are presented as in character conspiracy theories and or dubious accounts posted onto a Shadowrunning forum. Then these accounts are spiced up by commentary of other Shadowrunners.

                                    Winternight themselves are presented through a lens of a WMD investigator who got recruited into serving them through addiction to visions of himself as a grand Übermensch warrior and threats of blackmail against his family. The man snaps and murders his family and blows up his offices, posting his account into the forums.

                                    As presented, Winternight:
                                    • Is a secretive cabal of evil shamans serving the destructive aspect of Raven and Wolf (Banes in WoD parlance)
                                    • Their goal is to achieve Ragnarok at their own conditions, enabling them to ascend into full Godhood and take over the world that is to come
                                    • Towards this goal they seek to use nuclear weapons imbued with magic or biowarfare to incite a form of Fimbulwinter and triggering Ragnarok
                                    • They have no scruples and are willing to do anything to anyone if it furthers their goal (after all, Godhood is on the line). They will lie, cheat, infiltrate, kidnap, murder, addict people, torture people and even force their own members to do horrible things to their own families
                                    • One of their chief tools is a special type of induced euphoric trip that convinces the user that they are the greatest and most powerful berserkers in the world, seeking glory, blood and the death of their enemies. The trip is the best feeling ever overloading their senses and adrenal system into pure ecstasy, but slowly eroding their sense of self and selfcontrol
                                    • This makes the users willing to do anything to get more of this power (done through uploaded stimuli in Shadowrun, but I could see Dominate or Thaumaturgy being used to do this) Of course the trips are rationed by the members of Winternight to get more out of these people
                                    • They work in secrecy, coopting or murdering anyone that finds them, using intermediaries and PC's to help them out
                                    It just sprang to me last night that this spin could be used for the Brood of Wotan - eschatonic ideas to empower him and the leaders into true Godhood, and a Ragnarok on their own terms - they don't seek the Apocalypse as the Werewolves would see it, but could be collecting artifacts and weapons preparing for the ultimate rush. The fact that they manipulate and coopt humans and human organizations already stinks of Vampires and the whole "drug induced berserkers addicted to more blood and glory" is great fodder for mooks or servants, especially if they are fed blood as a part of their trips.

                                    What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.

                                                Shadowrun can afford to be more gonzo because the supernatural is obviously known in its setting. The WoD is "supposed" to have the supernatural more low key in order to preserve the idea that it is present, but hidden from most of humanity. So any idea stealing has to take that into consideration. They can act in a more direct and obvious way than the Camarilla, but if you want to keep the setting somewhat similar to the real world, you cannot go too far.

                                                I think the berserker angle has already been addressed by the idea that there is a certain kind of fomori that mirrors this. You may just need to twist some flavor text to bring more of a Winternight angle it seems to me.

                                                I do agree that in order to try to prevent Ragnarok (or gain advantage when it happens) that the Host could have no scruples and engage in a lot of things through its various camps (vampires, fae, mortal cults, etc.) Kidnapping, murder, blackmail, torture, etc. And it does somewhat fall into line in the early canon detail that Brynhild was responsible for the assassination of Swedish Prime Minister Olof Palme (although why she would do that was never explained; it was just a cheap way to attribute all sorts of mysterious events to vampires).

                                                But that is easier to say than do as an ST because we would need concrete details on specific things they'd do. Why would they kidnap or murder this person? Why would they sabotage or attack this power station or other installation? And so on? Whatever they did would need to be directly tied to preventing Ragnarok, and how would they determine that? We'd probably need some kind of "Prophecies of Odin" akin to the Prophecy of Phoenix that provide something actionable. They could very well be wrong in their actions (because they interpreted the prophecy wrong), but it would provide guidance to them. Another possibility is that such extremist actions is not to prevent Ragnarok, but done in retaliation for someone who defied them or prevented them from doing what they wanted (deliberately or inadvertently).

                                                I do think one way this could be translated was the Host taking a more active role in locating and obtaining occult artifacts. That is more low key than someone obtaining a nuclear weapon, and does dovetail into the idea I was just mentioning about artifacts in general. A missing bog mummy head from a Danish museum does not create the same sense of panic like one of Russia's nuclear warheads going missing. Some of these could just be drawn from a list of artifacts already associated with Odin or the Host. But others could be unrelated, but that the Host has identified. These could be things the Garou are also after - like one of the Talons of the Wyrm. Simply because the Garou has possession of Narlthus or something else does not mean the Host thinks it is in safe hands - they may want it because they think their hands are even safer!
