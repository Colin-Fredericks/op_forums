    One angle that could be interesting as a possible route to explain the enmity between Pegasus and Minotaur is what I’ll call the Athena Connection.

    (Disclaimer my inspirations for this are some half-remembered sections of Graves & Frazer and the thankless task of trying to justify after the fact what seems from a Doyalist perspective to be White Wolf’s cavalier practices of sprinkling liberally from randomly selected Greek Mythology references onto Black Furies and other Hellenic touching parts of the setting)

    The main things I want to touch on for this is Theseus, Perseus, & Bellerophon (and maybe Orestes tangentially).

    Starting with Theseus. Theseus is interesting because his stories actually have very little relation to Athena proper, despite him nominally being her polity’s most famous hero. There are however several notable female characters that Theseus interacts with I want to note; including Medea, Ariadne, Phaedra, & Helen. The most directly relevant here of course is Ariadne, oft-speculated to allegorically represent some sort of priestess figure who may have been co-opted into the upstart Attican revolt against Cretan power. Ariadne’s betrayal of her father for Theseus and then subsequent betrayal by Theseus is something I want to note in particular (in the first part taking the role of Athena as comprador I’ll describe later on and in the second part taking the role of victimized priestess figure, an interesting conflation). Also notable: Theseus’ opposition with Medea in the role of wicked step-mother (another possible glimmer of aristocratic youth in revolt against a female spiritual authority), Phaedra (Ariadne’s sister and Theseus’ wife who will later take on the role of Hippolytus’ tormentor in that sacred tragedy), and Helen (who needs no introduction and whose Theseus abduction of is arguably the root cause of the Trojan War, also note her sister Clytemnestra).

    That was laying some foundations but let’s really get into the meat now with Perseus. Somewhere in Graves, he puts forward the hypothesis that Perseus represents a certain class of aristocratic male youth of the archaic period (Who Frazer also characterized by their wanderings, their sudden rise to kingship after performance of heroic feats, their later mystified origins, etc.) who carry out a form of coup against older matriarchal religious systems. Perseus in particular gets pointed out for his role in the death of Medusa. As part of his wanderings he receives divine aid from Hermes & Athena, both of whom are high ranking stooges of the Olympian sky-king. Hermes as his son and intelligencer (proficient as he is in roads, disguises, tricks, thievery, and deceit) and Athena as his self-begotten daughter and comprador in the mytho-historic defeat of the old feminine cults. They give Theseus the aid he requires to sneak up upon Medusa and the Gorgons unseen and slay them, which Graves identifies as a major point of usurpation of the customary religious rites.

    And we owe her a satire on the memory of the man who first tilted European Civilization off balance, by enthroning the restless and arbitrary male will under the name of Zeus and dethroning the female sense of orderliness, Themis. The Greeks knew him as Pterseus the Destroyer, the Gorgon-slaying warrior-prince from Asia, remote ancestor of the destroyers Alexander, Pompey & Napoleon
    The above is the prelude given by Robert Graves before his poem “The Destroyer”, from somewhere in his Greek Myths. Sorry for not having the page number on hand, I’m transcribing this from a screen shot.

    (As an aside: I’m not going to defend the rigor of Frazer or Graves as historians of religion or Ancient Greece, but IMO their ideas are fitting for the romantic-influenced gothic-punk setting of the World of Darkness)

    Athena shows up again in the story of Bellerophon as the intercessor who grants him the golden bridle necessary to tame and master Pegasus. If we remember that Pegasus was born from the blood of Medusa landing upon Mother Earth (or erupting from the stump of her neck), this gift of a bridle, a classical symbol of superiority and domination, could be interpreted as another instance of Athena offering aid and assistance to that class of warrior-princes who would be the foot soldiers of religious insurrection against matrilineal custom (as she herself is, being born of father-alone). Notwithstanding that Bellerophon eventually oversteps his bounds and forces Zeus to reassert ultimate patriarchal authority, Bellerophon still accomplishes his purpose by slaying Chimera and mastering Perseus (however briefly). This added wrinkle in my opinion makes a lot of sense for the Black Furies’ position in the setting.

    Plus, Athena appears as a major role in the final part of the Orestaia, wherein she oversees the trial of Orestes for matricide in Athens. Here Clytemnestra (Helen’s sister btw, offering another connection to Theseus) has famously avenged the sacrifice of her daughter through the murder of Agamemnon (the proverbial king of kings in Homer) and is now being represented by the cthonic daemons the furies as Orestes’ prosecutors. In opposition to them is that son of Zeus and lover of civilization, Apollo, serving as Orestes’ defense. The trial takes on pretty strong undertones IMO of a Primordial/Cthonic/Feminine/“Savage” system of vengeance/justice which values mother-rite over father-rite and a Modern/Olympian/Masculine/“Civilized” system of vengeance/justice which values father-rite over mother-rite.

    Read this way we might even interpret Orestes as a sort of representative of the Minotaur. We first see the Minotaur represented as the victim of a usurpation against an old order, with hero slaying monster (depicted as Theseus overcoming the Minotaur with Ariadne’s aid; although perhaps the Minotaur’s subjection to Minos already represents a decay of a more pure BF mother-cult) and ends the cycle with the more ambiguous Orestes (of the generation after Perseus, Theseus, and the like) who instead of opposing a witch-sorceress or a monster is forced to conspire (with his sister Electra, in a role arguably analogous to Ariadne or Athena) against a purely mortal woman (the narrative of mother against son is sublimated in Theseus’ struggle with his step-mother Medea but then fully embraced the following generation with Phaedra & Hippolytus) [EDIT: actually scratch that, iirc Hippolytus is Theseus’ son by the Amazon-queen Hippolyta, so that’s still technically not incest; although Theseus & Heracles subjugation of the Amazon-queens is also another interesting wrinkle to add to the Black Furies reason to loath these warrior-princes], and ends the second play/begins the third wandering the countryside like a beast and whether or not he’s a civilized man or a lawless animal (in the eyes of the law/society) is not without question until the end of the third play.

    Perhaps somewhat tangential to the main thrust of this thread, but relevant enough as a possible lens for interpreting the Black Furies and the related stories that it felt worthwhile to include. Plus I do generally just find the idea to be a fascinating one.

                            Originally posted by Shadeprowler View Post

                            I don't think this was ever factually true. Unless I am unaware of some text you are referencing, I think you are misremembering some things.

                            In the Outcasts sourcebook, Minotaur is mentioned as just one of several abandoned or ignored totems that the non-Sam Haight Skin Dancers have made contact with. Minotaur is specifically named, but the others are mentioned as "others whose names I do not know" by the in-character narrator. In other words, there are more such spirits. The narrator just knows the name of one of them. Furthermore, collectively they don't teach a whole lot of Gifts. The narrator explains "a few minor gifts have been learned." This is a far cry from the statement that Minotaur can teach any gift or knows every gift. As written in Outcasts, the spirits who teach gifts to the Skin Dancers don't seem much different than any other spirits who teach gifts.

                            The one thing that is different about the Skin Dancers learning Gifts is that they don't have rank (made explicit in Players Guide to Garou, but only assumed but not stated previously), but can learn gifts of higher rank. This is unusual because all other spirits only teach levels of Gift to Garou of appropriate rank, presumably because this is part of the Pact (not to mention done at appropriately ranked caerns (lest they offend the spirit), something mentioned in the corebooks in the Gifts section, but often overlooked by players and STs). Yet Minotaur and other inclined spirits seem willing to teach Gifts to the Skin Dancers despite not having rank (or being at appropriately ranked caerns).

                            This places Minotaur and these other spirits (which I believe are best represented as his spirit brood) in a special category. It's like Minotaur is some kind of outcast or criminal spirit who operates outside the Pact either because he's some kind of "criminal spirit" acting in defiance of it, or perhaps it does not apply to him for whatever reason. I was planning on mentioning this in a future post, but wanted to mention it specifically here.

                            I don't think that means Minotaur is a more powerful spirit, or that he is more potent in giftlore than other spirits. But he doesn't seem to abide by the pact. I think that's most likely because the writers simply forgot that there is a limitation within the game of when one learn's gifts. I doubt it was intentional. However, I do think it is appropriate and interesting that somehow Minotaur ignores the rules for whatever reason. It makes Minotaur seem more mysterious and even menacing as a rulebreaker. That adds to making a the faction more interesting as an antagonist.

                                I think the W20 corebook, in particular, has some things that are intriguing. It talks about Minotaur’s spirit brood and its relation to Skin Dancers learning Gifts. In short, Minotaur’s broods can teach about any Gift (at least up to level 3) even if for higher XP costs than even out-of-tribe/breed/auspice are for normal Garou. Presumably level 4 or 5 gifts are also available, but are far fewer in number (to be decided by the ST).

                                So we know Minotaur has a spirit brood. Furthermore, this spirit brood is willing and able to teach Gifts despite Skin Dancers basically cheating the system. This not only has relevance for Skin Dancers, but I think it offers a source for Ronin to also learn new Gifts. Like Skin Dancers, normal Gaia spirits won’t teach Gifts to Ronin. But if Minotaur’s brood will teach Gifts to Dancers, why not Ronin as well? While Ronin are not supposed to be able learn Gifts and can’t advance in Rank, the Skin Dancers don’t have rank yet are able to learn not just Gifts, but Gifts higher than one level.

                                Though obviously Skin Dancers have some patronage relationship with Minotaur, and Ronin do not, that does not mean Ronin can’t petition Minotaur at all. The ST can allow Ronin to pack under Minotaur, and possibly even allow Ronin to learn Gifts without having Minotaur as a pack totem. They’d just need to establish some good Chiminage with these spirits - perhaps honoring or worshipping Minotaur, donating Gnosis, agreeing to kill a child of Pegasus, or whatever it is the ST sees fit.

                                This does not mean all Ronin would take advantage of it, but it’s a way to not only allow some Ronin to learn new Gifts, but to build up Minotaur as an antagonist in the game. The Outcasts sourcebook teases that Ronin may be gathering at a mysterious “Cranberry Caern” and that this may be a low level caern in New Jersey controlled by a pack of Skin Dancers.

                                So this provides two options for us. The first is that some Ronin can still learn new Gifts from spirits provided they make deals with Minotaur. The second option is if some Ronin are flocking to Minotaur or even Skin Dancers to learn Gifts, then now Ronin can also become members of the "new" Skin Dancer tribe. This makes the Skin Dancers a lot more dangerous. Realistically, there are probably only so many kinfolk who can successfully become Garou through the Rite of Sacred Rebirth. The requirements are very steep. On the other hand, there's plenty of Ronin out there. And now they have the chance to be accepted as children of Minotaur as well. Indeed, they can probably become the majority of the tribe very quickly.

                                Ronin learning Gifts is one thing. That won't substantially alter the setting. But allowing them to join the Skin Dancer tribe would probably be a big thing in any chronicle. I'm sure not all STs would want to allow this, but for those that do, it can be a way to generate a good crisis for the local Garou or even the Garou Nation as a whole.

                                                        Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                                                        Given the hidden history of Minoan Crete in the World of Darkness, I think it’d be cool if there was some kind of unique occult secrets based on here. We have the fertility secrets of the Apis, the lore of the Toreador antediluvian (who given her masquerade of various love/war fertility goddesses with connections to sacred bulls might have a lot of connection to Apis associated societies), potential ties to the labyrinth of the Black Spiral, and the demonic rituals of the Baali. I’m calling this group of lore as the Cretan Mysteries.
                                                        The Enlightened Society of the Weeping Moon from the Wild West supplements comes to mind. They were a masonic type sorcerous affiliation with a distorted view on Gaian lore - in part influenced by BSDs and/or the Tal'Mahe'Ra - and an internal structure that mimics the role of Garou Auspices. The society dissolved shortly before the first World War.

                                                        I think they would make a good predecessor organization for a modern Cretan Mysteries society and could act as a structural template. Their 'Theurges', called Horned Moons, practice a form of spirit control that is vaguely based on Saturnalia. Probably not a big stretch to assume they may also develop a shapeshifting path based on Lupercalia that ties them to the Skin Dancers and fertility Rites.

                                                        Diaspora of the Weeping Moon could have picked up on lore from an older Cretan society and fused their own into it to make up something new. I think they are pretty compatible with the mix of influences.
