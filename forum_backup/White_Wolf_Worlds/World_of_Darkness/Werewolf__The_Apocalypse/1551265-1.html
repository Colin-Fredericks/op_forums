    Grondr

    Now we come to the Grondr who haven’t gotten covered much since Accounting for the Dead. They get just shy of 40 pages.

    Preferring kinfolk with Gnosis is interesting, how common is that in the Savage Age? I assume more common than the incredibly rare ones in the modern era.

    I don’t remember the Grondr being so clearly aligned with the Destroyer Wyrm in earlier products. It is interesting that some Fera of this era are trying to split various members of the Triat into parts and claiming to only serve one. A constant trickle of Grondr falling to the Corruptor Wyrm is not going to help them avoid issues with the Garou.

    Spiritually active fungus is a nice detail. We already knew that the Apis are some of their strongest allies. Are some Grondr the first Black Spiral Dancers? That sounds terrifying.

    The section about the Grondr and Apis working together to control spiritually and physically corrupted plants is excellent. And probably the kind of thing nearly no Garou would understand.

    The bit about twin births being more common among Grondr kinfolk is cool. Letting the Werehyenas hunt any of their kind they feel are tainted is a good detail. We haven’t seen a ton on the Werehyenas, so details like this help establish them in the setting more.

    The details about how their territory is kept is very useful. Some Garou would probably see modifying the natural environment as Weaver and/or Wyrm taint depending on the changes. Also it may give players hints that they are near a Grondr sounder based on some of the stuff you mention.

    Also a description of their normal combat style is useful for a storyteller needing to run a group of Grondr warriors. And the mention of a bane cleansing gift they shared with the Gurahl is great. I don’t think it has been detailed so hopefully it shows up in the gifts section of this book.

    The symbols section is also very helpful. Painted and tattoos really help with a visual description and once players understand some of the basics you can give them hints of things without having to draw as much attention to them.

    And we get a quick shot of the Grondr view of the Umbra; their practice of showing up and calling out Fera for stuff is not going to make many friends. The details of the shift of their umbral realm into a modern one is great.

    A small section on the Grondr and Gurahl relations which also touches on the Apis. Reinforcing that if a campaign involves one of them extensively the others should show up sometimes. Garou attacking a fold or sounder might find that members of the other Fera are there. And a Werebear surrounded by a bunch of Wereboars is a very different problem than just the Wereboars.

    A treaty negotiated with a specific group of spirits is very interesting. Especially since other Fera are also involved and it has some unintended consequences. Acid Flux is a cool idea, and would make a great plotline in a campaign, even a non-Grondr one that has to deal with a Wyld corrupted Grondr in their territory and the fallout from that.

    More detail on the various “tribes” of Grondr, this type of detail is very useful in the Savage Age where regional differences can help make things more interesting. There was an American tribe as well, mentioned in Accounting for the Dead, it would have been good to have a few lines on. I also like the detail that much of their organization is matriarchies.

    And now their side of the War of Rage, probably the most interesting note in that the Wereboars fight especially hard in the “Near East”. Of course the Garou believe that no other Fera could know things the Garou don’t. The details about their totem, aligned spirits and the Wyrm is excellent.

    The creation of the Skull Pigs now makes a lot more sense than Accounting for the Dead’s version. It would be cool if a group of Wyld corrupted Grondr also survived.

    The Grondr had a long list of allies, and an equally long list of enemies. Again we hear about the Ajah-Abah. A tribebook on them would be cool.

    Now we get to the new mechanical stuff for the Grondr. 7 new gifts, several tied to the Acid Flux Wyld tainted Grondr mentioned earlier. Wyld poop is a very random thing to have an NPC do. One reused rite that is amusing. One fetish and 3 talens; the fetish is powerful but dangerous, the talens interesting and potentially the source of adventures. The three totems are useful.

    Gurahl

    The Gurahl are a conservative Fera, one that we have only really heard about one tribe of, the one that joined the Garou Nation. But the Gurahl have always featured heavily in the official write-ups about the War of Rage. Most of them will be driven into hibernation and at some point the Asian tribe driven to extinction. We already knew the stone eaters preferred neanderthals now we learn that they are preferred by the Gurahl across tribes. This suggests that some of the elders that sleep for millenia and return in the modern era are neanderthal rather than sapiens.

    A first Great Council implies at least a second, if not more.

    I am not sure exactly what is being gained by adding another, dead, tribe of Gurahl.

    The phrasing of the Dragon Slayers section is odd, the information is interesting and shows the damage that the Fera fighting among themselves is causing. But it is written as if the events are actively happening; also the War of Rage lasts for millenia, how long are the Gurahl preparing and how long does an individual Gurahl live?

    We get some details about the Gurahl social structure, which sounds somewhat different than modern. The sacrifice and rebirth stuff is very interesting. The Lifework is a cool expansion of their love of craft in modern times.

    There is a lot of extra stuff here that will make running a non-Stone Eater Gurahl much easier in the Savage Age. How they form groups, their relationships with kinfolk and many other details that will round out a story.

    The details of their annual social gatherings are super cool. These events should be used as parts of any Gurahl focused campaign. Information about the wider world can be provided, potential storylines started, allies, or rivals, made.

    Gurahl and Ratkin not liking each other makes sense, though my guess is the Ratkin have literally orders of magnitude more of their kind and can just overwhelm a Gurahl with numbers.

    Neanderthals turning to the Wyrm for survival is a cool detail that won’t end well.

    Now we get Savage Age names of the modern Gurahl tribes which is a nice detail. And in these short tribe write-ups we get plenty of story hooks. It is something I think the Savage Age has done well, that there is plenty going on that doesn’t require the War of Rage to dominate (or even appear in) a campaign. It sounds like a significant storyline could be done on the nightmare kings. Ice Stalkers being the most liberal of the tribes is interesting. There being a break off group of Stone Eaters that don’t join the Garou Nation is interesting. I would think they eventually get absorbed into the other tribes, probably mostly the Mountain Guardians.

    The rest of the Gurahl not being cool with a bunch of what the Stone Eaters do with the Garou Nation makes sense. The Rogijo are a good idea, and opens the option for a player character. And the Garou basically only seeing the world in black and white forces the Gurahl against them.

    The idea that the Gurahl did some kind of ritual to prevent the stone eaters from passing on certain things to the Garou is cool. I like that the Savage Age has a bunch of different groups doing incredibly powerful rites and rituals.

    Other Gurahl being the ones to put down the last of the Stone Eaters is an interesting detail along with the Death Bear’s response. Even in a non-Gurahl campaign, having that happen out of nowhere and shutting off Umbra access during a key moment is the kind of thing players will remember. During that time, are any Fera in the Umbra stuck on that side? Or are they forced out?

    Now we get into the mechanical side of the Gurahl, I have always thought their rotation through the various auspices is a cool idea. I like how here the general time when they transition from one to the next is given.

    Eight new gifts including one Stone Eater specific gift. Purge the Soul is a great gift and makes complete sense for the Gurahl. The reincarnation gift is also really cool. Three new rites, all are super cool. Two fetishes and a talens, the idea of a fetish being a powerful “true name” is very interesting, the others are good. Four different totems, again it is good to see totems for all sorts of different Fera.

        Appendixes

        We get a couple of pages on the idea of gods in the stone age, which is very helpful for people running campaigns where mortals are involved a bunch. I like that every big Savage Age book has some details on the time period to help with gaming in it. Then we get a list of what Fera are around in different eras; interestingly the Ananasi don’t appear on this list except maybe under “Insect Shifters” in the oldest era.

        Overall Thoughts

        This book is packed with information. Significant expansion in detail on all the Fera covered. In most cases basically the equivalent of getting a tribebook on each of them. One of the useful things is different Fera getting specific totems for them. As always there is great art.

        This book reinforces that the Garou won the War(s) of Rage because they were able to work together and their enemies weren’t. If the Apis, Grondr, Wlewa and their Hamaal allies, Ajah-Abah, Khara, other Bastet and Gurahl had all actually worked together, the Garou were doomed.

        I like that this book covered a bunch of Fera that hadn’t previously had a lot of detail in other Savage Age books. Granted I haven’t read ever single Savage Age product published prior to this, but I doubt the various Tribebooks, comics, short story collections and a sourcebook mostly on items really dive into the Gurahl, or Bastet. Again this book builds on everything that has come before and will add value to almost any Savage Age campaign. It is not easy to make that the case with book after book. Even if you were doing say a Mokole focused campaign, the information about other Fera you will encounter would be useful.

            Ancestral Memories: The Advanced Player's Guide to the Savage Age Vol 2

            First I should note that I got a free copy of this book. This book covers 5 Fera that we haven’t seen a ton from in other Savage Age products I have seen so far. I know there is a Corax book that I need to pick up at some point, but honestly the pre-Ice and Stone Ages stuff doesn’t really interest me. The other four, Mokole, Nagah, Ratkin and Rokea get mentioned in places but there hasn’t been a ton of detail. I suspect that will in some ways make this different from Ghost Lineages since most of those Fera had at least some detail in previous books. There are two appendices at the end covering more general Savage Age stuff. This book is also much shorter than Ghost Lineages.

            The Gurahl short story at the start is great, kind of surprising it is a Gurahl story when they aren’t a focus of this book. Still Gurahl are great.

            Corax

            I have always found the Corax interesting, though I think they can be difficult to include as a player character. They make great NPCs, and are super useful for getting information to the PCs that may otherwise be difficult for them to know. They can be a great way to introduce the concept of other Fera to players who have only dealt with werewolves.

            So the Corax are now the last member of a larger grouping of Fera called the Rapax that seem kind of like the Bastet in that it is a grouping of similar types of Fera. Are we going to see more about these Corax Memnae? That would be cool.

            Overall 33 pages on the Corax including several particularly good pieces of art. There are several pages of pre-pre-history for the Corax, it is well written but I don’t really care about ancient Mokole kingdoms from 10s of millions of years ago. The Mind-of-Thought-and-Memory is a cool idea. Corax with Mnesis, I would like to know more; though it would seem that ability dies out in the Savage Age. Still that would be a cool Corax to use as an NPC in the modern era. Corax helping to shape the Umbral realms of the dead is cool.

            The different philosophies of Corax are cool, and having both rooted and rootless Corax can be useful for a campaign.

            I knew real life ravens were smart, I didn’t realize they were as smart as the information in this book shows they are.

            Using slight modifications to feathers to store information is cool. Their reasons for liking trees and disliking fire are logical and interesting. The details of their Umbral realms are good; it would have been cool to have a nod towards Yggdrasil with their world tree.

            The three camps are all interesting and could each be interesting characters.

            The Corax part on the War of Rage should be interesting, they are one of the few Fera that get along with nearly everyone else. At the same time, knowledge is power, and few in the War of Rage want to share power. I suspect that most of the Corax of the era are killed and given how Corax create more of themselves their population growth is very slow, it will take generations to recover from major losses. The Wlewa being the ones who really target the Corax makes complete sense.

            The section on Corax use of language is really good, and can help give a Savage Age game a certain feel.

            The allies section gives us a little bit on the Werebats of the Savage Age, who I know have their own book but are not mentioned often elsewhere. I am interested in learning why they change their name. In the enemies portion there is mention of the Wlewa wiping out another Fera, when did this happen, I don’t remember it from the Ghost Lineages timeline. I really like the idea that the Rokea hate the Corax and the Corax don’t know why.

            The basic Corax mechanical stuff is good. I like that an entire Garou Auspice and Tribe gift lists are suggested as possible options to consider, that significantly expands the options available to Corax which would be important if you have 2 or more in a group, especially an all Corax campaign. 16 Gifts, including ones that only some Corax can take. Phoenix Raven is particularly cool. The combat gifts give some interesting options.

            Two interesting and potentially useful rites. Three fetishes and 2 talens all useful and fitting for Corax.

            This section ends with another great piece of art.

            Mokole


            I am somewhat skeptical about all the changes the Savage Age has made to Mokole ancient history. Let’s see if this section changes that. 39 pages for the Mokole. Another awesome art piece. I still dislike that the Mokole seem to have wildly gone against the will of Gaia, even more so than other Fera and seemingly suffered few consequences. Again the stuff about truly ancient times is well written but I just don’t care about that era.

            I find it interesting that as we get to the Savage Age section it says they are now only werecrocodiles. In the modern era, there are other reptile Mokole, including of course Komodo Dragons. I am not sure how I feel about the Mokole already seeming to be withdrawing from the wider world in the Savage Age.

            The details about how the Mokole organize are useful, but not really anything new. Experimentation on homids is interesting, hopefully we get more about that.

            I am slightly confused about why there appear to be no Mokole in the pure lands; the gators and crocodiles in Florida and nearby areas have been there a long time. The bit about colours is an interesting detail. It is always good to get details on how different Fera view and use the Umbra.

            We have a mix of existing and new streams, which I think is good. I like that they aren’t just regional groups, they have different organizational styles and alliances and rivals. It makes things more interesting, gives more replayability to the Mokole and could be a source of conflict if members of one stream visit another.

            And we get more detail on the various animal-kin types of Mokole. The Ao go extinct at some point, likely during the War of Rage. The Kratii are interesting, the idea that the Nagah and Mokole at one point were one Fera is interesting. I suspect the Nagah helped the Rokea finish them off.

            What is this about reawakened old ones? Can the Mokole slumber like the Gurahl?

            Mokole often being most concerned with preventing others from accessing Mnesis is interesting and could lead to some interesting adventures, or an excuse to have some Mokole NPCs show up somewhere unexpected. I was hoping the bit mentioned in ghost lineages about Denisovan bloodlines with a form of Mnesis would show up in this section, so I am glad to see it. Interestingly it is expanded to a bunch of different Fera and the Mokole don’t seem to realize that list includes the Grondr. We also get some idea of when this happens as the Khara are apparently “wiped out” presumably the european variety, since the Mokole don’t seem to have a presence in the Americas. That would mean this happens after 10,000 BC, which is slightly odd given the Denisovans should be gone by then, unless the ability transferred to Sapiens lines that mixed with them. The Ajah-Abah are listed here as the Ajah and the Abah, so perhaps at this point have different types of Hyena kinfolk and are different “tribes”. Of course the immediate explanation the Kucha have is that the Mokole are wyrmspawn.

            Overall the War of Rage stuff is good but fairly expected. I wish there was a bit more about Africa with the Kucha there. Their list of allies and enemies is mostly what you would expect.

            Mechanically Mokole are somewhat complicated, with their many different auspices and different Varnas. Getting a bunch of glyphs in this section is cool. Every Auspice having two different Garou lists to potentially draw from is a huge increase in potential options. 6 generic gifts, 4 Rites, 1 fetish, and 2 talens add a bunch of new options. Mnesis Venatus is a really cool gift, though all of them are interesting. The rite that allows for Mokole Metis to actually be born is a cool, twisted rite that could cause a ton of drama.

            Sorrow Stones are really cool, you would have to be very careful using them. Both talens are cool.

                Nagah

                A 35 page section on the Nagah. They have always been an interesting Fera that is difficult to use in a modern campaign due to being hidden from all the others unless you are running a pure Nagah group. Assuming they operate more openly in the Savage Age you could actually use one in a multi-Fera campaign.

                The most interesting part of the early history stuff is the Nagah once being considered the experts on the Umbra.

                Nagah conflict with various asian Bastet, probably mainly Khan, is logical and could be very interesting to game. Their more controlling nature over their kinfolk and even regular humans is different in a good way from many other Fera. As with most other Fera we get details of their organization, which is different than when they go into hiding during the War of Rage. Given their dependence on water, organizing based around river systems makes sense. Nagah being healers is interesting, they don’t have a ton of overlap with the Gurahl so they won’t really get in each other’s way.

                The European Nagah not needing water to access the umbra is interesting. And we get a specific named Nagah which is rare in this type of Savage Age book. Nagah valuing precious stones is also a nice detail, having information like this makes it easier to describe things in different ways for different groups.

                Like the other Fera in this book we see information about the Nagah’s view and use of the Umbra, also very helpful.

                It is good to see a version of the Nagah’s involvement with starting the War of Rage included. Them often being busy fighting Ratkin is an interesting way to keep them out of the main conflict. But the Nagah stabbing everyone from the shadows escalates things rather than calms them down, you can’t half ass killing your way to peace. All the stuff about them in the War of Rage make sense, and is well written. Again the Garou win, because their many enemies can’t work together.

                I find it interesting that the Nagah are worried about falling to the Wyrm. And things have gone badly enough that at least one regional group of them are entirely gone. We do get some details about Wyrm corrupted Nagah though which is cool. There are also details about the formation of the Sesha which will lead the modern version of the Fera.

                Their list of friends is short, and even those are shaky at best. Their two main enemies probably the most populous Fera.

                As usual we get a basic overview of the mechanics of Nagah, and like other Fera in this book there are suggestions for Garou lists to consider for different auspices. 7 general gifts, 2 rites, 1 fetish, 4 talens and 3 mutually exclusive merits that each unlock a set of 5 gifts.

                The gifts, rites and fetish are all decent, nothing stands out as particularly awesome or bad. The talens being incenses that has different effects on Nagah and non-Nagah is really cool, I like it when Fera have talens, fetishes, rites and the like that do or use things the Garou would never care about. The merits and the gift lists they unlock are probably the best part of this section.

                Ratkin

                Oh the crazy skaven, I mean Ratkin. Like the Nagah they can be hard to use in modern nights, though Garou having to deal with a nest of these can be a nasty problem to deal with. From what little I have seen on Savage Age Ratkin they aren’t quite as insane as in the modern era, so perhaps could join a mixed Fera party. This section is 53 pages, that gives them easily the largest section so far, larger than the first Savage Age tribebook.

                It is interesting that the Ratkin are not worldwide at this point, and aren’t in significant parts of europe.

                The Banestorm is a cool idea, especially with it leading to the first Formori; very skaven coded thing. This seems like a really cool era to game, even if you aren’t playing Ratkin. The impacts on the gauntlet from this are also nice. Getting judged by the Nagah and Mokole for a 65 million year old deal that they don’t remember seems on brand for all involved. This whole situation also messing with an Ajah-Abah rite is an extra cherry on top. Speaking of those werehyenias, they have been mentioned in a bunch of places but have never gotten their own time in the limelight, hopefully that changes soon. Different Fera pulling off powerful rites throughout the Savage Age is a good thing, they can make an excellent backdrop for a campaign to be happening during. An Ajah-Abah campaign hunting Ratkin would make a great campaign.

                And it looks like the Savage Age is officially pushing aside the Ananasi as the ones responsible for the Neaderthal cull in their version of events. At this point I doubt the Ananasi even exist in the Savage Age version of the world.

                Rats of different colours favouring different roles I think is new. A befits a Wyld aligned Fera, their organization is rather chaotic. Calling their pack sized groups Mischiefs is good.

                Losing the Bards will break the Ratkin entirely.

                Blood memory sounds like a slightly different version of Mnesis. What happens to a Crinos-born Ratkin if they aren’t exposed to the Birthing Plague?

                Detailing the various ways the Ratkin work to keep human populations under control is good. These can make for good early tasks for a new party to do.

                Symbols and colours importance to any Fera is good information to have and helps allow a storyteller to build a description of their caerns or other areas of control.

                As usual we also get information about the Ratkin and the Umbra, honestly I would have thought this section would be bigger given that the Ratkin flee to the Umbra for millenia.

                The Ratkin being somewhat mercenary in the early millenia of the War of Rage is cool and could lead to some interesting places. An entirely disposable mercenary Mischief of Ratkin could be a surprise to a party who thinks they have all their enemy’s tactics on lock. We also get a detailed look at the Garou wiping out the Bards, I think the most detailed version I have read.

                The Ratkin viewing the Gurahl as allies is interesting, as I doubt the Gurahl feel the same. Their list of enemies is nearly everyone.

                Mechanically Ratkin have a lot going on, they have a ton of different auspices, each with multiple inbuilt advantages and disadvantages. 5 new gifts, some restricted to certain auspices, 1 new rite, 4 fetishes and 3 talens.

                The new gifts are all good, the one granting a new form especially. The rite effectively grants a temporary totem to the group and all 3 options are potentially useful. All the fetishes make sense for Ratkin, the portable umbra tunnel should have any players dealing with Ratkin very paranoid. The talens are all cool and useful. 4 Totems, all make sense for the Ratkin.

                The other mechanical stuff about blood memory Warpspasms and Wyld Minds really help set a Ratkin game apart from other Fera.

                    Rokea

                    And now we get to the Rokea, who even for Fera often feel like White Wolf didn’t actually want them to be playable. Their section is 32 pages long.

                    We get a bunch of details on how they live and their use of islands in the ocean, and presumably major seas like the mediterranean give a situation where they can encounter other Fera. Rokea river sharks causing conflict with other Fera is also interesting, presumably they would go to Un-sea more often as well.

                    The information about how Rokea communicate is interesting.

                    As usual we get some details of how the Rokea interact with the Umbra.

                    Rokea fighting mostly Nagah and Mokole makes sense. It doesn’t seem like either side wins. The information about the flooding of Doggerland is the most interesting part of this section. When in doubt and the Garou don’t understand they assume Wyrm corruption and usually make everything worse.

                    The Kor come back in the Savage Age? That is new, who are they fighting? Their enemies list is expected.

                    Mechanically it is good that homid Rokea are not literally unique snowflake characters. As always it is good to get an overall mechanical overview for the Rokea. Again we get suggestions of Garou gift lists for each of the Rokea auspices. 9 new gifts, 2 rites, 2 fetishes, 5 talens and 3 breed background merit/gift lists.

                    Most of the gifts are interesting, nearly all of them are directly combat related, and flying air shark is a silly gift. The 5th level rite is a very cool one that is a campaign centerpiece event. Both fetishes are weapons. The coolest talens are the non-combat ones. I think the merit accessed gift lists are more interesting than the generic ones. Overall this mechanical section seemed heavily combat focused.

                    One of my concerns with this section is I still don’t really see how the Rokea can be used very much in most campaigns.

                    Appendices

                    These are nearly identical to the appendices in Ghost Lineages, so there isn’t much to say about them. It is only a handful of pages, but still feels odd.

                    Overall Thoughts

                    The art in all the Savage Age books is excellent, but the art in this book is on another level.

                    One thing I have noticed, the weird mostly empty pages are largely gone in recent books, which suggests better book planning.

                    I don’t really care about the pre-ice/stone age stuff so a significant portion of the histories of most of the Fera in this book was on stuff I am not interested in.

                    One of the best things about this book is that every mechanical section has suggestions for Garou lists to consider letting players take gifts from.

                    Overall I don’t think this book is as worthwhile as Ghost Lineages, it is over 25% shorter, and has a bunch of pre-Savage Age content. I think that moves it from Ghost Lineages excellent value to simply being good value. I do really like that the Savage Age team is still iterating on what they are doing with their books; I have already mentioned several times one of the best examples of this, every Fera in this book has ideas for other lists that gifts could potentially be drawn from. Also the different breed merit/gift lists is a cool idea that could maybe be ported over to other Fera.

                    101 Savage Age Kinfolk

                    Savage Age Kinfolk is a small and cheap book that has 109 Kinfolk listed. There are no actual stats, just a paragraph or sometimes two about them. There are a few pieces of art, probably more than you would expect for a book this cheaply priced. First we get a pile of Garou Kinfolk, 10 Anupu, 9 Nahau, 9 Bilai, 9 Kucha, 9 Saksom Ankami, and 9 Siberakh. Then we get to the “independents”, 6 Ajah-Abah, 10 Apis, 9 Grondr, 1 Kor, 2 Naghaluu, and 2 Urkama. The Bastet with 6 Ceilican, 9 Khara, and 9 Wlewa.

                    Some of these write-ups are more immediately useful than others, especially the ones that describe the kinfolk in some detail. I do like that not all of them are human kinfolk. And that it is at least suggested that some of them have abilities that aren’t completely natural.

                    I think this book would also be super useful for someone who wasn’t really sure what type of character they wanted to play. A quick read of a few sections and hopefully one of the listed kinfolk gives some ideas as most of these could be the starting point of a more fleshed out character.

                    This a super-useful book, it gives lots of great ideas for how you can seed the communities that the players encounter with interesting kinfolk.

                        Tribebook: Urkama

                        This is the first book I wasn’t able to get as part of a bundle. Average length for a Savage Age Tribebook, and for a Fera that technically exists in the official lore, but really hasn’t had much written about it.

                        The first short story is excellent, it conveys a lot about how the Urkama view things. The second one also helps define them, and must be relatively late in the War of Rage if he is dealing with the Garou.

                        Tribal Background

                        The Urkamas Gaian Mandate is super interesting and a good example of how the Fera are dealing with things that aren’t just in the “real” world.

                        The whole idea of Bat once being a servant of the uncorrupted Wyrm and escaping being corrupted, at least for a time, is a good plot idea that already existed within Werewolf, I am glad it has been kept.

                        Other Fera not trusting the Urkama is very on brand with how Fera strongly dislike anything they can’t immediately understand and tendencies to assume the worst.

                        Caches of weird and/or interesting fetishes is intriguing, hopefully that shows up in the mechanical section.

                        The Garou choosing to target them towards the end of the War of Rage makes sense, some Garou are going to want to constantly find a new threat to fight and prove themselves against. Also it would be cool to get some more details about the various Imperial Moots, since I assume it wasn’t a regularly scheduled thing.

                        And the Garou, led by the Bilai, mess with what they don’t understand and, as usual, never considered the second and third order implications of what they did. You would think at some point the Garou would realize that just because they didn’t understand, didn’t mean there wasn’t something to understand and that messing with it without understanding would bite them in the ass.

                        Bat starting to crack as early as the end of the War of Rage gives thousands of years for that to play out.

                        I like how the Urkama are tied into the American ones becoming the Camazotz.

                        Bat getting directly involved with trying to get a bunch of Garou Totems to back off is an excellent detail. The things the Urkama were keeping at bay allying with the Wyrm both makes sense and helps explain how the Wyrm got both more powerful and more broken after the War of Rage.

                        I always like seeing Litanies for various Fera.

                        Secrets Beyond Sight

                        I like that we are getting some details about what it is that the Urkama fight at the edges of reality.

                        Also their need to manipulate others to either attack stuff or avoid stuff could be a good way to include an Urkama in a campaign, have a high ranked Urkama occasionally tell the player character stuff that needs to be massaged into a different place.

                        Of course other Fera don’t like the Urkama manipulating them, especially into situations where they lose people.

                        The Nightmare Gods

                        Given that I think we were just talking about these guys in the last super short chapter, it organizationally feels odd to split them.

                        I like how we get several different Fera’s ideas on where the Nightmare Gods come from. The rite of the hollow sound doesn’t sound like a good thing, but also the kind of thing that some Garou would be very willing to use. Hopefully we see both versions of this rite.

                        Blighter of Souls

                        Now we get details on a specific problem spirit the Urkama have dealt with, which I think is excellent, early Savage Age products were often hesitant to pin anything down. Things need to be pinned down so there are some parameters for storytellers to work from.

                        We specifically have Urkama called shamans, hopefully there is more about that.

                        The details about fighting this infection would make for an excellent campaign. The Urkama fighting in places the other Fera are unaware of and those Fera thinking the Urkama mostly bailed on them is a good detail. My one confusion with this is was this mostly contained to one region? Or pockets across reality, linked in ways that make no sense to a physical mind? I would have liked it if at least 1 place was called out.

                        Who They Were

                        Some Urkama never making the cut for the deeper mysteries feels very cult like, but also gives them useful cutouts who legitimately don’t know about the stuff they have going on.

                        Semi-regular umbral gatherings are cool. I wonder if so many of their leadership being regularly in some of the weirdest, most distant parts of reality actually hurt them long term.

                        Being relatively isolationist among their own kind is a bit odd to me, most bats live in huge colonies, I would assume the Urkama are the same.

                        The Urkama performing their rituals on very small children is an interesting distinction from most other Fera.

                        Their relations with other Fera are what you would expect. Their distribution of kin feels odd, earlier it seemed like many Urkama were loners, now it seems like they hang in groups.

                        Urkama in modern nights is interesting, this could be tied into the ideas from the Wlewa book for if they survived.

                        The blood song and its effects on Urkama and the future Camazotz.

                        Breath of Life

                        Apterous form doesn’t have wings but has a penalty to athletics and dodge while not flying? The other core mechanical stuff seems fine.

                        31 Gifts listed, a mix of re-used and new. With the 28 gifts the Camazotz have in W20 changing breeds, most of which would still make sense for the Urkama, you have a decent number of options.

                        We start off strong with Anchor the Soul, which seems like a nearly mandatory gift for any Umbral focused Urkama. No escape is also cool. The level one gifts give many good options for several different character ideas. Luna’s Luck is probably my favorite of the level 2 gifts. Pathways through Darkness is a classic rpg idea, and Many Wings Form is cool, all the level 3 gifts are useful with different builds. All 4 level 4 gifts seem very on brand for the Urkama but I would have liked at least one unique level 4 gift. All but one of the level 5 gifts are new, and they are all very cool, Seek the Soul’s Lament seems very important to their Gaian mandate.

                        Now we have 5 rites, which might be the most in any Savage Age Tribebook. It makes sense that they would have some unique rites though. Mind hardening, den realms, forcibly adjusting spirits, emotion deadening; all of them are cool, though I think the mechanics of the second 5th level rite are a bit confusing. I think it needs 40 gnosis in total, requiring 1 character to generate 40 gnosis is impossible.

                        Two fetishes, no talens interestingly; both fetishes are useful. We also get 4 interesting totems. All could be good for a player group or npcs.

                        And some details on how Urkama determine renown. Always good to see that spelled out. And we get 5 character concepts, I like these better when they have ideas for how to build that character. It helps new players get going or storytellers build quick NPCs.

                        Appendixes

                        The first section is a bunch of information about the Umbra, umbral travel, and a few umbral realms, very important to the Urkama and good to get some additional details on that.

                        Next we get details on blood magic, the magic section of the Wlewa book was super cool, I have high hopes for this. 5 rites, all are interesting and rather dark. All 5 mostly combat related gifts. This stuff could be be very useful for a modern game, have a mortal wyrm-tainted shaman or even some Black Spiral Dancers or other corrupted Fera with access to this stuff. A new, unexpected problem for the players to deal with.

                        Last we have an appendix on actual bats, these sections are always useful to help colour player interactions with this type of Fera.

                        Overall Thoughts

                        I like the level of detail we are getting about specific things in this tribebook.

                        I don’t like the art in this book as much as that of nearly all the other Savage Age books, that isn’t to say it is bad, it just isn’t as good as the others.

                        Because of their Gaian mandate, the Urkama can end up being reasonably useful even in a campaign that doesn’t star them. Player characters can be diverted to dealing with a problem they found, or can be seeking some fetishes or talens the Urkama have hidden away somewhere.

                        I think this book gives enough information that you could run an Urkama campaign largely using just this book. I do worry that unless you are dealing with corrupted Urkama with access to blood magic if there would be enough variety of gifts, but that is a problem basically all the Fera that aren’t Werewolves face on some level.

                            Overall Savage Age thoughts

                            So we have reached the end of the Savage Age products I own. I know there are bunch of other books they have done that I don’t have yet, like a few other tribebooks, a fiction anthology and a few one shot adventure books. But I have all the main books that cover the Savage Age.

                            First, the art is almost universally great. They really help build the feel of the setting, even official books often don’t have this much high quality art.

                            Second, the Savage Age does both a really good job of showing the big picture of the time period, leading up to and throughout the War of Rage. The Fera created or expanded on for the Savage Age are really cool and generally well fleshed out. The ideas for several different Canid-shifters eventually becoming the Garou and the early Garou Nation having a Gurahl component are amazing.

                            It also does a good job at the player level mechanically, detailing exactly how to build all sorts of Fera characters, with more gifts, rites, fetishes, talens, and totems. Many of those things are useful even outside a Savage Age campaign. Especially if you are wanting to use a Fera that never got a lot of love from the official books.

                            Where I think the Savage Age struggles is giving storytellers a more middle level picture. What I mean by that is details on caerns/septs/folds/sounders/chiefdoms/sebios and various NPC characters. The type of stuff you would find in books like Rage across the Amazon. I 100% get that picking a specific time/place with the Savage Age would be hard, since it covers thousands of years and things change significantly over that time. Entire species of Fera go extinct or others think they do. And the Savage Age has gotten better over time about giving specifics. But it is a significant hurdle for storytellers who want to set an entire campaign in the Savage Age without having an area to hang that story on. And I don’t know if I would have prioritized a Rage Across the Savage Age (or specific location Rage Across _____) over any of the big books it would have replaced. But I think now is the time to add something like that.

                            But that doesn’t make the Savage Age bad by any stretch, just that getting a Rage Across ___ book would be the icing on an already good cake.

                            And the Savage Age does a really good job of building up, each book makes the whole project better with Tribebooks, Savage Age Volumes and Advanced Players Guides all adding details to the larger setting.

                            I think anyone with even a little bit of interest in the time period or who wants extra ideas for some less focused on parts of Werewolf would benefit from getting at least the Savage Age Intro Bundle.

                            Also it feels like I have kind of been ranting into the wind with writing all of this. If anyone is still paying attention, what did you think of the Savage Age? What did you find most useful from it?
