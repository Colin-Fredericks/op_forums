                Originally posted by Asmodai View Post

                That is pretty cool. I'm not surprised this idea popped up before, and it wasn't fleshed out a lot here, but it would be cool to play up. Any specific details you went into and how they functioned narratively?

                For a bit of context, the campaign was set in the 12th century in Aquitaine (south west of modern day France).
                The three PCs were sent in a quest to investigate the recent disappearance of some lone urban garou.

                They found a cell of what they assumed were inquisitors with some rather impressive resistance to gifts and even garou claws. After dispatching them, they found an artifact marked with garou glyphs.

                After following the trace of the artifact, they arived in an abandonned caern in the ardenian forests. Corrupted garou had attacked the caern in order to free the spirit the caern was built to contain.

                And yet the spirit apparently murdered it's would be liberators alongside it's jailors.



                ​​​​​Following that event, the campaign turned more and more dangerous. Each session was about a small mission, or errant, either choosed by the players or proposed by me as their caern's elders demanding something.
                but these apparent side quest were excuses for me to instore a growing sense of hostility toward the PCs. People were reacting in a manner similar to the Curse, but with agressivity rather than unnease. News of nobles rewarding the hunting down of wolves multiplied, and the caern received more and more stories of inquisitors identifying garou and kinfolks with strange ease.


                Finally, the spirit revealed itself when the group's theurge lend the pack following a vision of a teenager, in a primitive tent, with blooded hands and tears.



                They met it in the umbra, in a small realm reminiscent of a prehistorical village, whose walls were lined with wolves' corpses.
                The spirit, appearing as the bloodied adolescent of the theurge's vision, presented itself as Silvered Hatred, the incarnation of the human backlash against the impergium.

                As a character, I played him as a particularly driven human, rather than the way I typically portray spirits, capable of rational discussions, but ultimately, hellbent on exterminating all garou.

                He was also partial on garou depending on their tribes, remembering that the ancestors of the warden of men and children of gaia were against the impergium. Not that it would do them any good, since their only reward would be a quick end, while the spirit desired to hunt down the other till, hiding cowardly from their new hunters, the garou lived what humans lived during the impergium.

                And you don't want to know what he planned with the red talons.

                After escaping the realm, the campaign became pretty much a story of survival. The influence of Silvered Hatred spread pretty much naturally amongs humans thanks to it's chosen ones, the "avengers", humans who suffered at the hands of garou, and who became easy receptacles of the will of Silvered Hatred.

                In terms of mechanics, in all cities the avengers went, I turned all delirium reactions of fear to pulsion of violence toward garous.



                In the end, after losing their own caern in a tragic battle against an army assembled by the avenged, the PCs ran away, persuaded that there was no hope. Until they found Sister Hepatia, an ananasi, who, tbey would find out, was the one who tricked the corrupted garou into freeing Silvered Hatred.

                Sister Hepatia, obeying Ananasa, planned for the extermination of garou.

                Following that, the PCs, after a lot of umbral shenanigans, were able to speak with Ananasa, and after a pretty impressive line of arguments from one of the player, was convinced that even after the extermination of garou, Silvered Hatred, being driven by a millenia of hatred, will never stop, and would inevitably hunt down her children.

                Hence she reveals to them his weakness: if at least one of his receptacles were able to renounciate their desire for vengeance for the garou, he would be massively weakened.


                So they found one of them, the only one they hoped to convince, since he was someone they met early in the campaign, a farmboy whose sister was murdered by a garou, suspecting she was about to turn fomori.


                ​​​​​Short story short, they (barely) suceeded, and with that break of his concept from one of his own chosen one, Silvered Hatred was weakened enough to be vainquished. But not destroyed, for his existence was dependant on the still existing anger of humans towards garou, even if for most humans, that anger was unconscious.

                So the pack trapped Silvered Hatred, and like the founders of the lost caern, dedicated themselves to keeping him emprisonned.

                The campaign ended on a last dialogue between the spirit and the group's theurge. The first taunting the garou that he'll inevitably break free and finish the job, the latter hoping that one day, humanity may forgive the garou, taking away the spirit's nature.


                Fun fact, during the campaign, one of the player wondered that, if the hatred of humans during the impergium manifested as a spirit, then the fear of garou caused by said period, and manifesting during the delirium, should also create a spirit.

                And after a discussion on the matter, we agreed that there's indeed such a spirit, but that for garou, it's existence will only ever be theorical, since being born of the fear of garou and being fed by yhe innate delirium of all of mankind, such powerful spirit use it's powers will perpetually avoid meeting them, even in vision or memories, to the point that garou couldn't even see it's trace or even remember other spirits mentioning it. (Yeah, we basically gave it the equivalent of obfuscate 9)

                                                Personally, I consider apocalyptic records to be a disappointment compared to beckett's jyhad diary. For multiple reason.

                                                One I won't really take account of, since it would be pretty unfair to AR's writters, is that BJD could count on many well known and loved character, while WtA has far less of that.

                                                ​​​​​​
                                                ​​​​​​The bolk covers a lot of ideas, many interesting ones, but there's a few issues

                                                - First, and for me, the biggest, is the idea of divinding the book by aupices. This to me was a bad idea, because it pushed many disparates ideas into a single chapter, and due to the format, only a few of them are truly develloped into the "here's what you can do with it in game" part at the end of a chapter.

                                                BJD had a lot of chapters each dedicated to one event/location/concept with the exception of the omega chapter. This allows the "for the ST" part to be more develloped.

                                                Now look at the last part of AR, the philidox chapter. My personnal favorite. It's chock full of ideas, including the plots of many tribes' fall in the Apocalypse book, or the weird rumors and new ideologies spreading across the younger garous.

                                                And yet, due to the format of five chapters, all these will barely be mentioned in the "for ST's eyes" part.

                                                ​​​​
                                                ​​​​​​A Division of chapters similar to BJD would have been better. For exemple one chapter about the garouboros and abominations and another on the amazonian war's ending, and another on Bat's redemption.

                                                Second, many things in the books seems to not really fit in a BJD like book, by that I mean that they don't add anything to what we know of the setting or set up anything for the future.
                                                For exemple the chapter on the captured BSD who ends up not being redemptable. Something that was the status quo since first edition. Or the part where a group of garou has some sort of first contact with a bastet. Which adds up nothing on garou-fera relations that we don't know since feras were introduced.

                                                these and some others made me feels like the devs knew W20 was coming to an end, they still had some ideas and wanted to implement them in a boof even if that doesn't really match it.

                                                And that to me is doubly an issue, since the space used for them could have been used to devellop the good new ideas of the book, like the idea of a rise of abominations or explore other mysteries of the setting, like the machine awakening or t he stargazers cosmology.

                                                Which is sort of a third problem to me. BJD did a stellar job of taking account of most of the main plots and mysteries of VtM. AR seems to miss most of them. From the many exploratory camps and factions (wagnerians, bone gnawers deserters, etc).


                                                Fourth one, and that's a personnal peeve, the tbeurge chapter is criminally empty. There's so much mysteries in the umbra that were not taken account of. From the deep umbra, the lost stargazers leaving earth, the search of the wagnerian for another gaia and hell, effing antelios!

                                                The chapter seems empty compared to, say, the philodox one.


                                                And finally, although a little one compared to the other, as a kickstarter participant, I can't help but feel we have had false promises of certain points, notably that we would have the point of views of fera and even wyrm servants.

                                                Well, we don't have wyrm servants POV, and the ony fera appearing is a bastet that simoky says that the place the narrator's pack is entering in is her territory.


                                                So in overall, many good ideas, but a very messy book.


                                                Andbon another note, Howls of the apocalypse is truly terrible, but I don't think that's the time and place for speaking of that one.
