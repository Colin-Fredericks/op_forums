    Incoming judgement

    06-26-2022, 03:39 PM
    Here's the context.

    I'm dm'ing a changing breed campaign, the characters are fera at the exception of one werewolf, which is the subject of this post. All lives around the french city of Lille.

    So Sargon is a fostern (rank 2) from the glasswalker tribe. He's a member of the local cairn, even though he rarely interact with it's members outside of meetings and other important occasions.

    He'd rather spend his time cultivating his network of supernatural contacts, which includes the other PCs, as he think that with the largest perspective, he'll be more able to do the right thing for the world.

    During last session, Sargon goes to a suddent meeting of the sept at the cairn. Following a vision and the pressure from one of it's elders, the sept was going to lauch an all out attack agaisnt the vampires (un)living in Lille.

    Problem, Sargon has nothing agaisnt the leeches, worse, some of his contacts(and, while he would never admit it,friends) are vampires. After trying to dissuade the elders from validating the attack, he's considered unreliable on the issue due to his contacts(a fact known to the sept) and ordered to stay out of the operation.

    Sargon decided to secretly messages his contacts to warn them of the impending attack.

    The following day, Sargon is convocated at the cairn, he faced the hateful gazes of the warriors, wounded and with two losses as the warned contacts gave the message to all vampires of the city, which were able to prepare themselves and ambush the garous.

    So at the end of the session, Sargon is detained at the cairn and accused of putting his brothers in arm in danger to protect wyrmlings, to have disobeyed a direct order of the elders. And many claims that his action was direct betrayal.

    So here I am, organising the next session, which will be his judgement.

    Considering his actions, what punishments would he risk?

    Things to consider: he will be judged by three elder: the sept chief, a fianna that allowed many transgressions from Sargon, but is out of patience with him, the elder that called for the attack, a theurge of the children of gaia, obsessed with the elimination of vampires, and a glasswalker, who many time defended Sargon agaisnt accusations of betrayal for his contacts with vampires that were formulated earlier in the campaign.

    With them will be the guardian spirit of the cairn, a being that manifest the concept of binding promises.

      OK, I understand this probably isn't the best answer for keeping the game fun...

      But death. The Sept isn't going to be debating if Sargon is going to die, they're going to be debating how horribly he's going to die. The Garou are a military society and he gave vital intelligence to the enemy that cost Garou lives that was not part of an approved counter-intelligence operation. In war, you do that, and you get executed. In the eyes of the Garou Nation he's a traitor. He did something there's pretty much no coming back from.

      Oh, and that's before getting to the specifics of the Sept you've outlined, which all make this worse:

      - The Elders judging him are already in a 2/3s majority against him, and the one Elder that might take his side is going to be under immense pressure not to because in the eyes of the Sept all that time trying to defend Sargon's actions were misguided; if said Elder doesn't side with the other two to come down hard on Sargon, he's risking his own Rank and position in the Sept.
      - A Fostern disobeying an Elder is technically grounds for execution (if rare in modern times because Garou can't afford such punishments taking away their population). If he'd done this and no Garou died because of it, him not being executed would be seen as an exceptionally merciful ruling.
      - There's no way to spin this. Everyone knows what happened, and everyone knows why it happened.
      - A Caern Totem spirit of Binding Promises is at risk of abandoning the Sept if they do not deal harshly with a Garou that violates the Litany like that.

      And since he's already captured and awaiting trial... he's very screwed.

      Basically, you've left yourself two options: he dies, or the other PCs rescue him, and then he'll be declared a Ronin, his Rank will be stripped from him denying him access to a ton of things (spirits will stop teaching him Gifts and he'll only be able to learn them from his non-Garou friends), and he'll be hunted by the local Garou for the rest of his life unless he flees somewhere else, and even then he'll constantly run the risk of being killed by Garou who can then claim a reward from the Sept for taking care of their traitor.

      I'm not sure how you and your group handle things, but unless you're comfortable deviating significantly from the books here, or rewinding events to make things less harsh on him, events have gone in a direction with very few options.

          Well, it depends a bit on your style of play. 1st Edition WtA had a different outlook on garou/vampire relations than revised Edition, for example. It seems, like the sept knew about his dealings with the leeches and at least tolerated it - something that would not fly well in revised edition, but seems to be acceptable in 1st Edition WtA.

          Despite contradicting circumstantial evidence in regards to specific vampires, there is no doubt in the minds of the garou society at large that vampires are wyrmish by nature, even if they don't know of the Wyrm or further Wyrm agenda actively. So, the attack ordered is beyond reproach and staying out of it is a direct order. The person issuing the order was, without doubt within the sept, of higher station.

          So, in addition to significant social stigmata and renown loss, there are two Litany tenets violated. "Fighting the Wyrm" is continuously not adhered to, but the sept has not yet brought judgement down on this, possibly because the character delivered results in the fight against the Wyrm in other areas. Failing to "submit to those of higher station" however is the more pressing issue here, and the circumstances are, that this cost the life of garou.

          So, besides any relatives of the fallen asking for retribution (there might be a Rite where a Kinfolk might be executing a garou in such a case) there are plenty of Punishment Rites that are applicable, and some that aren't a Rite. Banishment comes to mind, since people might not want to have anything to do with such a traitor (I mean, even when he opens his mouth, that might be a reason for a Rage roll by the packmates of the fallen garou). Also, since his words caused the death of garou, the Rite that changes the subjects voice might be possible, even though it is usually for liars.
          Being hunted to the death also could fall within the scope of what might be a suitable punishment. Really, the bandwidth is wide...

              Garou informing the enemy about an incoming attack by his sept, and that information resulting in the enemy killing an wounding Garou of the sept, constitutes treason in my opinion. The level 5 Punishment Rite Gaia's Vengeful Teeth is the most likely outcome after a very quick trial.

              Now if the information provided to the vampires only meant that those vampires escaped, no other vampires were informed, and the sept's raid was successful as planned, but the information leak was discovered anyway, then a lesser Punishment Rite would be more likely. Wanting to preserve one's contacts or informants is at least understandable. And while the Garou should not have done that without the sept's approval, at least in this scenario it did not cause casualties. In that case, I might choose the Stone of Scorn instead. At least potentially it might have caused actual harm (which it actually did), so a more severe Punishment Rite than the Voice of the Jackal is needed.

              It is perfectly OK for some Garou to maintain informants and contacts among the enemy. Glass Walkers and Shadow Lords are two tribes were this is more common. But this either needs to be done in secret or with the approval of the sept's elders. And it should not result in blowback. But a Garou disobeying direct orders to warn his vampire allies, who then warn the rest of their kind, and this results in higher casualties? That is not OK.

              Now since this is a PC, and you may not want to actually kill the character because of the negative impact on the game, you may want alternatives. One would be to subject the Glass Walker to permanent ostracism. Perform the level four Rite of the Lone Wolf instead. It makes the Garou a Ronin and outcasts him from the Garou Nation, with the idea that any Garou could kill him on sight and not be subjected to any penalty. Since the other PCs are not Garou, this would allow you to continue the chronicle with all PCs. You just have to make Garou NPCs very hostile to the one PC. The Player can then decide how he wants to proceed. Embrace being a Ronin? Start working to try to redeem himself? Something else?

                                                          The specifics of the attack don't matter. The Sept went from having the element of surprise, to being the ones that got ambushed instead.

                                                          Disobeying a direct order regarding battle is absolutely something that warrants death if the Elder deems is so. The books have been clear over multiple editions that while death is not a mandatory punishment for disobeying an order like this, it is a valid one in the eyes of the Garou. There are many circumstances where killing a Fostern for disobeying a direct order isn't going to be a death sentence, but during something that clearly falls under the "times of war" element of the Litany that directly relates to battle is not where those circumstances live.

                                                          As for external forces... which of them actually matters? Not even Jonas has the authority to just Moonbridge into a Sept and take over local Sept business because he heard some rumors. The Judges of Doom and House Gleaming Eye aren't recognized authorities with independent powers to act as they wish. A bunch of Judges of Doom showing up at the Sept to intervene can be told to go screw off and mind their own business and the Judges would have to be Athro or Elders to even consider challenging that, and they would need leverage do convince the Sept officers that any challenge they made was honorable. There's no law in the Garou Nation that a Sept has to allow the Judges of Doom in to enforce the law because they want to. They're not Interpol. They only have jurisdiction when Septs agree to let them have it.

                                                          Outsiders would need to call for a Concolation, which requires five Elders of five different Tribes to all agree to it, gathering Garou from multiple Septs in the area, in order to force their way into Sept business. The threat of that is not enough to make the Sept care about what outsiders think when they have their own to keep satisfied. The rest of the Sept wants Sargon to pay for what he did from the description given. That's a much bigger concern than the idea that someone with enough power is going to care enough to use it just for this.
