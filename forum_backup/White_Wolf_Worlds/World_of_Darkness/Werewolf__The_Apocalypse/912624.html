    Heretical Thoughts on Griffin (and the Red Talons)

    06-12-2016, 12:04 AM
    This is yet another thread where I look at a tribe and its totem, and discuss ideas on how I'd develop it a little differently based on certain facts or legends the original writers seemed to ignore. Or at least interpret certain canonical ideas and concepts differently than is usually portrayed. While I start with Griffin as a totem based on its legends, this is actually more about the Tribe and a way to allow them to be better rounded and more playable.

    The Red Talons are yet another tribe many people have trouble with. They seem very limited in a dead-end way, and people can't figure out what to do with them. So I think ANY new ideas would be very appreciated. By moving beyond a fairly one dimensional portrayal, we give more opportunities for PCs and more interesting depictions of NPCs.

    Griffon is the tribal totem of the Red Talons, and in Garou lore he is the patron of predators and protector of those animals hunted to extinction by man. As a pack totem, he won't take any children who are homids. In essence, he is the spirit of nature red in tooth and claw. But in actual myth and legend, Griffin is many other things, few of which are incorporated into the game. But real myth and game lore can be reconciled.

    Rather than a summary of various things, in this thread I'll be talking about different ideas in their own posts. If I try to include everything, there is just way too much for one post or even two or three. Rather than post everything at once like I usually do, I'm going to post them individually. I'll do a few now and allow people to comment before I post some of my other ideas.

      Totem of Leadership

      Because the lion was traditionally considered the king of the beasts and the eagle the king of birds, the griffin was thought to be an especially powerful and majestic creature. Therefore, the griffin was considered to be the king of all creatures – both those on land and in the air.

      The combination of falcon (totem of Silver Fang) and lion (totem of White Howlers) is interesting. Both tribes are known for the white fur which is strongly associated with polar conditions. In Garou history, this marks them as tribes existent during the Ice Age, and thus are likely the two eldest tribes.

      By adopting this totem, did the Red Talons purposefully or inadvertently challenge for leadership in the Garou Nation? Was it a not so subtle rebuke to the Silver Fangs that alternate leadership was possible? Did lupus from one or more of these tribes depart in protest on the ending of the Impergium? Or is it just a coincidence? Might Red Talons today consider themselves a potential leader for the Garou Nation because of the nature of their totem – or at least leader of the lupus Garou? Perhaps the formation of the Red Talon tribe is much older than expected, perhaps even done in opposition to the leadership role displayed early on by the Silver Fangs.

      This may explain why the Lion totem because part of Griffin's brood after his tribe fell to the Wyrm. It is possible that Talons envision doing same for Falcon if the Silver Fangs ever fall as well.

      Red Talons, as a lupus only tribe, have always been connected to the lupus of other tribes. By interpreting Griffin as a “royal” totem though, this could make Red Talon interactions with other totems even more interesting. Perhaps Red Talon “diplomats” are in constant contact with the lupus of other tribes, particularly Silver Fangs, reminding them they too have a leadership pedigree. Red Talons won't be machiavellian manipulators or particularly intent on seizing leadership for its own sake. But they could put themselves up as a candidate should the Silver Fangs fall or lose enough prestige.

      Realistically, the Red Talons have no such chance among the homids. But it might just threaten a split with the lupus of the other tribes simply because of the nature of the totem (and not the merits of the Red Talons themselves).

          Guardian of the Divine

          In antiquity, Griffin was a symbol of divine power and a guardian of the divine. It is possible to reimagine its role as a sacred protector or divine punisher, as being specifically in regards to humanity – Gaia created Griffin specifically to punish man when he trespassed the natural order. At least, that is how the Red Talons would put it. Other tribes would disagree. But clearly, it would work well that Griffin’s anger towards humanity is based on something essential – perhaps some mythological crime of humans against Gaia.

          I originally thought it would be interesting to tie it to the Prometheus myth – for bringing fire to man, Prometheus was punished by being chained to a mountain and a monstrous eagle ate his liver every day. Since the mastery of fire was a major moment in mankind’s history and allowed humanity to overcome the natural limits, it has the earmarks of something Griffin would really hate. And a monstrous eagle could easily turn out to be a griffin. However, I think man's mastery of fire is just too old for the purposes of the game. I'd like it to be something that could take place in a decent timeframe.

          Then I thought that agriculture might be a good “crime”. Griffin is a predator and heavily associated with predatory species. Primitive man was originally a hunter, and ostensibly at that time could have had some kind of relationship with Griffin. However, by choosing agriculture, man stopped being limited by the constraints placed on him by nature. He began “mastering” nature at that point. I could see that as being something that caused Griffin to decide man had trespassed against a law of Gaia. Since agriculture is tied to the Impergium, it also makes a lot of sense.

          Being the divine punisher of men for a specific crime (whether mastery of fire, or beginning agriculture) also helps water down the general hatred of mankind for the Red Talons. Their original “role” was not to exterminate man. They wanted to enforce divine retribution for Man's transgression. This keeps the anger and hostility towards Mankind in general, but channels it to particular individuals or groups instead of the species as a whole. That punishment could be poetic reprisals against individuals; or enforce a few collective punishments. Not wholesale slaughter of every single human (which should be limited to the Lodge of the Predator Kings). One does not waste, squander, or misdirect divine judgment or vengeance. That would be impious. Instead, it is brought on the person who violated that taboo. For bringing fire to mankind, Zeus did not punish men, he punished Prometheus specifically. (OK, Zeus also tricked Pandora to unleash evil against mankind.) However, such a myth would give the Red Talons a basis for opposing the help other tribes (particularly the Children of Gaia, Glass Walkers, and Bone Gnawers) gives men to overcome those evils. They are going against Gaia's ordained punishment of Mankind.

          At the same time, the fear of not wanting to misdirect divine judgment of specific individuals (against collective mankind) could serve as a good reason to explain why Red Talon “moderates” are holding back the Lodge of the Predator Kings from open warfare with humanity. There might be all sorts of taboos and spiritual explanations against open warfare even though the bulk of Red Talons detest humanity. Instead of a general hate, these Red Talons could point that Griffin's ire is specifically directed towards certain behaviour, and that Red Talons can have some relationship with humans who don't violate certain taboos, or who may offer proper chiminage in recompense to minor offenses.

          Of course, nowadays the existence of pure hunter-gatherers is very limited so there is practically no one who would not be considered to be a target. But I still like the taboo-violator idea as it provides some sort of basis for Red Talons to not seek outright extermination of mankind, or at least give some window for certain Red Talons to utilize the homid form and interact with man.

          It also provides a basis for the Warders of the Land as something other than just the boring example of "ordinary" Red Talons. Perhaps there is something more specific about the type of land they ward. Something that keeps them away from the bulk of humanity so that it limits the interaction between the tribe and humanity, despite the Red Talons desire to want to go to war.

              Griffins love Gold

              Griffins are known for guarding treasure and priceless possessions. Griffins love gold.

              The supposed love of gold by griffins, and other attributes of protecting treasure, is similar to dragons. In both cases, it represents protecting the natural wealth of the world in its purest, un-mined and unrefined forms. This ties back to the idea that the wilderness and nature are not to be spoiled, but left alone.

              The love of gold by Mankind is certainly a strong theme, and Griffin may seek to protect it precisely in order to deny it to Mankind, perhaps because it knows the love of gold allows evil to enter the hearts of men. Their love of gold also represents a possible source to use for chiminage and rites – returning gold to griffin (in essence destroying the wealth or surplus bounty of Mankind; ritually helping return man to his original, primitive state) pleases him.

              This could have other symbolic consequences. If Griffin wants to destroy the wealth of mankind, historically the best way for wolves to do that is attack livestock. Perhaps a way for very young Red Talon cubs (and perhaps older Garou too) to earn favor/pay chiminage to Griffin's brood for their Gifts is to seize a nearby farmer's chickens, sheep, etc.

                              Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
                              As much as I like your ideas, I do think they are taking the tribe a bit too far from their concept. Red Talons are meant to be the opposite of Glass Walkers, the wolves to their humans.
                              That is interesting because in my eyes, nothing of what I've posted has really taken the Red Talons away from their "core" concept. Even though these are "heretical" thoughts, meaning they are not what is depicted as canon, I do my best to keep the general attitude or concept of the tribe the same - at least as they were originally written in the corebook even if not the tribebooks.

                              The most obvious way to begin this is to look at their actual totem and see what real myths and legends are associated with them, and then figure out how to include them.

                              The Red Talons is a problematic tribe because most people don't know how to include them in a game. Part of my reason for this thread, is to try to find ways to open up the Talons and give them some additional depth and variety. At the same time, I want them to be recognizably Red Talons.

                              The Red Talons kill humans aspect is extremely one dimensional. However, it makes no sense that they are trying to kill all humans right now. Even though the World of Darkness is a variant of our world with lots of more terrible things, ongoing massacres of humans just can't happen without it making the world so different it becomes unrecognizable. So while I want to keep the antagonism and hate towards humans, the actual killing needs to become something that is manageable.

                              Otherwise the result becomes that the Talons are all talk and no walk. For all their speech about wiping out mankind and going to war, what are they actually doing? Are any settlements being wiped out in the depths of the Canadian or Siberian wilderness? If they were, wouldn't this become huge news? How many people have ever played games where this actually happened - even as background noise much less as part of the main story for the PCs? Instead of being a feared bunch of Garou, they become pathetic losers who don't actually follow through.

                              Neither scenario is good for the game.

                              The game has not been consistent in its depiction of the tribe. They were originally presented as all genocidal except for a few members. Then when camps were introduced, it seemed to put the real exterminationists in the Lodge of the Predator Kings, put the bulk of the tribe in the Warders as "moderates" who are hostile, but not actively killing, and then a small faction of mostly younger Garou who are curious about humans in the Whelps Compromise. Then this distinction is completely forgotten and the entire tribe portrayed as all killing humans.

                              I think the tribe, as depicted as canon, doesn't really work. I think many people like the general concept though, but struggle to figure out how to include them (even as NPCs in their chronicles, much less as PCs!).

                              I'm trying to come up with things that explain both the Red Talons hatred of humanity, and the fact they really don't go on genocidal rampages as suggested in the book. Somehow these things need to be reconciled - the extreme position written in the books, and the almost complete absence of attacks in everyone's chronicles.

                              I'll be posting new ideas later on, and hopefully they'll be something you can use.

Maybe a modern day Garou historian going through oral legends and history of important Concolations notice that Red Talon leaders were given a lot more prominence and role in them than they are now. This could have importance for more than just a flavor text of a bitter people remembering "when we were kings". The Red Talons might remember stories of lore of certain monsters, great banes, and other enemies that were active in 6500 BC and how to defeat them, that the other tribes forgot. When the PCs learn of the great evil the ST intends for them to stop, they're only hope of learning the macguffin that can defeat it is to find out what Red Talon remembers this, especially since the sept the story is most associated with was destroyed more than two thousand years ago when the city of Lyon was founded by the Gauls.

                                              Another is the role of Ancestors for Red Talon characters. Putting an emphasis on the Ancestor spirits that Red Talons are in communion with is another way to distinguish the tribe. Perhaps some of the stories involving human sacrifice and the like is a requisite of chiminage for such Ancestor spirits?

                                              Last would be that this could be the basis for a story in a chronicle. Maybe there is a member of another tribe - a Shadow Lord or Children of Gaia - going to sept to sept to educate them on Garou history with an edited form of a legend which omits the Red Talon contributions in preparation for a future concolation, or some other political plot. Or a Red Talon galliard who travels sept to sept with the "correct" version that homids have forgotten. Maybe one or the other is secretly killed to prevent the "wrong" version coming out. It could be the basis for a nice murder mystery or big political plot.

                                                  Here is another one.

                                                  Griffin's Brood of Wisdom

                                                  Similar creatures exist like the Anzu, Sphinx (particularly hieracosphinxes), Simurgh, Ziz, and Lamassu/Shedu. Indeed, Sphinx and Simurgh are listed as part of Griffin's brood in Axis Mundi. So we can also look to these legendary creatures for inspiration. These creatures are mainly associated with wisdom.

                                                  But what kind of wisdom? This can't be the kind of rational or philosophical thinking preferred by homids or the Stargazers. This has to be wisdom gained through instinct or what the Taoists call “no-mind” or others cal crazy wisdom, not contemplation or rational thinking. This would be a wisdom totem of Primal Urge and one's animal instincts. The inborn and intuitive responses. Wisdom not from the Weaver's thinking mind, but of the Wyld's unconscious mind which means dreams, insights, creative inspiration, and gut instincts. Lateral thinking (solving problems by an indirect and creative approach, typically through viewing the problem in a new and unusual light) instead of step-by-step logic. Huge leaps of logic that nonetheless leads to the correct conclusion. These might be Red Talon theurges uttering koans and acting like Buddhist gurus, or Red Talon ragabashes acting like contrary warriors and speaking in riddles.

                                                  There would be a separate Red Talon “wisdom tradition” within the Garou Nation. It would probably appeal a lot to Lupus Garou, but I could also see homids and other tribes seeking out renown Red Talons wisdom teachers, especially from Stargazers and Uktena. I could even see Gallairds coming to such teachers hoping to rediscover lost creativity or overcome an artistic block.

                                                          Protector Against Evil and Slander

                                                          The Achaemenid Persians considered the griffin "a protector from evil, witchcraft and secret slander".

                                                          Griffin as a general "protector from evil, witchcraft and secret slander" is an interesting angle, especially since humans, not animals, would want to evoke that. That aspect, although unusual, need not be a dealbreaker for Griffin's traditional hatred or dislike for humans. There is always “deep magic” going on.

                                                          If that is an aspect of Griffin, then the appropriate chiminage and so on makes it happen. Perhaps this role happened very early on before Griffin determined it needed to punish man. If we consider agriculture as the taboo crime, then it theoretically becomes possible Griffin might have allowed early hunting pre-agriculture man to offer chiminage in exchange for limited protection.

                                                          If Griffin is a protective spirit, then we have some intriguing ideas for the Warders of the Land camp as Warders are responsible for protecting what area they “ward”. This gives this camp a bit more to do than simply being bland ordinary default Red Talons. The camp might possess certain secrets and defenses that counter “evil” or “witchcraft” particularly that which affects nature (including the agriculture of men). Droughts, disease, fierce storms and floodings, earthquakes, and other natural disasters are in the purview of this camp. Such natural disasters were associated with witches and malevolent magic.

                                                          Protecting against “secret slander” is also an interesting choice for the Red Talons, but also appropriate for a tribe not known for their guile and following a particularly regal and majestic totem. I think it also gives them an additional role to play within the Garou Nation – a protector of one's reputation, and therefore Honor. If the Red Talons, or at least the dominant camp among them, is somehow bound against “secret slander” it makes them a particularly trustworthy group. Whatever their faults, they do not lie or cheat behind your back. There is some kind of role here for a Red Talon philodox. I imagine this camp makes them not particularly liked by many Shadow Lords whose preferred method of attack against their political foes is often “secret slander”.

                                                          There is a tie here to the earlier post as a guardian of the divine, and this aspect is probably a sub-aspect of that. It is the idea of a "natural order" that Griffin upholds, and when it is violated in other forms, Griffin still goes after such a trespasser or violator.
