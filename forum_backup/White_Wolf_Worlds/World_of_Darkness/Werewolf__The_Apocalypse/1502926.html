    Interested to hear any real-play experience with Garou-Garou romances.

    02-13-2023, 09:57 AM
    The Litany is obviously interpreted in a few different ways, so some Septs are more or less tolerant of non-fertile Garou couplings, such as those including Metis, or those where the two parties can't conceive. I'm playing a Metis with a huge heart who can't really romance a human (his Rage is too high at 5, even for strong-willed humans it'll eventually upset and frighten them, plus when he sleeps he turns into Crinos form), or a Kinfolk (very few Garou would allow it, though not impossible if the Kinfolk is in charge of themselves and doesn't care about having kids, or wants to adopt), or wolves (there aren't any in the country, even if he wanted to).

    Garou are basically the only option available to him, but of course even if a pairing is non-fertile it does arguably break the Litany. It doesn't say thou shalt not make metis babies, it says Garou Shall Not Lay With Garou. So I was interested to hear any real-play experience with the subject; for instance I don't think my boy would want it to be a secret if it happened, he'd want to proclaim his love loudly and with passion. He'd understand if an Elder said "no, that breaks the Litany", or "yes, that's okay", but "you can do it... but be discreet and never mention it at Moots" would rub him the wrong way. It's either right or it's wrong. If it's right, what does it matter if he kisses his partner in front of guests? If it's right, why couldn't they be Honourably Mated or get married?

      There is a Rite described in Wendigo TB 1st Edition, that suggests a Garou can stay in a form for weeks, so sleep, unlike being knocked unconscious, doesn't require to return into breed form. So, no need for the Metis there, unless the character has Flaws forcing such a transformation.

      In our LARP game we had have characters breaking this Tenet. But there were some Garou engaging in relationships and being hunted for that. Generally the idea is, that the tenet is condensing several legends of the Garou that try to teach us, that romantic relationships among Garou are leading to disastrous consequences, sooner or later.

      The first couple was sentenced separately, since the players weren't available for one single play. The male was able to fast talk the Get Galliard Septleader by proposing stuff and then declare them as fact - like what the Tenet is all about, Metis babies or relationships. So, the the Stone of Scorn was used as punishment, as well as a banishment of sorts. The female was banished and lost her Fostern rank. She later let slip that she did later break the Litany in that tenet again and therefore was target of a Hunt. Her own pack chose to be the hunters and eventually caught up with her and killed her.

      In another case both character left the sept, but later were found out to be at the same place, despite having left the sept for different quests. A spirit was sent to investigate and later a pack was send after them. Actually they should only apprehend them, but discovered that the place they were hiding was also housing a bane and both, as well as the metis baby were deemed to be corrupted and slain.

      There is always the question, what kind of behaviour is accepted in any society. What is wrong with two elderly while males kissing each other on the mouth in friendship? It doesn't happen so commonly that I wouldn't find it strange - but I also find the hugs and kisses the French exchange somewhat uncommon, and I used to live 10 km from the French border. So, when Patrick Stewart and Ian McKellan kiss each other on the mouth on US Late Night TV to make a point I can't help but think 'that is strange' - but that is because it's nothing I'm exposed to usually. There is nothing else, that is inherently strange about it.
      So, a Moot of the Black Furies is possibly filled with women, that are close friends with each other, and might have a greater tendency to physical contact than let's say a moot of Shadowlords. Children of Gaia, likewise, might find shows of affection totally okay, that might be inappropriate for a Silver Fang court. So, the same goes when guests are at a moot.
      Do you demonstrate what is okay in your community openly, even to make a point in a provocative matter, or do make concessions, so you don't offend the guest or force the appropriateness of such actions on the agenda.

      The thing is, there is no definition of "hounourably mated". What about two males and two females, who happen to be two garou and two kin-people, and have a domestic partnership as a group. Some septs might find such acceptable, others a reason to kill the offenders...

          I don't have actual examples of Garou to Garou matings in PC roleplay. Tabletop rarely lends itself to that, and I haven't seen that in the relatively few Werewolf LARPs or Online games I've played. In general, players don't want the Renown hit. If they want to play House, then they find Garou-Kinfolk romance options.

          However, in terms of how I present Garou-Garou romances as NPCs in the chronicles I ST, it is simple. They are considered to be the same as incest in the real world. It does not matter if children is not the result. It is intrinsically wrong. There is a very strong religious taboo as well as practical taboo (because of Metis deformities). It is just that Garou mating with Garou occurs more frequently than actual incest because of the unique dynamics of Garou society.

          The tribes in general take a very strong stance against it with the Fianna being the strongest opponents. However, the usual suspects of the Children of Gaia and Bone Gnawers are at the other end. It's not so much that they excuse such mating, but that they are more accepting of Metis and more lenient in the punishment of violators. These two tribes as a whole are still against such matings, but there are a minority of degraded fools in these tribes that don't see a problem with it. How much of this is pathological altruism and how much is Wyrm taint/corruption depends on the exact NPCs (and reactions of the PCs to the scene).

              "A minority of degraded fools" - while this makes sense as an in-universe POV, from an out of universe POV it's pretty clear that parts of the Litany are meant to be wrong.

              Gaia didn't come down from the clouds and tell Garou not to have sex with eachother. The Litany was a series of traditions and rules cobbled together by consensus from the pre-existing traditions of the various tribes, at the Concord. This is why some Breeds have rules against making Metis, why some can't or don't at all, and why some have no restriction against it. It is a religious and cultural institution of hatred and intolerance, not a matter of obeying Gaia's will.

              IMO there is some wisdom in discouraging Garou-Garou pairings, but it's about Kinfolk, not Garou. Garou are Gaia's warriors. They struggle with wisdom, they can be rash, they can be headstrong. Their packs are a bond SO tight that nothing else compares. And if you could sleep with, mate with, marry your homies in your Pack... Kinfolk would end up neglected. Without a Kinfolk Wolf population the Garou would cease to exist. Without Kinfolk Humans they couldn't survive. Without Homid and Lupus offspring, Garou couldn't continue the line. There are sensible ancient biblical-style reasons for outlawing Garou-Garou relationships, but none of them make any sense in modern times with the exception of Metis being resource-intensive and risky to raise.

              To be 100% clear, the Litany is very clearly meant to be a backwards institution with a mix of reasonable and bullshit rules. Garou loving Garou doesn't lead to Wyrm taint - societal rejection, repression and fear do. Metis aren't cursed, they have two Garou parents and are flooded with an 'overload' of Werewolfness.

              As for why Children of Gaia have so many Metis, it isn't because they are 'Charach'. They take in the dirty indiscretions and bastards of other Tribes. Same with the Glass Walkers. GW and CoG are organized and modern in their thinking, and take in the Metis cubs produced by all Tribes and by Ronin, all those who would otherwise be killed or abandoned. This is a good thing, and it's the reason GW and CoG are the largest Gaian tribes, and the fastest-growing. W20 makes it clear that them making these Metis in disproportionate numbers is an in-universe myth.

                  Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post
                  "A minority of degraded fools" - while this makes sense as an in-universe POV, from an out of universe POV it's pretty clear that parts of the Litany are meant to be wrong.
                  I have to disagree here. I don't think any of the Litany - meaning the 13 main precepts - are meant to be wrong. Overall, I think it's an effective code that provides enough contingencies and flexibility to adapt to any situation. It is certainly an effective code for a pre-modern people. I think where there are issues is that the Garou Nation collectively is still a pre-modern people, but living in a modern age. They've failed to adapt adequately to a modern human society, and the forces of the Wyrm has taken advantage of it. If you took some hunter gatherer from the Amazon jungle or New Guinea mountains, and threw them into a developed country, they'd have problems adapting as well. And of course, almost all players are coming from a modern society and have modern sensibilities, and are going to dislike some aspects of the culture of the Garou Nation. But I don't think that is the fault of the main precepts of the Litany per se, but the failure of the Garou to adequately adapt the Litany to very different circumstances that now exist compared to the many thousands of years when technology and science were much less advanced.

                  Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post
                  Gaia didn't come down from the clouds and tell Garou not to have sex with eachother.

And while we don't have Gaia manifesting and laying out the rules, I think once the Garou noticed that children produced by Garou-Garou mating were always sterile and had serious birth defects, I think it is obvious why the Garou would think "Gaia does not want us to do this." It's just like if you keep sticking forks into electrical outlets and getting shocked, you eventually learn "This is a bad idea to do this" and not think "This is all the fault of the evil electricians union!"

                  Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post

We're not given the specifics of the Litany's origins, but it appears to have been created in the far distant past, perhaps even before the Gauntlet when the spirit world and material world was one, and forged at the legendary Table Rock.

                  I also want to note that while the Litany is commonly seen by players as only the thirteen major precepts, it is also very clear the actual Litany is much, much longer and that each tribe and caern have its own versions of it. But that is often overlooked by players or treated merely as flavor text with limited impact on games.

                  I can't comment on the other breeds' law codes because I am not as familiar with them. I seldom use the Changing Breeds in my chronicles. But if I remember right, one reason why some of them do not have something similar in their own law codes, is either because such matings do not produce offspring at all, or such offspring do not have the birth defects the metis do.

                  I disagree. This precept of the Litany is about stopping birth defects. Although the means by which they enforce the Litany could very well be more a matter of intolerance and hatred rather than being effective.

                  Certainly one could say, "Fine, they'll simply adopt perfect birth control or only have homosexual relations so children aren't an issue". And while you'd be right that children are no longer an issue, you only bring up the points you made earlier - can society simply allow certain matings, but not others, and retain the ban's effectiveness? It wouldn't, so it needs to be applied uniformly.

I've always interpreted the game as preferring an approach where the PCs keep what is best in Garou culture and reform and update the long form Litany as needed, but not that the thirteen major precepts are in themselves wrong.

                  Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post
                  As for why Children of Gaia have so many Metis, it isn't because they are 'Charach'. They take in the dirty indiscretions and bastards of other Tribes.
                  Yes, I agree most Children of Gaia are not Charach, and much of their metis population is adoptions of other tribes. They ignore all the problems ignoring this part of the Litany produces because they over value one particular virtue over the rest and ignore any evidence of the problems. This is what I meant by pathological altruism. But again, to what extent that is the case and the causes of it are up to the ST in their own chronicles. In some chronicles it may not exist at all, and in others it could be a serious problem despite being a small fraction of the tribe. It all depends. Neither approach is incorrect.

                  Same with the Glass Walkers.                   I think you mean the Bone Gnawers and not the Glass Walkers. However, I don't think it is necessarily the case that simply because a tribe's numbers are growing that it means it is successful. It could be a sign of it. But there may also be other facts related to it that hide problems. I think one issue (though not the only) that divides us may be the idea that all NPCs have the exact same character creation rules of the PCs - they always have the same build points, options, etc. Players build their player characters based on what they want to play - their either pick the least debilitating deformities (I'll be hairless because the -1 social penalty will rarely come up in combat, or albino because the other characters can make up for my penalty to Perception) or because they want to specifically roleplay out the issue. And that is fine. But as ST I want to take a real life approach to metis NPCs (and NPCs in general, I use the character creation rules as a guideline for NPCs - many NPCs have less build points and xp than if they were PCs). While I may give some Metis less debilitating deformities along the lines of what PCs generally have, I give others major ones and don't limit myself to the standard possible examples listed in the corebook. You can have a Metis with such severe mental impairment they will have all their Mental traits effectively at zero. Their bones may be so fragile they are completely useless as warriors and even require help to do normal tasks so that even their contributions won't offset the burdens they are creating. They may have such emotional problems that nobody around them can be physically safe. No player would ever pick these options for their PC, but I think they are totally valid examples of NPCs. So I don't think it is always a case of "We only get stronger by letting metis live and join our tribe." It depends on the kind of metis.

                  You asked for opinions, and this is how I handle it.
