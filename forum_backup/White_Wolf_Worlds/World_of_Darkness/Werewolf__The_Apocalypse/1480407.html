    The difference between Werewolf and Vampire's themes

    05-07-2022, 05:05 PM
    I wrote this over in the Tzimisce thread and thought it was relevant. Basically, my idea about the different themes of the games and what they emphasize.

    I was curious how you saw the difference between the two games and what gets to the heart of them.

    The difference between V:TM and W:TA

    Well focusing on that, I suppose it's not just what VAMPIRE: THE MASQUERADE is but also what WEREWOLF: THE APOCALYPSE is. I've mentioned that V:TM is Gothic Punk horror fiction about sin, damnation, and remorse from your activities. It is about coping with a dreadful curse and dealing with the fact that you are now divorced from whoever you are while alive. Also, the fact that you are immortal and trapped in a feudalist exploitative super-capitalist society where the rich are literal predators on the poor.

    W:TA by contrast is Tragic Heroic Mythology. Whereas vampire is Jane Eye, Wuthering Heights, and The Anarchist's Cookbook, W:TA is Beowulf, Oedipus Rex, and Last of the Mohicans. Werewolves have a lot of curse-like elements but their actual curse is the fact that they are going to lose Ragnarok and die fighting the Dragon and realize they've married their mother.

    Werewolves don't have the kind of self-loathing narcissistic tragedy that vampires do and, generally, all love being werewolves (I'm simplifying). They, however, are destined to throw themselves into the jaws of death to try to save the world. In V:TM, you are the monster. In Werewolf, you are not THE monster but slay the monster. It's just THE monster is going to win.

    And like all great heroes like Sigurd or Gilgamesh, they're horribly flawed in some way that is going to make their loss inevitable too.

    (Why I actually think Lunars/Werewolves fit into Exalted just fine)

    Souleaters don't work for V:TM because, while there's nothing wrong with it, Angel: The Series is not how you're meant to run being undead. Generally, most vampires are not heroes and fighting evil. A single vampire or coterie of vampires fighting evil is fine, I've run many a game like that but they're an aberration. Most vampires do not give a shit and if they found out there was a corporation making a deal with Satan to make more money, 50% of all vampires would react with, "Okay, THAT is a hot stock tip. Where do I invest in this Pentex?" Another 25% will go, "Okay, not my problem." Another 10% will be like, "Ah ha! A rival!" With maybe 5% appropriately horrified.

    Werewolf: The Apocalypse, by contrast, is a game where you need an assortment of monsters and horrifying cannon fodder because they are defined by throwing themselves into the jaws of Fenris. It doesn't matter that Formori are victims of the Wyrm, they're people to be killed because a werewolf lives for violence and dies by violence.

    At least that's my view.

    Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

      To me, there is also the difference in themes of living and seeing the world between them.

      In Vampire, your goal is to survive. Night in and night out, you do your best to see another moonrise. Everything you do is to ensure you may survive and be in favour of the group of vampires you hang out with and the vampire society you follow. You might be interested in the mythology of Cain, but you might as well not be.

      In Werewolf, your goal is to see the world survive. Inch by inch, you fight to fix what was broken no matter how desperate. You have a pack, your pack is your closest family. Your focus in growing your power is not to survive, but to fight the foe better. You often live more in the mythology of your species than in the modern world, depending on the tribe.

                      Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                      Well focusing on that, I suppose it's not just what VAMPIRE: THE MASQUERADE is but also what WEREWOLF: THE APOCALYPSE is. I've mentioned that V:TM is Gothic Punk horror fiction about sin, damnation, and remorse from your activities. It is about coping with a dreadful curse and dealing with the fact that you are now divorced from whoever you are while alive.
                      There are two ways to look at this. The first is that difference in intended themes by the developers of the game. "Personal Horror" versus "Savage Horror". whatever that might mean. Then there are the implicit differences from embedded pop culture themes between the two kinds of monsters.

                      For example, Vampire's game mechanics makes Humanity important. Werewolf has no such mechanics. So as designed, Werewolf is much less about "personal horror." But the game designers intended Werewolf to be about giving a damn about the world around you (when will you Rage), so the setting (in terms of the Litany and antagonists) is designed to bring that about.

                      In contrast, there are certain implicit themes. Many werewolf movies and books have an implied personal horror theme, even more than vampire. Because vampires, despite needing to live by feeding off the blood of humans, generally aren't presented as caring about that. But since 1941's Wolfman, werewolves are often depicted as not wanting to do the things they are compelled to do. Personal horror should be part of the genre. But W:tA's setting and mechanics generally play it down. It only happens if a PC or ST really push it in roleplay. On the other hand, cinematic werewolves are often depicted as being in opposition to other monsters, because the "thinking" side of the monster - the human who doesn't want to kill - sympathizes with the victims of the monsters. So the Wolf Man fights Frankenstein's Monster, Dracula, and other monsters. So WtA having fomori pretty much emulates the same thing.

                      And there are plenty of other implicit themes. If you explore the Wolf Man, Howling, American Werewolf in London, Wolfen, Werewolf of Paris, Wolf, and many other werewolf media, legend, and folklore, there are lots of other themes. We've gotten into some of them in the other thread talking about the movie Howling.

                      Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                      Also, the fact that you are immortal and trapped in a feudalist exploitative super-capitalist society where the rich are literal predators on the poor.
                      My only comment on this is you are wrong about limiting it to capitalism. Humanity is a hierarchical species. We see the same behavior in humans as we see in chimpanzees. Capitalism has nothing to do with it. It's why explicitly anti-capitalist societies like communism, various non-communist socialist societies, and pre-capitalist societies are just as exploitative if not more. There will always be an elite. And there will always be a need for somebody to shovel shit.

                      Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                      W:TA by contrast is Tragic Heroic Mythology. Whereas vampire is Jane Eye, Wuthering Heights, and The Anarchist's Cookbook, W:TA is Beowulf, Oedipus Rex, and Last of the Mohicans. Werewolves have a lot of curse-like elements but their actual curse is the fact that they are going to lose Ragnarok and die fighting the Dragon and realize they've married their mother.
                      This is definitely the culture of the Garou. They are an aristocratic warrior culture. However, the setting does not define what the PCs will do. Just as vampire PCs won't necessarily act as appropriate in the feudal culture that defines vampire society, PCs won't necessarily act appropriately as in the Iliad.

                      However, Werewolf has Renown which is a very different mechanic than Status. Status doesn't drive anything. As a marker of reputation, even Anarchs could have Status of a kind. Renown, though, drives things to a much greater extent because Renown means Rank, and Rank drives what Gifts one could learn. And while it is always possible for even Ronin to learn Gifts, in practice it's much more of an obstacle. Thus the mechanics push PCs to uphold standard Garou culture in a way that Vampire does not push Caitiff and Anarchs to do so.

                      Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                      Werewolves don't have the kind of self-loathing narcissistic tragedy that vampires do and, generally, all love being werewolves (I'm simplifying). They, however, are destined to throw themselves into the jaws of death to try to save the world. In V:TM, you are the monster. In Werewolf, you are not THE monster but slay the monster. It's just THE monster is going to win.
                      This really depends on the ST because there are at least concepts in the setting which delve into the same categories. Werewolves have Lunatics, Harano, and the Curse. An ST who wants to highlight those can create elements which come close to the same things in Vampire. And many STs in Vampire don't play particular attention to Humanity and personal horror because they get in the way of sect wars or other combat activity.

                      Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                      Werewolf: The Apocalypse, by contrast, is a game where you need an assortment of monsters and horrifying cannon fodder because they are defined by throwing themselves into the jaws of Fenris. It doesn't matter that Formori are victims of the Wyrm, they're people to be killed because a werewolf lives for violence and dies by violence.
                      Werewolf as a game has outside antagonists. You are monsters fighting other monsters. So yeah, PCs kill them. The Vampire setting generally ignores other creatures. It's very insular. You only deal with other vampires and humans because the game's sourcebooks never really address other creatures as potential antagonists. If they did, then vampires would probably treat them just as well as werewolves do - you kill them because they don't matter. Because Vampire was the first WoD game, it had to be insular. There weren't other games to reference. While each later game had to reference the games before it to some degree. But there isn't anything stopping a Vampire ST from including certain creatures/splats if they wanted. If they don't, it's more because of inertia (because the sourcebooks tend not to include them) as opposed to them somehow being intriniscally incorrect to do so.

                      But werewolf definitely designed fomori to avoid mortal entanglements. Once someone becomes fomori, it's pretty much unredeemable. You can't "un-fomori" or "de-fomori" them as written. There's no myth like Golconda because things are written from the perspective of the Garou. Garou probably ignore any evidence that there is something like Golconda for vampires too.

                                      Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                                      Yeah, I don't think most vampires even know what Golconda is, and if they ran into a vampire they'd probably just kill it. That said, presumaqbly vampires in Golconda don't trigger Sense Wyrm, so it's possible that werewolves wouldn't even notice the vampire around in the first place.
                                      As written, any vampire who has a Humanity of 7 or greater doesn't register with Sense Wyrm, and I believe Gangrel don't detect even at lower Humanity (I want to say not until Humanity 4, but I can't remember what book states this) and there can be special merits which prevent it at all. So vampires who have achieved Golconda must by definition be Humanity 10 and won't detect as Wyrm.

                                      This is one of the big reasons why 1e WoD was open to some interaction between vampires and werewolves. Certainly many Garou wouldn't care at all - if the encounter any vampire they will destroy them regardless of whether Sense Wyrm goes off. But certainly the Children of Gaia and some others might be more tender hearted. And the Glass Walkers and Bone Gnawers tend to make pragmatic decisions with the local vampires so that it is not constant warfare. And some camps are into working with one group of Wyrm minions to turn them against other Wyrm minions, or just to exploit them for their own purposes.

                                      So while I've noticed many players refusing to tolerate any relations between Garou and werewolves, early WoD made an effort to show it could happen. If anything, the multiple instances in 1e that showed vampires and Garou getting along bothered enough players to stage a counter reaction. While it was always supposed to be an exception, many players thought it happened way too much. So the fanbase started reacting by saying it would never happen. Personally, I do think it's OK in the setting to allow there not to be total warfare all the time. But every ST and player group will have a different tolerance level as to when it happens too much.
