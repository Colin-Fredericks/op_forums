                                The narrative of the game should punch-up, even if the setting punches down (especially if the setting punches down) because the game is punk by its nature and punk is punching up.

                                The game itself however, not the setting, should NOT be punching down. The sheer fact that it’s against the spirit of the game and is imo morally wrong aside, it’s just not pragmatic. The benefit-cost ratio that comes with terms like ‘M*tis’ is obviously slanted towards cost. Every player of an indigenous or socially conscious background who puts the book down and dismisses it after seeing the term used the way it is (or the tribal names of the Pure Brothers) is entirely within their rights to do so and has actively lost the company a sale, particularly in the RPG market where buy-in to a franchise is so important for its success and growth.

                                The term simply doesn’t offer enough benefit to merit its inclusion imo. It was born out of a lack of understanding for the context and it’s been kept out of habit. There is no reason why it couldn’t be exchanged for a more irl neutral term, even an in-universe pejorative term. Calling the children of two Garou ‘Mules’ or ‘Crinos’ or ‘Runts’ or ‘Garou-born’ or ‘Wastrels’ all accomplishes the in-universe goal without using an irl term for any disadvantaged group.

                                It costs nothing to use a different term and its actively beneficial in many regards, that’s why I’m always surprised (we’ll not surprised anymore, but annoyed) when I see this argument reoccur.

                                    From a purely simulationist standpoint, in-universe, the WOD is indeed full of slurs and horrible people saying truly despicable things. I don't disagree with you there. If the game was purely a "what-if?" simulation to build the most plausible World of Darkness from initial assumptions, yes it's plausible for white Garou to casually keep using a racial slur in that way. But promoting racial slurs isn't even necessary to make the WoD an awful place. And in-universe the Bastet, Ajaba, Pure Ones, Grondr, and probably Stargazers and Silent Striders would still use completely different names, which they don't currently in canon.

                                    A game, or literature, or work of music or visual art, is more than just a private exercise in academic simulation. It's a public statement. That creates potential problems even beyond the pragmatic problem of alienating large numbers of potential players. Any society, and global human society overall, is made up of the words and statements and discourse that we say to one another, and with which we surround children. This is why I asked you if you know what it's like to be bombarded with constant messages of hate while growing up. Words matter. Names can absolutely break the spirits of children and adults if they are part of an oppressed group. Words alone can and do drive 10-year-old children to commit suicide. If this isn't part of your personal experience, then understandably you don't know what that's like, but please have the respect to believe other people who have lived through that. Don't invalidate other peoples' experiences just because it hasn't happened to you personally.

                                    Words change the world. They are our real-world Awakened Magick. Words create politics and social movements. Words teach little children to love or hate themselves and their neighbors. Words teach people that violence against each other is acceptable, or not acceptable. Words teach children who counts as a person, and who is subhuman. Words are not neutral things that exist only for private simulation. The choice of words and depictions in a game or novel or album have a real, tangible, long-lasting effect on the real world and the lives of real people. A popular work can leave cultural and political effects for centuries or millennia after it's created. Those effects are not morally neutral, and wilfully ignoring them is not OK.

                                    Writing with awareness of the power of language doesn't mean we shouldn't heap up awful things in the World of Darkness. It does mean that we should be responsible in how we use awful words and situations. To draw an analogy, rape and forced pregnancy are real and surely common in the WoD, too. Of course the BSDs engage in that, but the game makes it clear they are "bad guys" and their behavior is not OK. BSDs aren't meant to be played, and their behavior isn't front and center at the beginning of the book. Writing about a sept of the "good guys" who force all female Garou to bear at least one metis whether they want to or not, for an ostensibly justifiable cause (in Changing Ways) is not OK. Gratuitous rape scenes, rape used shallowly just for shock value, and rape jokes would also not be OK in a game.
