                                Speaking of Loch Ness, here’s some interesting tid bits I think are cool and could be useful for a variety of settings.

                                •Aleister Crowley (yes, that Crowley; The Great Beast 666, Most Evil Man Alive, Prophet of the Aeon of Horus, etc.) had a manor on Loch Ness (Boleskine Manor; later owned by Jimmy Page and burning down in 2015) where Crowley as a young man still tied with the Golden Dawn supposedly carried out an incomplete Magickal Working. Apparently some occultists think that Crowley’s incomplete working might have kicked up some occult juices around Loch Ness which made the appearances of the Loch Ness Monster more significant.

                                Similarly there’s also an anecdote where Crowley claims to have seen a water nymph while climbing up a waterfall-rock face at Loch Ness.

                                Related with that field of odd tid bits about Fun Fact and speculation from various esoteric groups, there are some claims that the Lock Ness Monster is not a cryptic or living fossil but actually some sort of spiritual entity related to a pre-Christian Dragon-Serpent Cult (perhaps one brought to Britain by the Vikings), veneration of which may have been preserved in folk rituals up through the Industrial Revolution (where supposedly villagers and peasants would toss the first batch of beer every year into the water as an offering in hopes of good harvests and bountiful sea produce such as fish, sea glass, drift wood, no drownings, etc.

                                Any number of the above could be used as interesting plot hooks I think and could be used as inspiration for any region, not just Loch Ness imo.

                                I can easily see the Crowley story as either a Mage or Werewolf plot hook where a prodigy occultist leaves a ritual undone and the players might come across summoned entities that have been running loose ever since.

                                One could have the idea of a pre-Christian Serpent cult be tied either to the Fianna, Get of Fenris, or even Black Spiral Dancers kinfolk.

                                                    The fall of the White Howlers is something that has dramatically changed a lot through the editions. There have been major changes which impact the setting severely.

                                                    In first edition Book of the Wyrm, the White Howlers themselves were superb specimens of Garou - the only ones who could truly defeat the legions of Rome (and the fomori who came with them) despite their relatively small numbers. As a result, the Wyrm tried a different approach. Somebody - perhaps a fomori, a Bane, or some other Wyrm minion - activated a secret Pit in Scotland that summoned Banes which quickly possessed the White Howler kinfolk and deformed them. One pack went into the Pit to destroy it, but they failed and became the first Black Spiral Dancers. This single pack them came out, killed many of the White Howlers (presumably by ambush and surprise) and forced the others to walk the spiral. Cororuc (note the spelling) was simply the only member of the first pack to enter the Spiral to not be corrupted, and the others killed him before they ambushed the other Howlers. So in this version of events, there is definitely no "shame" for any other Garou. Nobody could have expected what was going to happen. And a strong argument could be said that even the White Howlers are not really to blame for what happened given the speed by which it happened. (And Cororuc - instead of being the last White Howler - was actually the first one killed by the new Black Spiral Dancers.)

                                                    This was heavily based on Robert E. Howard's stories of Bran Mak Morn. The Howlers themselves mirrored the physical perfection of Bran while the corrupted kinfolk mirrored the degenerate status of the Picts themselves.

                                                    In second edition Book of the Wyrm, the corruption and decline of the White Howler kinfolk, instead of happening relatively quickly as a result of some nefarious Bane activity, occurred over a longer period of time. The White Howlers themselves allowed their Rage to control their behavior too much. And this was possibly the reason the tribe routinely descended into Malfeas to test their worth. The Howlers themselves choose to make dark pacts with infernal forces to stop the Roman advance into Scotland. These corrupted Howlers waged war against the uncorrupted Howlers, and they killed Coruroc (note the spelling change) as the last uncorrupted White Howler. Presumably this was when the remaining, corrupted, Howlers walked the Black Spiral and became the Dancers.

                                                    A different version was given in the 2e Players Guide. In this version, the states the bulk of the tribe, filled with pride and hubris, went down a Wyrmhole and came out as Black Spiral Dancers. It was only then that the Dancers corrupted their kinfolk!

                                                    In these versions, again the rest of the Garou have no reason to have any shame. Except now the White Howlers themselves are responsible for their own demise. So this is very different than what happened in 1e.

                                                    I don't think there was ever a version for Revised. It just kept the 2e story (or stories since there are two versions of them).

                                                    The W20 corebook version is different still. There's no more talk of degenerated kinfolk. Instead, the White Howlers decide en masse as a tribe they are going to fight the Wyrm in its lair in Malfeas and achieve great glory. Instead, they end up walking the Labyrinth and all become Black Spiral Dancers at once. Only Cororuc escaped and managed to inform a Fianna sept before he died. Unlike 2e, the White Howlers are not responsible for their own corruption. It was just a tactical attack against the Wyrm that went horribly wrong.

                                                    There is yet a FIFTH version in the W20 White Howler tribebook gives us a variant of the W20 corebook story. This time we're told that the Howlers had to deal with Rome and an outbreak of Wyrm activity at the same time. It looked like the Romans were going to conquer Caledonia. So the tribe gathered and debated what to do. They decided the entire White Howler tribe would travel south and attack the Roman fortresses and destroy the Roman legions and any fomori and Wyrm creatures also there. This took months to do (despite that by using Moon Bridges they need only take a few days at most to put themselves into position), and when they finally attacked they didn't find many legions or Wyrm minions.

                                                    When the Howlers came back bloodied (presumably by butchering thousands of defenseless civilian humans), but victorious they found that while they were gone all the Roman legions and fomori had devastated their homeland and their kinfolk had become corrupted. (At least that missing detail from the corebook is now filled.) Many had become fomori and the Howlers spent years destroying them as well as sealing off the Pits or Wyrmholes the Roman fomori had created. But still the land and kinfolk remain corrupted. But eventually they made their way to a great Pit in northwestern Scotland which they found lead to Malfeas itself. (I should note that in second edition Chronicle of the Black Labyrinth, it's clear that the passage that leads to Malfeas is in southeast Scotland somewhere near the county of Fife so the geography does not even match.)

                                                    This lead to a big discussion among the White Howlers on what to do, and they eventually decided to invite all the tribes to help them invade Malfeas. The other tribes didn't respond, so the Howlers cursed them and then went in alone and became the Black Spiral Dancers.
                                                    ​
                                                    It is this fifth version, and ONLY the fifth version, that lays out any blame to the other Garou tribes. For me, the W20 White Howler tribebook gives the worst origin of the Black Spiral Dancers. It doesn't make sense on several levels. It departs the most from actual history. It has several plot holes. It gets the location of the first Pit wrong. And it is the only one that smears the rest of the Garou Nation. Blech.

                                                    I personally like the first edition version the best. The second edition has some merit, but by making the White Howlers corrupt BEFORE they entered the Labyrinth, it robs the significance of the Black Spiral itself. The W20 version just smears the Garou as a whole. The latter editions really wanted to make the Garou their own enemy. Which is OK to a limited degree, but too over played.

                                                    The major flaw of the 1e version is that it is incomplete, and the ST needs to fill in a lot of details. There's a lot of vagueness of the Wyrm did this and the Wyrm did that. It would be much better if we know who exactly activated the Pit that corrupted the kinfolk, what kind of fomori they have transformed into, which kinfolk might have been "saved" but later tainted the Fianna to some degree, how Whippoorwill (or more likely Nightjar since Nightjar is native to Scotland and the Whippoorwill isn't) became the tribal totem, how this single Dancer pack killed all the other Howlers, and other such details. So we can pillage some bits of the other editions to improve it. But I think fundamentally it is the best version of the five we've been given.
