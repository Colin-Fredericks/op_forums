    What makes the Rage track so much trickier than the Hunger mechanic in V5 is that there the idea is simple: The less Hunger the better, increase bad, decrease good. And so other mechanics can orient themselves around that formula. With Rage there are negatives awaiting players at both ends and depending on the situation, you either don't wanna be caught up in low Rage (and lose the wolf) or high Rage (Brutal Outcomes for non-violent actions and WP damage for going over 5... and there are more out-of-combat Rage gains than in-combat ones where presumably more Rage checks could decrease the Rage instead). And so any mechanic depressing Rage gain or increasing it would just end up further complicating the timing issue of where players would wanna be on the Rage track depending on the situation. Also, how much do you want players caught up in managing the Rage track or how will players react when they don't do that and for example have high Rage in a section where they don't wanna be in combat but Brutal Outcomes are making it hard not to seek violence?

                                                What strikes me about Rage and Rage dice is that these seem to solely exist to punish players. Were I writing a new edition of Werewolf: the Apocalypse, I would have introduced something like the following:
                                                • Rage is a trait representing the primal power of werewolves, and their connection to their animalistic nature.
                                                • Rage is gained in various contexts but, seriously, we are going to give some proper, details examples, rather than just a bit of vague guidance.
                                                • Each point of Rage a character has provides one Rage dice. You can choose to add Rage dice to a dice pool, which represents werewolves calling upon their primal energies. Adding these dice increase the chance of success on a given roll, but also present the chance of a brutal outcome; unlike the current version of the rules, brutal outcomes are not necessarily failures, but normally present downsides.
                                                • An example: a Glass Walker corporate spy is attempting to sneak into an office building. The character doesn't have any suitable gifts, and, for various reasons, the character is not able to use the Umbra as a shortcut. Does the character add any Rage dice to the roll? These would increase the chance of success, but also increase the chance of a brutal outcome (maybe the character would lose patience trying to pick the lock, and just batter the door down instead).
                                                • So, players have meaningful choices; do I draw on my Rage to increase the chance of success on a given roll, but with the chance of a brutal outcome?
                                                • We want some guidance to brutal outcomes, beyond vague hand-waving, and brutal outcomes when rolls are successes can be as interesting as when they are failures. Remember the classic werewolf trope of victims being killed in a way to suggest that they have been attacked by a wolf or monster? That's going to happen if you defeat an enemy with a brutal outcome. It may of course, be worth it.
                                                • And then sometimes a brutal outcome won't matter at all; trying to defeat a nexus crawler? Throw all the Rage dice you have at it, you're going to need it, and there won't be any consequences for ripping off a tentacle or two with your teeth.
                                                • If we want to have penalties for having Rage which is too high, have a mechanic forcing Rage dice into a dice pool for every point by which Rage exceeds Willpower. They are still bonus dice, mind.
                                                • And taking actions to reduce Rage are perfectly legitimate; we don't want sidebars telling players they shouldn't take actions just to reduce Rage. There's nothing wrong with characters being aware of their Rage, and taking actions to moderate it.

                                                So, the above is not significantly different from Rage as written, but with some subtle differences to make Rage dice a benefit, rather than a penalty, and giving players a bit more agency to how Rage intersects with actions of a given character.

                                                (And this is my five-minute version; with a bit of effort and playtesting, you should actually be able to arrive at some mechanics which you might want to appear in the game.)

                                                    Another weird bit in the writing is that in the section for Rage-checks it says at the end: "Rage decreasing in this manner doesn't necessarily represent the Garou becoming "less angry" but rather models how the Rage is expressed, the pent-up energy manifesting as Gifts or a more vicious supernatural form." But that principle doesn't hold for Brutal Outcomes. There Rage isn't just "pent-up energy", instead it does represent some sort of anger that calls for violence and destruction. And so a Garou with decreased Rage could be considered "less angry" as Brutal Outcomes are less likely to occur.

                                                    But from how the mechanics work, I can absolutely see a game that reflects the description from the H5-corebook: "Werewolves rampage against the structures that have elevated humanity from the poverty of the past, devoting themselves to a furious primitivism enforced by fang and claw.​" After all, Rage becomes much less of a headache and even beneficial when you embrace violence. Like, any Brutal Outcome when you try to damage or harm something or somebody means that two or more 1s or 2s are translated into four automatic successes. You have that and combine that with the physical dice-bonuses of the Glabro/Crinos/Hispo and you're gonna win any combat-encounter.
