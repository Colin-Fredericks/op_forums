                            Originally posted by Psicose View Post
                            Speaking of the Fianna's habits, they normally don't like Metis, but there is a mention that if one of them is born with servant horns he is seen as blessed by their spirit, how does that work?
                            I’d assume that ought to read ‘antlers’ based on Stag being patron spirit of the Tribe and a perceived connection to that patron trumping the instinctive Fianna dislike
                            of being reminded that great passions can have ugly consequences (Something visible in their treatment of those whose birth breaches the Litany, as well as their tendency to emphasise the positive and forget the negative).


                            Regarding the Fianna as a whole, I’d say that their great charm is the ability to make being a Garou look FUN (In a way that only the Bone Gnawers come close to matching) and their great failing is a tendency to pursue the Joys of Living in a way that makes life harder for others (Sometimes for their own kinfolk, always for their rivals and all too often for their other neighbours).

                            Also, one wrinkle in the fabric of their makeup that isn’t always emphasised is that the Fianna aren’t just werewolves, they’re Fairy Tale werewolves: one element of 5th edition that I really enjoyed (Despite giving an actual groan at seeing the name for this tribe’s successors) was the notion that the Hart Wardens more or less run on Fairy Tale logic (Especially when it comes to their rules of Hospitality).

                            I’m not going to lie, were one putting together a Hart Warden I would absolutely take Folkloric Tells as a flaw: it fits my conception of the Tribe quite beautifully.

                                I feel like there are some people here wouldn't really like how IRL wolves handle other wolves, especially puppies, with visible deformities or external signs of disease. I feel like part of the fun/horror is having Garou needing to deal with the compounded version of wolf aversion to new/different things combined with human tribalism/xenophobia. Literally being afflicted by instincts and spirit-pacts that would seek to compel you to act like a savage person and the whole of Garou Culture and Religion which seems to broadly indicate that such violence and intolerance is a good thing and 'if you don't like it, look what we're up against and tell us we're wrong for being violent and paranoid'. I think it is important in a horror game that the protagonists not be wholly righteous, and I think in that 90's feeling, even the 'good guys' should have detestable aspects that allow the PCs to be countercultural to their own side.
