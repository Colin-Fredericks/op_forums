    Punishment rights and you!

    04-02-2016, 12:11 AM
    So, what's to stop someone handing out punishment rights willy-nilly, because mechanically you can do it to anyone.

    Say the swords of heimdall (The worst racists of fenrir) come across a female black lupus get that might even have some fera blood in them. Can they just perform punishment on such a character? Rite of renunciation, stone of scorn, even gaia's vengeful teeth... The character has done nothing wrong, but they believe themselves to be in the right, and a skilled ritesmaster is a skilled ritesmaster.

    Alternatively, a shadowlord has a rival. He knocks his rival down a peg with punishment rites. His enemy decreased in honour and rank is now a lesser threat to him.
    Lastly, with the shadowlord, he can engineer his rival into the position for a punishment rite (framing or just being put into unwinnable situations) Would this affect rites?


    Also I've theorized that if Gaia's teeth should be survivalable. The question being; How long does it last. Infinite? well, of course 'nobody survives this rite' applies, if it's infinite. A month? Difficult. A week? Doable, less? Very possible, all depending on what gifts you've got. If you're a ragabash or gnawer or silent strider or uktena you'll maybe have some tricky gift that'll outpace the pack without causing you to burn against too many things. (we can debate on metaphysics and the occult when it comes to luna's blessing, but Great Leap alone is an excellent escape tool. There are many other gifts and possibly fetishes which would be of great value here.)
    Like, yeah, sure, if you're fantastic at escaping you likely wouldn't have been around long enough to be subjected to the rite in the first place, but that unlucky character or particularly daring trickster might attempt a post-rite escape (the point of the rite is the humiliating chase, after all, might as well utilize that in knocking low some pride)

    I argued this with a friend, he got very mad. Apparently, the point of the rite is that the recipient dies, and you can't survive it. I feel like that really takes the cleverness out of it. If a player is clever enough to escape, they you shouldn't railroad them to death, Escaping is worth massive renown (possibly counterballanced by whatever they did to be victim anyhow) and they will no doubt will feel awesome about it. If a ritesmaster goes through the trouble to use this rite and it doesn't result in a dead victim, then they're a fool for not simply executing the offender outright.

    Now the rite of hell made flesh, there's a fun one to figure out how to escape from.
    (i've already got ideas)

          Minor nitpick: you can't force Renunciation on someone. It's an Accord Rite and has to be performed by the Garou seeking to change Auspices.

          In general though, the Punishment Rites section is pretty clear that the ST is free to have such rites fail if used without cause (and even backfire to cause those trying to use them against their intended purposes) specifically to stop any such shenanigans. This is actually a quote from the general system section for Punishment Rites from W20: "Because these rites are enforced and empowered by the spirit world, truly unjust punishment rites may fail automatically, leading to a sure loss of Honor for the one who ordered them."

          So no, you can't just use them willy-nilly; or rather you can, but you're just going to lose Honor like mad for abusing your authority while the subject doesn't suffer any repercussions.

          It doesn't matter what the Ritemaster and any helpers believe. It matters what the spirits know, and if they know the use of the Rite isn't just, they'll refuse to empower it.

          As for Gaia's Vengeful Teeth, I wouldn't really take the text to mean that the Rite itself causes death. It has a set end condition for its effects. Surviving it is impossible more because at least six Garou have you subdued well enough to perform the Rite, and are now trying to actively kill you once the Rtemaster is done.

                  Level Five
                  As one of the greatest punishments among the Garou,
                  this rite is reserved for traitors, those who consort with
                  the Wyrm or cowards whose actions (or lack thereof)
                  cause the deaths of many others. At least five werewolves
                  drag the traitor to a spot of hard, cracked earth and
                  stones. The ritemaster then stabs a sharpened twig or
                  stone into her own hand as she recites the traitor’s sins
                  against Gaia. Smearing her blood over the traitor’s eyes,
                  ears and forehead, the ritemaster cries in grief and rage.
                  As the blood and tears drip to the hard ground, the rite
                  takes effect. From that moment on, whatever of Gaia
                  touches the traitor transforms into razor-sharp silver so
                  long as it touches his flesh. Crinos hunters then chase
                  the traitor like a dog. The ground beneath the traitor
                  chews into his feet, and his death becomes an agonizing
                  ordeal. The offender’s name is then stricken from
                  all histories, and it will be spoken only as a curse from
                  that moment forward.
                  System: As long as the ritemaster’s blood touches
                  the traitor’s body, the traitor cannot step sideways into
                  the Umbra. No one survives being subject to this rite.

Good luck using Hare's leap ( or other variations) that landing is going to hurt, the pain is going to drive the victim to frenzy very quickly. It is possible to wash the blood off but there are still at least five garou in pursuit.

                      I believe Punishment Rites can only be approved as a result of the sept's decisions during Moots, specifically the Cracking of the Bone. If a Garou accuses another Garou of violating the Litany (as interpreted by that sept there), the sept judges it and pronounces justice according to sept tradition. That punishment can include one of the Punishment Rites. At that point, a Garou who knows that Rite will perform it on behalf of the sept.

                      I don't believe the kind of vigilant justice (more like injustice) is permitted within Garou society. Of course, if you perform the Rites the Rites still work. But once word gets out that this is being done, the Garou of the local septs will begin hunting down the perpetrators. When they find them, they'll judge and punish them. In addition, for any Garou who was unjustly punished, their local sept will take the necessary steps to "reverse" the Punishment Rite innflicted upon them.

                      So what's to stop someone handing them out willy nilly? Garou society itself. A person who does so will be driven into exile from the nation, if not killed outright. He will be stripped of Renown and may even become Ronin. The behavior you described is like a Judge deciding to form a star chamber and hiring someone to perform extrajudicial executions. No matter how much he justifies it, his actions are completely illegal and have no force of law. He is engaged in criminal acts himself.

                      Frankly, someone who even seems like he intends to do such things will never be allowed to learn such Rites in the first place.

                      And if there is even a sept of such Garou who do such things, the other local septs will likely judge them to be of the Wyrm and go to war with the intent on destroying them.

                                                  The "No one survives" is not in the fluff, it's not in the oral stories, it's flat out in the system. The system states no one survives. Its a method of execution, so yes it's main point is in fact death, a terrible death meant to leave a message but it's end result is death. The WoD games are famous for their not covering or thinking about every little contingency that can occur (such as all the gifts you list). So it's rather neat and clean for a WoD game by saying "no one survives" (again not "it is said no one survives"). If you disagree with my reading into the rite that such gifts wouldn't work, that's fine. It does however state "no one survives" so there is no reason to believe that the effect of things turning to silver would eventually end before the subject dies. You may think this is a lot to ask from a rite but also keep in mind it's a level 5 rite. These are the highest rites the Garou have (I only know of a single lvl 6 rite and it was a Fianna rite that let you sleep like King Arthur, I was not impressed), so I don't see why its expecting a lot.

                                                  Also if the point of a rite is to guarantee death, yes that's sufficient enough to guarantee death.

                                                  Also, you can rule it anyway you want at your table. But the consensus seems to be that if you're looking for support here at these forums, you may not find what you seek.
