            The Dark Mystic sounds cool, I definitely see them as a solid example of a Shadow Lord Theurge and a good player-facing sample but I agree with others who said that it they don’t quite see how the Dark Mystic needs to be a Lupus in particular with the write up it gets.

            Agreeing further with others I could definitely see a greater focus on a sort of primaeval mysticism that the Lupus are in contact with as worth exploration. After all even Animism is going to be developed by centuries of experience from a human perspective with the human premises of subsistence and survival. A Theurge who began life as a wolf, a creature with radically different premises, should view the mystical realm differently and perhaps with the Gnosis 5 that Lupus get their deserves to be some word-count dedicated to the Primaeval Mysticism that Lupus would be in touch with and how it differs from even the Animism of Human/Homid practices. Perhaps a Lupus Theurge can be differentiated from a Homid because they have an instinctual spiritual sense, they have lived fully as an animal without human mediation, and unlike Homids who must carefully observe relationships and customs the Lupus naturally knows it’s position and relations.

            Of course there also would remain how to distinguish the Lupus Theurge in general and the Lupus Theurge Shadow Lord in particular. I like the suggestion that they feel a natural sensitivity for weather phenomena. Perhaps one could represent this by having LTSL be storm-whisperers or weather-whisperers who get some type of bonus to rolls having to do with comprehending the weather.

            Anyway all that aside I agree with what No One Said about the geographical vibe of the Shadow Lords, it does seem that they tended to get more of a Southern/Hispanic influence that was interesting.

                One thing I just noticed is the relatively low Primal Urge (for lupus) in the templates in Ways of the Wolf. All are at 2 except for one template which is at 3. Since Primal Urge is pretty much the primary Ability a wolf needs to survive in the wilderness, 2 seems low to me. It pretty much acts as multiple Abilities as long as you are in wolf form. Each character has 2 or 3 dots in Survival which you only really need if you are in homid form and are trying to survive in that form. But any problem you have would best be solved by shifting into lupus form and using your Primal-Urge. Those points in Survival can then be spent on other things. A strong case can be made that Stealth and Alertness would also be covered. Only if you are in homid or glabro would you need to be using those other skills. I understand STs wouldn't want it to too useful, but wolves don't think rationally. They use their animal instincts, and in animal form this would work best. Without having to put points in Skill to get those, you can make Knowledges your Secondary and get more points in Garou specific knowledges like Enigmas, Rituals, possibly Law to represent the Litany, and the always popular Occult.

                I have a feeling those points put into Alertness, Stealth, and Survival are trying to cover functions that Primal-Urge would better cover those functions for one of the lupus breed. Most likely any dots in Animal Ken as well. You can argue against that in an urban setting heavy campaign where lupus may spend a lot of time in homid form and need to know a more human way of doing things, but this is in Ways of the Wolf and the templates are presumably for use in a wilderness oriented chronicle. Staying in lupus form would be the normal state, and you would only switch to homid in special circumstances.

                I would expect at least a 3 for all lupus and can easily justify lupus characters having a 4 or 5 at character creation. But perhaps that is just my perspective, and other players will disagree.

                I am also surprised we don't see more Kinfolk background where the tribe's human kinfolk are there to assist the lupus in fitting in among human society. That would make more sense than for the Dark Mystic than Resources. But I also notice that many players don't see the utility of the Kinfolk background, and I have always found it very useful for characters especially those who want to do things in the human world, but may be limited because of their Rage and the Curse.

                                Human Mediator (Child of Gaia Philodox)

                                So, two curious things about this one right off the bat. First is that this is the only one of these shown in human form, and because of that is the only one whose gender (female) is instantly recognizable. Second is that among the premade characters in Rite of Passage, that Philodox is also a female Child of Gaia (a Homid). I'm not exactly why this was a recurring thing. I admit that the Children of Gaia are a tribe that slots very easily into the "balanced mediator" role of the Auspice, but I do find it a little surprising creatively.

                                She was taken from her mother at a young age by human "researchers" (no idea why it decided that needed to be in quotation marks), and ended up bonding with them. She was then taken away by another Garou around age two and underwent the First Change and induction into her tribe. She realized the reason she was so comfortable with humans is that she had the seed of humanity within her. (That's almost verbatim.)
                                Because of her background, she is all in on her tribe's ideals of peaceful coexistence with humans, and seeks to take advantage of her ability to assume human form to approach, interact with, and hopefully educate humans about accepting Garou and healing Gaia.
                                This was back when the idea of restarting the Impergium was thrown around a lot, and this character holds herself up as an alternative to that, feeling that her life shows that humans and wolves can live together.
                                As a Half-Moon, she sees herself as an agent of balance, especially in resolving conflicts between Garou and humans (and it doesn't say this, but I presume also between Lupus and Homids as well). Her ideas about peaceful coexistence don't extend to the Wyrm, but she feels that Wyrm influenced humans should be converted and reeducated rather than automatically killed.

                                The image is of a young female who looks about like you would expect for a circa 1993 GenX female outdoorsy type from the Pacific Northwest, Midwest, or Northeast to be dressed, complete with flannel shirt and jeans. Perfectly fine, and honestly wouldn't seem out of place if you saw it in an early Vampire or Mage book either.

                                For Attributes, Physicals are tertiary, at 2/2/2. Socials are primary, with 4/4/2. I do kind of like the idea of a Child of Gaia with a high Manipulation trait. And, ironically compared with the later Black Furies book, we have a female character with an average appearance. Mental is secondary, with 3/2/3. All in all, I think this is a solid spread for a character like this.

                                For Abilities, Talents are actually secondary. She has 2 each in Expression, Primal Urge, and Subterfuge, and 1 each in Brawl, Dodge, and Empathy. I think I might have moved a dot from Primal Urge to Empathy, as I think that might better fit this character's back story and concept. Skills are primary, with 3 each in Animal Ken, Leadership, and Performance, and 2 each in Stealth and Survival. Regarding Animal Ken, my read of the 1st and 2nd ed is that a lot of it is understanding how animals - including prey animals - think, and being able to predict and manipulate their behavior. So I can see wolves and Lupus getting some mileage out of this as a means of being better hunters and in understanding when some sort of potential danger has disturbed the local fauna. I'm not entirely sold on the idea of this character needing three dots in that skill. And like Primal Urge, given this character's back story, I could see a case for Survival only being a 1. I could also see a case for having Skills as secondary and Talents as primary, for a heavier focus on Empathy and Expression. Knowledge is tertiary, with 2 in Investigation and 1 each in Enigmas, Occult, and Rituals. I think maybe I would have dropped Occult and moved that dot into Enigmas or Rituals.

                                Backgrounds are 1 each in Allies and Pure Breed. The Ally is supposed to be one of the researchers who raised her, who apparently may not know she's a werewolf. Under 2nd ed rules, I'd probably put another dot or two in Pure Breed. Maybe also one in Fetish, as I could see this character getting some good mileage out of a Harmony Flute.

                                And, like the rest, we have the default traits for Rage (3), Gnosis (5), and Willpower (4).

                                For freebies, I'd probably bump WP up a dot or two, and focus the rest on Abilities like Empathy, Expression, and Leadership, as well as maybe some Alertness and higher Dodge.

                                Overall, I think this is a pretty good character. It's very simple and straightforward, but seems to be a decent representation of her Auspice and Tribe. I think the back story helps explain why this character is a lot different from the rest, including the fact that it's the only one presented in Homid form. I think the stats might benefit from some minor tweaks, but generally speaking, I like it just fine.

                                                            Howler (Fianna Galliard)

                                                            So, 1st ed tended to present the Galliards as essentially Bards. And by that I mean D&D Bards who are primarily focused on musical performances rather than actual Celtic Bards who are keepers of culture, lore, history, and knowledge. And this template certainly keeps with that. To the point where the term "Howler" is presented as some sort of Garou title for said bards. And, obviously, this was often the go-to Auspice for Fianna back in the early days, similarly to how Ahroun were for Get of Fenris.

                                                            Anyway, this character was born to a small wolf pack in the eastern US. (Again, the idea that this must be the result of efforts by the Garou to preserve said breeding packs anywhere they can.) Far more interested in communicating via howls than their pack mates, their sire eventually returned from a long absence to reveal that they were a werewolf and took them off to become one of the Fianna. Now they wander the world, alone or with a pack, and eagerly howl their new discoveries to the Moon. A "happy go lucky" type, they seem to enjoy entering Homid form for the novelty of a new way of communicating.

                                                            And that's about it. There's not a whole lot here. A vast amount of the word count seems to be eaten up with regurgitating basic info about what Fianna and Galliards are rather than who this character is and what they do.

                                                            The image is a wolf in mid howl. It's a fine image. Just sort of unremarkable.

                                                            For Attributes, Physicals are secondary, with 2/3/3. Socials are primary, naturally, with 4/3/3. And Mental is tertiary, with 2/2/2. I don't really have anything to say about this. It's fine. Nothing stands out, really.

                                                            For Abilities, Talents are secondary. We have 2 each in Empathy and Primal Urge, and 1 each in Alertness, Athletics, Brawl, Dodge, and Expression. Skills are primary, with 3 each in Animal Ken, Performance, and Stealth, and 2 each in Leadership and Survival. Knowledge is tertiary, with a 2 in Enigmas and 1 each in Investigation, Occult, and Rituals.
                                                            I feel he should have more points in Expression, even before freebies come in to play. I'm not sure if swapping Talents and Skills in regards to primary and secondary would help or not.

                                                            For Backgrounds, we have 1 each in Allies, Kinfolk, Past Life, and Pure Breed. The write up explains the Ally as a fellow Galliard, likely a Homid from either their own tribe or another. I'm not sure why so many Allies in this book are just another Garou of the same Auspice. The Kinfolk are described as being of Irish decent. This book just seems to assume that any Lupus character's Kinfolk must be humans rather than wolves. I think this is one of those things where the earlier material was still trying to find its footing in certain areas. I think this could benefit from some focus. Perhaps a few dots of Kinfolk to represent their former pack. And the rest in either Past Life or Pure Breed.

                                                            The defaults for the remaining starts are Rage 4, Gnosis 5, and Willpower 2. This was back when the Fianna got really hamstrung on WP. Thankfully 2nd edition would raise this to 3. Even then, the amount of Rage over WP is going to make interaction with humans difficult. Obviously, you'd probably want to throw a few freebies into the WP stat.

                                                            I'm not sure how I feel about this character. Possibly because, like I said, there's not a lot here. On the one hand, it's not a bad basic start of a concept. A Lupus who is really fascinated with verbal communication, both their own original howling and the new forms opened up via shapeshifting, is a potentially interesting idea. But I think this needs to be fleshed out a lot more. Spend less time repeating basic info from the core book's character creation chapter, and more talking about how they've tried to learn about and communicate with humans.
                                                            I'm not sure how well this works as a Lover of the Ways, but like I said, this was back when Galliards seem to focus entirely on the Performance skill and not a lot else. I think if it talked about trying to express the feelings of wolves and a love of nature to humans and Homids, it would help. So, this character isn't terrible. But there doesn't seem to be enough meat on the bones to be good either.
