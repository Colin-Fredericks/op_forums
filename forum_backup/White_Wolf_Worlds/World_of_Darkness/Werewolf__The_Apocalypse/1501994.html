    running a werewolf the apocalypse game set after the apocalypse. need ideas

    02-02-2023, 10:29 PM
    OK so the idea is simple, the wyrm came to earth and actually was the red star (or the red star was a portal to Malfeas). This happened around 2000 to 2010. After it happened the forces of Gaia were able to defeat it and basically drove it off at great cost, over 90% of all the shifters (including Garou) as well as animals and humans were destroyed and the wyrm was only temporary defeated and driven back in return for the lives of the vast majority of every living thing that existed, including many incarna who were slain permanently.

    Now it is over 100 years later and society has moved into a neo-mediaeval era though there is technology in some isolated regions, thanks largely and part to vampires who still remember how to build stuff with simple materials, some of the leeches survived, and the wyrm has once again begun to encroach on the world and the present era is new dark age that is rising up to become a new civilization.

    The reason I'm doing this is because my players have no interest in doing this game in a historical era where they have to know any history and thus prefer that we are free to make up a new dark age style society since they don't want to play modern (too many limitations on behaviour around breaking the veil).

    Can anyone help me out with some ideas?

                              I don't agree and I'm going in a completely different direction here. Basically once 90% of the population was wiped out in the initial first decade of conflict, a lot more people died of starvation. With the world totally warped and devastated the option to simply rebuild using knowledge out of books was just not there, the fact is there is not much of an opportunity to basically have the such advances in technology as that would require a huge number of people actually engaging in industrialization and suitable education and that's just not an opportunity for the survivors.

                              The few pockets of civilization that survived were forced to go all the way back to meagre subsistence farmeing and totally dependended upon subsistence farming for food, as a result, once we run out of food we all die, once there is no supply chain we run out of food. That means everybody has to rely on making their own food and that becomes top priority number one as well as getting shelter. As such, the few pockets of civilization that exist are initially going to be entirely dependent on their ability to perform subsistence farming as there may be no trade at all and people are just as likely to kill you and take your stuff as they are to deal with your fairly.

                              Furthermore, a lot of advance knowledge is absolutely useless in the hands of a single individual or even a group unless they have the formative knowledge to engage in the things those books cover (like engineering, metallurgy, medicine advanced beyond first aid). I've looked over books on chemistry metallurgy biology etc., they won't help me to build a factory and if we experience a catastrophic radical apocalypse over a very short period of time, my most pressing concern will be to get water, food and shelter so I live out the day or the week.

                              Lastly, the presence of supernatural monsters roaming the planet openly, doesn't exactly encourage a very high likelihood of humans forming a cooperative educated and industrialized society with the necessary transportation and telecommunications required to support such a thing. No I could see many vampire run communes been quite successful in all of the aforementioned arenas, whether or not they would share their knowledge and power is another thing entirely though.

                              That being said I really do want to point out that this is just my opinion and I am not an arrogant jerk, but I am willing to acknowledge that you might be right and I could be wrong, but I tend to think that an apocalypse would basically be a catastrophe and once the food chain runs out we are all going to die very quickly and that rebuilding society could easily take a century and that provides that we even have enough literature that didn't get used as kindling and toilet paper

                              Oh and PS, I totally agree about fallout three and four. The fact that they even have old surviving buildings 200 years after the fall and that they are filled with Blamko macaroni and cheese, like the cardboard box its found in would not have dissolved after two centuries, strikes me as ridiculous. The fact that there are rolls of duct tape just laying around and that buildings are still even there aside from the rarest and most resilient strikes me as total bullshit. Even if you go with the RIFTS model where everything is mega damage capacity, you are still looking at a world where virtually everything that isn't MDC would be long gone and we either would have rebuilt or we would be in the Neo medieval.

                                  Another possible source of inspiration is the Hellboy comics, particularly the BPRD series of comics titled Hell on Earth, which is basically the Apocalypse and the end of the world. There were a lot of aspects of it that matched my own ideas of what the Apocalypse would be like (I admit that the scenarios in the Apocalypse sourcebook were far too tame for me. I envisioned a true "The stars are right" scenario of the Great Old Ones waking up again. The scenarios in that book seemed far too focused and too timid.). I think it did a good job of showing how modern human civilization would handle such an event, and how a "post-human" age of the world was being formed. Very terrifying. And while it may give little guidance to what a future post-Apocalypse world might look like, there is a lot you can pull from it as events that happened in your version of the Apocalypse and determine what the lasting after effects would be in your new setting.

                                  One aspect from that which I brought into my chronicles is that PENTEX is like the comic's Zinco Corporation. PENTEX became less about a group of people wanting to bring about the Apocalypse, and instead people who decided it was inevitable and were using it to allow them to survive it in the best possible manner. So you might want to consider some part of human industrial/technological civilization has survived - but it's basically the successor to PENTEX, and any humans in those areas have made their arrangements with what forces of the Wyrm they could and adapted accordingly. They could be a very sick and twisted remnant of humanity (or post-humanity) at odds with the other human survivors.
