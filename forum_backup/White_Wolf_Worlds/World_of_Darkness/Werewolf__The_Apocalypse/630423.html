    NIghtshade / Veil - How would you rule it works....

    08-17-2015, 07:48 PM
    So, situation is this. A PC activated a Nightshade talen, tuning himself into shadow and therefore virtually invisible in his dark small room. Problem is, in a few seconds SWAT will break open the door and search the room.

    What do they see in the room once it is illuminated by lights they have under the barrels of their guns?
    Does the shadow of the talen remain, even in direct beam of the flash-light? If so, the policemen will see something inexplicable/supernatural.
    What if the garou turned Crinos. Would his shadowy figure cause Delirium, even is not completely visible?
    Or will the talen effect cease when in bright light, for the flash-light while still mundane is a strong source of illumination causing the essence of shadow to vanish?
    What about the PC himself, can he end the 1h effect prematurely ?

    I'd love to read your opinions on the matter.
    As a secondary information, in the room from where the SWAT is approaching, other policemen already have two other members of the pack in homid form held at gun point.

          I mean it is a general problem, with perception rolls, in my opinion. A gift like "Blur of the milky eye" reduced difficulty of Stealth checks, but if you want to sneak up on a group standing in an empty parking lot in broad daylight, the difficulty even with such a reduction would be high since there is no cover. The difficulty to see you would be as low as the rules permit. A gift like "Invisibility" on the other hand would work, for one is truly invisible. Nightshade however is unclear on the mechanics. It states one is turned into shadow (which I guess doesn't mean incorporeal nor 2-dimensional, for it would say so if it was so). But also states a person is all but invisible, which is kind of strange - since there are circumstances a shadow is pretty clearly visible - usually if there is a bright light around. And the flash-lights of the Policemen certainly count. And I don't think Nightshade is supposed to be a D&D Shadowdancers "hide in plain sight" that allows to use stealth as long as shadow is in close proximity.

                          Seems your post was cut short....

                          "Sonder" means special, as does "Spezial". The name of german SWAT-like teams are SEK, which can mean Sondereinsatzkommando or Spezialeinsatzkommando. It's basically the same thing, but since each state can name their stuff like they want....

                          The "Bundesgrenzschutz" (Federal Border Guard) was once basically that, the guards at the border. But with the EU's "Schengen"-Treaty there aren't usually any border guarding any more (only on the border to Switzerland). So, the Federal Government made the Federal Border Guard their own police force, which was kind of a disputed move, since the Federal government didn't have a police before and internal security is a state affair. Well, the federal ministry of the interior hadn't, the ministry for finance already had and still has their own police for custom enforcement, smuggling, product piracy, unregistered employment and all other forms of tax evasion.
                          Well, around the same time, the Bundesbahn (Federal monopol railway) was privatised, so their own police, the Bahnpolizei, was incorporated into the Federal police aswell. But the GSG9 retained their old name, from their Bundesgrenzschutz times -> Grenzschutzgruppe, which is "Border Protection Group" - the S not meaning "Sonder" or "Special".


                          But back to the topic. The whole affair became kind of mood, since the PC managed to evade the cops. His alpha used Jam Technology and the few turns in total darkness were enough to flee the room.
