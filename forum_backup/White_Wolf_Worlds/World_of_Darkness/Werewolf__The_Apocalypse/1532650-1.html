        Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post

        Honestly, in terms of a writing analysis, this is interesting. Because it is very clear they tried to do the V5 "Gehenna came and went and now the problems are mundane" thing and tried as hard as they could to remove any and all reasons for the PCs to take action beyond their own backyard.
        Not only that but Gehenna-as-a-cycle is a bit in V5 that marks it as a continuation, something added to the canon of what came before. There's no reason to step things up similar to this in W5... Maybe this Age of Apocalypse is a game of telephone as concepts like that were first brought up by the By Nights Studio version of a new WtA-edition and by the time things got to the version the Paradox in-house team worked on, that idea just became a thoughtless auto-include, like everybody was acting like "Yeah, of course, the new WtA-edition takes place during the Age of Apocalypse. It has always been thus.". And so nobody even questioned this part or why it is part of the new edition once we got to the people who worked on the version that was released.

        Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
        Like, the reason why the apocalypse is happening is not because they wanted to address it. It's like that because they wanted there to be absolutely no chance to affect things in the larger scale. The same reason the Nation was shattered, it was too global and too impactful.

        This is basically dooming the world, because doing something would matter too much. And boy what a message THAT is XD
        Yeah, that's why I don't like the comparison of Garou in W5 to activists. Because that would make W5 a game about how environmental activism is pointless. The message about the effectiveness of Garou as it's envisioned in W5 is made clear on pg 212 as the book talks about the distinction between systemic (for example, climate change) and local (for example, a pack of BSD) antagonists: "The other axis is that of systemic / individual. Systemic antagonists are large, overarching entities or ideals. Systemic antagonists are massive and often decentralized and, in general, cannot be directly defeated. They can certainly face setbacks at the hands of the characters, but total defeat is almost never an option. Most systemic antagonists have local proxies that can be defeated, thereby hurting the global antagonist’s plans and relieving tension — the “little victories” that mean so much in Werewolf, because they address the player characters’ immediate goals."

        "Werewolf - the Apocalypse: The game about little victories". Wow, what a tagline...

                                                            Which, of course, comes to two rebuttals:

                                                            1. That doesn't make a very fun game about being a magic furry Klingon

                                                            2. Plenty of RL activists do, in fact, consider this to be the absolute wrong attitude to have.
                                                            And I think it says something that in WtA the ones who do the polluting and destroy the environment are also servants of the Wyrm. And the game shouldn't be about: "Please be nicer to the servants of the Wyrm.". Just because you'd rather pat a Fomor on the head than fight him doesn't mean the Fomor will suddenly do nice things. WtA is heightening the animosity and danger of polluters by associating them with the dangerous spiritual entity of the Wyrm. And so there's a heightening to a similar degree in what Garou are as the opposing force and what they do. Putting the restrictions you'd like to put on IRL-activists on Garou is silly therefore. Garou aren't a one-to-one analogue to IRL-activists, the same way the IRL-polluters aren't mutated freaks with spirit-powers.

                                                            In the case of W5, this brings up two problems where it shoots itself in the foot:
                                                            1. If collective action is supposed to be favored then why is a key-part of W5's setting about Garou failing to do that and mocking that attempt? The Garou Nation is described as a failure and a bad thing (as we can see in the described indiscriminate rejection of the Litany). W5 doesn't think highly of the Garou's attempt at collective action and therefore mocks the idea of collective action being pursued within the game.
                                                            2. Rejecting radical action categorically is a pro-status-quo-position. And that just gets you into a position where you end up defending environmental destruction and pollution. Somebody will dump industrial waste into a river while you're arguing it's more important to scold the resistance to that for being too harsh and radical.
