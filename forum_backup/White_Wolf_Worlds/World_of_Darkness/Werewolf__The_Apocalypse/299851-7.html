    Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
    So, on one hand, Werewolf is all about playing an eco-fascist, and I think it's fun to try to inflate the body count with a misanthropic extremist. That gift of falsifying the cause of death is delightful.

    On the otherhand, and this I think is the purposeful irony of the tribe; just killing extra humans isn't actually productive. In some ways it's counter productive. If you want to destroy humanity, you either need to commit to a genocidal campaign (very costly), or you need to commit to getting humans to kill eachother, which you can't really do when you've dedicated your tribe into shutting out human influences and don't understand their triggers.

    How easy would it be for red talons to ignite, say, a race war? How easy would it be to escalate a the Goerge Floyd riots? You hang a few blacks, shoot a few cops, butcher a few white supremacists... wolla, America in flames with little chance of nukes. But the Red Talons don't understand how to do that because they don't really see what's going on and suggesting the idea would label you as too close to the humans/weaver. The Ratkin probably don't want to do that (The Rodens are likely as ignorant as the Tallons, and the Homids would fear the impact on their kinfolk) (as for BSD, I generally consider the Smart ones as Lazy, and the active ones as stupid, so they're not gonna go with such a plan. It might be counter to their long run interests )

    False flag attacks are for monkeys, not noble wolves. Even if they'd serve the RT's interests a lot better. I really like treading the RT line of 'the good wyrm' and 'the bad wyrm' for all their atrocities. But, unfortunately, the RT's lack of human understanding limits them to individually murdering more people when they have an excuse.
    This has been an interesting thread to follow; WtA isn't my area of expertise, but I'm always looking to learn in order to satisfy my crossover-addiction.

    I feel like MyWifeIsScary's post flicked on a bit of a light-bulb in my head, maybe in connection to the above remarks about Red Talons potentially being an "assassin" Tribe. See, it made me consider parallels with the Euthanatos Tradition of MtAs. Like the Red Talons, the Euthanatos tend to treat killing things like a hammer and tend to treat most problems like a nail. An older member of the Tradition, Senex, is said to be bothered by how his peers have become too preoccupied with violence/destruction as a solution, rather than pursuing alternatives.

    A possible way to 'rehabilitate' the Red Talons a bit is to have their modern Tribal theme of "kill the tainted monkeys" be a degradation/corruption of their original purpose. The Red Talons are not the Tribe of hating humans; the Red Talons are the Tribe of preserving lupus Kinfolk and keeping the "wolf perspective" represented within the broader culture of the Garou Nation. The decision to accept lupus Kinfolk and Garou exclusively was, originally, not about seeing humans as inferior, but instead making sure that one Tribe always viewed wolf Kin as a priority. Red Talons may have always been primitives who rejected tools and the trappings of 'civilization,' but they may have once been able to tolerate and understand it enough to go among humans and influence them in less violent ways, teaching them to appreciate the perspectives and strengths of wolves, working as a mediator between the two breeds the way shamans mediate between the material and spirit worlds.

    The modern Tribe's active hatred of humans, instead of being the 'natural state' of the Tribe, is more of a response to how history has turned out, an overreaction, and a mistaking of what was supposed to be merely one means (killing humans to defend wolves, when necessary) as instead being the end-unto-itself (kill all the humans, because everything bad is their fault). Given the relationship between humans and the decline of the wolf population, a Tribe that views protecting wolves as their sacred duty would likely be inclined to develop poor views of humanity. Of course, that would likely be as much a failure on their part, and the Garou as a whole, as it is humanity's, but it makes their perspective more relatable, and provides an idea of what a more moderate Red Talon might look like.


    Regarding the previous debate about whether or not Red Talons especially represent the Wyld, and whether or not humanity's "strengths" are "natural," I would like to quickly point out that while it's fair to say that human use of tools and social organization is indeed "natural," that's not the same as saying it is "of the Wyld." The comparison to ants makes it especially clear: ants are of the Weaver, not the Wyld. That being said, I do agree with the idea that the concept of the Wyld does seem to go beyond "nature." If we want to equate the Red Talon perspective with "nature," perhaps their views (originally, at least) should not be regarded as closest to the Wyld, but instead to Gaia as embodying the balance of the "natural cycle" of birth, life, and death?

                                                Actually animals cause other animals to go extinct all the time. Usually that is the case of invasive species that humans brought with them, but in other cases when an animal population are able to travel to a new land itself, the same result occurs. Cats, for example, have wiped out many species of birds and small animals particularly on islands and Australia. Discarded Burmese Pythons are doing the same in the Everglades.

                                                This generally occurs when a predator is introduced that native animals didn't evolve with, and they lack the natural defenses. But sometimes it's a non-predator that is simply better suited for survival in that environment than the native animals, and they outcompete them for those resources and drive them out which leads to population decline, then collapse, and then extinction. Sometimes this is due to natural climate change, and the environment has changed sufficient enough for animal populations to migrate. The last is probably the reason for the earliest extinctions in the Quaternary Extinction Event (although extinctions that occurred later are likely due to humans).

                                                That doesn't change the fact that humans, because of our success in living in many environments and changing habitats, are responsible for a vastly accelerated extinction rate. But before humans, there was still a normal extinction rate, and other animals are a part of that cause.

                                                    Originally posted by Justycar View Post
                                                    Animals kill to eat, to reproduce or to protect their territory or children. But animals do not wipe out an entire race or species as humans do (buffalo, for example).
                                                    OK, getting away from the term genocide, since that's a fairly loaded word to try to apply to non-humans based on our linguistic biases, there's a more useful term to use: surplus killing.

                                                    It's a pretty easy to understand phrase. Surplus killing is when an animal kills another animal in excess (or surplus since it started focused on predator-prey killing) of their survival needs. Humans are, obviously, notorious surplus killers. We do it to each other and other animals all the time.

                                                    However, surplus killing is exceedingly common in nature. The evolutionary history of the behavior is relatively safe to assume: animals that evolve to kill more than they need are more likely to have killed enough if times get tough, while animals that kill just enough to survive are constantly at risk of not having killed enough to survive (not just eat, territorial competition, mating competition, etc.).

                                                    For example, spotted hyenas murder lion cubs and adolescents whenever they have a chance. There's no consideration for the lion pride's health, or sharing resources. The hyenas might not be trying to wipe lions out, but they'll kill lions as hard as possible to ensure lions pose as little of a threat as possible even to the point of wiping out a pride if they can. Every dead lion is one less lion the hyenas have to compete with during difficult hunting seasons, so hyenas proactively kill lions even when there isn't direct competition. Lions, of course, kill back too.

                                                    Dolphins are one of the most notoriously murder happy species besides humans because they kill animals for no reason we can actually explain. Besides being highly violent creatures in their internal socialization, dolphins have been repeatedly documented killing other species that aren't threats to them; no food competition, no territoriality, no physical threat to the dolphins. Since they mostly seem to target smaller porpoises, one of the best guesses besides "they're just violent assholes," is that they're practicing their murder skills for killing the young of rival dolphins. Running around murdering smaller animals like that is something humans associate with violent sociopaths.

                                                    And yes, wolves are well know surplus killers too.

                                                        There's this sort of pervasive belief among people, that nature is harmonious and balanced. It emphatically is not. It's a whole bunch of organisms trying to maximize their survival through whatever mechanisms available to them, regardless of context. What we see in a stable ecosystem is a hard-fought equilibrium, born of various factors that got good enough at working around each other that nothing goes extinct. This doesn't mean those organisms won't wreck that ecosystem the moment the situation changes.

                                                        Just as rabbits will literally fuck their environment to death, regardless of utility, a predator or even just particularly deadly prey animal would destroy an entire species given the chance. They follow their instincts, regardless of utility.

                                                            One of the best parts of Red Talon writing for me, and one that is important in seeing the tribe as more complex, is that they are very much architects of their own misery.

                                                            The Red Talons could have affected the Litany, they could have joined the discussion. But they refused because they saw it as too human. When the Litany then forbid eating human flesh, they complained that they had to obey it and developed rites to cheat it.

                                                            Then there is the fact that some Red Talons have chosen to become African Cape Dog or Dingo to aid Gaia, while others complain about those garou not being 'true' Talons. The tribe has an interesting take on adaptation vs tradition. In that, some Talons adapt easily without sacrificing their morals, while others are so adamant that they live by the old ways as to harm their chances of survival.

                                                            In that, Red Talons are actually very human. It was their refusal to adapt and change that made them such an isolated tribe, it was their perception of the world that drives them to defy Litany out of sheer stubbornness.
