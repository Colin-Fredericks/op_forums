    Hmmm.. the recently (as in last year 2021 recent) canceled and completely forgotten about Bloodlines 2 begs to differ.

    And Werewolf Earthblood was not received well at, with middling-to-negative reviews. It also generally was not marketed well, so I doubt it had a wide reach (spoiler: the same thing is going to happen to its tabletop counterpart, for reasons that I broached in the previous parts of the thread).

    Wraith Afterlife had some positive reviews floating around, but since it's a VR game, it's a niche-within-a-niche in a specific gameline that was the most niche of the "mainline" WoD games. I doubt that sold particularly high.

    Most of the Vampire stuff as of now is relegated to Choose-Your-Own-Adventure style games, which I doubt any of them sell remotely well at all. They had released an action battle royale for Vampire called Bloodhunt, but as of now it's just full of dead servers and long queue times. It also has the "free-to-play but we have a bunch of microtransaction goodies you can peruse in-game" scheme.

    Problem with that, as with all free-to-play games, is that players can just play the free content as much as they want; and when they feel that the free content allotted is running thin and they keep getting bombarded with non-subtle nudges to begin buying stuff, they'll just stop playing and move on to either non-free-to-play games that are worth their money, or play other free-to-play games that have better quality.

    Overall, Bloodhunt got overshadowed by an indie developed game called V-Rising.. which is much better Vampire multiplayer online experience, both in reviews and how much it probably sold. It also is a survival diablo-like sandbox game, which definitely has more replayability than a pure action battle royale game.

    So I don't know where you're getting that the "video game" side of WoD is doing "fine" right about now.

        Originally posted by thebiglarpnerd View Post
        it sells well because its in more places than just physical shops
        Just to clarify something from my post on this:

        This is what the ICv2 rankings try (in a very flawed way) to quantify.

        ICv2 asks retailers (aka those physical shops) how various products are selling. If WoD5 is not regularly on the ICv2 lists, it means it's not selling that well in physical shops. ICv2 methods are flawed as fuck, but they're only organization that even tries to gather data on this that doesn't come from trusting companies to tell you honestly what their sales are like.

        Now, WoD5 might be hitting Paradox's internal targets just fine. I have no idea, because I have no idea what those numbers are. But the physical shops are not reporting WoD5 as a major seller for them.

        While we have a very incomplete picture of things, ever public metric we have says the same thing: V5 sold well at jump, and sales for the WoD5 books since then are steadily decreasing across multiple retail options. Physical sales aren't there. DTRPG sales aren't there. Props for the number crunching on Amazon showing sales are slumping there too. So we have a pile of numbers that all say sales for WoD5 are not doing well (and the loss leader argument does not work if there's no profitable product coming that relies on the loss leader, the supplements aren't raking in the sales, and the video games that don't need TTRPG sales in the first place aren't either).

        There is nothing on the other side that points to the WoD IP under Paradox doing well financially. Just a giant hole of a lack of information that could have positive numbers in it. Or even worse ones.
