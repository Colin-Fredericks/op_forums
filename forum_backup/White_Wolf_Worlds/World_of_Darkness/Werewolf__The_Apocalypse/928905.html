    I need recommendations on all things WtA

    07-09-2016, 05:24 PM
    Hello there,

    So I discovered Werewolf the Apocalypse almost a year ago by collecting the Rage cards in my local comic book shop. I only very recently found out what it was, a role-playing game, and I'm completely smitten on the concept and lore. I don't have many friends that are interested in playing the game, but I don't care I really just want to start reading and collecting the books. So I ask the veterans here: Which are your favorite novels and/or resource books? Your least favorites? Which ones should I avoid and why?

    So far, my favorite of the novels is Breathe Deeply, but I haven't read any of the World of Darkness books or the "When Will you Rage?" analogies. My favorite resource books are "Book of the Weaver" and "Hengeykai: Shapeshifters of the East". I haven't read a least favorite yet.

    Bonus question:
    My favorite concept in all of WtA are the Skin Dancers what would you say is the best resource for them?

    Thanks for your time.

              One thing you have to remember with WTA is that Western werewolves are behind the times when compared to African or Asian werewolves. The latter groups have united with the other Fera (shapeshifters) to create a united front against the Wyrm. The Western werewolves still think that they can do it on their own...

              Of the Fera, Ratkin have the most potential for weak groups while Mokole have the most potential for strong groups. A Gnosis 10 Mokole can create a rather frightening hybrid form that can resemble Godzilla if they want, while the Ratkin swarm (there are potentially millions of Ratkin, it just depends on how many Kinfolk the Ratkin are willing to expose to the Birthing Plague).

                  The W20 line as a whole has been quite solid. I recommend getting W20 Rage Across the World. It covers Septs and caerns and what its like within them as well as a variety of locations across the world and number of Totems, Gifts and etc. W20 Umbra is a maybe as while you'll use spirits you may not use the Umbra as deeply and as thoroughly as depicted in the book. Changing Breeds is likewise a maybe. If you like the Fera, the Beast Courts and their African counterparts, it's worth picking up. W20 Book of the Wyrm is another highly recommended book as it collects a lot of knowledge on the various Wyrm-aligned foes of the Garou from Banes and Formori to Pentex and the Black Spiral Dancers.

                  The White Howlers Tribe Book is something I really only recommend for fans of the Tribe or those wanting to complete their collection. Don't get me wrong, I like the book but it's very much a niche thing. It's set at the eve of the Tribe's fall, back in the Roman Empire and the conquest of Britain and unfortunately while the back of the book trumpets 'Ideas for using the White Howlers in a story set in the modern nights' it doesn't actually give much on what a modern day White Howler Tribe could look like. For something they list as a feature, they dedicate a page's worth of material at best.

                  The Skinner SAS is good and if you like the Skin Dancers, it features them and their founder, the infamous Samuel 'the Ashtray' Haight. Though its not something I'd use for starting characters and new players.

                  Of the W20 fiction, I have all three but have only read the Rites of Renown anthology. On a whole, more stories are good than bad and I absolutely love the story The Lost.

                  Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. They're indomitable. Indomitable.

                                                          I'm glad you had a chance to discover this amazing weird creation. You're discovering W:TA at an interesting time. As a longtime fan, here's some of my favorites and other recommendations:

                                                          Fiction: The tribe novel series is probably my all-time favorite fiction in W:TA, but it can be hard to find some of the books. The Time of Judgment tie-in novel The Last Battle takes place soon after the event of the tribe novel series, but can be read separately, is just as good as any novel in the series, and is still somewhat easy to find at used bookstores and online.

                                                          The other great ongoing fiction in W:TA is the saga of King Albrecht. The second edition main rulebook has a great short comic that shows how Albrecht formed a pack with Evan Heals-the-Past and Mari Cabrah, characters you're probably familiar with from Rage. His story is continued in the novel The Silver Crown, the Revised Silver Fangs Tribebook, and the aforementioned Last Battle. The anthology When Will You Rage has a few good stories, and the newer releases Rites of Renown and The Poison Tree are great.

                                                          Getting away from the fiction, some of my favorite sourcebooks in W:TA are a handful of older books that are usually pretty cheap and easy to find. Rite of Passage will seem a little dated, being one of the earliest releases for W:TA, but it has some decent art for the time, it walks you through a solid Rite of Passage story, and it introduces some elements that continue to be referenced in later sourcebooks. Rage Across Russia is a classic that explains huge metaplot elements concerning Russia, and Rage Across Appalachia is one of the most interesting and charming settings in the series.

                                                          If you have some money to spend, the Player's Guide to Garou, Hammer and Klaive, The Book of Auspices, and most of the Revised Tribebooks are either very useful, great reads, or both.

                                                          Happy hunting.
