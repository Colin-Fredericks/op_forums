    Query from a Casual - Why is the Garou Nation so much worse off?

    02-17-2026, 04:01 PM
    One complaint I'm seeing a lot of is that 5th Edition is much harder on the Garou and nothing but how much they suck.

    However, I remember 1st Edition Werewolf and early 2nd Edition. In fact, that's "my" W:TA because I didn't follow up on Revised and other works.

    The point was that the Garou had failed and the Apocalypse was imminent...because of all the reasons given in the 5th Edition book about them being too violent, unable to get over their differences tribally, and the Children of Gaia being ignored. It was why Evan-White-Boy-Saves-Us ( :kidding, kidding: ) existed as his whole thing. The Garou needed to make serious systemic change or everyone was screwed.

    I'm not a big fan of 5E's take but I am curious why this specific complaint is coming up because it seems like it was just following up from the original presentation that all of the War of Rage, Impergium, and other things had come to roost.

    Change or die and all that.

    I wonder if this is related to the editions I missed and the fact that 5E was going back to the original versions.

                  I think the biggest thing that gives W5 the vibe of being too hard on Garou in my opinion is the decision to nuke the Garou Nation. On one hand, it takes the air out of a lot of points of tension in the setting. Because what is even the point of bemoaning the flaws of the Litany? The Garou Nation is gone. Or conflicts between Septs, Tribes or Packs. It's less about striving to overcome and deal with the flaws and more just making the flaws the natural status quo. And on the other hand, despite this fallen and failed nature of the setting, it's just rehashing quite a lot of things from previous editions. It's assumed you play Gaia-loyal Garou in a Pack that might belong to a Sept where still the same old Moots happen and most of the time the goal is to fight the Wyrm. That's what makes it feel so much like this Frankenstein thing, I think.

                  The Garou Nation is gone and a failure but the game has done next to nothing to explore the notion of "So now what?". It just goes back a lot to old stuff strangely.

                  And as an aside, I think another issue is that with the new stuff W5 is doing, between W5, previous editions and WtF the W5 ideas are comparatively the least interesting ones. Like Kinfolk from previous editions, Wolfblooded from Forsaken and the Kin in W5. The last one is the blandest one of those. Or take Tribes. Even with the more lore-light approach of Forsaken, those Tribes are more interesting than what W5 has done with Tribes. Especially how Moonlit Path explored Tribal Patron Spirits. Now in W5, Tribes just revolve around the Patron Spirits looking for Garou with certain character traits and getting the Spirits favor comes with various demands and expectations. But that's pretty much it. I understand the idea of getting away from tying the Tribes to human ethnicity and culture but in the end it really has turned into more of a "throwing the baby out with the bathwater" situation.

                                                      Offering a good faith reading of the W5 metaplot progression, I think it suffers from two diametrically opposing approaches. One wants to serve as a continuation and progression of the setting as it previously existed and one wants to shake things up and go a radical new direction. I don’t have an issue with either option in theory and would be willing to judge the product of either avenue on its own merits but trying to do both at once is necessarily going to lead to, at minimum, a muddled and internally confused product.

                                                      Obviously a continuation of the previously established status quo was the safest option, that’s essentially what W20 was (alongside some experiments with optional metaplot progression and trying to create a final unified set of rules). But after you do that once with W20, why would you need to do that again? A new edition, to justify itself, would necessitate shaking things up either mechanically or narratively (probably both) but would risk alienating a section of fans.

                                                      IMO I don’t have an issue with ‘Gaia is on its deathbed and the Nation is shattered’ as the conceit for doing a Werewolf story but it would probably be better served by being a ‘Dark Mirrors’ style alternate setting. This would let the writers off the hook and give them permission to shake things up, slaughter sacred cows, and really present a radically different setting and mode of play. No guarantee that it would take with the majority of readers but it would probably get a fan base of its own at the very least.

                                                      (Like how in comics you can’t really mess around too much with a character’s mainline status quo but you can do whatever you want in an alternate universe book [not gonna make a judgement on if that’s actually good for comic books in the long term as a medium but it’s a thing because, on some level, it really does just work]).

                                                      tl;dr: It’s hard to simultaneously satisfy corporate mandates and the tendency towards franchise conservatism while also telling a radically different and new story, so if you’re in that position you should probably just decide to do one or the other and then succeed or fail on the merits of the product and it’s reception
