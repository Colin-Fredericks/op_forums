            Regarding cultural appropriation and playing characters outside of your own ethnic group, I think there are a couple of points that need to be considered. And I definitely think that the conversation needs to be prefaced with the fact that this is a complicated subject, that I really think tends to work best when approached on a case-by-case basis rather than trying to come up with sweeping principles you can apply without regard to context.

            I think the first issue is to discuss what is meant by the term "cultural appropriation." I've seen it used in two or three different ways, with distinctions that determine whether or not is an inherently negative phenomenon, versus a more neutral and context-dependent one. The first sense is one which uses "appropriation" rather aggressively, and is inherently negative. This is not about cultural sharing, but cultural theft and commodification. This is forcefully taking a piece of a culture you were never part of, twisting it and maiming it however you wish to make it into what you want, and then maybe throwing a price-tag onto it at the end. It's inherently disrespectful. And it becomes even worse when the things you start taking without permission are sacred (or profane) to the people you took it from.

            The second usage of the term I've encountered uses "cultural appropriation" simply to refer to the exchange of cultural ideas and values in any context, and thus whether or not it is positive or negative depends on that context. The sort of stuff discussed above is still wrong, obviously, but it leaves room for cultural exchange that can be regarded as more positive, or at least neutral. But even that gets messy sometimes; pineapple on pizza has actually become a topic of international diplomacy. So, to move this to the context of W5, whether or not it gets to be the good kind of cultural appropriation or not depends on whether or not that sharing is consensual and respectful. And thus far, that does not look to be the case.

            It's worth noting that this is one of the many things where it's not good to become puritanical. Earlier comments in the thread have hit on how the earlier editions of WoD where a combination of positive visibility for certain aspects of Indigenous culture and identity, while also being quite disrespectful and ham-fisted in many other ways at the same time. And while those earlier editions absolutely could have been better, they have had a positive influence on many people as well. There are few things that are purely good or bad, so constructive conversation focuses on how to make things better. But part of making that better has to be actually letting the people whose ideas you're copying have their say, not talking over them and insisting on your interpretation of their lives.

            For the second point that I want to discuss, I'm going to borrow a convention used earlier in the thread when discussing mature vs. "mature" gaming.

            Are you going to play a Native American / Italian? Or are you going to play a "Native American" / "Italian"?

            Barring the exceptional cases of sacred/profane matters, I don't think that people from a given cultural group, or some other group-identity like sexuality or gender, is going to be opposed to a roleplayer making and playing a character who shares some aspect of their identity, as long as that character engages with that culture or identity in a way that is thoughtful and respectful. In fact, I think that many would probably appreciate someone who would take them time to learn their history, study their culture, and make a genuine effort to understand them so that they could play the role well. I think most people want to be understood, as long as you aren't forcing your way into things they consider personal or private.

            But if you're just going to play as a bundle of offensive stereotypes that you saw in a 40-year-old movie that you laugh at without really thinking about too deeply, I don't think it's at all surprising that people are going to call that out as dumb and trashy behaviour. It's intellectually lazy, and it contributes to the continuation of stereotypes that can destroy the lives of real people. And, once again leaning on the wisdom of previous posters whose names I can't be bothered to dig out from somewhere in 10 previous pages, this is a complicated issue with a lot of potential for real-world harm that the people making the game simply don't have a lot of control over; they can't force a gaming table to insist on not allowing racist stereotypes or not allowing in-game racism to serve as a cover for out-of-game racism. So they need to really focus on making sure that they get it right with the parts they can control.

            That was a lot of rambling, so I'm going to try and boil it down a bit:

            1) The World of Darkness unintentionally took on a super-complicated bag that many other TTRPGs manage to avoid (somewhat) when it decided to exist in a reflection of our real world, with the real people in it. I don't necessarily think that was a mistake, but it is a big responsibility.

            2) To all the folks getting outraged at the idea that you're not allowed to play X because you are Y: I don't think that any reasonable person is saying that. They are saying that you need to think about context, and you need to take it seriously, because you're not just playing with Tolkien-expies anymore. Do It Right, or Don't Do It. And some times, Do It Right means Don't Do It.


            P.S. I couldn't find anywhere to fit it easily into my larger argument, but I do also want to mention that one of the things that can make cultural appropriation that could otherwise be neutral into a negative is when the group taking the culture is allowed to profit or enjoy that culture in a way the original group are not. For example, one of the reasons why cultural appropriation of Black hairstyles is such an issue in the US is because there is evidence that Black people with those hairstyles experience discrimination in the workplace while White people are more likely to get a pass, or even praised for it. Or when Elvis became a huge hit because he was a White performer who could perform a style of music that was popular but, until then, had been dominated (and originated) by Black performers in a still-very-openly-racist America.

                I'm Black (with a bit of Scots-Irish and Romani in there from my Dad's side) and I've played lots and lots of characters who were White, Japanese, Korean, Brazilian, and Mexican.

                I also happen to be queer (but tbh, I dislike that term), poor, part of a religious minority, and disabled. And yet I've played straight characters, wealthy characters, Christian characters, and all of my characters were of able body and mind.

                I've also played as women despite being a man but tbh, I was assigned female at birth, so that's a bit more of a complicated topic.

                I honestly don't give a flying fuck if I'm playing a "stereotype" or indulging in so-called "cultural appropriation" and I don't care if anyone at my table does either, because I know it's fictional and so does everyone else at my game table.

                As long as it stays at the game table and within the realm of fiction, I'm of the view that it doesn't matter if something is "problematic" so long as the players and the Storyteller both know the difference between fiction and reality.

                The World of Darkness is not real and it is not the real world. It may have some superficial similarities to the real world but it is a very stylized and over-the-top fictionalized dark fantasy version of the heavily world filtered through gothic romanticism and pop culture aesthetics, which for better or worse, have roots in so-called "problematic" tropes. Those "problematic" works of fiction and art also happen to be some of the most enjoyable works of media of all time and I find them far more preferable to the sanitized media of today, despite being a marginalized person myself.

                Who cares if something in a FICTIONAL game is racist, sexist, or whatever? So long as the person playing said character is not actively bigoted themselves and knows the difference between fiction and reality, I don't care.

                Even if the artist themselves had bigoted views or has done problematic things in real life, I still enjoy their works anyway as is. I love H.P Lovecraft's short stories, for example. Most of these people are long dead anyway.

                I'm just a trans guy and gaming fan, unabashed hedonist and unusually upbeat nihilist, fairly laid back. He/Him.

                If everything is political, then nothing is!
