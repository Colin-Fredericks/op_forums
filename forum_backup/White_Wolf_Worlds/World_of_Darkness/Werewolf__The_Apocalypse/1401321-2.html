That vampires have to make eye contact is not the same that the victim can just choose to ignore it because he can moves his eyes later. Otherwise everyone could defeat Dominate quite easily. Once the vampire makes his Dominate roll, the victim is dominated (besides any normal Dominate rules, and special exceptions because of powers). Breaking eye contact after being dominated is not one of the exceptions. That is just silly.

        And of course, purposefully going into Frenzy as an escape mechanism creates problems of its own. Doesn't mean it's not an option, but there could be some costs to it. And as an ST, if the Dominate was done subtly and the player didn't realize it was happening, I wouldn't allow him to roll after I informed him. I would just do it if the PC realized it at the time it was happening. Not every encounter between Garou and Vampires happen when the two are obviously in conflict, and both sides know what the others are. Not every tactic is a combat tactic.

        presence can also be resisted with willpower
        Which is the same for everyone else. Nothing special for Garou.

        blood bond is useless,
        you can just spend a willpower point to succeed on the Gnosis roll,
        and even if you fail the roll, it's just a level one blood bond,
        getting a full bond is unrealistic outside of very niche cases
        I'm sure that there is a crossover rule somewhere that rules this, but it's contradicted within the game multiple times. So it's one of those things that an ST must decide what to do.

        In Berlin By Night, the Gangrel Daryl Lutz has blood bonded several Garou. I think it's a crap sourcebook, and there are definitely things in it I ignore (like vampire snakes), but there it is. Nor is it a case that this is simply Vampire showing off. It is supported in Werewolf too. In Rage Across Appalachia, the Garou Lisle Eyes-of-the-Forest-and-Knower-of-Secrets has a past where she was once blood bound to a vampire. The Garou only became free when she managed to suppress the blood bond barely enough (because of cruel mistreatment, something standard in Vampire) to explain her service to the vampire to another Garou, and the Garou lead his pack to destroy the vampire and break the bond. And in Rage Across Australia, it is implied - though not stated - that the Gangrel vampire Diana Wentworth has blood bonded the Silver Fang Darius Winchester (although this is clearly a reciprocal love affair). The word "infatuation" is used which sounds like a "tell" to me. Even if not, it is an implied threat since we're told she's even contemplating embracing him. In Under a Blood Red Moon, while it doesn't have vampires blood bonding Garou, it does have an optional scene where it is possible that Gulfora the succubus can make a Garou her thrall in a similar manner as a blood bond.

        Those sourcebooks are dated from 1993, 1994, and 1995. I suspect any contradictory rules occurred in the Revised era when the developers of each game line were in a pissing contest and started banning crossover elements and coming up with rules on why their splat was superior.

        Whether or not it is realistic for a vampire to blood bond Garou is different than saying Garou is immune to the blood bond. An ST or PC can always figure out a way to do it if the circumstances are right. If you want to make that a House Rule, go ahead. But canon has examples that show it's been done. If you want to excise those portions of canon, that's fine to do. I do it myself with a lot of things I find stupid. I don't find this to be one of them. But everyone's tastes are different.

                    Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                    While I agree that Obfuscate does not extend into the Penumbra, I think there is a caveat.

                    Persons in the Realm don't normally show up in the Penumbra. Only a few rare "spiritually advanced" individuals do so. So Garou can't normally see vampires in the Penumbra anyway. (It isn't like the Shadowlands where Wraiths do see the real world around them and are affected by the Rule of Ouch.) This only comes into play if the Garou decides to Peek from the Umbra into the Realm.

                    When a Garou peeks from the Penumbra into the Realm, they aren't looking in the Umbra. They are extending their senses into the Realm. They are no longer sensing what is in the Penumbra around them and are, in fact, completely oblivious in the Penumbra at that point. The Garou is incapable of giving or receiving communication, and it is said his enemies in the Umbra can act unopposed. That seems to me that the Garou - for any mental or sensing purposes - is no longer "there" even though his body is. It's like when someone astral projects and abandons his body - he doesn't know if anything happens to it. Peeking is much the same way.

                    Furthermore, their sensing of the Realm is much less than what it would normally be - what they sense is slightly distorted, monochromatic without color, and without fine details. Only scent is unimpeded.

                    I understand Obfuscate is actually a Mental discipline which effects people's minds, instead of actual camouflage or cover. So one can argue that the Garou - being in the Umbra - is unaffected. However, the vampire does not need to be actively imposing Obfuscate on known people. Once it is on, it is on and affects everyone - even if the vampire doesn't know that they are there, or if the person suddenly enters the area the obfuscated vampire is in. So it seems to me that once a Garou decides to peek into the Realm, there is an argument to be made his mind becomes affected because his mind and senses are present in the Realm (since they certainly aren't in the Penumbra).

                    I am sure there is some crossover ruling somewhere that says that Obfuscate doesn't work. But I question whether that should be the case. Often crossover rulings were unofficial, not extensively playtested, and not well thought out. Now that I've given this some thought, I really question if the logic holds up.

                    If my ST stated that, I wouldn't waste much time contesting the ruling. But I think I would at least bring it up for group discussion. But now I'm not sure what I would rule myself. I'm currently thinking I'd rule against it. I might allow a spirit Charm like Realm Sense "see" the obfuscated vampire because that works on different principles.
                    I totally agree and would handle it the same way if I was ST.
                    It wouldn't be RAW, though.
