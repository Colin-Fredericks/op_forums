    How do you challenge the Master of Challenges?

    02-02-2022, 01:45 PM
    Situation:

    The current Master of Challenges is a fostern Shadow Lord philodox. An adren Bone Gnawer philodox has just joined the sept, and wants to challenge to be the MoC. Seeing as the Bone Gnawer is the highest ranking philodox in the sept, they can't simply appeal to a higher ranked philodox to adjudicate.

    Question:

    Would the MoC simply determine the challenge itself, with his honor being on the line to make it fair? Would they appeal to the athro ahroun Grand Elder to come up with the challenge? Journey to another sept to find a higher ranked philodox?

      Depends on the sept, but here are few options I've seen/think that fit;

      - Another philodox of the sept is asked to serve as the judge. Preferably a fostern.

      - The Grand Elder will judge.

      - The current MOC asks for a ranked philodox they know to judge.


      The important thing is, which of these will involve your players the most? Will a PC philodox get to judge? Or do they have to get the 'trusted' judge for the Lord?

              It's important to remember that the Master of the Challenge isn't necessary to settle a challenge. Their real job is to keep Garou challenge culture from getting out of hand and disrupting Sept life. If a challenge is offered, the terms agreed to, than accepted, Garou are expected to acknowledge the results. The Master of the Challenge gives weight to this by observing the process as a neutral party, but they aren't the source of legitimacy.

              So, baring any other options, the two can just settle it between themselves. It's not ideal, but Garou society isn't supposed to grind to a halt because of situations like this.

              If the Sept has a Truthcatcher, they'd likely be the top choice of who to go to if necessary. The Elder(s) after that.

              Ana Mizuki very right regarding play to put emphasis on the players and their interactions with all this.

                          The Players Guides define the sept positions and expectations of the Garou who fill them, but they aren't always clear on how these positions are filled. The necessary bits seem to be scattered in different places and even different books. So like many things, we need to work things out. (And in this case like many others, I believe many games with large player bases - LARPs and online games - have often come to their own conclusions and standardized things in a certain way that may not fit best with what's actually been stated in the game. It doesn't mean they are wrong. It just means there can be alternate methods to do this.)

                          First, I think the Sept Elders must define the minimum responsibilities for each position. This is going to change from sept to sept because there are too many individual circumstances that come into play. A large sept with lots of experienced candidates can have very high requirements, while a small sept with fewer experienced Garou might set a low minimum and may waive even that if circumstances are exceptional. But I do think it is important that the Sept Elders have some kind of standard to be met - they don't want an unqualified Garou somehow achieving the position by mistake. So in my opinion, I think it's fine if the Sept Elders tell someone "No, you can't get this position" as long as they can explain why and thereby inform the Garou what he must learn or do before he is ready. Ideally, these qualifications should be openly known throughout the sept. So I imagine certain septs with certain caern purposes and caern totems could be more mysterious and require sept members to figure them out.

                          Second, the challenge should be done during the Cracking of the Bone at a moot. This is something that involves the whole sept. And we are given some guidance for leadership challenges here in the Players Guide to the Garou. 1) The challenger must be recognized by the Truthcatcher. So this is a good way for the sept to insure the candidate is qualified. A Truthcatcher can simply choose to not recognize the challenger as eligible and deny it. 2) Once someone is accepted as a legitimate challenger by the Truthcatcher, the challenged can either step down or accept the challenge. 3) The Truthcatcher determines how the challenge should be played. The Truthcatcher can let the challenged determine it, decide for himself, or defer to the Master of Challenge. I also think he could defer to the Sept Council/Grand Elder if needed.

                          Third, the challenge must be played out. It might be done at the moot, but it could also be set for a specific place or time. The challenge will typically either be done based on some form of Duel (ritualized combat), Gamecraft (mental challenge, often Riddles using the Enigmas ability but I would alllow any other kind of appropriate mental based challenge), or Facedown (social challenge).

                          Once the challenge is resolved, the winner is determined.

                          These steps were not clear in earlier editions of the game, so the revised Players Guide to Garou gives us a lot more detail. I think it makes a lot of sense, but there isn't any reason why individuals septs can't have different methods. I like to think that most things explained in the game are simply "the typical" sept and gives us a baseline. It is not the end-all and be-all. So if you favor another method, go with it.

                              So if I was going to run such an encounter, here's the specific things I would do.

                              A) Define the minimum knowledge needed to be Master of Challenge. Since this seems to be a small sept and has let a Fostern (!) be its current MoC, the requirements are probably not high. But IDEALLY, a Master of Challenge should possess the following:
                              • skilled in Gamecraft
                              • skilled in physical combat
                              • be able to referee and evaluate the contestants in the above as very often he will determine the winner
                              • have good reputation for honesty and fairness
                              • have knowledge of klaive duelling
                              • be good at organization as he needs to set time and places for challenges so they don't disrupt other activities at the caern
                              B) The Truthcatcher will need to accept the challenger as valid (probably easily done since I can't imagine an Adren not being at least as good as the current Fostern, but it is always possible). Then the Truthcatcher will need to determine how the challenge is to be determined. The Truthcatcher should have spoken with the Sept Elders and other sept members to know what he "should" be doing so it's not out of left field.

                              This can be tricky because here politics could come into play. On paper the Adren Bone Gnawer Philodox is probably qualified. But I think you said before the sept is Shadow Lord oriented, and the other tribes don't like Bone Gnawers in general. So there is the possibility the Truthcatcher could stack the challenge against the challenger. That might mean letting the current Fostern decide, creating a challenge the Truthcatcher knows favors the incumbent, or something else that is less than entirely fair (but probably in the real of justification). Or it could be a fair fight. You know what's going in your sept.

                              So it can easily be something the ST determines on his own, and simply put it into the mouth of whatever the appropriate NPC is.

                              If you want it to be decided by the PC Philodox, I suggest you to lay some groundwork first that explains why he's involved in this. For example, the sept may want to begin grooming the PCs to eventually take over leadership positions. Making them some kind of assistant to existing sept leaders would be a good apprenticeship. So figure out a way to get that PC involved in the Truthcatcher. Maybe one of the other Garou encourage the PC to seek out the TC. Or the TC calls all the Philodox PCs and NPCs earlier and begins to tutor them. Or something else you think of. Then when the moot and Cracking of the Bone occurs, you can have the TC ask his apprentice/assistant what he thinks (or maybe ask for suggestions from the crowd and allow several NPCs and the PCs to offer suggestions) and then he picks the right one. Just be sure to not present this as the TC being clueless or incompetent, but clearing trying to engage the sept or mentor the PC. This also allows you to modify (or even reject) the PC's suggestion if it doesn't meet the standards you need.

                              C) What do you want the challenge to be focused on? You can have several different ways of selecting a Master of Challenge - is it sufficient skill in one area, showing wisdom in ascertaining the victor or determining appropriate challenges for others, displaying integrity and fairness, or something else? I can easily see one sept focusing on who is the better klaive duelist, or gamecraft riddler, or who can devise unique and interesting challenges for others, or who is the most honest. All of these could work. Since this sept, I believe, is a caern of wisdom with a caern totem of Owl, I would probably lean towards something that shows the Master of Challenge would be wise. If you have a preference for these methods, I'd make sure to include that in prior IC conversations NPCs have with the PCs, so they can pick the right approach.

                              Change and fiddle things as necessary for what you think is right for the sept, your game, and the PCs so that's fun for them.

                                  Thanks for all the feedback, everyone. Blackfox, that was great stuff - I'd not appreciated the Truthcatcher's role in these proceedings, nor the fact that it should all go down during the Cracking of the Bone. Excellent considerations for future challenges, and I'm bookmarking this page.

                                  I ran this last Thursday evening, and it went great. As the Fostern SL held both the MoC and Truthcatcher position (It had been a tiny sept until the recent influx of packs) I couldn't turn to the TC either for adjudication. As such, I had the MoC ask the PC Philodox to run the challenge, as the PC had shown wisdom in previous encounters.

                                  The player was thrilled. He took about 45 minutes irl to think it through, then went with the following: he asked each contestant a difficult question, then rolled Per + Emp to determine if they believed their answers, or were posturing so as to win. He finally asked a third question of them both, which in short was to make their best argument as to why the other contestant was qualified for the role.

                                  I did my level best to interpret the Litany from each NPC's pov, and the player rolled good checks, so that at the contest's end he determined the NPC's to be tied. As such, he deferred to rank and awarded the position of MoC to the Adren challenger. All in all it turned out great, and the best part was that the player was thrilled to be thrust into the spotlight.

                                  Thanks again, everyone!
