                            I do not really see the existing Rage rolls as a problem that needs a solution. Garou without high Rage are less combat capable than Garou with high Rage, but they suffer less of the negative effects of Rage. And temporary Rage can exceed permanent Rage (unlike temporary Gnosis or temporary Willpower), which becomes a problem when Garou see the moon for the first time during a night because Rage rolls default to temporary Rage if temporary Rage exceeds permanent Rage (as they gain a set amount of temporary Rage unless the moon is in their auspice). A Ragabash with permanent Rage 1 who perceives the full moon will go to five points of temporary Rage until they can work through the excess temporary Rage, which means that they can go into frenzy quite easily. An Ahroun with permanent Rage 5 in the same circumstance is not particularly disadvantaged because she refills her Rage up to her permanent Rage (and she can burn Rage much more quickly than a Ragabash with permanent Rage 1).

                                    But the point of Rage is that it is a limited resource (at least as far as W20 is concerned). Fera have to conserve Rage because it can mean the difference between life and death. While a character might gain a point of Rage before the beginning of conflict, while being humiliated, or when they botch a critical roll, they are only guaranteed Rage recovery when they see the moon for the first time during a night.

                                    If Rage stops being a limited resource, then characters will just keep accumulating Rage during a conflict until they frenzy, and they will keep accumulating Rage during the frenzy. There is no real reason not to accumulate Rage since the only penalty is frenzy, especially when characters are probably within a combat situation when characters are accumulating Rage. This is especially true if the character can just go meditate away the Rage. It also gets rid of many of the combat differences between auspices, as the only difference comes down to how much Rage can characters burn for extra actions, especially since a Ragabash accumulating one point of Rage per turn for 10 turns pretty much has the same effect as an Ahroun accumulating two points of Rage per turn for 5 turns.

                                        I think the biggest design reason to adjust how Frenzy checks work, is because rolling to fail works poorly in the system. And that's how Frenzies work: you roll Rage and hope you fail. The system works considerably better if the players roll to succeed.

                                        So, a house rule that I've used to success that addresses some of Story Letter's concerns (long before V5's Hunger mechanics):

                                        Resisting Frenzy is a Willpower roll with a difficulty equal to half of the characters Rage - higher of temp or perm as usual - rounded up, plus 0 to 4 based on moon phase, plus 1 if it's your moon. Failure is a normal Frenzy, botch is Thrall, you can't spend WP on the roll it self.

                                        This means even a Ragabash on a new moon could still fail their roll (if rarely since it's a diff 2 roll at Rage 1). And Ahrouns aren't quite as boned as they are in the current rules if they're keeping their WP equal to the Rage to avoid the penalties for not doing so. At Rage 5 on a full moon the base difficulty for an Ahroun is 8, so with WP 5 they're at around 64% to pass a Frenzy check. Which is actually pretty close to their current risk odds, but Ahroun players can invest in more WP too.

                                        This also slots easily into the existing mechanics. Rank bonuses that increase the difficulty on Frenzy checks now decrease it by the same amount, etc. Rage rolls still risk Frenzy as well, but that is something purposefully built into anything that rolls Rage as a dice-pool as part of a risk-reward for the power it offers (Remaining Active, using Rage to replace Intimidation, etc.).

                                        While I agree that letting characters have unlimited access to Rage as long as they're willing to risk the Frenzy isn't a great move (unless you want to completely redo a lot of other things), I do disagree that low Rage characters aren't dangerous combatants. They just use different tactics than the furry blender default. Low Rage - and esp. higher Gnosis - characters are much better suited towards using active Fetishes and Talens in battle (activating them is Gnosis based compared to relying on the passive benefits of something like a klaive). A well built grappling werewolf (though the idea that Hispo and Lupus forms can't initiate grapples is always kinda weird to me), doesn't really benefit as much from Rage actions, etc.

                                                            That is ultimately true !
                                                            And indeed I am planning on changing what is needed to be changed.

                                                            In order to help you, I will tell you how I ran Rage and what effects it had on the other elements.

                                                            Because we ran huge and long non stop sessions, which could easily count as multiple chronicles, but it was indeed one large chronicle that lasted an entire year, broken mostly in 3 parts.
                                                            Characters had many options to replenish Rage, Gnosis and willpower, since a 9-12 hours session real life could be from on day to a week in game. Players learned to control Rage in such manner that it was extremely rare to enter frenzy but quite easy to keep temporary Rage in aidful levels, our philodox had rage 5 with extremely high willpower the ahroun had equal rage and willpower etc etc. Of course they increased their stats with the appropriate role playing, but given their higher rank status things became even easier for frenzy checks.
                                                            So even though Rage in a combat was easy to be maintained, the players often claimed that even Rage 10 is not enough, because the more you have the more you burn, maybe the reason was that I allowed Rage expediture for automatic succs on physical actions and damage-soak or to forfeit stamina roll for regeneration. So players had more options and thus Rage evaporated even faster. On the other hand I only had 2 frenzies because of Rage rolls and they both happened in the same session, one was thrall of wyrm too, but guys 2 frenzies in a 3,5 year old campaign is a realy bad rate.

                                                            More often was the Curse effect than frenzy, yes we had many frenzies because of Remainig active rolls but only two from normal frenzy rolls, and that happened because of good Rage management, ok being a smart werewolf is worty for Renown awarding but I don't think the developers had that in mind when creating Rage mechanics.
                                                            Not to mention that no player ever lost the wolf, because the most times a werewolf was about to use his last willpower it was for a realy desperate reason, and when he succeeded it was so awesome that he actually gained it back, that of course happened extremely rarely as well.

                                                            In parallel with our Apocalypse campaing we where running a crossover WoD and in our party we had a werewolf whose frenzy was a major issue, he entered frenzy in avery session and he killed or decimated at least 3 player characters. That made me think that THIS is the real frenzy that makes people have nightamares for werewolves, though I prefered frenzy to be some kind of a danger rather than a great possibility to happen and victimize player characters.

                                                            So I am trying to find a way to make Rage an element that poots a player deeper to the monstrosity a werewolf is, to thrill and terrify the player, not to make Rage just one more game element.
                                                            The greatest issue is that creating mood-wise rules sometimes come against playwise rules. As Aya Tari mentioned above, what will I do about the moon phase rage replenishing, or with losing the wolf. All these are considerable issues, so far the changes I made aid some Rage related Gifts, but indeed leave behind some system mechanics.
