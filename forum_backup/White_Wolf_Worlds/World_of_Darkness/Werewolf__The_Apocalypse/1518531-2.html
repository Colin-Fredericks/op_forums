                                    So, I'm back with Ragabash tales. I kinda bounced hard off the Ragabash chapter and had a problem with working out my thoughts on parts of it in a way that I hope will sound reasonable to folks. For that reason each time I started to write this thing down I ended up not wanting to to engage with what bugged me the most. It was not helped that I managed to lose most of my written summary because I was silly enough to keep it in a browser window instead of elsewhere.

                                    In essence it comes down to the fact that in general I feel that Ragabash are one of the hardest Auspices to play properly in Werewolf. It's easy to interpret them as simply juvenile pranksters or as rote contrarians. But the actual purpose of the Ragabash is to use humor and a skewed perspective to challenge and direct the other Garou. They are there to be scouts, teachers, innovators, challengers and experts at defusing problematic situations. At times too clever for their own good, of course, but the true mark of a Ragabash is being Clever about being Garou and instilling their insights into others.

                                    Which is why reading the Ragabash section left me rather confused and troubled by the perspectives we see. So far the book has presented Garou being dumb in a number of ways and then calling them out for being dumb. The Ragabash chapter has the most spectacular selection of Garou being dumb so far. And yet, there is the least critique of the people who collected this material and a number of behaviours that should at least be criticised, are not.

                                    In the Ragabash section we have the following:
                                    • A Ragabash frenzying while trying to protect his kin, and proceeding to murder all of them indiscriminately
                                    • A Ragabash walking into a family gathering, being disgusted by their behavior, leaving and coming back only to murder them all
                                    • A Ragabash blowing up a JFK airport terminal with 27 dead casualties (and probably around a hundred wounded)

                                    The first instance has the Ragabash regretting what they have done and used by John North-Wind's Son to demonstrate the need of thinking and coordinating before acting. Great.

                                    The second instance has the Ragabash acting like a psycho, and there is no punishment nor deeper introspection for them practically excising three generations of their own blood, just because they didn't know better than to be complacent consumerists. Oooook, someone is really keen on hammering the fact that Ragabash too can act on Rage, but the fucker left and came back. That was premeditated murder - of their own family. And is this called out? Is this pointed out that it's a violation of at least two tenets of the Litany? Nope.

                                    The third one has the Ragabash believing they were super smart and efficient at killing a major Wyrm player by bombing a major transportation hub, killing a shitload of innocents and pontificating how they are a weapon, and a weapon must kill. No comment. No critique. No calling out the fact that he cannot even be sure he got the target he was aiming for. What the flying fuck? This goes somewhere along with The Vienna bombing (v5) as a brilliant example of how insane events that would practically cause immense problems not just for the character but in politics and police action are treated over casually causing mass mayhem. And even not dwelling on that, the actions of that character are a horrible example of a Ragabash's job. Again, the author was keen on showing how broken the Garou can be from their war, but forgot to actually point out that this is NOT the way to do things and call them out

                                    It's bizarre to see that the Ragabash are the worst Garou we are yet to see in the book. And it's even more bizarre that this was something a lot of focus was placed on. I am not sure what was the goal, and the message here? Combined with the fourth crazy Garou in the chapter (who I kinda liked), it's as if I was reading a Twitcher splatbook, not a Ragabash one.



                                    Chapter IV - Ragabash Tales​
                                    The chapter opens with a cool picture of what I assume is a Ragabash on a motorcycle blowing up a Circinus factory. What detracts from it is that they are smoking in front of the Evil Cigarette factory. I guess smoking characters are supposed to be cool in an action scene like this. Still it feels mismatched. What, did the character pick it up in the Factory? Did they have their own collection of cigars just for this opportunity? If it was any other thing being blown up, I wouldn't even have noticed it as much.


                                    Stories of Home
                                    The first tale we encounter is the first incident I outlined above. Pentex is grooming youth to become their new agents by promising them great things and compatriots while giving them superpowers. It's very much Pentex selling fomoridom as gaining X-Men powers (Free-X). This seems like an interesting antagonist for a specific sort of game, especially the insidious grooming and how deep it can cut into Garou families. I do feel I'd prefer to play down the X Men cues, though. The tale itself is all about how a Ragabash tried to find out what was wrong with his Cousin, and followed up a trail of grooming and systematic co-option only to snap and kill her and her family in a Frenzy.

                                    The second one is a creepy tale of a Young Persons Care home where the inhabitants were taking part in a ominous "Breeding program". The whole thing was taken down by cops and there is a lot of uncertain details, but the director implies that they all belong to the "family". This is framed by a Ragabash that's trying to figure out if the rumors this ties into the Kinfolk is true and could it be tied to the BSD's or the actions of the local Sept. A lot of it is termed in very unclear hearsay and postulation, but I've never been a big fan of the whole idea that a "good" tactic for Garou is to make breeding camps. Considering a lot of allegations what Werewolf is about, maybe a stronger message against such horrible anomalies would have been great as this can be interpreted in quite a few ways. Some of which will just be seen as another proof of how horrible Garou as a whole are.

                                    The third is the letter of a wife to her husband, a Garou. It speaks of how she and her child were assaulted by strange assailants in the night, and even though she doesn't remember what happened, it's clear her child shifted and tore apart/drove away the attackers. The mother decides to take away the child and go far from the husband as possible, but it's clear that will not end well considering she now has a Garou on her hands. It's good, solid writing, and something I can see being a thing for many Garou who have to leave their families behind.

                                    The final section, Memory, left me very confused. The core story itself is strong - it speaks of inter-garou conflict, it speaks of attempts to cure fomori (tying into the Theurge section) and how things like these can be an excuse for Garou to murder other Garou over prejudice and fake allegations of corruption. It's told from the perspective of the last standing member of a pack that got wiped out, remembering their packmates and honouring their memory by being the lone guardian of their tale and their Caern, which will now be dedicated in the name of the fallen. The twist at the end if that the story is being told to the Garou who led the attack on the Caern, just before they kill them.

                                    Ok. That doesn't sound that weird. But the details are weird.
                                    • It's a Caern somewhere near Providence where Mother Larissa went to die. Wait. What? When? How? We just saw her in last chapter being her usual self? I cannot imagine her dying anywhere but in the Sept of Green, she is deeply, deeply tied to this place
                                    • The person retelling this tale seems to have a male homid body and a female lupus body. I don't think I've ever seen something like that before.
                                    • The child they cured from the horrifying bane has done a lot of horrible things and most likely remembers them wakes up happy and overjoyed to be saved.
                                    • And the person the story is being told to has come here without their pack to be torn apart by the narrator. Now this is probably the weirdest bit as everything that follows will be a shitstorm. If they killed the pack leader, the rest of the pack is coming back and a lone Garou won't take them. Worse yet, it's a lone Garou defending a Caern that is known to be without defenders, more Garou are definitely coming to take over. This is framed into being somehow backed by the Margrave, the resident ominous Garou leader
                                    • But what the hell does the Margrave even have to do with the Septs on the East coast of the U.S. ? The dude operates in Europe and that's his power base, this ties again into my rant about how people seem to miss the bigger picture and the geography in Werewolf for the big characters




                                    Monkeywrenching
                                    The weird choices of how parts of the text are organised vex me here quite a bit. There is a strong theme of Monkeywrenching and different approaches and infodumps on how Pentex and its members operate, however it got split up with a number of other things happening in the chapter that are not thematically related to them. The last section is also spread through the book, but the chunks are so much more self contained. I'll try and list them out here as it's going to be easier to process them in context
                                    • Intercepted board meeting of Endron - a transcript with a lot of backstabbing, manipulation and casual employee murder
                                    • Outlook Post #7 - a transcript of a soldier manning an outpost in the jungle noticing way too strange things going on - I loved this bit and it was some excellent world building and paranoia and typical Pentex Callousness
                                    • One Head Falls, Another Rises - The confession of the JFK Bomber Ragabash above and how he aimed to kill Ian Robertson, one of the up and coming Pentex directors as well as a news article about the event and how essentially it changed nothing in the bigger picture
                                    • A recounting how a Sept in Virginia turned the Skull Pigs against Mike Dragon's-Wrath and used them to distract and hurt him enough to take him out
                                    • A detailed report on monkey wrenching in action at GRC Consultants - support abuse, grassroots pressure, driving people in charge crazy, organising the staff...
                                    • Pentex has issued Kill Orders for a number of senior personnel to First Teams - can the Garou utilise this (intriguing plothook, wish there was a bit more detail)
                                    • All in all Three dead Pentex Directors in one chapter, and only one of them killed by a Garou being an idiot.

                                    The Inheritors
                                    I'm calling this section like this because it deals with a theme of what the world is like in the absence of the Lost Tribes and Breeds, and what some may think it will lead to. The first of the tales in this section is about the "anti-dingo enthusiasts" of the Outback, and how the local Garou hunt them down while avoiding the dissaproval of the local spirits. We get a glimpse into the Dreamtime and how strange it is compared to the usual Umbra and how its populated by strange and intriguing giant beasts. Just made me feel like I'd have loved to have seen a proper Rage Across Australia.

                                    A Kinfolk doctor is exploring where the shifters came from, and how certain breeds and tribes have changed their biology to suit other animals. She postulates that the Rite of Skin Walking and Minotaur may be something related to the Apis. Finally, she seeks out the Gumagan so that she may learn the rites that allow people to change their animal forms to help her own Tribe adapt to the coming world. Short, sweet, loaded with ideas and potential.

                                    We learn of the Crow-Biting-Ferals, a pack that had a silly Kinfolk bagged and tagged by some weird biotech company after they submitted their DNA Ancestry Kit. Only to realise that there are horrors in there and that the company seeks to make strange hybrids and utilise the harvested DNA to create mockeries. I love this little vignette for the simple reason that it's pretty much an adventure of its own

                                    And then comes Glen-Seize-Trouble. Now Glen-Seize-Trouble is a posterboy for being too clever for his own good. He has this theory... that the Mockery breeds are not really the servitors of the Wyrm, but actually Pentex rediscovering the lost breeds and doing Gaia's work for her. He believes them to be Gaia's Backup plan. And his story continues by him travelling to Mozambique seeking to meet with what he believes to be a Yeren. He ropes in some local Glass Walkers to help him out, and they meet Jeremiah God-Buyer, the slickest, skeeviest used car salesman in all of Africa. And Jeremiah proceeds to provoke the Walkers and murder them with his security team which included nigh-unkillable Kerasi. We also get our first illustration of the Kerasi, but it's sadly 4 pages further​! An interesting feature of the Yeren is that he did all this in plain sight, and managed to easily control the witnesses and the police into submission.

                                    This leads Glen down a doom spiral in which he believes that his theses are wrong, and that the Wyrm has won. I believe that what follows is an episode of Harano, and its a harrowing experience for Glen, one in which he is not only confronted by his errors, but also with the errors of the Garou as a whole and how they must do better. His next step seems to be taking out one of the Pentex Directors (Pochard), so that does seem like he got better.

                                    Of course, it's not just the new breeds or the Garou having to pick up where the Bunyip were, because they also inherited some additional problems. We learn how Operation Totem unleashed strange things from under the earth, and how the Bunyip, just like the Older Brother kept horrible things underground. And the most horrible of them is about to wake, and no-one knows how to put it back to sleep. Interestingly, it implies that "his Tribe grows stronger". I'm not sure if this is a BSD totem or something entirely else?


                                    The Tricksters
                                    This includes probably two of my favourite parts of the Ragabash tales. The first one is written from the perspective of two Black Spirals on a grisly mission to murder some Kinfolk who the spirits say may become Garou. They are as disgusting as should be expected, one of them looking forward to do horrible things to the girl, to which the other just says that they should do it when she's dead. Peak BSD. But the cool thing about the story is that they're walking into a trap, and using misdirection and trickery a Ragabash and a Theurge play them against each other and manage to get them to murder each other. It's brilliant and I'd love to see something like that pulled off in a game.

                                    Also, one of the BSD's is called Bleeds-The-Night. I found this hilarious as there is also a Rokea called "Bleeds Night". Not going to cause confusion ever. Nope.

                                    The second tale is about Raccoon being interviewed by a young Ragabash looking for Wisdom. And Raccoon is being a total loonie here. His tone and way he communicates are priceless and he may be my favourite spirit in the book. He tells the Ragabash a tale about the Atahasia (the hidden metis Ananasi) rising up from giant cracks in the Earth and overtaking the city of Seattle, murdering most of downtown and setting up residence in one of the skyscrapers. There is of course a question if this tale is true, will be true and was ever true by the narrator, but the Raccoon tells him the tales are here to educate people (I do like the scene itself, even if doing it in Seattle would probably be an almost Apocalypse level scenario). The whole point of the tale is to speak of how Garou do war, with a Ragabash who brings news to a haughty Ahroun only to be executed, and how another clever Ragabash rather lets the Ahroun get killed and saves the rest of the Garou from dying like fools. It's a great one, it's educational, it criticizes certain Garou tendencies.

                                    ... and it's all an elaborate con by Old Man Manyskins who (as Raccoon) uses it to not just teach the young one a lesson that needs to be learned, but also point him towards looking for Turtle - another thread pointing toward the return of the Totem of the Croatan.


                                    The Fate of House Blood Red Crest
                                    Aaand Pattern Recognition is back. And demonstrating their brilliant diplomatic acumen by sending over one of their Geomids to talk to a certain lupus Garou in the middle of Tibet. That, of course, doesn't end well. Thankfully, they manage to convince Nightmane, the Theurge of the Silver Fangs to take them from the Shigalu monastery into India and into the courts of House Blood Red Crest. They make an excellent argument that one of the best ways for Garou to understand each other is to tell tales of different experiences and different rulers.

                                    They travel from Shigalu with Nightmane into Khasi Hills, the domain of King Nayar of House Blood Red Crest. We see the local Garou participating in a festival, one of their Ragabash blessing marital unions, and ultimately King Nayar presenting his solution to the lack of new Garou being born to the house - they shall take any and all Crinos-born under their wing for the glory of the House and Falcon!

                                    It's a strong statement and one that makes sense. I liked Nightmane as a very sensible sort and Dalaja of the House Blood Red Crest sounded like a fun character. Hope we'll see more about her and the king in the pending Icons of Rage. I was sorry not to see details about the other parts of House Blood Red Crest that operate further to the East or any of their interactions with the local Hengeyokai, as in my games the House has to have extensive dealings with them.


                                    Plot Hooks

                                    This may be the least interesting plothook section of the book so far. I've had better hooks in the text before it that were not even touched upon in the summary in the back.I'd probably single out the story seed where certain elements of the Silver Fangs see the choice of House Blood Red Crest as shameful and seeks to sabotage or destroy them, and places the PC's between the two sides. This one has the makings of a solid chronicle.

                                    What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.

                                        Just fyi, when written, the "Family Sickness" is in fact the same family as "Free-X"/"Freaks". The daughter's name is the key there. So that Ragabash attended the birthday, but when he says he shouldn't have returned, it's in light of the Free-X events. Not exactly premeditated slaughter of three generations of Kinfolk. Not much better, in terms of outcome, mind you. Nan is old, Rowan has lost his parents and sister.

                                        I wonder what becomes of an orphaned Kinfolk child?

                                        I'd like to point out that Jasmine's scream was supernatural. The tale teller isn't lying when he says her scream literally burst his ear drums. I took that power from Freak Legion. It's subtle, until used. Jasmine didn't know she had this devastating power, or maybe she did and she was being groomed as a "Black Canary" equivalent? Regardless, it's not something that Ragabash was expecting, and that's where he went wrong, really. He didn't look hard enough, or investigate long enough to realize she was already a Fomori, and was caught by surprise by a power his niece didn't understand, that caused injury and Frenzy-with-a-capital-F - even in an Auspice with the least starting Rage. If only he had Sense Wyrm? Or, given the status of the house per Family Sickness, would that even have worked? And she may have had some other power that alerted her to the Ragabash's presence beside her bed - or maybe he was a bit too confident sneaking around and failed a stealth roll?

                                        Anyway, it was supposed to be a tragic and cautionary tale - even the supposedly cleverest and least Rage filled Garou have blind spots, make mistakes, fall prey to Frenzy and the Wyrm Urges within their hearts.

                                        Those tales were meant to lead into the Crow-Biting-Ferals raid. Again, the names should link them, however I'm aware that the order in the final book makes it a bit trickier to follow.

                                        And the CBFs are Glen's pack. And they get frustrated with him when Ben Spears-the-Web conveys the Jeremiah God-Buyer incident. Because just maybe two extra Garou could've turned the fight? But the Ragabash pack leader held himself and Ben back. oh look, trying to be clever and cautious probably saved their lives but maybe, just maybe, they would've been victorious? But also, the Kerasi and Yeren were unexpected. And powerful. And the Yeren's Gift for dodging blame/responsibility is quite incredible. And utterly terrifying for Glen who's maybe done some bad things in his life and been rightly punished (or corrected, or disciplined) and (hopefully) learned from his misdeeds however much he regrets them - yet this Yeren guy just calls on a magic trick to avoid all that?! No responsibility, no accountability - do whatever you want and pin it on someone else to suffer consequences. Glen's working through the implications, you just don't get that depth in such a short tale.

                                        Poor Glen. Falling deep into Harano and still desperately trying to fight the Wyrm until he goes somewhere that just maybe he shouldn't... but I don't think you've hit that connection yet so #Spoilers!
