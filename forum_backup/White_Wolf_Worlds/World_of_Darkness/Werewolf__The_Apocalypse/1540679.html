    [W20] Wyrm-taint, what does it actually do mechanically?

    08-18-2024, 09:57 PM
    Greetings,

    As the title says, what Wyrm-taint does it actually do mechanically? I can not find rules on why it is bad, except for the Taint of Corruption flaw that specifically makes you more vulnerable to those of the Wyrm.

    I feel it is the gluten of the Apocalypse gameline players: most people don't want to have it in their body but don't know why it is bad for them.

    Also, the setting itself is inconsistent on non-mechanical reasons why it is bad... As a poster mentioned: "Wyrm Taint isn't Alignment; a Garou who just climbed out of a pit where they slaughtered a hundred Banes and twenty BSDs will reek of the Wyrm.", so you can be Wyrm-tainted and then what?

      It's Werewolf's take on the Shinto concept of kegare, kind of. It's a spiritual and ritual uncleanliness that sets them on edge because of how the universe has been set off-kilter by Destruction turning into Corruption. It also tends to attract spirits with a similar "feeling", much like how the stultifying uniformity of the Weaver attracts more spirits dedicated to homogenizing things, establishing rigid control, etc.

      The flaw, Taint of Corruption, specifically notes that there's a deep, internal source that needs to be dealt with. This could have a large number of causes, potentially even just not having managed to get rid of taint that you accumulated after crawling through multiple bane pits for an extended stretch.

          This might help.
          Someone put together some workable backend mechanics for an ST to track character taint, and the discussions around it are interesting.

          At my tables, I tend to do similar to the above linked, but more importantly, I pay attention to what corruptive actions the player character(s) are engaged in. Rather than track it points-based, I start introducing aspects of corruption that are drawn to the character's behavior and choices. I'll often pursue this in ways that draw the attention of one's packmates, making it more of a plot point that could potentially help drive pack unity and offer a moment of character growth. I'll often approach the player between sessions and let them know about it; they may have some ideas for how it can be a little more personalized to their character and can give their character a more pronounced role in a given chronicle.

          Given the number of urges that exist, I usually start there and then branch off into that particular Urge's bastards to seek out inspiration. A clever ST may find ways to introduce some of these bastards as things that don't on the surface appear either good or bad, particularly with characters that don't have Sense Balance or other Gifts that allow them to precisely know whether some of these spirits are Wyrm or not. There are some to be certain Urge Wyrms and bastards a little too conspicuous for subtlety, but again these are just ideas!

              This is how I use Wyrm taint in the chronicles I ST.

              1) Wyrm taint signifies something is wrong. Lots of things cause Wyrm taint. Many of them natural (meaning not supernatural, things that happen in the real world), but none of them good.

              2) Wyrm taint does not justify Garou killing everything tainted, but it does justify Garou investigating more.

              3) Wyrm taint allows Bane possession. Banes cannot possess people without Wyrm taint, though relatively few tainted people are ever possessed. This is probably the most important consideration.

              4) Wyrm taint, that is not cleansed, often leads to personality changes. Just like stress makes people more angry, bitter, intolerant, resentment, etc., Wyrm taint does the same thing. Wyrm taint that is allowed to fester creates a lot of problems down the line.

              5) Wyrm taint is a kind of spiritual pollution. As long as it exists, it will attract more taint elsewhere as Banes and other Wyrm minions will congregate towards it.

              6) Wyrm taint appears when anything is "un-Gaian", meaning it is a violation of the real world. Anything supernatural that violates the laws of nature, registers as Wyrm tainted in some capacity. Vampires, mummies, and immortal mages all register as Wyrm tainted to some degree because humans are not mean to live beyond their designated lifespan. But certain people will register as more Wyrm tainted as others based on their criteria. Just like high humanity vampires do not register as Wyrm tainted, or Gangrel with even less humanity, most mummies and mages won't either, but they could if their immortality results in further violations of morality and nature.

                  Strictly speaking and answering the question as posited, there's no rule.

                  What we have is that Wyrm-aligned spirits congregate around taint, they're drawn to it. But this is purely narrative by RAW.

                  The same goes for the influence of those spirits, they work more and easier upon tainted targets, to the point that all Fomori can be safely surmised to have been deeply tainted before the Bane started possession.

                  Other than that it is a purely narrative tool that will have only the rules it needs on your story. As said, it is based on Kegare, which means that while it can be produced by certain behaviors, it works a lot like filth.
