Humans are a component of the main characters of werewolf, one that plays host to a lot of errant individuals that are up to no good. What's more, while a Changing Breed can't survive the loss of its beast kin, it can survive the loss of its human-alike component, as the Mokole did (it was a long, long time between when the Kings died and when the Dragon Breed adopted human form). Humans are basically the less vital component there.

        In contrast, Vampire and Mage don't stop talking about humanity this, humanity that, and anything else is an afterthought.

                    Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post
                    It really wouldn't, because that would promote too much health, at least as far as the fungi are concerned. What else can promote timely breakdowns of dead matter so that they can be recycled and also knock the teeth out of radiation in some cases? The establishment of "fungi are Wyrmy" is really a knee-jerk, early installment bit that can be chalked up to baggage from D&D's mushroom men. Just as W:tA can adjust to getting new science in about hyenas, it can steer away from tarring fungi and parasites. Also worth noting that in spite of the association of cephalopods with Qyrl, there are still octopus-spirits that are Gaian. This isn't W:tF, the line isn't that cut and dry about weird associations with certain animals over others vis a vis the Hosts and the like.
                    The thing is, with time the game adjusted to get new science about the Wyrm, too.

                    While you're completely right if we assume the Wyrm only in its current, corrupted iteration, the Wyrm isn't mainly about corruption. It is mainly about destruction, sudden or gradual, and decay, which are completely natural, healthy and necessary processes. In the Triatic cosmology of Werewolf you really don't have a good reason to associate those spirits to the other two: fungi and parasites promote growth and order as aftereffects, but their direct action is promoting the decay of other organisms.

                    Of course, you can have any particular spirit belong to any given "class" for any number of reasons, and you may have most such spirits having "switched sides" in the past to avoid corruption. But if the only reason to avoid linking them to the Wyrm is its corruption, it won't change that they're originally Wyrm spirits, that they're the most likely to represent the concept of still uncorrupted Wyrm Spirits, and the matter at hand: that the Wyrm would favor such "plant/animal" spirits over other, regardless if the majority of those spirits are Tainted, because this assertion isn't about the majority of Fungi spirits, but about the majority of Wyrm spirits.

                    Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post
                    It focuses on them as a part of werebeasts, but that doesn't necessarily make them special the way that Mage or Vampire see them as special. Humans are a component of the main characters of werewolf, one that plays host to a lot of errant individuals that are up to no good. What's more, while a Changing Breed can't survive the loss of its beast kin, it can survive the loss of its human-alike component, as the Mokole did (it was a long, long time between when the Kings died and when the Dragon Breed adopted human form). Humans are basically the less vital component there.
                    Being able to justify it by the lore doesn't change the irony observed. While Werewolf is about humans not being special for all the reasons you pointed, it still makes humans special in practical terms, both by making them have ghosts instead of spirits and by making them the singular common trait among all the Changing Breeds.

                    Even, by the way, Breeds that loath humans from the bottom of their hearts. We're told that humans are the least relevant aspect of the equation, but we're never given direct evidence. All Breeds use humans, no one ever shown the possibility of changing that. Or better, a few did: Pentex's artificial Breeds include one or two without any human side, but I hardly think they're a good evidence that this is along the natural way of things.

                    This is just irony. The game is meant to tell stories about wolf-men to and by human players, and it came out in the early 90's, there's no need to take those facts seriously if you don't want to, the game is clearly meant to be serious about humans not being special. But it is an option for any ST or group that wants to play humans as being special, without actually breaking the setting since we only have the Changers word for it, so unreliable narrator and all that. It is also relevant because the settings are distinct but meant to reference each other, so Werewolf can't really prove humans aren't a big deal to not break the already tenuous connection with other lines.

                                                        This is a difference in what the setting traditionally says versus what makes sense.

                                                        ​I understand what Saur Ops Specialist means: no matter how much those spirits were originally Wyrmish, in the lore most of them cut ties with the Wyrm when the whole corruption debacle happened. So they're Gaian, but under no member of the Triat. It is less about what they represent and more about their active decision to take a side.

                                                        On my part I know this, but I'm not completely convinced of its logic. But more importantly, in the context of the thread it has indeed more to do with the nature of those spirits than this decision, that is, their original link to the Wyrm explains why Vampires aren't necessarily linked to the corruption just because they're linked to the Wyrm, and the existence of rare non-cirrupt Wyrm spirits reinforces that.

                                                        Vampires may very well be older than the Wyrm's corruption and actually occupy a natural niche modern werewolves just do not understand (as most of them also do not understand non-corrupt Wyrmish spirits, or even heard about them, yet they're a real thing). It may well be that the modern Beast is a development from after the Wyrm's fall and a consequence of it corrupting them, when high-Humanity/Path Vampires may be more free from this corruption.

                                                        Golconda, in this sense, may very well be about freeing the vampire from the Tainting influence and accepting their Gaian niche, which would be a very interesting way to portray such vampires in a Werewolf game and add a lot of nuance that would be interesting in a game that seeks to explore contacts between the two from a Werewolf perspective.
