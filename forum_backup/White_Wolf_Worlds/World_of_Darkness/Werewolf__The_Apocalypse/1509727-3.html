                                                        These things do happen, but it's probably a good idea to add something into the briefs they give to artists about not tracing or plagiarising art (which they may already have), as well as something about taking extra care with representations of ethnic minorities and marginalised people (which they also may have), and then giving people the time to do it right (as well as allowing proper time for an approvals process). If I had to guess, it's probably that last part that's the stumbling block, not the first two.
                                                        Rushing things for GenCon, for example, seems to be a particular problem in the RPG industry. Nearly everyone seems to do it, and it nearly always results in more mistakes than in those RPGs which weren't rushed out for GenCon.

                                                        In the case of plagiarism, copying something from Devil May Cry, for example, is bad and may cost you money and/or street cred, but it will be a lesser ethical problem than representing real life people and cultures.

                                                        ETA: Re-read my last paragraph if you think this is giving anyone a pass. It absolutely isn't. In fact, it's quite the opposite.
