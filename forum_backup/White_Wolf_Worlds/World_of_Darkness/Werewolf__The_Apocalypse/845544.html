                      In the game mechanics, there is no rules for that. Unless the person is kin or supernatural, Delirium always hits. However, there was a reference in 2nd Edition, that certain groups who are per se more in tune with the supernatural, the Delirium might not be as effective. Native americans were an example. And, iirc, the rural asian population was described as being aware of the supernatural, but considering them an anathema so they don't talk about them and ignore their doings.

                      From 2nd edition:
                      The Delirium: Garou in Crinos form create a blind, instinctive terror in humans who view them. Humans' dependence upon rationality is so total that they cannot accept the truth of werewolves' existence; they will believe any convenient rationale ra ther than concede the existence of Garou. Most simply forget what they have seen. The end result of this reaction, known as the Veil, is the predominant reason why werewolves are generally thought to be creatures of legend.

                      I always found that the Delirium as a means of staying sane on the humans part made more sense, then an millenia old fear inherited in humans.
                      Several questions remain unanswered: Are children, toddlers, infants immune ?
                      Will repeated exposure in a short time frame drive one insane or lead to immunity ?
                      Are sorcerers and ghouls immune ?
                      Are already insane people immune ?

                      Also, I found that the "the impergium is the reason" statement made more sense if it was the Children of Gaia blaming the other garou, rather than an out-of-character game book statement. But then again, I still hate the "human as cattle and lower race" statements in revised, as opposed to a sheppard culling the herd like it was described in 2nd. One makes all your ancestors look like wyrm-worshipping douchs, the other looks like a function that needed to be taken, to keep the planet in balance...
