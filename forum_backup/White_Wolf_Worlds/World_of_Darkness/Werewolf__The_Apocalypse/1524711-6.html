        Originally posted by Toby Weingarten View Post
        One Background that has returned is Totem, now renamed Spirit Pact. So far, there is not much that goes into this BG. Since there are no rules for communal traits, all such Pacts are with one Garou, not an entire pack, thus limiting the strength of this BG. Basically, this allows a Garou to have spirit around to help out. It doesn't provide any Boons, like those of the Tribe Patrons, but they don't have Bans either, unless one purchases a Flaw to do so.
        There is still a Rite for getting a spirit to become a Pack-Patron-Spirit but the Rite reduces the choice to it having to be one of the Tribe-Patron-Spirit. And I think the main-reason for that they didn't bother to figure out any underlying framework to answer the question of how to represent Spirit-Allies or Pack-Patron-Spirits in terms of dots or in general. As anatgonists or allies they can just present these stat-blocks which are these lists of abilities with dice-pools but transforming that into dots of a Background or a Pack-Patron-Spirit takes a bit more work - which wasn't done in the W5 corebook.


        Originally posted by Toby Weingarten View Post
        Finally, Renown is still around, but in an altered form.
        Here, I would mention that now both Chagrin (the name for reducing Renown for doing something unworthy) and recognizing a Renown-worthy deed for the sake of whether Renown can be advanced with XP is all now firmly in the hands of the ST. The social component of things like Moots or a Galliard having to turn it into a tale isn't central to this anymore. How it's presented in the W5 corebook it's all just the ST making judgment-calls. So rather than social Garou-events being the pivot-points, W5 is now fine with Renown (advancing or being recognized to have done something shameful) just happening on the ST-player-level. I don't think it's intentional but more or less just a victim of avoiding lore/worldbuilding and once again just presenting a system that is entirely reliant on the ST's ability to make it work.

        Originally posted by Toby Weingarten View Post
        While using just one power source for multiple things makes the game easier, the economy does not. Even if not spent, Rage can be lost so fast, that the game is almost rigged to suggest that playing as a normal human might be the best option.
        Ironically, the system will help you the most if you stick to high Rage and consistently go for a brutal/violent approach. Also starting arguments with NPCs whenever you can also helps.

        But the system can get quite swingy if a player is unlucky. And there are some ways around it. You can take a Harano-dot to immediately go to Rage 5. You can enter Crinos and voluntarily enter Frenzy... You look at those Rage-pools of your players... Abstractly it's pretty easy: High Rage for combat, low Rage for social scenes.

        The first issue is the imbalance of what threats a Garou-PC can handle. There's a vast gulf between what you can throw at a PC with 1 or 2 Rage and one with 3, 4 or 5 Rage. You only need to look at the NPCs at the end of scenarios like Deepest Of Wounds and No Matter How Small. Huge dice-pools get thrown around. And since the Crinos-Form is in the mix... why not? But in W5 there are times when PCs can't use their Crinos-Form. And if the PC "lost their wolf" and lost access to their supernatural forms entirely, they can't use a lot of Gifts either.

        Another issue is what narratively happens. It's modeled after Hunger but the dynamic is easy to grasp in that case. The higher it gets, the worse for the vampire and since it's all related to bloodthirst and the Beast there are easy ways to understand what's going on when Hunger is rising in terms of dots. But the way Rage is structured both high and low Rage are bad. And the low-Rage-dynamic is essentially "the Hulk who isn't angry enough to transform". The point of Rage is that it's supposed to be that you can't control your anger. It isn't about how you sometimes run out of anger at inopportune times. It's also why the Hulk feels like a curse to Bruce Banner (well, depending on the version and who writes him, of course...). What is a scene about that says "I would like to do something but looking at my Rage-pool, my character doesn't have enough Rage to do something."? In a way that's a moment where the player feels more rage than the player-character.

        I think one problem in the conception of Rage in W5 is that it missed an important detail: Hunger is about thirst. Hunger is entirely about acquiring a resource external to yourself. Anyone can understand being thirsty is about and how to relieve it. But Rage? It's something inherent to Garou, it's something that comes from within and yet... it's treated like a resource that requires action to acquire. Rage should be at 0 because your character exhausted themselves, did all they could, can do no more - and not because the player was unlucky with some dice-rolls. What one worries about gaining too much Rage too quickly and losing control. Why would you make it a bit to worry about not having enough Rage? Losing the wolf is a stumbling-block, nothing more. I mean, what does W5 want you to do with a PC who has "lost the wolf" except just grounding their ass until they can howl at the moon...? What is supposed to happen in this time-frame where this PC has "lost the wolf"...? What is the point of making a PC go through that? That aspect feels like a waste of time.

        Also, making PCs "gather Rage" gets old pretty fast.

        ​
        The final rules are mostly about the new Harano and Hauglosk mechanics. While they are extremes, one can suffer from both at the same time. To be honest, I really can't see how to incorporate both into the same setting in a single character. Maybe one or the other, but not both. In fact, Hauglosk almost seems to be beneficial in certain regards.
        I think Hauglosk in the W5 corebook is the way it is because it's the remnants of Karim Muammar's vision of "Fenrir=Nazis" and linked concepts like Hauglosk. They were able to reach a compromise unlike in the Hunters Entertainmant version - but you can see just how nearly immovable Karim's position on the idea has been in the way Hauglosk doesn't make sense. But we've heard from what James F. Sambrano shared with the public that for Karim Muammar the conflict with the Fenrir is the essence of W5. And it doesn't seem like he treated this like a point that was up for discussion on the writing-team during the Hunters-Entertainment-version at least. Can't imagine that attitude changed much when they switched over to the in-house-team. What we got in the eventual book is the compromise with an extremist position. The Fenrir aren't outright Nazis but that was the starting-point and then they just toned down a couple of things.

            The funny thing?

            I actually understand where Muammar is coming from. I mean, I think he handled it in the worst possible way imaginable but I also can follow his train of thought unlike most executives.

            1. Divorcing the Garou from Human Culture

            There's a good financial reason for wanting to have a universalist system out there and it's the same reason that they got rid of the Kuei Jin. Suddenly, you have a literal entirely new species to represent Asian people. This way, there's 12 Tribes to draw from no matter where you are in the world.

            2. The Nazis are good punching bags

            We live in a time of Right Wing ascendency and W:TA has always been a vehicle for expressing anger at a world gone horribly wrong by late stage capitalism, fascism, and authoritarianism. Having political relevancy is hardly a bad thing.

            One of my book series is all about the fun to be had in slaughtering the Hydra equivalent that superheroes massacre left and right in the Supervillainy Saga.

            3. The treatment of Native Peoples in Werewolf has always been cringe

            The [Blank] guys were never handled particularly well. In addition to the use of the taboo name and as another type of werewolf, there's the fact that they were always treated as essentially dismissively in their anger over Indiginous oppression. But it goes beyond that, there's also the fact that the gameline really did throw them all together in a blender that basically was always going to be greatly disrespectful.

            The Bunyip for example is a particularly notable one given their place in the Garou Nation as a tribe wholly wiped out and forgotten with their culture as well as spiritual connection to the land severed to their great shame.
            ...
            Except there's a million aborigines in the world right now and they make up 4% of Australia's total population. Twice more than those in America and that's stretching numbers. They're not GONE and neither is their culture (despite many attempts).

            The problem seems to be that Muammar seems to have two major issues:

            1. The Get are really fucking popular among people who hate Nazis

            A huge chunk of gamers in Europe and America who absolutely fucking hate Nazis love the Get because they're awesome Klingons and werewolf Vikings that are great concepts regardless of white supremacy.

            2. The issue was over and done with

            It was settled twenty fucking years ago and decisively so in a way that most people were fine with. This is like raising the issue of slaughtering orc babies in Dungeons and Dragons and setting it as the PRIMARY focus of 6th edition.

            3. Native Erasure is kind of a shit solution to the problem of cringe

            As someone said about removing the Native woman off the Butter jars but leaving the landscape, "they took the land but got rid of the native." The general take I've heard from W:TA has always been, "The representation isn't great but bad representation is usually better than no representation."
            It's also NOT A HARD THING TO DO to keep it.

            Sidebar: "Gale Stalkers and Ghost Council have very strong camps related to Native Americans in the United States and numerous other indigineous people. They are heavily involved with their mortal relations and the issue of Native rights."​

            There, done. Make them a camp if you're going universalist for the tribes.

Yet, their PB involves violet eyes, silver-tinged white fur that self-cleans, and an aurora-like battle aura. Yes, really.
