                Originally posted by heinrich View Post
                I know that some books, possibly in their in-game perspective, give the mandate to never let a mage near a caern, but why? There is that idea that mages will suck dry caerns, but does that make sense? Nodes have value to mages and caerns could be regarded as nodes of a specific ressonance. Sure, Mage not allied with the garou could try to employ Prime to alter the caern to suit their needs better, which might "suck the caern dry" for garou and spirits of the middle umbra. If mages wanted to get rid of garou in their area, they might come to the conclusion, that the garou will move on, once their caern is destroyed, therefore making it a strategic target. Also, the Mage might with Prime have ways to use the supposedly existing spiritual energies as quintessence, therefore becoming more powerful when in the vicinity of a caern. Which is bad for an enemy mage. But all this wouldn't necessarily hold true for a mage who is also kin and allied with the garou.

                Garou usually meet for moots in the vicinity of the caern, not at the caern heart but within the bawn. And, going with the PGttG, which is the only non-in-game description of typical kin activities I know, the kin would be present for parts of the moot. Not necessarily for Inner Sky and Breaking the Bone, but for the Stories and Songs and to welcome back the garou after the Revel. This is an important part of knitting the community together and it wouldn't make sense to exclude the mage, especially if one wants/needs to bind him closer to the group (emotionally), as well as keeping an eye on him. Also, if the mage does use this powers to help the sept, why not let him profit from easier access to more powerful spirits and lower gauntlet. Also, garou (and supposedly kin with the merit in question) have an easier time meditating for Gnosis renewal when at a caern, so extracting some of the spiritual energies of a caern is not unheard of for garou, either.

                On the other hand, what would be gained if the kin had a sanctum somewhere in the protectorate and would try to communicate with the spirits of his ancestors by summoning them to his sanctum, possibly away from the caern?

                I get that this "don't let them near the caern" mentality was introduced into Werewolf-lore as a general approach to mages. But it isn't explained in-game, iirc, apart from the vague dangers that the mages might harm the caern. So, this practice might be a remanent of time gone by, when mages were the protectors and eventually ruling caste of the human settlements, protecting them during the Impergium and in the ages after that. Does it hold up today? Maybe, maybe not. It comes down to how careful you want to be with the "Don't cause a caern to be violated" tenet of the Litany.

                But there are different approaches. I vaguely remember a story: The Hengeyokai have a similar rule about dragon nests, still there is a story where they decided to have a meeting with all local powers, including eastern vampires, mages, fae and so on, to debate if outsiders (get of fenris in that case) should be granted access to the dragon nest. So they even decided to invite their enemies to a diplomatic gathering, much to the frustration of the get of Fenris.

                So, I guess, there is a bit of different approaches on what exactly handle a mage at a caern and in general how fragile or resilient a caern is. Sure, the mage can't be allowed to exploit the caern, but neither can any garou. But to bar the mage kin from the caern doesn't make too much sense, since he already might know where it is (if he grew up in the sept before he awakened), and might have means to access it anyway, using spirit/correspondence, even from afar.
                This actually brings up a question. One I hesitated to ask initially. Wasn't sure if it belonged in the MTA section or not.

                Is a mages presence pure detrimental to a caern?
                Given what a fully realized spirit sphere can do; could a mage cultivate a caern?
                Could a mage CREATE a caern? Ive read exerpts in which some archmages created their own GODs for pocket realms they created. Sure manipulating a caern for the benefit of garou.

                I understand it would need for the mage to somwhat understand a garous connection too caerns, and properly incorporate this knowledge into their paradigm along the garou's beliefs.

                farfetched, but with potential. Imagine.

                what if: A cabal of mages organize and capture a caern with intention to "enhance" it and the garou using it.

                given the information presented:
                -this could essentially make the cearn useless to wolves. They leave. Boring ending.
                -the mages succeed, supposedly enhancing the caern and garou. As well as likely alienating them from other tribes.
                -the mages fail horribly and warp the caern and tribe. Essentially creating a new BSD offshoot.

                each of these is a viable avenue for story creation. Whether as the misguided mage or home defense werewolf.

                    Originally posted by Devilturnip View Post
                    Is a mages presence pure detrimental to a caern?
                    Not inherently. Mages don't passively suck up a Caern's energy just by being near it. At worst, given how much Garou rely on their relationships with spirits to do everything, a mage could be detrimental passively from that. But if allowed, they could also do lots of beneficial things for the Caern and things that would help appease any spirits not happy about them being around too.

                    Given what a fully realized spirit sphere can do; could a mage cultivate a caern?
                    "Cultivate" is a fairly vague word to address this specifically.

                    Could a mage CREATE a caern?
                    No. A mage could create a Node that is attuned well to the needs of the Garou, and even help attract a spirit to bond with the Node, but the Garou would still have to use their own rituals to sanctify it as a Caern for their purposes. Though IIRC it would make life much easier on the Garou since there should be easier Rites than Caern Building to claim an existing one that's not properly aligned rather than having to go through the process of making one from scratch.

                    -this could essentially make the cearn useless to wolves. They leave. Boring ending.
                    -the mages succeed, supposedly enhancing the caern and garou. As well as likely alienating them from other tribes.
                    -the mages fail horribly and warp the caern and tribe. Essentially creating a new BSD offshoot.
                    These are all rather extreme results. Breaking a Caern to the point of requiring it to be fixed is a lot more likely than breaking it beyond the point of ever being used as a Caern again. Lots of Septs have allied with other supernaturals over the stories in the books to various results; including going well. Alienating whole Tribes because a risky alliance worked on one local spot is a bit much even for how much the Garou default to hating mages. The bond between the Garou and their Caerns isn't so strong that some massive Paradox backlash would cause anything like warping whole Tribes. Besides making them really angry, it wouldn't twist the local Garou in a metaphysical sense. The worst types of Paradox smack the mages, not the target of their magic.
