    Radical (Homid Ahroun)

    I guess at the end we finally get to the one that seems to hew to the most obvious tribal stereotype. Not that this is necessarily a bad thing. But it is kind of the equivalent to the Anarch Terrorist in the Brujah clanbook.

    This one starts out with a quote from Chairman Mao Tse-tung, perpetual favorite of radical chic intellectuals and head of one of the most murderous regimes of the 20th century. Also, ironically from a Werewolf perspective, a man who ordered his regime to essentially wage war against nature itself as part of the Great Leap Forward, causing massive environmental damage within China. And equally ironic from a Black Fury perspective, a man who kept hundreds if not thousands of women as concubines (read sex slaves), some as young as 12. I honestly consider the man to be human garbage on a number of levels.

    Anyway.

    This character killed her abusive father during her first change. (Only physically abusive, thankfully.) Her brothers apparently followed dad's example, so she grew up having to learn how to fight. Depressing, but certainly fitting for a Black Fury's backstory.
    She's a natural born leader, with a lot of inherent charisma and passion for her cause. She's pretty straight forward, what you see is what you get type character, and I think it works pretty well. It's simple, but strong and focused.
    It mentions that she has a fairly narrow world view, and specifically mentions that this is why her Perception is so low. I appreciate this little detail. It also mentions that she feels guilt over her family (I presume what happened during her first change, but possibly also other issues.) I like this, as it offers up a good avenue for future character development and growth.
    Her equipment is political literature, which makes sense; a camera, which feels a little random, but OK; and a light revolver, which also makes sense, I think.

    The art is of a very angry young woman in Glabro form. (Interestingly, her silhouette is used for the Glabro form stat adjustment block on the expanded character sheet.) It's a very good image, one that fits the character very well and feels very in keeping with the game.

    For Attributes, Physicals are secondary, with a 3/3/2 spread. Socials are primary, with 3/4/3. I kind of feel like maybe the 4 should be in Charisma instead of Manipulation. Mental is tertiary, with 1/3/2. The write up discusses the low Perception, which I think works well as an aspect of this character.

    With Abilities, it appears Talents are secondary with three extra dots. 3 each in Brawl and Expression. 2 each in Alertness and Intimidation. 1 each in Athletics and Subterfuge. Skills are primary, but one skill, Leadership, is at 4 (with a specialization in Speeches), and there's only the thirteen dots. There's also two freebies left unspent, so she seems to be missing a dot here. Etiquette is at 3, which feels slightly random, with Drive, Firearms, and Performance at 2 each. I'm not sure what the best place to put that missing dot. Knowledge is tertiary, with 2 dots in Law and 3 in Politics. I feel like this character might benefit from a dot in Medicine and/or Empathy, given how the write up describes how deep commitment to protecting women and children.
    Regarding the Skills, I sort of have this image in my head of her maybe being from an upper class family where everything seemed perfectly fine on the outside, but violent and dysfunctional on the inside. So in between an abusive home life, she was doing all the usually debutant type things like etiquette classes and piano lessons, with plans to go to one of the Ivy's for pre-law. There's nothing in the character write up to indicate any of this. I just think it would be an interesting thing for the character.

    Backgrounds are Allies 3, Contacts 2, and Kinfolk 1. This seems fine. Three of those are bought with freebies. I feel like maybe the Kinfolk is a little unnecessary.

    Rage is 6, Gnosis is 2, and Willpower is 4. That's the second lowest WP of the group. I feel like maybe that dot used in Kinfolk could've been better spent here.

    I rather like this character. Like I said, she's probably the most stereotypical representation of the tribe, but sometimes you need that and I think it works well. She's also a pretty good representation of her auspice, being able to both fight and lead. Overall, I think this is probably the strongest of the bunch.

    So, that's our collection of Black Furies.

    Final Thoughts

    Overall, there aren't any bad ones here. However, while there's nothing really bad about any of them, a lot of them aren't all that great either. I think the Ahroun is the strongest of the group, especially when it comes to representing her auspice. The Galliard is a really fun character, but maybe not the best representation of her tribe or auspice. If we'd had 10 templates, with two of each auspice, I think she would've been a great "outside the box" example to contrast with a more archetypical counterpart. Similarly with the Vagabond and Missionary.

    With that in mind, something occurred to me about half way through this. So consider the following thought experiment: Shuffle around the auspices. Make the Street Rat the Ahroun, the the Vagabond the Philodox, the Missionary the Theurge, the Warsinger the Ragabash, and the Radical the Galliard. It's not entirely perfect, but I think if you did this, you would end up with five characters who all feel like they might be stronger representations of their Auspices.

    As far as alternate template ideas, I would mention the idea of a Freebooter Ragabash tomb raider, except that game and character didn't debut until 1996. I am however a little surprised that we don't have a Wiccan style character, especially given that Brucato was the Mage developer at the time.

    Next will be Ways of the Wolf.

                                    So, setting the Wayback Machine to 1993, and one of Werewolf's earliest releases, Ways of the Wolf. This was the guide to Lupus/wolf-born, something that was a pretty good and useful idea for a game in which a large chunk of your PCs are from a completely non-human background. (Unfortunately, it would be several years til we got something comparable for Metis.)

                                    The book was written by Steve Crow, and the art is credited to Bryon Wackwitz and Dan Smith. However, both have very distinctive styles as well as noticeable signatures, both of which the template art lacks. Comparing with some other books, this appears to be Josh Timbrook, once again not being credited. I can imagine that this must have been a continuing annoyance for him.

                                    Because this is 1st ed, it uses a different Renown system, with scores in the hundreds. I'm rather glad 2nd ed decided to streamline this. Also, none of them have spent their Freebie Points. So I'm going to mention some thoughts about spending them.

                                    Classic Trickster (Wendigo Ragabash)

                                    So, no pop culture/media quotes for any of these. I'm almost a little surprised.

                                    No indication is given of the character's gender, so I'm just going to use "they".

                                    Back in 1st ed, the No Moons were pretty heavily presented as tricksters/pranksters. So naturally this leans into that. They originate from the Mexican-American border region, one of the last few survivors of a pack. Between this and the fact that the Wendigo stronghold from Book of Caerns was in Arizona, I find it kind of interesting how much 1st ed material focused on the tribe in the region. (Still sad we never got a Rage Across Alaska book.)

                                    The character's sire, the other last survivor of their original group, was killed by a hunter's trap that they managed to avoid. You'd think this sort of loss might drive one to their First Change, but ... well, the writing is unclear. It just says that later a pair of Garou found them and guided them through their Rite of Initiation. Because of their "Amerindian origins among the Wendigo tribe" they look upon themselves as the personification of Coyote, and play the role of trickster to the fullest. They are are tolerated by their pack, but not well liked.

                                    I have mixed feelings about this sort of presentation of No Moons. I get the idea that trickster style characters are often a bit annoying to others, and I think 1st ed Ragabash sometimes ran the risk of veering into FishMalk territory. But looking elsewhere in the book, it seems to really double down on the "Ragabash are considered the trash Auspice by all Lupus" thing. In hindsight, I'm very glad that later material would go more toward the Questioner of the Ways concept and just what that could mean for different characters.

                                    It's mentioned that the character's Mentor is another Ragabash in their pack. That should probably say Sept, and I'll chalk it up to it being 1st ed and certain aspects of the setting and how things work still being hammered out. Also, the character's Allies are other Ragabash. I'm not entirely wild about that. There's nothing technically wrong with it, I guess. But I feel like it would've been a chance to do something much more interesting, like having some sort of minor trickster spirit as an ally.

                                    They character isn't a coward, but prefers to pick their fights, using stealth and cunning. It says this is unusual for a Lupus. I dislike this phrasing. Wolves pretty much survive via cunning and teamwork. Lupus characters should, IMO at least, be fairly straight forward and somewhat less complicated than Homids, but not to the point of being thick headed.

                                    No equipment is listed.

                                    The image is of a wolf. It looks fine. Although the apparently laughing look on its face might be a little much.

                                    With Attributes, Physicals are tertiary, with an even 2/2/2 spread. Socials are primary, with 4/4/2. And Mental is secondary, with 3/2/3. Even if it is a little stereotypical for a trickster character, I do appreciate seeing a Lupus with such high Socials.

                                    For Abilities, Talents are primary. We have a 3 in Dodge, 2 each in Primal Urge and Subterfuge, and 1 each in Alertness, Athletics, Brawl, Empathy, Expression, and Streetwise. Skills are secondary, with a 3 in Stealth, and 2 each in Animal Ken, Performance, and Survival. Knowledge is tertiary, with a 3 in Enigmas, and 1 each in Investigation and Rituals.
                                    This seems like a mostly solid spread of abilities. Obviously, you'd probably want to bump a few of them up with freebies. I'd probably like to have Alertness, Athletics, Brawl and perhaps Investigation up to 2. Maybe take Subterfuge up to 3 to further the trickster thing, and perhaps Dodge and Stealth to 4.

                                    Backgrounds are 1 each in Mentor and Allies, as detailed in the write up. This was back when Wendigo only got two background points to start. Thankfully that changed with 2nd ed. Personally, I'd go with Mentor 2 or 3 to represent an older Ragabash who has a position among whatever Sept the character's pack is associated with. As for the remaining points, there's a number of possibilities. I already mentioned the idea of some sort of minor trickster spirit having attached itself to the character (a raccoon or raven or or even Will o' the Wisp type thing perhaps). Past Life might also work. The book has an interesting fetish called a Tool Stick that a trickster could get up to all sorts of trouble with (especially if you decided to spend a couple of freebies on a dot in Repair).

                                    Rage, Gnosis, and Willpower are all the default for Auspice (1), Breed (5), and Tribe (5, it would be 4 in late editions). I might spend a dot or two on WP, but the rest are fine, even if the write up suggests raising Rage (again, this is when it was mainly a power stat and how it might impact role playing wasn't as bit a consideration).

                                    So, overall, this isn't a bad character. I do like seeing a Wendigo that isn't bitter and angry. But at the same time, it's kind of the lowest of low hanging fruit, and may be considered to lean a little into an ethnic stereotype. It's fine. Not bad; not great. Just OK.

                                                I get this idea also that in the early editions of the series there was this idea why the Ragabash would be the de facto Omega/Whipping Wolf of the pack (which may also be part of the Bone Gnawer-Ragabash connection that also gets made) but eventually some combination of learning about how the imperfections of those (in)famous wolf studies, how annoying it could be as a player to be the de facto “your friends have permission to shit on you now” class, and the aforementioned risks of having a class be defined as Trickster/Prankster (an archetype so fundamental it should be represented but is unavoidably going to flirt with the excesses of FishMalk/Chaotic-Dumb behavior).

                                                I agree with those above, the Classic Trickster character is fine, but also kinda boring and uninspiring. Yeah there should be characters like this but it doesn’t pop as a sample character. It also suffers from early edition confusion about what the Ragabash is/does.

                                                The question of how a Lupus Ragabash ought to be compared and contrasted with a Homid Ragabash is interesting though. Off the top of my head, the first classic notion is that the Lupus will simply not get the first premises of human society and will constantly be forcing homids to try to explain themselves (and therefore interrogate things they may accept as givens). I imagine older Lupus Ragabash might actually go so far as to take on ultra-human personas to further highlight the absurdities of human conventions, whether they do that in the most vulgar slapstick fashion or if they’re painfully dry satirists.

                                                Other archetypes for the Lupus Ragabash might be the Garou who best seek to embody the wolf tactics which honor crazed humans might reject; stealth, stalking, ambush, overwhelming force, etc. and applying those fundamentals of hunting to other facets of Garou life. I do also think there ought to be room for Lupus Ragabash who do adopt the whelp/omega type social status and use their low rage and low standing as a means of giving their fellow Garou implicit permission to relax and concern themselves with unimportant matters (such as what happened to all of their shoelaces and what that has to do with the strange buzzing outside…).

                                                    Dark Mystic (Shadow Lord Theurge)

                                                    This one was born to a small wolf pack eking out an existence in the Louisiana bayous. As I mentioned before, I have always just sort of taken it as a given that because of certain Garou efforts, there are small populations of wolves scattered about in hidden out of the way corners of places like the Rockies, the Adirondacks, the Scottish Highlands, and so forth.
                                                    Also, I've always sort of had this impression that the North American Silver Fangs have tended to be more dominant along the East Coast, New England, the Midwest, and Great Lakes, as well as eastern Canada, while the Shadow Lords have tended to be more dominant in places like Mexico, the Southeast, the Southwest, and the West Coast. I suspect this template was part of the start of that idea.

                                                    This was back when the Shadow Lords were presented as being fixated on acquiring power. (They were sort of the Werewolf equivalent to the Tremere, with the Silver Fangs as the Ventrue. Because the early WoD material tended to keep hitting a lot of the same notes.) This one is primarily interested in mystical and spiritual power, especially some of the forbidden secrets of the Wyrm so that they can be turned against it.

                                                    This isn't a bad idea for a character. I can't help but wonder if this is where the idea of the Bringers of Light camp got its start. I think it even holds up with the later material where the tribe was more focused on the idea of ruthless pragmatism rather than just power for power's sake.

                                                    The character won't yield to the Wyrm, but it does say that what it considers "yielding" and what its pack mates might consider "yielding" may not always agree. They're an outsider, even within their own pack. They're willing to sacrifice a pack mate "for the greater good", but only as a last resort.
                                                    I feel like this is maybe a smidge stereotypical for a Shadow Lord, but I think it can work well as a starting place, with the player getting to role-play the character's gradual growth and stronger integration into the pack, to the point where they're strongly loyal to them.

                                                    Part of the write up is eaten up by a discussion of why the character has Talents as their primary abilities, as well as about their Backgrounds. The Background discussion doesn't bother me very much, but I feel like the Talents justification is word count that could've been better spent on something else.

                                                    The image is another wolf, this one mostly darker colored and with a much more serious and grim look on its face. I rather like it.

                                                    Physicals are secondary, with a 2/3/3 split. Socials are tertiary, all at 2. I do enjoy seeing a Shadow Lord who isn't socially focused. The write up does mention this, saying they prefer to use strength and cunning to overcome opposition. Mental is primary, with 3/3/4.

                                                    For Abilities, Talents are primary. The write up acts like this is some sort of weird outlier for Theurges, saying that the character needs those abilities to maintain their fighting edge as a Garou and handle the political intricacies of the Shadow Lords. I find that justification unnecessary. Honestly, unless you are using secondary abilities, it's actually impossible for a Lupus to spend 13 points on Knowledge, as they've access to only four of those abilities, which would max out at three prior to spending freebies. I just sort of presume that most Lupus are going to have Talents or Skills as primary.
                                                    Anyway, said Talents are 2 each in Brawl, Empathy, Intimidation, Primal Urge, and Subterfuge, and 1 each in Alertness, Athletics, and Expression. I'm maybe mildly surprised by the Empathy. It doesn't seem entirely keeping with the character as presented, but I think it does offer a potential avenue for the character growth arc I mentioned early.
                                                    Skills are secondary, with 3 in Leadership, 2 each in Stealth and Survival, and 1 each in Animal Ken and Melee. I'm not sure that this high degree of Leadership fits with the way the character is described as an outsider who lurks on the edge of the pack. I'm also not entirely sure Melee is needed. Maybe raise Animal Ken to 2 and shift a point from Leadership into Stealth?
                                                    Knowledge is tertiary, with 3 in Occult and 1 each in Enigmas and Rituals. That seems fine. Obviously, you'd want to raise Enigmas and Rituals a bit with Freebies.
                                                    (Alternately, I think you could switch Skills and Knowledge, having those three Knowledges all at 3, and having the Skills be Stealth and Survival at 2 and either Animal Ken or Leadership at 1, and raising those with freebies later.)

                                                    In 1st ed, Shadow Lords got four Background points. These have been allotted 1 each in Fetish, Past Life, Resources, and Rituals (what would thankfully be renamed Rites in 2nd ed so we didn't have a Background and Knowledge of the same name). According the the write up, the Resources are supposed to represent a small power base they've built up among mortals (Minor Quibble: that really should say "humans", as "mortals" is the sort of term I'd expect to be used in a Vampire book). I'm left asking myself why? Nothing about this character indicates any sort of interest in human society or in acquiring power within it. If anything, this would seem to be a distraction from their primary focus on uncovering mystical power within the Umbra and among the spirits. I feel like this is here only because the character is a Shadow Lord and so is somehow expected to be involved in such things.
                                                    The others are explained as "the mystical powers you command as a Theurge." That's fine, but I feel like you would be better picking one or two to specialize in. The character has no equipment listed (none of them do), so I've no idea what Fetish this character might have. I'd honestly have gone with 2 each in Rituals/Rites and Past Life. Under 2nd ed rules, where you'd have five points, I'd probably put that last one in Rites. Alternately, I might could be sold on the idea of putting it into Contacts, to represent that the character has a single small inroad into the world of human mysticism that they've not been able to fully cultivate as much as they'd like.

                                                    The rest of the stats are all as their defaults. Rage 2, Gnosis 5, and Willpower 3. I'd probably spent some freebies to raise WP to 5.

                                                    So that is our Theurge. I mostly like this character. I think the concept is pretty good, and fits the Auspice and the Tribe, while also feeling like it has a good general Lupus mentality. However, I think it needs some tweaking with the stats, and would benefit from a cleaner write up (and some of the later material about its Tribe).
