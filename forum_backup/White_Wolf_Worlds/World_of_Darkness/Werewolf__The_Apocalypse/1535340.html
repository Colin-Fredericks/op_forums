    W5 videogame/text-adventure "Book Of Hungry Names" - Review/First Impressions

    05-09-2024, 08:03 AM
    So, the bad news first... I nearly finished the game but then a recent patch nuked the save, so now I have to start all over again...

    But I think I can at least offer some impressions of that first run and how the setting and the characters are portrayed.
    • Setting/Lore: Like the supplements from Renegade, the descriptions of the setting couldn't remain as unspecific as the corebook but I also don't think this is the exact same take on the W5 corebook as the Renegade-supplements' and it also doesn't stay wholly true to what's laid out in the corebook. It's a setting where the Pack is the most complex level of social organisation left and there are various Garou around who don't even have a Pack but act independently instead. There's a Sept/caern which is part of the main-story but it's a fallen one. The Umbra is dangerous although not because Garou can't survive in it but because all the spirits either are corrupted by the Wyrm, want nothing to do with Garou, are angry at Garou for the state of the world or the Spirits in question have just gone insane since the Gaia's Howl event. Or for example, since I chose a Silver Fang, one Garou mentions that in this continuity this Garou heard of this rumor that the last Silver Fang with prominent ancestry died in the early 90s in Russia and since then the standards for joining the Silver Fangs have been loosened. That's already more lore than what you get from the corebook. Oh, also you meet a Drone in the story (the Weaver-equivalent of a Fomor). Something else the W5 corebook didn't adequately cover.
    • Tribes: The way it's portrayed it really is more about describing a Garou who has made a pact with a particular Tribe-Patron-Spirit. And because of the sort of people who can make a Pact with a certain Patron-Spirit, stereotypes for these Tribes emerge. But it's not like the Tribe is treated like some sort of other social organisation within the Garou Nation. But the game also mentions Patron-Spirits at the heart of caerns (which the corebook doesn't really cover despite having this caern-management-system) and there's talk of Pack-guardian-spirits (in other words, Pack-Patron-Spirits).
    • Themes: What I appreciate about the game is that it certainly has a more nuanced take on the W5 themes than the W5 corebook has. You still have the Age of Apocalypse and the fallen Garou-Nation but the focus is much more on charting a new course and what that new course looks like. For that reason, you get these trackers with opposing ideas of what the new priorities are.
    • It goes like this: "The Litany has failed - Rebuild the Litany", "Humans must pay - Humans must understand", "Our weapon is Rage - Our Answer is Spirit", "I Just Want to Survive - We can still win", "Find purpose through dignity - Find courage through laughter.". If you were to take a look at the W5 corebook, the authors kinda have made up their minds already on some of those matters without leaving it to the people at the table to decide how to approach these things.
    • Garou-characterization: In my first playthrough, I focused on building connections with the Garou and ignore the humans and I gotta say... horrible decision. There isn't a single Garou I would call likeable. The main-issue is that this game really hammers home just how debilitating the whole Rage-thing is. These Garou are always super-combative with each other and ready to start arguments at nearly every opportunity. Especially the Pack you start out with... Man, what a bunch of assholes. The game starts with you killing some Wyrm-spawn but then it turns out that it was just some riding-animal for a Fomor or something and the Garou-Elders in your Pack immediately mock your killing and declare that since it was the mount it doesn't count. That's the other thing: The whole Elder-thing is often used by Garou to lord that over younger Garou. But Elder also just means a Garou who's older than you. So there's situations where one Garou-character acts like an Elder-asshole to you but then when an even older Garou shows up they then act like an asshole to the Garou who was just being an asshole to you.
    • But also that initial Pack are such assholes because the leader of that Pack additionally starts eating the Wyrmspawn and talks about how the Litany is a waste of time. And then he immediately gets sick and starts dying. I mean... hell of a way to introduce W5 Garou...
    • Cult of Fenris: So they're described as Nazis but it also a bit confused because it's described that one of the Fenrir was wearing a Vegan Reich shirt. It felt more like this hodgepodge of all kinds of different kinds of conservative extremism. What the dialogue focused on more, though, was the Fenrir talking about how they're the true Garou Nation and all the other Garou are too weak to get the job done. But the politics were there more as aesthetics. Well, it all felt a bit weird. Like in one instance where one Garou said "You know, I'm not one of those Garou who's racist towards Bone Gnawers."

              Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
              So, basically most garou are just doing whatever, the Fenrir are Conservative and Gaia's Howl is a thing.

              Does the setting feels like something you could play in, compared to W5?
              The setting feels better than the one outlined in the W5 corebook. And it feels more substantive as well. But in its street-level-only outlook, I really couldn't help but think that WtF has the better premise for that sort of thing. So, the essential structure is that you start with this Pack of Garou who are assholes and they fuck up. You go looking for help and that's how you stumble into this bigger story-arc of reawakening a fallen caern. But you're also collecting members for your own/a new Pack along the way. That's the story-structure. And one of these instances is about a harvesting-spirit (that has gone insane because of Gaia's Howl) holding a rural town hostage which has become a weird cult. And one of the Garou has been caught up in this. But all I could think about was that this would work better as a WtF-premise. You're better off doing that in WtF than in WtA.

              There's also a similar issue I have with H5: The horizon is just so limited. Okay, you restored the Sept/caern and... now what? W5 has no answer for you on that front. The same way that anything beyond being a local hunter-cell is unimaginable in H5. Because the issue is that all this stuff in the setting got torn down in H5 and W5 but it doesn't feel like these two corebooks imagine any players successfully rebuilding stuff or go beyond the limitations of their origins. "It's the Age of Apocalypse - but remember that the world is already doomed to die." or "It's the Age of Reckoning - but remember that any gathering of mortal hunters who want to kill supernatural monsters that goes beyond a local hunter-cell is corrupt and fundamentally bad.". Neither game is very good at justifying the outlined struggle of prospective PCs within the setting.

                                                          A few points I would like to make in regards to this:
                                                          • Clay doesn't mock the Litany when eating the Fomori at the start of the game, you do. He claims "The First Share of the Kill For the Greatest in Station" at which point your Garou goes "That probably was applicable back during hunter-gatherer days but what the fuck does that matter now?" Clay gets poisoned because he is strictly adhering to the Litany without thought. Cuz y'know, Elder.
                                                          • W5 does mention patron spirits of Caerns on page 194. It doesn't specifically mention they need to Tribal-related, because they don't, but there's no reason you couldn't have them be an aspect of a larger Incarnae. Likewise Pack Patrons can be done through the Rite of Patronage just 10 pages before. Considering the Patronage bonuses are all 1-die and a Ban, they are perhaps the easiest thing in the world to Homebrew if you'd want to.
                                                          • In regards to:
                                                            It goes like this: "The Litany has failed - Rebuild the Litany", "Humans must pay - Humans must understand", "Our weapon is Rage - Our Answer is Spirit", "I Just Want to Survive - We can still win", "Find purpose through dignity - Find courage through laughter.". If you were to take a look at the W5 corebook, the authors kinda have made up their minds already on some of those matters without leaving it to the people at the table to decide how to approach these things.
                                                          • Most of these are just Chronicle Tenants and a lot of them are just example ones from W5, or related to it's stated themes. These are definitely covered and supported by the book.
                                                          • Your starting pack being assholes Is The Point yeah. They're old bastards lamenting that they lost and all their old glories are gone to the past and taking it out on you by proxy. There's a reason your new pack reacts to them by going "hey fuck this guy". Though I had a pretty interesting mentor/student relationship with Black Tarn as a Theurge.
                                                          • I personally can't agree on the new pack being unlikable. Elton, Podge, Melodie, and Nim were all incredibly charming to me. Like yeah they argue and get heated about it which has been a core Garou thing since I believe the inception of Garou as a Thing. But it's not like there's debilitating infighting, just ideological disagreements. Everyone still works together and obviously likes each other.
                                                          • For the record I did what you did too, I played a Silver Fang Theurge with a focus on maxing my Honor out as much as possible and a character focused mainly on rebuilding a New Nation. It was an incredibly satisfying playstyle and I'm really glad I went through it to the end, even if I'm a bit mad that I couldn't replicate a decision I made due to the aforementioned auto-save glitch (which according to patch notes is now fixed! Yipee).
