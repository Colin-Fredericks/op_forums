                How the Cult was handled was one of the most frustrating things about this, made even worse by outside info like the behind-the-scenes stuff with Karim, or how they've been handled in other W5 supplements. When the character is first told about them, there's a very brief mention that "They're not all white supremacists, but the ones we have around here are" then proceed to refer to most of their idols, their spirits, the tribe at large, etc as some form of "Nazi" or "fash", with the latter word or "fascist" being so overused in the book I thought I may have slipped into someone's social media ramblings whenever I was in a Cult section of the book. Barely-a-spoiler:
                You have the opportunity to call Ratatoskr a Nazi as one of the 'humorous' options.
                It's also as if disproving the somewhat-joking, somewhat-serious sentiment from people seeing the Cult in the corebook and thinking they're the only ones being competent and fighting the Wyrm was mandatory. They're utterly incompetent here and don't even have Hauglosk to semi-excuse them, and (going into spoiler territory if people care) do such things as:
                Starting a supervillain monologue about your pack's sudden but inevitable betrayal (then getting killed while monologuing), messing up a completely unrelated info-gathering mission of yours *that might help their goal* just because, working hand-in hand with the Wyrm instead of it being more like the Wyrm taking advantage of their blind hatred, revering an idol that reads 'Hitler' but not knowing it was of some female student with the same last name,
                and so on. And as they are intertwined with the plot, these sort of things crop up often.

                You do get the opportunity to try to infiltrate them or work with them at some point, but it's not very well done even if you do it correctly. With the former, I had an interesting page that mentioned, "It helps that you're white" (as you can pick your char's ethnicity) while some random neo-nazi lady screamed slurs at my character to see if he was hip enough to be told about the Cult's plans, then giggled and let me in the know when he successfully didn't react. I really wish that there would be some W5-related media that doesn't just do the "FASH" thing with the Cult idea or just make them BSD-lite for some reason, and would actually provide some interesting scenarios about luring Garou with the promise of doing something when most of the Garou are bitter and moping about, instead of the Cult just being ridiculous villains. If there was any book that could have capitalized on the concept, it's this one, where most of the Garou are acting exactly that way when starting out. Instead, it's just making things somewhat uncomfortable, like Knightingale said, where falling to Hauglosk (or the general idea of being a fanatic since it's not name-dropped anywhere in this) automatically means you're white-supremacist or adjacent.

                                                    I think the W5 premise is just stumbling over itself in that regard. Because without the fascism-bit, you would have the Cult who say the other Garou are weak and they're the only true Garou around and then, well, the other Garou in question believe the Garou have fucked up, the Garou-Nation has fallen and Gaia's dead/dying. That doesn't feel like there's much of a disagreement there. But the Cult of Fenris at least has a take on the "So now what?"-question. Meanwhile, with the good Garou it's all a bit up in the air as to what the answer to "So now what?" actually is.

                                                    It would all work better if there actually were some sort of alternative to the Cult, like "The Cult thinks the Garou should do this but they're wrong because we should actually do this instead.". But what we've seen in the W5 works so far is that the good Garou reclaim caerns, deal with spirits, try to earn Renown, try to destroy Pentex-facilities or other kind of Wyrm-minion-holdouts... I mean, yeah, no shit, that's what Garou have already been doing in the past. It's not like the idea to hold and protect caerns, make pacts with spirits and try to take down Wyrm-stuff are grand revelations to Garou-kind. And yet according to the W5 premise this has gotten the Garou precisely nowhere. There's a finality to the failure of the Garou in W5 you didn't have before.
