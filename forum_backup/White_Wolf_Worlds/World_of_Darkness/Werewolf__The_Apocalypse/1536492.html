    W5 Storyteller's Kit - Review

    05-29-2024, 07:01 AM
    Renegade have been releasing these little Storyteller's Kit booklets alongside Storyteller-screens. 10 bucks for the pdf, 30 bucks for the physical Storyteller Screen and the booklet in physical form. Now the thing with these is that the booklet itself isn’t worth 10 bucks in the case of H5. Because what the booklet leads with is a section on example-actions (and the associated rolls) and a description of the advanced combat-options. Both these things are for the most part copy-pastes of the material in the Advanced Rules pdf - which you can get for free! Originally the idea even had been to continuously update and expand that Advanced Rules pdf but hey, wringing a couple more bucks out of people is more important, I guess. But the booklet also has some extra material and a short introductory Chronicle. In the case of H5, the whole thing is really not worth 10 bucks because the extra material is just a list of VERY short descriptions for 50 NPCs and then you got the short Chronicle.

    The W5 Storyteller’s Kit is a little bit more substantial, though.

    The first thing it adds is a section on Brutal Outcomes. Just as a refresher: You have your Rage-Dice that replace normal dice in your dice-pool. Roll two 1s or 2 on those Rage-dice and you get a Brutal Outcome. What is a Brutal Outcome? Well, according to the W5 corebook pg 133: “A Brutal outcome causes a test to fail (usually with something being wrecked, harmed, or destroyed), unless the aim of the test was to cause damage or injury, in which case a Brutal outcome yields four additional successes.” Here’s the first surprise: This Storyteller Kit released by Renegade has a different take on this.

    On one hand, it adds a bit more nuance than “causes the test to fail” by talking about how Brutal Outcomes should be about adding consequences and making things more interesting. It isn’t about completely obstructing the player’s activities, though, and Storytellers should be careful about that. Thank you! Would’ve been nice to see that mentioned in the Corebook… And here’s what it also says: “Brutal outcomes can generally be treated as critical successes, particularly in a combat situation.” No “four additional successes”, just an uncomplicated “Treat it as a critical success.”.

    Then you get a list of physical, social and mental actions and examples of what it could mean to get Brutal Outcomes. Like, for example: “Sneaking (Stealth): The Garou’s Rage is so great that as they attempt to move silently past a guard, they cannot resist the need to growl threateningly in the direction of the perceived threat.” Good and helpful to have that list. Some are a bit weird, though, like using the Computer-Skill and Observation:
    • “Observation (Awareness): The item being watched is destroyed, or person attacked and injured. Alternatively, the location the Garou is observing from is damaged and rendered useless, forcing them to try a new approach.” That one feels a bit like saying “Your Rage has caused Schrödinger’s Cat to be 100% dead.”
    • “Snooping through Digital Files (Investigation or Technology): The Garou becomes so incensed at what they find that they start deleting whole batches of files, causing an internal firewall or other security system to trip and lock them out.” Okay, weird. Your frustration leads you to… delete the evidence…?

    And then there’s a general one:
    • “Any Situation: The Garou screams or howls in frustration, unleashing their Rage upon the ears of every creature within 2–3 km (1–2 miles), drawing lots of attention.” That one’s odd because of the distance. It felt odd when I read it and I mean, just some quick googling reveals that it’s completely wrong. The editor really slipped up on that one to not fact-check this bit. So in a scenario where it’s a wolf-howl, it can be heard up to 10 (!) miles in open terrain. If it’s a human scream, it can be heard from around half a mile to close to a mile (the sources on Google differ). But with background noises, it’s more like 100 meters. That would be a hell of a scream if you could hear it from three kilometers away!

    The next bit is a section on Spirits. There’s a table with twenty examples of what flavor of personality you could give to a Spirit. And then you get a long list of 43 Spirits. What’s a bit weird is you got Tribe-Patron-Spirits like the “Avatar of Gorgon” or the “Avatar of the North Wind” – but you don’t get the full list of all the Tribes. For example, Falcon gets listed once as an Animal Spirit and then you get the “Avatar of Falcon” as the Tribe-Patron-Spirit. But Owl is only listed as an Animal Spirit. And Griffin isn’t mentioned at all. Meanwhile, Lunes are listed – even though they already got an entry in the corebook (and it isn't a copy-paste as well, it's new text). So what they chose to put into this section was a bit odd. But other than that, it’s a really helpful and good addition to what you get from the corebook.

    Then you get a couple of new Talismans as well as a Table that lists the Background-costs of all. There are seven new ones. Not only that but you get two story-hooks for how you could introduce these into a game or an event you could play out related to that particular Talisman. Another good addition.

    Then you get an adventure called “What the waters hid”. Overall, a good adventure, I’d say. The premise is that a lake is being drained thanks to climate change and a nefarious corporation (which isn’t affiliated with Pentex, though) in California. A Pack made a pact with the lake-spirit in the past to hide all their kills. So they’ve been dumping all the bodies of their enemies in that lake for decades. Now that the lake is slowly being drained, the spirit is getting angry (while also dying) and that leads to the danger of revealing all the bodies that the lake-spirit had hidden away.

    Now the weird part about this adventure is that in this case, it’s clear that the main-reason for why the Garou are losing is simply a lack of numbers. There’s one Pack that’s been occupying the caern in this region since the 80s. But since then the Garou of the Pack have been dying one by one. And that then leaves in the early 00s with only two remaining Garou. One of them suffers from Harano and retreats into the mountains to become a hermit which leaves only one active Garou. And according to the adventure, there haven’t been any other Garou in the area until your PC-Pack arrives in the area in the present day.

    The enemy is a bit of an odd one. It’s the director of the corporation. His parents were killed by a werewolf when he was a child, then he grows up, joins the FBI and that’s when he’s able to figure out the existence of werewolves and that they have a society. He’s then fired from the FBI for being a little too obsessive in his hunt for werewolves. Then he somehow becomes the director of this evil, greedy corporation who somehow are also aware of them having a “werewolf problem” and their stuff being sabotaged by werewolves. But it isn’t tied to Pentex… for whatever reason. Seems like there are lot of people around in this adventure who are completely aware of werewolves existing…

    He’s tied to the adventure in a particular way. Hmm, not sure about how my players will react to the twist towards the end but it isn’t too bad of a twist, I think.

    Overall, I think it's a good addition to the corebook. The bit on Brutal Outcomes and the long list of Spirits as well as new Talismans are helpful additions. In this case, I think it's actually worth it to get this as a W5 ST. Well, I mean, relatively speaking... It's Renegade-pricing, of course. All their stuff has above average pricing for their books.

      I just realized I forgot something important: One of the Spirits listed in this book is called "Elder Garou" and in the Manifestations/Possessions-section it says: "The Garou are blessed in that they can summon the spirits of their spiritual ancestors for guidance and with knowing that their faith and belief can put them in contact with the voices of those that came before. An Elder Garou chooses hosts depending on its mood towards whomever summoned it: they may appear as a stern teacher when a harsh lesson must be learned, or as a kind friend when aid is required. In the Umbra they may reveal themselves in the form of a patron spirit considered sacred to them, or as a spiritual wolf shrouded in mists from beyond the grave."

      There have been references to Ancestor-Spirits before in other W5 supplements and here's where that concept now officially gets reintroduced. And it's in the form of turning them into Spirits you can summon via the usual methods as described in the W5 corebook. It's a a bit messy when it comes to the worldbuilding of the W5 corebook but well, here it is. Ancestor Spirits are now truly a thing again in W5.
