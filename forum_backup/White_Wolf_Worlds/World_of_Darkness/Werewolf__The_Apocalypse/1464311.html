    The Dunsirn as a setting element in Werewolf

    10-18-2021, 01:21 AM
    As many of you know, the Dunsirn is sub-family within the vampire clan of Giovanni. Introduced in the original Clanbook Giovanni, the Dunsirn were given ties to Werewolf by making these Scottish cannibal bankers into lost Fianna kinfolk that still occasionally gave birth to (ronin) Garou who were killed by the rest of the Dunsirn if discovered. They would be occasionally mentioned in other sourcebooks. In Victorian Age vampire one of the sourcebooks tied them into one of the precursor organizations of the Pretanic Order. So there is plenty to play with for an ST wanting to use them in a Werewolf chronicle. This thread is to discuss how we can use that.

    What information we're given suggests to me that the Dunsirn were originally Pictish kinfolk to the White Howlers, and were among the kinfolk incorporated by the Scottish Fianna when they drove out most of the Black Spiral Dancers. However, the Garou born by these kinfolk often descended into despicable behavior like man-eating. This man-eating was carried on by their kinfolk in secret too. Some weren't caught, but others were. It's even possible that they still practiced other blasphemous rites in secret dating back to the corruption of the White Howlers. But these were likely debased rites since they weren't in contact with the Black Spiral Dancers. Eventually the Fianna caught on that there was something wrong with a branch of the kinfolk and purged them, slaughtering all that they knew, but some survived in secret.

    Now the Dunsirn as a concept seem to have two inspirations. One is the Scottish banking traditions that started in the 18th century. The other is the legend of Sawney Bean that supposedly happened in the mid-17th century. So it's possible that the mid-1600s is when the Scottish Fianna caught on and exposed the crimes of these kinfolk, Some secret survivors though showed up in Edinburgh to start over, and this is when they started using the name Dunsirn to hide their identity. Before that, they used other names.

    These Dunsirn presumably pooled together their resources (probably including some of the loot stolen from victims they robbed before they ate them per the Sawney Bean legend) and created a money lending organization (it would not have originally been a bank, but some 17th century merchants did engage in short term credit). Now that they were driven off the land by the Fianna, they would make their living by money lending. They slowly built up their bank, and by sometime after the Act of Union in 1707 it was one of the more successful banks in Edinburgh which was now becoming the second most important financial center of the United Kingdom after London. It's likely that the lack of Dunsirn ties to the now deposed Stuart Dynasty put them in good stead with London. That would give the Dunsirn an advantage over their rivals once free trade with England was made as well as in the new colonial trade with the Americas.

      The first Clanbook Giovanni does not give us a time when the Giovanni approached the Dunsirn, but the revised clanbook tells us it was during the early 1700s. We know is that it was the Dunsirn that enabled the Giovanni to break into commerce with North America, and as such the Dunsirn are one of the most heavily favored of the other families. Scottish banking really didn't take off until later in the 1700s so we should interpret “early 1700s” liberally. Scotland's first bank (originally chartered as a monopoly) was only in 1695, and its second in 1727. If we want to keep to real history as much as we can, any actual Dunsirn bank would need to postdate that. To give the Dunsirn enough time to establish themselves as very successful I'll put a date around 1740 as a good time for Granddaddy Dunsirn to be made into a vampire. The Golden Age of Scottish banking was yet to come, but the Dunsirn vampires would be in a great position to benefit from it.

      The Giovanni don't seem to know about Dunsirn ties to the Lupines (this was mentioned in an OOC section of the clanbook), but are aware of their cannibalism – but it's not clear if this was known before the first Dunsirn was embraced or after. The Dunsirn's banking skill seems to be of more importance than gruesome practices related to the dead (we're told the Dunsirn aren't interested in Necromancy at all).

      We also know that the Dunsirn are supposed to have hedge magic associated with them. The first clanbook says other Giovanni vampires speculate that the Dunsirn have ties with the Verbena, but it's probably more to do with secret knowledge retained from BSD kinfolk practices of Malfean origin. So while they may know some standard Paths of Sorcery, there may be some Infernalist paths as well. We're supposed to believe that the Dunsirn were such a prized jewel that Augustus agreed to give them various concessions for joining the family. So the Dunsirn have to be more than just skilled bankers. It was their other occult skills gave them leverage. If they were simply ordinary mortals, I don't see how the Dunsirn could even begin to “negotiate”. Granddaddy Dunsirn just would have been embraced. So I think this sorcery and lore must have been the reason for the Giovanni's interest.

      In the Victorian Age Companion we're told that there was a Dunsirn involved in the Two Princes of Tiernmas secret society. That society seems to be the source of the mysterious “Scotsmen” that joined the Order of Oriental Fellowship mentioned in one of the chapters of the Chronicle of the Black Labyrinth. This would have been about a century and a half after a Dunsirn first became a vampire. So the family clearly had some knowledge of Black Spiral Dancer (“Pretanic”) occult practices.

          In the first clanbook, the Dunsirn appear to have been one of the more successful families outside the Giovanni itself, especially for a non-Italian one. It said there were around 100 Dunsirn vampires, but this has to be bad writing. Metro Edinburgh population is less than a million, and Scotland's entire population would have been around 5.5 million when the clanbook came out. Even the United Kingdom as a whole would have been around 55 million. That would have made the Dunsirn one of the largest vampire clans in the UK even assuming some Dunsirn dwelled elsewhere. But this is common for the book which tends to put far larger numbers of Giovanni vampires than can be reasonably accounted for (even the “few” vampires who are married into the Giovanni are said to be around 50!) Scotland is supposed to be a bastion of Toreador power, particularly Edinburgh. The Giovanni clan shouldn't be much of a factor there.

          Personally I would put no more than 5 Dunsirn vampires in Scotland with a few more in England or elsewhere. There's probably no more than 10 or 12 Dunsirn vampires in the entire world. Different STs will have different interpretations of their numbers, but it makes sense there should be relatively few Dunsirn vampires. The eldest Dunsirn is not quite 300 years old yet. That would make him an elder in North America, but perhaps not in Europe.

          I have atheory why the Dunsirn hunt and kill any Lupines born to them. They don't want to alert the Scottish Fianna that they survived their earlier purge. If their Lupine children live, the secret will get out and the Fianna will want to wipe them out. Still, some “Ronin” (actually Lost Cubs) had to escape. They may have joined the Fianna or been slain by them depending on the exact circumstances. But by the revised clanbook we're told that some Dunsirn estates have been raided by Lupines.

          Now not all Dunsirn may know that the family has vampires. They may be ignorant or attribute it to family legend. So it's not a given that a Lost Cub Dunsirn has anything of value to tell the Fianna. Though if they exhibit any signs of man-eating, the Fianna might kill them outright. But there may be enough information gathered – whether about cannibalism or vampirism or something else they find suspicious – that would convince them to begin “raiding” Dunsirn estates. I am interpreting “raiding” to be more like information gathering attempts rather than outright attacks. The Scottish Fianna first want to establish what exactly the Dunsirn are up to. They likely have not yet connected them to a kinfolk branch wiped out almost four hundred years ago. But it is conceivable that will happen at some point.

              So what do the Dunsirn offer as antagonists?

              We have an extremely wealthy and influential family. They probably control several major banks all around the world, but the most important is the one in Scotland (in my chronicles I choose to name this the Bank of Lothian). There's plenty of opportunities for Dunsirn influence in other areas – medical services, legal services, shipping and logistics, and various other industries. That's a lot of assets that can be used to bring pressure against a sept of Garou. And lots of reasons why the Dunsirn might do something that offends or harms them (like buy up land near a caern and develop it) on purpose or incidentally.

              There's probably around 10-12 vampires, some a few centuries old and a number of ghouls. They presumably follow the Giovanni practice of raising members of the family to be vampires and other family members as ghouls. I'd probably make the eldest Dunsirn vampire be of powerful Generation. Again, if we are to go with the idea that the Dunsirn negotiated a good deal, it makes sense Granddaddy Dunsirn made sure he was of potent blood, perhaps between 5th and 7th generation. This would also mean the other Dunsirn vampires are mostly potent generation as well since they have to be embraced by the eldest originally. So they should be stronger than your typical vampire despite their age.

              There's also a secret society of cannibals and sorcerers with some knowledge of Malfean occult practices. It's likely that a number of Dunsirns are now part of the Pretanic Order and have access to other occult powers, rites, and fetishes they can use as kinfolk or mortals. This is probably used as some kind of support for the elder vampire, but could be an independent power faction that rules much of the family. After all, this is probably the main reason the Dunsirn are supposed to have some leverage within the Giovanni clan politics.

              A lot of the White Howler/Black Spiral Dancer holy sites in Scotlands as described in Chronicle of the Black Labyrinth are supposed to be in the historic county of Fife which is just across the Forth of Firth from Edinburgh. The Dunsirn could easily own any such land if they wanted given their wealth and knowledge of the area. That provides lots of opportunities for the ST.

              Most of the family is concentrated in Scotland, especially Edinburgh but they should have 1-2 members in Glasgow which is Scotland's largest city and most heavily involved in the transatlantic trade during colonial times. Other Dunsirn live in England, most likely London. They probably have a strong branch of the family based in North America. I would think Canada makes more sense than America. Toronto would make the most sense. It's anglophone, and Montreal was already well described in Montreal By Night, and I don't think there's much room for Dunsirn there. Even though Toronto is supposed to be Sabbat as well, we can slide them easier there without stepping on canon. There may be a few other Dunsirn scattered about here and there. I could easily see one in Boston. It'd be nearby. A Dunsirn vampire there might have even been the original vampire to bring the Milliner family into the Giovanni clan which would give them a powerful ally in the northeastern United States. And while never mentioned, it would make sense if they had a presence in Australia as well.

              Dunsirn don't seem to have direct ties to the Black Spiral Dancers, but they should know about them by now. As long as the Fianna don't make the connection of their old kinfolk to the Dunsirn, it might be safer for them to stay away. But if the Fianna do make the connection and make plans to destroy them, then the Dunsirn could probably make contact very quickly and make an alliance. At that point, they would then stop killing their Lupine children and turn them over to the BSDs. They would fit in well with the Cluithi camp described in the second edition Book of the Wyrm. It would complicate control of the family though by its vampire masters. But it's better than just waiting for the Fianna to wiped them out which is certainly within their power.

                      Those are interesting thoughts. I my own version of the WoD, the Dunsirn are revenant ghouls (like they did with the Rossellini) who can restore their blood pools through cannibalism. The Dunsirn family does seem like they would make good protagonists for a werewolf game. In fact, for the size and power that the family would reasonably have, its reasonable for them to be a major villain of a WtA chronicle that can be defeated by a group of player characters.

                      Depending on how the ST presents them and introduces the conflict, they could be defeated rather easily (a small group of non-elder vampires leading some rich humans) or a powerful force pulling the strings behind many lesser adversaries with money, power, and dark magic.

                      They do seem like natural allies of the Black Spiral Dancer, not only because they could share ancestry but the entire twisted, incestuous, cannibal, necromancer family thing just goes well with the BSDs. It does seem like a waste to kill their werewolf family members, when they could potentially have those family members of powerful allies. In fact, if a ST wanted to give the family access to a bit more muscle for PC fights, there could be some Dunsirn werewolves.

                          I don't see the Dunsirn as a threat to the Garou Nation as a whole, but I do see them as a possible significant thorn to the Fianna. They could easily fulfill the role of the major villain in a chronicle. Or just as a minor element if the ST wanted.

                          The ties to the Fianna and the Pretanic Order make them an obvious foe for a Werewolf game in some capacity, but they only seemed to be mentioned in Vampire books. I don't recall them being use in Werewolf sourcebooks, but that may just be my ignorance. But I knew I wanted to figure out their role so I could provide real details for my own chronicles. Since I had to do the work anyway, I thought to share it here.

                          I wonder how many actual Fianna caerns (and where) there should be in Scotland to understand the actual threat the Dunsirn might prove to be for the local Garou there. Realistically, are we talking about 10 Scottish Fianna? 20? More? I would think 10-15 seems to be most appropriate given its small size and population, but things can be easily fudged. But with such low numbers, the Dunsirn could be a very major threat to the Scottish Fianna alone. Especially if they become allied to even one Black Spiral Dancer pack that may be there.

                          Asmodai I like the use of the Giant's Causeway! I'm a big sucker for using any real world myths and legends in the game. I do think given the description of the Dunsirn that they have potential to be active opponents outside of Scotland, but Scotland must be the headquarters of their operations.

                          Sergeant Brother, at first glance it does seem a waste to kill lupines born to them especially since Dunsirn vampires could blood bond them. So I had to think of an explanation - and hiding themselves from the Fianna made the most sense. The Giovanni clanbook description seems to imply that the Fianna knew the Dunsirn were bad eggs and just gave up on them, allowing them to become lost kinfolk. But I don't think that makes much sense from a Werewolf perspective. Sure, some kinfolk do get lost in the crowds of humanity. But kinfolk who have a persistent pattern of producing man-eating Garou? Would the Scottish Fianna really not care about that? So it made more sense to me that the Dunsirn once had a different name and faked their own death, or at least that the surviving members did after a Sawney Bean style family of kinfolk finally provoked the Fianna to wipe them all out.
