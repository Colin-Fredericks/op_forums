                                                10-18-2021, 11:38 AM
                                                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                                Caveats about anecdotes and so on:
                                                I know a ton of WtA groups that dropped that rule while playing 2e. I know one that brought it back in Rev. or W20. The one ST that I know that did that doesn't have a group that lets him run games any more, because he actually expanded that rule, and made the consequences worse, and nobody felt like playing with him because they don't want to have a 1/10 chance of retiring a character at the start of every session because random badness happened between scenes.
                                                I think the rule was misinterpreted then. Let's see the rules:
                                                At the Storyteller's option, every character who is attempting to live a human life can be required to make a Willpower roll between each story; the difficulty is the Rage score of that character. If the roll fails, something has happened to ruin or hinder the character's relationship with the people among whom he has been living. The Beast has emerged, the Delirium has engaged, and never again shall the character be trusted. This penalty may be lessened should the character be so fortunate as to have Kinfolk.
                                                A botch indicates that not only has the character lost control, but agents of the Wyrm have discovered her whereabouts and may be in the process of an attack. The player and Storyteller should immediately go into roleplaying mode and deal with this issue.
                                                The text in the DriveThru version isn't manually corrected OCR, so there might be spelling mistakes above...

                                                So, last sentence indicates, we are not in roleplaying mode when this happens. Not that the book tells of "modes" a lot. But I guess "roleplaying mode" is a scene, while elements not in a scene but in a story are downtime, according to page 206 of 2nd Ed core rules. Elements between stories are are also downtime I guess, interludes in the chronicle - paraphrasing page 52-55 of 2nd Edition Core rules.

                                                Roll is Willpower against Rage. So, WP 10 against Rage 10 has a 31% percent failure rate. But, next to every human runs away in panic anyway, so the garou wouldn't have had time to build a relationship with humans, right?
                                                Let's see WP 8 Rage 6 -> slightly under 8% failure chance. So, not so drastic....

                                                But what even happens with a failure?
                                                "...something has happened to ruin or hinder the character's relationship with the people among whom he has been living..."
                                                So, no need for character retiring at all. I mean, ruin relationships with humans or hinder them? That can totally be a lot of things.
                                                Question is, what was the character trying to have as a "human life"? Is it family, job or both?
                                                What ever the case, something happened that made his Rage come to the surface and therefore lowered the difficulty of some Empathy roll so that one or more humans felt a glimpse of the garou's true self and it backfired.

                                                Found a job as bank teller? Great, until that one day, you got frustrated with poor old hard-of-hearing Mrs. Walters. It was just your expression that you couldn't control for a moment and she realised something. Next Monday, no senior citizen stood in line at your counter and when the last of month came, you were let go.

                                                Had a family? Great, until that one fight you had with your wife. Your grabbed the bowl full of hot soup, ready to hurl it at her. You didn't, but little Tommy saw. He is afraid, your wife is afraid, you are afraid yourself. Nothing to bad happened, but the bond of trust is shattered. It's best to move out, maybe only temporary. Trust need to be rebuild. You can't force that.

                                                The rules don't say something about Frenzy, Carnage, Murder, Veil-lifting, Deaths or wounding or any of the things that destroy a character. They tell about destroying (or hindering) things the garou had been building that resemble a "normal" human life.

                                                There is also not stated, the ST should dictate terms, or prohibit these Downtime events lead into scenes. On the contrary, page 206 describes that Downtime Play can and should develop into a scene organically. So, build scenes around how the character reacts after the fight with this wife, or how he feels alone in the motel that night, or couch surfing at his packmates later that week. Does the character use Persuation to keep his job as bank teller, possibly settle with the bank manager to transfer to another branch after a short sabbatical?
                                                Also, the ST should work with the Players, as described in the Storys section of the Rules.


                                                A lot of topics in these forums have several users stating, that they don't see garou holding an every day job or human life, because of the Curse, and also peer pressure as mandated by possible renown loss for not being garou full time. But it was 2nd Edition "Family Tragedy" mechanics that made this Setting Element an actual game element, instead of a mere Setting description. And that is, why it should be back in W5 and be used (in a way that enriches the game).
