    According to Justin, the process of Tribe selection - in character - is going to be much more personal and spiritual. There's nothing the Garou in W5 can do about their children seeking to join a different Tribe, since it seems like the whole concept of familial heritage and induction via rites of passage are out. After the First Change, new cubs are going to be spiritually drawn to Patrons that fit their true selves. Their parents, and the extant members of the other Tribes, basically don't get a say if the cub and the Patron spirit agree that the cub belongs in that spirit's Tribe.

    The W5 Tribes are going to have to put a lot more work into how they raise their children if they hope to mold them into future members of the same Tribe, and it looks like Tribal cultures will be rewritten to have an "adoption" focused mindset where they take in Garou that aren't of their blood but are of the same "spirit" as the default way of gaining new members.

    While this certainly sounds idealistic from the sense of the Garou getting to be parts of their found families that fit them the best, it's very much not ideal for the Tribes themselves since they become much more dependent on factors outside of their control for new members. Hopefully W5 does something with this new tension they're adding to the game... but I'm not holding my breath for it.

        I want to go deeper into why I think Red Talon should have been cut because I love the tribe and do want them to get a fair shake.

        For one, the player response to Talons has been rather meh over the years. The person who made the TV Tropes page for WtA outright states only psychopaths would play them and a Talon in a game would make other players uncomfortable. While I do not think a Talon would make others uncomfortable, I think they are a tribe often played in a way that stalls the story for everyone else. In online games, a Talon cliath throwing a hissy fit at a Glass Walker elder used to be common.

        Getting to play a Red Talon is akin to pulling teeth and for a good reason.

        Second, even in the source material the Talons rarely if ever speak for themselves or are a factor. The revised tribebook's culture chapter is told from the POV of a Shadow Lord and not a Talon. Most often, Talons are mentioned in passing when discussing human eating being forbidden and how the lupus are dying out. The general mood I get from most Talon content is that they are alien and they have all but given up on the Nation.

        Which does not a good Player Character splat make. Every tribe needs some research, but the Talon need you to basically do in-depth research on grey wolves (and then have to accept that the canon for Talons STILL lives by the Alpha/Omega dynamic) and basically play a character that is mostly ignorant and mostly unwilling to change their viewpoints.

        Even if the Shadow Lords and Red Talons are treated as the darkest tribes, the fact is you can speak to a Shadow Lord. You can't to a Talon.

        The way I have made the Talons work for me is by treating their want to be true wolves as something very human. The Talons are defining what a 'wolf' is, while both lupus and regular wolves often fail to match that ideal. They still have writing, rites and general tribal culture, so they are hypocrites in my House Rules.

        But those are my House Rules, not the canon.

        Red Talons are a tribe that is antithetical to the 'everyone can be everything' ethos of W5, their very tribal concept is isolation. Predator Kings in Forsaken are based on Talons and they too are hidebound and refuse to see things differently.

        To me, in order to make the Talons accept everyone, much of the tribe has to be scrapped. While W5 is a reimagining, holding onto the tribal name and totem makes clear connections to their old version. So if the tribe is nothing like the Talons, the references are essentially misleading.

        Besides, you could slot the White Howlers into the tribal list and they would work better than the Talons.

                Nobody has really been able to speculate a good answer to that (I proposed the same idea in another thread, as there are at least three non-playable Tribes in the setting as well to assign Renown too, if not more if the Lost Tribes make it in some fashion and get a Renown assignment).

                                In regards to the Tribes, when Outstar and Justin Achilli talked about the Creeds in H5, they talked about how it promotes freedom for players. And how that was done, they said is that, for example, the Faithful Creed never specifies what that hunter's faith actually is. Maybe it's a religion but maybe it's science or some virtue or some other kind of ideal. Faithful just means believing in something strongly and it's great how it's all up to the player to interpret what his Faithful hunter-player-character believes in. And I could picture that all the Tribes will be these very broad descriptions where it's left to the players to come up with the specifics of what it really means to be a member of a Tribe. The Tribe isn't supposed to limit any choices in how to portray a member of the Tribe. And that's done by going very broad and vague with the portrayal of the Tribes. I think that's what we'll ultimately get. There will be references to what these Tribes were in old WtA but the goal will be to tell players as little as possible about how to play the Tribes (because that part is supposed to be up to the players).

                                                            Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                                                            Being so fluid with this thing just undermines the part where Garou are monsters literally driven by rage.
                                                            It undermines the value of using the old Tribes so directly for the new edition; all while saying they're changing enough to not consider it a direct continuation anyway.

                                                            Forsaken uses a similar approach to Tribes without undermining the part where werewolves are rage-monsters. If anything, if you're trying to organize creatures known for being rage-monsters into larger scale social units, it makes way more sense to let them join what feels right to them. Trying to force them into groups they might not really care about on a personal and spiritual level is just adding another source of anger, frustration, and tension that'll lead up to Rage fueled blowouts.

                                                            It makes the characters idiots if they think that forcing the Garou to live in family focused units is going to cause less and less severe Rage based domestic violence, than if Garou are expected to be in social groups with less innate social stressors. The Garou don't want Garou murdering each other and kinfolk. Having blood-families be the default situation until their old enough to join a pack, and still giving them pressure to join their birth Tribe, is being really stupid about how Frenzies are known to work in-character.

                                                            In a normal human family, parents telling their teen aged children that they're going to join the family business just like their parents did, and their grandparents did, and so on, is a recipe for shouting, saying things that are hard to take back, and slamming doors. With the Garou it's a good way to have family dinner ruined by fangs, fur, blood, and death.

                                                            Taking cubs away from their families makes the most sense if you care about teaching them to get the basics of controlling their Rage down first and foremost, so they can even make it to joining a Tribe proper.
