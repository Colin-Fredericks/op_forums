            Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
            Hedge magic is supposed to require a certain amount of innate talent, combined with intense and long lasting training. Many traditions of it aren't inherently cheap either.
            I agree completely. The amount of justification to have such knowledge is often overlooked because mechanics-wise, you just buy it with XP. In "reality" though, you have to devoted your life to mastering such esoteric knowledge. Any form of magic is a kind of spiritual discipline, but that doesn't show up when it comes to acquiring it in game.

            I would not be surprised if at various times, a secret lodge among the kinfolk arose that had knowledge of hedge magic. However, the Garou are jealous of their status as Gaia's defenders, and if they find out about it - which they eventually will - things will not turn out well for those kinfolk hedge wizards.

            Players often have a "we're in this all together" so it's great for the Garou if their kinfolk are mages, hedge wizards, or whatever. This is a very naive view of how protective of their turf people are, and how much infighting occurs within organizations.

            Even if some kinfolk only had access to Ephemera - which is justifiable based on the kinfolk base of knowledge - I don't think the Garou would be supportive of it. The kinfolk are usurping the role of the Garou and being uppity. If such things exist, it's probably very limited - with specific paths being available within specific sects/traditions of specific tribes, and being overseen/approved by elder Garou to make sure such magic if done "properly" and controlled.

                                            My post was *essentially* correct; that is, vampires *can't* use mortal hedge-magic (sans the Golden Rule, of course, but that should go without saying), and that's been the case since 1st Edition. Vampires can bastardize hedge magic via adapting their own blood magic/vitae to "make shit work", but that is metaphysically a very different thing in the broader picture. Vampires lose their connection to the living forces of the world, and as a result lose the ability to work both True Magic(k) and mortal hedge magic (this was covered in several different books pre-Revised, then stressed again in Revised, particularly in Sorcerer, which was of course all about hedge magic). It is why vampiric Thaumaturgy (by whatever name) is usually more potent (generally a given dot rating in Thaumaturgy/a Path has more "oomph" than a Sorcerer's Paths do) but often less flexible (Sorcerer paths -- and some of their Psychic Numina, where overlapping -- have more flexibility/facet-use than the Paths of Blood Magic that vampires can practice). Also, don't confuse/conflate the narrative aspect that some sorcerers may use animal sacrifice/blood, or their own blood, as a component of a given spell/ritual as that then being "blood magic" -- it isn't. The Blood Magic that vampires practice is metaphysically quite different from what mortal hedge wizards do; sometimes the results may be nigh-identical, true, but the specific underlying *how*/*why* is very, very different, and this shouldn't be overlooked as it in turn is what leads to a number of misunderstandings about the metaphysics involved....
