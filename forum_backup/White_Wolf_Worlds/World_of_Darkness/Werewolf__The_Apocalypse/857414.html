                                  I wouldn't get too hung up on the letter of the law, err rules, or what level of Willpower creates what effect and the what percentage of the population it is. Make the reaction appropriate considering the circumstances. Most people won't be able to fight effectively, but an army likely already has a different character than the average population - people who have seen close combat, who have killed before and been injured, who believe that God is on their side. If the army has a diverse composition, it might make sense that the peasant levies are too scared to fight, but maybe the knights can work up their courage to do so, while a few men at arms flee while others hurl themselves at the enemy in a rage. The rules are there to help, not constrain, you. Make the reaction of the army what you think the reaction should be.

                                                  One thing to keep in mind that a uniform group of soldiers is not likely to have the same random willpower as the populace. There is a certain kind of people who end up in the military and those people are usually not the high willpower people. High Willpower people are extraordinary, I mean, look at this.



                                                  And, define what does it mean to "see" a Werewolf in Crinos. If you're talking about a medieval army you're talking about the shifter being quite near them. The horror kicks in only after they actually realise what it is they are facing. As long as a Werewolf is at least 30-40 meters away, they will surely just think it's some big madman wearing wolfskins. Once he gets closer he will start provoking the delirum, but before that he might easily get peppered with arrows or a cunning commander may try to fall back to protect his men.

                                                  However, once he's among them or clearly visible... they are fucked. Truly and utterly. Not only is a horrible death dealing machine which they can barely touch and that kills one of them per its hit among them,but all the primordial fears and terrors of the wolves breaking down your hovels and murdering everyone around you come into your head. Your sanity and sensibility is bound to take a hit. So yeah. Delirium works just as intended.

                                                  Now if you really feel its overpowered and you're actually playing a Dark Ages game in Europe (i assume) you could easily say that all the reactions on the willpower chart are 2 higher, because the Ajaba are not part of the European cultural fears. I wouldn't in your place, because it's counterbalanced by the fact that someone will remember, the fact that the shifters enforce the Veil... and frankly, what do you think a Vampire with Presence does to an army?

                                                      Originally posted by Innocent Flower If the white howlers really wanted to, they could have driven rome out with a single pack and not needed to have risked their leaving their home unprotected.
                                                      Apart from the vampires, mages and other garou that had invested interest and a presence within the roman legions. Also bear in mind that this is the world of darkness so lets say a pack of howlers forced a legion to retreat/run away, what happens to every non legionnaire they encounter as they fall back. The fear caused and damage done would create a massive breeding ground for the wyrm.

                                                      As for delirium in mass combat, I tend to increase the willpower of regular people when they are in groups, people who are trained for and/or have seen combat may have higher than normal willpower. Private security drawn from military personnel are more likely to fall back in an organized fashion with suppressing fire, a street gang not so much. One roman legionnaire or man at arms vs a pack of werewolves, they will run, if they live that long, a hundred there will be panic sure but they will mostly fight, a thousand is a different story entirely.

                                                      At the end of the day it is up to the ST to either use a bit of common sense and weigh up the numbers and experience of the enemies or go the other way and ramp up the absurdity because that can be fun once in a while as well.
