    One of the odd things to consider in WtA games is the idea of 'looting the bodies' takes on a completely different meaning when even the clothing of your enemy may be contaminated by the taint of the Wyrm. I generally rule that any object used by the servants of the Wyrm in the violation of another creature (or any object that housed an object used in the violation of another creature by a servant of the Wyrm) may be contaminated by the taint of the Wyrm. That means that the weapons of servants of the Wyrm should probably be destroyed to avoid allowing them to spread the taint of the Wyrm to innocent people and that the former apartment of a servant of the Wyrm may be so tainted by the violations performed by the previous tenant that anyone moving into the apartment will begin to suffer the taint of the Wyrm.

            I have to preface my explanation by saying that in my personal ST code , I don't like to babysit players and hand hold them , not when they have experience with the game. Furthermore it makes me uncomfortable as a ST to step in and tell a character &quot; hey don't do that&quot; or &quot; hey do it like this&quot; because I firmly believe in consequences , especially for not paying attention. Even more deeply , through personal experience( overseas and on the streets) I know that sometimes in the heat of battle , and as a response to fear and adrenaline , you will hit your own people , and sometimes it's lethal.

            I say that last thing to paint this picture: A dead garou was leaning against a large tree. Characters come upon it. Instead of healing him with mothers touch , they let him die. The massive Raven hops onto the dead garou's chest and starts pecking out an eye. While he is doing his Corax thing I state that something is rumbling inside the dead garou's chest and looks like its moving upinto the dead garou's throat. I then state that whatever is inside the dead garou is moving in such a way that it may start coming out of his dead mouth (remember large raven sitting on the things chest facing the dead garous face). The two GoF in Crinos (one with a spear , the other with an axe) state the following &quot; WERE GOING TO HACK THE SHIT OUT OF IT! (meaning the dead garou).

            At that point I asked the Raven to dodge , I didn't even raise the difficulty for not facing the Get or anything.....mind you the players where at the same table , and start congratulating and high fiving...because theyre not paying attention what so fucking ever even after reminding them before the game and during the game TO PAY FUCKING ATTENTION.

                            Another thing to consider would be to have the contaminated bones create Mental Taints in the people who are carrying them because their greed gives Vorus, the Urge of Greed, a channel to corrupt them. Every day, secretly roll the Willpower of the Garou who is carrying them, at a difficulty 6, with them suffering Mental Taint associated with Greed if they fail their Willpower roll.

                            After they suffer the Mental Taint of Greed, you require them to make a Wits plus Primal Urge roll, difficulty 6, to avoid giving into their greed for a scene. If they spend a point of Willpower, they can control themselves for a turn. In addition, Vorus would whisper into the ear of the character, trying to corrupt them, and the character would have to succeed on a Wits plus Enigmas roll (difficulty 8 [difficulty 6 if he is aware of the nature of the Urge Wyrms]) to realize that the thoughts are not his own.

                                            Originally posted by wonderandawe View Post
                                            I'd let the player have the bones, give them disturbing dreams, and have the elders go "WTF are you doing bring those tainted things into the Caern, cliath?" Maybe even make it into plot. "Thanks to you dumb asses, we got a bunch of banes heading our way."
                                            ​I love this kind of stuff. I was going to write more than what I'm going to write now, because Karlgust made some similar points to what I was thinking, but I'll still add in a bit.

                                            First, one of the best pieces of advice I've ever received as a gamer AND a ST/GM is: "Don't say no to another player." Note, that's not the same as, "Don't say no to another character." And, also keep in mind that this is coming from a place of, "What can we do to make this player have fun?" That doesn't mean the player/character gets away with everything willy-nilly. In the scenario wonderandawe gave, not saying no causes some cool story shit to happen. Similarly, it's also not the same as, "Give them their way." In this particular case, the "Don't say no" concept could simply be turned into, "Okay. What part of what bone are you trying to take?" Limit him without outright denying him. If he has a laundry list of bone pieces, as soon as he gets one or two, say that the rest of the skeleton starts turning into dust and soil. Manifestation of an earth elemental, after all! If the bone pieces he now has ever touches earth again, have them do the same (just to carry through with the story). If he starts asking a bunch of ridiculous questions to challenge why you did that, shrug and smile and say, "Good question. Perhaps ask your Theurge." Or whatever. Push the scene back into RP.

                                            Second, my last long-running group had a problem player who sounds a lot like this guy. We ultimately had to part ways with him, but that was because he was only showing up to half the games. What helped curb his dumbassery was advice brilliantly put by our ST: "Okay, interesting idea. But first, I want you to think: what is likely to happen if you SUCCEED?" Often, players like this just get caught up in Moon Moon's "GONDA DO THING, GONDA GET THING!" style of excitement. Reign them in by making them think about the consequences of their actions. In attacking the body, it's that nudge of, "What will happen if you succeed?" // "We'll kill whatever THAT is!" // "You might hit your friend. You might miss whatever it is that's about to happen, because you don't know what's happening." If they really are that trigger happy, then give them another situation when they act first and think later, only this time, they've lost a critical key to their goal in the process. Whoops. Shit's about to get a lot harder, and now you've pissed off your pack. Then let the pack handle it! With said problem player in our group? I whacked him with a Falling Touch one time when he was about to do something that would get us unnecessarily hurt (... I was playing a Ragabash, so it was also rather amusing to him as a character). The character (and probably the player) was pissy about it, but guess what? It stopped him enough to think about his actions.

                                            If two players are being peas in a pod, don't let them sit next to each other, for one (and be honest and tell them it's 'cause they get disruptive together). And, second, maybe encourage one of them to make a new character who is considerably different than the other's (and different than their current attitudes). They're both Get? Sounds like they're both just wanting to run around smashing the thing. If they encourage their Moon Moon behavior, alter their presence at the table.

                                            Ultimately, though, they may just not be a good fit for the game.
