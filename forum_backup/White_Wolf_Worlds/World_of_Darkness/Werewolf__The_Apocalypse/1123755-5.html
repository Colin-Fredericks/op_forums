    Originally posted by LokiRavenSpeak View Post
    I disagree on part of this, i like that PB is a objectively truth on the setting. Not the mechanics of it, like i mentioned in my post even if just related to how likely a true born is to be born.
    I don't mind PB if house ruled, because those house rules tend to remove the issues I have with how the RAW interact with the themes the background deals in.

    Like my own house rule posted up thread where I split it into two Backgrounds. My Heritage concept is, like PB, something you just have, but it lacks the "high PB characters are inherently superior" thing because the bonuses aren't universal to all Garou, and the increased likelihood of breeding is (since I tend to ignore the books that put numbers to this) both nebulous and only "superior" in a social context. A Silver Fang might be more likely to have trueborn kids from a monogamous arranged marriage, but a Bonegnawer can compensate by having a ton of kids from a bunch of partners to effectively create the same number of trueborn children. Both strategies have pros and cons to them, even if Garou society is heavily biased towards mated pairs.

    The objectivity that gets me is the social bonus of all-Garou application.

    Because it gives the Garou nation a point that otherwise would sorely lack. Make their abominable action have at least one slight point that cannot be constested and that gives them more nuanced.
    I'm also not sure I agree with the idea that the Garou are lacking in objective truths to hang their cultural justifications on. They have direct interaction with the spirits, including their Tribal Totems (if rarely) and possibly even their primary deities in Gaia and Luna (exceedingly rarely). Having the spirits watching, judging, and rewarding/punishing/aiding them is already a fairly huge objective thing to hang stuff on. You can't deny the objective truth that the Garou are rewarded for many of their actions we find morally reprehensible by the spirits they consider the source of their cultural morality. They don't have to take such things on faith, (or humanism style ethical assumptions).

    Now this introduces a moral question that we dont face. PB being an objective truth makes harder to dismiss it. It doesnt validates it completely either because one only has to look at the Garou history as also objective proof that the classicism of nation is a hidrance.
    That's... not really a question though. That's more of a cultural dilemma.

    As well, why do we need PB for this? Falcon can just show up and say, "The Silver Fangs are born of noble blood, and chosen by the spirits to rule the Garou Nation." to add everything PB does without it as a trait to this issue.

    Originally posted by Wilson View Post
    To me, Pure Breed is something that characters respond to instinctively and intuitively, rather than rationally.
    Classism isn't perpetuated by rationality, because IRL it isn't rational. Just like physical appearance, social norms associated with signs of class vastly impact subconscious impressions.

    There was an amusing Mythbusters episode (which is backed up by more formal studies, but it's way more fun to watch) where they have large groups of women rate the attractiveness of men based on pictures with some demographic information (job, kind of home, location of home). The pictures remained the same, but the demographics were changed between sampling. Higher paying jobs, and better living conditions, always increased attraction ratings despite the same visuals.

    Interviews with their test groups even revealed that the women were generally aware of such biases, but acted on them anyway.

    I think this kind of personal magnetism, more related to physical beauty, athleticism, charisma, confidence, etc., is a better real-world analogy to Pure Breed than classism. On the other hand, Pure Breed is also a spiritual vibe that invokes a gnostic memory of ancestors, for which I don't think the human world has any good analogy.
    The problem is that the game has a (poorly implemented) trait for this: Appearance. Appearance is a general trait anyone can take.

    Pure Breed's problem is that it isn't just super-Appearance, because it's linked to "class" in the form of Tribes and family ties that make up the classes in Garou Society. The Gnawers and Glasswalkers are Urrah Tribes, and those the lowest class of Garou society, and denied PB completely. Silver Fangs are explicitly the royal Tribe that maintain noble bloodlines to maximize PB, and as the highest class of Garou society, they have to take it.

    That's how the classism part comes in. PB's distribution is directly linked to class in Garou society. Even in all the Tribes in-between, higher PB characters are given more social standing, and the "middle class" Tribes still have to play the PB game to maintain that social standing.

    The way I see it, Pure Breed isn't something that is "justified" or that Garou are "obsessed with." It describes a natural trait of the species without appeal to or need for any justification. It's more like the advantages of being one of the Beautiful People* in human society, that may not be fair or justifiable, but for better or worse, it's real.
    It does, however, completely lack the nuances that things like visual appearance have in terms of impact. Especially since beauty is a social construct, what one group considers a sign of attractiveness is not what all humans will respond to.

            For me, my purebreed headcanon relates to what the tribes have to do to -have- those looks.

            For example;

            Silent Striders have very short fur, they may have an undercoat, but they generally do not take to colder weather well. Then again, due to breeding for solid black coats, days in the desert are not exactly fun, either.

            Silver Fangs breed for a white coat and blue eyes, essentially risking massive deafness and blindness among themselves. What is more, due to the various houses living in different areas, they have to nearly line breed to keep their coats white and eyes blue.

            Wendigo meanwhile relies on spiritual ties with their totem, and to a mutation to keep their purebreed looks. Essentially, to get a white haired and blue/purple eyed person from Inuit and Yupik stock, they need to be leucistic to match the looks. Due to Wendigo's influence, their fur is not reddish but bluish. But things like light sensitivity and sunburns are still common.

            For Black Furies, Shadow Lords and Red Talons, their looks come from favouring certain mutations of colour. For the Talons, this means mainly reddish coats. For Furies, clear markings of black and white are favoured. And the Lords? Well, if they look a bit dog-like with their pure black coats, that is acceptable.

                While I might be sounding like a broken drum, I think that the solution for the Garou is just to have Pure Breed influence social interactions with Garou and Kinfolk of your Tribe. Since the Bone Gnawers and Glass Walkers are more meritocratic than other Garou Tribes, they do not have Pure Breed. In addition, change Pure Breed for Kinfolk from reproductive bonus to a similar social bonus, and just change the reproductive probability.

                In that framework, Garou would have a 10% chance of producing Garou with normal humans (40% chance of producing Kinfolk) and a 20% chance of producing Garou with Kinfolk (80% chance of producing Kinfolk). Kinfolk would have a 2% chance of producing Garou with normal humans (40% chance of producing Kinfolk) and a 4% chance of producing Garou with Kinfolk (80% chance of producing Kinfolk). I think that these two changes would balance Pure Breed and give a real reason for Garou of every Tribe to prefer mating with Kinfolk (and for preferring Kinfolk to breed with Kinfolk) without the weirdness of Pure Breed.
