    Gurahl (Mechanical Deep Dive)

    04-10-2025, 02:22 PM
    Werebears! The Gurahl are Gaia's healers, chosen to protect the living and to fight disease. They have animal kin among the various bear species, and their numbers were hugely impacted by the War of Rage, in which they were among the most impacted victims.

    In this thread I'll examine the mechanics of the Gurahl, comparing them to other Fera and most especially to Garou.

    Overview
    • All Gurahl start with a base Willpower of 6; they're very stubborn and changing their minds is extremely difficult. And this is compared to a Garou. Gurahl get +1 success on Opposed Willpower rolls relating to defending and protecting, anything attempting to control their mind or possess them suffers a +2 difficulty, and finally when they are in a contest of wills with another over serving Gaia they receive an advantage determined by the Storyteller.
    • Gurahl enjoy a -2 difficulty to all scent-based sensory rolls in all forms but Homid, and in all forms except Homid they suffer a +2 difficulty to all entirely sight-based sensory rolls.
    • Werebears reduce their Initiative score by 2 in Winter months.
    • All Gurahl must buy Mentor 1, as a minimum, and the Kinfolk Background grants fewer Kinfolk.
    • Gurahl have access to all five of their Auspices, moving through them across longer periods of time, driven by changes in their character and story.
    • Every Gurahl has an aspect of Bear as their Totem, for free, gaining a relevant benefit. This is +1 Survival, along with an option of (Cub: +1 Expression and +1 Larceny), (She-Bear: +1 Empathy and +1 Expression), or (Death-Bear: +1 Brawl and +1 Enigmas); as with Auspices a Gurahl may shift between different faces of Bear as they go through their life.
    • Gurahl cannot Step Sideways by default, but have a few ways of crossing the Gauntlet, most especially Umbral Glens and a purpose-made Rite.
    Rage and Frenzy

    Gurahl may spend Rage for a few additional purposes, compared to other Fera:
    • 1 Rage for +1 Strength (no higher than double their current form's original Strength) lasting for 1 turn per permanent Dot of Rage
    • 1 Rage for +1 Stamina (no higher than double their current form's original Stamina) lasting for 1 turn per permanent Dot of Rage
    • 1 Rage for an additional Health Level lasting for one scene, any health level gained will based on your current level of wounds, so if uninjured you'll gain additional Bruised health levels
    As a tradeoff they don't regain Rage from things which would impact most other Fera; a threat to Gaia's sanctity would restore Rage while a fierce argument would not.

    Gurahl very rarely Frenzy; their Rage roll to Frenzy is difficulty 8, and they need 5 successes, or 8 for Thrall.

    It's worth noting that W20 doesn't explicitly say that Gurahl can spend Rage for bonus actions, but it also never says or implies, as far as I can see, that they don't get bonus actions from Rage.

    Bhernocht

    Akin to Harano, this is a state where a Gurahl willingly fails to uphold their duties. They lose Willpower continuously until they fall into hibernation, and they also lose all the benefits they'd typically enjoy regarding Willpower defenses and rolls. While this is unlikely to come up in play for a typical PC in a typical session, it's worth keeping this in mind. A Gurahl simply can't refuse to attempt to carry out their duties, if they do the impact is severe and ramps up very swiftly over a course of days.

    Breeds

    Gurahl may attempt to reproduce with one another, but this always fails; either the pairing is sterile or the child will be stillborn. Since conception is possible, given that a stillbirth can occur, it seems feasible that a Metis Gurahl could come into existence through powerful interference, but for all intents and purposes Gurahl are divided into Homid and Ursine.

    Homid Gurahl start with a Rage of 3 and a Gnosis of 4.

    Ursine Gurahl suffer the typical Lupus drawbacks regarding Backgrounds and Abilities are character creation, and start with a Rage of 4 and a Gnosis of 5.

    Much like with Garou, the animal Breed does have a significant advantage... but when all is said and done the difference is much, much smaller for Gurahl. A difference of 3 freebie points worth of traits is pretty negligible.

    Auspices

    Instead of having one “birth” auspice that governs an individual throughout his lifetime, Gurahl migrate through five different auspices in the course of their lives. There is a natural progression of auspices, and the vast majority of Gurahl transition through them in that order. In more peaceful times, a Gurahl would take an entire lifetime to experience all five auspices. In modern times, however, many Gurahl rush through all the auspices in a few years, finally settling on the one most suitable to their temperament - or most necessary to the responsibilities they’ve taken on - as a long-term auspice. This sometimes means going back to a previous auspice, or remaining in one for an extended time period, if duty requires it.
    Arcas is the Auspice of the New Moon, and it reflects a time of learning, exploration and growth. You might think of it like the Fool Tarot, it's a starting point full of potential. Typically a Gurahl advances to Uzmati when they enter a period of solitary travel, leaving their first mentor for a time.

    Uzmati is the Auspice of the Full Moon, reflecting a stronger draw towards battle. While in this Auspice a Gurahl is expected to be more of a defender and take on a greater burden in times of war. As a result some Gurahl stay in this Auspice for a very long time (which makes sense with the Apocalypse on the horizon) and some leave it very swiftly, having little taste for battle. Kojubat begins when a Gurahl mates, marries, or settles down into a more reflective role.

    Kojubat is the Auspice of the Gibbous Moon, and it handles lore and understanding. Well respected for tending the knowledge Gaia imparted upon the Gurahl, this phase tends to end when the werebear turns towards teaching.

    Kieh is the Auspice of the Crescent Moon, focused on mentoring, mysticism and spiritual matters. Finally, when a Gurahl reaches a position where their wisdom is called upon by others to settle disputes, they reach the final phase.

    Rishi is the Auspice of the Half Moon, focused on leadership, judgement, arbitration and peace-keeping.

    A Gurahl can move abnormally swiftly through this rotation, and later return to old Auspices, or even repeatedly go through the cycle. Traditionally it was deemed wise to go through all 5 in order, very strictly, and then eventually settle on your 'true' role in later life. Given genocide and apocalyptic chaos, Gurahl just figure things out as they go these days.

    The Totem a werebear is currently affiliated with is also loosely connected to their current Auspice, so a Gurahl deep in Rage and despair in their solemn wandering as an Uzmati would likely find a closer kinship with Mangi, the Death-Bear, while a Gurahl who enters Uzmati while keeping their innocence might cling onto Ursa Minor.

    Forms

    Gurahl utilize 5 forms, Homid, Arthren, Crinos, Bjornen, and Ursus.

    This post isn't a substitute for reading W20 Changing Breeds. Suffice it to say that Gurahl are very, very strong and sturdy in their Crinos, enjoy a +5 bonus to Strength and Stamina, along with a -1 penalty to Dexterity.

    First Thoughts

    Before getting into Gifts (which are split into General, Breed and Auspice), it's worth noting that the Gurahl are very flexible. They have 5 Auspices which you can move vaguely freely through, bound more by the tides of the story than the laws of the book. Naturally a hyper-optimized path between the Auspices won't fly at a good table, but you shouldn't want that. Just relax and remember that if you desperately need some Gifts from a certain Auspice, chances are you'll find yourself working your way back to that Auspice.

    The immediate potential for a high-Rage Gurahl to temporarily have a Strength or Stamina of 20, while in Crinos, is extremely appealing in itself. Gurahl lack a few Garou advantages, but they make up for what they lose with a lot of powerful bonuses. So before getting into Gifts, the basekit is... perhaps slightly better. If I had the option to move my Child of Gaia Metis through the Auspices, flowing between them over the years as he matures and changes and faces different challenges, I'd immediately take it, even if it were a 7 point Merit or some other huge investment. Gurahl promises a character who could, if they weren't in a team-building game where a 'pack' of some kind comes together, probably manage alone. Such characters can, with thought, function amazingly in a team. You could be the generalist, or specialize in something important (like combat) and then shift into something else (like being a face or mystic).

    Unfortunately the big problem for Gurahl is bonus actions. W20 isn't crystal clear about this, but across the history of Werewolf my impression is that Gurahl don't get to spend Rage for additional actions. This may be the intent in W20; in W20 corebook we are told that Gurahl spend Rage "differently" before we're given their special uses, implying that they might not get the standard uses. Presumably they can still use Rage to transform and ignore stunning, and the RAI seems to be that you can't get bonus actions.

    This is a huge downside, and somewhat unique. Gurahl and Nuwisha are alone in lacking bonus actions, to the best of my knowledge, and it really does suck both defensively and offensively. Do the Gurahl's unique options make up for it? I'd say, all in all, no. If you have a very high Rage and you wind up with a Stamina of 20... well, then you're somewhat unassailable. But if you can't produce enough Rage to get the locomotive going, you're going to suffer. With all this in mind, a Garou (or rough equivalent like Ajaba) is generally going to fare better. But in a team? You can be the slow burn powerhouse who gradually heats up until you are unstoppable.

    The other big change is that you can't typically Step Sideways. To do so you need the Step Sideways Merit, or the Rite of Rending the Gauntlet, or to find an Umbral Glen. This is a negotiable problem, especially if you have the correct Rite, but should be kept in mind.

      Green Gifts are often must-haves, or have applications which are incredibly game-changing, story-impacting or overall powerful. Amber Gifts are solid, their utility is often determined by how likely you are to need the given effect or whether it's important to your character concept. Red Gifts are, sadly, too weak to typically be a good investment. A Red Gift might have an insane cost for its effect, or have too hard a roll to reliably pass, or be entirely redundant, and so on.

      See my Garou thread for details on pre-existing Gifts.

      Garou Gifts: Ranking, Thinking, Overthinking - Onyx Path Forums

      I'm going to break down Gifts by level and category, bit by bit. If it's Wyrmish, Fera-only, obscure or lost it's going to feature much much later, if at all. This is intended to focus on the Gifts you will actually be getting in play, hence starting at level 1 and working up. I'll be considering utility (both inside and


      General Gurahl Gifts

      Desperate Strength, Healing Tongue (Mother's Touch), Ignore Wounds (Resist Pain), Sense Pattern Breaker (Sense Wyrm), Ursa's Cleansing (Resist Toxin), Wyld Resurgence
      Calm, Grisly Aspect (True Fear), Roused to Fury (Para Bellum)
      Calm the Savage Beast, Ease the Fevered Mind (Lover's Touch), Survival of the Bear (Adaptation)
      Heart of the Mountain, Hide the Bear (Bury the Wolf)

      Nature's Plenty (1): Find food and herbs necessary to look after someone, no matter where you are or in what conditions you're working.

      This costs Gnosis and requires a difficulty 7 Perception + Survival; given that you will likely reduce the difficulty to 5 (good scent) and you get +1 Survival from the Bear Totem, this is very easy to pull off. The addition of herbs, as well as food, could make this decently handy in isolation. I maintain, as always, that a supernaturally enhanced superbeing can find lunch. And given that you have Mother's Touch, you don't have much call for mundane herbs in most scenarios.

      Sentinel's Warning (1): This is one of those Gifts which is just lovely in how absolute it is.

      The Storyteller warns the character when danger is imminent (though not the nature of the threat) one turn before it appears. This Gift’s effects are permanent.
      No cost, no rolls, you just always know a second before danger shows up. Good moment to boost your Stamina or Health Levels or yell to those under your protection to get down. This is a character-defining Gift which could save your party countless times. As far as we can tell there is no way around this Gift, so truly ambushing you will be nigh-impossible.

      Treeshake (2): Spend 1 Gnosis, shake a tree, get enough food to feed (Succour) people. The lack of a roll is nice, feeding a number of people is nice... but you have superpowers, you can find lunch. Like almost all Gifts of this ilk, this is very weak.

      Dreams of the Buri-Jaan (3): Spend 1-2 Gnosis and roll Wits + Occult (difficulty 4-9); on a success you can send dreams to a Gurahl you wish to guide and mentor. 1 Gnosis covers a cub you know and mentor, 2 Gnosis covers any Gurahl cub who wishes to learn. The difficulty is based on distance.

      This is mostly a story-based power which will be used by mentoring NPCs. In all honesty you could make this a core part of the Gurahl, a free benefit of being a Buri-Jaan, or a Rite, and things would be fine. This is basically a 9xp "be someone's Mentor" tax, and I'd gladly give this Gift for 0xp to relevant PCs.

      Purely as a 'power' you can pick up for your character this is pretty poor.

      Masking the Hunted (4): Use natural terrain to hide up to 12 individuals, who can each have a size up to that of a bear. Spend 1 Gnosis, roll Manipulation + Stealth (difficulty 7), and this lasts for 1 hour per success.

      I wish this was Survival, not Stealth, but the overall effect of the Gift is pretty neat. The annoying thing is that this lets you conceal creatures using the natural terrain. This is good for hiding, but it's far from an invisibility power, and at Rank 4 you're expecting proper invisibility powers, not a clever den. I could easily imagine pulling something like this off with a relatively weak Fetish. You could also easily remove the Gnosis cost, or the roll, or just set the duration to Succour hours, and so on.

      All in all this is handy if you need to hide a whole party using natural terrain... but at this Rank you expect someone to have a Gift or Fetish which does this better. Effecting up to 12 characters is handy, absolutely, but the difficulty 7 roll and the Gnosis cost mean this is far from free or reliable.

      Gaia's Breath (5): This Gift is hugely important to the Gurahl in terms of role and lore. It lets you revive a shapechanger, just the once.

      Spend 1 permanent Gnosis and 1 permanent Willpower, then roll Charisma + Occult (base difficulty 6, +1 for every hour since death). A single success restores them to life with 1 health level restored, additional successes remove more levels of damage.

      Whether you succeed or fail, you can only attempt this once, ever, on an individual. So you can only restore a dead Garou ally once, if she dies again she's gone forever. Oh and a botch means a powerful Bane possesses the corpse.

      This is very, very good, and could do a lot to circumvent terrible situations in which recovery would otherwise be impossible. The loss of Gnosis and Willpower is a significant xp sink however! It's also worth noting that this Gift doesn't always compare favourably to the Rite of Fighting the Death Bear, which costs 0xp to acquire and doesn't cost any Gnosis or Willpower.

      As a result, while Gaia's Breath is very good, I'm leaving it unrated.

      Gentle Soul (5): Ignore the drawbacks of The Curse, passively.

      The problem is that this Gift is inferior to Beyond Human, which flips The Curse from a drawback into a positive, as well as granting the ability to buff your Social Attributes with Gnosis. I'd be very quick to replace Gentle Soul with Beyond Human, or some version of it. My version of Gentle Soul would permit the Gurahl to spend Gnosis to buff their Social Attributes, and invert The Curse so that it makes them come across as a creature of wisdom and importance, instead of Beyond Human's effect of making you seem alluring and intriguing.

      Universal access to Mother's Touch, Resist Pain and Sense Wyrm adds up to a great mechanical core. Sentinel's Warning is incomparable and outweighs the whole lists of some Tribes.

          Homid Gifts

          Master of Fire, Persuasion
          Dolorous Countenance (Staredown)
          Shape Matter (Reshape Object)
          Spirit Shield (Spirit Ward), Ursa's Coat (Cocoon)
          Fearless Unveiling (Part the Veil)

          Ursa's Light (1): No cost, reach towards the sky, make a Charisma + Occult roll (difficulty 6), you can fill a 100/200 foot area with a soft light, or make a 100/200 yard directional beacon; if you can see Ursa Major or Minor you get the higher number.

          The lack of a cost, at Rank 1, for such an obviously supernatural ability is very funny to me. A Homid Gurahl can just spam this ability. But is it useful? Well, a 3xp lighting option which will fill a huge area is actually pretty neat. I'd wager you get get a similar effect with a 1 point Fetish, but I'd say this is probably a worthwhile investment of 3xp. That said you could skip it and possibly never miss it. If the light had a rider effect, like helping find individuals who are carrying disease or madness (e.g. Black Spiral Dancers) then this would be much improved.

          Climate Control (2): W20 uses both celcius and farenheit, and there isn't a 'measures' portion where it establishes which it prefers. As a result this Gift either adjusts the temperature by 5 degrees C, or F, and we don't know which it is. Given that +5 degrees celcius is equal to +9 degrees farenheit, this does matter!

          Let's assume celcius, because we're all sane. No cost, roll Manipulation + Primal-Urge, difficulty 6, and each success lets you increase or lower the local ambient temperature by 5 degrees.

          So, for example, you could go from 20 degrees up to 50 degrees, in a micro-climate, by spamming this Gift until animals start falling out of the trees dead. I'm very glad that the servants of the Wyrm don't have access to this Gift. "If Gaia had given this Gift to Garou, your Spiral Dancers would have melted the ice caps by now." "Our dancers? You'll die for those words bear!"

          How good is significantly warming up or cooling down an area? It's handy for healing, keeping sick people warm is very important. It's good for roleplay and for comfort. It could help solve puzzles, like in a mysterious umbral temple you could freeze all the water making new areas accessible, which is very Zelda. It could save your life in extreme conditions. It could help you keep a rare plant or animal alive. You could also mess with humans non-violently, driving them away by making it too cold for their Veil-risking picnic.

          All that said... for 6xp this is definitely worth it, but not a game-changer.

          Sense Need (3): This is the Superman Gift, essentially. Reach out and feel for people in a pretty big radius (Succour miles) who need help or rescue. Perception + Empathy, difficulty 6, and more successes gives you a more clear and determined pull, with the word 'clarity' implying that you get some kind of impression of what vague category of help you might provide.

          In a mixed-Fera game this makes you a bit like the first responder to an emergency call, you can triage, decide who goes where, and so on. In Downtime this Gift could help you to save a lot of ordinary humans from the work of Banes, Pentex and so on, and it could also pull you towards cubs in danger. This Gift being used would be a great way to introduce a Gurahl NPC for example.

          Honestly I'd just bump this over to the General Gurahl list, but, as it stands, this is amazing. If Children of Gaia could learn this Gift I'd absolutely love to get it on my boy, and a Fetish which does something similar would be ace. No cost and it opens up infinite possibilities.

          The immediate problem is that the Garou Homid list is incredible, that if you're playing a Garou or Ajaba or even a Bastet you'll have a great Gift list if you're a Homid, and this is... pretty bleak. 10 Gifts total, and they're pretty bad as a cohort. I'd strongly suggest giving Homid Gurahl the Homid Garou list and removing a few Gifts which would feel off (City Running doesn't feel slow and ponderous, for example). But, RAW, the Gurahl don't get much from this Breed list. It's not only bad for Homid Gurahl, it's bad for Ursine, who have fewer out-of-breed options they can easily purchase.

          Oh and as an aside... can I say how horrible most of the renames of Gifts in W20 are? Changing Staredown to "Dolorous Countenance" sounds like someone swallowed a thesaurus. I love using cool and interesting words, but you can use those when you're naming the Gifts in the first place. Only bother renaming a Gift for IMPORTANT lore reasons, like replacing Wolf with Bear or changing the name of a major spirit like the Weaver to fit the culture.

          And finally, isn't it really weird that Gurahl don't get Grandmother's Touch? That's weird right? Just add it to their General list at Level 2.

              Ursine Gifts

              Create Element, Heightened Senses
              Burrow
              (No pre-existing Level 3 Gifts)
              Gnaw, Sweet Swarm of Vengeance (Infest)
              Bear's Bounty (Totem Gift), Call of the Cave Bear (Song of the Great Beast)

              Voice of Woe (1): This Gift is a distress beacon, essentially. Spend 1 Gnosis, roll Charisma + Primal-Urge (difficulty 6), all Gaian shapeshifters within successes x 5 miles know who's in trouble, why, and what direction to go to get there. This Gift makes me sad. Because what immediately comes to mind is that all Garou, save for BSDs, will hear the call of this Gift. Gurahl will have cried for help only to draw in their killers in some cases.

              In a typical game you can expect to use this to summon other PCs, and friendly NPCs. Just be mindful that rivals and outright enemies among Gaia's shapeshifters will be drawn in. Also keep in mind that if there is an Ananasi PC, or NPCs, they won't be able to hear this Gift's cry, as they aren't "Gaian" in origin!

              The cost is a little annoying, but having a handy distress beacon which will typically reach 5-15 miles and bring in a few Fera to help you, in many games, is great. There is an edge case where this Gift helps reveal a non-Gaian shapeshifter for what they truly are; a Gangrel or Fomori or Mage or Changeling who has momentarily come across as a "werewolf" because they turned from a human into a wolf won't be able to hear this cry, and a BSD won't be able to hear it either.

              Weather Watch (2): +5 dice, passively, on all rolls relating to predicting the weather. Boo. Short of weird Umbral situations this is awful. A Gift which simply lets you perfectly predict the weather, including getting a warning when a supernatural change in the weather is about to happen (Vampire, Mage, Werewolf etc. are about to start a giant storm with magic) would be far better and easily fit at Rank 2.

              A good Storyteller could work hard to make this Gift useful for you, but that to me isn't high praise. This is one of those Gifts which would be very handy IRL (especially for a farmer, gardener, hiker, explorer etc.) but doesn't work well in a TTRPG.

              Pull of the Chosen Land (3): You can always find the shortest and fastest route back to the land you protect. This would work, for example, in Malfeas or a Pentex blacksite, leading you to the nearest exit, and from there back home. Naturally the shortest and fastest route won't always be the safest or smartest, but "fastest" will probably guide you towards a viable exit instead of something non-viable, and thus extremely slow.

              Unfortunately this has a Gnosis cost, and a roll, which is just unnecessary. Many Gifts could just remove their cost and/or their roll and be just fine. Still, you pay 1 Gnosis and you roll Perception + Primal-Urge (difficulty 7). On a success the Gift works.

              This is... handy. Especially in weird locations PCs end up in. There are many situations in Werewolf where you can reach a location by some means (Pentex van, Umbral storm, dam explosion, forcibly teleported, went through a portal that closed) and end up in a place where you don't know the way back. I'd prefer this at Rank 1, absolutely, but it's just fine here too.

              Shelter of the Earth (3): With cover, and slow movement, and by avoiding any 'sudden actions', you can become concealed. This costs Gnosis and has a roll, Dexterity + Stealth difficulty 4, and at that point you might as well remove the roll. But still, this is a sort of invisibility power, and that's nice.

              The Gnosis cost, and the reliance on cover (veils, falling leaves, trees, come to mind) are a bit annoying. Personally I would make the Gift cost no Gnosis if you're using a natural source of cover (thick mist, rainfall, a burrow) and only cost Gnosis if you're using a manmade form of cover (privacy screens, a wardrobe). All in all this is handy enough, good for ambushes, good for hiding, though there are better invisibility powers out there.

              What a woeful list. A bunch of Ambers, almost no Green. I feel like Gurahl deserved a lot better from their Breed lists in all honesty; they don't even get Tribe-specific Gifts, despite being split up into "bear type". But the good thing is that they move through their Auspices freely... the issue there, however, is that the Auspice lists are also pretty small.

                  Arcas Gifts

                  Typically the first Auspice a Gurahl experiences, you'll move past this when you leave your mentor's presence and begin working alone more, even when staying in touch with your mentor. Returning to this Auspice later might relate to you realizing that the fool, trickster, cub etc. have the wisdom of knowing that they know nothing. Arcas is associated with the New Moon and is akin to Ragabash of course. Why they didn't just go "use the Ragabash list" I'm not sure.

                  Since you're going to start here, you'll probably only pick up Rank 1 Gifts for 3xp, and from then on you'll be paying 10/15/20/25xp, or, 6/9/12/15 if you circle back to Arcas.

                  Open Seal
                  Pulse of the Prey
                  Trackless Waste
                  Favour of Ursa Minor (Luna's Blessing)

                  Walk Like a Man (1): For no cost, with a difficulty 7 Dexterity + Larceny, you can leave behind your choice of human footprints or bear prints, regardless of what form you're in.

                  This is a nice QOL upgrade which could easily be "always on" with no roll. Gurahl being able to leave human footprints in their Crinos form, for example, would be really neat. All in all this is handy and worth getting. Presumably if a Garou or Corax copied it over you'd be leaving wolf or bird prints.

                  Cajole (2): Spend 1 Gnosis, roll Charisma + Empathy, and on a success you can get an "individual" (presumably includes animals, spirits, Fera and humans) to give you something. With 5 successes you can apparently get a human to give you their house.

                  We don't get any details on how Veil-compliant this is, or whether the spell 'breaks' afterwards, like "Jesus Christ why the hell did I just give you my house I need somewhere to LIVE", but all the same this is very powerful. It's unusually on-the-nose mind control for WTA with a very broad list of uses, akin to Dominate 2 from VTM. I love Garou, but the WoD would be in a much worse state if Ragabash got this, I won't lie.

                  Safe Passage (3): Spend 1 Gnosis, roll Dexterity + Primal-Urge (difficulty 6), 1 success lets you leave no traces of your passage for a scene, every additional success lets you include a +1, so with 5 successes you could guide 5 people through a snowy meadow without leaving any footprints.

                  The trouble is that this lasts for a scene, not "12 hours" or whatever, and it costs Gnosis. The fact that you could get 1 success and fail to include your allies also really sucks, though given that you're a giant bear monster it's believable that you could carry them all on your back!

                  For Gurahl I'd say this is Amber, but keep in mind that there are Gifts and Fetishes which do this job far better. Spending 9/15xp for this could be Red in plenty of games.

                  Rejuvenate (4): Breath of the Wyld, a Level 1 Black Fury Gift, but instead of lasting for a scene it lasts for (Succour) days. +1 die to all mental rolls for, say, 7 days is basically akin to having +1 to Intelligence, Wits and Perception. The +1 difficulty to Rage rolls is also very good for a Gurahl since it takes the odds of you frenzying purely from anger from "unlikely" to "basically non-existant", which makes you much more reliable arguably.

                  What does +1 to mental stats tend to get you? Well you get +1 to the dice rolls, not the stats themselves, which is an important distinction sometimes. If you have any Gifts that key off of Wits, Intelligence or Perception they'll benefit somewhat from this, especially at low difficulties.

                  Natural State (5): This Gift is meant to act as a 'reset' button, restoring an area to how it was 'naturally'. This is an interesting thing, by the way. Does a farmhouse return to a meadow? Does it return to the forest which was cut down to make way for the meadow? Does it return to whatever came before the forest?

                  Still, you spend 1 Gnosis, and you make a difficulty 8 Manipulation + Primal-Urge.

                  I hate Level 5 Gifts thinking they need high difficulty rolls. The difficult thing about doing this is that you need to be Rank 5, and you need to spend a bunch of xp and then some Gnosis. Making it difficulty 8 doesn't make me think "wow, this must be BIG!" it makes me think "what a pain, gonna waste some Gnosis getting this to actually work". Arguably high Level Gifts should have low difficulty for their activation rolls since that makes them more powerful and coveted. But anyways...

                  More successes means a the reversion to nature is more "absolute". So with 1 success you might fill a whole building with branches and moss, as a new glade starts to choke the structure, but with 5 successes the whole building might be torn apart by trees and disappear into thin air, along with all its contents. The results are very much in the hands of the ST, but the description of the power is very impressive.

                  The area you cover is Honour x 20 yards. So with an Honour of 8, say, you'd cover 180 yards as a radius, or 0.1 miles ish. Go on an interactive online map which lets you draw a 0.1 mile radius around, say, your local train station, or airport, or whatever.

                  Yep. This is strong. It's awkward to use, but it's very strong. Being able to just destroy a Pentex installation is great, even if you get middling results with 2 successes, for all intents and purposes you have destroyed the building. I'd argue that difficulty 6 would be just fine, but the Gift works as is, and it's a suitably powerful 'epic' Level 5 Power.

                  Honestly this list isn't awful, it just needs like... ~5 more Gifts. It's too small. And yeah, they kept the lists small because Gurahl move between them and can gradually learn everything they want, but they really overdid it with making the lists tiny. Grab a few Ragabash Gifts, say 2 Greens and 3 Ambers, and add them on IMO.

Naturally having additional Incapacitated health levels has its utility limited by the fact that if you're Incapacitated you can't act, but this Gift can very strongly protect someone from death, whether by injuries or disease or poison.

Assuming that, I'm going to say this is a must-have, but it's very annoying how expensive and late it is. If you're a Rank 5 Gurahl you ought to get this (for action economy, which is a hugely important aspect of any combat TTRPG), but that doesn't make it amazing either. Green through circumstance.

                              Kojubat Gifts

                              Akin to Galliard and tied to the Gibbous Moon, Kojubat is typically entered when a Gurahl becomes a mentor, most likely a Buri-Jaan, for the first time.

                              Eyes of the Soul (Scent of the True Form)
                              Mind to Mind (Mindspeak)
                              Shadows by the Firelight

Anyways beyond that Mindspeak is a Level 1 Gift, so learning it at Rank 3 ain't great. I wouldn't pay 9xp for that Gift personally. Learning Scent of the True Form at Rank 2 instead of 1 isn't great but the Gift is still incredible.

                              Stonesight (1): This Gift lets you appraise anything made of stone or rock. This helps you to find hidden minerals, fossils, precious items hidden within stones, and would also help you to appraise a magical object which is made from stone or rock.

You hear the most significant event which has taken place in the last (successes) days, within the presence of this earth. So you might hear a conspiracy being plotted in a remote glade by touching the earth there, or you might hear a roaring argument in a Pentex rep's office a few days back by touching the soil in his potted plant.

                              This is pretty great. Even if you assume this will only ever work outdoors, and almost never work indoors, the Gift is a great detective tool which will help you find out a lot of useful stuff in a Chronicle.

                              We don't get any clue as to how this would help in a fight, but I imagine any good Storyteller would highly value "knowing everything your target is going to do a second before they do it" in mechanics, helping you with dodges and parries and so on. Great for interrogations, but you can't just read someone's history like a book.

                              Probe Thoughts (4): Spend 1 Gnosis, plus 1 Willpower if you're targeting a supernatural being, then roll Wits + Empathy, difficulty of the target's Willpower. Every success lets you find out one thing they're trying to keep concealed. Worth noting that "trying to keep concealed" could get you a lot of additional info which might be interesting but not directly relevant, like "I am the father of the cub being raised by the Smiths".

                              Aversion Therapy (5): Attach a strong negative emotion to something for a year and a day. The example given is that a thief could be made to feel terror every time they contemplate stealing. This would be great for gently helping a criminal, a gossip, an addict, and so on. The ability to make someone feel strong disgust every time they consider harming a human or bear would be a good way to peacefully disarm a threat, for example, if it were a creature which couldn't otherwise know better (like a feral beast).

                              Spend 1 Gnosis (and 1 Willpower if supernatural), roll Wits + Empathy difficulty of the target's Willpower. Nice and easy, 1 success gets the full effect.

                              I really like this one, and it could very strongly define a character's role in society and in a story, it's got the right "absolute" quality a Level 5 Gift should have. But I wouldn't call it a must-have that every Rank 5 Gurahl should make sure they pick up.

                                      Kieh Gifts

                                      Tied to the Crescent Moon and akin to Theurges, a Gurahl might become a Kieh the first time they take up the role of Buri-Jaan.

                                      Spirit Speech
                                      Sense the Unnatural
                                      Exorcism, Name the Spirit

                                      Getting Sense the Unnatural at Rank 2 is excellent. Name the Spirit arrives slightly late.

                                      Diagnose* (1): Perform a full medical examination with a glance.

                                      So, I'm well within my expertise as a doctor of medicine to say that this would be equally important to tending to the sick as Mother's Touch. Actually examining a patient properly takes time, and it involves exhaustively checking things you're 99% sure will be fine, only to occasionally go "wait... I can't hear ANY sounds from his left lung..." when looking over a patient you thought was completely fine. Being able to do a full appraisal of someone's health using the Medicine Knowledge without having to get their shirt off and grab tools and put them in various positions is very, very handy.

                                      At a glance you might note that a random mook has lung cancer, that a Pentex rep has a rare blood disorder which is being treated but not cured, that a powerful Garou champion will suffer from seizures if exposed to flashing lights. So, if your ST is actually interested in you being Doctor House the werebear, this Gift could be absolutely character-defining. If your ST is forgetful, or lame, or creates undetailed encounters with unfleshed-out characters, this won't do much at all.

                                      It's also important to note that this Gift gives your character information that you as a player will often have, and will often bleed into character knowledge. For example say a character in your team has been poisoned and is taking Aggravated damage every turn. You, the player, know that they've taken a bunch of Aggravated damage. The character, meanwhile, might not. But a type of benign meta-gaming we all mostly accept is that you'll throw out a Mother's Touch to heal the person, despite you having no IC way to know that they are suffering from damage which is worth you 'wasting' a point of Gnosis on. This Gift basically alleviates that discrepency and gives you a way to have a solid grasp of who needs healing and who doesn't.

                                      This is Green, but heavily caveated. If you know what you're doing as a player and character, and invest in the Medicine Knowledge in a world where the health of people and animals matters (which a good WTA game should focus on!), this could be incredible. It could also be a flavour tax if your ST makes shallow white-room dnd-like encounters.

                                      Refresh (2): This is Resist Pain but targeting another, it requires touch, and it costs Gnosis instead of Willpower. Whoever you touch with this Gift can ignore Wound Penalties for the entire 'combat'.

                                      Given that all Gurahl get Resist Pain (or "Ignore Wounds") at Rank 1, this is much more useful for helping Kinfolk, Garou/Fera allies and so on.

                                      That said, there is a very devious application of this Gift. Use it on a BSD or other Wyrm-touched enemy who feeds on their pain, who draws power from it, and you might significantly weaken them. Think Darth Sion in KOTOR 2 - he literally can't live without his pain, the power he draws from it is all that keeps him going.

                                      All in all this is a solid amber, worth grabbing especially if you have allies who need it or if you're consistently left with Gnosis left over. That said, to be quite honest, if you have time to touch an ally who is suffering from wounds... you'd probably just use Mother's Touch. Yes, you can use this on someone who hasn't been wounded YET, it isn't useless, but in many circumstances you'd be better off just using Mother's Touch! If beneficiaries of this Gift reduced their Soak difficulties by 1 this would be much better.

                                      Spirit Healing (4): Charisma + Occult, difficulty of the target Spirit's Willpower (so 7-10 in the case of most powerful spirits). If you succeed, you're permitted to spend Gnosis to heal them, at a 1:2 ratio, so 4 Gnosis would restore 8 Essence.

                                      This is god-awful.

                                      This is both weak and redundant. Skip it.

                                      Spirit Shape (4): Disguise yourself as a spirit, in a way which will convince spirits, for days at a time. This costs 1 Gnosis and you have to pass an Appearance + Enigmas (difficulty 7) roll.

                                      You take on the 'desired' appearance (so perhaps "Bear Spirit", or "Dolphin Spirit", or "Electricity Elemental" etc.) for (Wisdom) days. You can also end the Gift when you wish.

Disguising yourself as a Bane so that you can walk around the most hellish places in the Umbra with impugnity, restoring purity and health where it's needed most, would be a great use. Hell, imagine playing some Garou PCs and finding that one of many Banes you encounter in a hellish wyrmscape turns into a human when attacked, declaring themselves an ally who was just disguising themselves as a bane in order to help Gaia. Do the Pack trust this stranger?

                                      More high-level Gifts need to be like this. Absolute wording, no "if you get 1 success you'll only convince Spirits who don't beat 1 success on a Gnosis roll", no nonsense, and it lasts for days.

                                      Image of the Sky Bear (5): Spend 1 Gnosis and roll Wits + Rituals, difficulty 6. 7 points of starlight appear around you, giving you 7 'charges' of this Gift to expend.

                                      A charge can be used to:

                                      1) Instantly heal 5 Bashing or Lethal from a target on touch

                                      2) Instantly heal 3 Aggravated from a target on touch

                                      3) Grant mystical body armour which is equivalent to Kevlar in protection (3 armour)

                                      It's unclear whether the 'kevlar' option inflicts the -1 Dexterity penalty which is associated with such armour. Presumably not, but ask your ST. If they're cool they'll say "no, it's weightless mystical armour, obviously" though it was silly of the writers to not just make the Gift say "grant +3 soak to the target".

                                      For 1 Gnosis you get the ability to heal 21 Aggravated, or 35 Bashing/Lethal, or some combination of those. This is an incredible party healing ability which would let you restore a small crowd from near-death to being fighting fit. This Gift also lets you heal yourself, and remember that RAW werebears don't get Mother's Touch! It also helps you to heal those who would otherwise be very hard to heal with Mother's Touch due to their high Rage.

                                      Basically in any situation where you're healing people, this Gift is probably superior, unless you have a single turn to heal 4+ points of Aggravated, in which case you should use Mother's Touch and just pray for 4+ successes. A great saving of Gnosis, plus a great way to grant armour to allies. This is among the better Rank 5 Gifts.

                                                  Rites

                                                  The werebears also know most of the rites common to Garou, having taught many of them to the werewolves to begin with.

                                                  A simple Rite you can set up before you sleep or hibernate, which gives you visions of things that take place during your slumber. This means you might awaken from a year's sleep, or a night's, fully aware of important events you need to know. You tend to see stuff which is local (in your territory) or very relevant to you. The less relevant something is, the harder it is to see, which is reflected in the difficulty of the dice roll.

                                                  This is excellent, easy to learn, and basically allows you to encounter information you'd otherwise really struggle to come by. For example as a Gurahl you might awaken from your slumber with dreams of Garou having moved into your territory and already showing signs of high aggression against your Kinfolk; that information could absolutely save your life!

                                                  Read the future using the guts of a fish you've just pulled from a river. Pretty standard.

                                                  Rite of True Mating

                                                  Create a talisman which pulls you towards a suitable Kinfolk mate. This Rite might help you to find one Kinfolk in a city where you otherwise could have found none, so immediately it's pretty handy! It will default to someone who is fertile and would suit you as a mate to produce more Gurahl with, it's entirely practical, but if you're looking for love and you're compatible with the opposite sex this could work quite well.

                                                  Rite of Fighting the Death Bear

                                                  Fight Mangi, the Death Bear, and if you win you can reclaim someone who has died. This doesn't cost permanent Gnosis and you can do it at any Rank. Mangi is supposed to be statted in a way where he will "rarely" be beaten by mid-ranked Gurahl. If this Rite is one you feel comfortable and confident calling upon, your Storyteller is going too easy on you!
