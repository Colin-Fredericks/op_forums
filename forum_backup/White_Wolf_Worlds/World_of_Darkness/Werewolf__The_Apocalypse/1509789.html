                      So, the thing about mages, is that the possibilities are technically endless. When a Garou learns a new Gift or Rite, or attunes a new Fetish, they get singular package that work how it works. When a mage increases a Sphere, they get a infinitely large set of new things they can do: both in expanding the power over the subjects of the Sphere, and combining this new power with their other Spheres in new and different ways.

                      The biggest thing about learning Mage is not really "what can a mage do?" but rather, "why can't this mage do this thing?" In Mage, mages are literally willing reality to change into what they want it to be (if usually temporarily). What holds them back is that the universe is far too complex to just will it into shape solely with the knowledge that reality can be shaped in the first place. To shape reality mages need a way to understand reality and give themselves a scaffold their still human minds can use to frame what they're doing. This overarching philosophy is called a Focus (in M20 anyway): a set of beliefs in how magic works (paradigm), the way beliefs can be used to manifest changes (practices), and the specific methods to enact it (instruments).

                      This Focus is necessary to let mages know what they're doing, but also limits them because what they do has to fit into that Focus to work. A mage that believes their magic is enabled by praying for permission to a higher power to alter how that power made the world can't just build a wand that shoots lightning bolt when you say the activation word. If they want a magic item of burning things, it has to fit their religious style.


                      Sphere's are simply a measure of which parts of reality a mage knows how to manipulate

                              "Sleepers" is a word that's complicated by having mechanical and in-character definitions that are not always 100% aligned. But the essential thing is that all "humans" are mages waiting to "wake up." So every human (or at least every human that still has their magical potential intact) is subconsciously shaping reality too. In modern times this is called Consensus: the collective subconscious effect of Sleepers defining a mutual reality. So, basically, Sleepers are inadvertently casting countermagic at any magic that violates Consensus. The only way for their innate magical potential to express itself is to help reinforce Consensus (or the local rules of Reality in places that Consensus doesn't dominate).

                              This also metaphysically explains why non-mages don't suffer from Sleepers witnessing their powers: mages need to be Awakened to learn Spheres and understand reality well enough to try to counter powers that aren't reality warping mage-magic.

                              The effect of Sleepers is also very weak once you leave Earth (either physically go into space, or go to the Umbra). If you drag a Sleeper into the Umbra, they have no inherent effect on mages in the Umbra with them, because the Umbra is too strongly controlled by vaster entities than one mere human's latent magical potential. Umbra exploring mages have to contend with the rules of Realms like any other Umbra explorer does. So Sleepers impact on the whole universe isn't so grand.

                              Mage also has a concept called the Earthly Foundations: axiomatic truths so strong that even mages can't change them. Mages cannot create a rock so heavy they can't lift it, effectively, is the rule of the Mage metaphysics. Of course, who created the Earthly Foundations - since magic itself is impossible without them - is not known. Gaia and the Triat are, however, a great set of candidates for a WtA game.

                              The number of Spheres is currently nine because both the Traditions and the Technocracy spend centuries coding that into Consensus. So, in theory, it can be changed. In the past there were different numbers of them, and in the present variations have become more commonplace. So nine is not an Earthly Foundation. It's not an immutable rule. But it's a strong enough rule that breaking out of it would be extremely difficult. However, whatever the number is, it's always dividing up everything in the universe. In the Dark Ages most mages studied four Pillars (which were all different based on magical Fellowship), and those Pillars covered the same things as the nine Spheres... just arranged differently.
