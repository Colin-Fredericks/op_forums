                                  Deed Names are hard because by their very nature they should be names OTHER people come up for your character based on what they see are your most distinguishing characteristics. Hopefully these would be well educated Galliards with the cultural context and creativity to assign good names.

                                  In actuality, it's the player with a conception of the kind of character they want to be that gives their own character the name, which usually does not really fit the character. Often I am confronted with a character with some badass name, and then when it's explained how they got that name (from their backstory), I'm like "What? Based on that, this is what you call yourself?" (Sometimes I feel, "That is very easy for you to say in your backstory, I want to see something equivalent during game.").

                                  I also don't think "deed names" is the best description for the concept. Should people really be known for a "deed" (and I use the term loosely) they performed during their rite of passage? They probably do far greater deeds later on - yet the name rarely changes because the player wants that to be their name (although I occasionally see exceptions). I think of Garou names as more like poetic epithets as a nickname. They are descriptive of the person, but not necessarily of deeds. It might be based on how the look, a particular habit they have, or anything else that might be memorable about them. And they should be something that makes it easy for people to remember as that person is mentioned in the oral epic poetry of the Garou.

                                  I also think that perhaps these names are simply the English translation of what their "Garou Tongue name" means that they are given in their equivalent of a baptism/naming ritual done after their Rite of Passage - probably given by some teacher who liked them when they were a cub. While many people don't know the meaning behind their name, thinking it as just cool sounding, real names actually have real meanings. Michael means "Who is Like God?" Henry is "Home Ruler". Kevin means "Born Handsome". Marcus means "Consecrated to the God of War." They all have a real mening even though they come from a different language family (Hebrew, German, Gaelic, and Latin). Although I don't know for certain, I think many Native American names like Red Cloud, Sitting Bull, Black Elk, etc. - which are probably the origin of the concept of Garou "deed names" - are probably just the same way. It's just that we no longer intuitively know what our names mean so they just sound like random sounds to us. But in the past, people knew exactly what they were calling their children. It's different for people who write their language in Chinese characters as the logograms have specific meanings so that one absolutely knows that Zedong means "East of the Marsh" because that's what the logograms mean (of course someone who knows Chinese is going to correct me as to the proper translation now!). We lose that with phonetic spellings.
