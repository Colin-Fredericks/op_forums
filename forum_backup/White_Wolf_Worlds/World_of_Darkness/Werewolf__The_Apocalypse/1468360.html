    Spirits, Gift teaching, and Ronin

    11-19-2021, 02:35 PM
    For a while now I've been thinking about ways to expand the use of Ronin in my chronicles as an ST. I think they are often an underutilized part of the setting. One issue that I've had is that Ronin don't have Rank and thus can't learn Gifts from spirits since Gifts are linked to Rank. Instead, they learn them from other Garou which essentially means other Ronin, and that limits what Gifts are available. Another option is to learn from Banes, but that is limiting from a character and ST perspective because it becomes a corruption story at that point.

    However, I now think I may have been too restrictive on that. After rereading what's written on both Ronin and Skin Dancers, it seems clear that they can learn Gifts from spirits provided those are “unassociated spirits” as the W20 corebook describes them. And that these two Garou, despite not having Ranks, can learn Gifts of any level... provided they can find spirits willing to teach them.

    So that got me thinking, what kind of spirits are willing to teach Ronin Gifts?

    Obviously none of the spirits that belong to one of the spirit broods of the Garou tribal totems shouldn't do so. (There could always be atypical individual spirits that would normally be part of a brood, but for whatever reason doesn't belong to one despite similar spirits belonging. But they wouldn't be normal.)

    I then wondered whether any of the broods belonging to any of the other Fera might be willing to do so, especially those breeds most antagonistic to the Garou. But I decided against it. Ultimately those spirits follow the same laws as everyone else, and they shouldn't be willing to teach Gifts to an outcast, rankless Changing Breed as it would shortchange their own Fera who need still need to follow their own Rank = Level system.

    So these “unassociated spirits” should either be spirits that don't belong to any brood, or belong to some theoretical brood that is clearly separate from those broods associated with the Garou and Fera.

    We know Minotaur is one such spirit, and he might have a small brood of his own (the Minotaur pack totem description says “places his children in contact wit spirits that can teach them Gifts, and thus his children can learn most Gifts of Rank Three or lower.”). What other kind of spirits should Minotaur have access to that he could put Skin Dancers into contact with them?

    Tick, from the Skinner SAS, could be another. That spirit is clearly looking for power and willing to not follow the normal rules. Since he's shown as a Skin Dancer totem, it's likely he was already one of those spirits Minotaur used to teach Gifts to children.

    What other “abandoned” or “ignored” spirits would be willing to defy the Pact? Minotaur is not a direct servant of the Wyrm but does bear a faint touch of taint from the Beast-of-War. Tick is mentioned as a Totem of Cunning. While I don't think this means that the canonical Gaian totems of Cunning mentioned in the sourcebooks fit here, I do think we need to thinking about spirits that would seem to fit into the Cunning category. And we could also look at spirits that might be partially corrupted, but not yet fallen.

    So Bat might make a good example if we follow the idea that Bat is schizophrenic with one aspect fallen and one aspect redeemed. Since Ronin are inbetween these two extremes, surely some Bat spirits should be open to this.

    The W20 White Howlers tribebook gives us Carrion Bird totem. This totem primarily serves BSD packs, but it also implies that Gaian Garou can do so well as well provided they are willing to lose Honor Renown and be viewed as possibly Wyrm tainted by the other Garou. So once again we have a totem that straddles the line between corrupt and Gaian.

    In terms of non-canonical spirits, should we follow Tick's example and look at other parasitical animals like lice, leeches, lampreys? Disreputable animals that spread disease like houseflies? (We know not all such animals would fit here – the game has Gaian totems like Cuckoo, Flea and Tsetse Fly, and the Wendigo brood includes tundra mosquitoes.)

    Are there examples of spirits the Garou have ignored, spurned, or offended who also might fit into this category?

    I'm especially looking for ideas of non-corrupted Gaian spirits that may nevertheless operate outside the restrictions established by the Pact. They might be dark or treacherous spirits, but not tainted. The scorpion, for example, is an animal often associated with treachery because it is in its nature per the fable the Scorpion and the Frog. It might be a spirit that is open to teaching Gifts to such outcast Garou.

                      Rank and level of Gift are connected, but the book also says explicitly that this can be waived if the spirit so decides. I see it as first indicator that any spirit may be persuaded into breaking the rules under the right circumstances. Not that Ronins don't gave a tougher time, but that exceptions may occur in many ways.

                      I don't remember reading much on the Pact, so I would like some points to where go for it to have a better grasp of this part. I personally never liked the notion of the spirit world bowing so much to the rules of the Tribes, but that's another thing.

                      Also, there are other situations that outright indicate that spirits not always do things by the rules, sometimes in far more glaring ways than teaching Ronin, like how Cockroach is kind of adopting the Samsa Breed and teaching them Gifts.

                      On which spirits would be more likely to do that, I think it would be the more overlooked ones. Spirits that have no contact with the peoples the Garou live among, spirits of things too inconspicuous or easily mistaken as other, more well known spirits.

                      The dubious nature of spirits like Tick and Minotaur makes sense for relatively high-profile spirits dealing with Ronin, as those are the spirits more likely to be pressed by circumstances to position themselves and sought by Garou when their position is known. Spirits of little-known species, almost unheard-off technologies and so on, especially if they're too easily mistaken with other well known spirits, are the most likely to be out of any broader accord or so rarely sought to not make a difference.

                      You can also decide to focus on spirits from the High Umbra. AFAIK the Garou have little understanding of the place and its spirits, but it isn't impossible for them to travel there, just harder. Those spirits are unlikely to be bound to the same rules and expectations as their Middle Umbra "cousins", and are a good way to give Ronin potential access to Gifts above level 3.

                      But I think the main point should be to let the PCs come up ideas on how to find a spirit that may teach them, and think what are the intrinsic difficulties that would justify most Ronin not going that route. Individual spirits that teach Ronin may exact heft prices or be far more recluse than others of their kind, and must be tracked specifically, as they're individuals. "Forgotten" spirits may be willing to teach only those who can not only find them, but identify them correctly, maybe even having a reputation for refusing Garou because they don't teach Gifts when mistaken. Astral Spirits are naturally hard to reach and harder still for Garou to understand.

                          In our LARP game, I tend to create obstacles for a lot of player ideas involving "finding a spirit that should have a certain power advantageous in our situation and make it help us" by making spirits not that cooperative or stating that the players need to find certain items to summon a spirit powerful enough to do, what they want, because, spirits are relatively rare, and those you find are most often gaffelings.                               On the conceptual side of things it’s definitely going to be spirits that exist on a more dangerous, or at least a more individualist and unorthodox sphere then what Garou society is typically comfortable with.

                              Garou society despite being as antisocial as it can sometimes be is still heavily social, with packs as the atomic unit of Garou society and both the wolf and human social instincts manifesting with heavy investment in community. While human influences from the homids can lead this to become a society of hierarchies and orthodoxies, the matter remains that Garou society is highly social and this expands to the spirits it has long standing relationships with.

                              So them for a Ronin (or Lone Wolf as I usually prefer calling them), they have the difficult task of finding spirits that will be willing to work with them and thus most likely they will not have major pre-existing relations with the Garou Nation or have a natural suspicion or contradiction for working with creatures that have become alienated from society and tradition.

                              So as far as animals are concerned then, it seems that any animal with a strong association with family or social values is going to be off the table, alongside traditional tribal totems and Garou spirit patrons. Spirits which have associations with independence or unorthodoxy will probably be more amenable to negotiations, such as many Big Cat spirits or several Reptilian spirits.

                              I also imagine that Lone Wolves could have some success with spirits of natural phenomena that are either agnostic or even contradictory to tradition and society. Spirits such as weather phenomena like Thunder Storm or Wildfire might provide patronage for lone wolves, although they’re no doubt going to have strong Wyld-influences. This however seems somewhat unavoidable as I imagine that most Weaver-influenced spirits are going to be disturbed by a Lone Wolves rejection of social order while most wise alone Wolves ought to avoid Wyrm-influenced spirits lest they become recruitment targets of the Black Spiral Dancers (although that’s not to say Maggot or Carrion Bird couldn’t work as hypothetical spirit patrons for a Lone Wolf).

                              To briefly cover other concepts that come to mind - I think most material and plant spirits would be difficult to work with as they’re probably already comfortable with ancient relationships established to the Garou Nation although perhaps things like Rivers or mountains prone to Landslides or Seismic Shifts might be more amenable to a change of direction as could plants that are more inconstant and transitory or perhaps even are of an invasive nature. I think most spirits associated with humans will probably be uncomfortable with social outcasts (except of course those human spirits who are themselves associated with outcasts such as Circus, Thief, Pariah, Stray, Revolutionary, Ecstasy, Wrath, etc.).

                              Dealing with many of these spirits would likely be dangerous and I agree that it would probably likely include heavier investments for establish relationships rather then calling on pre-existing ones but I also think it could be done and could even be used to demonstrate how there are other ways that things can be done then what is the orthodoxy of the Garou Nation.

                                                  I asked before and will ask once more: where does the wording and circumstances of the Pact can be found and read?

                                                  This is relevant, because while I understand the focus of the thread is to figure out spirits more likely to deal with Ronin and teach gifts, I'm strongly under the impression that you put yourself in a tight situation by making a too strict read of the spirits, how they behave and to what are they bound.

                                                  As far as I can tell, most or all the Werewolf lore have one problem in this regard: an unreliable narrator. Even when the narrator is an OoC Third Person they can be unreliable, as the rules on spirit interaction are purposefully written from the PoV of the Garou Nation. AFAIK there are indications that the reality is somewhat different from the perspective of the spirits themselves. And although it wouldn't be enough to make a Ronin's life as easy as a Tribe Member's, it guards the crux of the matter.
