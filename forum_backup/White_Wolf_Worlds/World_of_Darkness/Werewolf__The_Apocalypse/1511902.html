    Garou Gifts: Ranking, Thinking, Overthinking

    05-17-2023, 03:59 PM
    I'm going to break down Gifts by level and category, bit by bit. If it's Wyrmish, Fera-only, obscure or lost it's going to feature much much later, if at all. This is intended to focus on the Gifts you will actually be getting in play, hence starting at level 1 and working up. I'll be considering utility (both inside and outside combat), room for creativity, fun ways to get the most enjoyment out of a Gift, when you should take it, why, and so on. Accessibility is also going to come up when relevant.

    Given how cheap Gifts are, especially the level 1 and 2 gifts, finding value and fun in a great number of them can lead to a fun and varied playstyle. Enjoy and let me know what you think! I'd love to hear if I missed any whacky or obvious applications.

    Let's begin with Rank 1 Homid Gifts!

    Note that I will be dividing Gifts into three descriptive categories intended to give a really quick distillation of my thoughts. Think of it like traffic lights. Green are must-haves, or possibly must-reworks depending on your table and how adversarial or cheeky the environment is. Do what works for your table of course. Amber are fun, cool, perhaps niche or minor effects, have good RP potential perhaps, and are ultimately worth taking if you like the concept or have spare EXP. Thankfully most Gifts are, based on what I know before starting this thorough review, Green or Amber! Red are Gifts which are underwhelming, overcosted, too pricey to use, have minor or ultra-niche effects, and don't have any "hook" for RP that makes them worth taking. I'm hoping very, very few will be Red, but I can think of a few!

    Apecraft's Blessings: we start off VERY strong here. Even with a modest Wits + Crafts pool, -1 or -2 difficulty to most practical tasks you can imagine is exceptional. This Gift is good enough that it would still be viable at Rank 3 or 4. It's taken very frequently at char-gen and with good reason; it's one of Homid's superpowers, and STs should be cautious about Ancestor Spirits teaching it to Metis and Lupus, even with the extra cost. Driving, shooting, cooking, sewing, pole vaulting, piloting an airplane without any training... the near universal applications are incredible even if you were to limit the difficulty decrease to 1 or 2. RAW there's no reason you shouldn't shoot for a 5 or 6 success Wits + Crafts check.

    If you find this one is creating problems, adding a Gnosis cost or making it once per scene wouldn't be too absurd. See what works at your table.

    City Running: this one is pretty neat. Spending a point of Rage can hurt especially if you don't have much to spend, but it's a fun ability which makes you impossible to catch for many. Picture someone scaling a fire escape as swiftly as they could run the 100 meters. The difficulty reduction is also nice, and while this one is somewhat niche it's bound to come up in a traditionally urban WoD game; back alleys, fire exits, rooftops and so on are pretty likely places to be chasing Fomori, Vampires and so on.

    On the proactive front, having an urban base which is almost impossible to access without this Gift could be a pretty neat setup. You can also try to drag your confrontations and battles onto scaffolding for cool cinematic fights in which you are much more able to navigate easily.

    Master of Fire: underrated because people don't imagine their Garou burning to death, and because spending Gnosis on a niche ability seems eh. The thing is that while you don't often run through burning buildings, fight hunters with flamethrowers or arsonist blood mages, nor will you frequently run into a Flambeau duelist or a fire-breathing Bane/Fomor, all these threats do add up to coming up at least once per campaign I'd wager, and perhaps much more often than that.

    Aggravated damage is still hellish for Garou, as you can't soak it in your Breed form (unless you're Metis) and you can't regenerate it either. Turning fires into Bashing damage makes them somewhat trivial sources of damage, and also allows you to proactively use fire as a weapon. Yes you won't often have an incendiary grenade thrown at you, but a packmate chucking napalm or a fire-starting Gift at you while you have an enemy pinned down... it's messy and risky, but "I take (3-soak) bashing per turn, you take 3 aggravated damage per turn" is a pretty good trade off and will make you look pretty badass.

    Persuasion: They did it again! This one's just Apecraft's Blessings but for social stuff! A wonderfully strong social Gift which you'll see being utilized more frequently than any other Gift in some PBP servers, Persuasion is a neat power which could also do fine as a level 3 or 4, not that it's necessarily a problem at level 1. The lack of a Gnosis cost does stand out.

    My only serious suggestion to change this power which should be used at all tables is to note that "has the potential to change long-held views", "successes are much more powerful than normal social checks" etc. should only apply to regular mortals. If Garou could un-bigot eachother, or re-bigot eachother, the setting would look entirely different. It's also a power which plays up the necessity of having some form of check to notice when supernatural powers are being used, much like Presence does. Garou should consider utilizing this Gift upon other Garou without good justification to be inflammatory.

    Noting its lack of cost and it lasting all scene, the most optimal way to use this Gift is forever, always, in all situations. It is however not very fun. If you are left to police your own usage of this power by a relaxed ST or a server where homebrew is avoided, I'd advise utilizing it only when you can picture the character going "hmm, this is tricky - perhaps I need a little help". Your Garou will come off as more relatable and less like a character in a game applying their standard buff before a fight.

    Smell of Man: This one is fun and quite niche, and I quite enjoy it. I don't have much to say besides the obvious; it'll be handy when people have guard dogs, angry cats, wild wolves, other animal kinfolk and so on. If you want to actively utilize this power aggressively, dragging a fight you're having into a situation with aggressive domesticated or wild animals will have the favour tilted slightly in your direction, as you are treated like a friend by guard dogs and attacked less savagely by wild animals, but the benefit is very mild compared to the effort you've taken to arrange it. If you are a Galliard this combines very well with Beast Speech, since not only can you talk to domesticated animals they will also treat you like a great friend.

    Much like City Running and Master of Fire, this is one where you can go out of your way to try and trigger it to your advantage, and you can build a fun concept around it. The crazy cat lady Bone Gnawer Galliard, the security firm owning Glass Walker Ragabash who has a charmed touch with vicious guard dogs, the Child of Gaia vet who helps to rehabilitate traumatized and abused animals from fighting rings. If you want the fun RP flex of animals adoring you this is a neat one, and that alone is worth 3 EXP.



    As you can see Homids start off quite strong! Two Greens and three Ambers, and the RP potential and fun held within these offerings is pretty great. Given how prevalent Homids are, it's good that these Gifts are fun to use and RP with; they're some of the most common Gifts in WTA!

      RANK 1 METIS GIFTS

      Create Element: This one raises a lot of questions. Do you have to see where you're summoning the water/earth/fire/air? Does your ST have a good sense of volume and understand physics? Are people likely to be dying of thirst in your game? How much time are you going to be spending in abandoned Etherships with critical air supply? Is inflicting 1-3 Agg damage on a target within 60 feet a good attack? Can Create Element be dodged? Can earth appear in the air to drop?

      My take is that the material can only appear in places you can visualize, check for a good comparison when size is important, talk to your ST about expectations, and earth appears connected to the ground. As earth is want to do. The attacking potential is kinda interesting given that a Metis Ahroun might, potentially, lack ranged options. And a cubic foot is much bigger than you think.

      If you're forced to hold your breath for any reason, you could fill your huge Garou lungs with this Gift. And if you're on the run and can't stop for a drink, you can potentially fill your stomach with water. Both uses are niche but could save your life if they come up.

      Spending 1 Gnosis per pop is expensive, but the RP potential of the Gift is neat. It's obviously magical in a fun-ly demonstrative way you could show to a Fallow Kinfolk or new Cub. It has religious significance; pure elements conjured from Gaia's Gnosis, a sign that she lives, that creation is still possible, that there can be abundance again. It's among the more "obviously magical" level 1 gifts and I like that. Amber in most games, but Green if your ST is super permissive with it and access to water/air/fire are critical in your game.

      Primal Anger: Given that my Metis PC doesn't hate himself, this one raises interesting questions for me. Is self-hatred a necessary component of these Metis Gifts, or is it granted to Metis by the spirits because a typical Metis deals with shame and repression?

      Emotional stuff aside this power is wonderfully strong. If you have Huge Size or a means of healing, taking 1 Agg isn't that bad. This also combines really well with any powerful Gift that utilizes Rage, such as Channeling. Getting your Rage to go above your usual max could also be useful if you need to get yourself into a Frenzy for some reason, say to evade mind control or provide a threat which can't be reasoned with. I don't think this power needs any changes at all, it's a perfect Green; strong in an engaging way, without straying into feeling 'busted'. It's good on all Auspices, it's good for all Tribes, and it combos amazingly with the various powers you'll pick up as you go up in Rank.

      Rat Head: I adore this one. It has a body horror feeling to it which is nice and creepy. If your ST will let you use it in creative ways it's even better; can you fit your whole arm into a space you can fit your fingers into? If you can fit your face into a pipe, can you hide your whole body in it? The idea of my giant Metis Ahroun moving through someone's walls like a rat or snake is horrible, and I love it.

      The Gnosis cost... really isn't necessary. But it's there, and so this one takes a bit of a hit. Not only that you have to spend Gnosis and also pass a difficulty 7 roll. In all honesty this Gift would work with no cost and no roll, but we work with what we're given. It's neat, it's fun, it's a conversation starter.

      I am a Strength 9 Dexterity 6 Rage 5 Ahroun with Razor Claws. I am in your walls.

      Sense Wyrm: if you aren't an idiot, one of the best Gifts in the game. Top tier. Wonderful. Such a life-saver. Sense Wyrm on its own makes you a useful pack member. Any Pack which lacks someone with Sense Wyrm is going to be metaphysically blind to the enemy in a way which could cause real trouble; always have a Metis, Theurge or Elder Brother!

      I'd suggest rolling for your PC with this one, sometimes. "Roll at difficulty 9" gives meta information; there is a really subtle Wyrm taint nearby. Roll some dice. Maybe there's no taint. Maybe it's really subtle. Maybe it's really obvious. Then tell them what they detect, based on whether they succeed or fail, or whether there was taint at all. Make sure that your fellow players / your table understands that Wyrm Taint isn't Alignment; a Garou who just climbed out of a pit where they slaughtered a hundred Banes and twenty BSDs will reek of the Wyrm. A serial killer might have a similar taint to someone using a highly tainted perfume. Oh and don't use it in Malfeas. Or do, I'm not your dad.

      Shed: This one is really interesting! You can slip out of ropes, chains, all kinds of bonds, and grapples too. Given that many Wyrmish enemies cover you in nasty gunk from various orifices which can have all sorts of bad effects, the ability to 'refresh' your fur is also pretty great. If your ST will let you leave behind a fake pelt with this Gift it's also pretty neat.

      Strictly RAW you can only lose a part of your fur you need to lose to escape from something. This also leads to a complicated full body rope tie intended to help you shed all your fur, so the strict RAW approach might be a little silly here. I see no problem with letting people shed freely with this power, if they succeed on the roll of course.

      Shed also feeds into this "gross survivalist animal-themed powers" vibe Metis have, which is neat. I like the idea of Metis being granted Gifts by a generous spirit world that wants them to survive in spite of mistreatment. So it ticks some nice RP boxes too.

      ========================================

      Still looking good! No Reds yet, which doesn't surprise me. Level 1 Gifts are staples with a lot of attention paid to them. We'll get to the bad ones eventually.

          RANK 1 LUPUS GIFTS

          Level 1 Lupus Gifts lose out compared to Homid and Metis somewhat, so you'll lean more on your Tribal and Auspice lists. Let's get into it!

          Hare's Leap: Passing a roll to double your jumping distances for a scene is handy. Tripling with WP is also pretty good. Given how far you can leap in Lupus, this is pretty amazing.

          For a running leap you roll Strength + Athletics difficulty 3, and in Lupus you leap 7 feet per success. With Hare's Leap you can get 14 or 21 per success. Given the absurd distances you can leap with this power, it is in fact Green. I always wrote it off as kinda meh, not too good not too bad, but a significant weakness for Garou is that they are better in melee than in ranged. Well the Corax with the razor sharp agg damage feathers isn't laughing now, is he.

          If a Metis grabs this, reflexively shifting into Crinos midair makes for a fun combo. You can use this to reach helicopters, distant boats, ranged Fomori, snipers, and you can proactively set traps where leaping away 60 foot or so is the only way to avoid harm. Fighting in places with horribly complex platforms and the need for constant jumping is also pretty handy.

          In a way this is just a superior City Running, and it's one that your ST isn't likely to gatekeep; I'd argue it's worth 5 EXP if you've got it going spare.

          Heightened Senses: The Gnosis cost is painful, but if you're Lupus you've got Gnosis to spare. +1 Primal-Urge and -3 Perception difficulties in Crinos and Hispo are pretty amazing. Technically RAW you have -4 Perception difficulties in Hispo, as we're only told that the power doesn't stack with the Perception bonus of Lupus, but that's clearly not the intent.

          This is a great one for hunts, tracking and investigations. It also lets you pull off insane wolf-like scenting while in your Homid form, making this a handy investment for detectives in urban scenarios.

          The RP potential of becoming a super-detective is neat, and a private eye lupus who sniffs crime scenes to learn the brand of aftershave of the murderer's landlord is fun. 1 Gnosis is costly for that, but you have 5 to spend if you're a Lupus and the investigation may happen in a session of its own where there aren't other calls on your spirit power. In a PBP server this one would let you pick up stuff like who's bunking with who, which is bound to make you very unpopular, but also very knowledgeable. Could combo well with a Shadow Lord concept.

          Sense Prey: You are a Werewolf. You can find dinner.

          The fringe RP benefit of "I provide for my whole pack" is neat and all, but your pack should be thriving with the help of a supernaturally gifted transforming spirit-touched generation-spanning ultra-leader. To reiterate, you are a Werewolf. You can find dinner.

          Predator's Arsenal: If you're in a scenario where you don't mind leaving a body with claw or bite marks, you're in a scenario where you can use Glabro, Hispo or Lupus, or perhaps even Crinos. If you desperately need a claw in Homid form, you can do a Partial Transformation. This Gift is just plain bad, especially since the weapons you manifest only do Lethal.

          EDIT: Revising this up to Amber after feedback; it's still lower down in my opinion but the subtlety of it is neat. A situation where having claws/fangs (even subtle ones) is something you can get away with but slipping into Glabro isn't is a tiny bit niche, but such situations do exist, and of course you don't have to spend WP or do a tricky roll to get these Lethal claws. The Agg claws accessible through partial transformation are awesome but they are also very obvious and would look weird on a human body! Basically this one fits Amber, on reflection, but I don't have to like the gift either :'D

          Prey Mind: Each success on a difficult check, one which will typically be difficulty 9, gives you +1 die to checks to run away, escape and hide. The niche is already narrow. You need to be on the back foot and in need of escape, forced to fall back.

          Even if you get 2 or 3 bonus dice, those might not do you much good; impacting difficulty would be much more powerful. Furthermore you're now outrunning your Pack.

          If you need a swift escape, Hare's Leap in addition to the natural speed of Lupus form has you covered. Prey Mind is too hard to use, too narrow, and only helps you when you're losing. Having Gifts for emergencies is good, don't get me wrong. Gifts that heal or help you survive are awesome. But this one is just so marginal and so sad. Can only give it red.

          ================================================

          Sense Prey, Predator's Arsenal and Prey Mind are very sad. I would suggest keeping Sense Prey as is; it's a meh power for a PC to have, but it's meant to solve a problem that matters more "IRL" than it does in a typical game. For Predator's Arsenal, simply use the Partial Transformation system as the gift's mechanic; the Gift makes it so that the partial transformation of growing claws and fangs happens without spending WP or making a roll; that's handy! Finally Prey Mind would work better if it influenced difficulties instead of adding dice.

          I was shocked by how useful Hare's Leap is however, when you get into the fine details of the mechanics.

          If you're playing a Lupus, I'd suggest spending more EXP on your other lists at Rank 1, or saving up for a good Gift from another Breed.

                                          That's not actually how the book states it*, and plenty of Gifts specify being difficulty 6; which would be unnecessary if unstated was 6 anyway. Considering how few Gifts call for a roll and don't specify a difficulty or method of determining one, there's more evidence towards this being an oversight rather than all the instances of the Gift diff being a flat 6 being overly cautious (and if the authors were going to be clear that the diff is 6 if not stated, they should use the Rank 1 and 2 Gifts as reminders of that when it comes up).

                                          Create Element is also a Gift the screams for a quick chart of different difficulties for how exact you're trying to be with it (filling an empty tub in front of your PC vs. filling the driver's side of a car that's speeding by) to help create a more consistent expectation of what the Gift can do (or at least can do reliably).

                                          * - by the Chapter 5 rules, if the difficulty is unstated by the book, the ST gets the option to make it whatever they want, and only if neither the book nor the ST sets a difficulty do you default to 6.

                                              RANK 1 RAGABASH GIFTS

                                              Time for some RAGABASH GIFTS - these five are meant to support being the mercurial trickster, questioner and intrigue delver. Let's see!

                                              Blur of the Milky Eye: this one's interesting because its mechanics and flavour diverge. We're told that the effect is visual, which is an interesting limitation, but the mechanics tell us that it effects difficulties of Perception checks; implying it applies to scent, smell etc.

                                              Given that my PC is blind I find this gift interesting. I'm going to assume for the purposes of ranking that it impacts all five senses. "Perception rolls made to detect him" is interesting because it could apply to mystic senses too; that wording is very broad. Have fun arguing that even if a Hermetic Mage scries your location, they should have a hard time actually spotting you in the scene they witness.

                                              A Difficulty 8 Manipulation + Stealth is hard though. Ultimately the gift is a very solid Amber; it's a great one to have, but the difficulty to use it is very high for some reason. With a good dice pool you'll get great mileage out of it. Remaining undetected and leading ambushes is a very great swing in the party's direction as turn economy is everything, and a round without resistance will help bring a fight to a close much sooner.

                                              Infectious Laughter: Much like Liar's Face, this one is a sort of pseudo-mind control situation. You can make people lose hold of their anger and forget what made them angry. That's potent! One thing to keep in mind is that it says nothing of the memories of those who were not angry. Spirits are noteworthy in the sense that what makes them angry is quite different to what makes others angry. You could make Great White Tail forget you did not greet him respectfully, which is great. However the Squirrel Jaggling that follows one of the Caern's Elders around might have been more curious about you than angry, and later remind their friend of what you said that angered them.

                                              In a way this is a sort of "undo" button in RP. A CTRL+Z. A rewind. The utility of this in roleplay with both PCs and NPCs is pretty amazing. One question worth resolving with your ST is whether people who are familiar with the power can recognize it being done. Do people wonder if they were recently mind wiped when they hear Ragabash laughter?

                                              And of course you should remember that if there is suspicion of fuckery, you could be dragged before a Philodox so that truth can be sorted from fiction.

                                              This Gift is best used with good intentions, for the good of all present. Don't cover up your fuckup. Even if you're only caught 1 times in 10, that will tank your Honour. Cover up the missteps of your friends and enemies to help keep the peace. Cover up the insult that almost caused a fight, the faux pas that enraged a thin-skinned Fang, the random insult from a stranger which was about to make your Packmate frenzy. In a way this Gift is a wonderful tool for a Child of Gaia. Knowing when not to use it and when to use it makes it Green. If you use it indiscriminately it'll come out as Amber.

                                              Liar's Face: This one's a bit of a puzzle, fittingly. I won't often be mentioning the teachers of Gifts, but dear god finding a Platypus spirit sounds like a chore. This Gift is a kind of selective mind control, forcing people to disbelieve any true statement you've made, or more precisely making them believe that you lied. "A giant wolf monster tore these people apart - I'm one of them" "they're headed to the helipad!" "we're going to try and escape" "you definitely shouldn't leave us unguarded". How this will play out will depend both on how cleverly you use it and how adversarial your ST is about such things. This is Green assuming you are clever, that the ST is happy to give you your Ws, and that humans will be showing up reliably. If every single npc you interact with is a Garou, Fera, Fomor, Mockery Breed, Psychic, Sorcerer, Mage, Vampire etc. then this Gift's utility drops significantly. Getting more successes than the target has Willpower is hard, and you can't rely on it.

                                              A niche yet noteworthy application of this Gift is tricking low-WP spirits. Some spirits do indeed have a low WP score, and if you manage to trick a Bane into believing you're a BSD with a "oh yeah I TOTALLY serve Gaia" then you'll be having a good time. And of course an ST could have a Ragabash Dancer hit a spirit with "oh of course I am ABSOLUTELY a Black Spiral Dancer".

                                              One thing that does stand out about the Gift is that the target believes that you were lying. Thus a liar. This can bring scrutiny onto you. A player might have their character tell the police that the gunfire they heard came from THAT house officers - pointing to the one their packmates are inside. This is effective in the sense that the cops believe this is a lie, and thus the gunshot came from another house... but they also now have a civilian lying to their face about the source of a suspicious sound.

                                              As a result it's wiser to use this flexibly, defensively and in response to questions. Don't volunteer true info to get it to be disbelieved unless you can see an immediate chance to benefit from it without putting yourself at risk. Instead tell the truth when interrogated and make it feel like a lie. One aspect the Gift's text doesn't quite get into is whether the target is able to consider the possibility you are mistook, so YMMV. We also can't be 100% certain how the Gift interacts with Fetishes and Gifts that tell truths from lies. Green is the absolute best case scenario. It may come out Amber in the wash.

                                              Open Seal: part of what I love about Garou Gifts is how absolute they can be. "We need the key to this sarcophagus" - or perhaps we don't, as even if it's protected by magic you can get it open with a point of Gnosis and a Gnosis roll. For ease of access, try bringing such objects into deep wilderness or Caerns before attempting to bust in.

                                              I love this Gift and have no suggestions to change it; it works perfect. I rate it as a Green because it has great utility and does what it's meant to do pretty much perfectly. "Closed or locked physical device" leaves some room for interpretation, but personally I wouldn't include a locked phone or laptop; to my taste the Gift opens physical locks, overcoming their intricacies through deeper spiritual magic. Keep in mind that you won't be able to close the lock again unless there is some mechanism available to you; a padlock can be clicked back into a locked state, an intricate mechanism you've forced open without the key code won't necessarily close again.

                                              It also emphasizes the importance of guards, watchful spirits, and magical defenses in Garou society. A vault full of Silver Fang Klaives is 1 Gnosis roll, and perhaps 1 point of Gnosis, away from any greedy Cliath Ragabash... were it not for all the Garou, Kinfolk and Spirits guarding it.

                                              Fun uses include securing a place to hide such as a storage container, breaking into a target location, breaking out of a cell, performing "street magic", stealing supercars, forcing the door of a vehicle open during a car chase, escaping a commercial airplane, getting a stuck window open, stealing a window, opening a container an antagonist needs the contents of so that said contents will spill or fall out during the action, and so on. It's niche yet versatile in a fun way.

                                              Scent of Running Water: flavour text says you can entirely eliminate your scent, which has implications outside of there being +2 difficulty to track you. In theory an enemy who relies largely on scent would have a hard time locating you. Worth bringing up now and then.

                                              Given that you'll often be traveling with others, having your own scent suppressed has limited utility. This power is great for clever and tricksy plans where you need to travel alone, but it's niche by design.

                                              Part of what's cool about the Gift, however, is that you can suppress it at will. And I would argue that this is stealthily the best part of the Gift. "He has no scent, we're relying on his pawprints" is hard to track. "Oh fuck his scent is thick, we can track him easily - wait where the hell did the trail go?!" is harder. You can play all sorts of clever games by turning on and suppressing your scent at will. Lead your enemies into traps. Divert those pursuing you. Go into a trapped location with your scent turned on, and leave the way you came with your scent turned off.

                                              ================================================== ====

                                              The new moons start off strong with some flavourful and strong Gifts. Next up will be Theurges, and some obvious Greens come to mind immediately.
