                But in any case it should be rare. Sorcery requires a type of dedication and willpower not every one can simply pick up. Just like most people could train to be an Olympia level athlete, but most do not.

                    Originally posted by Günther View Post
                    Hi!

                    In the older editions how complete the loss of connections to the mortal world was and how it affected Vamp Hedge Magic was handled variably. Only Revised tried to push personal horror and divide the WoD systems at all costs. There are many reasons why I consider Revised to be 80% manure, 10% ok, 10% good. In older books there are strong hints that Thaumaturgy is not the nicest version of hedge magic, but just a different variant of all the other hedge magic traditions out there, which makes more sense, especially since despite the Vamps being supernatural they are part of the natural WoD ecology, an "unnatural" balance of nature. But keeping it like that would have prevented new money-making books and dividing the settings more..
                    Nothing of what I posted invalidates that. Vampiric Thaumaturgy is a *bastardization* of existing, mortal Hedge Magic. That is, if you put a given Tremere (particularly one from several centuries ago) next to a Hermetic, who had a certain Verbena/Old Faith-subset witch next to him, and a couple variant Aeon sorcerers sitting over in the corner, the average observer would think that each of them were performing essentially the same ritual/spell/what-have-you that specifically does "X" (as those specific characters I'm citing have several points of ritual overlap with each other in several regards). However, metaphysically, each of them would merely have similar trappings about them but would otherwise being accomplishing their mojo in *very* different ways: The Hermetic and the witch may use the same arcane symbols, Latin, and herbs that the Tremere is, but the first is harnessing power through his [Avatar] and (likely) channeling power from the High Umbra to accomplish "X", while the second is calling upon natural elemental forces (and possibly no spiritual entreatment at all), while the third is simply using the arcane symbols around him to channel the innate power of his undead vitae to manifest the same mystical effect. The two Aeon sorcerers in the corner are harnessing the power of remaining mythic threads that "permit" "X" to still occur in the world, but this is through no direct innate power of their own, inherently, unlike with the Tremere (whose power solely comes from within), the Hermetic (who god-shard empowers him while the Umbrood he entreats gives his magic purpose and meaning), and the witch (whose mojo actually combines facets of raw elements coupled with a few bits linked to how the others all do their thing... only a little different).

                    Not only are each of those characters doing things differently on one level, they are also doing it differently on a different metaphysical level (the two Mages via True Magick, the two Sorcerers via the mortal Hedge Magic, and the Tremere via his vitae descending down from Caine/Lilith). While that means nothing to Joe Schmoe, personally, it makes a big difference mystically: the Mages can potentially pull off a bigger "showing" of mojo than the others, but at the risk of Paradox due to operating on a level of True, Dynamic Magick; the Sorcerers' ritual may or may not work, depending on how "impossible" the end result is (Hedge Magic will outright fail in the Modern Times if it in too-direct opposition to Reality(tm), but do so without risk of Paradox; the Tremere, on the other hand, can have his mojo work regardless of the viewer and/or most of the broader Reality(tm), but at the cost of flexibility of power as well as having "paradox" manifest in a different manner for him (vampiric vulnerability to sunlight, etc., as translated into Mage).

                    Just as only the very rarest of Mages can ever possess any numina/static hedge-powers, vampires can't utilize the mortal hedge magic that *living* beings can access (as indicated by some were-cats and Uktena being practitioners of Hedge Magic, in addition to the broader human sorcerers) any more than they can access True Magick; they can, however, adapt their own inherent essences to mimic/bastardize "X" via their vitae, but as indicated this is a separate metaphysical process with marked strengths and limitations when compared to the other witchy-pooh being bandied about.

                    A very important metaphysical distinction that should *not* be overlooked....

                        Anywho...

                        *****

                        Some tribes -- and even more on point/specifically, some packs -- would be more pre-disposed to having kinfolk learn Gifts/Hedge Magic than others, so it really just depends. It's easy enough to see certain Shadowlords finding value in a given kinfolk who has the innate talent/pre-disposition for a Gift or two, or who developed a Path or three of Hedge magic, to be of great use in plotting against others (both trans-and-inter-tribal, as well as with outsiders in general), while most other 'lords would see it as a threat/liability. Plenty of Black Furies would be perfectly fine with their "less-blessed" sisters developing Hedge Magic -- this is most especially true with the Greek Furies who still maintain ties with their Amazon sisters who themselves practice both True and Hedge Magic(k). The average Get wouldn't get caught dead having his kinfolk get "that uppity", while the Red Talons would, by-and-large, see the entire system of monkey-magic undone altogether.

                        So it really just depends.

                        As stated by myself and some others earlier, however, it *should* still be an uncommon/rare thing -- more common compared to the broader human population, certainly, but still a matter of only a few hundred, at the very most, out of a given 100,000 (and even then, a large part of that represents individuals who simply have a Gift or two and maybe a handful of dots in one or two Paths -- true, full-on Sorcerers should be counted in the scores at that point).

                                                            So... how does a Garou investigate the difference between a Hermetic spirit focused magician (regardless of mechanics), and a shaman, that allows them to actually draw a distinction between, "this is a true shaman that honors Gaia and operates according to her ways," and, "this is someone that has been deluded by human magical powers to thinking that's what they're doing, but they're actually being Name Breakers just like wizards."

                                                            They have powers that ignore magical style and just detect magic use. They can investigate, but most of their powers that try to defeat lies work against malicious lying, rather than simply believing something factually incorrect. They, essentially, have extremely limited ability (if any) to figure out anything more than, "this human works a form of magic, and is trying to do right by the spirits."

                                                            But they're culturally taught not to trust humanity, let alone human magic. Even if they're going to be nicer to the shaman than the wizard, they're still stuck in a default position of having little way to prove the position of, "any unsupervised human magic is too dangerous to allow," incorrect, and thus forced to either press-gang shamans and keep a close eye on them, or tell them to stop or else, rather than just killing them like they might a Hermetic or Technocrat stepping where they shouldn't have.

                                                            Also, frankly, WtA is full of the Garou being idiots by trusting their gut instincts instead of developing deeper understandings. The Garou are pretty much stuck in an ageless cycle of not thinking things through well enough before acting, and their actions ending up being massively bad choices in hindsight. WtA is supposed to be a game where the pressure to act regularly beats the need to develop deep understanding of issues. The Garou don't have time to spend months determining the fate of each human magic user they meet. They have a Wyrm to defeat.
