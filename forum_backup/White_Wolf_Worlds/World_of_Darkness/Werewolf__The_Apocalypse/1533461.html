    W5 Adventure "Reclamation" Review

    04-01-2024, 05:41 PM
    I've read the now-released-for-the-public adventure Reclamation and I wanted to share my thoughts on it.

    First, let's talk about the premise. On a meta-level this is very much supposed to be an introductory adventure. And it isn’t just an introduction to W5 but the adventure comes with pre-gen characters and the first chapter of the adventure is an in-media-res opening-scene of action that the PCs can easily win. Story-wise the adventure is about a caern which has gone dark and nobody knows what has happened to its guarding Pack of Garou. The PCs are asked to investigate and then encounter some challenges as well as enemies that need to be defeated.

    Where the adventure works quite well is in how straightforward it is. The narrative milestones are well-connected and it’s easy to see how things progress from point A and point B while the simplicity of the sketched-out plot makes sure that players can’t get off-track very easily. It’s quite obvious what the goals in any particular chapter are and what the obstacles are that need to be overcome. So as an introductory adventure it works quite well in that regard (both as in being an introduction for new players but also new STs).

    The biggest issue I had with the adventure is its worldbuilding. And I don’t mean the W5-part here. It’s the timeline of what happens when that is weird at times when you start to tug at the seams of this weave. Also, the writing on the introduced drug tells me that this was written by someone who has never dealt with drugs. Also, while I welcomed the integration of the pre-gen-characters into the adventure by having Backgrounds linked to particular NPCs within the story, I don’t think the adventure really thought through the implications of these connections existing.

    But overall, I think it’s fine. As a one-shot or an introduction, it’s perfectly serviceable. Well, as long as you don’t have somebody in the group who’s very inquisitive, I think this works well enough to introduce someone to W5.

    It’s a gentleman’s 6/10, I would say.

    (From here on are spoilers as I go into detail about what I described above)
    • Here’s the premise in its entirety: There’s a section of a generic city called Noble Green. It got a veteran’s hospital, then the city hit on hard times economically and a private company called Beckett’s Biolabs moved in. They developed a drug called sNap. It’s a highly addictive drug. The private company leaves. People keep passing around the drug as a new designer-drug and criminals are brewing up new stuff as well. There’s a caern in Noble Green that has gone dark a week ago. The players investigate the caern and find out from its spirit that the Pack left to battle the Wyrm and hasn’t returned. And the place where they went is the empty Veteran’s Hospital that has fallen into ruin. Once they’ve gone there the PC-Pack find out that the hospital is corrupted by the Wyrm. And they find one of the Garou from the caern’s Pack. That surviving Garou has fallen to Hauglosk, though, and is now working together with a Bane Spirit to bring about the end of the world as quick as possible. They defeat the bad Garou, the Bane-Spirit flees or gets destroyed as well and… you get your patented W5-“small victory”.
    • The timeline-issue: The reason the timeline doesn’t line up is mostly because of how the adventure starts. It says that the Sept-leader who is a Shadow Lord has Stormcrow-spirits to check on caerns and one week ago the spirits of the Noble Green caern have shut themselves off to the rest of the world. First of all, what happened in that week? Why wait a week? But then this whole timeline of this one Pack apparently going off on this attack on the hospital without informing anyone in the Sept…? And then this drug being around long enough for Garou to know about it – but somehow nobody connected it to the hospital in all that time before? And that fall of this one bad Garou happened quite a while ago, though …?
    • The drug: It’s called sNap and this is how it’s described: “Used by “snapping” a smelling salts-esque packet under the nose and eyes, sNap is actually a brittle crystalline substance that creates powerful fumes inhaled by the user. It creates an almost instantaneous euphoric high that lasts for upwards of an hour, during which time the user is said to have “got snapped”. It is powerfully addictive, easy to use, and surprisingly inexpensive when compared to other designer drugs.” Sure… if you say so, buddy… I mean, come on! It’s “crystalline” but it “creates powerful fumes”, you put it “under the nose and eyes” and then you “inhale”… It’s described as a “smelling-salt”! It’s like the writer here didn’t quite get how a to describe a “smelling-salt”-like drug. And a high of “one hour”! Seriously?! At least make it better than Heroin! But sure, “it is powerfully addictive, easy to use and surprisingly inexpensive when compared to other designer drugs”… whatever you say… This really would’ve worked better if it were either far more supernatural or someone had done more research on drugs to write a more delievable description. It also feels a bit weird that the Garou know about this drug being this attempt at making humans immune to the Delirium. And it does do that – except it gives people a high for an hour, does weird things to their body (they feel pain as pleasure, for example), hallucinations and over time erodes the person’s sense of reality. The opening chapter, that in-media-res action scene is the PC-Pack shutting down a criminal facility that produces sNap.
    • The challenge and the pre-gens: At the beginning when the Sept-Leader chooses the PC-Pack to investigate the fallen caern, another Pack challenges them to replace them. The Sept Leader goes along with that and there’s a ritualistic duel. The problem: If you use the pre-gen characters this gets really weird. You have the Pack-leader Gallowspeaker (a Black Fury Theurge) who has the Sept-Leader Stormchilde (Ahroun Shadow Lord) as a Mentor (a bit weird that this connection exists… it’s just explained as Stormchilde thinking Gallowspeaker shows promise). Not only that but Pile-On (Bone Gnawer Ahroun) has a member from the Pack that challenges the PCs as a mentor (Curbstomp – Bone Gnawer). That’s a bit weird. But also, I’m fully on board with the challenge when the Sept Leader is clearly doing a favor for somebody close to them by picking the PC-Pack. On a positive note, though, I was glad to see that the caern hadn’t been handed to the PCs after the adventure although it’s a bit weird to give it to Curbstomp as the last surviving member of that rival Pack as well (they got demolished by the traitor-Garou with the help of his Bane-Spirit-ally)…
    • Hauglosk is weird: I guess this will always be a weird thing in W5. Depending on who’s writing, Hauglosk can be anything between specifically Garou fanaticism and generally just plain insanity. Here’s how the bad Garou who turned traitor Michal Stitches the Soul is described: “Nearly giving in to Harano as his pack, Crescent’s Edge, witnesses failure after failure amidst their territory’s fall into corruption, Michal began searching for new ways to serve his brethren when the Prophet of Loss began whispering to him. Now, months later, Michal has gone all the way to the other end of the spectrum, Hauglosk driving him to believe that flooding the community with sNap and the recipe for making more hastens the coming of the Apocalypse and the rebirth following from the end of the world.” (This also brings up the timeline-issue again… supposedly the caern has only gone dark a week ago…)

          A couple more thoughts:
          • There is mention of the Umbra but it is only perfunctory. The biggest scene is when the Pack goes into the Umbra and they talk to the spirits of the caern that has gone dark. Also, a sidebar on pg 14 highlights something I've always thought was a bit of a problem with W5: "If the players are using the-generated Garou characters that came with this story, there are two characters who know this ritual: Kai Gallowspeaker and Elliot Fuller, Fairblade. If the players have created their own characters, at least one should know the rite. If this has been overlooked, the Storyteller can grant the rite to a character on an ad hoc basis." If I were to run a W5 game again, every PC would automatically know the Umbra-Rite, the Rite of Rage and the Rite of Tranquility automatically. The latter two are about raising and lowering Rage respectively and you need the first one to enter the Umbra. There's nothing fancy or interesting about making PCs use up XP to be capable of doing that. Especially the Umbra-one... It's such an essential part of WtA. Locking a PC-group out of entering that realm because nobody bought that one Rite for their PC is ridiculous. The adventure offers the right kind of advice here but this sort of thing should've occurred to the people who wrote the corebook and should've been avoided from the get-go.
          • I think it's a bit of a miss that the adventure never tackles Harano/Hauglosk as a mechanic and how it interacts with the adventure and that Renown-Chagrin isn't mentioned when that's something the ST is expected to enforce.

              Just some speculation, but I have a feeling that this was very much intentional from a design aspect. The lead designer(s) didn't want umbral travel to be an automatic, inherent, default ability that all werewolves can do. By making it a Rite, it allows an ST to prevent characters from accessing the Umbra. So that an ST who doesn't want to have to deal with the Umbra can lock characters out from going there ("Sorry, you don't have the rite and won't be able to learn it. No Umbral travel for this campaign.")

              This can make werewolves more friendly to crossovers or LARPs (which I think was a prime design point) by preventing them from traveling away from the "real" world. To be clear I don't like that, but I have a feeling that's why Umbral travel was locked behind a rite in W5, rather than being a basic part of the werewolf toolkit like it used to be.
