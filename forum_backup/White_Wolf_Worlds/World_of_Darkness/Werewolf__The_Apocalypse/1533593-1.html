    In my own chronicles, I often have Garou bite Lost Cubs, Lunatics, and kinfolk who are suspected to be Garou, but who have not been able to change. The trauma of the bite often (but not always) is successful trigger that jumpstarts the First Change. The adrenalin of being stalked/hunted and the pain of the wound works well to push late changers into their First Change. It's my way of including that horror movie trope in a setting that otherwise doesn't allow it.

    I make a lot of minor setting changes like that in order to accommodate a lot of folklore, literary, and cinematic werewolf tropes.

                            I'm loving the talk involving WtE, though I need to make some clarifications. I'm writing them here because it's on topic.

                            To be more specific, The Bite in WtE only works on unborn and kinfolk, and even then a person is just as likely to die from it as they are to change. This has made The Bite a modern topic of controversy among Garou. The Advent of The Bite comes from before the Dark Ages, and saw a resurgence in the Age of Apocalypse (nowish). Getting a bit ahead of myself. Let's talk about unborn!

                            For the first time in the history of the Garou, the homids are facing a same date their Lupus cousins have been despairing over. Their population is dwindling. This is a truncated summarization, but effectively the Garou lost almost an entire generation of Cliath, and didn't realize what was happening until the damage had already been done. In short, the Wyrm's bastards suppressed the wolf spiritually within Garou. Over many years fewer and fewer potential Garou were experiencing the First Change, while older Garou kept getting older.

                            Over time, the Garou have been noticing a steady increase in Lost cubs coming out of the woodwork, all of them describing a similar experience: They were bitten by some kind of animal, often described as wolf-like. They then undergo what can be described as the worst fever of their life, spanning an entire month. In this time they grow increasingly aggressive and irritable until finally they snap and shift into crisis, immediately frenzying. Unlike your typical First Change, without supernatural intervention the frenzy will last about 24 hours. Lastly, where all other Garou thankfully black out, the bitten are lucid, cognizant, and remember every last excruciating detail of their frenzy. The entire transformation experience leaves the bitten permanently marked. They are rendered sterile by the fever, and gain the Mark of the Predator flaw.

                            Their supernatural nature is extremely hard to hide and others find it unsettling. The Unborn will subconsciously seek out Wyld spaces, and are magnetically drawn to the supernatural. The Black Spiral Dancers have greatly benefitted from this, recruiting more BSDs at a rate never seen before when newly Bitten come right to them.

                            When the tide of Lost Cubs didn't seem to be turn, initially elders of tribes feigned ignorance, denying their involvement in these bites, but eventually they admitted that indeed The Bite was being employed by tribes. When facing the reality of impending extinction brought on by the Wyrm suppressing a generation, and acknowledging the Black Spiral Dancers had already begun using it, the other tribes were forced to adopt it in kind to prevent more of their own from falling to the Wyrm; a tragic arms race.

                            The outlook on Bitten is often one handled on a Sept to Sept basis, and often mirrors that Sept's treatment of crinos-born' Garou. Several Garou strongly dislike the bite. It's a major risk of a kinfolk dying while undergoing the process, and many are demanding a better way to address Unborn. With the BSDs happily churning out more Garou than ever before, for the time being, it's become necessary.

                            There are benefits to this of course. The Stolen Moons are seeing their numbers plateau. Where a Kinfolk no longer needs to undergo the rite of sacred rebirth, the bite is a much more attractive option. It also lets Kinfolk get more directly involved in the war against the Wyrm. Crinos-born are enormous fans of the bite, because it means that at long last, they have a vector for creating new Garou that wouldn't have existed before.

                            Is the bite good in the long run? That's for Players and Storytellers to decide.

                            Author of Hearthbound and Project Director for Werewolf: the Essentials
