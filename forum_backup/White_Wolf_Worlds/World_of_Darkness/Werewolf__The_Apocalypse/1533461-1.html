    Yeah, it feels more like a roleplaying-beat. It's more like a personal thing he's doing for himself than this actual Garou-Rite. I don't think you need to fill up that slot for the Rite at character-creation for something like that. Maybe the Rite of Tranquility would work better as something more functional.

    Also, one thing that makes it incongruous with the W5 corebook is the depiction of the older Garou. You'd think they'd go for a nastier portrayal, with the caern going dark and the general situation getting worse for Noble Green. But rather they come off as well-meaning and show dedication to making things better. There's no "Let's exploit the Litany to browbeat young Garou." as it's suggested in the W5 corebook, for example. Instead, three of the five pre-gens have the Mentor-Background and the Sept-Leader is supportive while when it really matters during the final fight Curbstomp from the rival Pack will help the PCs as best as he can.

                                Originally posted by MrornX View Post

                                Yet with how the adventure panned out it would be more along the line of "the younger generations is finally in a position to lead the garou into a brighter future" since all the pregnes didn't have any problems the garou nation as a whole (the sliver fang is outright stated to be a student of a garou hero)..

                                Again it's another thing in this adventure that runs counter to the corebook.
                                Yeah, it's the timeline that's a bit weird. You have the one Pack leaving their caern to attack the ruined hospital, they get killed except for one. That one survivor goes insane (suffers from Hauglosk) and now works together with a Bane-Spirit to manufacture the drug and spread it. Months pass as this happens. Then at some point the Sept Leader is sending out Stormcrow-Spirits to check on the caerns and realizes that this particular one has gone dark. Then a week later she gathers the Garou to choose the PC-Pack for the task of investigating what's going on.

                                It's easy to fix, I think. You remove the Stormcrow-Spirit-stuff, you say that the Garou are all busy with taking care of the drug-dealers and various manufactoring-sites while they try to figure out who's passing around the formula for the drug. So the Sept was distracted and at some point the Sept-Leader is like "Hang on, whatever happened to that one Pack? They haven't checked in for a while now...". Something like that.

                                And there's actually yet another thing in where it goes against the corebook: The caern. At the end of the adventure, it's Curbstomp who gets to claim the caern and become its guardian, not the players. They instead (if you pick the extended ending that's supposed to connect to more adventures) witness the Sept-Leader get handed three talismans from that historical Garou-hero Starmantle and it's suggested the Sept-Leader has some task in mind for the PC-Pack in connection with that. But that's weird in the sense that the W5 corebook is very explicit about assuming the PCs will end up managing a caern. And yet here's this golden opportunity to push players into that but instead the adventure deliberately chooses to turn the PCs path away from that.
