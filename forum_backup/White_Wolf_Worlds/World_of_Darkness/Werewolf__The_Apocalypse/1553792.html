      There isn't really any equivalent in the main WtA games (either the old canon, or the W5 one).

                      The thing is that this is not how tribes work in the canon.

                      What defines a tribe is its relationship to spirits, expressed in the forms of the Tribal Totem and the Tribe's Gift List. Everything else is accessory. Above all else, tribes are not defined by their other resources, even if they have an invested interest in them. You can make up for low numbers with kinfolk and/or induction of lost cubs. You can make up for most other resources through multitribal Septs. Tribes like the Siberakh do just fine with extremely limited numbers and resources, and any Siberakh wanting to improve can just join other Septs without giving up their tribal identity, it's just not a thing.

                      Middle Brother, the Bunnyip and White Howlers all got Lost because of spirit connections, not physical hardships. One was ritually sacrificed, so the spirits wouldn't recognize the tribe anymore even if their culture, kinfolk and resources were mostly intact. The one who fell to the Wyrm cut ties with spirits in the process (or brought them to the Wyrm). Bunnyip got destroyed because their animal kinfolk went completely extinct, and that has spiritual ramifications even if their human kinfolk still existed.

                      If you still want to make it, what would happen mechanically is that the Stargazers would mostly cease to be. They probably would make a Camp within the Children of Gaia, that would keep some of their Gifts, and a few could be incorporated into the CoG list, but most would be lost. Chimera may become a minor totem or not, but would cease to be a tribal totem as the ex-Gazers embrace Unicorn. Some rituals, Merits and Fetishes of the Stargazers may become available to the Children of Gaia, but some may lose relevance or power because of the tribe not existing anymore.

                      Basically that. The main mechanical consequence is one tribe simply not being available anymore and most of their mechanical options being lost. A few may be made available to the other tribe, but this other tribe would be mostly unchanged, or just changed as much as the ST wants, mixing things up a little. Unless you're really wanting to make this unified tribe more powerful than the others, the end result would still be a list of options with mixed elements, but the same size as they were independently.

                      From a strategical standpoint it's a loss for everyone, as that reduces the variety of resources available to their Septs. In a version of the setting where the tribes are more independent and fighting for resources more than cooperating on regional Septs, this merge may be a necessity. But under the setting's usual assumptions, nope.

                                          There's no need for a clean cut answer to things like access to Gifts. The only mechanical issue is if you're paying in-Tribe or out-of-Tribe costs (or Camp if one goes that route).

                                          Generally the books present this as an issue of getting the spirits to recognize you as worthy of learning a Gift. Things like existing pacts and lineage are just ways to simplify the process by having the requirements of certain spirits be well known, how to find the right spirits be common knowledge amount the group, and those spirits well inclined to teach them to members of a given group. There are extremely few Gifts in the game where it's impossible to learn them even if you don't have a relevant spirit pact or the right birth (the two books that made the most use of this were Book of Auspices and Rage Across the Heavens). There's a reason why certain secretive Camps are very cautious about revealing their special Gifts: anyone could try to learn them if they know they exist.

                                          Broadly, to be true to the setting as presented, it's going to be a case-by-case basis. The Incarna Turtle and his direct avatars aren't teaching Middle Brother's best Gifts to anyone, period, though weaker turtle and tortoise spirits are still around that might be able to be convinced to teach Gifts within their purview. The fuzzy areas where character traits, story, and role-playing come in when it comes to things like if you can contact an Ancestor spirit of Middle Brother, and even if you can, can you get information on a Gift considered lost with that Tribe so you can potential earn the right to learn it.

                                                  Originally posted by Shadeprowler View Post
                                                  with the proper breeding, breed, ancestros, pure blood, and tons of other factors a Cub could born with the potential to be a Howler eventually, but it is up to Lion to make the move and claim that one lonely cub in a desperate attempt to rebuild his Tribe
                                                  This part is completely unnecessary, and mostly easier to do than it seems.

                                                  A lot of people have Pict blood, so this isn't rare to begin with. And it isn't necessarily relevant because this is not exactly the link a spirit looks for, or needs. All other aspects are actually spiritual in nature, even Pure Breed has more to do with being marked by the spirits. Ancestor spirits are spirits, and may choose to approach someone for spirit reasons. What all that means is that Lion can make those things happen, from his perspective they're not a requirement, just a process.

                                                  In fact, Garou that have all those things exist. They appear both among the northern tribes and the BSD and are indistinguishable from what you would expect to be a modern White Howler, except for the fact they're from another tribe.

                                                  What Lion needs to rebuild the tribe is actually two things that can build up each other: enough power to be a Tribal Totem and followers that can build a tribe. In fact, every Lost Tribe can be made anew in this way. From a purely technical standpoint it's easier than it seems, and if this was the goal of their totems they would most likely have returned already, or at least be well in the process.

                                                  Spirits don't do this, though. For whatever reason, most spirits just don't build tribes even when they're powerful enough to do so. This is simply not what they want. We can guess some probable reasons, it seems that becoming a tribal totem is actually a big commitment, something most spirits don't want because their commitment is elsewhere. Tribal totems are extremely important for the Garou, but not exactly all that important to the Umbra compared to other spirits of similar power level. For the totems of the Lost Tribes the events that fell their people extinguished their interest in becoming tribal totems again more than anything, that is why the tribes aren't around anymore.

                                                  Now, the exact details are fuzzy and can be worked in a chronicle by a group wanting to resurrect the Lost Tribes. Lion lost power and seemingly interest in rebuilding the tribe, but it's certainly possible to build up a Lion pack or confederation of packs to the point of recovering his power and will and reignite the White Howlers. You can also go the traditional route of the Last White Howler trope, but that's more of a Garou legend/trope than necessarily the most likely or more interesting avenue to doing so. Turtle, Bunyip and the Rainbow Serpent are all described as in deep slumber out of grief, but that's not necessarily true, what's certain is just that they don't contact Garou anymore. If they're found, they can be convinced to return and build back their tribes.
