    Specialization doubt

    08-22-2017, 10:03 AM
    So, some of my player are picking their specializations in a few fields, but some of their requests sounds a bit too convenient to me.

    i.e.: brawl specialization: claw attacks. Stealth specialization: hiding (from sight).
    Aren't these fields too broad and common? I mean, doubling 10s on every claw attack seems a LOT to me, especially when the suggested specs on the manual are mostly combat styles unusable in Crinos form. But bites and claw attacks should be, by logic, legit specializations for "brawl". Same thing for hiding: sure, you won't double your 10s on sneaking or shadowing, but cmon, 60% of rolls include sight.

    Would you allow them? Do you have suggestions on how handle or specialize that kind of natural attack?

          I would consider those specializations too broad. Situations for specialties should be infrequent in most games, or if they occur more represent an actual, legitimate field of focus (like taking the specialty Brainy Surgery if you have Medicine 5, and then having your day job be a brain surgeon).

          A Brawl specialty in Claws or a Stealth specialty in Hiding likely covers 50% or more of most uses of that ability in most chronicles. Specialties are not meant to apply that many times. This is almost as blatant as having a Brawl specialty in "Fighting" and a Stealth specialty in 'Undetected".

          Using the W20 examples, the listed examples of Brawl specialties are all a particular style of fighting. They'd mostly apply in formal competitive settings (not real combat, but sports), but I could see situations where they'd apply in real combat sporadically (Boxing could mean a brutal punch of Bashing damage in Homid form; Wrestling to restrain someone, Martial Arts to throwing someone).

          The Stealth specialties are all specific situations when the specialty would apply (only when Shadowing someone, only in the Urban environment, only when Taking Point ahead of your pack, only when hiding in Crowds, only when Hiding Objects).

          So for Brawl, I'd let them pick one competitive sport (and discuss what real combat situations it would also apply in), or one of the lesser used non-damage dealing maneuvers or high difficulty damage maneuvers (disarming someone, blinding someone (which is DC 9), sweeping, or jaw lock).

          For Stealth, I'd ask them what specific situations would the specialty apply. "When I hide" is not a specific situation. It might be a specific terrain (urban, woodland, prairie, swamps - and there may be certain terrain not allowed as it provides too few opportunities). Or a specific circumstance (when I have a prepared ambush spot, when I am shadowing someone and following them, when I try to lose myself in a crowd). Or certain kinds of tasks (I am smuggling goods into the country past customs, when I want to hide objects so they aren't taken by the enemy, when I carry information out in a spy mission).

          Specialties are as much as a roleplay thing as they are a mechanic advantage. A specialty should be something that distinguishes the character as a true expert in a focused field. It is something they are devoting a lot of time and effort to master, an actual subset within that discipline. When something is way too broad the ST is definitely empowered to say that is not a legitimate specialty.

                                      It seems to me you want to turn this into a discussion of which martial art is better in real life and not how specialties should work mechanically.

                                      Because if I understand you rightly, there are very little situations where you don't get the benefit of your specialty when fighting. The player can always say he is using his fighting style. A specialty is not something that should be used near 100% of the time. It should only be used when certain conditions are meant.

                                      Or if you as an ST are saying certain styles are mechanically better than others, then you're essentially doing the same thing by pushing the players to one specialty. "If you take Krav Maga, you always get to use it in combat. But if you take any other martial art, which I consider inferior, then you'll only get it some of the time."
