          I would consider the technology updates the less challenging task, reframing the concept of the Weaver into a contemporary context is far more difficult imo. A whole lot has changed and things have progressed very differently from what anyone in the late 90s could have predicted.

          In general, I think the book would have to shift its focus from 'hard' to 'soft'. Technology is not the only thing this applies to, but it's a good example: When the book was written, it was still thought that the main problem of advancing technology would be the machines themselves. Dirty cars, belching factories, agricultural monocultures and so on. Yes, proliferating infrastructure still devours an insane amount of resources and destroys nature, but the general attitude towards machines has shifted. When environmental considerations started to be included into technological advancements, machines started to be seen as not just the cause of the problem but also a possible solution. I feel this would need to be reflected.

          On the other hand, the Information side of things has been severely underestimated. This doesn't only mean software and the internet, but really anything that can be classified as information. For example, one topic that should be addressed is the increasing abstraction from physical reality: ask anyone to describe the sequence of physical events that occur when their finger touches the screen of their phone to start an application. My guess is most people will already struggle to explain how a touchscreen works, to say nothing of how a program is pushing bits through circuitry. Or ask anyone about the biochemical processes that happen when they take painkillers. The point is, the layer of abstraction between cause and effect is getting ever thicker and to non-experts it might just as well be magic. That isn't anything new at all, but with the rise of information technology it has (ironically) accelerated significantly. And of course, with every increase of abstraction comes a loss of control - that's significant, because it takes agency away from humans as a whole.

          Then there is the impact the increased flow of information had on society. I mean, holy shit have things changed since the 90s. The whole shtick of information, misinformation, parallel societies and how the internet is an open battlefield between Weaver, Wyld, and Wyrm needs to be addressed. I think any W20 Book of the Weaver would need to come with an updated Digital Web for M20 to even start to cover it all. At the very least, the section on how the Weaver tries to mold humanity closer to her ideal should be expanded.

          The toybox section of the book is fun, and aside from a general bump up in technology that it needs, I would try to include more examples for bio- and social engineering. At the time of writing things like CRISPR and using genetically modified tobacco plants to produce vaccines would have been WeaverTech, and imo deserve more prominence. Some of the electronic gadgets still hold up surprisingly well because they use magic to circumvent mundane problems.

          I would absolutely want it to expand on AI and the Weaver Children- not the pseudo-AI Machine Learning programs like ChatGPT and so on (although those are interesting for the discussion on abstraction as they seem to be tailor-made to dilute information), but the conscious kind that has real agency. As the book is made to be five minutes into the future, the appearance of true AI in the WOD would be something immensely significant and interesting to explore.

          For the Weaver's Brood, a slight update would be enough. The concepts for the Spirits carry over well enough, maybe a few less 90s Hacker spiders and a few more Spirits with a focus on social manipulation and control. Again, more Weaver soft-science focus.

          As much as I would love to see an expanded chapter on the Ananasi's connection to the Weaver, I would rather have an updated Breed Book that discusses this in detail. A little more on the different Fera's relationship to the Weaver would be nice though.

          Drones: Still work great, imo. If anything I would like to have them expanded to include semi-Drones among sleepers, and I would plunder Autumn People for this. In general, flesh them out more to make them more attractive antagonists. I feel in comparison to Fomori they lose a little on the background material they have.

          As for the Factions, updates that go together with M20 for the Technocracy. And then, DNA needs a little makeover. The 90s esthetic of them is super nostalgic and I kind of expect they still issue orders via fax in 2024, but they need to get a bump in their awareness of the WoD and progress in their projects to make them relevant again. They have good potential for a Weaver controlled SCP-Foundation type organization and would be an option for half-splat PCs.

                  Brief note: the Digital Web got an update, in M20's The Operative's Handbook. That said, the Virtual Adepts Convention Book, Revised, introduced an interesting notion that could be built upon here, namely that the Digital Web is part of a larger “information Hypersphere” — that the business of encoding, storing, transmitting, and transforming information in the form of digital code is just one aspect of a larger field of information. Perhaps that could be leveraged to provide a way to address voidshaper's point about how Information has been severely underestimated, somehow? I mean, Digital Web stuff lightly touches on this with the idea that it's possible to go “outside” the structure of digital zones to a place where one can catch a glimpse of what VAs call “the Matterhorn”; but I feel like they could do so much more with this. And the Weaver might be just the thing to use to get there.

                  And yeah; Dogma would be a part of it. A big part of the current situation involving, for lack of a better term, information warfare, is driven by promoting dogmatism, with each side in a conflict trying to get people to unquestioningly accept whatever their guardians of truth tell them, while at the same time getting them to dismiss any conflicting information as “misinformation” or “fake news”. As Saur Ops Specialist points out, dogma is by no means limited to religious dogma.

                  On the other hand, to what extent is the increasing unreliability of information a Weaver thing, as opposed to being the Wyrm corrupting and tearing down the Weaver's edifice of knowledge? Which taps into, but expands on, the point about the Digital Web becoming an open battlefield between the Weaver, the Wyrm, and the Wyld.

                              In my mind, if we take the garou perspective as true or at least enlightened, part of what drove Weaver mad was the fact that out of the Triat it was the only one which, by nature, ascribed meaning and value to things. I would want to see that part, which sees meaning in everything and longs for total preservation (maybe taking inspiration from Royce Brecket and 'the Process' from transistor), in conflict with another internal part that works more like the Auditors from Diskworld, that is to say longing to put hierarchical value on everything and dispose of all things unnecessary or of lesser value, a sort of living occams razor; maybe add a third part that seeks to develop the webs and patterns that sort of embodies a longing for more storytelling, more labels, more technology.

                              In this way, the same way that Devourer, Defiler and Destroyer Wyrm echo each part of the triat, we get echos of the triat within weaver as well; I think this would be good because it would offer opportunities to play as weaver influenced/weaver-controlled PCs and have some nuance and ambiguity to your goals and motives despite the otherwise all-consuming nature of onesong ( onesong, which I love and would like to see reprised). For that matter I think it gives some nuance to characters dealing with weaverish forces that they might not know which aspect of the weaver they face, or, given its uniform nature, that there even are aspects that define some of the vicissitudes in its behavior.

                              There's an interesting concept that I stumbled into when I temporarily forgot the difference between metaphor and reality that I just recently learned the name of, Egregor. I had been calling them noosphere lifeforms or genius loci, but neither was quite right, but from what little I've read Egregorians seem to have been developed by people who fell under the same delusion as me and I think they fit perfectly in the semi-gnostic animism of WtA. The idea is that anthills are alive in a manner distinct from the ants, analogous to how you are alive in a manner distinct from your neurons and blood cells. In this same way there is a level above us that we are similarly blind to as the individual ant is to the colony, a collection of memes and ideas, or of motives and instincts, that border on meaningless in the individual but cause an emergent phenomenon in the collective. A living thinking creature that emerges from humanity.

                              In this case I would emphasize the difference between these creatures and the normal echos of the collective unconscious in that these creatures particularly arise in places where artificial roles and technological support are particularly strong and routine. Corporations where people routinely discard their own families or well being, defying their self-interest and animal instincts in order to follow their role, and where everyone from the top to the bottom is constantly hooked into some manner of communication network, giving them directives and incentives; in that kind of place one of these Egregores could arise, in this case, cultivated by weaver servants. And yes, the underlaying motive would be to expand this level of total dedication, total subjugation, total routine to wider and wider swaths of humanity.

                              Adding on to the aside on the Onesong, I was in a game where the ST had several major Wyrmish antagonists for our pack be people who had been exposed to the Onesong and managed to tear themselves away, and who subsequently became convinced that anything they could do to avoid that stasis was moral and just no matter how destructive. They were very self-centered, naturally, but on the rare occasion they would consider other people, they were convinced they were doing us all a favor.

                                  I wouldn't give the Weaver the same kind of Traitic personality break that the Wyrm has. That was specifically a result of the Wyrm's role of balance and trying to comprehend what went wrong in its application of it and relationship toward the rest of the cosmos, the other two Supernals in particular. The Weaver, in contrast, doesn't see anything as having gone wrong, or rather, that the previous state was what went wrong and how it has to log and record everything in order to avoid losing it... which leads into its present state of wanting to freeze everything as it is to understand it. The other Supernals are obstacles to this end, and the Weaver has no reason to care about why they do what they do. Like the Wyld, it does what it does, and has little capacity to care about the consequences.

                                      Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post
                                      I wouldn't give the Weaver the same kind of Traitic personality break that the Wyrm has. ... The Weaver, in contrast, doesn't see anything as having gone wrong ... Like the Wyld, it does what it does, and has little capacity to care about the consequences.
                                      I think that is a valid take of cannon materials and I agree that thematically it is very fun to have Wyld have no comprehendible faces, wyrm to have three comprehensible but fractured and faceted faces, and for weaver to have a single, universal face, by which it interacts with the world.

                                      There is a fun problem with trying to balance a three-body equation, I'll admit the math is a little beyond me in a lot of cases, but it seems to sometimes require infinitely precise balance to create a three body problem that has a predictable or periodic solution. This relates to the question at hand in that, in the imagined, prehistory wherein Wyld and Wyrm were doing their jobs perfectly, infinitely creating, infinitely destroying, what was Weaver's job? Why did Weaver choose to disrupt that balanced state? In my head I like to think that each of the Triat was susceptible to one of the three primordial poisons, Wyld to ignorance and delusion, Wyrm to hatred and aversion, and Weaver to ATTACHMENT. I don't think Weaver is or was ever emtional enough to love the things it gave meaning and pattern to, but I think that it may have gotten attached, taken pride in its work or the like, wanted to preserve what the Wyrm was called to destroy. In it's Wisdom it knew the only way to stop the other two was to strike first against the Wyrm.

                                      This is not supported by lore, but I feel like it could be inferred from it. With that in mind I think it could be fun to have elements within weaver be in conflict with each other.
