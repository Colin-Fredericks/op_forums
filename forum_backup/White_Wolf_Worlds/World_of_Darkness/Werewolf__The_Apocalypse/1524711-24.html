                        Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post

                        I'd rather not use D&D4e as an example, as 1) I prefer it and 2e to other editions, if I have to play any form of D&D, 2) 5e ended up having to use a fair bit of 4e, especially with 5e third party material that people like, such as Matt Colville's MCDM books. 4e, and 3) still very much D&D and not a skin suit of its themes. In fact, it's quite the opposite on number 3; while W5 is a grisly shell of its IP stealing the moons of the W:tA of old, D&D 4e just removed its own skin and pointed out how existing parts of the game worked with greater clarity, its guts open for all to see.
                        3rd edition D&D still generally resembled 2nd edition D&D and the setting was the same between both editions. There were some mechanical changes like streamlining Thac0, but overall you could see that 3rd edition was an updated version of 2nd edition. 4th edition was an entirely different game - some aspects were better, some were worse, but mechanically it bore little resemblance to the prior editions. Moreover, when you include the 100 year time skip for the Forgotten Realms, which caused the writers to toss much of the metaplot, setting lore and major NPCs into the garbage bin so they could do a "reboot" you ended up with a game called D&D 4th edition, but other than the names of things, it didn't feel like a new edition of the same game, but rather a new game altogether.

                        This is sort of where I'm seeing games like W5 or H5. V5 at least had some mechanical changes, but the setting is essentially the same one so V5 feels like a new edition of a prior game. But in neither H5's nor W5's cases is that true and both are functionally new games with new settings that simply reuse the names of the prior games. They feel less like new editions of an existing game and instead feel like brand new games.

                            I just looked at the section for Forms again and I noticed a bit I actually overlooked when I ran W5: From pg 134 "Shifting form is a two-dice minor action and requires a Rage check, according to the form’s cost. (This cost must also be paid for each additional scene in which the Garou maintains the form.)" I definitely didn't do that. There were times when a player was in Glabro for more than one scene. But also... come on! This also brings up the same issue like with the scene-based damage from being in the Umbra. They really need to change the framing of time to real-world time or just not invoke these "Pay a cost to do this and then pay again at the start of each following scene."-mechanics in the first place. Because for example when it says under Hispo that it can be used to travel long distances then it can only work if the ST turns the travelling into one scene. Any interruption (in other words "new scene") would lead to more Rage-checks. I also feel like it's a real bummer to have to start a scene as ST by making everybody pay a cost or do a feel-bad roll like the Rage-check because this mechanic would make you lose Rage for just being in the scene which the player also has no control over. It's like the mechanics want to punish the player for just being in the vicinity of the story.
