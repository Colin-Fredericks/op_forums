        Originally posted by Knightingale View Post

        To come back to the Loresheets, it's a near miracle that the W5 corebook ended up with any Loresheets. I'm always thinking of that section in the V5 Players Guide about Loresheets:


        (Emphasis from the book)

        They didn't put all the Loresheets from the OP books into the V5 Players Guide and the H5 corebook launched with no Loresheets at all. Instead of figuring out how to use Loresheets and make them part of a game like H5, they simply didn't use them. It's only now many years later that the upcoming Apostates supplement starts to think about what Loresheets in H5 look like. As there was little to no desire to engage with Loresheets as a tool, it's no surprise that they ended up being this weird mix of various things with no bigger idea of how they're supposed to fit into the game. They had a real attitude of "The best number of Loresheets to use in a WOD5E game is zero." back then.
        I guess Hite's design sensibilities didn't mesh well with someone else's. A pity; it's a good way to set up characters with connections to the world. Just as long as your ST isn't being a complete jerk about what they want to do and trying to keep PCs out of any kind of influence or importance ohhhhh... I think I get why.

        This one's especially odd. The boni are very weird. Take the first dot "The Difficulty to return to the physical world from the Umbra by using the Rite of Shadow Passage is reduced by 1 when you’re the Rite master (see p. 180)." But here's what it already states in Rite of the Shadow Passage: "The Difficulty to return to the physical world from the Umbra using this Rite is reduced by 2​​." This has the feeling of the left hand not knowing what the right hand was doing.
        I thought that it would stack, but with this game, who knows.

        And then the other boni are also strangely constrained. Like whenever an effect is described, somebody felt the need to add a cautious "But..." to it as if the writer feared making the dots too good.

        Second dot: You can attract the attention of pattern spiders... once per scene... if they are present... and they don't necessarily like you.
        Third dot: You can see ghosts in the Umbra and in the real world - unless they use powers to hide from you. And you can communicate with them - unless they don't want to talk to you.
        Fourth dot: A very good one! You only take superficial damage when you run out of WP in the Umbra.
        Fifth dot: You know some sort of "monumental secret" about the Umbra - except "whatever puissant spirit the secret is about knows you know.​"
        Everything but the fourth dot just seems like padding. Like the Storm Warden merit from Mage Revised, with lots of extra steps.

        I am curious how they're gonna handle Black Spiral Dancers in the next W5 supplement, Wyrmtide. They're still supposed to be antagonists but this Loresheet really set up the template of them being more like misguided bad boys that aren't that bad after you get to know them. Tellingly this isn't a Loresheet about how you study this one type of enemy and get better at taking them out. It's about how you hang out with them and know their lingo. And the ultimate ability is to talk someone out of being a Black Spiral Dancer...
        Normally, the end result is the other way around. Truly, this is a backwards crazy edition of W:tA. "Learn Wyrm Lore. If your level in the Knowledge is higher than your Willpower, remove a derangement from target BSD character."

                                                    Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post

                                                    Heere's a question the writers did not ask; Why should they try to convert the BSDs back to Gaia? If the ruling philosophy of W5 is that the Nation is gone and everyone can do what they want, then what are the BSDs doing wrong?
                                                    Especially in the context of the Ghost Council and the Red Talons. The former is all about "the ends justify the means" and that includes allying with the Wyrm to overcome whatever the opposition is. Meanwhile, with the latter you have this:

                                                    In the point of view of other werewolves from the now-shattered Garou Nation, the Red Talon perspective borders on the heretical. Red Talons often think that it isn’t the Wyrm that’s the imbalanced element of the Triat, but rather the Weaver, with its ever-expanding restrictiveness and malignant expansion in the form of cities, development, and “civilization.” Among the most extreme of Red Talons — even expressing such a sentiment is shameful — the Wyrm is a victim of the Weaver, and the Wyrm’s tools have their place.
                                                    Once you get past "The Garou-Nation has fallen", "It's the Age of Apocalypse." and you get to descriptions like this one, why wouldn't BSD just be another bunch of Garou indistinguishable from the playable Tribes...?

                                                    The text is trying so hard to be dismissive and complain about the Garou that you get to a point where you just start to wonder "Does any of this even matter if all of it sucks this much...?". Why would you even give a shit about Garou traditions and Gaia being dead/dying if nothing in Garou society works and all of their goals, hopes and dreams are categorically unachievable? At that point, why wouldn't you look at Packs of werewolves in W5 as just a bunch of marauders traveling through a wasteland rather than these firm, organized ideological factions? But since W5 isn't willing to take that leap of faith and leave pretty much all of the WtA-premise behind (despite the developers' strategy to "redecorate" the WtA-setting using a sledgehammer), the standard for "good" in the setting remains unimaginative traditionalism. You just gotta get that caern and wait for the Apocalypse in it. There's nothing else you can do in W5.

                                                            The problem is that when you combine watering down the evil of the BSD with also making "heretical" beliefs among the playable Garou Tribes a more common thing, then you get this morally grey soup where no ideological position matters all that much. "So you're a Red Talon Garou who believes the Weaver is the true enemy and that's why you intentionally spread plagues...?" - according to W5 that's a concept for a PC. On the other hand, you can have a Pack of BSD trying to reclaim a caern that was formerly held by the White Howlers and who are also willing to fight against Pentex to claim that caern - according to W5 that's a valid story-concept. After the waters have been muddied like that, ideological questions become far more individualized and heavily depend on the circumstances at the moment. Just because a Garou tells you that they think the Weaver is worse than the Wyrm doesn't make them automatically a bad guy, for example, in this kind of setting. If anything, getting that granular about the setting in W5's context turns into a bit of a nothingburger. Gaia's dead/dying, it's the Age of Apocalypse, the Garou Nation has fallen... why would you even bother getting invested in some grand theory of why things are the way they are? You're playing out the immediate aftermath of a fuck-up of epic proportions. (Although it doesn't help that the worldbuilding and accompanying writing in the W5 corebook leave a lot to be desired...)
