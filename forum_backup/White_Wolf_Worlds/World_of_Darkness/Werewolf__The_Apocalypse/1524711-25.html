    PHASE FOUR PART SIX

    What Does All of This Mean

    Before I finish up, a recap. W5 would be an okay game once all of the errors are fixed, a game primarily targeting younger, less-experienced players looking for shorter campaigns. More experienced players might be turned off by the paired-down systems, vague back story, and limiting play styles focused away from lengthy stories. In particular, WOD veterans might find the disconnect from the styles of the other games, even the newer V5, off-putting. Specifically, fans of the older editions of W:tA will probably hate much of W5, as many favorite portions have either been eliminated or altered to the point of being totally different. As to the moral implications to the greater gamer community of the issues brought up in the creation of W5...

    When Paradox faced negative publicity on one of the first supplements to V5, they quickly got rid of the designers who they felt were most responsible and dropped Modiphius as publisher. The management left in charge was to start work on W5. However, someone had a particular dislike for Werewolf and had a distinct vision of how to change it. The first team created for this butted heads with the management on these changes, leading to the team being dissolved, while keeping management. A second team was brought in, but this one was dissolved as well. Finally, a third team was created serving under Renegade with a new designer brought in to help mitigate the excesses found in the previous teams. After some false starts, new material was coming out, including the surprise creation of H5. Then, W5 was announced in October 2022. The news was quickly met with decisive criticism, as both those for and against the changes voiced their views as interviews detailed the new game. By January 2023, the new designer left, just before the first of the previews came out involving traced artwork. As more previews came out, the social media campaigns for both sides tried to sway opinions, with those for W5 usually dismissing even the most logical arguments of those against. The harsh treatment of some of the first two teams came to light, again with some casting doubts on their veracity. Even after W5 came out, riddled with quite a few errors, much of the online opinion tried to make an argument that the game was still very good. As of now, late January 2024, there are still doubts about just how W5 will move forward and what the state of future games in the WOD5 line will be treated.

    I have three questions that need to be answered, and there might just be evidence somewhere that can justify this case.
    1. Was there a deadline to making W5? W5 feels rushed, especially due to the errors in it. One would have expected that the game was mostly finished when it was announced, yet we know that there were still parts that were being written/re-written. It feels like that there was something more than just booking printer time involved. Maybe it was just arbitrary. Maybe there was financial involvement in getting something out by a certain time. Perhaps there were legal reasons involving the IP and something had to be announced /published by a certain time or there would be legal repercussions. If Paradox had to do something within a certain time frame, then that could explain the unusual, faster turn-around for W5. If it was just booking a printer, then maybe there could have been a delay to create a better product. Something just feels off in this timeline.
    2. What was the social media involvement in the publicity campaign? In other posts, I have mentioned how some of the social media outbursts feel suspicious. Just slightly too hostile in some regards to those not liking how W5 was going. Also, some of the previews almost feel that they were chosen specifically to instill debate and publicity, such as what happened with a recent V5 product. While those directly working for W5 kept most of their own ideas unknown, fans were being overwhelming supportive. I'm just wondering if they were all "real," or was there some incentive to sparking such debate? The actual developers couldn't explain their own feelings over any problems with W5 without risking their employment, nor could they be too enthusiastic about W5 without sparking a false advertising claim. However, those not connected could expound on W5, if they were being truthful. If they were incentivized, then things begin to look murkier.
    3. Could W5 have been made to be not as perfect from the start? Companies have many reasons for making bad products deliberately. This might be a reason for the handling of W5. Maybe it was meant to be an off product, at least originally. Maybe the outlook changed and Paradox needed W5 to be a hit at the last minute. Maybe W5 had to be a hit for the financial outlook to come out right, no matter what. It is hard to tell. As mentioned, someone in the management change hated W:tA on some level. Maybe they wanted W5 to be a failure, but others needed it to be a hit. It is hard to say. However, if W5 was always meant to be a bad product, then this is a huge disservice to all of the older fans of the game.

    When W5 was first announced, I was able to come up with a basic scenario incorporating the changes into the existing metaplot that afternoon, using just my memory of the books. Since then, I have been able to that to many of the other changes as well. I have even presented some of them in this review, some coming up on the fly after feedback from my posts. [Basically, the Garou have to fight a three-pronged battle against the Triatic Wyrm in Europe, America, and Africa. Secondary battles in the Amazon and with the Beast Courts in Asia, and maybe a bit of Shattered Dreams thrown in as well. The Garou win, but lose the war as Gaia is severely weakened. I'm thinking of starting a thread about my scenario and asking for opinions on it.] Now, if I could do that, then so could have the dozens of others who had worked on this for five years, with access to literally everything that had ever been written about Werewolf. For W5 to come out the way it did is a disservice to those fans from the past thirty years. Even if it was always going to be a complete reboot, there shouldn't have been this many mistakes. There shouldn't have been this much online battle between generations. There shouldn't have been this much distrust.

    If W5 was just a game I didn't like, I would have ignored it and went on with my life. However, I feel I have presented enough evidence to suggest that something more was going on in the creation and publicity for this game, implications that one might not want to support this game in the future. While I gave W5 an Incomplete earlier, if any of my above questions turn out to have bad answers, then I will have to give W5 an F. I would hate doing so, but I would have to on completely moral standards. So many people worked hard on W5, even if I don't really like it, and I don't want their work to go in vain. They might not have known what was in store for W5, but most probably just wanted to work on doing something they loved, or at least needed the money for. There are just too many unresolved issues for me to do otherwise.

    Finished.

    THWarted

        For the "deadline" note, I think it's generally perceived that ParaWolf was really trying to get W5 out in time for GenCon 2023, especially given its long production history and staff changeovers. There's content in the corebook that I don't necessarily disagree with, but I do disagree with the emphasis and page space afforded to it, especially at the expense of other elements. If there had been time for editing, rebalancing, etc., maybe some of the stuff in the corebook would be more digestible to me and others. Illustrating the Garou's flaws without painting them as Gaia's failchildren at every turn, giving the tribes more detailed writeups*, etc.

        As for the "social media" element, I feel like we need to look at Achilli here. Even after leaving the team, he was still pushing for the book, and some of his comments about the old players didn't really go over well. But I do think there are parts that he had a vision for the book that he was trying to push for against the tide of what it was. Like his comments on how the Garou do have a rich, detailed, but contradictory oral history - the core book really doesn't do a lot to back that up, but I imagine that, if there was more time and someone in editorial cared, we could get more about the stories the Garou tell themselves than a lot of "I don't know."

        But then we get the Fenrir stuff, where it becomes clear that Achilli was likely trying to cover someone else's ass. Let's look at the many reasons he offered for why the Fenrir had to go:

        -"We decided to downplay all ethnic ties for the various tribes, so they didn't really fit." Almost as soon as he said this, everyone pointed out that there was still a tribe called the "Fianna," which led to the "Sorry, I'm sorry, I'm trying to undo it" move of renaming them the Hart Wardens.
        -"We couldn't really come up with four action verbs for them, like with the rest of the tribes." I'm not even going to dignify this with a rebuttal.
        -"If we removed the ethnic ties, we'd effectively have a tribe full of Ahrouns, and that would be boring." They removed all the Indigenous elements from the Gale Stalkers, and they're still a warrior tribe with a distinctive ethos. They removed all the Celtic elements from the Fianna/Hart Wardens, and now, their duties seem to be "what every Garou should be doing, but a little extra."

        Of course, if you trust Sambrano's accounts, all these excuses were meant to cover for the fact that editorial just wanted the Fenrir gone because they considered the concept toxic beyond repair. And even then, nobody felt the need to just come out and say, "We don't trust you fuckers with Viking werewolves who hate weakness."

        I'm not going to go so far as to say "This is Werewolf written by people who hated Werewolf," even if it seems like there are a lot of areas where editorial considered certain concepts to be utterly rotted and unsalvageable. But whatever aim existed for W5, it's clear the thumb was on the scale in some areas, and even noble gestures or attempts at nuance feel robbed by the effort to rush the book out before GenCon in a way that's anodyne enough not to piss the casual reader off.

        * Speaking of, I finally realized why some of the tribes didn't sit right after looking over their writeups. One of the major beats of the book is that the Garou need to fight beyond the war and focus on different fronts. Which would be easier, if some of these tribes weren't rewritten so that they're mostly focused on the war and the Garou.

        The Black Furies were once protectors of women and children, who they saw as the easiest targets of the Wyrm's deprivations. Now, they focus on vengeance, but the writeup emphasizes how much it's about their perception of injustice and restitution.

        The Bone Gnawers once associated with and fought for the unhoused and economically disadvantaged, who they saw as the easiest target of the Wyrm's deprivations. Now, they're Garou Nosferatu, focused on getting secrets and intel for the war through associating with the disadvantaged.

        The Children of Gaia were the tribe most likely to find the root of suffering among mortals and cut it out at the source before the Wyrm could infect the wound. Now, they're mostly dedicated to the secrets of Gaia.

        Younger Brother were staunch advocates for Indigenous rights and vocal decolonialists. Now, the Gale Stalkers just hunt, all the time.

        This goes back to my idea that the book seems to be coming from the perspective that the Garou have never fought the war the right way before in their entire existence, and that is why they're losing. It effectively rolls back the tribes so that any advocacy they did outside of kicking the Wyrm in the teeth was either less important or nonexistent. Further, the writeups don't have the "pivot" moment that shows how they might turn these tactics towards new fronts - or if it does, it's not exactly showing them thinking differently, like with some of the more contentious Red Talon sample characters. The only tribes who really seem to be focused on advocacy outside of direct war against the Wyrm are the Black Furies (who are painted as doing it for their own self-righteousness), the Red Talons (who are still fighting to protect their own personal patch), and the Stargazers.

                        So, I want to start this little tirade with the things that made WtA so annoying/disgusting for some people. These are not my opinions, just what I've heard over the years.

                        -It was racist
                        -It was too positive for a WoD game
                        -It didn't treat the garou monstrous enough
                        -The umbra and spirits liking the garou made things too easy
                        -Furry Captain Planet sucks
                        -It isn't about werewolves
                        -Red Talons were too edgelordy
                        -Get of Fenris symbol is bad
                        -It has bestiality in it
                        -It is toxic masculinity and all its players are all too


                        Now, looking at the By Nights Studios version of WtA and the ill-fitting additions to Changing Ways, you can see the direction of thought that had started in regards to what W5 would become. Things became more bleak, caerns were lost and the umbra became hard to traverse. The loss of Caerns and the Umbra's ease is a common theme in all the changes. It makes me think the setting was seen as too positive for a WoD game and where the PCs had elders they could trust.

                        If look at the Forsaken, its own game now as it is, the same mentality is present. Hostile umbra, less infrastructure and a 'darker' setting.

                        This mentality became the cornerstone of W5, with other changes being added as the world around it grew more aware of minority rights.

                        As an aside, most of these accusations ignore all the actual in-game troubles of the umbra and garou society.

                        Something that actually surprised me, given how common of a squick it is, the wolf-born have stayed. The garou born were a much,much,MUCH less mentioned breed and yet they were removed. It goes to show how most of the squick was people overthinking things.


                        In all honesty, W5 suffers from many things but the biggest is how unpolished it is. It spends pages detailing how the garou failed but then offers no correct way to proceed. It is so focused on the CoF that it forgets the BSDs exist. It refers to old sept positions even though they are no longer in use. Nevermind the Clement Lupus and the form descriptions ACTUALLY making the old bestiality accusation more likely and example characters worse than the CoF.

                        What do I think happened to make W5 like this? I personally think it was a mixture of clashing ideals and confusion about how to make the game 'fit WoD better' without making a completely new game. As I stated above, there have been a lot of accusations of WtA being too positive and not monstrous enough, simply because the game isn't as nihilistic as VtM is. I've seen many fan ways to 'fix' the setting and they all boil down to "Gaia is dead/never existed and garou fought for nothing."

                        Also, I think a major part of W5 being in limbo was the cultural nature of the tribes. Unless you played the game, looking from outside in it might have looked like a game of Captain Ethnic and so it might have been hard to get people to develop it. Even if you did, then justified changes like J.F Sambrano suggested were earned through a lot of fighting and arguing.

                            The deadline

                            It's hard to say how it's all correlated or why some things happened in the way that they did but I think there are three important factors to how this all turned into a very rushed project towards the end (despite the delays and switch in developer-teams). The first is Paradox's insistence on making it a Gen-Con-release. I don't think there's a lot of merit to having to do that in the age of the internet, digital releases and social media (like, for example, I bet getting Critical Role to play your game on stream is ten times better than having a booth at Gen Con with your new book in terms of publicity). But it is what it is... So this means there are only three release-dates in play: Gen-Con 2022, 2023 and 2024. Well, we got the first preview in October 2022 which is clearly too late for a 2022 release so that leaves 2023 and 2024. But one can understand that under that restriction you're gonna push hard for Gen-Con 2023. I mean, imagine... if they had delayed we still wouldn't have the book in our hands! Besides it wouldn't even matter to delay to 2024 because Justin Achilli decides to leave in January 2023. That's the second factor. And who knows why it turned out like that. But now that's another restriction. Also, it's unclear at what point it even became clear that this other "deadline" became a part of the development-process. But there's a third factor and that's the V5 Players Guide. That supplement is the rare exception where writers from the Paradox-in-house-team contributed to the book. Justin Achilli, Khaldoun Khelil and Karim Muammar (who also wrote parts of W5) contributed writing to the V5 Players Guide. Now the weird bit with the V5 PG is that it was originally scheduled to be released in Summer 2022 and was then delayed to early 2023. The official explanation from Renegade developers was that they needed time to "make the book better"... well, that sounds like PR-bullshit of course. But, interestingly, the development of this book fell into the very same timeframe as W5 went through the final stages of development (at least as far as writing was concerned).

                            It could very well be that a lot of what we're looking here as shortcomings - the "Who knows?"-approach to the worldbuilding, the preference to erase stuff instead of keeping things as they were or replace something with a new, original idea or the uncredited reliance on previous work from the Hunters Entertainment - are the result of a development-team through a combination of various factors just cornered itself into having to rush this project. It would also explain the Glass Walker controversy with the art. Say it's only in late October that the writers even got to the part of writing the archetypes of the Tribes in the Tribe-chapter. And then they turned to the artist and told him to give them 44 (!) photorealistic drawings in less than half a year that correspond to the archetypes. The result: He cut corners in the whole process (sourcing, how much he traced vs original art)... and well, the rest is history as they say. (BTW, I think Renegade at least has stopped using him because he was part of every WOD5E-book the last couple of years but his art wasn't a part of the most recent Bloodstained Love)

                            Social Media

                            Just two comments on that:
                            • What's striking is that Justin Achilli was on Outstar's WOD-news-show quite a few times to add developer-commentary to the release of the H5 corebook. And he was on the show again for the October preview of W5. But after he left the presence of developers on the WOD-news-show has disappeared. There were no developer-interviews for Blood Sigil and Blood-Stained Love and also not an extra interview for the release of W5 (like with H5). Now with the Renegade-books it's less of an issue because they're doing their own online-events as well but when it comes to what's going on with the in-house-team at Paradox... Who knows what's going on over there these days... (At least, Outstar has a cohost on her show now because there was a time when Outstar fell ill and that meant... there simply were no WOD-news-shows which was a bit ridiculous)
                            • Justin Achilli had this habit of randomly giving these hot takes about the WOD. Was it helpful from a PR-perspective to go on Twitter and shout random, uncontextualized ideas into the void? Not really. And there were weird moments like with the verbs-thing with the Get of Fenris. For example, in one show with Outstar he called H5 a "heist-game". Or he explained that Hunters in H5 don't need to have an equivalent to Humanity or something like Convictions and Chronicle Tenets because of something he called "Sober Play" because he reasoned that this would allow PCs to have "free will" and not be restricted by systems like a Humanity 4 in V5. Never understood what the deal was with these hot takes but that's how he operated. Also, he's pretty opinionated but if it were just a disagreement with his vision then it would be fine. But him talking about "Sober Play", for example, was meaningless because in effect it just meant H5 looked at the mechanics of V5, haphazardly removed bits and pieces and that was it. And also the term "Sober Play" never appeared in the H5 corebook. If it really had been such a cornerstone of the development-philosophy why was it never mentioned in the book? But when you read the descriptions from JF Sambrano it seems like Karim Muammar is quite the opinionated person as well. Maybe that's what H5 and W5 are in the end: The lowest common denominator between two very opinionated people.

                            Is the flawed state of W5 intentional?

                            I think Paradox at large is far more invested in WOD as a brand for videogames than any RPG-books (although even on that front... so what happened to the two announced text-adventures for Hunter and Werewolf...?). When you go to the new WOD-website that is now found on the Paradox-website you will have a far easier time finding the site for Bloodlines 2 instead of the page where WOD in general is talked about (and links to the books on the Renegade-website). The books are a sideshow to Paradox.

                            To Paradox, the only thing that matters right now isn't to plan for an M5 but rather to get Bloodlines 2 out the door in the hopes that it becomes a success. But I think similarly to W5, they are pushing for something to happen that simply won't. On the other hand, the only alternative to the problems mentioned above with the deadline would've been to delay the W5 corebook yet again. I would guess you would feel like you're going insane when you put people in charge of developing these RPG-books for the WOD and the projects keep getting derailed despite the changes you make to who's in charge and how the development is organized.

                            I guess we'll see what the future will bring. My money is still on Paradox giving the development of the next corebook to Renegade and just dissolving most of the in-house team.

                                    Two issues: Continuance and consistency.

                                    With the first, I just mean "You don't have to deal with that in the corebook." None of these WOD5E corebooks are meant to reproduce the tomes that are 20th Anniversary corebooks. And V5 even set a standard by launching with seven Clans in the corebook. You don't need all the options from previous editions to be immediately present (although I like the idea of making those other options available via free supplements like the V5 Companion whereas a specific supplement like Blood Sigils gets sold with the premise of expanding or offering more options for play). In that sense, it would have been fine to make H5 about local hunter-cells that have certain advantages and disadvantages/problems while hunter-orgs have different advantages and disadvantages/problems. And you just leave the door open for a supplement down the line that is all about playing a hunter working for a hunter-org. Instead, the H5 corebook is obsessed with making it clear that hunter-orgs are bad and that it's badwrongfun. The big issue with both the H5 and the W5 corebook are that they are very invested in closing doors for what potential supplements could explore.

                                    With the second, I'm talking about the changes W5 just addresses with erasure. The Crinos-Born and Kinfolk, for example. We're told that these troublesome elements needed to be excised because of the abuse, the breeding-talk and the social ostracization. But how about these three elements from V5: Thin-Bloods, Caitiff and the Blood-Bond. Is anything bad happening in V5 for those guys and whoever is affected by the last thing? In fact, let's quote the V5 Players Guide what it thinks of playing an All-Thin-Blood-Chronicle: "Players new to Vampire can benefit from playing an all thin-blood chronicle. Thin-blood characters don’t know any more about the world, or its history, or what vampires can do, than you do! This outsider perspective also allows players of older editions to painlessly reset their expectations of this new millennium of darkness." Imagine thinking that throwing a Thinblood-character into a Camarilla-run city is the ideal beginners-campaign. But by that standard W5 should definitely have kept the Crinos-Born and Kinfolk because they make for such tremendous "beginners-campaigns" according to the V5 PG. (Also, shoutout to the condescension of "allows players of older editions to painlessly reset their expectations".)
