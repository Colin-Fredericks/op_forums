                          I did some delayed updates to the Atlas. Caerns in the latest Mind's Eye Theatre: Werewolf the Apocalypse have been added. These are in their own layer, since MET usage of Caerns is different, and MET canon/continuity deviates pretty hard from Table Top in some cases.

                          I skimmed through some of the other newer releases and haven't found anything in the way Caerns (Wallows, etc) that were more than just a name mention from the following books:
                          W20 Changing Breeds (Some Caern names mentioned, no details)
                          W20 Core Rulebook (Kucha Ekundu Caern of the Bloodied Rock mentioned, no details)
                          W20 Shattered Dreams (Some Caern names mentioned, no details)
                          W20 Songs of the Sun and Moon (not surprisingly, but I wanted to check anyway!)
                          W20 Tribebook: White Howlers (Red Deer Caern mentioned, no details)
                          W20 Wyld West (Vague mentions only)
                          Here are some umbral Caerns I found mention of in W20 Umbra: The Velvet Shadow. There aren't many that I could find with much detail, but they might be good seed ideas if someone was looking:
                          Hive of the Jagged Maw [pg 46]
                          Location: The Abyss
                          Pit Level: not listed
                          Type: not listed
                          Totem: Eater-of-Souls is mentioned as being venerated, but actual totem is not listed

                          Graveyard of the Lost [pg 70]
                          Location: Pangea
                          Caern Level: 5
                          Type: not listed
                          Totem: not listed

                          Sept of the Bright Sky [pg 102]
                          Name of the Caern itself is not listed, if different from the Sept's
                          Location: Physical/Dark Umbra ? ("closely connected to both the Middle and Dark Umbra")
                          Caern Level: not listed
                          Type: not listed
                          Totem: not listed
                          From W20 Book of the Wyrm:
                          The Nameless [pg 99]
                          Location: West 64th street, New York City, "within spitting distance of the Sept of the Green"
                          Pit Level: not listed
                          Type: not listed
                          Totem: not listed

                          If someone catches something I overlooked from those books, I'd love to hear about it so I can add it. Ones on Laughing Hyena's list are still on my to-do list, as I got swallowed whole by RL events, but they're coming.

                                              I have officially found the Sept that has given me the most "wtf" in the ≈20 books I've gone through so far. I'm making this post mostly to step away from the atlas and vent for a moment, but if anyone has any suggestions or clarification I also gladly welcome it because I get pretty fixated and can miss obvious things.

                                              Alright. So I'm adding sites from Croatan Song right now, most of which don't have very explicit locations. I have to do some guesswork and make some decisions based on the territories of Indigenous Nations (who are thankfully mentioned often being Kinfolk to the Pure Tribes) and where seems like a reasonable location based on geographical features. It's actually a fun - if slow - process digging through these scraps of game-filtered history to try to solve the problem, and there's usually a spot to place the pin that feels right and makes sense at the end of it.

                                              Enter the Sept of the Pointing Stars (pg 52), and the computational paradox I'm stuck in.

                                              It's described as being twisted spires in salty waters on a towering desert plateau that is east of the Sierra Nevadas, and also in the territory of the Yokut, who are from the central California basin area. There is also a lake 60 miles across involved. Those terms are bolded to isolate the criteria for selecting a location for the sept map pin.

                                              In modern times there are no lakes even remotely 60 miles across, or long, in that entire region of the country. I went fishing for extinct lakes and found some interesting places, like Lake Cahuilla, which filled the Salton Basin. But it's too far south for Yokut territory, and way south of the Sierra Nevadas. The closest thing I could figure for the big lake is that at one point filled the San Joaquin Valley. Tulare Lake (links to an old map of when it was still there) seemed the most likely candidate, and was neat to learn about.

                                              Problems: as far as I can tell, there are no "towering desert plateaus" near where Tulare Lake was. The "east of the Sierra Nevadas" and "the Yokut fish and hunt here" parts are mutually exclusive. It's possible that "east" is simply a typo and was meant to be "west" but there are certainly (much smaller) salty waters and desert plateaus to the east which gives it credence. But as far as I've been able to determine, even in the past, Yokut territory has never been east of the Sierra Nevadas. It's possible another tribe was meant, but I have no where to go on that, and a conflating of east-west is a simpler and more likely answer to me.

                                              So it's looking like unless I find some new information, I have to choose between deciding between Yokut + lake, or east + towering plateaus. I'm inclined to go with the Yokut + lake option, which would also be exceptionally more tragic, as it means it would be dormant (probably more like 'dead') caern in the modern day, given the state of San Joaquin Valley.
