                        08-04-2023, 02:41 PM
                        To elaborate a bit more... I think a core-designing-principle in writing this book was to take every element and first ask questions like: "How do the Garou fuck this up? How are Garou bad at this task? What counterproductive strategy/tactic is employed by the Garou?". The idea wasn't, for example, to say that direct action in battling climate-change is bad but instead to make the whole thing about explaining why Garou are bad at it, do the wrong thing or employ some counterproductive strategy or something. The point is always to show that Garou aren't up to the task and depending on the context in the book you get an explanation for how they fuck up.

                        That's how you get sections like the Black Furies and Red Talons. You say they're about fighting injustice and protecting animals... but immediately what that followed up with is explaining how this Tribe fucked up, is counterproductive etc.

                        And for whatever reason, this game asks you to be one of those guys...

They are not unplayable, but here are massive penalties to avoid being taken in and against Rage, and you will have to do a story about them coming out of this emotional rut before it becomes self-sustaining." There, now it's a looming threat, but existential crises are now a thing - and the personal horror is intact while making it playable and the final fall being in the choice of players.

                                                    I find myself thinking back to the opening comic of W20, and how it revolves a young Garou and the Prophecy of the Phoenix, the prophecy that tells of the Apocalypse, and how the story reframes the meaning of the Prophecy from a portent of doom to a rallying cry for the Garou Nation, that even in the face of the Apocalypse, victory can be achieved. It honestly felt hopeful, that even if they can't stop the Apocalypse from happening, they'll fight it. That even if they all end up dying, they'll fight it. That willingness to fight in the face of the Apocalypse, even it's nothing more than a defiant last stand is noble, heroic.

                                                    And for WtA newcomers, that's something they could sink their teeth in. The heroism of stubbornly fighting in the face of certain doom can be quite appealing. Sure, the Garou have made many, many mistakes over the years and most will end up dying violently. Yet most stand defiant until that end, offering up their lives to prolong the world just a bit longer. And in that refusal to accept defeat there is hope that perhaps the Garou can one day win the war and save the world.

                                                    Do not go gentle into that good night. Rage, rage against the dying of the light.

                                                    Yet everything I've seen and heard about W5 can be summed up as 'You've lost. The Apocalypse is here. Any action to take is at best pointless and ineffectual, at worst only causing more damage. Have fun Garou!' before you even create your PC. So yeah, why should anyone play W5 in that situation? Like even in Apocalypse, the assumption was that you were going to use the book as an end for your ongoing Chronicle, not the starting point.

                                                    Honestly it's a spectacular failure to understand WtA on top of all the other parts of WtA they failed to understand. Frankly I'm almost impressed by not only how clueless about WtA this official WtA book is but also just how much the books seems to have nothing but contempt and hatred for Humans and Garou which is pretty weird given it wants you to play as Human-born Garou.

                                                    At this point I believe W5 is the worst 5E book to ever come out.

                                                    Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. They're indomitable. Indomitable.
