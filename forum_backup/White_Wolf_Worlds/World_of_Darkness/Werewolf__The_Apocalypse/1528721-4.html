    Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
    In such a setting, the way BSDs function is going to be radically different from Legacy. That is why I think they should be the most structured tribe, the one with the power in the age of apocalypse. No longer hiding in underground hives, now they can do whatever they want. And since the PCs aren't as beholden to the Litany as before, there is a strong incentive to let them to protect your own hide. It takes a lot of guts to fight back and take down a BSD pack, but then you have actually done something great by protecting your area.

    For the CoF, as I stated before, I don't want to focus on them vs what remains of the Nation. I want to focus on the meatgrinder of their existence. Hauglosk is still meant to be a sort of madness, so the tribe tries its best but is often running out of members due to them being a death cult and the enormity of their foes. So they try to get worthy garou to fill their ranks, or try to take over caerns in places they think are strategically important.
    I admit, if I was the line developer for W5, I would probably go in an interesting direction for the Black Spiral Dancers which is the fact that this isn't working out for them either. They believe the Howl is the death of Gaia but...nothing has changed. There has been no great reward, no hordes of Hell invading things, and no freedom of the Wyrm from the Weaver. There's a lot of interesting directions to go with the Black Spiral Dancer that can still leave them as a bunch of murderous thugs but also potentially having their own crisis of faith.

    Bluntly, the Wyrm isn't winning.

    The Weaver has.

    Assuming either cares in the slightest about all of this.

    But yes, the BSD should be suffering Hauglosk and Harano like the Garou are. Maybe also their own civil wars.

                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                Saying, "Don't fight the Wyrm" requires whoever says I'm saying that to not actually be reading my responses but we know that. The examples given are where the Garou Nation showing how badly they've done it and mistakes they've made in believing wiping out the werebears was "fighting the Wyrm." So was the Impergium. So was the War in the Amazon (which as mentioned was supposed to have ended on a high note but didn't change the fact the initial set up was Jaguars and Mokole vs. Garou vs. Pentex).

                I've mentioned multiple times the thing about W5 is it's not saying not to fight the Wyrm. It's saying that fighting the Wyrm isn't the mindset you should be having. It should be saving Gaia (if such is possible) or even if Gaia isn't savagable, then doing as much good as possible anyway. Harano is literally the state of doing nothing becaue it all seems so pointless. Now this is confusing but it's something that really appeals to me as it allows a lot of chances for angst as well as brooding.

                My earliest experience with Werewolf the Apocalypse was Under a Blood Red Moon where my PCs broke the treaty with the Gangrel immediately, slaughtered a bunch of cops, and generally behaved as the worst sort of Garou stereotypes (we were fourteen). They didn't fight intelligently or with diplomacy but "Wyrm, Smash!"

                And that's a fun game.

                Antiheroes for the win.

                But it makes it very easy to understand why the Cult of Fenris as a group of villains should exist to show the kind of mayhem the Garou were engaged in was not only counterproductive but outright evil. Where is the new line, though? That is a question the Garou have to figure out themselves and what is or is not justified in the quest to save Gaia or whatever is left?
                Yes and it's a good outline that explains the bad writing in the W5 corebook. It all culminates in "Where is the new line, though?" and the game has absolutely no answer to that. Everything got rejected. Garou-Nation. Fallen. The Litany. Just sucks and gets exploited constantly. Umbra. Dangerous and pretty inaccessible. Gaia. Dead/Dying. The Apocalypse. It's here and can't be stopped. Garou. All doomed to fall to Harano or Hauglosk at some point.

                So where is that new line even supposed to come from...? Because you can reject all those things and make the game about that very new line. But W5 is not about that. You're still a Garou, you still chase Renown, you're still supposed to deal with spirits even though the Umbra has become more dangerous and inaccessible, you still hang around in Septs, you deal with caerns, when you encounter Wyrm-minion, Fomori, Bane-spirits, you still in the end just fight them (there's no revelatory alternate path within the game) and Harano and Hauglosk are these damocles-swords hanging over the head of your character that you can't escape from.

                W5 doesn't have an answer to what the "new line" is even supposed to be. Or comes even close to suggesting how there can be a "new line" in the first place.

                    The Garou can't beat the Wyrm with claws and attempting to do so has failed from probably the 20th century onward. Basically, once Pentex got involved, the Garou lost the war. At least with their current methodology. Its certainly FUN to go on a murder spree through Endron oil in EARTHBLOOD but I don't think anyone can argue that Cahill was making significant progress against Endron that way.

                    Instead, I *REALLY LIKED* Heart of the Forest and the fact that you can achieve your mission via protests, sabotage, and behind the scene work but the ending of the story has you find out that a Formori is going to sabotage all your efforts and you have to figure out whether to kill the guy or not.

                    If you're a Werewolf and want to do things other than claw things. What do you do?

                    * Use Rites to cleanse areas with magic.
                    * Go into the Umbra and slay the Bane of Pollution or whatnot so all of the oil damage or chemical waste dissolves in the real world (which I assume is what happens unless more is poured into the river)
                    * Fight the supernatural goons that the baddies are sending against you. The WOD is a comic book so while Roxxon Oil can only be beaten by Tony Stark money or the government, that doesn't mean Roxxon isn't sending the Rhino to kill protestors.
                    * Put a Glass Walker keyfob that will sort through all of the information in a hacking tool because MAGIC. Making hacking like it is in movies.
                    And so on.

                    Basically, SPIDER-MAN: MILES MORALES is a Werewolf: the Apocalypse game. Miles Morales and Phin both want to stop Roxxon Oil from putting up their toxic nuclear reactors in Harlem that will kill thousands of people over the next few years but they will continue to build because they make more money than they'll pay out in wrongful deaths.

                    Phin wants to blow up Roxxon's HQ and kill people but this just helps Roxxon look like heroes in the public eye. Miles Morales' mother works with the politics while Miles struggles to get the information they need to expose Roxxon in the public and fights the Rhino and otrher supervillains while sabotaging their work.​
