    razor claws gift

    04-17-2020, 08:32 AM
    The text says ;
    Rank 1 Ahroun Gift / Rank 1 Get of Fenris Gift
    By raking his claws over stone, steel, or another hard surface, the Ahroun hones them to razor sharpness. Either a cat- or bear-spirit teaches this Gift.

    System: The player spends on Rage point, and the Ahroun takes a full turn sharpening her claws. All claw attacks do two additional dice of damage and are made at -1 difficulty for the rest of the scene.


    The -1 difficulty is to damage or attack

                  There's still contention over the cost though as a Rage point and a full round action makes it a huge gamble to use in sudden combat.                           The idea of sharpening your claws taking a full round makes sense, but it's completely arbitrary that you need to physically sharpen them in that fashion. If the Gift was described as, "The Garou imbues their claws with their righteous fury, causing them to become sharper and deadlier than usual." It wouldn't make sense for it to have the action needed.

It's easy to say it's not asking too much when you're setting aside things like, "you might have to eat a round of being attacked because you can't defend yourself, and might potentially die before the Gift can matter."

                              Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post

It's easy to say it's not asking too much when you're setting aside things like, "you might have to eat a round of being attacked because you can't defend yourself, and might potentially die before the Gift can matter."
                              I agree that taking a full-round action with what amounts to somatic component makes the Gift not reasonably employable in many situations (where the Gift user is being ambushed obviously), however, I'm also okay with there being gifts that are made in such a way that they're only truly viable if someone anticipates combat. I would not have bothered to buy this Gift prior to the W20 version unless I were really and truly out of stuff to buy, and just had the XP "burning a hole in my pocket".

                              Having said that, the rule in Revised is "The player spends one Rage point, and the Ahroun must take a full turn to sharpen her claws[,]" and W20 is, "[t]he player spends one Rage point and the Ahroun takes a full turn sharpening her claws." I've never thought of this until discussing the Gift more in-depth now, but I find there's something to be said for it being a full turn rather than the entire round of combat. Given the fact that the full-turn activation is the product of the fact there's a physical action that takes a certain amount of time--a full turn--to perform (rather than being the product of some mystical rate at which the power instantiates itself (such as with Protean 2 in Vampire, wherein you spend the blood, and it takes a turn, because that's just how long it takes for the power to manifest it self), would it not be possible to spend a number of Rage points at the beginning of combat for Razor Claws and extra actions, and then use one of the later extra actions to fulfill the narrative, physical component of activating the Gift? As I understand it, from a game standpoint, every Rage action is the effective length of the non-rage (i.e., regular) turn--I'm not saying you can literally do anything on a Rage action that you could do on a regular action (because that's obviously not the case), but, certainly if the game positions this full-turn requirement as a matter of practical/physical necessity, I don't really see why you couldn't kick on the power on the front end of the combat round, and do the actual sharpening on the back end. You wouldn't benefit from the Giftthat round, but you also wouldn't necessarily be helpless.

Esp. for lower Rage characters (and the cost of raising your WP and Gnosis up to mitigate high Rage), spending Rage is still resource management. Is that bonus really worth one less extra action that fight? Or a different Gift that might be more useful?

                                  Razor Claws has also always had to contend with the Hispo form all Garou get automatically. Hispo gives a +1 damage to bites, which are already -1 difficulty compared to claws, and +1 Dexterity over Crinos, letting it capitalize on that lower difficulty attack even more. 5 XP for what amounts to between +2 and +3 damage dice (and that's with W20's buffing Razor Claws and normal claw attacks), and a lower hit rate compared to a free power you have that don't cost a full turn to activate is less of a no brainer in its wider context.

                                  Or for the more weapon Fetish happy Garou, there's the consideration of activating Razor Claws, or activating a weapon Fetish (esp. since the Garou are fans of daggers which might not have high damage but do have inherent low difficulty to attack).

                                  So there's some serious competition from tactics where that XP would be wasted because other modes of attack are the focus. Though bitey Garou have an odd curve since bite enhancing Gifts, which tend to be really good, are also high level, where there's a lot more low level claw boosters.

                                  I've never thought of this until discussing the Gift more in-depth now, but I find there's something to be said for it being a full turn rather than the entire round of combat.
                                  The two are effectively interchangeable. A full-turn action uses up your character's entire turn, including being unable to use Rage for extra actions, for the combat round. Something that takes your full turn means it's the only non-reflexively thing you can do that round. Though W20 doesn't really explain this well, and the only time it really specifies this is in an odd place (reloading firearms), but in previous editions/FAQs/etc. that's always been how the "full-turn" requirement has been stated to mean.

                                  So you can't activate Razor Claws and spend more Rage for extra actions, or put Razor Claws into a Rage action.

                                      Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post

                                      First, I basically agree with you about the intent of the text as written, however, that's only because that interpretation is (I said above) the way I always thought the power worked until I took a really close look at it today. Having said that, even if it's my kneejerk (and historical) interpretation, I don't know that I can make a textual argument for it being inarguably the correct reading. I can't speak to "prior editions, FAQ's, etc.", because I don't necessarily know where I'd be looking for this besides core books, but Revised is basically as poorly written in this respect as W20 (as I get to below).

                                      Re-reading the most basic parts of the systems and drama rules (i.e., this is a turn, this is action, etc.) in any of these books for the first time in years, I have to agree that W20's is written extremely poorly--imprecision and sloppy drafting abound. Where the book explains the action economy, and describes "Multiple Actions", There is no mention of anything called a "round", but then there are several mentions of "Rounds" (i.e., things that either take, expire, or last for, "a round") in other parts of the books, and particularly in the descriptions of the mechanical operations of various Gifts. They use "Turn" to not only mean the discrete allotment of time a character has to act during combat pursuant to the initiative order, but also use the word "turn" the way I would use the word "round", such as when the books says, "[f]or each point of Rage spent, the character gets to take an additional action that turn." By using the word in seemingly two different ways, often in the same, or back-to-back sentences, they're basically creating boundless potential for ambiguities.

                                      However, with specific regard to what W20 says about the rules for reloading, I think there's something fundamentally distinguishable about the reasons provided by the book as to why you can't reload a revolver on rage actions, and the act of sharpening claws against a surface.

                                      W20 provides as follows:

                                      Revolvers can be tricky to reload. A character with a speedloader can slap a new “clip” into his gun, as above. If he doesn’t have a speedloader ready, he needs to take a full turn to reload that pistol manually. No other actions are possible that turn, not even when spending Rage — the gun and bullets can’t move any faster. A character with a Firearms rating of 1 or better can reload without making a roll under normal circumstances, although certain complications (badly hurt, in a moving car, etc.) might demand a Dexterity + Firearms roll. Reloading a clip or speedloader with fresh bullets, however, requires a Dexterity + Fireaarms roll (difficulty 6) and an entire turn. (Again, extra Rage actions cannot speed this reloading time.)
                                      W20, p.297(pdf)

                                      So the reason they cite for not being able to load a revolver on Rage actions is that (unlike with Time dilation in Mage) the Werewolf is physically moving faster, but the gun and bullets cannot. Mind you, I don't understand why it is that a semi-automatic's upper receiver and magazine can move faster, or that a speedloader and a revolver can also move faster, but individual bullets and a revolver cannot, or why you wouldn't be able to just load single single bullet into a single chamber in the cylinder per rage action--but whatever; it is what it is. I digress--sharpening claws should probably not be bound by the same limitations: the claws are part of the werewolf, so they necessarily moves as fast as the Garou; and the surface upon which the claws are sharpened doesn't actually move at all, so I don't think that's a consideration either.

                                      If I had to head-canon this so that it makes some sort of sense in the narrative, I would posit that it's less about making sure the claws are made sharper (though that's part of it), than it is a part of some per-use chiminage or similar obeisance that must be made as part of the initial spirit pact--yes, the user needs to sharpen their claws against some surface to get them to that razor's edge, but the fact that they're taking no other action that round is spiritually important to the activation of the power, rather than an incidental byproduct of the claw sharpening process (as if you there's something about physics or material science that would prevent the werewolf from sharpening their claws as fast as they can rake their claws against whatever surface).
