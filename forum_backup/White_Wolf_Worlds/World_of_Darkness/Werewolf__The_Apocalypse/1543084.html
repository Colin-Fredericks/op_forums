      I'm going to dig into some Gifts now. I feel the need to preface this by noting that I never really felt great about the widespread use of perma-Renown values in W:tA Gifts, being fairly comfy in the weird and off the wall Trait combinations that you saw (Manipulation + Craft to convince a bunch of machines that they shouldn't work!), so Gifts using Attribute + Renown, especially when it's only three types of Renown and some may or may not fit, irk me by default. Adding on the lack of Gnosis and focus on Rage, and you just have too few variables to work with.

      Originally posted by W5
      Native Gifts
      Because we'd rather not even remember lupus Garou. And Crinos-born? HAH!

      Renown 1
      The equivalent of starter-tier stuff, even though "starter-tier" in W:tA had a lot more to do with spiritual attunement and favor, i.e. the reason that Rokea got Teeth of the Skin at Rank 1 instead of 4, or why Bastet could all be counted on to have the really boss Catfeet Gift at Rank 1.

      Catfeet
      Well, that kind of takes the wind from the sails of the Bastet... not that the people in charge probably care.

      Works in any form, keeps you from slipping without a roll, ignores fall damage under a certain height, can be activated reflexively... but requires 1 Willpower. A bit of a nerf, since it doesn't aid in a bunch of other balance-related rolls like grappling. I suppose it could have been worse; the tenor of a lot of Gifts is that some assmad Vampire stan got really vengeful reworking stuff, even if it was way subtler than, say, anything that altered Crinos to be more effective.

      Eyes of the Owl
      Your eyes glow and you can see in the da... come on, guys. This is blatantly just Protean 1.

      Hare's Leap
      Your jumping distance is now based on Strength + Glory, but at least it didn't get driven into the ground.

      Penumbral Senses
      Basically, you're straddling the gauntlet. Spirits can see you if you're physical but you're still invisible to physical beings if you're using it in the Umbra. Past editions did this largely via peaking, a natural ability that Garou just had.

      Raging Strike
      You can... add your Glory to your unarmed attack pool for one Rage check? This is like a meaner version of Trick Shot for mauling people. Again, it seems like the Vampire stans didn't like the less direct approach for a lot of Gifts and just added on some kind of Celerity effect.

      Staredown
      You don't need to roll against non-magical humans, they just beat it, but other games' characters and such get to resist. Can't be used if anyone started swinging first, which is a nerf, as is only getting one shot at it. Fortunately, not so nerfed that you have to be out of Homid.

      Total Renown 4
      Intermediate for this category, in other words.

      Sharpened Senses
      Blanket reduction of difficulty for sensory rolls, but it costs one Willpower.

      Spirit of the Fray


      A kind of poster child for how the Vampire hardliners for crossover Do Not Get It, this Gift's effects in W5 are what every Garou could use Rage to do to make multiple attacks, limited by Dex and Wits. Except that you can only get in one attack per target, so no straight-up mauling one dude with a vengeance. Each extra attack requires a Rage check. The only silver lining is that if you used something like Raging Strike, it applies to every attack.

      This is a huge nerf, because in addition to this being a basic part of Rage, Spirit of the Fray also used to let you either go first (pre-Revised) or likely beat the Initiative count. Did it make Garou really dangerous to attack? Yes, but in those days, it was also mostly limited to Ahrouns (others could get it for a higher price), and also who cares unless you're trying to one up the Garou in a combat game, which is just flat out a losing proposition.

      Thwarting the Arrow
      Only usable in Crinos, it makes projectiles curve away from you. Still not a consolation for anything, really, as you need to be as preoccupied with Honor as Prince Zuko in order to really make it work.

      Total Renown 7
      Top of the world, ma! But not really, as you'll soon see.

      Body Shift
      You know that weird Gift from the 1e player's guide that allowed you to change your Physical Attributes? It's that, plus Rat Head. Given the limits of how many dots you can move around, I'm not sure why this is the equivalent of an elder-tier power, except that the power balance of this game doesn't actually exist and it was pointless to take Rage extra actions away.

      Jam Technology
      Getting really transparent that the Vampire crowd hated some effects here. Not only is it reliant on following in the footsteps of Zuko for some reason, it also won't let you keep sharp objects from cutting. Given that it also calls out bicycles as being really difficult to use it on, I can't help but think that they were trying to enforce some kind of safety rule for a Kids on Bikes crossover. The only saving grace is that it lasts for an entire scene, which was way longer than it did in past editions, but at what cost?

      (Also, its description is functionally the same as in past editions and it has the same misleading name. Reboot or reimagination, my ass!)

      Tongue of the Beasts
      "You are now friends with a stick bug. The stick bug will now DIE FOR YOU."

      That's but one example use given for being able to communicate with non-human animals, but it's kind of weird that it was. This is also starting to burrow beneath the surface of the mindset that no one should have when writing W:tA but numerous authors clearly did for this book, where it starts to discount animal cognitive faculties because in addition to not having read any W:tA books since 1993, I can't imagine that they read up on anything about comparative cognition more recent than the 80s. I know it's common for trpgs, but again, for Werewolf, it's jarring in the extreme, since it generally doesn't posture about human cognitive capabilities. That would ruin the atmosphere of the entire game.

      Time to go and do something else. I'll be back tomorrow.

                          Ragabash Gifts!

                          Total Renown 2
                          The required Renown is higher for Auspices. These are still the basement level, first of three.

                          Blissful Ignorance
                          This one is coming a bit earlier, unless your mindset is to compact pairs of ranks together. Oddly enough, still requires Stealth if you plan on waiting for an ambush. Also requires Wisdom to be really effective, which is another count of "Wouldn't this be better if you had, I don't know, Cunning and another type of Renown?" because that's a bit of an odd fit.

                          Crow's Laughter
                          This one is even more off - a troll laugh that's supernaturally difficult to ignore and directs all attention toward you. For some reason, it requires HONOR! (I'm going to be hitting this Ember Island Zuko thing a lot, sorry not sorry). Causes Emotional Damage (Superficial to WP).

                          Gremlins
                          This one is even earlier than Blissful Ignorance, and at first seems more effective than the older version of the Gift in some ways, as it has a five meter reach. However, your dice pool is going to be... markedly different. Better farm that Glory, cub. Also requires a critical success to perma-brick something, and it's trimmed back in the way that Jam Technology was, so overall, not great, but not the worst change that a Gift has had coming to W5.

                          Spider's Song
                          A lot of this text is copied wholesale from the W20 entry. Nerfed here, as it requires a Resolve + Wisdom roll to listen in, and a fair number of successes to actually hear everything.

                          Total Renown 5
                          Intermediate.

                          Blur of the Milky Eye
                          This one clocks in a lot later, as it used to be an intro Gift. This one is odd, in that it is a stealth Gift that requires a Rage check to use. Yet another reason that Gnosis is really needed. Now functions to make you like the Predator, but only until you actually attack.

                          Open Seal
                          Vampire main bitterness, rising. Also a former intro Gift, now relies on HONOR! and makes opening mystically protected doors way harder. Stay mad, vamps; odds are you'll miss something important!

                          Pulse of the Prey
                          Now requires a resource expenditure and works on Death Note rules. Nerfed, clearly.

                          Scent of Running Water
                          One gets the distinct impression that Vampire and Werewolf had really, really different priorities in how they rated powers. Hmm. Who would have thought. At any rate, this eliminates all traces of your passage and makes you harder to track via Renown, so it's kind of buffed a little, particularly in being able to bring your pack along for the ride.

                          Crucially, though it can be used in Lupus and is turned on or off at will, there is no callout made that you need to have it off to meaningfully interact with wolves. Probably because as has been pointed out, they don't think that's possible.

                          Total Renown 8
                          Elder level, I guess.

                          Luna's Blessing
                          Major nerf. Requires activation and doesn't protect against all silver damage, nor does it add those extra three dice that are only there to provide 1s. Quelle surprise.

                          Thieving Talons of the Magpie
                          Some of the pools get shifted around, and the Renown doesn't make sense (HONOR!), but the core of the power remains. WTF? I've heard more rants about this one than any other, bar none, but apparently it got the pass. Did not see that one coming.

                          Also, it refers to the level of a Gift as its Rank, which is not how Gifts seem to be referred to anywhere else. Ah, eto... bleh!

                          The Thousand Forms
                          Another bizarre dice pool and questionable Renown choice, and you can't turn into mythical animals. Nerfed. Also doesn't account for being able to turn into an animal that could be much, much better at communicating with humans than a wolf (if you turn into a parrot, you can utterly fuck with someone's head for fairly obvious reasons, and they're far from the only animal with a talent for vocal mimicry). Once again, kind of predictable; woe for the loss of writers chosen for knowledge of, you know, the natural world that the Garou are fighting for, and everything living within it.

                          Whelp Body
                          A nerf in some ways, since its range is rather truncated and it uses HONOR! for some reason. Some maniac got rid of the "only used once per target ever" part, though, so even if its effects aren't permanent, it can be heinously spammed without mercy. So, if you're playing W5 as a Ragabash, make sure to do just that. You'll need to commit to the ideals of both Prince Zuko and Kiryuin Satsuki to make it functional, however.

                              Two general notes on Gifts in W5:
                              • For most Gifts you have to be in a supernatural Form (Glabro, Crinos or Hispo) to use them. What this means is that you not only have the Rage-check for the Gift (or the WP-cost) but you also have to deal with the one (or two in the case of Crinos) Rage-check for shifting Form. In addition, in W5 you not only have to do the Rage-check for shifting Form but you need to do the Rage-check again for every scene afterwards. So a lot of Gifts are tied to being in a supernatural Form when at the same time W5 as a game discourages shifting often and for too long. There are a lot of Gifts with smaller effects (like Eyes of the Owl or Penumbral Senses) where it feels like a waste to tie it to having to be in a supernatural Form. And it feels like unnecessary word-count bloat to have to add "This Gift can be used in any Form." to the Gift-descriptions.
                              • The Renown-distribution is one of those things where you could have made it work with the three Renowns by giving the Gift-list a full overhaul instead of trying to crowbar old Gifts into this framework, I think. Also, the way it's presented it feels weird that the Tribe Gifts are tied to one Renown while Native- and Auspice-Gifts try to be balanced in what Renown is called for. That way it makes it seem like Tribe is the main-indicator of what Renown you're gonna focus on rather than your Auspice.

                                  Beginner's Level!

                                  Ensnare Spirit
                                  Apparently taught by a filthy, filthy Weaver-spirit of law, this Gift mentions mandates that Gaia gave in a mythic time. And to use it, you need HONOR! to keep the spirit at bay. But if it already attacked, you're SOL. It gives a slight penalty to the spirit's rolls, too.

Oh, and it heals boring, starchy, ham-filled superficial damage. Costs WP instead of Gnosis, and healing is more effective if your patient has less Rage.

                                  Shadow Sense
                                  Basically, it's Sense the Unnatural.

                                  Sight From Beyond
                                  Still based on the ST's whims. The more things change...

                                  Intermediate!

                                  Banish Spirit
                                  Huh. It's way, way easier to kick banes out of fomori, now. The damage is substantially less, especially if you get a good roll on this Gift. Which requires Glory.

                                  Grasp From Beyond
                                  Bypass the time-based Umbral travel damage by pulling your car into the spirit world!

                                  Mindspeak
                                  Now, I know what you might be thinking. "But isn't that a Galliard Gift?"

                                  Yes. It was. You can probably already guess the scourge that has overcome the Galliard Gifts. At any rate, this Gift has been nerfed heavily in some capacities, as it no longer allows groups, but you don't have to be in line of sight unless you don't know the target. It's also impossible to multi-task and act with your real body without breaking the Gift.

                                  Umbral Tether
                                  Pretty much the way it was, except, you know, for not being entry-level. Also gives a bonus for navigation. The book keeps referring to the Umbra as the Spirit Wilds, incidentally, and I don't like it.

                                  Elder Level!

                                  Command Spirit
                                  In spite of being higher in tier, it still can't force a spirit out of what it's possessed.

                                  Drain Spirit
                                  Not much has changed, except that they won't even give an Essence rating to spirits. Also, kind of weird to use Glory. Unlike past editions, explicitly called out as that one little trick that spirits hate.

                                  Feral Regression
                                  I guess it wouldn't do to keep calling it Feral Lobotomy. At any rate, much like Whelp Body, the book really doesn't want to cause permanent damage, so you'll likely only end up wrecking their faculties for the remainder of the story. Also targets Mental Attributes in general instead of Intelligence.

                                  Living Ward
                                  THE LIVING WARD! MWA HAHAHAHAHA! [/The Critic Version of Orson Welles]

                                  If I'm comparing this to Spirit Ward, it's way more limited in its exclusion radius and doesn't auto-damage spirits in said radius. With noticeable backfire potential, it's a big nerf.

                                  And that's it; no Pulse of the Invisible, no Malleable Spirit, no Ultimate Argument of Logic, definitely no As In The Beginning. Not really surprising; if diehard Vampire partisans are aching to nerf anything, it would be the Garou capacity to interact with the Umbra and deal with spirits. How dare those characters in the other game do something better in such a clear manner?

                                      Philodox- Keeper of the Ways. What Ways? Look in a pre-W5 book!

                                      Beginner Level

                                      Ancestral Conviction
                                      Now, W5 beats the "Garou are worthless and have nothing to help outside of a fight" drum more often than Exalted: the Lunars beat its "we don't need no Solar Exalted, we don't need no social control" drum. This Gift cuts against that notion by allowing you to add your HONOR! to Persuasion against other Garou. Why is this significant? Because it means that the Philodox can actually iron out disputes, in spite of the assumption of the writing that no one can even try to keep the folks around them from going to shit. The text is saying one thing while the rules are going in the opposite direction.

                                      Gaia's Candor
                                      It's Truth of Gaia. It uses Glory. Why? Degenerate game states.

                                      Porcupine's Reprisal
                                      It's the Justice Field from Red Dwarf. It also uses Glory, and does superficial damage as an auto-hit counterattack effect. For some reason, even though its name mentions it, the first example spirit to teach it is a hedgehog-spirit.

                                      Sense the True Form
                                      Someone decided that Scent wasn't evocative and went with a general-use Sense. It's a lot harder to tell if someone is a non-Werewolf, and it costs WP, continuing in the baffling trend of "this was free to use in prior editions, but will cost you now."

                                      Intermediate Gifts

                                      Beast's Fealty
                                      It's King of Beasts. Except that King of Beasts didn't go out of its way to highlight another case of "This animal will die for you." What do the writers think we are? Animalism-using licks?

                                      Command the Gathering
                                      Yet another case where the Garou do, in fact, have ways to convince and sway others, in this case, crowds of them. Can't be used if violence breaks out, but no one can say that you didn't at least try.

                                      Fangs of Judgment
                                      Instead of being a combat Charm for use against beings that broke the laws of Gaia, it marks a single enemy for death and gives a +1 damage bonus. Seems kind of paltry for an intermediate Gift in the hands of someone how can already dish out huge gobs of damage, probably, but it does last until the enemy is dead and benefits your entire pack.

                                      Scent of the Past
                                      Psychometric visions of a location. Except that since this game wants to ignore the past where it pertains to Garou and their society, it's highly limited and can't reach back for more than a few years. Weaksauce; most Gifts that were mid-tier in past editions could reach back way farther than that.

                                      Elder Level Gifts

                                      Geas
                                      "I, Lelouch vi Britannia, command you to sign all your shares in the company away to this trust..."

                                      For some reason, it can't get people to tell you information. Which I guess is just another count on the great, big "I dunno!" of the entire edition's backstory. Since the effective duration of this Gift is truncated down to a scene and it can only hit one person at a time, I feel confident saying that this is horribly nerfed, and I could also be extremely uncharitable and suggest it was because Vampire mains don't like it when Garou mind-whammy their characters instead of the other way around.

                                      That aside, another example of how Garou do, in fact, have tools to use for non-violent ends. Perhaps you won't have the roller coaster ride of world domination that Lelouch did in Code Geass spamming it whenever you can, but you'll probably be able to deal some terrible blows to organizations by getting close to their decision makers.

                                      Oathbreaker's Bane
                                      Great for a contingency, but it doesn't do much to magically punish breaking an oath; it's merely an assurance that you know when it happens and where they are.

                                      Take the True Form
                                      No one shapeshifts in front of Zuko. The opposed roll means that you might not be able to make it work, though, so boo! Boo! Boo!

                                      The Philodoxes made out well, as far as anyone did, but the book seems to think that their Gifts don't exist, or somehow could never possibly change the world, even though this is some pretty nice social power. Yet another reason that you should probably just scrap whatever they have planned and refer to the history in Revised. Tomorrow, we'll delve into the terrible fate of the Galliard auspice in W5. I guarantee that if you know about the writing background for sample Galliard characters, you absolutely will not be surprised in the slightest.

                                              And some powers that were similar to a particular manifestation of Presence no longer are. But that's several splats down.

                                              Galliard Gifts

                                              The first part of Galliard Gifts goes on to describe... Song Gifts! You can only be using one song at a time! Because Galliards are artists which means music and being musicians. No historians could ever possibly be in this splat. Oh no.

                                              Beginner Level

                                              Animal Magnetism
                                              Shit name of the power I'd always wished they'd remove or seriously alter. But here, it's just a fairly solid social boost, which is yet another strike against the supposition of "Garou do not have the tools for dealing with people outside of violence".

                                              Because they do. It adds to all social pools for a scene. I wonder if they'll ever address that in whatever books might come.

                                              Howl of Assembly
                                              This is like Strength of Will from legacy, which was an elder-level Ahroun Gift. But here, it can only give back a single point of WP and can only be used once per session. Sad Zuko noises.

                                              Song of Rage
                                              Gives a point or two of Rage once per session. This is a stark departure from its prior form, where it would just get anyone - even if they couldn't normally frenzy - to potentially lose their shit and frenzy. Wonderful for making sure that your enemies couldn't employ coordinated actions in combat. Here? It's just turning Galliards into helpful bards.

                                              Song of Serenity
                                              Literally the opposite of Song of Rage in function, and can also pull someone out of frenzy. Clear evidence that Song of Rage should make people frenzy, though it doesn't.

                                              Intermediate Gifts

                                              Call the Ridden
                                              Since W5 is extremely non-committal about the Wyrm being bad for some reason, Call of the Wyrm got turned into just attracting possessed characters. For some reason, no longer uses Manipulation. It does use HONOR!, which seems like yet another case for Cunning.

                                              Eyes of the Cobra
                                              Aside from the pluralizing of its name and the dice pool used, pretty much the same thing as Eye of the Cobra. I guess ChaGlo works well enough. There's also a weird subsystem for not looking into someone's eyes; I guess you could use that to avoid Dominate, too. Since it ends when your quarry gets there but can last for an entire scene, you can potentially keep falling back just a bit to get them into dangerous situations that Call the Ridden wouldn't, and it works on anyone with functional eyes.

                                              Song of Valor
                                              Tell me, Mr. Anderson, how will you sing of the deeds of ancient Garou heroes... if you have no idea what they are? Assuming that your character actually has material to work with, they can expand the rerolling via Willpower system for everyone for the scene. Can only be used once per d- I mean session. Certainly Galliards are not blatantly just bards!

                                              Song of Inspiration
                                              "Okay, you got us. Galliards are totally just D&D bards for Garou."

                                              No use restriction, too, so you really can just go around playing music for inspiring your pack members just like Elan from OOTS.

                                              "Elder" Gifts

                                              Against the Odds
                                              Oh, now here's Strength of Will!... but only for your pack, and only once per story. It's nerf or nothin'.

                                              Break the Shackles
                                              Formerly a legendary Gift that also allowed you to free others from being physically restrained, now just for breaking mind control. I suppose we're lucky it even does that, but the fact that there's an ST-ruled, arbitrary difficulty for doing so based on the power of the one that initiated control doesn't bode well.

                                              Defy Death
                                              Ah, see, now here's how to nerf a power! For reference, the Silver Fangs' secret of death, Ignore Wound, allowed them to flat out ignore a killing blow. This? This slightly cushions it by allowing you to roll your HONOR! to try and claw back from the edge of death. Given dice probability, I think you'll end up dying if you try to use this even once... just like my incredibly unfortunate Flagellants in Darkest Dungeon on the first Death's Door. [OMINOUS BELL TONE]

                                              Dreamwalk
                                              Like Gift of Dreams, but surprisingly improved in a number of ways, as you can peep on peoples' dreams as well and you're not limited by distance or the need to breathe dreams into someone. A rare win, here.

                                              So, now you know why practically all the Galliard characters in the books are musicians - someone seemed to literally think that "Galliard = D&D Bard" and wrote it into their mechanics. There was no time for historians; if you can't play music, you can't be a D&D bard! Except that not even D&D bards are that restricted, I think. So you're really more like a caricature of one, with some combat leadership that used to be the Ahroun's bag. Which probably raises a justifiably terrifying question - if the Ahrouns aren't inspiring others to fight anymore, what do they do?

                                              (Combat and scaring people. It's mostly just combat and scaring people.)

                                                          Ahroun, 56709.

                                                          We're given a text box for Claw Gifts. They have the same restrictions as Song Gifts, but for claws. Someone hated power stacking something fierce.

                                                          Beginner Level - Whoopsie Doodle, the book lists it as Renown 1 like the Native Gifts!

                                                          Halt the Coward's Flight
                                                          This is very informative, in that the design staff that settled on this thought that being an Ahroun was so synonymous with being a Get of Fenris, that they had less work to do rather than more to emphasize the distinction between the tribe and the auspice. In any case, this Gift is very similar to a Fenrir one of prior days, and it at least makes sense for it to use HONOR!, I suppose. It's slightly more effective here, believe it or not, because it slows down targets way more than by half and can slow down vehicles. I'm not sure that the staff really thought that one through, but I'll take it, such as it is.

                                                          Rapid Shift
                                                          This really should be a Native Gift. And not have a horrible potential drawback (damage if you miss the roll by the number of missing successes). Or maybe you should just put Primal-Urge back in.

                                                          Razor Claws
                                                          Almost as impressive as the W20 version, this slow-trickles damage buff in the form of relying on your Glory, but not like, all of it. Also causes you to grow claws in forms that don't have claw attacks, so it basically also packages in Predator's Arsenal, if you squint. Doesn't explicitly say that it works in said forms, so it may have been that Gifts could originally be used in every form by default.

                                                          Sense Danger
                                                          Broadened from Sense Silver, I suppose. It's a perma-buff to your capabilities in case of immediate danger, so basically Spidey Sense.

                                                          Intermediate Gifts (Correct Renown amount listed)

                                                          Primal Anger
                                                          Pretty much works as before, but it's a full tank up, since Rage is so low now. Also limited in use, probably because the game hates opportunities for you to use Rage and wants them to end in three turns.

                                                          Snarl of Challenge
                                                          "You dare to refuse my batchall? Prepare to feel the wrath of the Falcon's Claws!"

                                                          You're basically marking opponents like in D&D 4e, here, and not with the one at a time powers, either. You get some temporary HLs if anyone that you succeeded against attacks you. At least one part of the auspice that's not "Me smash! Me scary!" still exists.

                                                          True Fear
                                                          Norms shit bricks. Other monsters might shit bricks. Somewhat on par with its prior version, as it can be resisted, but rather expensively.

                                                          Wind Claws
                                                          Nerfed severely, because it doesn't completely ignore armor, doesn't do so well against magic armor, and can't be stacked with other Gifts that increase claw damage or the like.

                                                          Elder Level Gifts (if that is their real name)

                                                          Closing the Gap
                                                          This is inferior to a Celerity 3 power, like, far behind it. Weaksauce, especially since I was hoping back in the day that Rage would just allow it without the need to get endgame-tier Gifts to focus on it.

                                                          Kiss of Helios
                                                          Heavy nerf, since it doesn't do extra agg anymore (only a small amount of superficial to people who touch you). It does still protect you from heat and fire, but not nearly as well.

                                                          Luna's Armor
                                                          Only of use against silver or fire, rather than granting broad protection that includes special cases against silver, so it's kind of like a kneecapped version of itself combined with a kneecapped version of Master of Fire. As usual, consistency is not to be found and balance is a joke.

                                                          Silver Claws
                                                          A nerf so pointless that it cannot be properly expressed in writing, this does one agg to you per turn, and it takes a roll to fight through the pain each turn to keep it active instead of, you know, providing Rage. I don't really get why this is end-tier when Unstoppable Warrior exists, but I guess that's certainly never something that we're going to see. I'm not sure that I can ascribe Vampire main writers being mad about it, either, since it has jack all to do with threatening anyone but other Garou and most Fera. That's why it was a step up in legacy, but not the end of the line.
