        Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post

        And this is why the "Eh, can't save the world" mentality doesn't work. Because it is presenting real life issues and problems and then asking the player characters how they can solve them as chosen of Gaia.

        So when the faction meant to be bad is the only one taking action? It says action is not the way to go.
        It feeds into a larger theme I believe I've discussed before: W5 is plainly written from the perspective of a Neo-Liberal Centrist who has already given up.

        It's not that the world has actually ended yet. It's that the ecological (and societal) collapse is drawing ever closer. And the Neo-Liberal mindset cannot conceive of actually fixing or arresting this process. At least, not while maintaining the Status Quo.

        It's Capitalist Realism, baked into the fabric of an edition of Werewolf. It's easier to imagine the end of the world than the end of Capitalism. Which is why W5 takes space out of its book to defend Capitalism and absolve it of systemic responsibility for causing most of our problems. To a Neo-Liberal Centrist, forcing Capitalism to change or even die to save the world is unthinkable. And anyone who thinks it's a good idea is either a child who needs to be paternalistically dissuaded from "rash action", or is a villain indistinguishable from the Fascists marching in the streets.

        Of course, Neo-Liberal Centrists often have a very impoverished understanding of what Fascism is, why people are radicalized into it, and how it differs from leftwing praxis. To a Centrist, "both sides are the same". Hence, why Hauglosk is a blunt system for tracking one's "corruption from wanting action", applied equally to the Cult of Fenris (who are all but stated to be literal Nazis) and any Garou who want to take the fight to the Wyrm "too much".

        Earlier editions of WtA asked "When Do You Rage?", because it took for granted that, in the face of systems trying to kill the world and everyone in it, Rage is not merely acceptable, but the only RATIONAL response. It was merely a question of how far you need to be pushed to act.

        W5 answers "When Do You Rage?" with a resounding "Never, Because Rage Is Always Bad".

        An attitude that is not merely unhelpful in an age when the Status Quo freely uses all types of violence to protect itself (physical, economic, legal, etc), but deeply unsatisfying as the premise for a game.

        Magestuck: The Ascension

            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

            Agreed.

            Because the game absolutely isn't about giving up and giving up is viewed as a mental illness (neuraotypical here). Depression and defeatism is as bad as fanaticism.

            It's just acknowledging the Garou can't fix things with talons and can't do it alone.

            HUMANITY (us because this is the real world) might save the world but the Garou have to reorientate their thinking. They have to basically do what they can do and have faith in humanity that it will do its own part in all this.
            Thematically, I get that but in terms of game-design this is terrible. W5 still asks players to play Garou. So if Garou have no impact on the larger setting because it's actually up to humanity to save the world then that means you're asking players to play characters with no agency. And if the PCs have no agency then you don't have a story. That's the issue: What story are you even supposed to tell with Garou-PCs in W5? A Pack of Garou-PCs kills a bunch of Fomori, saves the day but as ST following the guidelines of the W5 corebook, you step in and remind everybody that this hasn't changed a thing ("Anyway, the world is still doomed, Gaia's still dying unless humanity can get its shit together and you Garou-PCs beating the crap out of some Fomori has nothing to do with that... Anybody excited for the next adventure?"). That's not a game.

            And W5 is absolutely about giving up - by omission. The path to make a difference is not in the book. Even mechanically Harano and Hauglosk are inevitable for every Garou-PC. That's another way you could refocus W5: By just admitting that playing a Garou means playing a concept that's all about "doomed to failure" and similar to a PC in a Cthulhu-game there are only various types of bad endings waiting for the PC. The game isn't focused on that, though, but it's partially already there in how punishing Harano and Hauglosk are and in how pessimism dominates every description of Garou in the book.

            Also, refocusing on that type of horror in this case seems a bit silly to me. This is a game where you're playing werewolves. Your character can shapeshift into a super-powerful monster (aka Crinos) and use supernatural spirit-powers (Gifts). I don't think the horror should be focused on futility in that context (because then why make it a game about werewolves in the first place...?). And the tagline is "When will you rage?", anyway. That isn't a tagline for a game where it's all about "acknowledging that Garou can't fix things".

            If W5 strived to be radically different from previous editions, it failed... but it also failed in honoring what previous editions were going for. W5 is in that awkward place where it ended up doing neither and ended up being unfocused because of that. When it comes to W5's future, the game has to pick a lane: "When will you rage?" doesn't work for a game where Garou can't fix anything and the relentless horror of the Age of Apocalypse, Harano and Hauglosk doesn't work with a game of chasing Renown, beating the crap out of your enemies and honoring Tribal Patron-Spirits.

                                                Now, to be fair you can have fun (and cathartic) roleplaying experiences with a nihilistic "nothing your characters do ultimately matters because a bomb is going to go off and blow up the planet at the end of the campaign," setup for RPGs, but it's the kind of thing that the players and the ST need to walk into with eyes wide open and fully understanding what the theme and the mood of the game is about. Now though, the game starts with the premise that the world has already died, it's just that most people don't realize it yet.

                                                To make a comparison to a Noir movie, imagine a detective movie set with the premise that there's a huge asteroid getting ready to smash into the Earth. The movie follows the detective as he solves some crime, arrests someone and then in the closing seconds of the movie, the asteroid slams into the planet and everyone dies. That might seem like it would be a pretty absurd movie, and it is, but that's essentially what W5 is doing - the world is ready to end, neither the PCs nor anyone else can do anything to stop it, so run some games during the final gasps of Gaia's death throes.

                                                In W5, there is no meaningful "cosmic battle between good and evil," the battle is already over and good has lost. Again, that's a huge thematic change from earlier editions and it's not something that everyone is going to be interested in or enjoy.
