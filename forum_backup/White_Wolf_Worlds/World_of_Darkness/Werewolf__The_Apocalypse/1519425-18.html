                            Originally posted by Manfr View Post


                            To be fair, the book is not written like this.

                            There is a fairly relevant chapter about the Umbra, and llot hooks about the Umbra are costantly underlined and brought about. I would say most of the plot hooks presented in the book involve spirits.

                            The marked difference is in a reboot of the Umbra concept and in a lore element

                            1. Reboot- The Umbra doesn't have a map or a geography, it's a shadow of our world but it's marked by different events, so it is constantly changing; you are not traveling in different Umbral realms with a map, you are reaching a place of spiritual importance and enter the Umbra to deal with the relevant spirits

                            2. Lore- The Umbra is suffering at human hands, so it's strengthtening the Gauntlet to try separate itself from the ever growing power of the Wyrm in the real world
                            This is a locking off of the potential for the Weaver to be the party at fault and the villain. This takes something very important away from the setting, and positions the Wyld as less troubled. This has decreased the possibilities for conflict.

                            Also, the Umbra being indistinct just doesn't work out well. If it's really the soul of the world, it ought to be a bit more substantive, especially since its well-being in the prior lore kept the physical world from crumbling. Spirit was viscerally important because without it, Matter could not last.

                            the Garou Nation is not something distinct from the players, and it's not even depicted particularly bad, it's a preApocalypse alliance which was shattered by continued defeats and the walkouts of Fenris and Stargazers, and whose laws (the Litany) were abused by ingenious leaders.

                            The setup is much closer, I'd say, to a Year Zero game (the Fria Ligan post apocaliptic signature game) , where you defend and reclaim your territory, and clear the Umbra.

                            BSDs are presented as nihilists, not just corrupted monsters, and Cult of Fenris as a cautionary lesson in militant factionalism and extremism.
                            The last bit also does not make fucking sense, and we all know that it's just a thin wallpaper over Karim's desire to make the Swords of Heimdall the dominant portion of the Get. Somehow. In spite of being very, very dead and generally as capable, it seems, as the Neo-Nazis from Black Lagoon. After all, we didn't hear many casualties when the Get fell on them like a hammer. Well, not for the main body of Fenrir; just the Swords, at near 100%. And afterword, if anyone had any sympathies for the fallen, they made sure to keep their damned shameful heads down and shut the fuck up.

                            De-platforming - it works!

                            Also, the BSDs are never worth listening to. They don't have any greater depth, and it doesn't serve the narrative to try and make them look deep and meaningful. They've decided that the world is already gone, and so, to them, it is, along with everything that might be worth preserving. This is not the truth, but they believe it. Looking at the self-loathing of the Wyrm, they became infected by it, and are more tools for it to blindly lash out at the world to try and free itself from a prison it can no longer comprehend.

                            Wyrm, Weaver and Wyld are not vague, they are cosmic forces, but the influence and presence of their spirits is the moving force of the game.
                            And somehow their influence isn't something that can be read or sensed in the slightest? Not even one track from where the Weaver bound everything too tightly, or where the Defiler squirmed through more easily for it?

                            By the way, "street level" doesn't really mean "everything is fucked, you as well, just hide to survive".
                            In this game, it does, because the street is a dangerous place, and being de-powered and having capability removed makes the protagonists significantly more vulnerable. You absolutely need that cool head in Crinos to not be a temporary thing, because this is a game about a war, and wars require going the distance with your armor on, because the consequences of taking it off are way worse. Just another way trying to match it on Vampire systems is doing it a grave disservice. Vampire is holding it back from what it could be, and here, it's a particularly strong and visible binding.

                            It is presented as " Build your legend fighting for your territory". You are not gonna charge in the maws of the Wyrm, but you are gonna do the very same things you were doing in earlier editions, you are just assumed to do them in your hunting ground, instead of globetrotting around the Amazons.

                            (And still, the game says you can go globetrotting around if you want)
                            Who the hell is going to remember your legend? Who, in a setting where the talekeepers aparently don't bother to remember anything? Your legend will be washed away by some inept Galliard's weekly consumption of Natty Light or Schlitz. There are no ancestors to contact, and no spirits with a sworn pact to recognize your achievements. When they took the Nation away, Renown stopped being a sensible Trait to keep. As something external, it has no holder in this setting. Keeping a measure of spiritual connection would have made way more sense, because it existed internally for all the Garou and Fera.

                            But no. Renown, somehow. Probably only because of Forsaken. None of these mechanical bits is really original; they're just adapted from Paradox, from Humanity, from every game that's about as far thematically from W:tA as possible.
