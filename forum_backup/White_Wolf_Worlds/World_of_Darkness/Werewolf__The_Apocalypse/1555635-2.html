    I've suggested more shared background elements simply as one possible way to help deepen the "why" of the group; I've also said going more Suicide Squad style is another option too (though even in the Suicide Squad stories, there's generally an idea that the more regular members do develop emotional bonds over their missions; they're not just mindlessly following orders until they do enough missions or finally bite it). The problem is that these are shallow character ideas... to the point where some people feel like these aren't fully characters yet.

    Whatever ideas I might have, I have no clue if you actually care. You haven't responded to any of the vaguer things I've posted with "hey, could you go deeper on that?" or even, "that's the exact opposite of what I want to do here," so I have an idea of what would help.

    This is your First Team. You haven't given us a context for why you're making them. Is this for a private game you're working on? Trying to build up stuff for a fan supplement to publish in the STV? Just something you're making to practice your antagonist crafting skills? Going back to a word I've used a lot, there's also their narrative purpose. Is this a just a squad that the actual PCs will probably kill in one session without ever learning about them (in which case, yeah... a bunch of our responses are too much detail but I'd then say there's too much detail broadly for some throwaway mooks)? Is this for a Wyrm game where this is the "B squad" to the PC's "A squad" so they'll show up a lot in "downtime" scenes and possibly big missions so the PCs might want to get to know them? Are you aiming to have them be more sympathetic baddies in a standard game where you want to make sure that the players get to reflect on their humanity even as their characters might not initially care about the carnage they wreck on a bunch of people dealt shit hands only to die pointless deaths?

    You're going to get as specific responses as you frame your ask for feedback. If you want more from us that's really going to mean something, you need to give us more to work with.

                I see here a lot of the difficulties we had with your Tzimisce.

                You're getting better at making character sheets, but you're still struggling with the concept of making characters, for a story. You think of their backstory but you don't seem to understand it's actually important, or to understand their role in the story beyond the stats.

                As far as derangements go, you have to conceptualize just how intense they are. They're about lack of control. They're about engaging a behavior even when it is completely against your best interest, and about not even wanting to not engage them when this is not the case. They're not about a functional person having personality issues, they're about a person not being functional. And here this is your main problem as far as stats go, their stats are otherwise ok.

                But why do they work together as characters? What would they do and why in a movie or book, where you don't need or have stats at all? If you want nameless mooks, they have maybe more background than they need. But if you want villains that will have more spotlight time and relevant interactions, then you have to think what they are when stripped of stats, that's not just a detail, that's the most important aspect of a villain, as their interactions, motivations and relationships will usually come into play way before the main confrontation.

                Watch an actual live play at youtube or twitch and you isn't likely to see any of a villain stats nor most of their rules-defined actions. While there are things those live plays don't really do like in a common game at our houses, this definitely is an aspect of the game. It is about the story, not just the stats, and stats alone don't tell much and don't make good villains, it's only one aspect of the game.

                        OK, so if the broader context is you practicing your skills... that's all the more important for you to push yourself to come up with stuff rather than us fill in ideas for you.

                        My personal suggestion would be to take a mental break from thinking about these specific characters or even the WoD itself, and just look at media about horrible people that you find compelling; and examples you don't find compelling too. I know people have mixed feelings about the value of TVTropes broadly, but the Villain Protagonist page is a giant list of things to look at in this regard because anything that qualifies means the badguy(s) have to be interesting enough to be the center of attention for the story. Even if it can be hard to narrow down a bit to works that focus on a group instead of individuals, it can be really useful to get a sense of both what you personally like in villains as characters, and even things that aren't your specific taste but you can still see why they make interesting characters.

                        My personal preference is to look at shorter works even if you're thinking about characters that might last a long time in a TTRPG game. If you can articulate what makes Mr. Pink from Reservoir Dogs an interesting character (or why you think he isn't) despite the fact that we know so little about him, you're getting down to the minimalist core of getting a concept into a character effectively to an audience.

                                    I didn't ask you to make new characters, if you're engaging in character making as an exercise that is going to happen on its own.

                                    I asked you to set the current characters aside to engage with some study of existing media to help identify character qualities that help establish more compelling antagonists.                                     And you stated a unifying goal, yes, but you haven't integrated it into the characters in any meaningful way.
