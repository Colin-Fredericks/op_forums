    My perspective on the Garou and Kinfolk is that there's a bit more give and take other than the latter being the slaves of the former. It's an uneven hierarchical arrangement but it's not quite the pyramidal relationship that vampires have with their Retainers (who are slaves, no beating around the bush). In simple terms, Kinfolk are the families of Garou and very often are the people who have raised and taught the Garou in question before they reach maturity.

    I'd also argue that the vast majority of decisions regarding Kinfolk marriages, raising children, and the more "culty" elements of their society are probably administered by the Kinfolk themselves. It is going to be the rare werewolf who has time to sit down and rule over the Kinfolk of their tribes and their lives with the majority of things handled by trusted members of their society. The Garou, by and large, are too busy fighting the Wyrm and plotting military strategy to handle the majority of logistics that fund as well as supply their war.

    I'd argue that a closer depiction of how Garou and Kinfolk interact would be Dynasts and the Dragonblooded. The Dragonblooded are the elite of their society, have superpowers, and are treated as demigod. The Dynasts, however, are still the nobility of the Empire. The Kinfolk are not "just" the beaten down members of Garou society but they are an elite compared to "regular" humans who they have a religion that tells them they are superior to.

    Because they are.

    It's also important to note that Dynasts and Dragonblooded have the same Venn Diagram overlap that Garou and Kinfolk do. Not all Dynasts are Dragonblooded but almost all Dragonblooded are Dynasts (accent on almost). Most Garou have grown up in the society of Kinfolk and have their friends, family, and worldview shaped by it.

    THE SILVER CROWN had Albrecht disgusted by the fact his father had his mother mate with another Garou in order to hopefully produce a new heir for Jacob Morningkill that inherited the Garou gene. However, his father tears Albrecht's view of the situation apart and says that his mother wanted to do it because she was disgusted by his exile. They viewed their role as a duty and Albrecht to be forsaking his.

                    I do think the Garou see each other as "people" and their kinfolk and the Flock as something less than that. But both kinfolk and other human beings (and wolves) aren't at the level of livestock. I think they are a blend of things in terms of concept and law for the Garou - they may be treated as livestock in terms of property damage and wergild, but in other ways they are full legal persons with explicit rights. Not as much as the Garou themselves have, but they still have some (within a range of course depending on tribe and sept). I think collectively kinfolk are a mix of livestock, pets, legal inferiors, and full equals to the Garou. It just depends on what context you are talking about.

                    If I had to pick one analogy for them, I think serfdom might be a good one. Serfs are dependent on their feudal lord and have additional duties and restricted rights compared to free peasants, but also had certain rights and were entitled to certain protection and justice.

                    I also don't see kinfolk as having less rights than ordinary humans. And ordinary humans too are supposed to be protected. In Renown Award examples, we're given examples for both protecting and not protecting helpless humans, and for having good or poor relations with nearby Kinfolk. The Garou term for mundane humanity is the Flock which I've always taken to have double meanings. On the one hand, wolves devour the sheep in a flock. On the other hand, it implies the Garou are the shepherds of the Flock.

                    My own model for Garou behavior and society isn't some kind of totalitarian version of Jonestown. It's the aristocratic warrior societies of Ancient Greece, the Ulster Cycle, Beowulf, and Russian Bylinas. There may be many objectionable things about Garou culture and values, but I and none of the people I have ever played with ever modeled them on the SS, NKVD, or Red Guards. If I'm looking for a flawed Garou with tragic flaws, my inspiration would be Agamemnon, not Lavrentiy Beria.

                    I also think it is important to repeat that this thread is about figuring out the guidelines or normal standard for Garou in terms of handling their Kinfolk and the demands that can be made from them. It is OK to mention outlying behavior or things that may be acceptable at fringe septs. But let's make clear that they are not typical. And outright criminal behavior should be mentioned as things that are not allowed (even though they may still happen, because Garou have their criminals as well, and not all criminals are brought to justice).

                    And the standards that we should be looking for are things that can used during play. Most players don't want to be the equivalent of gulag guards. They're seeking to emulate something like Lon Chaney, Jr. and Maria Ouspenskaya in the Wolf Man movies.

                    I think this thread has a real benefit to me and to others, so guys, let's not cause it to be closed. If you really want to talk about all the awful things the Garou can do, please start your own thread so if it is shut down, it doesn't affect this one.

                            I think that the majority of Garou authority over kinfolk is going to be of the soft variety, it is implied and/or cultural. You do what Garou want not because they will inflict violence on you if you don’t, but because that is just what is done. The Garou are Gaia’s chosen and you’re blessed enough to be in the position to serve them.

                            Anyway, that is what kinfolk might think who are raised in an insular world of kinfolk and Garou. But I wonder how many kinfolk and Garou have that kind of upbringing and how many grow up essentially in the modern secular human world. On one hand, I could see a group of kinfolk living in some kind of compound in the woods that have no electricity, no running water, no access to modern media, no cars, etc. Something like the Amish, maybe even more extreme because their culture might be even more hostile to the outside world than that of the Amish. On the other hand, I guess it’s possible to have kinfolk grow up completely immersed in mainstream modern human culture, going to public school, watching TV, playing sports, going on dates, and so on. My wife and I home school Our kids and, while we’re atheists, we know a number of other families who are part of the Christian fundamentalist homeschooling community and they are fairly often not familiar with aspects of culture that some people would think are universal and often they know about things or have ideas that many people are unfamiliar with.

                            I could see kinfolk falling into anywhere on that spectrum and the degree of isolation that they have from the mainstream of society is going to be tied to how normal they view obedience to werewolves.

                            In some ways, those kinfolk who are more connected to mainstream human society are potentially the most useful. A bunch of kinfolk living in a secret compound in the woods who are subsistence farmers and don’t know what a TV set is may be more obedient, but not as useful as a kinfolk who owns a law firm. Kinfolk with money and connections would be able to offer Garou access to things that most Garou would have a lot of difficulty getting for themselves, because it’s hard to be Gaia’s warrior and have a day job. But if your kinfolk cousin runs a law firm, chances are he won’t stand at attention quite as fast when you snap your fingers and he likely gained a lot of ideas about human rights from exposure to modern society. The law firm kinfolk would probably have to come from a more liberal Tribe that would accept their kin having that sort of moderate outlook and modern lifestyle.

                            Maybe ideally, Garou would want some limited degree of isolation from mainstream society, kind of like the fundamentalist home schoolers but who believe in Gaia instead.

                                    In terms of kinfolk communities themselves, there is a spectrum. We can use that spectrum to create a range of kinfolk under more control to less control by the Garou.

                                    Kinfolk communities that are severely isolated from the world. Completely adopt the Garou worldview, and even their open activities reflect it within the needs of the Veil. Visitors there immediately know "something is wrong." Emulates a lot of tropes in horror stories where the protagonists visit the place and immediately cross the threshold into horror as a result. Likely very rare in modern day, but could be present in very remote settings like northern Russia, Alaska, and Canada or isolated mountain communities that can be completely self sufficient. Best used in game to showcase antagonist factions or the more monstrous or alien aspects of Garou culture.

                                    Kinfolk communities mostly isolated from the world. Garou worldview incorporated into the community's culture, but an outside visitor could still believe there is nothing supernatural about the community. Many such communities hide as alternative fringe to the mainstream - hippie communes, survivalist homesteads, religious dissenters, cult compounds, etc. These communities emulate tropes from the folk horror genre. Movies and books like Wicker Man, Harvest Home, and Midsommar. Visitors to these communities know they aren't "normal", but accept them as mundane. It would require investigation to determine "something is wrong". These kinds of communities are probably a minority among kinfolk settlements, but represent a substantial amount of the "conservative" (or "radical" if more appropriate) view of each tribe's culture.

                                    Kinfolk communities divorced from the world. These communities appear normal at first glance, but divergences can be found if one looks close enough. Churches and schools have unusual texts that diverge from the mainstream, and there are many peculiar local celebrations and customs. If found, they can be disconcerting, but unless one spends much time there, they will simply go unnoticed. Kinfolk from these areas appear to fit in with the modern culture around them, but there is a conscious effort to hide what they "really" are. Many kinfolk communities make up this category, and it might be the one with the most plurality.

                                    Kinfolk communities integrated with the world. These communities are mixed kinfolk/human communities, and as a result make serious comprises with their Garou/Gaian worldview in order to co-exist safely with the outside world. Instead of controlling the dominant human institutions and propagating their views that way, they teach their values and lore in secret. Perhaps through "volunteer groups" and other private associations that can control membership. They may all belong to one church that is controlled by them as a cover. Many aspects of Garou society can either not be taught openly, or only taught once children reach a certain age in order to prevent inadvertent piercings of the Veil. Although the specifics of Garou culture are kept secret, these communities often reflect their values in ways amenable to the Flock. There are more of these types of Kinfolk communities in the modern world, and they are probably the default of most PCs.

                                    Kinfolk communities lost to the world. These communities are minority groups dominated by humans. Garou values are completely subverted by the dominant culture. They can only be taught as family secrets, although dedicated kinfolk families stay in touch with one another and support each other that way. Those that are in direct contact with the Garou can be expected to keep the faith. But families that are not in direct contact have a tendency to fall off the radar and become lost to the culture entirely. There is a paranoia among Garou and Kinfolk here about the Veil and losing kinfolk. Although the most "open" of all kinfolk communities, this means the Garou may be forced to use threats and intimidation to keep kinfolk within the fold. Probably the default community of must urban Garou and their kinfolk. Can also be found in rural kinfolk communities subsequently swallowed by suburbs.

                                    Lost kinfolk. These are not kinfolk communities at all, but kinfolk in human communities who have completely lost contact with the rest of the Garou Nation. Some kinfolk may still know of their heritage and cherish it, but fear losing the next generation. Other kinfolk know, but have rejected their heritage and seek complete absorbtion among humanity, often viewing their blood as "cursed" or "tainted" or otherwise undesirable. Other kinfolk have completely lost touch with their Garou roots. This is best used as background of Lost Cubs and Lunatics. This can also include families of Ronin that may know about werewolves, but are ignorant about Garou. Once rare, this now makes up a substantial part of the kinfolk population. More join every year, but many kinfolk family lines cease being kinfolk in future generations.

                                        I'll only mention sexual violence to say that I've recently learned that it is the policy in some LARP groups to include within their rules that the concepts of 'rape' and 'sexual violence' are completely foreign within their IC universe and that the concepts are entirely absent both in theory and in practice.

                                        I know that's not going to be done people's preferred solution but I personally admire the choice to make a firm stance to create a safe environment for their players.

                                        Anyway returning to the main subject of this thread, I agree with what many people have been saying that kinfolk culture exists on a spectrum but is more akin to the relationship ancient warrior societies had between the warrior-priest class and the laborer-artisan class. Not slavery or servitude, but cultures that resemble pre-industrial societies in many respects. Isolated, insular, and firmly hierarchical with leadership by an elite religious and military class.

                                        I also think that Black Fox's post above makes a good point about the different sorts of kinfolk communities that would likely exist on a spectrum from entirely isolated to a concealed subculture.

                                        It personally makes me imagine a situation where one can explore a variety of septs with distinct garou-kinfolk communities. From the most isolated territories of rural backwoods, to communes vaguely known to the outside world, to communities masquerading as members of a larger culture whether in the form of entire small towns and villages or in the case of neighborhood and suburb groups.

                                        I would imagine that the Garou Nation considers most of its kinfolk to live communally in secret of hidden communities alongside traditional high tribes with the truly isolated and the human-presenting kinfolk of tribes such as the Bone Gnawers, Red Talons, and Glass Walkers being the minority. Of kenning kinfolk this may even be accurate.

                                        However I also imagine that callow kinfolk vastly outnumber their kenning counterparts and that for every group of kenning kinfolk in a small town in rural America claimed by a few high tribes, there are five or ten more groups of callow kinfolk that are living largely ordinary lives either ignoring or ignorant of their ancestry.

                                        The growth of the modern world and the defection of kinfolk is perhaps the most significant existential threat to the Garou Nation alongside that of Industrialization, Environmental Hazard, The Mad Weaver, and The Mad Wyrm.
