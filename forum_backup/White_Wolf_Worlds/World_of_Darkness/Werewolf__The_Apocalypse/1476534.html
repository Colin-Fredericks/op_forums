    Why the struggle matters

    03-16-2022, 05:57 PM
    I've been in this fandom for years, long enough that certain mentalities have become familiar to me. In light of the 5th edition news, I thought I'd address why I think this mentality is missing the point of WtA.

    The typical idea goes like this; "Gaia is dead, there is no hope at all."

    Why this idea bothers me so much and why I feel it does not fit WtA, is that so much of WtA is about the struggle. Garou fight and argue and die to gain an inch of a better future. They might not succeed, but they must still try.

    It is this trying that makes the game for me. The desperate struggle against overwhelming odds is 90% of the game. At least for me. Not only does it reflect how it feels to be faced with IRL wrongs, but also it gives the garou pcs something to do. A clear motivation.

    Aside from the fact that if Gaia goes the universe does as well. If Gaia is dead and the fight is over, what exactly is the throughline of the game? If there is no longer a struggle, so much of the core WtA play no longer applies. The garou are no longer fighting against the odds, they lost and are now living on borrowed time until the universe keels over.

    This is actually a similar situation to if the garou had won. Loss of the overarching goal means suddenly there is nothing really for the garou to do.

    I'm guessing the idea is that despite Gaia dying, the garou would continue the war against the Wyrm. But that lessens the impact of Gaia dying in my mind.

    This is rambly, but I wanted to give my thoughts on this.

    My gallery.

              It might be an overreaction to the heroic high fantasy aspects of Revised. I thought many aspects of that era was more like a typical D&D campaign against a dark lord than the morally complex setting originally portrayed in 1e/early 2e.

              But if so, it's another piece of evidence of why I hate metaplot. The exact nature and current situation of the Garou's struggle is something best set by individual STs for their own chronicles. There's no problem if an individual ST wants to run a chronicle where the struggle seems over and the last survivors are killing time until the end. A "Wolf's Rain" style chronicle could be very fun. But it's not the only kind of story to tell. And chronicles where the Apocalypse might be decades or centuries away is just as vital to the game as ones where it seems imminent. All sorts of stories are fun. I always like the implied threat that PCs might be living in the end times, or the precursor to the end times, but it's not needed to make that explicitly so.

              Making a canonical decision invalidates what many STs are doing and unnecessarily divides fandom. Instead of telling us what to do, the game should be providing us tools to tell the stories we want, and illustrating options available to STs through local sourcebooks that can emphasize aspects of the game without telling us the entire world setting is like that.

              I personally find it silly when games tell us, "Yeah, Gehenna or the Apocalypse or whatever has already happened." The world can only end once. This idea that a major event like the Apocalypse has happened, but nobody seems to notice rings false to me. I always viewed that Gehenna/Apocalypse/Ascension/Doomsday/Endless Winter is an end of the world event that blatantly reveals the supernatural is real and forever changes Earth. How the Hellboy/BPRD comics approached this is more along the lines of how I envision the scenario playing out. The official WoD products have just been sad and pathetic in comparison.

                          I think a world where there is no hope doesn't make sense. If Gaia was dead, in a final sense, not dying/comatose/sleeping/sick, then this camp that sought to flee Gaia via the umbra to other planets/dimensions/whatever where right all along and nothing Garou did do mattered (being a dump on those who played 1st to revised Edition and well into the X20-era) and it doesn't make sense to start a new game in a 5th Edition - for it is hopeless.

                          Sure, there are games where that doesn't matter. I once played a game on the premise that every character would die. There were teelights in the middle of the game table in a dark room, and at least when the last one would go out, every character was dead. Still it was a fun game about moral with all the characters on a ship in a Titanic-like scenario.
                          But would it be material for chronicles and ongoing gaming - no, it wouldn't.

                          I think that line along the lines of "Gehenna/Apocalypse is over" turned up in the X20-era (especially that one Technocracy convent book revised never published), because authors and developers weren't sure on what the direction for the game lines was, back then when CCP had control of the property and the decision was made to allow OPP to produce more books than just X20 single volumes. And from all I see in V5th it is not post-gehenna. It is a meta-plot time-jump with a lot of changes to the Setting that are more or less accepted by the fans of the games up until the X20-era.

                          We got a similar meta-plot time-jump situation when BNS presented it's WtA-Setting. And I have now idea how well accepted these changes are in the LARP scene that uses MET. All chronicles I know in Germany don't use MET or the Setting by BNS.

                          At Paradox there is the possibility that the decision makers go two different ways. Make radical changes to rules and Setting, or make only those changes that are required by the V5th meta-plot (but tone them down), only make changes to the game mechanics and rules, but leave the Setting more or less untouched...
                          They could go a way, where the company developing W5 is allowed to make drastic changes but OPP might still be books based on X20-era materials and rules. But I doubt that. I haven't seen the release plan for OPP, but iirc they also started to make V5 supplementals and I don't see any vampire stuff for V20 anymore...

                          I fear, that W5 will be not to my liking and that W20 material will cease to appear.
                          Maybe I should pre-emptively switch to Steve Jackson's WtA for GURPS......


                          But, yeah, the struggle is necessary for WtA. And it is about topics that matter, not just survival.
                          It's Werewolf: the Apocalypse.
                          Not Werewolf: Apocalypse Now
                          Not Werewolf: Post-Apocalypse
