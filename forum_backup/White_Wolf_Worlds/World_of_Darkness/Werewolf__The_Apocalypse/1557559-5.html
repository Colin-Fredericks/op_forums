                        It's an extremely long-standing conceit in WtA that the Garou have the ability to communicate fully with normal wolves (though this is not immune to negatives like high Rage). While broadly the books acknowledge this is not the same as having a conversation with humans because of the significant differences in how wolves communicate.. the books are less clear in a way to represent this effectively. A lot of groups, and a lot of fiction writers for the game itself include stuff that's in some of the gaming books not just the novels, do it like a human conversation so that it can actually be role-played on some level, and then treat it as more of a "translated into spoken English so you don't have to read subtitles while actors speak a language nobody actually understands," situation.

                        Things like "do wolves have names at least internally to their packs?" is also kind of a difficult question to address because it really depends on what you want to consider a name, and our inability to understand wolf vocalizations since each pack has a unique set of them. There is ample evidence that wolves do develop unique calls to identify specific individuals as a recipient of a message; it's rather understandable to do when wolves frequently hunt spread out enough to not be able to use non-vocal communication and need to coordinate who's doing what to take down prey. Is that a form of name simply because it's a unique vocal identifier to a specific individual? Depends on your definitions. Do we have any idea if those calls have deeper meanings such as referencing physical features? Not in the slightest.

                        So you're kinda stuck if you want to have Garou interact with wolves. No option can objectively perfectly balance our best understanding of wolf communication and the needs of fictional supernatural beings that can "speak wolf."

                                Ultimately, to me, the issue just boils down to a matter of laziness. Because this is about worldbuilding. Sure, it's easier to go for the Disney-fied approach where everyone just has to pretend that whenever Garou interact with wolves, you're in a scene from the Lion King or something and all of a sudden animals are indistinguishable from humans in how they talk. But I think any werewolf-game will lose something essential about the duality of beast and human by doing this. And while with an animistic/spiritual setting like WtA I could get behind granting animals more sapience than in the real world, I think taking the shortcut of overly humanising wolves is too much of a drawback. And there's the issue that if you're fine with overly humanising interactions with wolves, overly humanising interactions with Spirits will quickly follow afterwards. If it's easier to just humanise wolves this way, why not also apply that to Spirits as well? At that point, you might as well turn the whole thing into a Marvel-movie-esque amusement-park-ride and everybody's just delivering sardonic quips left, right and centre.

                                And "it's a difficult issue to address" is what you have worldbuilding for. This werewolf-setup has created this specific issue in how something in the setting works. But when writers address this, you want to see their imagination at work, you want to see a creative answer to this issue. What you don't want is just for someone to shrug and proclaim "Uhm, what if... You know how we humans do this? It's just like that.".

                                I know it's tough to do this on the fly during a game when you're the ST but that's why it's so important for book-writers to create a framework (do worldbuilding) that will help STs deal with this issue. What doesn't help is for official releases to tell potential STs to treat wolves in their WtA-game like they're very human-like contacts that you can have discussions with and winning them over is as much a social persuasion challenge as it would be if it were a local photography-club.

                                    Bit of both?

                                    Forsaken combined Garou Tongue and the native language of spirits into First Tongue (removing the "speak with spirits Gift tax" and reinforcing the spiritual nature of werewolves in the game), which is labelled as the First Tongue.

                                    Originally posted by Knightingale View Post
                                    Ultimately, to me, the issue just boils down to a matter of laziness.
                                    Eh? I mean, unless you have something that would widely be agreed upon as a better solution to something that's going to be used in-play disliking the current state of things isn't really going to accomplish anything. W5 putting a bit less effort than some of the older WtA books to add more flavor and color to the texts when Garou communicate with wolves (or spirits for that matter) to highlight that it works differently without making it a giant translation exercise or require a degree in wolf behavioral science to do smoothly only address one book doing a particularly lazy job of it.

                                    I'm open to the idea that there's a better way of doing it, but I've tried a bunch and they all fall flat in their own ways. The idea that it's just a failure of world-building comes off as unthinking or uncharitable. The world-building isn't that wolves actually do communicate like humans among themselves and Garou can join in, the world-building is that Garou-wolf communicate is it's own unique thing. The problem is that world-building doesn't have an easy solution to use in play.

                                    Having put some serious thought into changing things to maintain easy of play and communication in the world-building, I haven't come up with something I think is inherently better in over 20 years of trying. The only thing you can really do is narrate with as much body language as possible to emphasize things like scents and tail position modifying meaning, without forcing your players to read between the lines so you still have to translate it to human comprehensible words. Some players get into it and start to learn how to be less "in translation" and more focused on how you (who as the ST probably isn't doing this 100% accurately either) try to make it less human. Other players just don't care about immersion on this topic.

                                    This is very analogous to the Aura issue in other WoD games where we have these aura charts where different colors and textures have different meanings. And that's great world-building and all, but most people don't use it because you have to consult/memorize a chart just to translate what your ST is saying into what your character would know instead of your ST just telling you what your character would know. People love the world-building of seeing auras and interpreting them... but it's just an utter pain to make fun in a game.

                                    But when writers address this, you want to see their imagination at work, you want to see a creative answer to this issue.
                                    So, what do you do when this is the best and most creative answer currently available?
