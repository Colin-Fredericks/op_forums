    Thoughts on the Savage Age Intro Bundle

    05-24-2025, 08:14 PM

    So I recently picked up the Savage Age Intro Bundle and thought it would be useful to write down my thoughts as I went through the books in the bundle. Including the fillable savage age character sheet, the bundle includes 5 pdfs, Accounting for the Dead, and Tribebooks: Celican, Khara and Naghaluu. I am going to start with Accounting for the Dead since I think it will give a better sense of the overall feel of the setting. It will probably take several posts to get through each book as there is lots to cover.

    Accounting for the Dead

    I like how the pages in this are laid out, the pages aren’t too busy. I find with some Werewolf the Apocalypse books the desire to have cool backgrounds on pages can actually make it difficult to read at times.

    Prologue: Caliah

    I like the first part about Fox declaring that they weren’t ready.

    I am not sure how I feel about the second part about the Mokole. First it seems confusing the way it talks about finding some of the genocided Fera, but I think it is referring to their spirits not actually members of those groups. Also it seems like something that would have earned them considerable punishment by a very powerful spirit, possibly even Gaia herself. After all it certainly seems to be implied by the collective write ups of the War of Rage that the Garou doomed everyone by wiping out the Apis and Grondr and eventually the Camazotz and nearly destroying the Gurahl. On the other hand, it is generally well written and isn’t necessarily true and I think Werewolf is generally a better setting when all the Fera have baggage and reasons for internal conflict.

    Welcome to the Savage Age


    I think this short part is very useful, far to many rpg books assume anyone reading them will have a bunch of knowledge about what the book is for and how to use it. I think it is worth a couple of pages to situate the book.

    The Khara

    I do like how there is a link to the tribebook if you had gotten Accounting for the Dead on its own. I like how their role is immediately tied to the role of the modern Baset and the immediate link to the ideas that knowledge is power, and power corrupts. I appreciate the editor’s note about what is overlap with their tribebook and what is not.

    I did find the mention of Braga-Khara and Dur-Khara confusing, I am sure their tribebook explains it, but this section could have used a very quick explanation. I think they are basically regional sub-tribes.

    This section does have a considerable number of gifts, twenty-three if I counted correctly. That is a lot of new options for a Khara PC which I think is really good.

    I am not sure what the point of being able to take both Scent of the True Form and Sense Moon is. The two gifts appear to be different versions of the same ability and Sense Moon along with another level 5 gift requires you to have the old Mokole book which many people will not have. Also clarification would be good, is the Khara version of Scent of the True Form still optimized for finding Garou? I went to check W20 Changing Breeds and the Mokole seem to have replaced Sense Moon with Scent of the True Form, though they call it Sight of the True Form; that book also does not clarify. If I was running a campaign I would make it clear that the Khara version is optimized for Khara/Baset not Garou.

    Personally I think it would be better when referencing gifts from other places to always make clear exactly what list the gift is drawn from saying Garou when it is a Philodox gift or Mokole when it is a specific Auspice is mildly confusing. And also needing the old Corax book for several gifts is not great, again I have access to that book, but many people newer to Werewolf won’t have it.

    I think some clarification on how Deny the Stalker and Whisper Catching interact would have been useful.

    I particularly like Accursed Secret, there is lots of interesting stuff both a player and storyteller could do with it. Are the Khara responsible for the Yava of all the Baset tribes?

    Overall I find the gifts section of the Khara part of the pdf to be good despite the concerns I identified here. They all seem to fit within a few roles that the Khara perform and allow for some very different Khara characters to be developed. It is clear that the writers did their research as they pulled gifts from four different sourcebooks, but I think using non-W20 books was not the best choice; without access to those books you lose around 1/5 of the options presented.

      Today I am looking at the next two sections of Accounting for the Dead, the Apis and the Grondr, both Fera that have a decent amount of information in W20 Changing Breeds.

      The Apis

      The expanded Apis background is very interesting, I like how they in many ways show how the Triat is supposed to work. Their ability to shift from one animal kin to another is interesting and I learned about some very odd prehistoric creatures in the process. It does raise questions of how they weren’t able to help other Fera make the same transition, which could potentially be some interesting plot hooks. Same with the idea of the rare mesonyx throwback birth, lots of things that could be used for.

      The different regional groups of Apis is another good addition even if there isn’t a ton of detail. I would have liked it if there was some indication of stat changes for non-aurox Apis. The modern nights ideas are interesting, including the idea of how a limited number of them could have survived, that page feels like it could have had more however or the previous part continued. There was more than half a page of extra space before getting into the more rules heavy stuff.

      The Apis gifts section is extensive with 20 general gifts that any Apis can take, and all of them are new. Some of them seem pretty powerful, though in line with the type of stuff Apis should have gifts for. There are another 15 Auspice gifts, 1 for each level for each of the 3 Auspices the Apis have. Again all of these are new. While I haven’t looked at Tribebook Khara yet which may be entirely new gifts, it feels like a lot more work went into the Apis section of this book.

      I have far less concerns with this section than the Khara equivalent. A few gifts are oddly phrased and some of them seem perhaps too powerful. While not a combat ability, Watch the Herd seems really, really good, even for a 4th level gift. 1 gnosis for full sensory sharing with permanent gnosis number of people for a month seems too much. Muddy the Pool should probably also have a resource cost to use it or require the Apis to be touching the creature or something. Stamp of Fury is also very powerful and doesn’t actually say how long it lasts for, I assume it is a scene or until you have used all your successes.

      Overall however the gifts section of the Apis portion of the book is excellent, lots of the gifts could easily generate plot hooks and with so many Apis unique gifts you can make characters that really feel different from others. Manifest the Chimerical Legacy is super cool, but use it in front of the wrong character and you could easily get declared to be some kind of tainted monster.

      I am glad to see that thought was given to Apis rites, however the one non-minor rite can only be used annually at a specific time which doesn’t seem that useful. It feels more like something that adds cool flavour to a scene players witness if they visited an Apis Fold at the appropriate time of year. While probably more of a background thing, description of a rite about shifting an Apis’s breed to new animals could have been really cool.

      Overall I like the Apis section, it is definitely better than the Khara section. I don’t think the Khara section combined with W20 Changing Breeds gives enough information to have them as PCs in a campaign but the Apis I feel like I could.

      The Grondr

      I like the expanded Grondr lore, but it feels like it is very broad but not deep enough. Lots of stuff is mentioned but not covered in enough detail to be super useful. Like they tend to be solitary or in pairs, yet also sounders; but we don’t really get an idea of how that works. I do really like the information about how the Grondr are some of the most likely Fera to fall to the Wyrm because of the filth they are constantly fighting. The bit about most of them preferring Neanderthals over Homo Sapiens is interesting and may help feed into their eventual extinction.

      The section on relations with the other Fera is good, it gives a clear picture of how they fit into the wider group. And I like that it isn’t the same type of relationship with each of the other Fera.

      Adding in some different tribal/regional groups is interesting, but there isn’t a ton of detail.

      I like some aspects of the War of Rage section, but other parts feel odd. The write-up suggests the war started around the same time that humans showed up on the scene, but that would mean that the war between the Garou and the Grondr lasted for tens of thousands of years which seems silly. And the whole, “then whoever was left fell to the Wyrm en mass and become skullpigs” seems very handwaved and simplified. Earlier it was suggested that the Grondr were already more likely to fall to the Wyrm, were those also skull pigs? Were some of those earlier fallen responsible for dooming the last uncorrupted members of their kind?

      Given what we know about the Siberakh it seems odd to see them mentioned here. Actually it feels odd to have any divisions of the Garou mentioned, since there is little context so far about any Garou organization in the Savage Age.

      The ideas presented in the modern nights section are interesting, but from a formatting point of view, the last page leaves a lot of empty space. That space could have been very useful in some parts of the Grondr write-up expanding on something.

      There are 26 Grondr gifts listed, all of them are new; it really feels at this point like the Khara got the short end of the stick here. The lack of anything mechanically separating any of the tribes feels odd, as does no expansion to have breed specific gifts. I think it would have been more interesting to have most of the gifts be tribe and breed specific; since other than starting rage and a few specializations, there is little mechanical difference in options between any type of Grondr.

      That being said, I really like most of the gifts, lots of them are not combat oriented and add interesting options. There is a typo with Speak, referencing Apis instead of Grondr, clearly it was originally an Apis gift it is unclear if it is supposed to be a Grondr one or if it was put in the wrong place. Petrifying Countenance is the one I am concerned might be overpowered, yes it has a high resource cost, but it instantly kills the target.

      I like the two rites, both feel logical though they are complicated and wordy. Given that the Grondr only have 1 existing rite, I would have liked a couple more. Earlier in the write-up extensive greeting rites to ensure the other Grondr aren’t tainted are mentioned, that could have been a really cool and flavourful low level rite. There is probably space on the last rites page for another short rite.

      Overall I like the Grondr section, perhaps not quite as much as the Apis section, but I feel like I have enough information combined with W20 Changing Breeds to use the Grondr in a Savage Age campaign.

          The Anupu-Ba-El

          The name is a mouthful.

          The initial fiction piece is good, it gives a good indication of the mindset of an average Anupu.

          The lore write up is interesting but it feels off for the Garou to work with them. If anything I would think that the Garou would dislike a Fera so close to them even more. Anupu NPCs could be very useful for planting plot hooks or giving the party information. A more umbral focused campaign would definitely benefit from having an Anupu character. Mentioning the Kucha Ekundu here is interesting, the modern Garou don’t know very much about them and all the sources I have read suggest that their numbers are small and have always been so. I would have thought they were a more recent thing than the time period Savage Age covers.

          The section never directly says how they operate but it is strongly implied that they mostly operate independently.

          The relations with other Fera section feels a bit too uniform in opinion. Fera frequently fail to understand the role of other types of Fera and judge them. It would have been good to see at least one Fera mentioned that the Anupu don’t really get along with. The Khara seem like an easy option, from their point of view the Anupu could be getting secrets the Khara would rather stay hidden from spirits of the dead.

          The Ohk-Rahm are interesting, I hope they will get more detail at some point.

          Their War of Rage section is interesting. The information about the Garou Theurges is very interesting. It would be cool to see a set of gifts for an “old school Theurge” with those hedge magics. I guess you could use some of the various hedge magic rules, but those don’t always interact well with the rest of the Werewolf system.

          Again some kind of information on how they shifted from jackal to wolves would be interesting, being able to shift your animal kin seems to definitely be more of a thing in Savage Age and some information about it would be cool, it seems like an interesting thread to build a campaign off of. I wish the modern nights section mentioned how the Ohk-Rahm could have survived.

          The Anupu gifts section has 25 general gifts half new, half taken from existing sources, mostly Garou but a few Nuwisha and one Kitsune. Re-using Garou gifts makes more sense for these guys than other Fera I think. Again the Khara seem to have gotten a raw deal. Pathfinder’s Cord is a really cool ability that several other Fera would love to know, but it’s rules are are unclear, I assume you have to spend 1 gnosis; but it would also be good to know how long it can last for, if some Anupu went into the Deep Umbra and was gone for weeks would one use last the whole time?

          Getting access to multiple rank 3 gifts at rank 1 seems very powerful even if they aren’t combat related. Space is repeatedly used to either list Silent Strider gifts or even list out specifics of the gift only to note it is identical to the Silent Strider gift, this seems like a waste of space that could better be used for something else. Gate of the Moon I am willing to give a pass because the Anupu can use it in ways the Silent Striders can’t.

          There are also 4 lupus breed gifts, half new, half re-used. Both the re-used abilities are gained at lower levels than the original source which I am not keen on.

          Overall I like the Anupu gifts, they seem very focused in a specific direction, so if you were running campaign that didn’t heavily involve spirits and the umbra an Anupu won’t have much unique to bring to the table. I would have liked to see at least one clearly combat gift. I guess part of my worry is with the set of gifts they have, their usefulness will be very swingy, if the session is focused on umbra and spirit stuff or you need to get a message somewhere quickly, this character will probably completely outshine the rest of the party. But without those things, they aren’t terribly useful.

          I like both the Anupu rites, Descent into the Dusk especially offers some really cool story ideas especially if someone is using sentients to power the rite.

          The two fetishes are interesting, but again a page less than half full, this easily could have had another two fetishes or another rite. It feels odd that the Anupu get two fetishes when it makes more sense for them to use Garou fetishes yet the Apis and Grondr don’t get any and their W20 entries don’t have any unique fetishes either. Neanderthals

          I do really like the inclusion of Neanderthals as separate from Homo Sapiens, I think that can add some really interesting and unique dynamics to a Savage Age game. I appreciate the quick rundown of who Neanderthals were, most people have nothing beyond the most basic understanding of how they relate to Homo Sapiens. All the various sections of this part are useful and the bits about Neanderthals and the Umbra help make them different from Homo Sapiens in a campaign.

          This section also includes 9 new gifts that Neaderthal breed Fera can take. All are interesting and several requiring cannibalistic activity create interesting potential story ideas as not everyone will be cool with such activity. I do wish they clarified the exact difficulty of the check with all the gifts.

          There are also 5 new fetishes and 3 new talens. Most of these are interesting, but adding a sex fetish doesn’t seem super useful and may encourage some players in directions that not everyone is comfortable with in an rpg session.

          Playing in the Savage Age

          Organizationally I feel like this section should have been the first part of the book, details of various Fera in the Savage Age are super cool but this section is the meat of the book. Reminding people of key aspects of this era is very helpful for situating how this Savage Age is different. Before reaching this section I didn’t feel super confident running anything in Savage Age. I probably still only really feel comfortable running a campaign focused around either the Apis or Grondr with NPCs from other Fera, I don’t think there is enough information in this book to run a Garou or other Fera game without significant work as the storyteller. Similarly I don’t think there is enough about the Khara from W20 Changing breeds and this book to use them as the focus and the Anupu feel too focused to run a campaign centered around them.

          Building a Character in the Savage Age

          Short but important and useful section. Formatting wise it seems odd to have it be 1.2 pages, that extra space could have been used for something, maybe a few “Quick Build” suggestions for attribute and ability spreads.

          Savage Age Animals

          Super useful short section to have. A few odd formatting things in this section and the last page had space for another 2 or 3 animals.

          Overall thoughts

          Despite what it may seem, I really like this book. My comments about various things that are odd or could use more information are from my want for the book to be great rather than just good. I think the Apis and Grondr parts are the best and most useful of the Fera sections and would be useful for any Apis or Grondr based campaign. Without having looked at the Khara tribebook (which is up next) I don’t think there is enough information to use them as the focus of a campaign between this and W20 Changing Breeds. And the Anupu feel like a Fera that using more than one of as a PC in a campaign would be difficult. I wish this book had even like 1 page detailing the Garou briefly in this era, lots of stuff is suggested and hinted at in various places but it would take a lot of work by a storyteller to run a Garou focused game using only this book to get the Savage Age stuff from.

          I should also note that the artwork in the book is consistently good.

              Tribebook: Khara

              Who doesn’t love the idea of Were-sabrecats/tigers? I feel like if you don’t I wouldn’t want to game with you.

              Prologue: Caliah

              This immediately starts off with calling the storyteller both a Qualmi and a Khara which is odd. The short stories are good and really help establish the character of the Khara immediately. Before getting into anything else we already have a good feel for them and how several other Fera see them.

              Chapter 1: Tribal Background

              The information presented here is good, like their distrust of writing things down in case someone else found it. And their nature making it difficult for even other Baset to want to work with them. Nearly everyone can understand how a smug, I know better than you type will cause problems. Formatting wise, this is not a large book, having a page that is ⅔ rds empty is less than ideal.

              Chapter 2: On the Nature of Secrets

              Like I said in my thoughts on the Khara portion of Accounting for the Dead, I really like how they are leaning into the Knowledge is Power and Power Corrupts ideas. I like that there is an entire chapter diving into how this task of finding and keeping secrets, especially powerful or dangerous ones impacted them and how they operated. There is lots of stuff that can be useful here in building interesting and layered characters.

              The idea of Khara conclaves is great. Such an event could be a really cool part of campaign, perhaps even one that doesn’t centre around the Khara, for there are many Fera that would love to eavesdrop on such an event.

              The idea that the Khara helped somewhat marginalize vampires is super cool, but how big a threat are vampires in the era of the Savage Age? Being a vampire in this era would be really difficult considering how few people there are and how dangerous travel would be for them.

              I still don’t really like the idea that the Mokole have genocided an unknown number of different Fera species over the ages. It feels like something that would have earned them the ire of powerful spirits, including possibly Gaia herself. Even if the “modern” Mokole have moved beyond the idea, their ancient sins should be a problem for them.

              The details of how the Khara dealt with secrets is useful and the potential for a secret the Khara thought safely buried away could be useful in a campaign, even one set in modern times.

              There is a weird formatting thing where the On killing secrets section starts on a page with other information, but the On language and painting section starts on a new page leaving another half empty page.

              The on language section is very interesting and the Khara’s inability to control a secret from spreading being their downfall is a very cool idea.

              The idea that some percentage of Caerns feed on the energy of powerful secrets is cool, and could be fodder for a plotline or two. A powerful enemy attacking a Caern to get to the secret buried deep beneath it, or someone other than the Garou building on the site to harness the energy…

              The last bit on the death of the Khara species is interesting, particularly the Balam-Naghaluu wars.

              I am slightly confused about why chapters 1 and 2 aren’t a single chapter. The stuff covered by chapter 2 isn’t purely about secret management.

              Chapter 3: Who They Were

              I found the information in this section good, but wanted more about how they interacted with their kinfolk. The idea that they preferred animal kin and breeds over Neanderthal and Homo Sapiens makes sense. The relations with other Baset and Fera discussed here make sense. Also I feel like there should be mention somewhere of the circumstances that could lead to even a temporary pack of Khara assembling; surely some secrets were too big, too dangerous for a single Khara to suppress.

              Chapter 4: Breath of Life

              Now we get into the meat of the book, all the basic information including the Yava is good. The expansion of the secrets and Den-realm backgrounds both make sense for the Khara.

              There are 18 gifts listed for the Khara, a mix of 7 re-used ones and the other 11 new. Interestingly the two starting gifts listed are not part of this 18 so it is really 20 gifts. Not super keen on having to track down where the re-used gifts are but it is clear from Accounting for the Dead that the writers realized that and added references in later books. One thing I would like clarified is if Khara can take general Baset gifts?

              Lots of interesting Gifts here, though the write up for Seline’s Mercy is odd, the gift it mirrors directly contradicts the flavour text. There is a good mix of combat and non-combat gifts. I worry that Piercing Bite is overpowered for a rank 3 gift, completely ignoring soak gives a combat oriented Khara a decent chance to 1 shot many foes. I am also not keen on Khara getting a Corax rank 5 at rank 4.

              Combined with the 23 gifts in Accounting for the Dead, you have lots of options. My concerns about some of those gifts remains but it is a healthy selection.

              This chapter also has 5 rites, one of each level. All are interesting, but the two most powerful rites don’t seem like things the players would frequently or ever use.

              The 5 new fetishes listed are all interesting and useful in different ways. Several of the non-combat ones could get your players very paranoid. The Level 4 one especially could lead to some interesting story hooks. I assume it is their spirit that is bound within and breaking it would not result in a Khara suddenly showing up very angry at you if it broke.

              The two totems are cool and providing a way to use some of the gifts in a modern campaign is a good idea. A rival Garou pack devoted to Homotherium could be an interesting problem for players to have to deal with, especially if they uncovered something that should have been left forgotten in an earlier age…

              Ending this chapter are 4 general character concepts, a good idea that helps establish some of the types of Khara characters that could be out there.

              Appendix

              Having some info about sabre-toothed cats/tigers is good, and helps paint the picture of the type of environment the Khara operated in.

              The ideas for modern Khara offer some cool ideas. Some of which could work just as well for NPCs or storylines for the players to handle. The small pool of Khara kinfolk that very rarely get a true Khara show up could be a weird isolated community that the players stumble into, maybe the only Khara around is still very young, only months or a year after their first change.

              Overall Thoughts

              I think for the size and cost of the book, you get a decent amount assuming you have other Savage Age books. I think you really need the extra options provided by Accounting for the Dead to really make good use of the Khara. I think it would have been better for both products if the Khara section of Accounting for the Dead was part of this book, with an appropriate price adjustment, and that space used to expand the other parts of Accounting for the Dead. Without Accounting for the Dead I think you could only have 1 Khara in the party or use them as NPCs. If the Savage Age hadn’t continued I don’t know how useful this book would be beyond an NPC for a short story arc or to augment a Shattered Dreams game. Thankfully there is an entire line of Savage Age products so that isn’t an issue.

              One thing I wish that could have been explored but wasn’t is: What happens when too much forbidden knowledge and secrets break a Khara? We rarely get more than general hints at fallen members of various Fera and this would have been an interesting bit to add.

              Like with Accounting for the Dead the art is excellent.

                  Tribebook: Naghaluu


                  Even bigger and badder wolves for Fera to be connected to? What could go wrong with that idea? Also it is good to see that this book is 70 pages vs the 44 in Tribebook: Khara. This difference matters even more when you consider that every book loses a few pages to covers, tables of contents and such; in the case of Savage Age books that seems to be 5 pages. Nothing wrong with that especially since it includes two good, full page art pieces. But for actual content it means 65 pages vs 39 pages or a 60% increase. This probably also explains why there is no need for a Naghaluu section in Accounting for the dead.

                  Prologue: Caliah

                  Like with Tribebook: Khara the four short stories at the start of the book do a good job of establishing the general idea of the Naghaluu. And of course modern Garou just assume they were Garou not a different Fera.

                  Chapter 1: Tribal Background

                  The ideas about the Wyld being far more dangerous in the Savage Age are cool and make sense, but if they are so dangerous, who is policing them in the Old World if the Naghaluu are only in the New World? The idea that they could be Wyld corrupted is also interesting. The reasoning for passing on the name of the Wyld corrupted Apuray to the Camazotz is cool, but should have been explained more. If their own pack is hunting them then why do the Were-bats need to be involved?

                  The visions and prophecy interest is interesting as I would think truly powerful Wyld threats would largely confound such attempts to predict the future.

                  Their use of powers from all three of the Triat is an interesting idea. I think it helps reinforce the idea that every time a Fera is wiped out, it makes it harder and harder for the surviving ones to perform their Gaia appointed tasks. I remain frustrated when chapters end with nearly empty pages.

                  Naghaluu hating Ratkin makes sense, since the were-rats are wyld-mad or at least they are in modern times. Various sources have suggested that got worse by their exile after the War of Rage. I don’t think they are as far gone in this era.

                  This section also does a good job of tying into stuff mentioned in Tribebook: Khara and that the Garou wiping out the old world Khara has ripples across the world. And I like how it is isn’t just using Savage Age specific Fera, with various Baset, Ratkin and Mokole involved.

                  Detailing the War of Many Teeth is good, and could easily serve as the backdrop for a pure lands campaign, mostly simmering the background but causes shifts in the opinion and relations with various Fera NPCs before bursting into full force at the climax of the campaign, possibly even ending the campaign with the player characters getting to fight attack after attack by once allied Fera. The Ratkin starting the war makes complete sense. The war becoming uglier and uglier as time goes on also makes sense. There is enough detail here to use the war, but not so much that it would limit a storyteller in what they wanted to do. The final tidbit about the last known survivors fleeing to antarctica pursued by some ratkin also offers some story hooks, potentially even for campaigns set in modern times.

                  The brief bit about the Tengger, the forerunner to the 3 brothers of the Garou tribes is cool and I think the most information we have gotten so far in the Savage age about any proto-Garou groups other than the Anupu.

                  Chapter 2: On the Nature of Betrayal

                  The first part of this doesn’t seem to actually give much information for the amount of words used. Though the ideas of separate until a pack was needed and clarity of purpose to cut through the chaos of the Wyld are cool ideas. Their interactions with other Fera makes the inevitable War of Many Teeth more tragic. The idea of rites being important to their combat style is cool, I am interested to see how that works out later in the book

                  The bits about what various Fera remember about the Naghaluu is cool, but I don’t know how useful pages of what various Garou tribes know in modern times is. The Lodge of Nine Knives is a cool concept, more small groups of different Fera should exist that aren’t as big as the Asian or African groupings.

                      Chapter 3: Who They Were


                      Organizationally I would have preferred this earlier in the book, before On the Nature of Betrayal. This is the Naghaluu book after all, I should know about them before I learn about how other Fera deal with their loss.

                      That being said, this chapter has lots of useful info, distribution across the pure lands, being more lupus than homid. And I feel like some of the detail here is what was missing from the Khara book. We have details on how their packs operated, though I think in this case when the book says pack, it is more like sept in the Garou understanding of organization.                                           The Age of Lupus

In some ways more Fera being lupus or equivalent helps explain how the War of Rage happened, since they seem less worried about the future, or thinking through the second and third order effects of doing something, they are less likely to see the impact that losing entire Fera species will have.

With humans much more successful in specific parts of the world, primarily the fertile crescent, and the major river valleys of India and China, Fera will be congregated in and around those areas as well. With probably not enough space to go around, conflict becomes almost inevitable.

                                                  The Wlewa’s War of Rage

                                                  There are 20 gifts for the Wlewa, in addition to their breed gifts and general Bastet ones; 11 are re-used. Decisions Well Made seems super on point for the Wlewa and all of them having it makes sense. The mix of Theurge and Philodox gifts is logical and I like basically all the gifts available. This is one of the shortest lists of gifts for any Savage Age Fera though.

                                                  5 solid character concepts, I would still like some general ideas of how to distribute stats to help new players or make generating npcs faster.

                                                  Given how much is packed in this book this section feels a bit light.

                                                  Wlewa in the modern nights

                                                  The idea of the Wlewa and the Garou kind of ending up in cold war is cool and could be a good what if scenario. Maybe something to throw a Garou party into if they are questioning if the Garou nation was right in the War of Rage.

                                                  It could also be cool for a more spy themed campaign.

                                                  Appendixes

                                                  We get a quick rundown of the various other Bastet that can be found in any real number in the Hamaal. Lots of these could be cool options for a pure lands campaign. And we get Yava, form stats and details of what gift list they get in addition to breed and general Bastet so they are easily playable.

                                                  Then we get an appendix describing Eldritch Sorcery which is cool, I like how it is somewhat moldable. This could be a scary thing to use even in modern nights for some kind of NPC Fera Sorcerer.

                                                  The last appendix is some details on the various animal kin of the Fera in this book. As usual a useful addition.

                                                  Overall Thoughts

                                                  For its length there is a ton of material in this book. It is probably the most space efficient Savage Age book so far. I got to the end of Mantle of leadership without really looking at the page count and thought I must be about to hit the mechanics stuff, nope turned out there is an entire chapter on the War of Rage. And it is important, this book got into a level of specifics that previous Savage Age books seemed hesitant to do.

                                                  The Eldritch Sorcery stuff is great. The mechanical stuff that is here is good, but I wish there was a little more.

                                                  Every Savage Age book seems to make the whole better, building a fuller picture of the era. I think there is now a solid picture of how the War of Rage goes down, and even though it lasts generations there are different cool elements to use at various points in the war for a campaign. At the same time it seems to ebb and flow in intensity, so even if you are doing a game that is taking place during the War of Rage it might never become a major thing.

                                                      Ghost Lineages: the Advanced Players Guide to the Savage Age

                                                      Before getting into this one I should note that I got a free copy of this book.

                                                      This is a thick book, I was not expecting nearly 300 pages. I like given that it is that size, that we are getting some introductory stuff to start out. Going through this book is probably going to take a while since in addition to being like double the size of the previous largest Savage Age book I went through, I feel like there is going to be a lot of time spent looking at other relevant material, like sections of Accounting for the Dead, Changing Breeds, and Rise of the Garou.

                                                      A consolidated list of Gaian mandates is good. And we see some people who haven’t shown up in other books, including the good old Werehyenas now the Ajah-Abah so likely paired with some other hyena like creature from this era as well, also the Kor who I know nothing about though I know there is a Savage Age tribebook for them.

                                                      Next we get a timeline, I am very happy to see this. I like that they are willing to say theirs is better than the official one. This timeline is extensive, running a little over 18 pages.
