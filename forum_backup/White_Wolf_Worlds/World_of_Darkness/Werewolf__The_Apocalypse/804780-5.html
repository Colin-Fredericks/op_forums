The Age of Apocalypse has begun, and the game starts in the middle of it... is it the very end, or is it the darkest hour before the dawn? The first new Caern in 15 years has been created, and suddenly Garou have discovered that some Kinfolk can be turned into Garou too. Obviously there have been great metaphysical changes in the world, but you might expect that as the Apocalypse starts to crank it up to 11.

I don't think you could be a Santcum of Gaia and a Judge of Doom at the same time, because I don't think you can be part of two camps at once.

                                            So I've been browsing the gamma slice so far and there are definitely parts I don't care for and from what I've read I'm not alone. First thing, the Garou Nation didn't really need to split as the whole thing with Apocalypse is that the Nation was just beginning to realize that they'd all lose if they didn't at least try to work together in the end. It wasn't a huggy, friendly kind of cooperation but their backs were up against the wall and it was a "do or die" situation. It also seems like the Tribes as a whole have actually taken a huge step back in their outlooks. The Revised TB for the Get had them not particularly caring if you were Metis, sure they were on a lower level by the nature of their births but if a Metis could prove themselves in battle then they were accepted. They also seemed to retcon them back into being racists who are starting to realize that racism is bad. The Get were presented in valuing strength and as of Revised they didn't care so much about skin color as a measure of value.

                                            Then they actually made the Red Talons worse, which isn't something I thought possible. The Impergium will never be a thing again and while the Talons have always been calling for a return to it, now they're actually going through with it and Lupus of other tribes think it's a good idea and might be looking to join in? Not in this day and age. If the Garou are dumb enough to try that again they'd honestly deserve extinction at the hands of humanity.

                                            Male Black Furies? Screw that. I get that they're trying to make the tribe more inclusive or attractive to the player base but it goes against the very nature of the tribe. It's not inclusive or progressive or making a statement, it's actually a step back in my eyes and makes the tribe into the status quo. Women are half the global population and yet considered a minority, men are in the vast majority of positions of power (yeah I know the book says males can't rise above 3rd rank), and what I think the ultimate result that this change will make in the game is that there will be more male Black Furies than female if we're looking at the demographics of the hobby being any indication. Once again, I think it's majorly missing the point of the tribe.

                                                            12 years is a long time. Stuff should've happened in that time. BNS MET effectively is a new edition, as we already had the Revised LARP stuff. The BNS tag already does differentiate it from previous editions. So that's not really the issue. The issue is that you don't like the changes, which is fine but also totally subjective.

                                                            If you want Martin to consider why certain changes won't suit many players, then you need to point out which ones, why, and how many people are against the changes. Otherwise, it's hard to take your email as anything other than an old player who doesn't like change, and there will always be those players with every new edition.

                                                            I'd recommend that anyone who doesn't like the changes say so on the KS and send in an email being specific. There has to be a sense that there's widespread dissatisfaction with the changes before they become serious enough to consider.

                                                            Honestly, though, from what I read of the interview with Martin, it looks like he has his own ideas and isn't particularly interested in other people's ideas. His comments about Revised Edition and later material, for example, do seem a little contemptuous of other perspectives on how the game should be played. I'm hoping, though, that he's mostly hands off when it comes to TT and that it's LARP and the online game that he's mostly interested in.

                                                            It's also worth noting that future products may change a lot because Martin isn't really targeting us as buyers any more. If TT is to become a secondary product to a WoD MMORPG, the focus might shift towards new players (thus widening the market) rather than old players (thus catering to a slowly shrinking market as we old-timers drop off).
