    Originally posted by Ermenegildo View Post

    In my experience, changing/challenging the metaplot is way more difficult than creating from nothing. Players can get attached to what they find in the books and could react badly when the storyteller changes something because "It's not the GAME anymore". Sometimes the along-the-line messages become "How dare you change something that I spend time learning" or even "How dare you change something that I studied making me equal to the novices".
    Everyone plays how they play and I'm not trying to say the way you play is wrong.

    But as someone on RPG.net so well pointed out; W5 is not a generic werewolf game.

    You can do Interview with Vampire in VtM, you can do Lost Boys in VtM, you can do Nosferatu in VtM. The game, though filled with a lot of lore, is not reliant on it.

    You can't do Teen Wolf in WtA, you can't do American Werewolf in London in WtA, you can't do The Howling in WtA. Unlike VtM which has no one true type of vampire, WtA has exactly one type of werewolf as playable, with Stolen Moon as pure antagonists that are not expanded on much.

    Trying to be a more open-to-interpretation setting is hampered by the fact that the garou still have auspices, still have five forms, still are implied to be born and still have tribes and patrons. Making up stuff is cool, but how easy it is to fall back on Legacy which already has every answer the players might be asking?

    My gallery.

                Originally posted by Knightingale View Post

                The flipside of that is that due to poor worldbuilding you ask four people what their takeaway is from reading the book and you get four different answers. That's why in those situations the burden of worldbuilding is shifted either completely onto the Storyteller or it's something you have to address during Session Zero. Like the book says it's happening during the Age of Apocalypse... so when did it start? Then if someone's read the book closely, you're probably gonna have a discussion about the capitalocene era and when people think that started. Or the event of the Fenrir and the Stargazers leaving? Or the Garou-Nation falling? When did this happen, ten years ago, fifty, a thousand...? Who knows...

                And W5 isn't like an Apocalypse World where beyond some tentpoles in worldbuilding (the post-apocalypse genre, the Maelstrom and what the Playbooks imply) you're mostly working with a blank canvas. W5 has a lot of distinct concepts but there are a lot of questions left unanswered as to how these concepts work. Also, unlike any good PbtA-game the game just either shrugs about giving information or just ignores it. There's no point in the book where it gives guidance on how to fill out the blank spaces or what sort of questions you might ask in Session Zero for the sake of completing the process of worldbuilding. When you play this game and in the fourth session somebody asks the ST about how long the Garou-Nation lasted, I certainly don't think giving an answer like "The Garou Nation, as it was known, existed for a brief period of relative success, but even there, its actual duration is unknown and varies by who’s reciting the history. Was it decades? Centuries? Millennia?" would be satisfactory. ​And you can't move it too far back into the past because that would imply the Cult of Fenris and the Stargazers left the Garou-Nation thousands of years ago. But if you put it too close to the present, you start to wonder why nobody knows a damn thing about it. And that's an example of how you're not working with a blank canvas here because "Fall of Garou-Nation", "Cult of Fenris/Stargazers leaving" and "What was the Garou-Nation like?" are all tied together while also being hamstrung by the book's careless "Who knows"-attitude towards worldbuilding.

                W5 is in the tricky spot where it does too much worldbuilding to allow the sort of freewheeling collaborative worldbuilding you do in a lot of PbtA-games but it does too little to serve as a reliable guide to how its setting works.
                I don't know, to me it seemed like they just meant that these questions don't need to be relevant and you can just play without having to worry about the Garou nation.

                    Originally posted by Psicose View Post

                    I don't know, to me it seemed like they just meant that these questions don't need to be relevant and you can just play without having to worry about the Garou nation.
                    If it isn't relevant you wouldn't dedicate a whole sub-section to it. And since you're playing a Garou in this game, it is relevant. Also this attitude in the book of "Who knows" lands in a category of authoritative anti-world-building. The moment you got a player who cares about the worldbuilding and is asking questions about the Garou Nation, you can't just be like "Ah, not relevant, who cares... let's move on.". And the point of a book like the W5 corebook is to help STs with that. Telling them to not worry about it isn't a solution.
