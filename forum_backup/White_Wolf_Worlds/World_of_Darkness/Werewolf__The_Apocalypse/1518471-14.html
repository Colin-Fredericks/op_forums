    Hey everyone, I decided to try and lay out my broader position on all of this, building up from the foundations to more specific issues.

    The Ethics of Giving A Fuck vs. Not My Problem

    I’m going to start by saying that I think most people are supportive of multiculturalism and diversity in a broad sense, or at the very least they don’t see it as some blasphemous social engineering being imposed by out-of-touch elites. I’d say the majority is probably just largely indifferent; they get their Vampire/Werewolf/Dragon game, read the books without thinking about them too much, roll the dice and enjoy themselves without caring much about whether a given identity-group is being represented accurately or fairly. As Saur Ops Specialist hit on above, this is precisely how wrong portrayals that started in Dungeons and Dragons found their way into World of Darkness, via authors who absorbed those ideas uncritically and regurgitated them without thinking about them.

    Now here’s the thing: I don’t really care that much about what the majority position is. Anyone who has studied history and isn’t an arch-conservative or religious fundamentalist needs to accept that majorities have often been happy to accept things that we nowadays, rightly in my opinion, regard as unacceptable and immoral behaviour. So I don’t care about whether or not the majority agrees, and I don’t care whether or not it is the law: I care about whether or not it is right.

    Which is opening the can of cans of worms that is moral philosophy, how do we decide what’s right? Lots of opinions out there on that, and I’m not going to claim that the ideas I’m sharing are original. But one way of looking at this issue is “giving a shit” versus “not my problem.” In the “not my problem” framework, there are lines drawn around your moral obligations, and anything that doesn’t fall inside that bubble of responsibility does not need to be acted upon. So, for example, if someone cannot draw a direct line between stereotypes in a roleplaying gamebook and hate crimes, then there is no moral obligation to remove or critically engage with those stereotypes, because it’s “not my problem” until I personally decide that the level of harm involved merits my concern.

    In contrast, what I’m calling “giving a shit” is about actively caring about what is happening in the wider world. It’s not just about drawing a line around the things you care about and the things that you don’t, but caring about everyone and everything, and constantly striving to make things better for everyone. And to be clear, that’s not something you can do perfectly, and the fact that you will make mistakes or not be able to do as much as you would like is something you grit your teeth and accept regardless of how much it sucks.

    So to be very clear, I’m approaching this matter from the perspective of Giving A Shit. I’m not interested in policing what individuals do at their private gaming table, but I am genuinely boggled by the argument that official materials should be filled with ignorant and incorrect stereotypes that will make a significant portion of the customer base uncomfortable or upset. Who wants to fight over the right to be aggressively and proudly incorrect? I actively invite people to engage with efforts promoting multiculturalism and diversity critically, to try and improve them by making reasonable arguments against the flaws and offering constructive advice on how to do better. But arguing that the entire premise is flawed and unwelcome not only seems inaccurate to me, but it is morally wrong regardless of whether or not it reflects the majority position, because I Give A Shit.

    And before you even try, it’s not your job to decide how much misery another person needs to experience “character growth.” The universe shits on all of us more than enough, thank you very much, so don’t try to make excuses for cruelty by arguing that it’s “for their own good.” Nor will I accept the idea that some minority of ignorant bigots are actually the victims because everyone else rightly treats their ideas as toxic.

    Diversity Matters

    I provided links above to the work of professionals who have studied the impacts of diversity in media, and how it contributes to the mental and emotional health of the groups being represented. If you combine Giving A Shit as a motive with said evidence supporting the idea that good representation helps people, then wanting good representation reasonably follows from there. Again, you can have criticisms about how the particular details of how representation is achieved; complaints about tokenism come just as often from people who otherwise support diversity and representation in media as they do from angry right-wingers freaking out about the Cultural Marxists brainwashing their children.

    This is also a good chance to touch on the contrast between corporate diversity efforts versus Giving A Shit. To the extent that Paradox is trying to avoid nasty stereotypes and invasive cultural appropriation, it’s not because they care about the feelings of their customer base. Individual workers at Paradox might, but the institution is involved in this to make money. To the extent that they have chosen a side in the “culture war,” it is because that is the side where they see the most profit to be made. To the extent that many of their efforts are either ineffectual or actively detrimental, a good part of that comes from the fact that the actual motive of Making Money interferes with the pretend motive of Giving A Shit; if diversity is too expensive, or creates some uncomfortable publicity, can it.

    Obviously, not every decision Paradox has made with the WoD has been the most profitable one, but I am pretty confident that they’ve run the numbers and figured out that the people who actively want them to pander to trashy outdated stereotypes are a minority compared to the customers who would be insulted by said content and would rightly take their money elsewhere. So I’m not very persuaded by any attempts to declare, without evidence, that our position is actually the minority one.

    Microaggressions

    In my earlier posts, I took the position that uncritically playing with game material that employs bigoted stereotypes runs the risk of those stereotypes bleeding over into your activity outside of the game, and that it even has the potential to ruin lives in extreme cases. The next couple of sections will be defending that position.

    First of all, I’m not at all trying to make the claim that you can consistently draw a direct causal link from “I read a bunch of racist stereotypes” to “I went out and committed a hate crime.” The problem is a systemic one, where no individual factor can be pointed out as singularly responsible, but all of them collectively contribute to the result. In the case of a bunch of gamers interacting with trashy stereotypes without critically engaging and educating themselves on the subject, the most common result is not going to be a spree-shooter, but a walking micro-aggression factory. They’re going to spew out ignorant comments that hurt people without thinking, often without even realizing. And unless they weirdly happen to live the life of an ethno-nationalist already without happening to be one, surrounded by people just like them who aren’t reduced to a bunch of denigrating caricatures in the fiction they consume, then they’re going to open their mouth at some point out in public and ram their foot inside at mach speed.

    There are two problems with this. The first being that they are going around hurting people. Whether or not it’s intentional both matters and doesn’t matter; it matters in the sense that they might genuinely be open to correction if they aren’t being harmful intentionally; it does not matter inasmuch as you don’t get to go around hurting people over and over just because you didn’t think about it. They aren’t evil, they don’t need to be punished, but they don’t get to cry foul when people tell them that what they are saying is wrong and that it is hurting people. If you don’t care about hurting people, fine, but you’re telling on yourself when you adamantly argue for your right to inflict your ignorance on the people around you.

    The Toilet Bowl

    But hey, I didn’t just talk about microaggressions, I talked about “ruined lives.” So let’s talk about how “innocent” bigotry can lead down a spiral that ruins lives. And boy, do I have a twist for you!

    We’re going to go back a bit and talk about what Heavy Arms had to say earlier about how people get their back up when they get corrected on this stuff because they want to just keep doing what they’re doing, without having to take responsibility or make an effort to change. Now let’s take that and apply it to our “innocent” little microaggression-eer from above, we’ll call him Bob. Bob is hanging out with Sally, and Bob spouts off some ignorant thing he picked up in an RPG book that aggressively argues against the need to accurately represent minorities and treat them with respect. I’m not going to bother with a specific example, because you can get the idea without me having to say anything stupid like that out loud. Sally is part of the identity-group that Bob just slandered, and she is understandably upset that her friend/acquaintance/etc. has expressed something so ignorant and hurtful.

    Let’s talk about intellectual and emotional labour. When you demand that a member of a minority group that experiences oppression take the time to explain that oppression to you, or even worse demand that they prove it, when you could easily educate yourself on the subject if you actually cared enough to bother, you are attempting to force intellectual labour onto them. And when you say something ignorant that hurts someone, and then demand that they remain calm and civil while you continue to say the hurtful thing because you “just don’t understand what the big deal is,” then you are demanding emotional labour from them. This is why people who regularly experience bigotry tend to get irritated when they are asked to prove something that they’ve already proved nine times this week to ignorant little shits who would rather treat the matter like a “gotcha question” then just take the time to learn about it themselves.

    All of this is meant to lead up to the fact that Sally is probably not going to be especially gentle with Bob when she responds to his microaggression. She may not try to be mean, but she’s probably going to say something that will make him feel uncomfortable in some way, because she is going to tell him that something he did or believes is wrong. Going back to the tendency for people to get their back up when being called out on their nonsense, Bob is pretty likely in turn to get defensive, deflect, make excuses, and Sally may escalate her challenge in response, leading to a cycle of Bob getting increasingly bitter as he keeps getting trouble over, in his mind, “nothing.”

    Eventually, though, Bob will find his people. Or they will find him. The people who don’t make him feel bad over his “jokes,” who don’t tell him he’s wrong not to care about this stuff. Eventually, they might start “showing” him how “those people” are actually responsible for everything that he thinks is wrong in his life.

    You know, the fascists.

    Once again, I have to be clear that I am not describing this as a slippery slope that everyone goes down under these circumstances. But it is enough of a trend that it’s pretty well documented on platforms like YouTube, a spiral of radicalization that people fall down to varying degrees. And you don’t need to go too far down in order to become the kind of person who’ll contribute to making another person’s life a living hell. Online death threats, doxing, SWATing. Your individual contributions don’t even need to be the worst to add to an experience that can be downright soul-crushing in its entirety. And at the most extreme end of the spectrum, you have the ones that end up killing people.

    The real twist? The ones who end up going down this spiral don’t just end up ruining the lives of other people. They often end up ruining their own lives as well, damaging or destroying healthy relationships and building up connections with a toxic and abusive community that could turn it’s wrath back on them if they are ever regarded as a “traitor.” And to be clear, this is not the worst aspect; I'm not going to be gleeful over people finding out the hard way that bigotry is almost as toxic to the bigot as the target of their hate, but the people they have been helping to hurt didn't bring it on themselves.

    Most of the evidence that I have on this stuff is not tied to tabletop roleplaying games specifically. A lot of what I just wrote is based on “The Alt-Right Playbook: How to Radicalize a Normie” by Innuendo Studios, can’t recommend it enough. While it doesn’t talk about tabletop players in particular, it does discuss a broader trend of alt-right efforts to spread into geek fandoms, because they’re finding a lot of fertile soil in the form of young aggrieved white men. I think that makes a pretty strong inductive case for the idea that casual bigotry within gaming communities is a problem that can spiral out of control if it’s overlooked or ignored.

    So can I Play a Nazi or Not?

    I feel like I need to introduce this section by making something very clear: I am not demanding that roleplayers only play “good” characters, or that certain kinds of “evil” characters should be off limits. I play monsters. I enjoy playing monsters, characters doing horrible things and treating the suffering of others as irrelevant or entertaining. I must admit, I’m not generally interested in playing as a bigot for the most part, because bigotry has always struck me as a boring and stupid kind of evil. But I’m not at all opposed to people roleplaying as bigots, in principle. Nor do I think that oppressed minority groups must always be depicted as flawless saints.

    And I’m also going to invoke the “I’m not your dad” principle once more, and make sure that you understand that I don’t think the Roleplay Police should be breaking into your kitchen-table game to make sure that you’re not tossing around slurs. You can do whatever you want. And so can we, by telling you that we think you’re wrong as long as you keep trying to carry on a public conversation about how insulted you are that the people writing books for a massive audience don’t want to shit on a portion of said audience.

    So, of course you can play a Nazi. No one is going to stop you.

    But how and why you play a Nazi matters. You could create a game set in WW2 Germany that seeks to explore the history and psychology of life in the Third Reich. It could be interesting and informative. And if one of the players in your group had his great-grandparents die during the Holocaust, it’s something you should PROBABLY discuss with them first!

    If you decided that you just wanted to play as a Nazi because it’s edgy and controversial and gives you an easy excuse to play a card-carrying, moustache-twirling villain, I’m probably going to find it uninteresting and superficial, but I wouldn’t think you were doing anything wrong (again, as long as everyone at the table is cool with it).

    If you decide to play as a Nazi because it gives you a cover to say things In Character that you wish you could say Out Of Character, then that is pretty obviously shitty and wrong, unless you’re actually a bloody Nazi.

    So yes, you can absolutely play as a Nazi. You can even play as a Nazi and be a perfectly fine and upstanding person. But if you’re choosing to play as a Nazi even when it makes the other people at your table uncomfortable, or if you’re going to start thoughtlessly repeating Nazi propaganda you stumbled on in a game book because you didn’t bother to educate yourself and learn the extent to which so much of how we commonly view the Nazis is based on Nazi propaganda, then people are probably going to call you on your shit.

    Conclusion

    If you are going to go to the mat fighting for your right to indulge in bigoted falsehoods, you’re telling on yourself.

    Or you need to make a much better case for the idea that the Roleplay Police are waiting outside your door, ready to swoop down and destroy your life for thought-crime. Other people giving a shit about being nice to other human beings is not Orwell’s nightmare come to life.
