    Yes, the Howler template leaves a lot to be desired. It's like the first draft of a basic character concept before someone spends some time refining it and adding to it. "I like to howl," just isn't that interesting of a character. It's an inferior version of the War Singer in the Black Furies tribebook - a lupus who likes to sing and discovers the capabilities of the human voice.

    As is, the desire to howl a lot is just a quirk, not a character. You need to do something more with it. The corebooks always have a section on howls, and it would be good to make some kind of reference to them. So besides the character's like of singing/howling, maybe you can do something to make him something of a herald or favored messenger between septs. Some references about the Anthem of War or Wail of Foreboding and the like could elevate the general concept.

    I think the Backgrounds are all wrong. A lupus in North America is likely descended from native wolf stock, not European which means the cub was simply seized when found with little birthright of the Fianna. So Pure Breed, Kinfolk, and Ancestors are mistakes. Even with the conceits of WtA, there just can't be many wolves from Ireland. And if you do want to go down that route, it should be an important part of the background, not an aside. There's no problem of some old Fianna lupus coming to North America and finding its own wolf pack to breed with, and this is that descendant, but if so that should be played up.

    Like the other templates in Ways of the Wolf, this is all very generic. The description and character sheet could be used for any tribe as is. This is probably because in 1e, tribal culture was essentially homid culture. Little thought was given about the totemic aspects of the tribe and how lupus characters - without any human culture - would be a child of Stag, Thunder, Falcon, Rat, etc.

                                    I completely understand why people want to see a difference in the roleplay and backstory of a lupus PC versus a homid. Not only do you want that for the origin alone, but you want to avoid the twinks simply picking the breed to get the extra Gnosis. But that needs to include other concepts than just the equivalent of an alien being materializing on the Earth and learning about human society for the first time. Lupus characters have equal intelligence to humans, and have already gone through training as a cub to get that first rank of Cliath. They should know how things work and can fit into human society when the game begins. There are probably kinfolk families of every tribe except for the Red Talons that foster newly changed lupus cubs and help them adjust. But even the Red Talons likely have worked out a system where they know enough to not stick out like a sore thumb. That's why while you are initially limited in certain Abilities when you assign them, you can spend Freebies on anything.

                                    If someone does want to play a lupus PC where they are still naive and somewhat ignorant, that can work too. But that's the choice of the player, and they should likely take an appropriate flaw Naive, Gullible, or even Twisted Upbringing. Roleplaying some sort of ignorance can be useful. There should be lots of details or idiosyncracies lupus do not understand. But then again according to the setting, so should many homid Garou because they were pulled out of human society after puberty and spent the last three to five years learning as a cub, and their understanding of human society is less of an adult's and more like those of a pre-teen child. It's not unique to lupus or metis.

                                    Strangely in D&D, people accept the idea of wolfweres being able to successfully disguise themselves as human beings all the time. So I've felt like if Harkon Lukas in Ravenloft can do it, why not lupus characters? Lupus characters are probably more "fantasy" based than "animal" based because you are combining elements together that do not occur in real life. It's just that you want that fantasy to fit into a setting of gothic horror in a similar world to the real world rather than a high fantasy one. The 1984 movie The Company Wolves has a lot of useful elements in that. I also find it good to steal a lot from books with anthropomized animal characters like Bambi and Watership Down. But that's just to build the foundation, not limit it to them.

                                    I generally look at the characters perspective and goals as a better indicator if they are "doing" a lupus right. If they are too anthropocentric, the character is probably better as a homid. But if they come from a more wilderness and wolf family centric, it is probably an acceptable lupus. You need to be an outsider to some degree when you are around humans. But being an outsider does not mean you aren't able to blend in, or are confused, or acts stupidly. It could just mean you feel isolated and unnerved. Or it could be you are curious and fascinated. Or resentful and suspicious. Or xenophobic and hostile. Or any other number of things.

                                    This kind of thing can be hard to describe in a character template which is probably why a lot of these in Ways of the Wolf seem weak.

                                                        Wolf Warrior (Red Talon Ahroun)

                                                        And much like the previous entry, this is probably the most stereotypical Tribe for this Auspice (save, perhaps, the Get of Fenris, which is what the Lupus Ahroun character in Rite of Passage is). Not that it's necessarily bad. It just is what it is.

                                                        Anyway, this character was born to a pack of "powerful" wolves. I'm guessing maybe that refers to the pack's size. It was lead by a Garou, and this character and one of its pack mates both underwent the First Change at about the same time. Their rite of passage was to kill a group of human hunters. It's a little vague, but one could make the case that this character may have eaten part of them and enjoyed doing so. This was a thing back in the early days of the game, with the Red Talons frequently presented as being by and large man-eaters. I think the idea of this, at least as a tribal-wide thing, started getting down played later on, especially once you had Camps within the Bone Gnawers and Silent Striders who were specifically singled out as being heavily Wyrm-tainted and corrupt for eating people and eating brains, respectively.

                                                        The character prefers to remain in either wolf or Hispo/near-wolf form, but has moved among humans in homid form. What they've seen hasn't impressed them much nor has it improved their overall opinion of humanity. So, like a lot of the Red Talons at this stage in the game's development, they're wanting to bring back the Impergium, only really being held in check by the potential power of human weapons and the Veil.

                                                        This write up leans heavily into the whole "you must think like a wolf" thing we've been discussing in the thread, relying primarily on instinct. They tolerate non-Lupus Garou but are a pretty staunch Lupus-supremacist. They're also largely devoid of social skills, being designed only as a killing machine. Like most of these, it suffers a bit from how limited the idea of the Auspices were in 1st ed. Ahroun were still pretty much "they fight. And fight. And fight and fight and fight." The idea of them also having leadership abilities and being "Protectors of the Ways" would really start to develop with the 2nd ed.

                                                        The image is of a wolf, all black save for what look like some ritual scar "tattoos" and a shock of white hair in its mane. It may be meant to be in Hispo form, but I'm not not 100% sure. It's not a bad image, all in all.

                                                        For Attributes, Physicals are, of course, primary, with 4/3/3. Socials, as mentioned in the write up, are tertiary, with 2/2/2. This leaves Mental as secondary, with 4/2/2. Yeah, it's basically a Kill Machine.

                                                        Abilities follow a similar pattern. Talents are primary, with 2 each in Alertness, Athletics, Brawl, Dodge, and Intimidation, and a 3 in Primal Urge. (Naturally, the Red Talon is the one with the highest rating in that talent out of all five.) Skills are secondary, with 3 each in Animal Ken, Stealth, and Survival. Again, this is prior to reorienting the Ahrouns toward leadership roles, but really the only thing making him a Kill Machine as opposed to the KillNinja trope I mentioned frequently in the Vampire thread is the lack of Melee and Firearms. Knowledge is tertiary, as it is for every character in this book (and admittedly, that makes a certain degree of sense with all the restricted abilities), with 2 each in Enigmas and Rituals, and 1 in Occult. I have to confess, this doesn't really feel like the sort of introspective character who would be big on Enigmas. I think maybe Investigation might've been more fitting. At the very least, it might tie in better with the mention that they've tried going out among humans, especially as this character has no social abilities outside of Intimidation. I'd probably spend a few Freebies on a dot in Subterfuge and at least one dot in Leadership if not more.

                                                        Backgrounds are 1 each in Kinfolk and Past Lives, and 2 in Pure Breed. The Pure Breed makes a certain degree of sense, I think, given that the character is from a wolf pack that was lead by a Garou and had another pack mate who also changed. (No mention is made of what happened to the other. I think that might've made for some interesting character hooks and role play potential.) The Kinfolk are also the pack they grew up with. The Past Life represents the spirit of a powerful Ahroun.
                                                        These seem sensible. Though if I were making this with the 2nd ed rules of five Background dots, I'd probably go with Kinfolk 3 (a ten member wolf pack) and Pure Breed 2 to start with.

                                                        Rage, Gnosis, and Willpower are the defaults of 5, 5, and 3. It recommends raising WP with freebies, and I'd certainly do that.

                                                        As with most of the characters in this book, this one isn't terrible. It's very basic. An Ahroun designed as a Kill Machine. I wish this had a little more depth to it. At least some sort of hook that pointed toward potential character growth. As it is, I think it's fine as something for a first time player to pick up and use. But it could be better, I think.

                                                        So, that's Ways of the Wolf. Overall, these are mostly OK, but not spectacular. This was very early in the game line, when the nature of the Auspices were still being fleshed out, and when Lupus were still something new and slightly enigmatic even to the writers.

                                                        I think the strongest one is the Human Mediator. I think she's the best representation of her Tribe and Auspice. Curiously, she's also the only one who's gender was actually defined. The rest are pretty nebulous on that front. The rest are all OK, but tend to lean heavily into their most basic Tribal and Auspice stereotypes but not a lot into why the character should be a Lupus as opposed to something else. The slight exception to that is the Wolf Warrior, but that feels like it's more a part of its Tribe than its Breed.

                                                        Next I'm going to look at the premade characters from Rite of Passage, because I think some of them are actually pretty interesting.

                                                            The Wolf-Warrior is a stereotype but as a template for a PC it needs a better hook, I think.
