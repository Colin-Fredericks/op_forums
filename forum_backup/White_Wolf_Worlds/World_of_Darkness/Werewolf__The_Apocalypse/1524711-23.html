        The lack of attribution was already bad but I'm also disturbed by how they used JF Sambrano's version of the Ghost Council as a blueprint for the version we ended up getting. Not only that but it comes off like a bad translation of what Sambrano intended the Ghost Council to be like. The core concept in Sambrano's version was that the Ghost Council are at their heart teachers who seek every kind of knowledge (even the dark, forbidden kind) and then decide what should be shared with other Garou and what not. And they're called Ghost Council because they will even consult dark sources like ghosts.

        The pitch for the Ghost Council in the released book is much weaker (pg 40): "Garou of the Ghost Council favor unconventional approaches and challenge taboos to use rivals’ weaknesses against them in service to Gaia." It also theoretically gets in similar hot water as the Red Talon depiction. "Challenging taboos to use rivals' weaknesses against them"... okay, so the Ghost Council Garou is breaking a taboo and thereby exploits an enemy's weakness. And so the enemy is, for example... a Pack of Black Spiral Dancers. So what does that mean? It isn't a very good concept - especially in light of the existence of the Cult of Fenris. The Tribe-section even has the phrase "the ends justify the means" to describe the Tribe's mindset. Another Tribe like the Red Talons who is easily just as extreme as the Cult of Fenris in terms of how they're described. And the Tribe is now called Ghost Council because for whatever reason the Tribe-members all call each other "Ghosts".

                                        PHASE FOUR PART FOUR

                                        Where Credit Is Due

                                        One last set of issues came up in the W5 previews. Some contributors from the first two iterations of the design noted that their work was being used. These creators wondered if they were going to be given full credit for their work. After W5 came out, it was also noted that some material from V5 had been lifted, sometimes altered slightly, and placed into the new work. This is important because, while each book was made under the auspices of the same company, different licensees worked on each one. While this means that technically such 'lifting' was done in-house, which is common and allowable, this instance is a little murkier as V5 was ultimately published by Modiphius via a temporarily reborn White Wolf and W5 was published by Renegade Studios (which also now does V5 since the new White Wolf no longer exists and the rights were shifted). A look at the credits pages of the two books will show an unexpected difference between the two games.

                                        The most obvious difference is that W5 alphabetized their listing while V5 did not. I don't want to hazard a guess at what V5's style is, but in might be based on the amount of work each person contributed. Of the five main writers of V5, only two wrote for W5, with none of the additional writers mentioned. It is worth noting that the two lead designers of V5 who didn't write for W5 did get secondary mentions, probably due to the fact that they may have come up with some of the basic systems that form the basis of all of the new WOD5 game lines. There may have been some friction with these two and the one who stayed, and that is why some systems were not used in W5 in some form, but others from V5 were. Also, V5 had three editors, one of whom was a lead designer for both games, while W5 only has one editor. The writers for the material taken from V5 might be listed here, but I can't prove it.

                                        Another aspect is that multiple groups were called in to check on the issues of diversity. From my readings, much of the direct human influences on the game had been removed, so the need for diversity might not be as paramount as when actual cultures were tied directly to the Garou. I guess problems with such matters were important to prevent based on similar events from other books. No one person is specifically named from said groups, so if they contributed any extra material, they weren't credited.

                                        Finally, no one from the first two iterations of the W5 design teams seems to have been credited.

                                        For artwork, V5 had three directors, only one of which continued onto W5. Aside for that art director, no other artist from V5 was carried over to W5. This doesn't include the photographers from V5, as W5 used illustrations only. This also brings up the fact that all of the photographers' models were given credit, as they could be identified. None of the individuals used as tracing models were mentioned in W5, possibly because their images had to be completely altered to prevent litigation.

                                        Multiple playtesters for each game were given credit, with more for V5 than W5. This includes a blanket thank-you to many testers whose names were not listed in V5. I am not sure if the lack of testers could have contributed to the issues many are facing with the game.

                                        There is one last glaring omission from the credits, the names of all of the designers for the four previous editions of Werewolf: The Apocalypse. V5 lists all of the creators for their previous editions, including a dedication to one who had died before V5 came out. Yet, W5 doesn't mention anyone. Now, not acknowledging the work by someone who was not an official member of the final version is wrong. Their names should be mentioned where credit is due. However, ignoring the hard work of those who lead the way for W5 is hubris on a whole other level. Sure, W5 is a 'retcon' more than a continuation, but terms and core principles were still brought over. And the game is called 'Fifth Edition' not 'brand new werewolf game.' I have mentioned how the lead designer seems to dislike, if not hate, at least portions of the previous editions, yet he was still given free rein to write W5. If he hates it so much, why was he even put on as lead? Does he really think of himself as that much better than those who laid the groundwork for him that he could ignore or rewrite history? This definitely could be the case, considering how different W5 is to both the previous editions of W:tA as well as to V5. Some would even say such actions are fascist in the ways they are trying to make everyone forget what has happened.

                                        One last post before the final verdict.

                                        THWarted

                                                        In terms of personal horror, you can see some attempts to get there with how punishing the game is (introducing Harano/Hauglosk as two quite unavoidable game-over-scenarios, for example) but as a horror-game it's quite bad. W5 just doesn't have enough of a coherent vision to meet the standards of a good horror-game. Harano and Hauglosk suck as mechanics in W5 (both implementations of when a Harano- or a Hauglosk-check should happen are just dumb game-design). The Crinos-Form in W5 can be a pain in the ass but if the dice-gods smile upon you it can also still make your PC the superpowered warrior Garou always were. The twilight-realm where your character can be quite powerful but also can suck ass (if the dice-gods don't like you) isn't a horror-game. Most of the "horror" W5 offers is just lackluster mechanics that don't do what they're supposed to do as they just thoughtlessly rely on setting up rolls that can be good or bad.

                                                        Of course, W5's big excuse is ST-fiat... An ST might raise the difficulty for a Harano or Hauglosk check if they so choose, for example... The Danger-track in H5 relies on a similar excuse for its bad game-design. Of course, the Danger-Track in H5 is much worse because it's an absolutely meaningless thing, if anything a waste of space in the H5 corebook (calling the Danger-track a game-mechanic would be an insult to game-mechanics everywhere). Harano and Hauglosk don't reach that despicable level thankfully but both in terms of Chronicle Tenets and handling of Touchstones there's a lot of work that has more to do with the ST at the table than the actual "mechanics" proposed by the W5 corebook. This is still some lazy game-design where the corebook leaves most of the work to the ST at the table.

                                                        Let alone the fact that Harano- and Hauglosk-tests are badly designed in the sense that a PCs best defense against them is accrue dots in both tracks at the same time. In terms of mechanics, a PC with Harano 4/Hauglosk 4 is less likely to fall than a PC with Harano 4/Hauglosk 0. That's dumb - but this is how the mechanics in W5 work.

                                                        The biggest problem with the developers' urge to make WtA with more punishing mechanics that this wouldn't be WtA anymore. With VtM the Humanity-track focuses on reminding the player that trying to be human while being a vampire is a struggle. And while they try to make it a struggle to be a werewolf in W5, these developers get just caught up in all these meta-debates of previous WtA-editions when it comes to the Umbra, combat prowess etc.

                                                        It isn't H5 (a game that just sucks and is badly written/designed) but you see the echoes of that failure in W5. H5 is obsessed with telling you how bad hunter-orgs are and completely loses sight of being a game about local hunter-cells. W5 similarly completely loses sight of what the actual game is when it constantly complains about Garou and how bad they are.

                                                            PHASE FOUR PART FIVE

                                                            Bathing in the Aftermath

                                                            Once W5 officially released, many of the rumors were finally confirmed. Those who were already predisposed not to like the game continued to point out the faults, while the supporters quickly tried to take the naysayers down. Reviews (from professionals, not like a regular fan such as I) were mixed. One extremely favorable review was revealed to have been written by someone who was doing freelance writing work on a supplement for W5, yet failed to mention the fact. However, the general trend was neutral to unfavorable.

                                                            Online, the feuding continued. Fans of W5 begrudgingly conceded that W5 did have many errors, but only about the same amount as first edition. Fair point. Counterpoint: First edition was written by about five or six people, max, maybe with some other help, in about a year; meanwhile, W5 was written by over a dozen people, on and off, over five years, yet still managed to have about the same percentage of mistakes.

                                                            The same W5 fans said that they had great success in playing games with new players. Emphasis on the 'new/less-experienced." Some of the bigger weaknesses of W5 (vague backstory, forced game play formats) are reduced when dealing with such groups. I've pointed that this seems to be W5's target demographic in previous phases. More experienced players could start to loose interest in maintained such limited possibilities. Few posts suggest that such players were familiar with other WOD games or even previous editions of W:tA.

                                                            I've mentioned how I feel that some of the online posts are suspicious in other places. So many are highly enthusiastic about W5, while also suggesting that those who hate the new game are wrong. Not just on their opinions, but on why they have those opinions. I cannot fathom why these fans dislike fans of the older editions so much, even when presented with clearly fact-based explanations. There could be some false actors involved. Maybe so outside incentives. Either way, such possibilities come close to false advertising. The fact that many of the people involved with the game, both those still with the publishers or not, and particularly those who left fairly recently, have been rather cagey about the writing process raises some doubts.

                                                            As of now, fans are starting to show their regrets about picking up W5. The hatred between groups are starting to slow down, but some W5 fans still are showcasing their views against those who aren't fans, more so than in the other direction.

                                                            With these final points, I feel that I have presented enough evidence to provide a final grade. While grading a work based not specifically on its content, but on its representation within the larger gaming market, is outside of the norm, for this game, I feel that the many issues that have surrounded it since its formal announcement makes such a grade justifiable.

                                                            Final grade: Incomplete. Too much is still unknown. However...

                                                            I can speculate on what is known and suggest what could have happened and what still might be happening. Next time in Part Six.

                                                            THWarted
