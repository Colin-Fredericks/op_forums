                            Crinos form shouldn't be limited like that. What limits it is the danger to the Veil; as such, you should adopt something more like Heat from the past two editions of Shadowrun, rather than an auto-frenzy mechanic. Call it... Exposure, perhaps. At low levels, no one knows that there's anything about, so any investigation complications or visits from other occult beings are cursory. Increase Exposure, and you start attracting game hunters who have heard about bears in the area; then it progresses to something like SAD agents or Pinkertons, then to deniable assets that have some kind of supernatural powers, and then more blatant enemies of The People. At the highest levels, it might even partially compromise the capacity of Crinos to inflict the Delirium, courtesy of pulling everyone through the Veil. Also, the other Garou might disappear you to save everyone else; certainly, you won't be allowed in a caern if someone is hunting for you.

                                    I'm uncertain what to think of this. This is certainly a change, but not an unexpected one.

                                    In older editions, there wasn't any real drawback to going into Crinos, other than possibly breaking the Veil I guess. You could spend most or all of your time in Crinos without any ill effects. I never had an issue with that, but it seems in W5 they don't want that to be the case. In W5 you won't be able to spend more than a handful of turns in Crinos since you'll drain your Willpower so quickly, so entering Crinos will likely be something a player only does once per session, and even then just for a few turns. If there's multiple instances of combat in a W5 session I think players will end up spending most of it in Glabro (if they want to use human weapons) or Hispo (if they want the aggravated bite).

                                    I don't know if that's necessarily a bad thing or not. In prior editions, Glabro and Hispo were generally useless since you'd just go into Crinos for combat. In this edition they're be much more important, arguably as important as Crinos is.

                                    Still, overall I feel like the rules for Crinos are a bit too punishing. Without doing any playtesting, and assuming that the "time limit" on Crinos is necessary to begin with, I'd lean towards making a player spend a Willpower point per turn, unless they successfully land an attack during the turn, in which case the willpower point is not needed (not just successfully kill a target) This can extend the time they spend in Crinos, while still limiting its duration. Plus if the player transforms and keeps missing they'll end up burning through willpower.

                                                        The drawbacks, complications and considerations of Crinos in W20 were sufficient:
                                                        • -1 difficulty to Rage checks is a double-edged sword; it can protect you, but it can also drive you into Frenzy
                                                        • Against Garou and Fera, it's typically an escalation to warfare, certain death; it declares that you intend to kill them
                                                        • Its size, especially its height and weight, can prove a drawback in some environments
                                                        • Delirium is a double-edged sword; making humans panic and flee can preserve the Veil, but it can also cause chaos or deaths, or cause people you're protecting to run into danger
                                                        • There will be fights where you need to multitask; if you need to speak normally while fighting, Glabro is better, if you need to mobilize very swiftly or hunt something down while fighting, Hispo is better
                                                        • A Crinos form Garou is more likely to encourage other supernaturals to pull out their biggest weapons: Vulgar Magick, Unleashings and so on, meanwhile human-like enemies are more likely to pull out explosives, silver, and weapons which are generally considered overkill
                                                        To my taste, if players are only ever using Crinos in a game, or only ever using Crinos+Hispo, it suggests a lot of samey encounters without complications and secondary objectives. In fact the fight should almost always be the secondary objective; if an encounter lacks a primary objective (protect the cubs, stop the fire, secure the data) it could probably use rethinking.
