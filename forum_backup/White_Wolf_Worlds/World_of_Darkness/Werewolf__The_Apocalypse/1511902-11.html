    8. Born Leader (1 pt. Merit), a -1 difficulty reduction on all all rolls involving Leadership.
    9. Natural Leader (2 pt. Merit), +2 dice for all Leadership rolls, prerequisite Charisma 3 (the most used Attribute for Gifts), awesome in combination with the previous merit.

            HE'S BACK! HE CAN'T BE STOPPED! HE'S GONNA FINISH THE RANK 4 GIFTS AT LEAST!

            Rank 4 Metis Gifts

            Gift of the Porcupine: As always, I love drawing on weird, small, nuisance animals. Great to see that the Umbra is kind to Metis even if Garou Society isn't! Spending a Gnosis point on activation is a bad start, but as always a great Gift can make up for that. Porcupine does that in spades. Inflicting STR+1 Agg on anyone you tackle, grapple or immobilize is amazing on its own. That's great, already, even for a level 4 Gift. Then you factor in that the power also applies a thorns-like effect against anyone who strikes you while you're using the power! They take their own Strength in Aggravated damage unless they get 5+ successes on their attack to hit you.

            The key advantage of this Gift is that it lets you passively deal damage while taking hits and while immobilizing enemies. You get to focus your attention on activating Gifts, parrying or dodging dangerous attacks, handling a non-combat objective and so on.

            It's worth remembering however that this Gift will grievously wound bystanders. You become a walking hazard with this power, especially if you're forced into a Frenzy. Used intelligently this Gift is among the best (non-broken) Gifts in WTA, as it simply goes with everything and multiplies your damage output.

            There are many clever ways you could use this Gift in a Challenge, for example boasting that you can win a fight without making a single attack, but the Aggravated damage make it deadly by default. If you were to run into problems with this Gift in game and find it was making the experience worse for everyone, changing the damage to Lethal could make sense, but IMO this is unlikely to be necessary.

            Worth noting that the language of the Gift is a bit sloppy and it isn't clear whether this Gift inflicts dice of damage or levels of damage. Given that it doesn't explicitly say levels of damage, I'm going to assume dice.

            Lash of Rage*: Spend a Rage, roll Rage. Target must be within 100 feet and takes Aggravated damage equal to your successes. This is Unsoakable! If you repeat this power within a scene it hurts you as well as your target. While this power would be unstoppable in the hands of a Spirit, as a Garou you have 7-8ish levels of health to work with, and you risk killing yourself if you use it unwisely. Having a healer taking care of you while you unleash this attack repeatedly is feasible.

            This is another Metis Gift which focuses on self-hate and being loathed by society, which is interesting to navigate if your Metis comes from a background of tolerance and acceptance. Still, regardless of the individual, the bigotry against Metis has left its mark on the spirit world and the gifts it offers them!

            How good is this? The first attack is a no-projectile ranged attack you can use on any enemy you perceive within 100 feet. There's no roll to hit, you just roll Rage and inflict LEVELS of unsoakable damage as per the result. You can't spend WP naturally, as this is a Gift activation roll. The lack of a given difficulty either means difficulty 6, or that you roll against your general Rage difficulty which is determined by the phase of the moon, your Auspice, and with a -1 modifier if you're in Crinos. Given the potential to inflict 5 unsoakable Aggravated damage on a foe who doesn't even know they're fighting yet, leaving them gasping for air and filled with their own hemorrhaging blood, the power is good, but only if you have a Rage of 5 or higher.

            If your Rage is too low, this Gift is just not gonna cut it at Rank 4.

            All in all I'd say that this power just makes its way into Green, but it's only an auto-take if you have decent or great Rage, and if you otherwise struggle with ranged options. If you have an Aggravated damage ranged weapon via Fetishes or Talens or a Gift, this becomes more niche, but far from useless.

            Rattler's Bite*: Unlikely to matter, but keep in mind that the bonus damage from this gift is a venom, which means certain enemies will ignore it. This Gift is bite-only, and as a claw-lover that's a mark against it, but bites are pretty great and my dislike of them is more personal preference (I'm a fan of the Claw-only Gifts, and I think that putting a Bane or Fomor inside your mouth is asking for trouble!). Difficulty 5 to hit with no setup. Doubling the post-soak damage is pretty great, and it only costs a point of Rage which shouldn't be a precious resource at Rank 4 given the variety of ways to regenerate it, especially for a Metis.

            Note that you activate this Gift when you ATTEMPT the bite, so it's possible to waste the Rage, which is a lil annoying but in line with the RAW.

            I'd say I prefer Coup de Grace to this, but that's a Tribe-specific Gift. Worth noting that Coup is much cheaper all the same! The * is mostly to say that if you are heavily invested into Claws via Gifts and a Brawl spec, this Gift likely isn't for you and would be better to skip.

            Wither Limb: Disability is an under-utilized theme in Metis Gifts, and I like to see it played up here.

            This debuff power adds +2 to dexterity difficulties for the target, and if you target a leg they move half normal speed. The roll to activate is Willpower, which ought to be good, against the target's Stamina +4, or Willpower +4 in the case of most spirits of course, assuming they are valid targets with limbs.

            The Gnosis cost, and this being Rank 4, is where problems show up. At a lower rank, or with a WP/Rage cost, this would be Green. But while this Gift is balanced, and I don't think any changes are needed, it's on the weaker end. There are lots of cool uses! You can cripple a foe out of combat as a warning or threat or punishment. You can hobble a prisoner so they can't escape. You can make a packmate look like a harmless weakling for the scene.

            It's also worth noting that if an enemy has a Stamina of 5+, which is very common in BSDs (unless you catch them in Homid or out of Crinos), you'll waste your turn, AND a point of Gnosis, AND you won't get a bunch of Rage attacks/dodges this round. So this Gift winds up being best against... weaklings! Enemies who you could beat easily anyways. Given the high cost of a Rank 4 Gift, this winds up Amber, though that doesn't mean I dislike it by any means.

            As a final note, by the point you're this deep in the game, you probably have a better debuff depending on your Auspice or Tribe, so this becomes even more niche. Why not hit them with Paralyzing Stare or True Fear? I find myself wondering how many times this power has ever been used across all tables, given what I've mentioned above. Perhaps more often by NPCs.

                Rank 4 Lupus Gifts

                Beast Life: Leaving this one unrated, but it could be Green depending on assumptions and setting.

                Isn't it weird that Lupus don't get Beast Speech? This is a complicated and weird Gift, but my initial reaction is "just buy Beast Speech for 5xp; you're a Lupus, it's easy to justify". However, Beast Life does a lot more than let you communicate with animals.

                It lets you spend Gnosis to draw in animals for miles around, up to 10 miles per success in fact. It also lets you give orders to the animals that show up, up to and including sacrificing their lives, so long as you pay or repay the appropriate animal spirit.

                This is a highly setting-dependent power. If you're in the Ice Age, you could get a small army of mammoths and sabretooths. If you're in South Africa, you could set a few Prides of Lions and a small family of Rhinos on your enemies, not to mention hippos. New York? All the rats and dogs and cats you could possibly ask for, along with 1000s of pigeons, crows and so on.

                That said, something about this power doesn't sit right with me. It feels like an Elder Animalism power. This ability is not in tune with nature, and even a Rank 4 Lupus isn't guaranteed to be a wise and sensible wielder of it. Yes, to reach Rank 4 you need to have shown "wisdom" (learning Rites, agreeing with and obeying your Elders, and perhaps shown some wisdom now and then, though that appears to be optional given the history of the Garou Nation!), but if all Rank 4+ Garou were sensible the WoD would be a very different place.

                If you can look past the "I get every animal to come to me and do my bidding" part, which is slightly easier to forgive given that it's a Rank 4 Gift, there's also the Veil implications, and the indiscriminate nature of the power. What you're triggering is essentially a natural disaster and ecocide. How many millions of insects die en route to you? How many pets run away and never come back? How much livestock break free? How many zoos and so on get torn asunder? How many Ratkin, Corax and other Fera now have you on the Kill List for completely fucking up the ecology of their respective species for a night, if not longer?

                Assuming you handwave all that and read the intent of the power, assuming they mean that useful animals who can safely come to you show up... it's good. Messy, but good. With enough assumptions to fill Noah's Arc, I'd call this Green.

                Gnaw*: This is a weird one. You roll Strength + 4 against a variable difficulty to chew through various objects.

                Here's the thing.

                You're a Rank 4 Garou. I don't care that you're a Lupus. So often it feels like the WTA writers imagined they were writing Gifts for WOLVES when they wrote Lupus Gifts. You are a Rank 4 Garou! You don't need a Gift to break a pair of handcuffs! You can hulk out at will by spending a point of Rage! This is where the insane power of the Werewolf basekit makes design space limited, but still, that's something you can be inventive with.

                The other benefit is +2 dice of damage to your Bites for a scene. I'm more of a Claw fan, but that's personal preference.

                Given that this costs 1 WP, and it adds +2 damage to your attacks IF you are into bites, I'd say that this is an auto-take for Bite Enjoyers. Yes, it costs 12xp, but 12xp for +2 damage to most of your attacks (again, assuming you default to biting!) is great. And the ability to gnaw your way through a titanium bar or similar is pretty great, though I still find it infuriating that we're getting Gifts intended to overcome wooden and steel objects at Rank 4, when you're standing at the base of the mountains of legend.

                Amber with a *, but scope to be Green if you're obsessed with Bite attacks.

                Scream of Gaia*: Spend a Gnosis, roll Rage. Everyone within 50 feet, besides you and your Pack, is blasted onto their ass and takes an unsoakable level of Bashing per success.

                Say you have a Rage of 3, 5 or 7. The difficulty is unstated, so it's presumably 6. 1-2ish, 3ish or 4ish damage on every foe in a huge AOE is pretty great. This could absolutely gut a small army of weak spirits with minimal essence! The effect of putting a load of enemies on their ass is pretty neat, but the benefit will depend on how the ST runs enemies standing back up, which varies a lot in my experience.

                Yes, enemies with regeneration and healing will recover, and you can't do anything else that turn, but assuming a Rage of 5 or more (*) this is pretty neat. It isn't your go-to, but it's a great option to adopt for only 12xp, which could help you to shatter a crowd of weak enemies, cow a crowd, and so on.

                Keep in mind that this power will hit friendlies, and within the range is basically a Small Animal Genocide. Cats, spiders, bugs etc. will all get messed up by this. That's great if you're fighting against a Pentex monstrosity which leaks subservient bugs everywhere, but terrible in a woodland. Used indiscriminately and repeatedly, you'll find yourself killing allies and bystanders, precious wildlife, Kinfolk and so on. Just because you can't see the hostages doesn't mean they can't get hurt by this Gift!

                Terror of the Dire Wolf: Spend a Rage, Manipulation + P-U roll, difficulty of WP, and can target groups. This power inflicts Delirium onto recipients, even if you aren't in Crinos, and also effects targets who are usually immune, such as Mages, Vampires and so on. If you are human, or were EVER human, this applies. Even Kinfolk fall victim, and only Garou/Fera are immune.

                That said, Delirium is already WP-dependent. Yes, many many many foes will flee in terror. But anyone with a Willpower of 7+ will still be able to fight you or mess with you as appropriate, despite their terror.

                This Gift can be incredibly powerful and can solve a bunch of problems on its own, but Delirium is a double-edged blade and can create as many problems as it solves. Used correctly this will protect against Veil Breaches and help to peacefully end fights. Used unwisely this could make things worse!

                I'd suggest using this against crowds, and to forcefully push away non-Changer enemies and leave BSDs/similar on their own.

                    I'm back too then! :P

                    Nothing much to add though:

                    For Lash of Rage and Scream of Gaia it's important to remember than all Rage rolls count towards the potential to Frenzy. Rolling 4+ Success means you Frenzy from using these (5+ when you hit Rank 5, and 6+ if you ever hit Legendary). That's a big tactical consideration for these two Gifts, since the better you can use them the more likely you are to flip out. And it means you have to invest in even more Gnosis to keep up with your Rage so you don't attack your packmates too. The difficulty for any unspecified Rage check is based on moon phase too, which complicates how reliable your damage output is going to be as well. Though a positive rules note is that you roll the higher of your temporary or permanent Rage when making a Rage roll, so even if your permanent Rage is 1, if you purposefully angry up your blood up to 10... you can unleash one hell of an attack if you're going in expecting to open with one of these.

                    A second note just for Scream of Gaia, it takes two turns to use, because you can't use Gnosis and Rage at the same time (at least until you snag a certain Rank 5 Glass Walker Gift).

                    I'm not sure it changes much, but it's worth remembering that Gnaw works in any form... even homid. Sure, the big draw is still +2 damage for biting for a scene, but it's really useful for when you can't hulk out. If you just want a universal lock pick, Open Seal is better (esp. for lupus), but it's really more for when you can't hulk out and still need to bust through something. A very specific use-case that has come up twice for me.... is that a homid or lupus in their birth forms can use Gnaw to safely chew through silver. I get this falls under "being creative" with it, but after seeing a Ragabash lupus troll a BSD by using Gnaw to trash the BSD's grand klaive during a recon of the BSD's camp... it has a stronger place in my heart than my opposing preference for biting getting more love from the Gifts.

                                Rank 4 Ragabash Gifts

                                Luna's Blessing: This is an innate Gift, if a niche one. You basically become resistant to silver when the moon is visible in the sky. If the moon is behind clouds, brand new, hidden by an eclipse, below the horizon, or it's daytime (though the moon is sometimes visible during the day of course) then this Gift doesn't function. Given that this is arbitrary in many cases (e.g. whether it's a moonless night is something the ST just decides, it isn't usually plot-relevant) it falls to the ST to either let this Gift work or be a dick about it!

                                Instead of dealing Aggravated damage, this Gift makes silver deal B/L damage depending on weapon type. It also adds a single 1 to the dice roll of an attacker, or two 1s during a full moon.

                                This is handy, all in all, but so niche that it can never be a guaranteed good investment. Assuming that Klaives deal Lethal/Bludgeoning with this Gift, which is arguably a push since they deal Agg to targets with no special relationship with silver at all... this is okayish. But the problem is that while the Gift does point-blank say that silver deals B/L, Klaives say that they deal A. Which takes precedence? I'd go for the Gift, since it's a niche Rank 4 Gift, but still. It isn't certain.

                                A really important part of the Gift, however, which is easily overlooked is the auto-1 added onto the roll. The impact on incoming damage, how often enemies hit, and how often you can dodge, might be great! But it remains niche since most foes won't be hitting you with silver clubs, though that is setting and campaign dependent. Kinfolk Rebellion? Silver mallets abound. Fighting spirits in the Umbra? Unlikely they'll be wielding silver weapons.

                                Regardless of the exact implementation I'd call this Amber. It's never going to be Red, since it has its uses, but it's definitely on the lower end of Amber in my book.

                                Umbral Dodge: This is just so fun that I can't put it below Green. Yes, it's niched by the fact that you have to spend Gnosis, and you have to be dodging a melee range attack, and you need to roll your dodge against the difficulty of the Gauntlet or +1 from the normal difficulty (so 7 minimum, typically, as you take the higher result)... but if the power goes off you can strand an enemy in the Gauntlet or the physical world! It's just such a fun maneuver and such an auto death sentence for vampires, humans, most Changelings, Mages and so on. Even the vast majority of Kinfolk, and some Fera, will really suffer if they're stranded in the Umbra.

                                But what about spirits and BSDs and Fomori? Those are the big guys you run into constantly.

                                Well, it's a great trap if you are confident in your Dexterity + Athletics. You're right there waiting to be struck. Your pack? They're on the other side of the Gauntlet awaiting a shocked enemy who they can pummel into the dirt. If you use this power to shunt enemies into curbstomp parties this is wonderful. Is the Umbra incredibly hostile in this area? Even a BSD might be in trouble if they're blasted into it without warning. Be inventive and cheeky and there are some really cool victories you can get with this power.

                                I just wish that the Gift also let you dodge by crossing the Gauntlet! It would suit the name so well, though it might be more fitting for a Rank 5 Gift.

                                Whelp Body: Permanent reductions to physical attributes for targets, gated behind some very difficult rolls. If you have good Gnosis and your target is weak it will work... but you'd only want to use this on a strong target.

                                If your campaign has recurring antagonists you can't kill for some reason this might help. But it's already niche, then you consider the Rank 4 cost of 12xp minimum. This is a power for NPCs in my opinion. You CAN make it work, but you're going out of your way to use the Gift for a bit of a silly power trip RP moment where you punish a lower Rank NPC Garou. I remember this power being used on a Vampire PC in a cross-splat PBP server set in Miami! Yes, in that server the Garou didn't kill Vampires on sight, and in fact showed up to Elysium meetings... it was weird. So yeah, if you're in a living world environment or some unusual game setup this power might have mileage, but even then, "I can permanently rob you of xp from your sheet" isn't a fun interaction, whether it's with PCs or NPCs.

                                    Rank 4 Theurge Gifts

                                    Blurring the Mirror: This Gift has a use, and it's good at what it does, but it's very sad for its Rank. Spending 1 Gnosis per target sucks. If you want to trap a BSD pack so they can't cross the Gauntlet, you need to hit them with 5 Gnosis for a 5 member pack. A starting Lupus can manage that, but spending all your Gnosis in a single action is a bold move, especially when it's almost certain you're the party's sole healer.

                                    I won't give this Red, since at the end of the day it's 12xp to gain the ability to fully block a Step Sideways escape for Garou and anyone else who can pull the old sidestep. And there is a niche use of it that people will miss; use it on yourself to stop yourself (or others) from being kidnapped into the Umbra using things like Grasp the Beyond! It's ST-dependent since it depends on how you interpret the power, but it's interesting to try.

                                    Part of what is awful about this Gift, for what it's worth, is that it only lasts for one scene. If it lasted for a variable period, or more time for more Gnosis, or a time depending on the difference between your Gnosis and theirs, it could be great. But instead it lasts for a scene! Which contradicts the flavour of the power being used as a punishment on Garou. If you're playing a Theurge I see no reason not to get this, but it's low priority.

                                    Grasp the Beyond: Being able to bring objects and people into and out of the Umbra without Dedication or other normal methods is amazing. This power is great for thieving, for kidnappings, for interactions with Kinfolk and various night-folk, and it is a power which many creatures simply have no way of fighting back against. If you get through the Gauntlet with a powerful supernatural being that can't step sideways, chances are they're really going to suffer. Their best case scenario is bargaining their way back to their world or becoming a disembodied spirit.

                                    I'd consider this a must-have on a Theurge. This is the sort of thing higher rank Gifts should be doing, letting you break important rules which restrict the Garou basekit. The WP cost is significant, but it's not abysmal either.

                                    A fun use for this Gift would be stepping sideways with a foe, even a Garou or other spiritually capable enemy, and dumping them into a trap or hazard they haven't had time to prepare for. A Spiral Dancer could have a lot of fun using this Gift on Garou in cities, given how toxic the Umbra gets there.

                                    Spirit Drain*: Resisted Gnosis roll with no cost. For every success you gain, a spirit will lose one Essence. For every two Essence taken you'll gain a point of Willpower. Any points exceeding your maximum are lost at the end of the Scene, however...

                                    This is a great way to regenerate Willpower, assuming you have a source of energy. If you have a very good relationship with the Umbra, acting like a predator and taking energy from Gafflings when you need it could work. This power is ripe for abuse, but I think it works well since abusing the power is an in-universe abuse of power, which will have consequences. If you treat the spirit world like your own food store, obliterating helpful spirits to nourish your soul, you'll invite in corruption and accusations of mistreating the Umbra.

                                    It's worth noting that the power makes no mention of taint. I would argue that stealing Essence from a Bane is a terrible idea, and using this Gift on dark spirits of all kinds is a bad idea. But I'm also relatively averse to using bite attacks on Fomori and Spirits since you're putting the possible explosion of pus or cancer inside your mouth, so I'm more cautious than most.

                                    Used as an attack, this is a pretty poor ability, unless you are fighting a spirit who is incredibly hard to hit (high WP) or damage (high WP) but whose Gnosis is very low, or has very little Essence. Against a low-Gnosis low-Essence spirit this is possibly an instakill, but a Rank 4 Theurge shouldn't be struggling to kill such a spirit.

                                    If the other Willpower-restoring Gifts, which are Rank 1, are inaccessible to you then this is a Green. If you can access other Gifts which grant or restore Willpower, this is arguably Red, and certainly not Green. If this Gift restored Gnosis instead of Willpower... now that would be a gamechanger!

                                    Spirit Ward: See Homid list. Noteworthy that this Gift is even better on the Theurge list since your starting Gnosis could be 3 or 5 instead of 1, and as a Theurge your opportunities to raise your Gnosis will be relatively common.

                                        Rank 4 Philodox Gifts

                                        Roll Over: It's kind of funny to compare this to Kneel from the Black Furies list, given the big disparity in Rank. Still, this Gift is great since it works on a crowd, and once you've forced a target to kneel or submit they are stuck submitting and cannot do ANYTHING without your approval.

                                        Here's the problem.

                                        There is no protection from being higher Rank, so a Rank 5-6 Garou is vulnerable to this power, and losing your Rank (say becoming a Ronin and dropping to Rank 0) won't depower this Gift. Supernaturals are effected identically to mortals, which isn't an issue at Rank 4, but still worth noting. And it's a simple contest of Willpower. If you have a high WP, this Gift can utterly destroy not only the balance of encounters, but the way the setting works.

                                        There is nothing fun or interesting about walking into a big confrontation with a crowd of BSDs and forcing them all to submit while the rest of your Pack dismantle the defenses and desecrated relics. If this power works the game loses all its stakes.

                                        It isn't that a save-or-suck effect is inherently bad, it's more that an AOE save-or-suck which applies to an indeterminate number of enemies in an indeterminate range, with no restrictions or caveats, is terrible. There is no cost, and it isn't like Willpower is something people tend to skimp on, it is one of the best things to invest in in the whole game.

                                        Redeeming factors I can think of would be if someone escaping the power can rally others to resist it, or if supernatural foes can defy you but suffer a significant debuff to all their rolls, or if the power only worked when you were enforcing the Litany or had a solid legal basis to assert authority. Hell, if this power only functioned inside of Caerns (including desecrated ones!) it'd be a step in the right direction, or if it relied on the Gauntlet being thin.

                                        I don't consider this Gift fit for use. Yes, you might get torn to shreds before you can finish executing the power, as anyone who has yet to fall prey to this ability is free to act normally (very all or nothing!) but the maximum possible outcome just doesn't work with the game, and a whole chronicle pivoting on the result of one Willpower roll fells silly. I also consider this power to be poor worldbuilding.

                                        Scent of Beyond: This is what I'm looking for. Birds-eye view of any place you're familiar with, flinging your senses across the physical or spirit world. Comparison to Scrying is probably sensible, and this power is superior in a few small ways. Your senses are sent, rather than just getting images. Scrying mentions that there are occult means of protecting against it, which will apply to many important places. This power doesn't require an item from the location. Both powers will almost always be difficulty 8, but Scent of Beyond is slightly better quality (all senses) and easier to use overall.

                                        Since Scrying is cheaper as a Rank 3 Gift, but it's a very Tribe-specific Gift (EB aren't known for sharing their Gifts freely) and so this Gift feels very relevant despite the similarities. One downside is that you're forced to look at the scene from above like a bird, which will make certain details much harder to spot. That said, Scent is free!

                                        I would argue that the ideal combination is to have Scent of Beyond AND Scrying, since you get two POVs, two opportunities, and there are situations where one will work and the other won't; Scrying might give better close-up detail (you're like a fly on the wall), while Scent gives sound and smells and so on. There are problems with this Gift and certain mysteries, but at Rank 4 it goes without saying that some mysteries will be expedited by supernatural powers. Garou have spirit spies and insane senses, so I don't see this as a problem, more a feature.

                                        Take The True Form: no cost, and with a relatively simple roll you can force a creature into its 'true' form. Manipulation + Primal, difficulty 7, the target is stuck in their true shape for one turn per success. This would be particularly handy against Homids and Lupus, Gangrel, Tzimisce, and even some Changelings and Mages. If the form you knock someone out of took a long time to create, or is expensive to enter, or takes a long time to slip into, this power is even better.

                                        It's also worth noting that this power has a 'healing' potential. If someone is turned into a pig or toad by a witch, or a spirit transforms a Garou into a rock, this Gift is carte blanche to revert them to their true form. A Sabbat Ghoul warped by Vicissitude reverting back to their true form could turn a sorry mess of limbs into a normal person, and while a typical Ghoul is no trouble, there are some horrors which would be significantly depowered by this ability.

                                        Use this on a Garou who has sprouted Pegasus wings with the relevant Gift to make them plummet to the earth as a human or wolf, or wingless Crinos. Use this on a Frenzying packmate to force them into their human or wolf form. Use this on a raging Khan to show him that you aren't interested in his ultrapredator roars and that you will be talking like adults. Use this on yourself when you're forcibly shapeshifted, transformed, or about to enter frenzy. The clever applications are endless and make me wish this was a Rank 2 or 3 Gift! But it fits well at Rank 4.

                                        Given that it is at Rank 4, I do wish there was an option to spend a point of Gnosis and make the effect last for a day per success, or similar, since that would help a punishment theme of the Auspice, but perhaps that would work better at Rank 5. This is a rare example of a Rank 4 Gift I'd want to buy on any character, even if it isn't in-list. I really find myself wishing this was a Metis Gift as well!

                                            Rank 4 Galliard Gifts

                                            Bridge Walker*: This Gift is very hard to rank because we aren't really given explicit mechanics for all of its uses. What's important is that we DO know that it can permit you to make a journey in 1% of the time it would normally take, that only you can make the journey, and that you can extend the duration of the bridge to a full month by spending extra resources.

                                            All in all, the ability to do what seems to be a teleport power, where you suddenly slip into a moon bridge and then reach your destination a moment later (or a few moments later if it's further away) is great, but the power needs further clarifications from the ST. For escapes and ambushes this power could be great. I have no idea how you're meant to rule the anime combo of appearing behind foes, since the book doesn't tell us, but I would argue that this combo is awful since spending Gnosis means you can't do Rage bonus attacks, which is part of what makes an ambush so valuable.

                                            This Gift gets a VERY tentative Green with a *.

                                            Gift of Dreams: This Gift lets you do Inception, sorta, that is you can breathe a really impactful dream about the beauty of mother nature into the mouth of a redeemable enemy and hope they wake up with an epiphany about not selling their business to that "PENTEX" group.

                                            Basically this is a plot dream. If you have a plan which involves using it, it becomes a bit of a plot device, with a variable outcome. This makes it so ST-dependent that I'm gonna leave it unrated. If you need this for the plot, absolutely take it. If you have it and you don't need it for the plot, it becomes a fun way to flex and do nice things for people.

                                            All of that said, a very nasty application of this power is that Garou tend to take imagery in their dreams and visions as gospel; assuming it came from the Wyrm without evidence costs you Renown! So a tricksy PC or NPC could use this power against other Garou to mislead them quite terribly. Just expect spirits to snitch on you if that's the case, if things get serious enough that the Garou is about to really mess up over the fake omens you gave them.

                                            Shadows by the Firelight: This feels like it is mostly a flavour or NPC power, and in fact, would be better as a Rite taught to Galliards. That said, the key to this Gift's balance is how it works with unwilling participants. You make a Manipulation + Performance roll against the target's Willpower, along with a Gnosis expenditure, and the 'spell' holds for a scene or until the target is attacked.

                                            There are a million interesting yet tenuous things you could do with this, if you had written a story which lined up perfectly with what you need to happen. This mind control could have someone open doors for you, solve problems for you, or generally do anything you need them to do. The problem with that is that you're narrating a story, and the power will be extremely obvious. More to the point, walking around a Pentex base using this ability to make an executive do all the things you need him to do to turn on the self destruct REALLY stretches this power's intended mechanics, even if I think it's okay with the RAW.

                                            Basically, this is a weird one. You could skip it and never need it. You could take it and use it solely to flex, though I think flexing with powerful Gifts is usually boring. If the Garou culture you're immersed in really values stories you might get good mileage out of this Renown wise, but at Rank 4 your problem isn't going to be increasing your Renown, it's going to be challenging someone to become an Elder, and generally overcoming the huge barriers between Rank 4 and Rank 5, and even moreso for Rank 6.

                                            I'm gonna call this an Amber, since 'fun but not necessarily useful' falls in there. If you find a great use for it, that's a bonus.

                                            Rank 4 Ahroun Gifts

                                            Body Shift: See Homid list. Sadly an Ahroun of Metis or Lupus Breed isn't any better at using this.

                                            Clenched Jaw: Not a fan of biting, though this caps off a 'bite build' quite nicely. While you're clinging on you can do a difficulty 3 bite each turn, and getting you off is much harder. I would wager that at Rank 4 you're probably dealing with foes who instead of trying to tear you off will instead be doing horrible things to you while you're relatively immobilized (though of course you can stop at any time).

                                            For me this doesn't do enough as a Rank 4 Gift, but it's good enough at what it does to be Amber rather than Red. If you are into bites this is a handy Gift, it provides some neat benefits, and there's nothing stopping it from stacking with various other Gifts. If you're more into Claws you could keep someone chomped with this Gift and use all your Rage Actions to attack with claws too.

                                            Full Moon's Light: spend a Gnosis, anyone within 1 mile who is actively working against you (whether as rivals or enemies, so this would highlight a prankster messing with your hunt as much as an assassin trying to kill you) gets a lovely glow which messes with invisibility, stealth and also makes them easier to differentiate from bystanders.

                                            This Gift is somewhat situational, but the number of situations you can MAKE it work in is exceptional, so long as veil breaches aren't an issue. For example at a Moot where you are acting as a guard, with the duty of preventing any breaches in the Litany or attacks on the Moot, an AOE crime detector is great. There are many situations where this Gift could pick out impostors, find assassins, thieves, the corrupt and so on.

                                            Since it's an Ahroun Gift, it's easy to think of combat situations, but this is one of those Gifts which is pretty universal in how you can apply it, again, so long as Veil breaches aren't an issue. In a murder mystery this Gift could highlight anyone who is trying to stop your pack from solving the mystery, which will at least give you a bunch of clues and help you to identify obstacles, even if you don't get an immediate reveal of who the killer is.

                                            The main issue with this Gift is that it's pretty blunt; you literally make people glow. Even if you're dealing solely with supernaturals, activating this Gift can cause a lot of different cogs to start turning outside or your line of sight. As a result I'd say that this could be Amber or Green depending on the intelligence with which it is used.

                                            Overall I give this a tentative Green since there are so many things you can solve with this Gift which otherwise you might really struggle with. I'm also a big fan of anything which lets a Garou contribute to tasks their Auspice wouldn't usually complement.

                                            Stoking Fury's Furnace: This is a great one for longevity. The most important thing this does is let you spend a single point of Rage each turn, so long as it is JUST one Rage, for free. So if you're spending 1 Rage to transform on your turn without a roll, instantly, and then you spend no other Rage that turn, great. If you are in a REALLY long battle where waves of enemies keep coming and coming, you can spend 1 Rage each turn to get a single bonus attack without eating into your Rage pool.

                                            Since you are an Ahroun if you have this Gift most likely, chances are you're gaining 1 Rage per turn with Silver Claws. That means that if you're regenerating 1 Rage each turn with Silver Claws, and you're spending no Rage each turn thanks to this Gift (doing one bonus attack, for example), you'll be recovering a point of Rage each turn, allowing you to save up for a big burst of attacks when a powerful enemy is vulnerable.

                                            This power is a huge QOL improvement, even before you get to the OTHER big benefit of the Gift. If you take damage in a given turn you'll regain 1 point of Rage. This is a buff to Primal Anger, and also lets you recover Rage consistently when you're under attack. Between this and Silver Claws and the 'free' Rage each round, this Gift makes a Rank 4 Ahroun MUCH scarier than prior Ranks, which is impressive. This is a good example of great Gift design; the floor for Ahrouns is already quite high, so it's tricky to design a Gift which makes them even better at their job without raising the ceiling too high. The consistency granted by this is spectacular.
