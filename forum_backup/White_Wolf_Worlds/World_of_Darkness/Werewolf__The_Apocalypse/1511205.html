    Did we ever get canon Tribal populations?

    05-10-2023, 06:29 AM
    I know that some 2nd ed and Revised books were interested in sticking populations onto things, despite these raising difficult questions (if there are only X members of Y, how are there Z numbers of subgroups).

    I'm aware that the BSDs are the largest Tribe, followed by the Glass Walkers, Bone Gnawers and Children of Gaia who are all said to be "the three largest Tribes" (of the Garou Nation, that is!). But how would it pan out for the rest?

    I would imagine that the Red Talons and Stargazers are relatively rare, for differing reasons, and that Younger Brother would be quite rare.

    If there are any canon numbers I would probably just hold onto the proportions and drop the exact numbers; "there are 3x as many members of Tribe X as there are of Tribe Y" is a handy statistic to have handy IC, and it helps give a sense that these are real groups and not just Things You Can Pick In Char-Gen; knowing your tribe is rare produces a sense of preciousness and pressure, a feeling that if you die pointlessly it will further doom a dying breed.

    Also worth noting that there's something that stands out about the four largest tribes:
    - the BSDs feel it is imperative to produce as many Metis as they can, the Dark Litany forbids nothing when it comes to Garou-Garou romance and breeding, and this pays dividends
    - the Glass Walkers are modernists who eschew many traditions and focus on the cutting edge, and are said to have more Metis than average, perhaps in part because they're a bit too human; they also have Kinfolk who take on more militaristic roles, and they've given up on Pure Breed entirely by choice
    - the Bone Gnawers focus on survival above other concerns, prioritize what works over what's "the way things are done", and adopt the castoffs, bastards and Metis of all the other Tribes
    - the Children of Gaia emphasize equality, harmony, seek non-violent solutions when they are the right way forward, adopt the Metis of other Tribes, and explicitly ignore the tenet of "not allowing your kin to suffer your illness", instead allowing themselves and their kin to reach a great age and continue to care for others and be cared for in turn

    What can we learn from this? Well it depends on your OOC/IC opinion, but I think it's pretty interesting. An argument could be made that tradition is an inhibiting factor for the Garou, or that inclusion and diversity have canonically proven to be strengthening factors. It's also worth noting that 2/4 of these Tribes lack Pure Breed entirely, 1/4 has a tenet of absolute equality, and 1/4 barely has Pure Breed at all (the vast majority of BSDs are Pure Breed 0, and only a select few with a direct line back to the Howlers have PB5).

                                                          Like we're told Fianna hate Metis. But then the NPCs you're meant to like don't. And none of the PCs do, obviously! And so you're left wondering who these notably Metis-hating Fianna even are!
                                                          Reading the book in question would actually help with this feeling you have. First of all, the early tribes are horrible caricatures defined by grimderp left and right - the less said about the Drunken idiots that the Fianna were and the Supremacist Vikings the Get were, the better (We can find many stupid ideas on the original tribebooks, but these two are good as most people know the bullshit by heart). Secondly, the Revised tribebooks actually go into the nuance of these things.

                                                          Of course the Fianna are prone to loose relationships and metis popping up, they are even worse about considering what it means for them and how to take responsibility over those actions. They actually go into that in depth and how the life of a metis works out in a Fianna Sept and what can be expected out of it and how it can go good and bad. It even has differing views by multiple voices on how these things work out so you have even more depth about this. This would continue with all the other developments of the Fianna spreading accross the world with the celtic cultures rather than the whole "Oh we must have the Irish in a US-centric RPG, and let's make them bad stereotypes". Of course, making them more sensible and less of a caricature didn't erase the fact that they can still be idiots and that their culture actually reinforces certain bad patterns that will bite them in the back and create tension for the players to engage with.

                                                          The Get are obsessed with prowess and strength. But they are not actually coddling a bunch of white supremacists and Aesir cryptofascists in their midst, they have fought their own battle against their supremacists and wiped them out for they were a stain on their honor and the fact that power comes from different places. The idea that the Get are actually the children of Fenris and that the Aesir are the villians of their tale was brilliant, removing the problem collecting camp was a solid plan. Pointing out that they take anyone who fulfills their criteria of Strength and how Pure Breed is not a facet of Strength was the right choice. Yet still they are proud, they are teritorrial, they are assholes looking for a fight and to prove to everyone how awesome they are. They are still a deeply troubled tribe, but not one staffed with morons but with people.

                                                          The idea is to make these concepts playable. There are still warts and problems to resolve and generate conflict, however it's not defined by there being flaws based around racial purity or being a drunk. Instead of getting caricatures we actually get organisations with history and context that stand on their own with the postulations we've had about how they should be working since the start. What was done there was the best effort to make Werewolf into a better game.

                                                          For bonus points, also back in 2000, Revised said that Metis and Métis are different things with different pronunciation, and that it was not the intent to invoke the people in question with that name.
