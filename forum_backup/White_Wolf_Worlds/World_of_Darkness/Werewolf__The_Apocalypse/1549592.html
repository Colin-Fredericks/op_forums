    Corax (Mechanical Deep Dive)

    03-27-2025, 07:18 AM
    Wereravens! The Corax are made, not born, and they are made to fulfil a mission from Raven. Corax are messengers, gathering and sharing information to help their own Breed, and others, to fight for Gaia. I'd argue that of all the Breeds, the Corax have perhaps stayed the most on-target. Ultimately they never pissed off the Garou, or any other Breed, enough to get genocided. Easy to dismiss this as dumb luck, but the Corax highly value wisdom and feel like a well-designed machine to do precisely their job. Reading through the Corax chapter you get a feel that Raven knew precisely what he was doing with these guys; if you imagine he had a limited number of points to assign, he made sure they were in the right places, and it's paid off.

    A brief mechanical overview:

    -1 difficulty to all Athletics checks (including dodging)
    +2 difficulty to soak Bashing damage
    Corax are able to see a corpse's life, either in a negative or positive light, by 'drinking' one of their eyes
    Shiny objects are very tempting to Corax, and they must overcome a Willlpower roll (difficulty 7) to avert their attention
    +1 to Subterfuge, Enigmas and Athletics

    Forms

    Corax have no Metis, and are made either from a suitable human, or from a suitable raven. If two Corax reproduce they simply create a normal human or raven, though such offspring can't become Corax.

    Simple and to the point, the Corax have three forms; Homid, Crinos and Corvid.

    Crinos gives +1 to all Physical Attributes and +3 to Perception, while Corvid gives -1 Strength, +1 Dexterity and +4 to Perception. Annoyingly we aren't told about any difficulty breaks they might have, akin to Lupus form, on perception rolls while in animal form.

    Also worth noting that all Corax begin play with Rage 1, Gnosis 6, and Willpower 3. This is exceptional; even if you want to play a horrifying war-raven who bathes in the blood of their enemies so that they may steal their secrets from the clues they leave behind in death, improving Rage is cheap, Gnosis is twice as expensive and harder to justify boosting. With a Gnosis of 6 you will have far better mileage with Gifts and Rites that cost Gnosis. In a mixed-breed Pack, such as those used by the Ahadi, chances are you'll slot in quite nicely as the one who guides the group across the Gauntlet and who handles certain spiritual matters.

    Gifts

    The Corax have one singular Gift list, and that's it. No division by Breed or Auspice or whatever; this is pretty on-brand. Raven seems to have designed these guys to do their job well, really well, and it does make me smile that this means they're extremely different from the other Fera. There's probably a lot of wisdom in this setup, and it seems to have served the Corax well, though perhaps their singular list could be a bit bigger!

    Garou Gifts on this list have entries in this thread:

    Garou Gifts: Ranking, Thinking, Overthinking - Onyx Path Forums
    https://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/main-category/main-forum/the-classic-world-of-darkness/werewolf-the-apocalypse-aa/1511902-garou-gifts-ranking-thinking-overthinking
    I'm going to break down Gifts by level and category, bit by bit. If it's Wyrmish, Fera-only, obscure or lost it's going to feature much much later, if at all. This is intended to focus on the Gifts you will actually be getting in play, hence starting at level 1 and working up. I'll be considering utility (both inside and


    Level 1

    Enemy Ways: This is basically Sense Enemies, and it's great. Perception + Primal-Urge, difficulty 7. Keep in mind that you get +4 Perception in your animal form! Enemies within Wisdom x 20 yards (so roughly 54 meters if you have a Wisdom of 3, for example, or 182 meters with a Wisdom of 10) are revealed to you; you know their location and their nature, with more successes giving more info.

    So, say, you're hidden inside a bush and you can hear horrific fighting as human guards are torn apart. This Gift tells you there are 6 Crinos-Form Garou at locations 1-6, and because you aced the roll the Storyteller reveals that they are dripping with infectious parasites and seem to be torturing the guards as they kill them. Alright, you're a wise little raven, sounds like a BSD pack have just busted into the place you were investigating, which is very interesting...

    Great Gift, highly useful, no cost, suits your job well and helps you to fight (know your enemy, ambush them one by one and so on) or flee, or any mixture of those things, far better. It also gives you plenty of info to give to a Garou. Even if you don't plan to play out Corax shenanigans "on-screen", as a Storyteller knowing that this Gift might be the source of something a Corax is telling the Garou PCs could help to inform what they know and what they don't, and add interesting tension. For example a Corax ally might know what the enemy is, but not what it looks like, since they never saw it directly.

    Morse: Spend 1 Gnosis and tap out a message; it's received by the nearest Corax. If you want to instead get a message to the nearest Gaian shapeshifter of any Breed (say the Garou down the road, your ally next door, or the random Ajaba asleep a few miles away) then this requires a difficulty 8 Wits + Expression.

    Normally the Gnosis cost would be tricky, but Corax start with 6 Gnosis. Not needing to make a roll to contact another Corax is great, and the roll to contact someone else is hard but doable. All in all this is pretty solid and can help you to send messages when it should be impossible, for example you could tap the bars of your cage with your beak or fingernail. This is very solid, and definitely worth getting, but the cost of the Gift combined with the difficulty of the roll (and let's be honest, if you're playing a Corax in a W20 game chances are your allies are nearby, and aren't all Corax, so difficulty 8 Wits + Expression it is!) makes this Amber.

    Open Seal: Very handy, especially with such a high Gnosis. See the Rank 1 Ragabash post.

    Persuasion: An incredible social Gift, total must-have, Green as always. See the Rank 1 Homid post.

    Raven's Gleaming: By looking at an object and spending 1 Gnosis, you can tell whether it's worth acquiring. This answer might relate to its cash value (a genuine diamond ring), or that it is generally useful (a well-made tool, a Fetish) or that fate or the plot of the game mean that the item will be relevant.

    This one is tricky to rate. You don't get any insight into WHY what you just picked up is valuable, and that to me feels like something that would be worth spending Gnosis on. Instead you focus on a single specific object and you get your answer. I would rework this Gift to be entirely passive; it lets you tell at a glance what objects are worthwhile having and which ones aren't.

    Then, if you pick up an object, spending 1 Gnosis would give you a rough idea of why it's important. "It's a Fetish", "It's important to a ghost", "It has something hidden inside".

    Scent of the True Form: Exceptionally useful. See the Rank 1 Philodox post.

    Spirit Speech: Always great to have. See the Rank 1 Theurge post.

    Truth of Gaia: Crucial to information-gathering; no point spreading around lies! See the Rank 1 Philodox post.

    Voice of the Mimic: Perfectly mimic any voice or sound you've heard; no roll, no cost. Absolutely buy this at the first possible opportunity.

    Word Beyond: Write a message in the Umbra which can only be understood by Corax.

    Wits + Expression, difficulty 6. 1 success gives a basic idea "safety this way", 5 successes can give a short essay. Note that this Gift's existence is a great thing to keep in mind even if you don't have it; the Word Beyonds left behind by other Corax are a relevant feature of the setting as a whole to you!

    The ability to leave messages behind in the Umbra is one of these things which is nice to benefit from, and boring to do yourself. Putting "danger, watch out!" in dangerous places in the Umbra is sensible and will save other Corax, but unless you're leveraging it to get more Wisdom or Honour Renown it won't make a big difference to your own play experience.

    "So when I was done grabbing the goods, I quickly set down a warning and flew off" - it sounds good, it's a nice thing to put in your report at the parliament, and it might get you more Renown. It might also help to bolster your reputation with other Corax. "I saw your note at the blah-blah Caern's nesting spot, good work" and so on.

    Immediately, this list has a load of stuff which is great for doing your job. To me, part of what makes the Corax good is that you could get by with just these Gifts and your natural talents. You don't need some ultra-powerful Gift at Level 3; if you grab all the Green Gifts here you're set, the rest is just a series of great bonuses.

      Level 2

      Carrion's Call: No cost, roll Perception + Empathy (difficulty 6), become aware of any cadavers within 1 mile per success.

      Pretty handy, feeds into the utility of your eye-drinking abilities, great for search and rescue (insofar as it tells you that the people you're looking for are either alive, or dead outside your radius), great for locating secrets (recently dead supernaturals are a great source of information, if only from searching their possessions and doing a bit of forensics).

      Given that you'll have +4 Perception from Corvid while using this, and it fits perfectly into the kinds of things PCs are often doing in WoD games (my son is missing, X and Y went off hunting and we haven't seen them for a few days, I wounded the bastard but he got away, he probably lay down to die in a ditch somewhere etc.) I'm giving this a tentative Green. The lack of cost, and the utility of what it tells you when it goes off and what it tells you when it doesn't... pretty neat. Even if you happen upon a recently dead human/supernatural who has nothing to do with the plot, the stuff you might learn from their body (killed by a Leech, killed by muggers, killed by disease) could have importance for the jobs of other Fera, who you can fill in with gossip next time you see them.

      Messenger's Fortitude: This version of the Gift is for flight, rather than running. See the Rank 2 Silent Striders post.

      I would say that this is slightly better, simply because as a Corax you're going to be flying significant distances on your own, even in a team game. Flying somewhere to impart a message then zipping back as fast as you can is just... part of what you add to the team! The Gnosis cost is also not too bad for a Corax. But the key problem remains that you can ONLY fly while using this Gift, you can't do anything clever en route, you're just flying as fast as you can tirelessly.

      Ultimately I'd call this slightly superior to the Garou version, due to the change from running to flight and the wider context of the Corax, but it's still sadly Red. Even if you're constantly doing long flights, Stamina and Athletics are great investments.

      Razor Feathers*: While in Crinos you may spend a point of Gnosis to unlock the Wing Swipe maneuver for the rest of the scene.

      Since you're in Crinos, remember that you could be using the Claw maneuver. Claw is difficulty 6, inflicts Strength +2 damage, and doesn't require a Gift. Wing Swipe is difficulty 7, inflicts Strength +2 damage, and requires activation of this Gift. Both do Aggravated damage. The main advantage of Wing Swipe is that your foe may be thrown off balance if they fail a difficulty 5 Dexterity roll, leading to a +1 difficulty on their actions during their next turn.

      That said, this Gift is useful as it converts the damage of two other Gifts, Hummingbird Dart and Bloody Feather Storm into Aggravated from Lethal.

      Generally speaking I'd say that using the Claw maneuver is superior. Lower difficulty and you don't need to spend Gnosis. That said, once you're Rank 3 this Gift becomes very useful for empowering Hummingbird Dart and making yourself a powerful ranged attacker. A solid Green, but only once you have Hummingbird Dart.

      Sky's Beneficience: This Gift allows you to drop something on a specific target or location whatever your altitude, without having to worry about the exact momentum or wind and so on. This could be anything from a message to an explosive. You just need to be able to see your target, and make a Perception + Melee (difficulty 7) roll.

      This is... alright? It's handy being able to drop something off without having to land, it's safer for you, and reduces the odds an annoyed Garou will rip you to shreds. Handy for pranks, and for ritual warfare, and for any little acts of terrorism you're eager to pull off. This is useful enough to be worth 6xp, but I wouldn't be in a rush to grab it.

      Speech of the World: See the Rank 2 Homid post. This is more useful for a Corax than it is for a Garou, but it remains a bit conditional; there are games where you won't need it at all.

      Swallow's Return: Spend 1 Gnosis and you'll fly home on autopilot, sleeping or healing as you travel. This is handy for traveling while you're grievously wounded and helps you keep up your 'stamina' on a trip. You don't need to worry about getting lost, and activating this before you're forced into a cursed sleep or similar might save your life.

      All in all it's a tad niche, and has an annoying overlap with Messenger's Fortitude. You can only go home, it has a Gnosis cost, and there are plenty of scenarios where if you need to fly home you'll just... do that. If this Gift had some other benefit, like circumventing anything that would be preventing you from getting home (the Gauntlet, insane distance, spiritual barriers) then that would be another thing.

      Taking the Forgotten: See the Rank 2 Ragabash post. If some Garou have a Fera relic, probably best they forget they ever had it; no good deed goes unpunished after all.

      Whisper Catching: See the Rank 1 Shadow Lords post. Great for capturing secrets to spread around. Combine with Truth of Gaia.

      Wire Sitter: Also known as Spider's Song. See the Rank 2 Ragabash post. The Gnosis cost which is typically annoying isn't much of an issue for a Corax, and since this feeds into your key role I'd argue that this is contextually bumped up to Green.

                  Level 3

                  Dead Talk: Now we have an even better use for Carrion's Call! If a corpse is less than 24 hours old you can interrogate it. Perception + Occult, difficulty 8, and the more successes you get the more 'talkative' the corpse is. We don't get told what kind of entity we're dealing with, but presumably the Gift simply evokes something approximating the dead individual to talk to you, rather than their actual factual Wraith or Ancestor Spirit or whatever.

                  Still, that raises questions. What if the corpse is of someone who would never answer you under any circumstances? Are they forced to be truthful? Are they forced to see you as someone they would like to talk to? The general lack of any clarity on how this Gift works means I'm leaving it unrated.

                  I would personally give clarifications like "it isn't the "true" soul of the target, it's based on what's in their recently killed brain, they're forced to answer truthfully and tell you what they believe from their own POV at the moment of their death, and they're probably a bit upset but it won't typically stop them from giving useful answers."

                  The real problem is that this has a Gnosis cost (fine) along with a difficulty 8 roll. So even if we had those clarifications... the Gift wouldn't be amazing. Difficulty 8, even in the best circumstances, is a real risk. But with the +4 Perception from Corvid you might be fine. If I were forced to give this a rating, based on my own assumptions of how the Gift is meant to work, I'd give it an Amber.

                  Eyes of the Eagle: Not to be confused with Eye of the Eagle (singular), the Lupus Garou Gift. Of course.

                  This Gift basically just lets you see through everything and anything, so long as it isn't an opaque object like a wall. So fog, clouds, shadows, falling leaves, none of them will impact your vision. Probably great for flying over bodies of water too! By default the Gift costs 1 Gnosis and lasts for a scene, but by adding 1 Willpower you can extend it until sunrise, so perhaps 24 hours.

                  Pretty handy, but could easily be a Level 2 Gift. That said ignoring clouds of darkness, fog, writhing tendrils of blood and so on in terms of vision is pretty great, especially when seeing stuff is so key to your job. The Gnosis cost barely matters with a base pool of 6, and the duration is great. All in all this is a very solid Amber.

                  Hummingbird Dart: My first ever exposure to W20 was, weirdly, reading W20 Changing Breeds. My first impression of a 'strong' Gift was looking at the Corax Gifts and seeing their limited array of ranged combat Gifts. Weirdly, I feel similarly now to how I did back then.

                  Yes, Garou and other combat-focused Fera have amazing combat Gifts, and amazing combat basekits, but it's hard to overstate how strong this is (even without bumping the damage up to Aggravated). You only spend 1 Rage, and for the rest of the scene you can attack by doing a Dexterity + Melee (difficulty 5!), up to 50 feet away, to hit with a (Dexterity + 3 + successes) damage feather. With a Dexterity of 5 (4, +1 from Crinos) your base damage on a 1 success hit will be 9 Lethal/Aggravated, no Strength investment required, and it's very likely you'll get more successes given that you are firing from 50 feet away, with a relatively good opportunity to hide or strike unawares. Aiming mechanics also work well, and when you aim your attack gains bonus dice for each turn of aiming, up to your Perception, so a maximum of +8 dice in Crinos.

                  But let's say you aren't aiming. Base of 9 dice of Aggravated. You can be doing this from a nearby rooftop or while gliding over the battle. There are lots of enemies that simply don't have an answer to this beyond "run away". Spirits will be fine, Fera with insane mobility will be fine, but all in all I really like this. It isn't 'busted', just nice and strong, and something you'd want to grab immediately. The Melee requirement is annoying, Athletics or Firearms would make more sense, but I can live with that. The fact that you can do this in your raven form is also pretty neat for assassinations.

                  Mynah's Touch*: Spend 2 Gnosis to 'store' the effect of a single Garou Gift of any Rank lower than yours. So, for example, at Rank 3 you could store the effect of any Level 1-2 Garou Gift. The writeup doesn't explicitly mention needing someone to borrow it from, but I'd argue this is strongly implied by the flavour text. We don't get told whether the person you copy must be willing, so that's a bit unclear.

                  This is infinitely useful... if you have Garou allies. But the sheer variety of Gifts you could borrow with this, especially at Rank 5, is staggering. Borrowing Mother's Touch from a Garou so you can heal them is a pretty good sample. Important to note that if a Gift is passive, you can release it when you wish and it will last for a single scene before disappearing. See the Garou thread for examples of strong Gifts to borrow in Green.

                  Scrying: See the Rank 3 Elder Brother post. Don't let this make you lazy! You should still fly places to check things out in person.

                  Sun's Guard: Spend 2 Rage and roll Stamina + Primal-Urge (difficulty 6). On a success you become immune to all forms of heat and fire, except for Balefire... for one turn per success.

                  One turn per success! I don't care about one turn per success! I want to be able to fly right through the heart of Helios' realm without suffering any damage! I want to be able to lure a powerful enemy into an industrial furnace and watch them melt! One turn per success sucks! And for 2 Rage, on my Corax, who might only have 2-3 Rage total!

                  If you're looking to fix this, remove the roll and make the Gift last for a whole scene. Easy.

                          Level 4

                          Attunement: Based on the Silent Strider version, so you either pick wilderness (Survival) or cities (Streetwise). This is a very strong Gift, and honestly I'd give it to Corax at Rank 3 since it suits their job so well, but it's very strong here anyways. See the Rank 3 Silent Strider post.

                          Airt Sense: A rare example of a Spirit Charm being available to Fera! I like this and wish we had one or two more examples of this.

                          The spirit has a natural sense of the “airts” (directions) of the spirit world, and can travel about without much difficulty. The spirit can create or find spirit tracks at will. The Storyteller rolls the spirit’s Gnosis if it has to locate a particular place or individual in the Umbra. Even spirits aren’t infallible, and a botch can lead them to an unforgiving Realm.

                          This gives you a very strong sense of 'direction' in the Umbra, which is more complex than 3D. Your senses might try to map it onto up, down, left, right, and so on, but there's more stuff 'stuffed' into the Umbra than fits into a 'map' as it were. Having a strong sense of spirit tracks, the ability to make your own, and the kenning to find your way into the realms of your choosing without any specialized rites of spiritual guidance is great. This is a pretty peerless Gift for Umbral exploration!

                          Bloody Feather Storm: This is another really strong combat Gift, on a Breed for whom combat is meant to be a relative weakness.

                          While airborne, spend 3 Gnosis and 2 Rage (!) to summon a storm of sharp feathers. Everyone in a cone beneath you, in a 15-foot radius (the height of the cylinder seems to be "everything below you", so if you're a mile up, this is 2.5 cubic miles, i.e. you could hit millions of spirits in Malfeas with this Gift) gets hit for Dexterity +4 dice of Lethal, and the dodge is both difficulty 8 and also requires 3 successes!

                          So, it's practically unavoidable (3 successes on a difficulty 8 dodge is a big ask), it deals up to 10 dice of lethal without a roll to hit, and it can very easily be made Aggravated. The cost is very high, absolutely. So, what do you use this against? Giant swarms of enemies, enemies who dodge very well, foes who have protections against attack rolls (e.g. +3 difficulty to attack, but you don't roll to attack with this Gift), and so on. Hummingbird Dart remains the superior option in most fights, but when this is good, it's going to be very good.

                          One thing I will say is that spending 3 Gnosis and 2 Rage just to roll 2 successes on damage REALLY sucks. So do keep in mind that 10 dice of Lethal (that's Dexterity 6) may be very good, but it's still going to give odd results. Sometimes you'll get 1 success, sometimes you'll get 9.

                          Flight of Separation: A 'murder' of ravens is mentioned, but the actual group noun is an unkindness, rave, treachery, or conspiracy. Crows have murders.

                          This Gift lets you explode into a whole flock of birds and fly off in all directions, which is a pretty neat power! This costs 2 Gnosis, and anyone trying to determine which raven is the real you must pass a Perception + Enigmas (difficulty of your Honour +3, maximum of 9).

                          Many, many foes in the World of Darkness won't have Enigmas. It's a Knowledge you expect in werecreatures, Changelings, certain Mages, and esoteric types among the other supernatural factions. A genius Pentex hunter may well have a Enigmas rating of 0, and the same may go for an all-mighty Technocrat. And if your Enigmas is 0, you're going to fail this roll by default.

                          All in all this is pretty handy - it's limited in the sense that you only need it to escape (well, typically) and that it costs 2 Gnosis, which will limit your escape somewhat in terms of Rage actions. But there are clever things you could do with this Gift like step sideways after using it and then wait, or hang around in Homid form while people chase after the ravens, then carry on with your investigation. The chaos and cover you create could also make a worthwhile distraction. Being able to go "I'll do X if you can catch me" to a spirit or Fera is also pretty neat for a Challenge!

                          This isn't a must-have but it's pretty cool and might save your life.

                          Gauntlet Runner: Gain the ability to roll Wits + Enigmas, difficulty 8, to lower the Gauntlet in a very small area. For every 2 successes the Gauntlet gets 1 point weaker in a 20 foot radius.

                          This is sadly terrible. The difficulty is too high, and 2 successes per point of change is too hard to pull off. You aren't going to get 4 or 6 successes on a difficulty 8 roll. Yeah, it has no cost, but in all the time you waste using this Gift you could be making multiple attempts to Step Sideways; this Gift is meant to be for swiftly stepping sideways!

                          Replace this with Reach the Umbra (see the Rank 5 Silent Striders post).

                          Kiss of Helios: Arrives a rank early! Helios loves these guys. See the Rank 5 Ahroun post.

                              Level 5

                              Deceptive Demise: Disappear in a cloud of feathers, leaving behind blood and ash. This is meant to help you fake your death, but I'm not sure how convincing the Gift sounds.

                              Sure, if you were in your bird form, and someone shot you, they'd be convinced you were WOUNDED by the feathers and blood. But ash? Where's the ash come from.

                              And if you were in homid? Hell, your Crinos form is big, where's that gone?

                              This Gift is okay, but not because of how it's meant to be used. This Gift allows you to spend 1 Gnosis and a health level to step sideways under any cirumstances, instantly. Reach the Umbra is better, for what it's worth. And this Gift doesn't even explicitly let you do it as a reaction when you get struck! Which is how you'd expect it to work, isn't it. Still, if you can't get Reach the Umbra, this Gift lets you step sideways against a difficulty of 10+, or in a place where it would normally be impossible, with no roll. That's neat. The cost is annoying, and Reach the Umbra would be better, but it's still neat.

                              Portents: Spend 2 Gnosis, roll Intelligence + Enigmas difficulty 8. More successes means more clarity in your vision of the future.

                              This is impossible to rate with any certainty, since it's so ST-dependent. But it's important to note that difficulty 8 sucks, and spending 2 Gnosis is very annoying. This is unrated, but it could very easily be Red.

                              Theft of Stars: Very cool name. With this Gift you completely rob natural light from a person, so that they can't see the light of the sun, stars, moon, biological sources of light, natural fire, and so on. Unnatural sources of light, like electrical lighting, or magic, remain.

                              So, this Gift lets you blind someone. That's... Level 1-3 Gifts territory. The mechanics aren't great either. You roll Willpower against the target, both difficulty 6, meaning that unless you're targeting someone with far worse Willpower than you this is going to be a 50/50 or worse. The duration is your Glory in hours. This might be neat at Level 1. Let's put it like that.

                              Thieving Talons of the Magpie: As you might expect, turns out this Gift originated with the Corax and was taught to the Garou. I wish we got this kind of lore more often, like if it turned out that Theurges only have Mother's Touch because the Gurahl agreed to teach it to the Garou under pressure, and later this turned out to not be enough; they wanted Grandmother's Touch and the Rites necessary to resurrect dead Garou etc. Anyways, this remains awful. 3 successes on a difficult roll simply isn't reliable, and you pay 1 Gnosis every turn to keep this going.

                                      The Corax do have their own unique Rites and Fetishes, but nothing else worth discussing here. If you want to create another Wereraven you're going to be spending some permanent Gnosis, and you need a witness who is of the opposite Breed (e.g. if you're human-born you need a raven-born ally to watch you do the Rite). It's more story-based, since a PC isn't often going to want to create another Corax (though "I recruited an extremely fitting human/raven into our society" might be a good cause to gain some Honour and Wisdom!).

                                      Final Verdict

                                      Comparing to a 'baseline' of the Garou...

                                      +++ Enemy Ways. It's that good! A "Sense Enemies" Gift is pretty amazing, especially at Level 1, and it's basically incomparable. That Gift alone gives you a pretty handy role which will outdo whatever Garou you've replaced, if we assume an all-Garou game with a single Corax for example.
                                      ++ Your Perception is peerless. With access to +4 Perception you'll have very few difficulties finding secrets and lies and hidden entrances and lost lore. A fair few Gifts use Perception.
                                      ++ You have inbuilt access to flight.
                                      ++ Reducing the difficulty of Athletics tests is pretty amazing given that this covers throwing things and Dodge!
                                      ++ Starting with a Gnosis of 6, and the option to leave your Rage at 1, is amazing. Getting this as a Homid is also pretty great; Garou may suffer with homi-lupus players, but you won't ever have to worry about that.
                                      ++ The Gifts you do have access to are excellent. Plenty of Greens, very few Reds.
                                      + You get various minor perks like eye-drinking.
                                      + Being vulnerable to Gold is an advantage over being vulnerable to Silver, since golden weapons are much more exotic and rare; while a Klaive-equivalent which uses gold instead of silver absolutely will exist, it's hardly going to come up in a typical encounter with Hunters or angry Garou.
                                      + Only having 3 forms is actually, in many ways, a pretty handy advantage. The successes needed to go from Homid to Corvid forms with a Primal-Urge roll are significantly reduced.
                                      + Most of the factions in Werewolf actually like you. Various Garou Septs and Tribes tolerate you far more than they tolerate any other Fera, almost every Fera Breed has some respect for you even if they find you very annoying, and you're generally trusted to serve Gaia by serving the other Breeds in their quests, bringing them news and warnings.
                                      + A free +1 to three different useful Abilities is pretty neat.
                                      + You may finding Ranking up to be slightly easier for you than it is for a Garou. Wisdom is relatively easy to farm and from what we can see Corax culture is relatively chill.

                                      - - - Your forms are very weak by the standards of WTA. You only get +1 Dexterity, most crucially, and next to no Strength or Stamina buffs. In a combat-heavy world like Werewolf you're absolutely the weak and frail member of the party, even if you optimize for combat.
                                      - - While your Gift list is amazing, it's somewhat limited compared to that of a Garou.
                                      - You are relatively weak to Bashing damage, which could really mess you up in some circumstances.
                                      - You don't really "come online" as a serious threat in combat until Rank 3, unless you invest in a ranged Fetish weapon, ideally a throwing weapon (Athletics) or a gun (Firearms). If you're going to be playing in a game with plenty of violence I'd consider something like a returning throwing knife Aggravated damage Fetish or similar to be a great investment for your Corax.

                                      All in all I'd say that if you're in a Beast Courts game, an average Corax might come out slightly ahead of an average Garou in overall playability and 'strength'. Access to the Court roles is a gamechanger for the Corax, since it gives them access to a LOT of excellent Garou Gifts they'd otherwise not get. In a different mixed-breed game... well it's apples and oranges. You're much much better at some things but you're far, far weaker in combat. You aren't directly comparable to Garou in most situations, and you'll get the best mileage out of your character if you act accordingly. "Corax who acts like a Garou" is an interesting concept for a Rank 1 character who is in for a rude awakening and lots of character growth, not a long term OOC plan!
