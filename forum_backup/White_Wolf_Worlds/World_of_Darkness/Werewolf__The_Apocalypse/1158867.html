    The crime of Captain Planet and protecting nature

    11-15-2017, 07:12 AM
    One of the parts in WtA that I love a lot is the focus on taking care of the environment and how modern society has hurt and still hurts it. Though, it is not blind to the fact that mindless destruction of factories and human hate aren't bad in themselves.

    So, you can imagine my confusion when one of the biggest complaints against WtA is the focus on nature and protecting it. I've seen multiple online games tote that they aren't 'Captain Planet with furries'.

    Now, I get that if things get a bit too simple, the game loses its fangs and purpose. I am guessing my own lack of watching the show might affect things as well. I watched Bamboo Bears and nature documentaries instead.

    But it still, often it baffles me when people push back against the environmental message of WtA with such fervour.

          Gotta be honest, I always thought that part of WtA charm was indeed the fact that you're given a setting where you can get down to fight to protect the environment in a way that's candidly direct. Now, I'm not saying that the game is one where there are no shades of grey and all things are easy, "perfectly good VS totally bad", but merely that to me having a central conflict which from a general perspective is so refreshingly clear while still having the tool the explore the nuances of, say, fanaticism and blind hate, is a big reason why I love the game.

          Apocalypse takes one of the issues of the world I wish I could do something tangible about and allows me to care for it as an awesome shapeshifter fed by rage fighting evil corporations and inhuman monsters, which is and always will be awesome. Let's not lie to ourselves about that.

          I never watched Captain Planet either and I watched tons of documentaries too as a kid, so perhaps that's a factor, but I feel it's all due an old problem with fanbases in general: to reduce things to a couple of words.Either because people find them easier to explain or because they never liked something in the first place and could not be bothered to admit there's more to it than they cared to explore, that's something that tends to do more harm than good. I mean, every single gameline discussed on this forum suffered from it and I think we can all remember at least one occasion where those tiresome "I think this game is just this, how dare you like it?" threads pop up and we got to defend what we like and explain the aspects of the game we care about over and over.

          It's a baggage that comes with popular properties, especially those who have been around for a while. Which is dumb, simplistic and harmful to both old and new fans, but I don't see it going away any soon.

                                      Cartoons have a history of problems when it comes to speaking to children, all too often assuming they are stupid or talking down to them. But Captain Planet in particular sits in a very strange point in the history of American television animation when cartoons had been all but driven out of daytime network television and channels like Nickelodeon weren't yet situated to fill the gap. So there was this unique moment that a man (Ted Turner) with a vanity project (Captain Planet) and his own cable channel (TBS) could exploit to reach a massive number of kids, but he had to work quickly because other companies like Disney were already moving to fill in the gap. And exploit it he did. I know in my area, Captain Planet was the only thing on in its time slot and I suspect that was the case across most of America. That lack of competition is probably why so many people saw and remember the show.

                                      But because they had to move fast (and also because Ted Turner was working with DiC Entertainment) there was no time to make the show varied or complex, so instead they used basic cartoon tropes to rush out something that was catchy and inculcated a general sense of environmentalism.

                                      Anyways, that's probably too in depth (although, hell, it's a good seed for a story about Glass Walkers vs Pentex fighting over the fate of an environmentalist television show) but it shows a bit of why Captain Planet is what it is.
