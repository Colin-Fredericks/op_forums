    The Curse and the Glass Walkers.

    01-18-2023, 10:44 PM
    Hi! For some time I've wondered about this. Glass Walkers Revised TB says:

    "Think about it for a second. What role do most tribes offer their Kin? Financial support for the caern? We’re a tribe that spent the last couple of decades run by those with corporate interests, and even if our budget isn’t unlimited, we’ve no gripping need of donations from our Kinfolk. Or how about sending Kinfolk to the cities to keep an eye out there? No good, we’re in the cities, and sending Kin into the wilderness is exactly the garbage duty most so rightfully hate. Acting as liaisons with humanity? Ever since we were the Warders of Men, that’s been what we do. We’re the tribe that deals with humans better than anyone else, and we can’t afford to let our people skills slip."
    ​
    The character templates in the same book give me a hint to how this is possible despite the Curse. Ragabashs and Theurges are the ones working in companies to make money and in office buildings full of Kinfolk to make a buffer between Garou and humans (Philodoxes gotta work with tougher people, like cops as in the TB's example), or perhaps all employees are Kinfolk (at least those who interact with other businesspersons representing the Garou CEO to other companies or they would be just co-workers to the Garou). Otherwise the Curse would make the company unstable due to employee problems (Galliards and Ahroun are a no-no). At Rank 4 Glass Walkers have the solution to this due the Bury the Wolf Gift and there's also a fetish for that, but the thing it's a level 4 one, not easy or common at all to have laying around.

    So I ask for tips to make the typical Glass Walker business person, approaching each auspice, considering the Curse as described in W20 and the Kinfolk quote from the Glass Walker Revised Tribebook. Kinfolk Unsung Heroes defines the Kinfolk role under a more positive light, they help the Glass Walkers a lot with their disguise, but Kinfolk20 agrees with the TB Revised. If you think Galliards and Ahrouns couldn't do that just express yourself as such.

    I thank you all who contribute here, see ya!

      Something that might be useful to consider in the modern age is whether you/your ST rules the Curse as being active across technological barriers (phone calls, recorded messages, zoom, etc.). Furthermore this post isn’t made with the mechanics of particular gifts in mind, but rather just off the top of my head thematically.

      So what we’re assuming here is that we’re dealing with a standard sort of Glass Walker who views being able to interact with the humans as part of their tribal purview (although I have to imagine that some would be more concerned with the larger infrastructure of humanity rather then individuals, which is an area that the Children of Gaia are also notably involved in). That says though, let’s consider how some of these Children of Cockroach might interact with humans in a daily setting.

      Ragabash are the auspice that have the most wiggle room imo when it comes to how they go about fulfilling their spiritual obligations (although it may also be noted that the predominantly Homid tribe, given that homid’s start with Gnosis 1, might not be as concerned with spiritual obligations and taboos as some other tribes). Ragabash are able to interact with the largest section of humanity due to their starting Rage and they come equipped with multiple gifts which help them influence social settings. I imagine that the Glass Walker Ragabash are the ones who are assigned the most socially dependent roles in human-affairs. The Ragabash are the ones sweet-talking clients, shmoozing at parties, doing prep work on deals, etc. Their is also a significant number of them involved with corporate espionage and similar affairs.

      Theurges have two things going for them imo. They have the second best ability to interact with humans* on a rage level. The reason I qualify that statement is because of their other quality. They are also the ones who work with spirits the most. It’s their obligation more then any other Glass Walkers to handle the other side of life, the spiritual. This spreads to every aspect of life. They protect the tribe’s assets with wards and guardians, they can bring prosperity by forming alliances with the right spirits, they can damage rivals in the same way, they can use spirits to not only provide information but to theoretically do any other thing that you might imagine. This is all phenomenally useful. The issue is that it doesn’t come for free. The theurges must either run roughshod over the spirits or negotiate and offer chiminage. Individuals who have chiminage with multiple spirits may become quite eccentric and this may easily interfere in their ability to operate socially with outsiders. Thus I imagine many GW Theurges are people who offer support from relative isolation or in positions that are allowed their eccentricities; they may be executives, skilled labor (Ex: programmers), creatives, or any other role which will not meddle with their spiritual obligations.

      Philodox are on the edge between those who can be reasonably considered to be social with humanity and those who cannot. The Philodox also have a particular set of skills and instruction to help reinforce their ability to use them. They can keep the camp running smoothly. I imagine the Philodox filling the roles of managers, Human Resources, counter-espionage, and negotiation. Their traditional role as ‘peace leaders’ and their training for leadership and mediation helps them fulfill managerial tasks (although the Curse is a mixed blessing in this respect; few Philodox managers are loved by their workers, but they are taken seriously). The Philodox can also be expected to keep tabs of those within their circle and monitor them for any signs of friction or unusual activity, making them prime fodder for HR or Counter-Espionage. With regards to negotiation, I imagine this will be a case by case basis. In some cases the light-touch of a Ragabash will be better suited. In some cases the no-nonsense Philodox will be the better candidate.

      The Galliard is where things begin to get somewhat dicey with regards to humanity. Their Curse can be trusted to disturb most people, but that doesn’t mean the Galliard doesn’t have roles they can fulfill. For one, depending on your interpretation of the Curse, their tendency to be charismatic and skilled performers can make them valuable public faces. Imagine a Steve Jobs-esqe figure, a charismatic figurehead who is feared and loathed by those who have to work directly with them. I can also imagine the Galliard fulfilling Garou-facing work, carrying out the order of the tribal leaders and making sure that the Glass Walkers are all on the same page. For most Galliard GW, the path will likely be that of one of keeping their fellow Garou on good terms or in some creative field where they can be kept isolated from the masses of humanity.

      the Ahroun are the least likely to be accepted by the rank and file humans and thus have to be handled delicately. Some GW operations might not have them interact with humans at all, which effectively creates the kinfolk job of ‘Ahroun Handler’ (which they’re only somewhat less uneasy about then a human would be). In the occasions where Ahroun do get a position on the mortal stage, it will probably be in very particular scenarios. Want to give the implication that some of your group’s silent partners are on another side of the law? Ahroun mobster. Want to have your company operate an internal security force? It’s managed by the Ahroun, who are the hardest motherfuckers there. Are you in an area where you might be interacting with local warlords or generals for some reason? Ahrouns may be the ones you want representing your interests. More than any other auspice though, the Ahroun is an auspice to be handled with care around humanity.

      Hope this helped and that you get the info you’re looking for!

                          Writers of the source material often seem to forget about the Curse and its impact on the setting. Regardless, we still need an idea of how the Glass Walkers can build influence in mortal society.

                          In my chronicles, the Glass Walker Garou tend to stay within private businesses and surround themselves with kinfolk. Privately owned businesses can still become big and rich, but tend to get less scrutiny. Since they also start small, it's easier to grow the business from a basis of kinfolk only (primarily their own tribal kin, but also including Bone Gnawer clan and to a lesser degree open to other tribes as well). When they do need to hire outsiders, they can make sure they stay at unimportant levels and don't interact with the "bosses" or they can stick them in some annex or remote office doing support work that never brings them in contact with the main office where the Garou might be.

                          Their businesses also tend to be local - based in one city or with regional offices. They aren't national so they don't have to hire many non-kinfolk who may get promoted or begin to ask questions on why they aren't being given opportunities. They also cooperate substantially with other Glass Walker or kinfolk owned enterprises, limiting the contact with outsiders. If they need financing, they go to some bank that already has Glass Walkers in it. If they need financial audits done they use kinfolk accountants. Such businesses also make it less likely for vampires, PENTEX, or other enemies to attempt to exploit or take over those enterprises because they seem less viable.

                          In some type of organizations, it isn't necessary for the Garou owners to mix with ordinary people. If you own a restaurant, you can have one kinfolk manage it, and it won't matter how many humans he has working for him. If you own a chain, most locations may not even need any kinfolk and run completely independently. A Garou who owns a local pizza chain may only need to staff kinfolk at the main office and maybe one actual location (if he really wants to pick up some pizza himself). If you own a local bank with various branch locations, most of those branches could be staffed completely with humans who never come in contact with "the board" or "the investors". You can do the same with real estate and various other businesses. That's also a good way to network with kinfolk of other tribes - some isolated kinfolk only village of the Fianna might appreciate the opportunity to work in a local bank branch (owned by the Glass Walkers) that can provide them a source of loans and investment while the Glass Walkers can expand and make good contacts.

                          And of course, high rage Garou might choose to enter careers that are filled with dangerous people. That's probably one reason the Glass Walkers are known to be involved in organized crime. In a normal business, you don't want people terrified of you or think you are a psycho killer. If you work in the criminal underworld, that reputation may be beneficial. There are also occupations where it isn't necessary to have much social interaction with people.

                          So if possible keep it kinfolk only. If not, surround the Garou with kinfolk, and keep the humans off site as much as possible. If the business gets bigger, make sure kinfolk are always at key positions to act as gatekeepers. It's all about limiting the exposure of humans to Garou, especially high rage Garou.

                              As people have covered it's probably a mixture of small businesses being staffed primarily by kinfolk, larger businesses having the Garou far enough upstairs that they don't have to interact with the normal humans, and the low-Rage Garou being the ones actually doing a lot of the business.

                              A lot of the issues come from the fact that the time of 1e was messy, and they were still working things out (Klaives didn't exist in the earliest days!). The writers wanted werewolves to have difficulty interacting with humans, they wanted a Tribe of high tech Garou who run businesses, and nobody sat down and thought about how they'd interact. The simple answer would be to offload a lot of the work to Kinfolk, but the GW have terrible Garou-Kinfolk relations. So we have to assume that a lot of them keep their Rage low or move into skilled professions where it is much less of an issue.

                                              I use several guidelines with the Curse to incorporate it as an important part of the game, but not utterly paralyze things.

                                              First, I use the traditional Willpower percentages table of Werewolf to reflect the entire population. A lot of people with very low Willpower will be small children and the elderly. They don't reflect the typical interaction with adults you find on the city streets. So if a Garou walks downs the city street, then a small child will cling to their mother for protection, but the mother will think it is more an issue with the child than the stranger the child seems to fear. If a Garou enters a bar, there may not be anyone impacted by the Curse even if according to the Willpower table there should be people who are impacted. In contrast, if the Garou enters a grammar school or retirement home, most of the people there would be affected even by a low Rage Garou.

                                              Second, I do interpret reactions of people affected by the Curse liberally. They do sense danger, but how they react to it depends. Some of them might think they are just being irrational and try their best to ignore it. Others might remove themselves from the situation. Others will just begin to keep their eyes on the person to see what they do. A cop might just encourage the Garou to keep moving. A storeowner may go up to the Garou and see how they can help them... so they can leave the story as soon as possible. Somebody else might start complaining the Garou is "looking at them" and try to get someone who will help them get the Garou to leave. My goal is to acknowledge the Curse in game as a flavor text, but not cause the scene to end or frustrate what the player is trying to do.

                                              Third, I keep in mind that many NPCs will spend their 1 point of WP to ignore the impact of the Curse for that scene. They don't want to show they are scared, panic, break ranks, etc. just because they have an irrational feeling.

                                              Fourth, I also use a House Rule that the Garou can spend 1 WP to "bury the wolf" for a scene. So if they want to meet someone at a public restaurant, we can have that scene and not have the Curse ruin what could be fun for the game.

                                              Fifth, I remind players that this is a good reason to have the Kinfolk background. Maybe it isn't the actual PC who goes out and does that stuff. It is one of their Kinfolk they go with the Kinfolk background. Have them develop that Kinfolk as a real person. That way the player can always be active, even if their Garou PC sits out that scene.

                                              I tend to try to keep Garou with Rage 5 or lower (all starting characters) to be able to accomplish most things they may want to do. The Curse is more flavor text than anything, but with the understanding that sometimes the PC may need to spend some effort to mitigate the circumstances. However, if the PC wants to increase Rage to 6 or higher, then I work with them in advance so they can make the necessary preparations for the changes in their life that it will require. A Garou with Rage 8 should definitely be leading to very different scenes when they interact with the Flock.
