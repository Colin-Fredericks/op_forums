    Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
    But for all that, I do think Uktena and Wendigo hold a very important role in WtA. They remind you that there are people out there who were hurt and you should not ignore that.
    I always get frustrated in these sorts of discussion because this sorts of comments get really strange. The Black Furies and Bonegnawers are all about people that were hurt too.

    It always feels weird that people attach specific Tribes special importance towards themes that aren't really unique to those Tribes. It also gets odd that specific historically oppressed groups get multiple Tribes, while others are all just shoved into the general Tribes like the Gnawers or CoGs.

    The message of the game gets muddy with such uneven things. If I want to deal with indigenous peoples' history in the Americas, I have two Tribes specifically to that issue. If I want to do with in Europe, or most of Asia, there's no Tribe focused on that (at least unless you use the BNS Wendigo expansion); at best I have the Siberakh. The Silent Striders are extremely focused on Egypt and then the Rom, making all the North African non-Arab peoples left out in the cold in representation (so they end up mostly Gnawers). It's really easy to miss the "remember all the people out there that have been trampled upon." when the games put a lot of focus on some peoples and virtually ignore others.

    Often they also serve as a way to notice when a game has some...issues with pocs as well. Banning them, the Striders and Stargazers is a very good indication that the game runner needs to check their views on race.
    This feels... presumptive. That's actual part of my issue with the every ethnic focused Tribes. If I'm running a game set in Turkey, why shouldn't I ban Stargazers (a tiny Tribe in a place without a major south east Asian population), the Wendigo, and the Uktena from the game? It seems a pretty straightforward issue of verisimilitude one which Tribes would be in that part of the world rather than an issues I have with my views on race. With highly ethnically associated Tribes, there's going to be settings they don't make sense in because those ethnic groups aren't really prominent there.

    And I'm glad that the writers grew up and wrote the tribes much more nuanced in later editions.
    Definitely agree that I appreciate it; even with the stumbles that occurred.

    Originally posted by WISDOMINCHAINS View Post
    Agreed this whole freaking out over it drives me nuts
    Eh?

    Complaints about the "ethnic" Tribes in WtA has been going on for decades, because there's always been an odd tension between the more conceptual Tribes that might have some ethnic groups they're associated with, and Tribes that are extremely focused around specific ethnic groups which frequently got weird. The various camps on this and how/if to fix it are pretty old.

                                                            We aren't asking to remove In Character racism. The Get and the Black Furies are still going to think poorly of the other tribe as a whole. We just want the more ethnic tribes to be presented without racial stereotypes.

                                                            Let's use an example that removes racism from the picture. The change to the Glasswalkers mentioned above? In Character, all the other tribes see Glasswalkers as Weaver loving cowards that live in their mother's basement playing with computers and guns all day. That is a stereotype that Glasswalkers have to live with.

                                                            However, Glasswalkers themselves are a diverse group of individuals who understand the Weaver is a Tool that can be used to defeat the Wyrm and control the spread of the Weaver. They understand how dangerous the Weaver is. They have more experience with the trait than any tribe.

                                                            Thus out of character, the Glasswalkers aren't Weaver loving bastards. They aren't a walking stereotype, but a tribe of individuals with an unique view point.

                                                            That is all we are asking for. The Garou Nation could still wrestle with Sexism and Racism, but the books themselves present all the tribes in a respectful manner.
