    W5 Red Tundra - Review

    07-03-2024, 06:46 AM
    Red Tundra is a W5 adventure that was released by Renegade for Free RPG Day. It’s supposed to be an introductory adventure people can run at a game-store to familiarize people with W5. That’s what it’s supposed to do.

    To lay my cards on the table right up front: It doesn’t achieve that goal. It’s clear the writer has only a passing familiarity with WtA at best but worse than that… it’s badly written. Reading through the adventure reminded me of sitting through truly bad movies like Morbius or Gods of Egypt… these bad movies where characters act weird and things happen in ways that make little sense or it’s hard to fathom what rhyme or reason is underlying any of the creative decisions. Maybe the writer was rushed or something in their personal life distracted them but the adventure is just… bad. This is the worst one of the ones I’ve reviewed thus far, I think.

    The book starts with an introduction of the setting to familiarize everyone with W5. It introduces the idea of werewolves and Garou in its basics as well as the Auspices with one or two sentences. But the most interesting aspect of the Introduction is the section called “The Apocalypse Is Now”. I and many others had discussions on this forum about the state of the world in the W5 setting. And it’s interesting to put the “Age of Apocalypse” in the context of V5’s idea of a “cyclical Gehenna”. Here, though, this all gets dispelled in a way that I found almost too matter-of-fact.

    What the section describes is that the Apocalypse in W5 is the actual physical and spiritual destruction of the world. It’s the literal end of the world. And you get descriptions about that like “It’s not a question of faith.”. But where it gets weird is when you get a section like “Sometimes the Garou call this the war against the Wyrm, but the true cause of the destruction is not in the spirit world. It’s right here. The only question is, what can be done about it? This torments the Garou. They can kill a corrupt cop possessed by a Bane, a Wyrm Spirit. But can they stop a vast corporation from logging in the Amazon?”. That almost makes it seem like caring about the Umbra, the Triat and Spirits is a distraction and Garou instead should just deal with humanity directly in some fashion.

    Then we get the introduction to the setting and the core-concept of this adventure. The setting is a small town in Northwestern Canada called Winter Creek. But this is where the strange/bad writing starts to appear. So the idea is that all the PCs are from this area and the human-born characters all went to the same school. But because the town is suffering from massive unemployment, they all left for the big city – but now they’re coming back. Also, there are wolfborn characters who stayed behind but somehow have fully integrated into human society. But also in the past they all joined the one Pack that operated in the city called the Lunabombers (terrible name) who is led by this hyper-aggressive Red Talon Ahroun called Marcus Oakheart. But before the PCs left for the big city, they also had a falling-out with Oakheart and founded their own Pack called… The Band (again, another terrible name). And now they’re back because… I have no idea. They’re just back. And the wolfborn Pack-members also supposedly never left but somehow are still part of the Pack… Look, it makes no sense. But the idea is that the PC-Pack has some history with the region, they left and now they’re back.

    The book comes with six ready-made characters who are the PC-Pack (two of whom are wolfborn) and oh boy… what a group. But I will get to that at the end.

    Then the book introduces the most important characters in the adventure. And the one character-description that should be highlighted here is Tucha. This is what it says in the description: “She is a builder and a problem-solver, making nests and even cleverly disarming simple traps with her claws.” The thing is… I left out the beginning which says that this is supposed to describe a wolf Kin before its First Change. And then it says stuff like “She will appear in the form of a black-haired woman in her thirties and her understanding of human society is based on past observations as a wolf.”. Also the image that goes along with this is of a black woman in her thirties with dreadlocks, tattoos and earrings… which doesn’t really fit the idea of what’s described. The drawing looks way too much like a person who’s already fully part of human society.

    In the description of Marcus Oakheart we also find out that he has fallen to Hauglosk which means… he’s really grumpy and angry. And the only other member of his Pack that is mentioned is a Red Talon Philodox named Jana Goodhowl. Again, just a weird character. This is what it says in her description: “Ironically, her habit of avoiding conflict is perhaps what has kept her so close to Marcus Oakheart even after his descent into Hauglosk: she is afraid to confront his extreme views and to remind him of the tenets of the Litany.” And what makes it even weirder is how she appears in the adventure but we’ll get to that…

    Before, Hauglosk was strongly tied to rightwing extremist beliefs which was odd but here it’s another kind of odd. Because the way it’s described during the adventure is that the Lunabombers-Pack-members and the PC Pack… they all know he’s suffering from Hauglosk but they all just accept it with a shrug a la “Hey, what can you do…?”. It’s accepted that he will make bad decisions because of Hauglosk but nobody feels compelled to deal with it beyond knowing that this will happen. It feels like it’s treated like a kind of dementia, some sort of mental illness that nobody can do anything about. The best you can do is operate around that Garou’s erratic behavior. I guess, it’s closer to how it’s described in Deepest of Wounds where the Cult of Fenris members may have different opinions but there’s no open hostility between them and the other Garou (at least until the kidnapping is uncovered). And if Oakheart and Jana don’t die during this adventure, they are willing to work together with the PC Pack if they want to stay (although on “strained terms” as the book describes it).

    Chapter One

    The PCs arrive in town and to showcase taking tests, the first scene that is set up here is… to evade the Pastor. Because he’s annoying. But anyway, besides that the real goal at this point is for the characters to meet up with coach MacKenzie. Remember how I mentioned that all the human-born characters went to the same school? One assumption in the adventure is that all the human-born characters REALLY care about this coach from their highschool. In fact, the adventure makes the coach the Touchstone for all the human-born characters. (Even though part of the premise is that the human-born characters had left the small town for the big city…)

    The characters go to the meeting-place but… BAM! Marcus Oakheart in Crinos Form attacks the PC-Pack. Somehow nobody’s around, so the Veil stays intact… yeah, “somehow”, sure… There’s a fight but after two rounds Jana Goodhowl appears and calms Oakheart down. She has a special Talisman whistle, you see, that can end Frenzy and the Spirits blessed her with being super-persuasive or something. It’s weird. The Talisman is called “Dogwhistle of Control: Blowing in this whistle calms any targeted Garou for a turn, no matter how consumed by Rage or Hauglosk, forcing them to consider their actions and listen to reason. [ … ] The talisman has been granted by the spirits to Jana Goodhowl specifically. It won’t cooperate with anyone else.” As a side-note, the Marcus and Jana have Gifts listed for them and the text is copy-pasted from the W5 corebook. The problem is that Gifts rely on specific Renown traits and neither of these two has their Renown-traits listed. So, here for example she has the Ancestral Conviction Gift which adds Honor as bonus dice to any attempt to convince other Garou. But it’s nowhere listed what Jana’s Honor-rating is. The same happened with Marcus Oakheart and his Gifts. It’s a bit of an oversight.

    The following dialogue between Jana, Marcus and the PCs suggested by the adventure is also weird. Here’s a snippet:
    “There’s no reason to spill Garou blood tonight.”, Jana says as much to you as her leader. “Try to remember, Marcus and the rest of you as well. These are the tenets of the Litany: Respect the territory of another, the Veil shall not be lifted and the leader may not be challenged during wartime. So please, what are you doing here?”
    You aren’t sure if this is wartime and Oakheart is not your leader but perhaps that is a debate for another time.
    Personally, I would’ve immediately called bullshit on this because Marcus attacked the PCs. The fault lies entirely with him.

    And then these two Garou are giving you exposition. There’s a company called the FOMO Hunting Company and they’re killing wolves in the region. They organize hunting-trips for rich people as well to shoot wolves from helicopters. And then they ask the PC-Pack to help them with the attack. The turn-about happens really fast. First, Marcus attacks the PCs, then Jana shows up and acts like the PCs and Marcus are equally to blame and then they try to ally themselves with the PCs while giving an exposition-dump about who the bad guys are. It’s not a good scene…

    Also, wouldn’t it be kinda hard to shoot moving wolves while being in the air in an also moving helicopter? It sounds more like a challenge than something you’d wanna do for fun.

    Or take Jana’s characterization… just a couple pages ago it said she’s afraid of confrontation and won’t mention the Litany to Oakheart and here in the very first scene she’s doing exactly that. If anything, this scene would make me think Jana is the Pack leader and not Oakheart.

    Then the characters proceed to the Silver Saloon which is the local bar where they’re supposed to meet the coach. He isn’t there yet and the book offers options for the characters to investigate what’s up. The one option that stood out to me here is the question of “Is it legal to kill wolves?”. And the adventure offers this 200-word sidebar giving a detailed answer on the real-world situation. But this is WtA… of course, it’s bad and has to be stopped. I mean, what, are the PCs supposed to think “Hey, how about we draft a lawsuit against the corporation instead of attacking the Fomori working for them?”. It’s so hard for me to imagine that in this setup, a player would organically wonder whether what the evil villain is doing is legal. It’s the villain! Of course, it’s not supposed to be a good thing! And you’re never going to deal with a Fomor-boss by dragging them to a court to convict them! That’s not what WtA is about…

    So you meet the coach except… he’s not a coach anymore. He lost his job and is now working for… FOMO Hunting Company! Oh no! You can interrogate this character but it’s a bit weird because he doesn’t have that much new information to offer. And other things you can learn in different ways later on. You can even try to convince him to just call in sick the next day as you go after the company. It’s assumed that the PCs should be very invested in keeping the coach safe.

    Chapter Two

    The adventure now has a distinct Day 1 and Day 2 for dealing with the company. Chapter Two deals with Day 1 (which is also the day before the hunt happens) and this is the best part of the adventure because it explicitly explores various routes the characters can take in making Day 2 easier for themselves while also making sure innocents have been protected/forewarned.

    But the weirdest part is the one route where the PC Pack contact the Trout Lake pack. Because you see, this adventure seems to believe that wolves have human-level intelligence. You’re going to the Lake Trout pack as one of the options to warn them that they’re going to be the target of the hunt the next day. And it’s described like this: “And then you can already smell one of their guards, a young male. You howl a little to make your presence known and soon you hear a reply that you should prepare to meet Nahanni. The leader walks majestically on a hilltop, the moon illuminating her outline. She looks down upon you and growls “Why have you come?” “. And then you can only communicate with Nahanni in Lupus or Hispo Form. Because the description I just quoted there isn’t for wolfborn Garou but for normal wolves! They even have names!

    Then there’s a sidebar on Kinseeking. As mentioned before, there’s this character called Tucho who is a wolf-kin in that pack. And the sidebar says stuff like “But they can try to force Tucho’s First Change by attacking her or by changing form in front of her..” Also, PCs can just sniff her out or use the Gift Sense The True Form. That isn’t the case elsewhere. There’s one specific Rite in the corebook to enable the ability to detect Kin. But the way it’s handled here, you wouldn’t even need the Rite.

    Chapter Three

    This is the day when the hunt happens and the book really let’s itself off the hook here. You get an hour by hour play-by-play of what would happen on that day if the PCs had done nothing and weren’t around. Essentially Oakheart and the Lunabombers attack a Lodge, kill some people, the Fomori take off in a helicopter and kill the wolves at Lake Trout. Oakheart and his Pack retreat, licking their wounds and mourning their failures. And then you just get this section called “What Really Happens” which gives a couple examples of what may change due to what PCs had done in the previous chapter and how they could influence the described events. It’s VERY free-form.

    And then it just goes to the conclusion and aftermath. If Oakheart is still alive, you can start a Sept between the PC-Pack and the Lunabombers. And I guess, Oakheart’s Hauglosk is just… not a big deal. Also, somehow the PCs attack against the FOMO hunting company gets it in trouble. And if the coach is still alive, he’s out of a job again because of that.

    The PCs

    You get six ready-made characters and the book even occasionally mentions how they could be relevant in particular sections of the adventure. All well and good but these guys… that’s one hell of a group.

    Especially because whoever filled out the char-sheets must have read the character-creation-section with their eyes closed.

    First, the characters are imbalanced. Some are stronger than others. You have one, for example, with 16 dots in Attributes in total and then another with 11. Second, the characters are badly designed for new players. Normally you have three Gifts and one Rite at character-creation. There’s one character with three Gifts and five Rites. The other have around six Gifts and Rites usually. Third, there are straight up mistakes being made. One character has a Spirit Pact Background with zero dots in it, for example. No explanation as to what it’s even supposed to represent.

    It's a mess!

    But let’s talk about the character-concepts itself:

    Shelley “Moonshadow” Le Borgne (human born Shadow Lord Galliard): She plays ice hockey. And she had her First Change during a game. It says “If not for the ice skates and the padding, she might have murdered both teams.”… Not sure, what exactly happened here. Also, part of her concept is that she plays ice hockey by day and “by night she prowls the streets, teaching the songs of the moon to other werewolves.” So why would she go back to Winter Creek…? Who knows…

    Darius Van Detta (wolf born Black Fury Theurge): He constantly fought with his wolf-pack before his First Change and so he was driven out. Then he fought with human hunters and realized the Wyrm was the true enemy. That’s when his First Change happened and now he’s somehow really knowledgeable about spirits.

    Ida “Ratbrain” Fong (human born Bone Gnawer Ragabash): She was an obedient straight-A student but she was always curious. She became a journalist and that’s when she realized just how bad the world around her was. That’s when her First Change happened. But I just love the last line of the description. So weird! “On the way, she has also become a disillusioned big city drug addict who has drifted more and more into marginalia with her strange opinions and attitudes.” ‘Disillusioned big city drug addict’… what a phrase!

    Ashley “Fernfur” Greeneyes (wolf born Hart Warden Ahroun): Another weird wolf-description here… “Ashley grew up a wolf, one of those wolves who loved the territory of their pack and did their utmost to protect its flora and fauna.” Ah, so that’s what wolves are up to, I see. But now I really wanna meet “one of those wolves” that don’t like their territory… Anyway, her territory got attacked, she underwent First Change and… attacked the humans in her new human Form. Then it says “Humans recognized her as Indigenous and she adopted the name of Ashley Greeneyes.” The phrasing is just so odd. They recognized her as “Indigenous” (?) and her name is “Ashley”…?

    Jonas “Sparks” Beaulieu (human born Glass Walkers Philodox): He likes machines. That’s why he’s a Glass Walker. But what about him being a Philodox, you may wonder… “After he changed, he felt a strange need to delve deep into the history and customs of the Garou as well as the laws of Canada, the territories and the reserve, becoming an impromptu arbitrator in many disputes.” I think this takes Auspice a bit too literal… You don’t need to become a lawyer just because you had your First Change under the half-moon.

    Jared Tierney (human born Galestalkers Ahroun): It starts like this “Jared may not have the book smarts but he has heart.” What is the concept? Well, it all revolves around him having a dark little secret… He killed his entire family during his First Change and then ran away. As a concept, it’s way too dark in my opinion. The only bright spot is that if there’s room, this is the last character anyone would wanna pick from the six pre-made ones. The reason is simple: He has the weakest char-sheet by far compared to the other ones. Four of his attributes have 1 dot and only one Attribute is a four-dot. It’s like the author hated this character or something.

    Conclusion

    It’s the worst one so far. The character-motivations are strange, their descriptions even stranger and the scenario feels a bit half-baked. And then add to that the weird ready-made characters. There’s really nothing here that would make me think you would get anything out of this.

    And the wolves! Hopefully, it’s just this one weird writer who convinced themselves that wolves are sacred nature protectors with human-level intelligence (even without a First Change) in the W5 setting.

                          It would certainly change things a bit for how wolfborn Garou can be portrayed if they continue to portray normal wolves like they're characters from The Lion King. At that point, the jump from wolf to Garou after the First Change isn't that big anymore. It loses a bit of the flavor from previous editions, I think, but it would certainly justify the approach from the W5 corebook to not really care about the difference between wolf-born and human-born.

                          Also, one thing I've noticed is that they love to give wolf-born characters the Illiterate Flaw. It's partly due to how the WOD5E system demands you to pick Flaws during character-creation, I guess, but I wish we could get something more flavorful and wolf-born specific. Especially because the way the Illiterate Flaw works is "Flaw: (••) Illiterate: You cannot read or write. Your Academics and Science Skills are capped at 1, and you can have no Specialty in them that incorporates modern knowledge." Capping Skills is a bit harsh. And sure, you can always negotiate with the ST to remove the Flaw during the chronicle for justified story-reasons but that's one of those Flaws that I think should be worth more than two dots (in the same way that Jared's Dark Secret Flaw as a one-dot-thing feels too low as well). But that's more of an overall problem with the WOD5E system. It's mandated to pick Flaws but the offering you can choose from is pretty small in number.

                          Another thing I forgot to mention is that the wolfborn characters have Nahanni, the wolf who leads the Lake Trout pack, as their Touchstone. And it's said the two wolfborn came from that Pack. But then Darius' backstory talks about how he challenged his wolf-pack-leader too many times and got kicked out of the pack (that's all before his First Change). It's just strange because that would be Nahanni in that case. I guess that's yet another thing that wasn't really thought through.

Because of how this is structured (with the standard being that you pick two dots worth of Flaws) unlike other traits in WOD5E, Flaws don't have a range of 1 to 5 dots but rather of 1 or 2 dots. That's of course quite limiting in representing the degree of a flaw. Personally with that expectation, I always thought it would have been better to create categories of Minor and Major Flaws rather than thinking of it in dots. Because one dot in a Background is a small thing while one dot in a Flaw can be quite big. But that was baked in since V5. Of all the things, this has been something H5 and W5 left untouched.

                                                          Additionally, V5 had that add-on for character-creation called "The Sea of Time" (pg 151) where you had three degrees of starting experience: Childer (nothing), Neonate (a decade or two of being a vampire; 15 XP) and then Ancilla (been around for near a century; dot of Blood Potence, 35 XP). Neither H5 nor W5 have anything like that. It's just some short remarks. I think the W5 corebook suggests at one point you could give more dots of Renown during character-creation for more experienced starting characters but there's no structure or examples for that.
