    Rite of Talisman Dedication, Pockets and Backpacks

    11-05-2016, 06:38 AM
    In our last session, the following thing arose:

    Our group passed through the Gauntlet under the lead of our most experienced char with umbral experience (Rank 3/Gnosis 6). He had a note in his pocket that we think is essential. The clothes themselves are bound with Talisman Dedication.

    From prior experiences, things like dedicated wallets do carry over their contents (here: money, credit cards, and change), and the same applies to guns with magazine and ammo in it. These contents have - up to now - been always stuff that contextually belongs together, and we handled it without re-dedicating them, even if the contents changed to some degree (getting different change, reloading the magazine) as long as the context stayed the same. That is not the case for whole new sets - like if the wallet is just worn up and gets replaced.

    Now, back to the slip of paper with the note. We had never paid specific attention to the behavior of tiny notes or such that were just tucked into the pockets and didn't belong to these 'given sets', and our GM surprised us with the notion, that the slip of paper was left behind. We discussed it, couldn't find a rule part about such and rolled with it to be decided between the game sessions - we sent someone to retrieve the note.

    Now, I did skim through the umbra chapter and the Talisman Dedication part, but couldn't find anything hitting the problem during the game session. But maybe you know better than all of us: How is the Rite of Talisman Dedication intended to handle pockets and containers (backpacks)? Did we make an error with our assumption of them carrying over contents tucked into them or did we goof up? Or did I just look at the wrong spots for answers that are hidden out there?

              Magpie's Swag is the level one Fetish that allows you, if it is dedicated, to count it (the duffle bag, backpack, purse, or other container) as a single item even if you fill it with other fetishes or non-dedicated items.

              To me, this implies if it is not dedicated, it normally won't go with you even if it is put in something that is dedicated which has a pocket or what not. You pick up that enemy thumb drive or ID card and stick it in your pocket and enter the Umbra? It doesn't go with you. If you want to do that, you need that fetish.

              I think slips of paper and what not is something that is very debatable, and I'd have no problem simply accepting whatever the ST decides as long as going forward I would know what that was (even if in some cases, it is dependent on circumstances).

              Thus, a wallet that is part of a clothing set which is dedicated can carry with it certain cards, IDs, etc. that continue to remain with it at the time. But since slips of cash comes and go, and thus is not part of the original dedication, you could risk losing that money everytime you shift. In today's world where credit cards and such can be continually used it is probably not an issue, but even several decades ago it would be a big problem if you couldn't carry cash as you shift in and out.

              This is probably how I'd run it, as I want my players to think hard before they change forms or what forms they choose, or whether all pack members should be changing. But not all gaming groups are like that, and many just want as much convenience as possible.

                          Something that's technically viable (if something most Garou wouldn't consider) to add to Black Fox there: swallow it.

                                      The way I read it (not necessarily disagreeing with your position, mind) is that the main thrust of the fetish is to carry twice what the bag looks like it should -- sort of an expansion of what I like to call the "fifty-foot rope" issue from D&D (the 50' rope is surprisingly useful, but nobody ever brings it as it counts against encumbrance). If you were wearing a set of non-dedicated clothing at transformation, it gets shredded -- that's explicitly stated in the book. But does it work the same way for stepping into the Penumbra? Although that seems to be the consensus here, if you step sideways in Homid wearing non-dedicated clothing, do you come out the other side naked?

                                      ​On an even less related note: What about moon-bridges? They traverse the Umbra. So, if you travel a moon-bridge and have non-dedicated items on your person, do those just drop off as you step onto the bridge?

                                      ​And something totally unrelated: How do non-dedicated and un-awakened items behave in Umbral realms (assuming you have a way of getting those items to the Umbra in the first place) in which the realm mimics the physical world? It's a place where guns do very much work, although those guns are actually emanations. What about other stuff, too? Could you bring a camera and take pictures of scenes in Battleground, or Wolfholme etc. ?
