    GURPS Werewolf is it worth it?

    11-18-2017, 11:57 PM
    So i have this love hate relationship with Apocalypse. I love the setting to death but i hate the mechanics, i hate OWoD combat as i think is where the system shits the bed but oh boy some of the design choices of Werewolf are specially rotten.

    My issues are damage rolls.

    Purebreed is the worst mechanic ever designed in gaming.

    Rage breaks an already clunky system.

    Gift go from OP to completely useless and is a mess of complication with the whole "Gnosis/rage" expenditure.

    Regeneration is needlesly obtuse.

    So many of the problems i can fix. Replace damage rolls with Attack vs Defense roll and the soak and increase health by 5 + stamina. Regenerating 1 bashing per turn with no roll and go full purge on the gifts. That said multiaction rage and the Gnosis/Rage divide is kinda hard to mess with.

    But then i tough about GURPS werewolf. I read that GURPS is a fairly solid system so i am wondering for those who try it. How was it? Whats the good and the bad system wise? Can it offer me a way to run Apocalypse without the issues i find problematic in the current system?

          My issues are damage rolls.
          GURPS is still going to have damage roll issues, mind you. Especially at higher dice values, like with explosions, where you could be facing, say, 6dx200 at ground zero of 50 lbs of TNT going off (#d always means #d6; the system only uses six-siders). Given how heavy weapons and high explosives turn soft targets into hamburger, you're going to need to play with the system to keep that under control.

          For reference, an assault rifle does about 7d and it only takes ten points of damage to seriously injure the average person and make the lose consciousness; anything more risks death, which is automatic either if you hit someone in an area that would kill them (i.e. slitting a throat or shooting them through the heart) or at 60 points of damage. 100 points of damage results in complete bodily destruction.

          Purebreed is the worst mechanic ever designed in gaming.
          First off, that's not really a combat concern. Secondly, I'm pretty sure that there are lots of D&D mechanics, original Mage magick rules, and all of FATAL to go through first.

          Rage breaks an already clunky system.
          Extra attacks are a hell of a drug, but you'll also get them in GURPS. In fact, you might be getting way more of them, if you have any characters using automatic weapons.

          Gift go from OP to completely useless and is a mess of complication with the whole "Gnosis/rage" expenditure.
          I don't really mind the Gnosis and Rage interference, but otherwise, yes. GURPS will actually solve this problem to some extent, as Gifts are going to end up as constructed powers using point values.

          Regeneration is needlesly obtuse.
          Are we talking the Stamina roll to recover lethal here? Because I do hate that. Though, in GURPS, regeneration is typically a book-keeping burden, since you'll be recovering 1 HP/s, and turns are one second, only you'll occasionally need to ready a weapon before using it if it's too unwieldy...

          So many of the problems i can fix. Replace damage rolls with Attack vs Defense roll and the soak and increase health by 5 + stamina. Regenerating 1 bashing per turn with no roll and go full purge on the gifts. That said multiaction rage and the Gnosis/Rage divide is kinda hard to mess with.

          But then i tough about GURPS werewolf. I read that GURPS is a fairly solid system so i am wondering for those who try it. How was it? Whats the good and the bad system wise? Can it offer me a way to run Apocalypse without the issues i find problematic in the current system?
          GURPS is solid for its wargaming roots, but you're going to need to get 1) a copy of the core book, which may be rather pricey, and the only pdfs on Drivethru are for a new edition, and 2) a fairly large hexsheet, since it relies heavily on positioning, and you'll have to tinker with the old GURPS Werewolf: the Apocalypse release a bit if you want to use any of the new stuff that was written for 4e (as GURPS W:tA was written for a much earlier iteration of 4e). Among difficulties you'll face, in addition to the ones above, are changing hit point totals (either as part of a template or because GURPS 4e bases HP on Strength), Crinos potentially being way less capable because of the way different lifting charts work, keeping track of damage types under different situations, random hit locations for anyone who doesn't aim at a specific part, and having to stat up spirits with actual stats instead of Rage, Gnosis, Willpower, and Power/Essence. Also, defenses may be a bit more difficult to manage, since IIRC you'll be looking at margins of success rather than how many dice come up a certain number.

          Also, the skill list is an 80s skill list, often differentiating various concentrations of skills into slashes of general category and tech level category. Unless you use 4e's Bang! Skills to cover multiple areas, you're going to be in a hell of Mechanic/Cars/TL 7, Mechanic/Cars/TL 8, Guns/ Pistols/ TL 5 (antique old west pistols), Guns/Pistols/TL 6 (early 20th century pistols), Guns/Pistols/ TL 7 (post mid-20th century to lateish 20th century), and Guns/Pistols/TL 8 (anything that's new). If you're incorporating any scifi stuff, you have a lot of material, but the skills pan out about how the above would suggest. Rock solid simulationism has downsides, and it comes in the form of a default sprawling skill list, as well as the question of how to convert animal forms; GURPS W:tA has conversions for the numbers for Garou forms, of course, but Lupus Attribute modifiers are very different from wolf Attributes. In some cases, such as with Gurahl, using the stat bonuses might leave a character substantially under-powered in 3e (in 4e, you determine the Strength of a large beast by taking the cube root of its weight in pounds and doubling the result, round up.

          (You can get pdfs of older stuff on Warehouse 23, the official site for SJ Games, but I have heard complaints about the quality of service more recently, so registrant and buyer beware.)
