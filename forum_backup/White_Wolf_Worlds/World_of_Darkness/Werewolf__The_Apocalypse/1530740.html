    Hypothetical - If we were to update WTA to 5th Edition using 20th...

    02-13-2024, 10:02 AM
    ICONS OF RAGE is awesome and OPP is the last gasp of the heavy continuity of the previous editions that we all fell in love with. So, the question would be how would you update the setting to the year 2024?

    Assuming that Gaia isn't dead or dying and the Cult of Fenris didn't exist.

    Feel free to suggest big changes but we're using W20 as a basis.

    Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

                      I tend to agree - showing wolf (and Lupus) populations on the rise would be a good way to show Garou having a positive impact on the world, without actually making life easier for them - given that just about everything else in the World of Darkness knows that if you want a shot at the Garou, hitting the wolves who go on four legs is a good place to start.

                      Also, seeing the Red Talon struggling to come to terms with the distinct possibility that humans have actually done wolves a solid for once would be something worth seeing (If only because this would potentially allow authors to back off a bit from making this tribe all Hauglosk, all the time).


                      It actually might be interesting to borrow some ideas from 5th Edition and see what the classic editions make of them - for example the very notion that Hauglosk exists and is an actual problem would almost certainly trigger some fascinating controversies throughout the Garou Nation (Not least because there would be some serious potential for ‘Well that explains a lot’ moments).

                      Seeing elements within the Glass Walkers advocate a shift from Cockroach to Spider would also be intriguing: if nothing else this would give us some interesting perspective on how a Tribe’s relationship with their Patron Spirit shifts across time (and whether it’s a Tribe that makes the Totem or the Totem that makes the Tribe).

                      The Black Spiral Dancers having a rather more bloody variation of the same seismic shift might also be intriguing (Especially if it ends with Bat, a patron spirit partly restored to Gaia, at the top).


                      In all honesty, though, I’m not sure how contemporary WW20 ought to be: to my mind it’s the ‘timeless’ version of the classic editions (Where on a good day it’s six minutes to midnight and on a bad day it’s never more than five), which need only touch upon current events if a Chronicle requires it to be.

                      Oddly enough my gut instinct is that the By Night studios version of WEREWOLF might be the more interesting one to update to AD 2024, since as an effectively post-apocalyptic (or at least Post-Apocalypse War) setting there’s more room to show significant changes having occurred.

                      Now I just need to finish reading that sourcebook to make sure this isn’t a mistaken impression!😊

                          The biggest change I'd make is reworking tribes. All Fera should have them, they should line up vaguely with biogeographical regions or taxonomic boundaries (at least before factoring in immigration and population movement), and they should look more like an actual ethnic group than the special interest group/political parties that we got. There'd be more than thirteen; the thirteen tribes we know would simply be the biggest ones and you'd be given instructions on how to create your own tribes for a chronicle. Camps would take a similar role tribes do now and be cross-tribal by default. Some of the traditional aspects would still be present, but they'd be downplayed and largely due to the tribe forming alliances with specific spirits. The Glass Walkers aren't all city-dwellers or good with technology, they just formed an alliance with urban spirits and that influences their gift list. The Talons aren't uniting around hatred of the human species, it's just that their patron spirit only allows wolf and crinos-born and they spend a lot of time hanging around the spirits of extinct and endangered animals, which tends to color their opinions.

                          This means that tribes can blend to some extent for Kinfolk; someone could be bitribal. Fera can't, at least not by how Fera figure it, because initiation involves rites to bind you to the patron spirit and those are exclusive. You can switch tribes or choose to join one that you aren't related to, but they won't necessarily take you and there tends to be extremely heavy pressure to assimilate if you do. This, and the lesser pressure placed on human and animal-born to assimilate, is a source of tension.

                          I'd get rid of mythological patron spirits; I think patron spirits should be organisms or natural phenomena in the region a tribe comes from.

                          Caerns would be emphasized as more than just a center of Fera culture. There'd be more rites that influenced a caern's bawn--potential choices might be ones to increase rainfall, shred the Gauntlet, reduce temperatures, or increase plant growth. Some of these could have minor regional effects, especially if done at a powerful caern. There would also be fetishes which would extend the range of a caern's effect or have permanent effects on the local bawn so long as they're fed gnosis regularly.

                          I'd make it so that normal humans and animals could become Kinfolk by hanging around caerns long enough. It's not assured, but it'd common enough if they're spending a year or so on the bawn--the energies of the caern allow for the patrons of the Fera to transform them. This would be a retcon. In addition, simply due to the numbers disparity and the Curse, Fera have to lean more on their Kinfolk and their Kinfolk are treated more (though not completely) equally. A squad of Kinfolk with guns is a significant asset, and there are many Crafts of Kinfolk mages or sorcerers. Nonhuman Kinfolk would get more time devoted to them and show up more often as NPCs. They would be supported as PCs again (and yes, this does mean you can play a lupus Kinfolk mage). I would also leave the Fera-Kinfolk birth ratio undefined, because the math makes no sense, and change the focus of Fera population problems from "They have a low birth rate" to "They die a lot." Fera births would still be uncommon, though. I think this would solve most of the problems people have with Kinfolk.

                          Crinos-born would get a new name, as would Younger and Older Brother. Bigotry towards crinos-born for being the result of a violation of the Litany would get toned down, but still be present.

                          In terms of metaplot, the Fera continue to lose the war but have started working out more of their internal issues--continuing the trend from Revised. The Ahadi are now a solid alliance that clearly isn't going anywhere. I don't really have metaplot ideas for other stuff, but since I'm interested in Werewolf/Mage crossover I would probably have the Technocracy starting to show up as an antagonist.
                          Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                          Also, seeing the Red Talon struggling to come to terms with the distinct possibility that humans have actually done wolves a solid for once would be something worth seeing (If only because this would potentially allow authors to back off a bit from making this tribe all Hauglosk, all the time).
                          The Talons aren't going to see it that way. While the death-cult that is the Predator Kings will decrease in prominence, the Talons aren't going to give humans credit for translocating a few packs, easing up just enough to make sure they don't outright go extinct, and then generally killing 30% of their population every year--especially when the whole mess was caused by humans in the first place. What probably happens is that both the Predator Kings and the Whelp's Compromise shrink.

                          I do think the Talons could have a major role in improving relations with other Fera, or at least animal-born Fera. Part of what I'd do if I was creating a new edition would be to make the animal-born, across species, start to see themselves as more of a cohesive group with unifying interests. The Talons would be obvious choices for Garou to get involved in those politics, especially since their African offshoots helped found the Ahadi. From a game design role, having the Talons be more politically progressive in some ways makes the times when PCs do clash with them more compelling and also makes them more appealing as player characters or PC allies.

                                              It also puts Red Talons and their example characters in a truly interesting light. Hauglosk is supposed to be about taking things too far and therefore breaking chronicle tenets. Yet, the Plague Dog exists and by all rights should end up in Hauglosk within 5 sessions if the ST isn't going for a 'humans are the real monsters' type of game.

                                              This creates a situation where some extremist behaviour is allowed no matter how harsh, but other such behaviour is not. Because we are given CoF as the strawman and poster tribe for Hauglosk and there is so far no non-CoF case of Hauglosk in canon, the message is not "Don't go too far." It is "Don't be a CoF."

                                              This completely ruins whatever message the mechanic could have had. Instead, it shows us a faction that exists only as a strawman for the PCs to put down rather than a nuanced example of how no one is immune to falling victim to extremism. Then we add the allowed actions per the character examples and you end up with VERY unfortunate writing.

                                                  The problem with Hauglosk is also that as a narrative tool it doesn't quite work. With Harano at least, it's easy to figure out the narrative arc you're pursuing. Shit happens, defeats, losses, failures... it all piles up until the point where your character just gives up. Easy to see what this is going for in terms of a narrative. Although, I don't think the write-up of Harano in the W5 corebook is particularly good as it takes the lazy shortcut of just going for "supernatural depression" but that's a whole other issue.

                                                  I mentioned in other threads how I would change Hauglosk and the first thing is that its definition needs to be fundamentally changed. Because as it's presented, the idea is that every single Garou is on track to potentially becoming a fanatical, self-righteous asshole. The only way this could work is if everybody at the table buys into the very negative portrayal of Garou you get in the W5 corebook. But Hauglosk is less a condition and more a description for a distinct characterization anyway. Even the way it's described shows that. It's about losing your way in how you fight the war. But that's a character-issue, something that applies to certain characters under specific circumstances. It isn't this state of being that every Garou is doomed to slip into.

                                                  Even in the examples offered, like this one from pg 52 of the W5 corebook:

                                                  An aging Silver Fang grows jealous of the achievements of younger Garou. The stories of his accomplishments fade; the enemies he fought, forgotten. Desperate to feel as if he still matters, he resorts to calling for the killing of everyone who’s ever worked at a certain Pentex Group front in the territory, from board members to drug store clerks. He argues that, once the corporate evil took root, the community could not be redeemed. The pack has to decide whether they agree with the policy of mass extermination and, if not, how to stop it. Is it possible to talk the Silver Fang out of it? If not, how can they bring the Silver Fang back to reason?​
                                                  It says right there at the start what the real issue is: jealousy. The guy clearly has some sort of inferiority complex. That's what you're dealing with. But of course, the book isn't saying that everybody in the Cult of Fenris has an inferiority-complex. And yet this case as described is thrown into the Hauglosk-bucket.

                                                  What the game proposes is that similarly to just giving up via Harano, characters also have this doom-spiral towards losing their way in how to conduct their fighting. And yet it wasn't considered that this should be a character-issue rather than a doom-spiral you force on PCs. I would say that the mentioned Silver Fang didn't experience jealousy because Hauglosk forced him to feel that way but rather because it's a flaw of his personality. And yet, what W5 is proposing instead is that Hauglosk should be blamed for this happening.

                                                  The mechanics are a whole other can of worms, of course...

                                                          I think Hauglosk is something that a lot of players are struggling with because it's very badly defined more than it is a bad concept. Part of this is the question of what exactly is the warning signs, how many Garou are infected by it, and whether recognizing its existence is a recent thing or not.

                                                          In fact, i think part of the problem is they don't flat out make an obvious point in the game clear that would actually GIVE A DECENT CHRONICLE GOAL of saying, "Yeah, a substantial chunk of the Garou Nation's leadership is corrupted by the Beast of War."

                                                          (And yes, I'm going to think Hauglosk should be directly related to that even if you don't smell of the Wyrm)

                                                          Make it clear to anyone LOOKING (and being strongly encouraged not to) that most of the Red Talons or at least their leadership are fully corrupted to Hauglosk. They just are smart enough to stick with the Garou nation for tactical reasons.
