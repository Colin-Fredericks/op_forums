        Yes, I have had my share of problem players. I try to give players warnings when they are about to do something incredibly stupid (my players tend to wince when I say the word 'interesting' or the phrase 'that is unusual' because that usually means that a character is going to die a horrible death if they do not rethink their plans). Problem players tend to learn during the first session or they leave my games after the second session because I can predict their actions after the first session and plan accordingly. Infernal Sorcerers with Mask of Innocence and a few levels of Youth, along with around 100 Soul Points worth of addition Demonic Investments, are my preferred teaching lessons for the sort of character that tends to attack everything (no matter how inappropriate) because they get that sick look on their face when they suddenly realize that they have just attacked something much, much more powerful than their characters without provocation.

                                I think there are a couple of problems with those assumptions. The first is that Lupus Garou aren't just wolves. They don't innately have less intelligence than other Garou and are capable of conceptualizing the same abstractions as other Garou, its just a matter of whether they have the experiential framework to make that conceptualization easier. The second is that you really have to define atheism more clearly. The existence of spirits and supernatural phenomena doesn't actually explicitly indicate the existence of any kind of god, nor does accepting their objective existence necessarily translate to any practice of faith. Christianity holds that the earth is God's creation. Atheists may not believe in God but that doesn't mean they stop believing that the earth exists.
