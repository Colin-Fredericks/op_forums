              Originally posted by Logothétēs View Post
              Especially Baali ones. You'd think the Dancers would jump at a chance of immortality and spreading the Wyrm's corruption forever.
              My understanding is that it's what Ana Mizuki said. I want to say that this is explained in some book or other, but it's definitely the sort of thing that required an explanation because the existence and use of BSD abominations by the Wyrm makes sooo much sense given even a basic understanding of what the Wyrm is, that they basically had to write in a specific reason why there wouldn't be a bunch of Wyrm BSD's. If you look at the language from 2nd Ed. Player's Guide, it seems clear that the Wyrm should love to have some Wrym abominations at it's ready disposal:

              "There is only one way to prevent [the degeneration of Gnosis]--the Abomination can fully offer what's left of his soul to the Wyrm. After making the black pact, the Abomination becomes a Storyteller-controlled character. His Gnosis is no longer at risk; the Wyrm sees to fueling his Spiritual energy. However, the Abomination no longer has any free will whatsoever and cannot so much as tie his shoe of his own accord."

              W:tA Player's Guide 2nd Ed., 191-192
              Mind you, the hierarchy of sins for Gnosis degeneration that they provide there is basically a bunch of stuff many BSD's are going to do anyway (warring against the tribes--level two on the hierarchy--is basically the BSD raison d'etre). The 'giving themselves over whole hog/no free will' thing seems like an across-the-board deal breaker until you stop to consider the depth of mundane human zealotry (in the WoD and in the real world); it's not hard to conceive of a person so devout to their god (pick your Abrahamic religion) that they'd give themselves over, completely and irrevocably, to it's/his/her service in a heartbeat. With that in mind, how hard can it be to conceive of a one or more BSD's feeling the same way about the Wyrm? It's not a stretch--so of course they put in a proviso that explains that, even though this line of reasoning seems like it make perfect sense, it actually doesn't, because reasons. It's almost the White Wolf equivalent of the Genie telling Aladdin that he can't wish for more wishes (and why can't he wish for more wishes? Because no.)

              My pet theory is that abominations--like many things in the classic WoD--are a first/second edition thing that all or most of the devs decided later that they hated (just throw them in with the True Black Hand), so they went out of their way to make sure that there was no efficiency anywhere in creating an Abomination, such that the only reason to make one would be as an act of chaotic evil cruelty. My further assumption is that it was probably too much to retcon from 2nd Ed. to Revised that abominations just didn't exist at all, and can't be made in the first place; you can just nuke Enoch to basically get of the True Black Hand, but getting rid of Abominations would require changing the "natural" laws of the WoD, and they'd basically be creating a bunch of plotholes in earlier supplements that featured one or more abominations.

                  A lot of it has to do with rarity. Abominations are very few, as the great majority of embraced werewolves just die. Even if a group of BSDs could actually find a vampire - never mind a Baali one, who are fairly uncommon by vampire standards - to agree to try a mass embrace of BSDs, there is no guarantee that they'd even get one successful embrace out of it. You could, at best, end up losing up to a dozen or more perfectly good warriors in exchange for one that can't operate during daylight hours, is in a perpetual state of depression, can't regenerate (without spending valuable blood), is limited in using Gnosis, and advances in Rank very slowly. And if the Abomination does pledge itself to the Wyrm, the new Wyrm puppet may run off on its own, as the Wyrm may have plans for it that don't involve its former Hive.

                                                          As mentioned, BSDs likely wouldn't want to become abominations for (almost) all the same reasons as Gaian Garou. Being alive and connected to the spirit world is deeply important to them.

                                                          With regard to swearing service to a powerful servant of the Wyrm, I look at the total loss of free will and injection of spiritual energy as being something more than just reaffirming their previous loyalty (for a BSD who already serves the Wyrm, that is). I think such an abomination has much of its self displaced by a possessing entity, or at least rewritten like a Drone, and so BSDs both alive and undead would look down on such an option just like they look down on Fomori and rarely choose to become one (despite the power boost).
