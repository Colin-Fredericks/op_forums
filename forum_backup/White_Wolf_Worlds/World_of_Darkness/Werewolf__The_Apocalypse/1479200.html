              If I were to do the Howling in a chronicle, I would run them as Ronin with the character of Dr Waggner attempting to cure or control their affliction (their Rage). But Eddie Quist (and his sister Marsha) would be falling to the Wyrm, and an obvious candidate for recruitment by the Black Spiral Dancers or possibly have already been approached. Dr Waggner might possibly be an actual Child of Gaia attempting to integrate Ronin into his tribe, or at least exposed to them.

              Most Werewolf movies are only going to match their characters to Ronin (or Lunatics) because the particulars of the Garou depart to much from pop culture werewolves.

              While I think the Howling is a very dated movie, it has a lot of elements I love. And I work hard to have my setting be able to emulate the movie in some capacity. It's one reason I increase the population of Ronin to a much higher number than is official. Maybe something like 10-25% of all Garou are Ronin in my setting - making them the largest "tribe" bigger than either the BSDs or Bone Gnawers. There's just too much cool pop culture werewolf stuff that can only be done through them.

                                              In my chronicles, I treat man eating in a similar way as drug abuse. In the general Garou population, about 5% of Garou have eaten people in the past five years, and 1% are pretty hard core ongoing eaters. Overall, there's probably 10-15% of Garou who have done it at some point in their lives. Incidents are probably higher among the Ronin.

                                              Now some of those Garou only did so by accident, or just once or a few times and never did so again. (It frequently happens with First Changers/Lost Cubs before the Garou find them and teach them how to control it.) But it's done enough that the crime is in the Litany for a reason. The notorious camps like the Man Eaters, Eaters of the Dead, and other canonical groups make up most of the 1% who do so continuously. But the rest is spread throughout the Garou tribes. The difference between being an addict who needs help and an internal enemy that needs to be destroyed mostly depends on whether they justify it (like the Man Eaters and Eaters of the Dead do) or admit they have a problem.

                                              The Garou break their own law just as much as there is crime in any human society. Most Garou follow the law. They aren't hypocrites. But there's always a criminal element of varying degrees. And most of their criminals don't become traitors. Just because they break one or more precepts of the Litany doesn't mean they're falling to the Wyrm.

                                              I like a certain amount of "normal" criminal behavior that results in Philodoxes actually using the Rites of Punishment in the sept. Otherwise the Philodox have a lot less to do.

                                                      Originally posted by Nail Eater View Post
                                                      I may also recommend you another old movie, Wolfen, with Albert Finney, Tom Noonan, Tom Waits and Diane Venora.
                                                      I've both seen the movie Wolfen and read the book (by Whitley Strieber). There are major differences between the two, but the movie is clearly based on the book and not just "inspired" by it. I don't remember Tom Waits in it. I do remember Edward James Olmos.

                                                      Strieber was an important horror author in the late seventies and early eighties with his popular books Wolfen and the Hunger which "updated" the vampire and werewolf myths to be more "realistic". Less supernatural and more alternative forms of life. But he's mostly forgotten today except for the UFO crowd which got into his non-fiction (but crazy) books on abductions like Communion.

                                                      Wolfen certainly has a lot of things I steal for my chronicles. One can arguably base a lot of Red Talon stuff on it (though in the movie which brings a lot of Native American stuff which is not in the actual book, it might be better to explain them as lupus of the Wendigo tribe). Plus that James Horner score (the same guy who did the score for Star Trek II: Wrath of Khan) is just awesome.




                                                      1981 is probably the peak year for werewolf movies with the Howling, American Werewolf in London, and Wolfen. All three are big inspirations for me. I feel I must be able to simulate those movies (if not duplicate them exactly) in some fashion, or I'm not getting the genre right. They're up there for me as Werewolf of London and the Wolfman. I haven't seen the Curse of the Werewolf (Hammer film starring Oliver Reed, based on Guy Endore's Werewolf of Paris which is supposed to be the closest equivalent of Dracula for werewolves) yet. I also want to see Ginger Snaps which looks awesome and would probably be great to steal from.
