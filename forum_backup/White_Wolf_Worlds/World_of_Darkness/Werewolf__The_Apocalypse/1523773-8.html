            Whisperer / Tormented Soul (Metis Theurge Uktena)

            And ... damn, I'm not really sure where to start with this one.

            Obviously this is when they were still trying to figure out some of the finer points about this Breed. They're born misformed and cursed. In this case, the character, apparently male, I presume, is completely hairless. And also either a paranoid schizophrenic or under constant assault by spirits of the Wyrm. Maybe both.

            Apparently he's had voices whispering in his head offering power and pleasure, apparently in the form of being able to violate others, since before his First Change. Apparently these voices also sometimes offered up the hidden secrets of others (which honestly would've been a more interesting angle to pursue her IMO). And now he's been tossed into this new ad hoc pack, apparently as much a way to make him someone else's problem as anything else, and he fully expects them to betray him and it's kind of implied that he plans to turn on them first, and most likely run off to join the Black Spiral Dancers.

            First edition Uktena leaned really heavy into the whole "they practice black magic" angle, with parts of their write up reading more like what you would expect from the Shadow Lords at times. And this character leans so hard into that aspect that it pretty much falls over on its face.

            The image is of a hairless werewolf in Crinos form, arms crossed and a scowl on his face. He's wearing a slightly ragged tunic, with a few adornments to indicate "Native American" or something along those lines. It's not a terrible image. Just not a very good one either.

            For Attributes, Physicals are secondary, with a 2/2/4 split. This seems to fit somewhat with the brief mention of his abusive upbringing. Socials are tertiary, with all points dumped into Manipulation giving him a 1/4/1 spread. Mental is primary, with 2/4/4. That's a lot of fours. I don't think this is bad, but I do worry about a character who is really powerful in his fortes and almost crippled in Social situations.
            One thing I noticed with Vampire's Nosferatu characters was that it was really easy to take advantage of their clan weakness and only spread your Social dots among two traits instead of three, often resulting in characters who were at least slightly stronger than average in Charisma or Manipulation if not outright powerhouses in them with a 4/5/0 split if you went primary. I kind of feel like this was trying to repeat that tactic using the Metis deformity rule as an excuse to not spend any points on Appearance (or Charisma, since he's an unstable introvert). I suspect that if I ever get to a Metis character who has more than 2 dots in Appearance, I'm going to get ridiculously excited over it.

            For Abilities, Talents are secondary, with 3 each in Dodge and Subterfuge, and 1 each in Brawl, Intimidation, and Primal Urge. Skills are tertiary with an extra dot, with 3 in Survival, 2 in Stealth, and 1 in Melee. Knowledge is primary with an extra dot from freebies, for a 4 in Occult, 3 each in Enigmas and Rituals, and 2 each in Investigation and Science.
            Generally, these are all OK. But I can't help but feel like he has these abilities just because that's what a Theurge would be expected to have as opposed to because they are part of a thought out background and character development.

            Uktena get 4 Background points in 1st ed, and this has one extra from Freebies. He has 2 each in Rites and Fetish (a Spirit Tracer), and 1 in Pack Totem. I'd almost question why a character like this would have the Pack Totem background, but I know it's because all of them have one dot in it so the players can build the Pack Totem. (I sometimes wonder if they shouldn't have just given every PC a free dot in that background.) As for the others, they make sense, but still have that "this is what a Theurge should have" feel to them.

            Rage is the base 2 for Theurge. Gnosis is 6. Willpower is 6.

            So ... There just isn't a lot here besides "he's crazy and/or corrupted and isn't going to play well with others". That in and of itself isn't a deal breaker for me, but I'd have to really trust the person playing that kind of character before I'd allow it in a game I was trying to run. I don't think it's a good concept for a scenario like this where everyone is likely to be new to the game, and possibly even new to roleplaying.

            I think if you toned it down a notch or three and fleshed out his personality and background some, you might have something worthwhile here. At least give him a hobby or some sort of hook you can use to build on the idea of him not betraying the rest of the PCs and becoming an NPC villain. But as it is, this feels more like a first draft of a concept rather than a fully realized character.

                Story/Character: Yeah, okay. This is just bad in terms of a starting character. For one, this is the ONLY Indigenous character of the lot. Second, this is also the only Garou Born template. This means that this character being a voice-hearing future-BSD really isn't sitting well with me.

                On a personal note, I don't really get the hints that this character was often going to be killed. They were spared as a cub and nothing in the backstory says they are particularly rebellious. Garou aren't really the type to keep a garou born cub around if they don't want to. They do abuse them, but generally don't try to kill them for nothing.

                Now, being a Garou Born, some grumpiness is expected. But the way Whisperer is set up, this PC does not play well with others and is almost always antagonistic towards them. And that is not something you want from a template. Nevermind that playing out a character like this takes a lot of skill and a lot of trust from the ST.

                The voices are a problem on their own, as they mean this PC needs even more ST attention than your typical theurge.

                Then there is the fact that the BSD bad guy from this book ALSO heard voices that pushed her to dance.

                This character requires a lot of skill to play in a group and even then they are a ticking timebomb. This is an EXTREMELY poor choice as a starting Garou Born AND a Ghost Council member. Especially as the text ends with a pretty big hint that this PC will dance the spiral since BSDs are the only ones that understand them.

                Which, given this character is the second ever garou born template, has some unfortunate implications. Joining the BSDs should be something a character is driven to during the course of the chronicle. Not part of the character's concept in a book showing example PCs.

                Characters like this are what I call disruptive characters. Characters whose issues take up attention and time from other characters and who spend way too much time going solo.

                Stats:

                In Attributes, I feel Perception and Wits should be shifted, as the backstory states this character is a bit TOO perceptive at times.

                The socials need some care as well, as this character is very much NOT a Manipulation 4 character. Not only does it imply that they are good at using others which is not what the text says, they are at 4 dice for any rites. Nevermind anything related to spirits. The image also is not app 1. Yes, they are hairless but not in an ugly way.

                Talents looks extremely clumsy. For one, no Alertness for a character constantly hearing stuff? The theme of a skilled manipulator continues with Subterfuge 3, which again doesn't match the backstory. Dodge 3 only makes sense if you assume this character has had to dodge a LOT of things, which then runs counter to the 7 dice in lying. Primal-Urge should be higher on a nature raised PC as should Brawl. Intimidation would make more sense for a character that isn't the punching bag.

                Skills has a random melee, but otherwise is solid.

                Knowledges looks good until we come to 2 dots in science. Where did those come from? What science would this isolated kid know? Unless it refers to the knowledge of poisons. As if this PC didn't need more obvious "This guy is EVIL" hints.

                Backgrounds have randomly a two dot fetish that is not mentioned in the backstory. Given that this character barely survived until now, having a fetish makes no sense.

                All in all, extremely volatile and hard to play with very little pay-off. The fact that the book says more than one lupus PC would be an issue, when THIS character is in the mix is hilariously tone deaf.​

                            I think this one is just bad. With this template the character starts out with these beats:
                            • fully embracing the idea of Metis as social outcasts disliked by everyone
                            • character hears voices (sometimes ghosts but the character also comes off as a paranoid schizophrenic)
                            • character believes the other PCs are plotting against him
                            • he has some sympathy for the BSD and if things get worse will join them
                            What makes this so bad is to make this character playable, the only thing you're doing is essentially running away from all of that. Because of the way things stand in this template, this character joining the BSD at some point is the reasonable conclusion to this template's story. He's being mistreated, he hears voices, he's paranoid to the point that he thinks of the other PCs as enemies and what some of the voices urge him to do is to join the BSD. The real challenge here is to conceive of a scenario where this character wouldn't be this obstacle to other players and end up joining the antagonists. Even in the best of times, you would need both a player and an ST working together to turn this character into something playable.

                            Stat-wise it's a very narrow selection of how the dots have been distributed. Already, having a distribution like Cha 1, Man 4, App 1 conjures a very extreme character. Then Occult 4 is also interesting because... how did he acquire that? But also with how his upbringing was described, how did he acquire Science 2? And with the Backgrounds, it's also a mystery where the Fetish has come from.

                            As for alternative depictions... If we stay with this one character being a Metis, you could go with Bone Gnawers, I think, and while he has been mistreated early on and ultimately rejected by his old Tribe, the Bone Gnawers have taken him in and now he wants to prove himself to be worthy of that mercy.

                                As someone who has done quite a few garou born characters over the...decades I've been playing, here are my fixes to Whisperer;

                                -Either voices or hairlessness, two deformities are a chore to keep track of and since this is a STARTING character, you need to offer some leeway.

                                -Make the character's existence something important, say a rescued BSD pup or an omen brought to life. Give a sense WHY this character would be tolerated in their sept.

                                -Whisperer is in a new environment that is not as hostile as their old home, plus with a pack that looks up to them for what they know about the spirits. Even if they were prepared to be grumpy and hate them, this new situation should make Whisperer pause and ponder.

                                -Make joining the BSDs something that Whisperer sees as an INSULT, they are GAIAN and they will not fall! Doesn't mean they can't, just that it is not their first option.

                                -Make socials 3/2/2. Nothing in the deformity description in the 1st ed book says it is a forced app 1. Just that you have penalties to your appearance.

                                -Remove fetish or give it a backstory reason.

                                                            The WWGS/Arthaus 3rd ed of Ravenloft came out just after the turn of the millennium. The Metis, being the result of spiritual incest, also draw on this trope, especially the very Gothic idea of the sins of the parents being visited upon the child.
