    As far as I understand this is not the final version of the book YET, so here goes

    1A) I was delighted to find "Media Sources of Inspiration" in The V20 Ghouls Sourcebook, which surprisingly also included the Anime Masterpiece "Puella Magi Madoka Magica" as a source for "devil's bargain stories"
    Unfortunately, no such bibliography/filmoqgraphy/animeography exists in W20 Kinfolk; would this be rectified in the final PDF?
    1B) if yes, might I recommend for the "abusive home life in tribal cultures narratives" stories the New Zealand Films "Once Were Warriors" and "Whale Rider", both of which concern the disintegration of Maori Warrior Culture in the Modern Household.
    1C) Not to mention the Kyoto Animation Series "Clannad" and its sequel "Clannad After Story", which shows thoough you are tied down by birth families that may shatter your hopes and dreams, love still ultimately holds it together; not to mention the themes of "the family you choose", "heartbreak induced parental neglect" and "reconciliation with estranged children"

    2) Hopefully the various types of complications that Garou bring into a family that were elaborated in "Unsung heroes: Kinfolk" but largely unexplored in "Kinfolk 20" will be included too right? Of particular need to be explored is how a Garou child will feel about His Parents, whom he is although on paper "superior" to, but still somehow feels love and respect for? How DO you scold a child like that.

            I imagine that, when we look at the effect of tasers in real life, it would require a Stamina (difficulty 6, 4 if they are drunk) roll to avoid taking one bashing health level of damage and to avoid being incapacitated for one minute. If you botch your Stamina roll, you take one health level of lethal damage and, in addition to being incapacitated for an hour, you have to make a second Stamina roll (difficulty 6) to avoid a heart attack. I think that would accurately reflect the ineffectiveness of tasers (being tear gassed felt much worse) and the lethality of tasers (you will kill someone you repeatedly tase them twenty or thirty times).

                                        Originally posted by FrostFox View Post
                                        Hmm. I need to read through Mokole stuff again in changing breeds but I thought it did describe them as guiding humanity after their fall from grace so to speak.
                                        Well, I'm back from buying, downloading, and reading, and KABA has the most vicious reversal of the prior Mokole-kin relationship that there possibly could be, crossed over with presenting distorted Mnesis as the order of the day. While the prior presentation might have been too far to one side, the breedbook made a good case for Mokole not mistreating their kin, in the form of Mnesis passing those experiences of abuse on forever. Hence, giving them a ceremonial kind of position and title as the "Eighth Sun", to keep noisome influences on the memory to a minimum. They have plenty to work with from the War of Rage, they want to avoid more.

                                        Rokea also have a bizarre writeup. Non-Betweeners are presented as being much more like Red Talons than immortals who intend to outlive humanity. Also, given the recent findings regarding Greenland sharks, the two to three years to maturity for maturity of shark-born and shark kin is... far shortcoming. To wit, the findings show that Greenland sharks live lifespans like early edition D&D elves, taking what seems to be about a century and a half to reach sexual maturity, a state that they enjoy for the next two to three centuries, or perhaps more. While there are a lot of sharks that seem to take less time and be less long-lived, two or three years is still way too few. There's also the additional problem of the fact that Rokea technically don't have Kinfolk; all Rokea-on-shark matings are treated the same way, unless someone is going to writeup material to contradict the breedbook, and it seems that no one actually has.

                                        Let's also talk about how much word count is wasted on the discussion of the term "gene". Since Revised, it was a case of "there is none, no, end of story" and then we'd get on with things, unrestrained from the painfully-like-Liquid Snake issues of prior editions. But For KABA, there's at least a half page about it trying to link being Garou to a single inheritable factor, whatever it might be. It does admit that no one can find it, or hasn't found it yet. It's also irritating for mentioning Progenitors as trying to get in on the action, because quite frankly, that setting and this one don't mix at all without everything blowing up.

                                        So, no, page 8, I don't accept what you're trying to sell me about most modern Garou referring to their ineffable state as being caused by a gene, even if it's in scare quotes. The ones who don't know much about it probably reject it as a term not becoming of spirit, and those that do probably reject it on the basis that there's nothing that they can isolate. Maybe next you'd like to convince me that someone is trying to figure out where their Crinos mass goes, using only typical instruments for gravity anomalies?

                                                Originally posted by Aya Tari View Post
                                                I imagine that, when we look at the effect of tasers in real life, it would require a Stamina (difficulty 6, 4 if they are drunk) roll to avoid taking one bashing health level of damage and to avoid being incapacitated for one minute. If you botch your Stamina roll, you take one health level of lethal damage and, in addition to being incapacitated for an hour, you have to make a second Stamina roll (difficulty 6) to avoid a heart attack. I think that would accurately reflect the ineffectiveness of tasers (being tear gassed felt much worse) and the lethality of tasers (you will kill someone you repeatedly tase them twenty or thirty times).
                                                If I were writing the rules for Tasers (as someone who has been hit with one -- in training), I'd do it this way (assuming an "Air-Taser"): Dex + Firearms attack roll, just like a handgun. If the roll succeeds, additional successes add to damage as normal (prongs hit over a wider area -- which worsens the effect), with a base rating of 6 Bashing (I'd also make "Animal-Tasers" -- which do exist, although I don't believe Taser International isn't marketing them anymore -- available with a code of 8 Bashing). Since it's Bashing, soak is possible, but with a twist: In addition to the basic Bashing damage, for the turn that the character is hit, any boxes of Bashing damage that he incurs in that round after soak, is tacked onto his Dex penalty (So if he takes 6 Bashing and soaks one, for that round he suffers a -7 Dex penalty -- -2 for the basic wound penalty and -5 for the number of boxes he didn't soak). If the target reaches Incapacitated, in addition to any overflow boxes (which become Lethal as normal), he has to roll Stamina -- a botch by a PC or important NPC results in a heart attack, with an additional box of Lethal each turn until somebody does an Wits + Medicine roll or otherwise heals him. A simple failure for an average NPC results in a heart attack.

                                                EDIT: For the turn that he get hit with the Taser, if the Dex penalty exceeds the character's total Dexterity (including form modifiers), the character is effectively immobilized and loses all actions that turn. A successful Rage roll (one net success) will negate this effect. Werewolves getting hit with a Taser must immediately check for Frenzy.
