        Here is another example of a vassal of Koschei.

        Gello

        Gello is one of the surviving Cappadocians of the Cult of Lamia. Raised in the cult as a mortal by her mother priestess and fathered by a nameless slave soon sacrificed after her inception, Gello was embraced by one of the Cappadocian vampires that belonged to the cult. Gello made her pact with Koschei after the diablerie of her clan's Founder and the ensuing pogrom against the Cappadocians by the Giovanni. The Pact preserved her, preventing her discovery despite the many divinatory powers possessed by the Giovanni.

        She now serves Koschei, but reconciles her duty to it and her worship of the Dark Mother by interpreting He Who Points as a servant to the Dark Mother, the Wise Old Man to the Dark Mother’s role as Wise Old Woman. She still controls a mortal group of the Cult of Lamia that has survived uninterrupted since the Neolithic, and even has made several childer in the centuries since her Pact.

        She resides in the wastes of the Atlas Mountains of North Africa, recruiting a host of monsters that haunt the Maghreb and slaughtering the occasional Garou who happens to come across her or her minions. She also has many minions among the local population. Some are ghouls, others mortal members of the Cult of Lamia, and even more are part of a secret society of Berber nationalists unaware they are controlled by an ancient vampire.

        As priestess of the Cult of Lamia, Gello and her small brood has access to many spirits, lore, and magic associated with the Dark Mother. Some mortal members of the cult have similar versions of these powers by using sorcerer Paths. Gello also has connections with various Bahari cults of vampires around the world who know her by reputation even if they do not know her actual clan. She is not a member of the True Black Hand, but those sect members who follow the Path of Lilith observe her with interest.

        The Silent Striders have identified a large area around the southern Atlas Mountains and northern Sahara as a kind of danger or forbidden zone. Since the fall of the dictator Moamar Gaddhafi, she has gained much influence in Libya as a result of its political chaos. Too many Silent Striders have disappeared in it. What few bodies have been found are mocked by strange black pox. They have heard about a mysterious figure called al-Kahina (the soothsayer) rumored to have supernatural powers, but so far have learned little.

            I conceive He Who Points as a universal figure, not limited to Russian folklore or geography (although I have no problem making him more dominant or prominent within that geography). So I think he should be present in the New World. This is my attempt at such. I really like the character of Chester R. Van Gelding in Rage Across New York, so this is my attempt at doing that kind of character with Koschei. But I also thought of doing such a character to an actual member of the "Five Civilized Tribes" who is a sworn of Koschei. Who knows, I may still do so.

            Hiram Van Tassel

            A Sworn of Koschei, Hiram was a Dutch fur trader in New Netherlands who made his pact with He Who Points (under the name Lox) during the Beaver Wars when he seized Indian jewelry from a dead Mahican. The jewelry was in fact one of the treasures of Koschei. He built a fur trading empire for over a century before expanding into real estate, textiles, and transportation as the newly created United States industrialized. The Van Tassel family's wealth would become the stuff of legend alongside the fortunes of the Astor, Vanderbilt, and Van Gelding families of the early United States.

            Hiram still secretly controls much of the Van Tassel fortune, but poses as a distant member of the Van Tassel family who always manages to be a member of the board of the Van Tassel Family Trust. As a result of his contacts from the fur trade, Hiram made alliances with a variety of bizarre Wyrm menaces throughout North America. Various Adlets, Basket Women, Kushtaka, Skin-walkers, Stick Indians, Water Babies and Wechuge serve Hiram as the emissary of He Who Points. Hiram's mortal family extends beyond those of the official Van Tassel heirs, as he had many wives among the Indian allies he traded furs with and sired many children.

            Hiram controls the Van Tassel Museum (officially the Van Tassel Museum of Colonial America and Native American Heritage) in New York City. Previously just the Van Tassel Museum of Colonial American History, it changed its name in the 1990s as a move towards political correctness. What has not changed is Hiram using the museum to hunt for additional treasures of Koschei that were possessed by the native Americans in the guise of historical preservation.

                    One reason I am posting various vassals of Koschei is that I want to make He Who Points a more universal figure so an ST can justify referring to the Talon regardless of the geography or time of her chronicle. Too many iconic concepts of the game seem to be limited to a specific time and place, so an ST who wants to be creative and set her chronicle in a non-standard geography or history could be left without many antagonists to be used. But I am trying to keep the "dark fairy tale" feel that seems associated with He Who Points for all of these vassals.

                    Nadilla

                    Nadilla is a Bygone, a Ghul, who resides in Iraq. She has hunted the wastes of Mesopotamia for thousands of years, but made her pact during the time of the Assyrian Empire in order to escape a potent vampire whose territory she had poached. While ghuls are naturally long lived, her pact with “Div-e Sepid” enabled her to survive far longer than usually in a hostile land. She has outlived the empires of the Achaemenids, Alexander, Rome, the Abassids, Seljuks, and the Ottomans.

                    Her last previous guise was as a wife to one of Saddam Hussein's sadistic generals before reinventing herself by devouring a returning Iraqi exile who worked as a human rights activist. This shapechanging cannibal is a cunning actress who always makes sure she has easy access to her next corpse meal. She is a sometimes ally to the Tal'Mahe'Ra and has ensorcelled several Nagaraja vampires to her will whose diet is similar to her own. She knows the resting spots of several ancient, torpored vampires. She protects them in exchange for favors of these Methusalehs' servants. Packs of fomori jackals and hyenas obey her call whenever she needs them. She's also made pacts with a local Nephwrack cult who dwell in a Haunt she occupies and can summon various Spectres to perform Oblivion's work in the Skinlands. Other less powerful Ghuls serve her in exchange for her favors and protection. While the fertile farms and cities of Mesopotamia are mostly safe from her grasp, she rules the desert wastelands.

                    Nadilla has accumulated a large fortune over the millennia she has survived, and buried great caches of wealth around the land of the Two Rivers. She is one of the most cunning creatures alive and has often survived shapechanged into animal form. However, she prefers the aspect of a beautiful, seductive woman, especially someone she's recently devoured. Her hobbies are to employ sadism and cruelty to ruin human lives, but has gathered a respectable force of supernatural protectors to fend off any who might seek to harm her.

                            Despite being a big Hellboy fan, I've been very inconsistent in the last decade at picking up the Hellboy/BPRD comics.                                     Going back to Baba Yaga herself in Rage Across Russia, does anyone still use her in a Werewolf chronicle? If so, is it just a running Rage Across Russia chronicle, or is she still some general setting detail in chronicles that do not take place there?

                                    If we go with the idea that Baba Yaga was destroyed (whether by the Garou, or as in Nights of Prophecy), what happened to her armies and her quest to recover Koschei's soul egg? Did they just fall apart without her?

                                    What would her new child Segei Voshkov do? He was extraordinarily powerful for being a vampire of just a few years old, but I think that was typical early edition sleight of hand that said young vampires could be given lots of powerful Disciplines temporarily by more potent, older vampires (like how Maldavis had many powers when the Primogen first backed her against Lodin, but they disappeared after they ended their support). Which would mean Voshkov would now have a more reasonable power spread and just have potent blood. Left by himself would he abandon Baba Yaga's plan and go back to doing whatever he wants on his own, or could he somehow be tempted to join Koschei by some means and begin rebuilding her forces?

                                    Could one of the Zmei take over? They'd be more likely to ally with Koschei on their own, and one does have the ability to shapechange into human form (Shazear) but certainly Rustarin with her Commanding Gaze power could become a leader, perhaps using Shazear as her tool to control mortals. Thought it is debateable whether the Zmei would elect to serve Koschei on their own.

                                    Another possibility is one of the Mages in her Army of the Arcane, who transfer their loyalty from Baba Yaga to Koschei thinking he can somehow help them inject more Dynamism into static reality. Like the Zmei, they were also tasked to find Koschei so they could know enough to try to make some kind of deal with it even if they never find the soul egg itself.

                                            If we're going to say Baba Yaga is gone and go down the list of her servants, this is what I'd do with each one.

                                            Viktor - 2000 year old Gangrel who was blood bond to Baba Yaga and is "General of the Night", leading nearly 25 vampires to carry out political and urban operations. It's said that only his love for Baba Yaga (which I imagine is something like a grandchild's love for a beloved grandmother) has kept him from "going into the earth to rest." So with her death I imagine he goes into prolonged torpor.

                                            Snaps-at-Shadows - Black Spiral Dancer who is the general of the Army of War, the collection of Black Spiral Dancers, corrupted Garou, and fomori who attack caerns and drain them. Probably just becomes an "ordinary" Black Spiral Dancer afterwards since not much has changed, but possibly lost influence among the tribe now that she's no longer supported by Baba Yaga. Possibly killed by the Garou during the past few decades.

                                            The Zmei - Can they still exists in the Realm now that Baba Yaga is destroyed? They only managed to remain within the realm as a result of her original summoning and binding. I think a strong case can be made that they should disappear back in the Umbra. They're probably among the most gonzo and overpowered elements in Rage Across Russia so many STs might want them gone. Might possibly let Shazear be the last Zmei to still be around if the ST wanted one to remain because he often shapechanges into human and has been active for so long. He's the least disruptive of the Zmei.

                                            Joseph Stranzvitch - Celestial Chorus leader of the Army of the Arcane. Assuming he became so corrupted by Baba Yaga that he is probably considered to be barabbi. Their major duty was to uphold and strengthen the Shadow Curtain, but also assisted the Army of Despair in trying to find Koschei. I assume with Baba Yaga's death, they have gone their own way. But Joseph (and his coven) remains a candidate to try to serve Koschei.

                                            Tamara Andreeva and the Volinchaya coven of Verbena - Since they were set up to be redeemable good guys to help the Garou in RAR, I assume they've recovered and have nothing to do with Koschei or Baba Yaga's remaining forces.

                                            Bezariel - the demon leader of the Army of the Void. Probably banished back into the Umbra.

                                            Sergei Voshkov - childe of Baba Yaga. A former spy and master manipulator, he's likely lost much of the powers he was given in RAR. But his potent blood and natural talents would still make him a powerful vampire for his age. The danger is that he's diablerie bait. Still retains control of most of his spy network, the "Eyes of Baba Yaga". Probably is part of the Camarilla using an assumed name, hiding his true identity. Still working on restoring Russia to great power status. He'll either stay behind scenes forever or eventually becomes a future Prince of Moscow, Justicar, or sit on the Inner Circle depending on the time frame.

                                            So if I wanted to remove Baba Yaga from the setting, but retain some sort of faction still loyal to Koschei using existing characters, I'd most likely have Joseph Stranzvitch be its new leader. Probably just call him the Black Monk or the Mad Monk to hide his identity and have yet another "Rasputin" analogue in the setting who can recruit new followers to help him serve Koschei whom he imagines is some kind of fairy tale king of Old Russia around which he can restore an Old Believer type church and turn back the clock to a more pure "Russia". Unlike Sergei Voshkov who follows the traditional Russian state, I imagine Stranzvitch to have a less state oriented model and more about some sort of cultural Old Russia that no state today could possibly embody. Very mystical and esoteric oriented.
