        Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
        So, does anyone have any thoughts on the Black Furies, Bone Gnawers, or Children of Gaia they'd like to share while I am waiting for post approval? Template ideas, thoughts about Camps, artwork or characters you really liked, etc.?
        Black Furies: Love the idea of the Tribe. Looking at gender through a mystical, primordial lens and taking inspiration from ancient practices is a great concept. I love the idea of the Tribe and what it stands for. On the other hand, the moment you try to bring it into present-day inevitably people will start talking about present-day-perspectives on gender (and maybe even start arguing about it). But historically gender is just such a more expansive subject, just reducing it to present-day-culture-wars takes away from an all-female-Tribe that's been there for millennia. Instead of expanding horizons on what gender means by looking at history and mysticism, things are just gonna trapped in the minutiae of present-day-debates. Great Tribe for when you know the people who sit at your table, bad Tribe for sidetracking things because gender is certainly quite the discussion topic. But I think the damage is done. Hell, we know that they killed the "all-female"-thing in W5 because one of the developers thought of the BF as "the feminist Tribe". Just sad that what the Tribe is and could be developed into often really just gets reduced to "the Feminist Tribe". Gender is a fascinating subject from a historical perspective especially since the WtA-framework allows for quite the grand landscape of various ideas/concepts over the millennia.

        Bone Gnawers: The focus for that Tribe shouldn't be pseudo-Nosferatu of the Garou-Nation focused on information-gathering but adaptability and survival. They are the Tribe who manages and organizes Garou-presences in the unlikeliest places you'd expect. Where the Glass Walkers manage to survive in the city because they're getting close to the Weaver, Bone Gnawers are all about surviving despite the odds. The places where no Garou wants to be, the struggles that won't earn you Renown as Garou conceive it and the battles that don't directly fight the Wyrm but indirectly do - that's where the Bone Gnawers are. They mingle, they adapt and they survive.

        Children of Gaia: I don't like the idea of reducing them to "peace-loving hippies". Rather than human references, the focus should be on what they represent in the Garou-Nation. The way I portrayed them in my games is that this is the Tribe that has no traditions - but beliefs instead. And they preach those beliefs. It's a Tribe more concerned with "mindset" than "legacy" when it comes to its members. They are the Tribe about what other Tribes think and/or do. Not just in the sense of approve/disapprove but they actively try to influence things. Whereas the Silver Fangs are all just arrogant assholes à la "nobless oblige", the Children of Gaia have a vision of what the Garou-Nation should be like (with Children of Gaia hardliners saying it has to be like that) and they're gonna make sure it turns into that. They say they don't want to be in charge but they have a lot of... opinions about who should do what and when in a Sept.

                    I actually like this Template a lot more than you. It's probably my favorite of the CoG templates. But it is crossover heavy and can lead to a lot of eye rolling. It's also a bit too hard on the nose and would have benefitted from less direct involvement with other games. But crossover was a good thing to be pursued during this time period of the WoD and not something that was frowned upon. So some adjustments would need to be done if translating this to newer versions that have a different outlook on this like moral vampires, Golconda, potential relations between Garou and vampires, etc. Despite that I think the core concept is OK. The character sheet is relatively OK except for the obvious mistakes these templates always make (why does a Supernmatural Investigator have zero points in Investigation?). The flavor text needs updating to whatever is appropriate for that player's chronicle.

A lot of White Wolf writers would write in-character text in a way that one must read it critically. I'd allow him to know one vampire as part of his background (maybe something like the Babe in the Woods or Lupine Impersonator in the Gangrel clanbook templates) and have a few leads on other kinds of creatures he could begin to encounter (information on a haunted house, a wise wilderness hermit of either the Dreamspeaker or Verbena traditions that is not obviously magical, a Stag spirit that knows where some faeries are located).

Given five different STs, this concept will have five very different experiences. Some STs will basically sandbag this kind of character because she does not want to spend the time and effort to master the lore of the other game lines (or create her own as replacement), and that is an acceptable decision for the ST.

                                Anodyne (Metis Philodox)

                                So, anodyne is an archaic term for painkillers, as well as a synonym for inoffensive. (Also, oddly, the name of the Stargazer signature character who first appeared in Rage Across New York, which was also written by Daniel Greenberg. I guess he just really likes that word for some reason.) Apparently this concept is supposed to evoke both meanings, as both a healer and as someone who tries to work as a mediator by not giving any offense to others. Which isn't all that bad of an idea.

                                Their parents tried to drown them after they were born, but they were saved and given to the Children of Gaia. The tribe is the only family they've ever known, and they claim not to care who their real parents were. If that's true, they're remarkably well adjusted.
                                They showed "special gifts" and are marked by the Tribal Totem. There really is no further development of the former and the latter is their Metis deformity, Unicorn Horn. And that's about as much as we get about that. But they are a skilled healer, both physically as well as emotionally/spiritually.

                                They try to act as a bridge between various forces and factions. As a Philodox, between passion and wisdom; as a Child of Gaia, between the tribes; and as a Metis between Homid and Lupus. The first two I can get behind. The third is maybe a little iffy, but I can understand the idea of having an outsider's perspective who isn't attached to either species.

                                The roleplaying hints describe them as being confident and authoritative, which is an interesting change from how Metis are typically presented. They also try to lead and teach by example, and aren't just all talk in their ideals.

                                As mentioned, the Metis deformity is Unicorn Horn. I honestly never really cared that much for this particular deformity, which was first presented in this book. I sort of feel like it was invented entirely for this character and is sort of a "I'm special" cop out for the usual breed weakness. The write up doesn't even mention any mechanics about how it impacts the character. Among other things, you'd at least think it was a major potential Veil breach.

                                Equipment is a walking staff, bandages, and a first aid kit.

                                The image appears to be human female, but as a certain androgynous quality to it that could be interpreted as gender fluid or some other non-binary. They are exceedingly thin and lithe. They appear to be wearing the bandages mentions in the equipment. Also a heck of a lot of jewelry. They almost look like they belong in a sword & sorcery/sword & sandals fantasy story. Plus the very blatant horn growing out of their forehead. It's not a bad image, even if the horn is kind of distracting. But like I said, it looks like they belong in a fantasy game rather than a modern "urban fantasy" horror game. Unless maybe they're part of some sort of weird isolated New Age cult.

                                They're listed as part of the Demeter's Daughters Camp. They're basically focused on trying to restore humanity's connection to the natural world. It fits the character more or less, but I wish the write up had worked this in somewhere.

                                For Attributes, Physicals are secondary with one extra dot from freebies, for 2/3/4. Socials are tertiary and all at 2. Mental is primary with 3/4/3.
                                I'm having a hard time making sense of this. The character is written as a healers and diplomat, someone who tries to build bridges between disparate factions. And yet their Social traits are all at 2. I feel like Socials should be secondary or primary, perhaps at 3/3/2 or 4/4/2. Physical should probably be tertiary here.

                                For Abilities, Talents are primary with one extra dot from freebies. Brawl (?!), Empathy, and Primal Urge are 3 each. Alertness is 2, and then 1 each in Athletics, Dodge, and Intimidation.
                                Skills are tertiary, with 2 each in Melee and Leadership, and 1 in Survival.
                                Knowledge is secondary, with 3 each in Enigmas and Rituals, 2 in Medicine, and 1 in Law.
                                These feel really wonky. The high level of Brawl especially. There's nothing in the write up to really explain this. The Medicine knowledge also feels slightly low given what a focus they have on healing. And as a bridge builder, nothing in Politics.

                                Backgrounds are 2 in Mentor and 3 in Past Life. It's nice to see something other than Allies or Contacts after so long. I feel like this is a character that could benefit from a Fetish, especially something that would hide that horn from humans.

                                Rage is 3, Gnosis is 3, and Willpower is 5.

                                Seven freebies were spent on an extra Gift, which I don't think is really necessary. I think those points would be better spent on Abilities or Backgrounds.

                                This is an interesting character. I don't exactly love it, but it seems a fairly solid and original idea. However, the character sheet is questionable in several areas. And I find the breed deformity kind of odd.

                                                        It seems like delayed posts have been able to go through but I figured I’d still share some ideas I’ve got floating around re the tribes covered so far, maybe it’ll be helpful for some folks.

                                                        •Black Furies: I covered a good chunk of my thoughts on them and interesting ways they could be used earlier in this thread. To sum things up briefly I think that the idea of them being avengers for violations of primordial and ancient laws is a solid avenue as well as the idea of them representing schools of thought and mysticism which were more active prior to what might be deemed the usurpation of the patriarch, a motif that may be particularly useful because a bunch of irl occultists and anthropologist types alike have written a lot on that notion so there’s plenty of inspirational material out there.

                                                        •Bone Gnawers: I think one can use the Bone Gnawers in a lot of ways. As the tribe of outcasts they can be used to represent everything from the weakest links culled by other tribes or proto-tribes who were destroyed before they could fully metastasize; perhaps you could flesh this angle out to have them also take on a dispossession theme to add to their rivalry with the Silent Striders. I agree with others who have said that the urban Gnawers are over-represented in tribal material and that the rural and itinerant (Bone Gnawer circuses?) sections of the tribe need more coverage. I do like the idea that the Bone Gnawers represent the largest tribe of Gaian Garou in the Final Nights, although the ranks of the Rabble make up a sizable chunk of that. A lot of Gnawers probably do live as Ghost Wolves, struggling to carve out chunks of privacy to live their own lives, away from the Nation. Rat I think would go along with those who choose to go that route, just as much as those who do stick around to fight. I’ve always liked the image of the Bone Gnawers as the Nation’s equivalent of the down-trodden and cynical plebs/peasants. Rallying young high-tribe Garou to flock to the war banner has always been relatively easy (it’s usually harder to let their tribal/Sept elders sign off on it), but rallying the large numbers of Bone Gnawers is relatively difficult. A lot of them don’t see any reason to join your own struggle without a good reason - even the active Warriors of the tribe have their own goals that they’d rather pursue. The age-old bargain is to offer the Gnawers territory, resources, patronage, protection, etc. in exchange for service - but they’ve been short-changed before and hard to persuade. As the Final Nights go on, fewer and fewer of Rat’s Children answer the war calls of even the most lucrative war packs. As a patron Rat is all about Survival - Rat is also a little crazy but what’s that between friends. Bone Gnawers often have their hands full dealing with the local Ratkin problems and the other scavenger and down-low types that go over-looked by other Garou. Something ironic about the Bone Gnawers is that they’re probably the most likely Garou to become involved in dealing with something causing problems for the average person - the Sept might not care about the fomor-run tainted gas station but the Gnawers who live nearby might. I think that Ragabadh is meant to be an archetypal Bone Gnawer auspice so I imagine all types of BG jokesters, clowns, trouble-shooters, investigators, etc. BG Theurges - Urban Shamans, Country Witch-Doctors, Dirtbag Pill Mixer (their batch will blow your mind right open). BG Philodox - Beleaguered parents/elders/siblings, community leader types, friendly deputies, itinerant folk judge. BG Galliard a Underground musician, newspaper/magazine hoarding info guru, folk preacher. BG Ahroun - Vigilante, Junkyard Dog, Enforcer, bounty hunter, etc.

                                                        •Children of Gaia: I’m fond of a lot of the additions and flourishes which Black Fox and No One of Consequence has added to this tribe. The idea of the tribe being concerned with protecting a universal purity and being guardians with a universal-ifying ideal clicked for me. I like the stuff with them being a competing leadership tribe. That said I do think there should still be a place for the hippies, the human-whisperers, and the moon-eyed mystics amongst them but those can all probably be camps or traditions amongst a variety of camps and traditions. I can easily imagine an interesting dynamic with competing camps who interpret Unicorn’s call for purity as a policing mandate which covers all Garou (perhaps all Gaians/life in general…) on one extreme and full new-age libertarian style camps on the other extreme calling for a peaceful reconciliation of all life back within the universal communion of the Mother. White Wolf tended to associate the Children of Gaia with hippies and new age stuff but tbh I do think interesting stuff could be done by fleshing out potential ties they may have with Christianity and Islam (as well as other sorts of universalist confessional type systems). I think the image of the Coggie peace keeper has been around for so long that I don’t want to lose it entirely from Werewolf but it would be nice to flesh out other interpretations Garou could come up for to match Unicorn’s mandate of Purity.
