So, 'vague request' or 'wrong spirit' are somewhat unlikely scenarios. Your example following not withstanding, or course.

    But, I also had NPCs like Mentors or pack totems recommend or even force PCs to learn a Gift they deemed suitable or necessary for the character.     Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    The OP mentions the Danger Sense Gift.     True, that could happen. But as ST I have the ability to communicate with the player OOC in parallel to the scene developing. So, if I wasn't sure what Gift the player was aiming for, I'd simply ask. After all it is a collaborative game to some extend. If there are obstacles the PCs can't overcome because of OOC reasons, like me not wanting the character to have a specific Gift (at this point or ever), I'd communicate that OOC. Just like I would tell players that they can enter Maleas with their Fostern pack, but every character will die or become otherwise unplayable.

    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    Let's also not forget context here like the Firebringer Gift. It's a Rank 6 Gift for a Ragabash to turn another supernatural's power into a Gift, which they can't use themselves, they can only pass that on to others. And they have to get hit with said power as well."In theory" arguments where things are technical possible doesn't address how easy or likely something is to happen. As noted finding a new or lost Gift is Renown worthy because it's special. It's work and effort that a character has to put in, not something for players to get because they read about a Gift in a Fera supplement and decide they know how to ask the spirits for it.
    It is worth 7 Wisdom to find a new Gift in W20. That is the same as finding three mates and procreate thrice. Teaching other Garou is worth 3 to 5, depending of field of study. So, it is kind of difficult the set things into relation here. But that is true with most Renown table entries, since they are short statements without context.

    In any case, I would think Garou finding completely new Gifts or rediscover forgotten ones happens rarely, since most tribes tend to teach knowledge of Gifts already known to their members and most are good with the options they have (and possibly live with the knowledge that going beyond these choices might be untraditional).

    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    Considering there's an entire Tribe of Garou that are dedicated to trying to figure out new Gifts to learn as technology spirits evolve as technology changes, and they've managed to work out less than a dozen Gifts from that process in the last half century, I again point out that "possible" does not mean," easy" or "likely."
    True. Although it can be up the the ST if he believes the Gifts printed are the only ones in existence. Or, if opening the game up to more possibilities. I mean, since the Storyteller's Vault is up, you don't even have to come up with Gifts yourself any more...

    It is maybe to sub-par editing on White Wolf's side that there are some Gifts that are vaguely identical to others, and some that have a similar theme but different mechanics - for authors seemingly didn't know that they were creating duplicates. Meanwhile there is a word count for books and I'm sure, there were plenty of more ideas for Gifts that could have made it to print...

    For balancing reasons, I usually only allow Gifts that are printed in my game's though. But that is an "a can of worms once opened"-problem. For a small troupe I could course-correct if a home-brewed Gift was turning out to be chronicle breaking. For a 20+ players LARP chronicle I can't do so easily.

    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    And? Let them try. Making it easy ruins the whole point. These sorts of things are supposed to be a challenge (or not, there are a bunch of pretty potent mind-control Gifts in the Garou lists, it might not be a neat package like Dominate but it's not like there's nothing there at all).

    The ST's job is just to make sure it's clear to the player how hard that task is going to be in their game so the player is on the same page as them.

    How much free time do your PCs have? How is their pack, Sept, family, and Tribe, going to feel about them going, "Hey folks, I saw this neat thing on TV, so I'm going on a months long quest into the Umbra to see if I can find a spirit that knows a Gift that will teach it to me even though I could just learn a well known Gift that's 95% the same thing!"
    My point was more like this:

    It is easy to find a reason why a Garou would find inspiration for power to desire - be it Galliard tales or modern media. It is a bit trickier to think of a spirit who might have a power like this on the 'Gift Lore' entry (assuming all spirits have this, not just the one's specifically printed in Axis Mundi). From there it is either to find the spirit in the Umbra, find paraphernalia to help in a summoning or outright summon a spirit, Call of Duty if a spirit name is known. And from there the negotiations with the spirit start. And the result can be that the Gift is leaned that is identical to a Gift commonly found by another Fera.

    One doesn't need to be a member of the Ahadi or the Beast Courts for that. If one is, then one is even more exposed to tales of the powers of the other Fera, but it isn't a pre-requisite.

    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    You... disagree with the idea that PCs should have an IC rationale for how they learned something? Would you let a Garou that's never held an external weapon in their life buy Melee? Or would you make them justify it via training and instruction?
    Depends. I could rationalise that a PC used mental attributes and therefore is allowed to increase them with XP. I'm not tracking if the player ever rolled any dice pool with mental attributes. Does the player have to say "my character is sitting down to solve sodokus" at least thrice to allow the increase. Is plastic surgery required for Appearance increases (thank god V5 got rid of that trait)? Is Charisma increased by training or would training in these field not raise Expression, Leadership or Subterfuge instead?
    How much devotion has a PC to show to increase Totem? How does a character show how in-tune with the world one is to raise Gnosis?

    Players usually have a rationale for why they want to increase a trait and there is often an easy way to find an IC rationale as well. I encourage players to write down a few sentences on why they think their character's traits should be increased (especially since for the LARP game, this runs through a web interface), so their personal character development is a bit fleshed out.

    And, we have a system in place to prohibit rapid increase in stats, so once you raised a stat, you have to use the new stat for a couple of games, until you can raise it again. In a tabletop game a ST would discuss this with the players but for fairness' sake and a collaborative play that isn't working in LARP. Also, we use the games that happen to measure, not the real world time to easily adjust for games that have to be rescheduled or canceled.

    Anyway, if we would use the Instruction skills rules from PGttG, the times to increase a stat would drastically change, and be partially up to luck (dice rolls). That might overall be a more accurate simulation of the real world, but then again, it is a role playing game not a simulation.

    So, I can totally understand that STs and players want an IC rationale for what the character learns or raises, but also totally understands if that is rather abstract or hand waived, too. It doesn't always contribute to the story or the game flow if the IC rationale is discussed in detail. Again, Gifts are probably something I would discuss in more detain, unless the group and I agreed on one of the alternative approaches to Gift learning as detailed in Axis Mundi.

    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    Beats are literally just XP with more player agency in when they get awarded instead of ST fiat.
    True, but that can make a difference in the feeling of how to are spend, too. MtAw had a passage that players could have their characters perform relatively simple tasks that they make a bit harder by adding conditions and therefore earn beats. And the rules state that this is okay, that is what training is. You make things harder for you to hone your skills. The WoD XP don't work that way.

    Personally, I found RuneQuest's approach rather nice. It had occasionally random increases in stats (due to rolling botches), but also had downtime with a specific number of hours of training and the players could award these to the skills they are trying to increase. The increases were not automatic, especially if the skill was already at a high level. Therefore the characters didn't increase in their abilities in a comparable way, but if the troupe is okay with that, why not.

    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    And? I'm not talking about that. I'm just saying that players need to explain the IC methods of how their character learned something before they get to spend XP on it. Something that applies to all traits, not just Gifts. Hell it even applies to things like Backgrounds, Renown, and Rank, and you don't spend XP on those.
    And I say, if the ST and players all are fine with one characters raise of medicine two to medicine three they don't need to detail the IC methods.
    If the raise is trivial, that is.

    There, for example, is no need to define if the part of the story three sessions ago, when the character did a lot of mundane first aid is a suitable reason to increase the trait now (since there weren't XP around then, but now are), or if there is need to get instructions from an authority or if autodidaction can be a thing for Medicine or if using "Mother's Touch" often is a reason, even when it is now (in W20) rolled against Empathy, rather than Medicine. Or even if the character needs to study for a month then have the instructor roll Manipulation + Instruction against 10 minus the characters Intelligence. If only 3 successes were scored, the character has to study another month, for the last remaining success to allow to spend 4XP. Because that is what the PGttG states.

    Why go through this, if in everyone's head the increase is okay and doesn't need a specific IC reasoning?

                    Off screen does not mean not discussed. It means it didn't warrant enough importance to come up at the table.

                    On screen: Stuff that happens at the table during scenes.
                    Downtime: Stuff that happens at the table, but outside of the focus of specific scenes.
                    Off screen: Stuff that doesn't happen at the table, but still happens in the narrative.

                    "Hey, this current story has left my PC with plenty of time to work on his clinic, would it be cool to raise [Trait] even though it didn't come up last session?"
                    "Yeah, that's cool."
                    "Thanks."

                    Notice that the player and ST are still on the same page, and if not there is room to make sure they can get there.
