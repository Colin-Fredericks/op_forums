    The Gift's writeup says it makes people go in circles as they try to follow you - to me that's powerful. In addition the resisted roll is stacked in your favour.

    It also says any attempt to follow you is frustrated - the Gift doesn't have a specific target, the writeup is written in a way which seems to protect your passage, a particular journey you take. It doesn't say you frustrate a particular group, or even require you to know you're being followed. If you're going somewhere secret, somewhere you need to go without being followed, you can activate it.

    As for it being overridden by Beat, and similar, you're dealing with two sets of absolutes. Beat lets you hear the heartbeat of your target growing stronger or weaker; Ley Lines causes people to innately go in circles, take wrong turns and so on. The Garou effected by the Gift would note that the heartbeat isn't getting any louder, they'd realize they're lost, but it wouldn't override the wording of Ley Lines in my opinion, both based on wording and the relative levels of the Gifts. If it happened at my table I might rule that having Beat of the Heart Drum reduces the difficulty of the Occult roll to track the Ley Lines user, and like I say it would help you realize you're being misled sooner.

    In the case of Pulse, someone using Ley Lines is attempting to hide, so you'd need to roll the Perception + Enigmas. Having an unerring sense of what direction your prey is from you is great, but if you're under the effect of a Gift which forces you to go in circles or take wrong turns... it's an absolute vs an absolute. Given that PotP has a resisted roll, and Ley Lines has a resisted roll, the simplest way to settle it is to do both of the resisted rolls separately and then interpret the results as fairly as you can.

    It's worth noting that Ley Lines isn't all that great if you have a poor Wits + Occult dice pool, but Silver Claws isn't that great if you have a poor Dexterity + Brawl pool; I don't want to make every single Gift a *! Ultimately my taste and my opinions will inform decision-making here, but I appreciate your take all the same.

        Rank 3 Glass Walker Gifts

        Control Complex Machine: Spend a Willpower, roll Manipulation + Science/Computer, difficulty determined by complexity, and on a success you can command the relevant machine. Given that you could command any device to unlock, alarm, freeze, shut down, provide you with information... hell, if you're desperate for money, this could get you a huge amount from a cash machine. Just make sure you disable the camera first!

        This Gift is a bit like a sonic screwdriver, especially when you have Control Simple Machine in addition. One thing that bothers me about the two Gifts is that Control Simple Machine covers the simplest of mechanisms, Complex Machine covers electronics, and there's a gap. Complex non-electronic devices aren't covered by either Gift. There's no level that will set off a simple non-electronic mine, or cause an old-fashioned printing press to produce a different headline. It feels like there should be a level 2 Gift in the middle; Control Simple Machine, Control Mechanism, Control Electronics, that sort of thing.

        Still, based on the RAW, this is a pretty handy Gift which I quite enjoy. The limitless potential of the Gift does make it a Green, but I will point out that Plug and Play is often a better choice. Sure, it's hard to wrap your N64 controller around the mobile phone or detonator a bad guy is holding, and this Gift will let you do some things that Plug and Play won't! But in most situations I think you'll get more mileage out of Plug. It lasts longer, lets you apply your skills to the device consistently, and gives you a degree of ongoing intuitive control which this Gift might struggle with; having to spend WP again, and roll again, could prove tiresome. Also Plug has no difficulty to set up, and you may find the rolls you have to make once you're "plugged in" are easier than the relatively high difficulty of this Gift. Getting a laptop to do anything at all is difficulty 8!

        Still Green, but use it wisely and try mundane methods and lower level Gifts first.

        Intrusion: Spend a Gnosis, roll Gnosis (difficulty 7), and for one minute all barriers you approach will unlock and so on to let you pass. This includes barricades, boards nailed to doors, vaults. It's cool to envisage a Glass Walker running up to a random car and piling into it without even having to pause to disable the lock.

        Still... we have Control Simple Machine and Control Complex Machine. We have Open Seal, which costs 3/5 against this costing 9/15. Walking around with the freedom to use other Gifts, and not have to roll to unlock anything, is neat. But is it worth 9xp?

        Furthermore it's worth noting that this gift doesn't open doors ahead of you, it unlocks them. If barriers literally sprang open as you charged at them this would be much cooler, and much more useful.

        Were I fixing this Gift I might remove the Gnosis cost, and make it so that the Gift causes barriers to unlock and open at your whim, unless they are magical. I'd then put in a specific system for overcoming such barriers. As it stands Intrusion gives you automatic successes on your checks to overcome such magical locks and barriers, equal to your successes on the Gnosis check. This combos well with Open Seal, funnily enough, but having both feels like a waste.

        All in all I'll call it Amber - it isn't god-awful, but I wouldn't take it personally.

        Electroshock*: This Gift is relatively auspice-dependent; it's naturally stronger the higher your permanent Rage rating. The good thing about this Gift is that it inflicts automatic, unrolled, undodgeable Aggravated damage. You aren't rolling 5 dice of Aggravated, you're inflicting 5 points of Aggravated. I'm slightly reminded of Create Elements' fire option, though that has a cap of 3 levels of Aggravated, and of course spending and rolling Gnosis is a poor combo for most.

        At the upper end, with 9 Rage, you can inflict 10 Aggravated against a target by spending 5 Rage in one go, which will kill most targets, or force a Remain Active check. 7 Rage is perhaps more realistic, and permits you to slam a Garou / Fera / other corporeal powerhouse with 8 Aggravated in one go. And a typical Ahroun/Galliard, with Rage 5, could hit a target with 6 Aggravated.

        One good thing about this Gift is how precise, how controlled it is. Slamming a Garou, or any standard character in the WoD, with 6 Aggravated is a great way to almost kill them; it's ironically a pacifist's ideal opening gambit. Yeah, we can fight, if you feel like it, but right now you're crawling and adding a -5 penalty to all your checks.

        The ability to split damage among multiple targets is nice-ish, but a bit niche. It could help you to kill a bunch of really weak spirits, or finish off a bunch of wounded foes, but you're unlikely to know precisely how much damage each of them needs to take to die! One possible situation where it could shine is if you're against a pack of BSDs where more than one has just rolled to Remain Active; it means you know that they're all very close to death, so applying 1-2 damage each could be hugely impactful.

        Of course having a permanent Rage of 7 or 9 can completely transform your life for the worse, especially as a Glass Walker, and it would be strange to rate the Gift on the assumption of a Rage rating that high! Overall I'd say that an Ahroun of this Tribe having the ability to spend 3 Rage to inflict 6 Aggravated (before soak of course!) at will, and in such a cool and appealing way, is just about worthy of a Green. It should go without saying that the * relates to your starting Auspice and what level of Rage you've reached by Rank 3!

        Elemental Favor*: This Gift doesn't quite explain what it does well enough. We are told that you can beg, threaten or cajole an Urban Elemental Spirit into doing you a favour. For instance you might convince a glass elemental to shatter its pane of glass. The other examples are forcing a door to remain shut, or making a car's breaks fail... which would be thanks to bribing a metal or plastic elemental, perhaps? We aren't really told how this gift defines an "urban elemental", we aren't told whether we need to be speaking to the spirit in the Umbra, we aren't told whether the spirit will understand us or not without Spirit Speech.

        Does this Gift let you walk up to a glass skyscraper, and threaten the glass elementals within to shatter their glass? From the real world? Who knows. And honestly... we have too many Gifts that do this! Gremlins would be ideal in the Glass Walker list, and I'd strongly recommend you simply replace this Gift with Gremlins. And ultimately Command Spirit is a level 2 Gift; paying 10xp for that pretty universal Gift beats paying 9xp for whatever this does.

        I'm leaving this one unrated. If you want to know how good it is, your guess is as good as mine. My best guess would be Red, since Control Simple Machine and Control Complex Machine, along with Gifts like Jam Technology and even Gremlins if you can access it, do all this and better. I'm left wondering what this level 3 tribe-specific Gift is even meant to shine at. What is it doing that the others aren't? What are its limitations and requirements?

        ================================================== =

        If you have 5+ Rage anyways, Electroshock might be worth purchasing off-list. And if you're dedicated to handling technology, Control Complex Machine might be worth 15xp... but overall I'd suggest going for Plug and Play. It's far cheaper (5 vs 15xp) and will help you handle most situations.

                Rank 3 Red Talon Gifts

                Elemental Favor*: Not again!

                This one is identical to the Glass Walker version, except it deals with the four classical western elements, fire water earth and air. While that part is easier to understand, I still don't know what this Gift does, or how it does it. Unrated for the same reasons.

                Render Down*: This Gift lets you roll your Rage, difficulty 7, or perhaps 6 if you're in Crinos depending on your reading of the Rage rules, and each success reduces 20 pounds of artificial substance down to its raw components. For instance you might convert a car into a pile of raw metals, oils and unworked leather, along with sand for the glass. This is a showy, magic-like and decently powerful Gift. It has no cost, so the potential to simply melt a whole village piece by piece is kinda interesting.

                The risk is that you could roll 4+ successes on the Rage check and Frenzy. So keep that in mind.

                Very few dangerous machines can stand to lose 20 pounds of critical materials; that could punch a whole in an engine, or a console, or the wiring, so you only really need one success to disable most machines. And yes we have too many Gifts which work to that effect, way too many, but all in all I like this one well enough. This is essentially a much better Call the Rust and Gift of the Termite.

                Calling this one Amber, since you irreparably destroy what you're targeting instead of disabling it, and in many situations you could simply destroy artificial objects using your insane strength and wicked claws. The ability to total a helicopter or car mid chase is great though, of course. This might be Green if you're playing a Red Talon who is immersed in a world of high tech contraptions, especially going after Glass Walkers, BSDs in cities, Pentex, Technomages and so on. In that case the world is made of wood, and you're a fucking flamethrower.

                As for why this isn't as good as Gremlins, it's a question of dice pool and outcome. Gremlins is Attribute + Ability, and has no cost. This is Rage, and has no cost. Similar enough, except realistically your dice pool will be 1-5 and you risk Frenzying when you use it, if you have a pool of 4+ dice. The risk might not be huge, but it'll happen eventually if you make frequent use of this Gift.

                Furthermore with Gremlins you disable the whole machine quite reliably, even with 1 success (though it's temporary). Render Down forces you to reduce a chunk of the machine to its base components, which is worse for the Veil (it's an inexplicable event) and also more obvious to onlookers. It's still good, don't get me wrong, and in the right game it could easily be Green. When all the risks and little differences are taken into account, and Render Down being limited to manmade materials while Gremlins will make a wooden club or shield fail, you can see how Gremlins works out better.

                Territory*: This Gift makes you a pretty superb guardian of a Caern or Sept, even if you aren't currently physically present. In a game focused on traveling this is less useful, but even then, marking your hotel room and seeing it freely for 5 days since is pretty great.

                The flavour isn't ideal, especially for a female garou in Homid form, but even in urban environments this is a great tool to spy on others. I actually wish this were a Lupus Gift; it seems a shame to attach it to a Tribe which is relatively rarely played in my experience.

                A difficulty 7 Perception + Primal-Urge is simple enough, and as a Lupus Garou you'll be doing well at Perception in your breed form, and also have the Gnosis to support this Gift quite well. Having 5 places you can view at will is pretty great for spying, tracking, patrolling and so on.

                Calling this one Green, but if you're constantly on the move, or are pushed into environments which you couldn't have possibly prepared in every session, it might wind up Amber.

                Trackless Waste*: Spend a Gnosis, roll Intelligence + Primal-Urge (difficulty 6), effect a 3.2km radius of wilderness per success - based on the Gift's introduction, this area of wilderness should be your home or territory, you can't use this to launch a psychic terror attack against another Sept! Within that area humans fail all attempts to navigate. Garou may roll Perception + Primal-Urge, difficulty unstated (so 4-6 depending on form, most likely), and if they beat the Talon's successes they can navigate freely.

                The Gift only lasts until sunrise.

                I like the absoluteness of humans being automatically effected. I like that Garou can resist. The short duration annoys me, as does it being so restricted in where you can use it. It feels a bit like an NPC power. I'm also unsure how Mages, Changelings, Vampires and so on would fare, as they aren't wholly human. I'd personally rule they get a high difficulty Perception + Awareness (or equivalent) to navigate normally.

                If your game is static, and features defending a Red Talon Caern from being despoiled by humans, this Gift could be Green. In most games I'd call it a Red, due to its very precise restrictions. Since it isn't useless, and the mechanics are easy enough to understand and utilize well, I'm calling it an Amber with a * - pick it up if it applies to you, don't if it doesn't.

                ================================================== =======

                Territory might be worth 15xp on other Lupus, and other high-Gnosis PCs, especially if you are committed to guarding a place or acting as a spy/patrolman.

                                Rank 3 Shadow Lord Gifts

                                Direct the Storm: I love this. It has amazing flavour; the puppetmaster, the director, the Beta who is secretly the Alpha. This Gift lets you guide a frenzying Garou to do as you please. Note that this works on someone in Fox Frenzy as well! So if a member of your pack has tucked tail to run, you can direct them to attack regardless.

                                Spend a Gnosis, roll your Willpower against a difficulty of the target's Rage; you control them for two turns per success, basically picking their targets. You can set them on anyone you choose, potentially even themselves! If you try to use this on someone in Thrall, it works just as well, but you risk Frenzying yourself.

                                A good use for this is against a frenzying BSD. RAW it doesn't work on Fera or Vampires however; personally I'd increase the difficulty but allow it. If your target lacks Rage, I'd set the difficulty to 9 by default; the frenzy you're controlling is alien to you.

                                This is incredible; it has utility as both a support power to back up a frenzying ally and make sure they stay 'on target', and also as an offensive power to mess with a rival pack or BSDs. The uses are pretty obvious, it's just a solid win for the Shadow Lords.

                                Icy Chill of Despair*: This one's a little vague. You become very intimidating and begin to grow larger, colder and darker as shadows grow around you. This is impressive, and a Veil risk. You concentrate for a turn (annoying) and spend a point of Gnosis (ditto), and roll Manipulation + Intimidation difficulty 7. Anyone meaning you harm has to roll Willpower difficulty 8 - if they fail then they have to spend WP to oppose you in any way, whether it's attacking you or speaking against your ideas.

                                The problem is that we don't get a duration. Does it last the whole scene? Who knows. Does someone have to spend WP to oppose you once, or a WP every time they want to oppose you? Who knows. You can make good guesses as to these, but it's hard to say. As a result I'm leaving this unrated.

                                A very problematic aspect of this Gift, however you rule it, is that spirits cannot spend Willpower RAW. By the books, a Nexus Crawler has dots in Willpower, but no temporary points. Same with Gnosis and Rage. They literally cannot spend a point of WP to ignore this Gift and attack you, which is mad. That, in combination with the Gift's vagueness, means I'm leaving it unrated. However if it works as I'd expect, you need to spend a WP each time you resist the target (lasting for a turn each time) and it lasts for a scene, it would be Green... despite the high cost of initiating it.

                                Paralyzing Stare: Concentrating for one turn sucks. A turn is the whole turn - it is composed of actions. But you concentrate for a whole turn, sure. Spend a Gnosis, roll Charisma + Intimidation (difficulty of the target's Willpower), and they become frozen for one turn per success (can't move or attack, but they CAN defend themselves). And they need to be able to see you.

                                This is perfectly fine. It isn't busted, it isn't awful. It's a tad conservative for a Rank 3 Gift, and it feels like most of its job is done by True Fear (which doesn't cost Gnosis, or take up the whole round, or stop you from doing bonus actions), but it's good.

                                The advantage of this Gift is that you can simply freeze an NPC and stop them from being an issue; True Fear makes foes run around in terror, which can cause problems. This neutralizes. That's pretty good! I'm making this one a Green, but not for its use in combat (though that is handy) - it's more for its use outside of combat, or in preventing combat. Outside of a fight, concentrating for a whole turn is a much smaller cost. And being able to completely freeze anyone who can see you is great!

                                Keep in mind that if you can't be seen (I'm a bit smug about my Metis being blind right now) this Gift won't do anything. Many Banes, and some Fomori, will be immune to this Gift as a result.

                                Shadow Cutting: Being able to attack someone's shadow is gold. I love this. Amazing flavour, incredible stuff.

                                Then you get to the mechanics... you have to spit into their shadow. Sure, a bit of flavour, fine. It makes it obvious, but that's a cool drawback for a strong gift. And you have to spend Gnosis... so you're in a fight and you have just given up your ability to do bonus attacks... and it only works with claws and Fetish weapons, such as Klaives. And only you can strike the shadow.

                                All of that would be fine if you had a -2 difficulty to hit or something. But all it does, ALL it does, is remove 2 dice from their dodge pool and make parrying impossible.

                                Many, many enemies don't have an action to dodge with. Of those who do, -2 dice is a minor hindrance. This is atrocious. And it feels like such a waste of such a cool idea!

                                Some ideas to change this Gift, which can be used in combination with eachother:

                                - Pack members can also strike the shadow
                                - All attacks, even bullets and so on, harm the target if they hit the shadow
                                - The target has +2 difficulty to defensive actions, rather than losing 2 dice from dodge

                                A niche and interesting use of this Gift would be to set things up, either through magic or lighting or just planning ahead of time (the sun will be behind him if we strike at this time of day...), so that your foe has a VERY long shadow. A person with a light source behind them can cast a huge shadow, and narratively speaking that would let you stab the air fifty meters away from them and kill them. But even then? RAW it's just -2 dice to dodge. The mechanics give us nothing.

                                Under the Gun: This Gift makes a target a magnet for bullets and other missiles - at least in theory. The name feels a little anachronistic to me personally, and I'd rather it be about bows or rocks or similar, but still, could be a modern rename. The Gift is taught by a Raven spirit after all, not a Gun spirit, so it feels potentially ancient.

                                You touch your target specifically with your fingertips, spend a Gnosis, and roll Manipulation + Firearms (personally I'd allow Archery as well). If you succeed the target is -2 difficulty to hit with missiles (arrows, bullets, rocks) for 1 day per success.

                                Part of the appeal of this Gift is that it helps to trigger a 'hunting accident', but there's nothing in the mechanics which suggests you are more likely to be accidentally hit by gunfire or arrows; it's -2 difficulty to someone trying to hit you. If you can get this off against an enemy before a fight it's neat enough, especially if you're luring a BSD into an ambush of Kinfolk with silver ammo... it's just quite restrictive.

                                The cost, the fact you have to roll, the slightly limited mechanics... it's going to shine most in the hands of an effective deceiver who interacts with enemies cordially with traps laid in the future. The long duration saves this from being an auto-ignore, and I like the flavour. Personally I'd go a little further and make it -2 difficulty to hit with missiles and -2 difficulty on the damage rolls as well; this would help feed into the idea of the 'hunting accident' - any stray rock or arrow or bullet that hits you is more likely to find a major artery or some vital organ.

                                Uses of this feel pretty obvious; handshake with a business partner the day before their attempted assassination, patting the back of a Pentex foreman for a job well done, but honestly I don't want to be spending Gnosis while making direct contact with mysterious NPCs if I can help it - it feels like asking for trouble.

                                ================================================== ====================

                                Direct the Storm and Paralyzing Stare are both worth getting out-of-list if you can, though Stormcrows are very unlikely to teach outsiders! If your Pack Totem is a Stormcrow you might fare better. Under the Gun, though meh, could be worth buying if you're a gun-obsessed BG or GW who wants to add another tool to their arsenal.

                                    Rank 3 Silent Strider Gifts

                                    Adaptation: You'll be unharmed by poison, disease and any environment (space, volcano, and so on) so long as you have this Gift active. Spend a Gnosis, roll Stamina + Survival (difficulty 7), and this lasts for one hour per success. Amazing in the Umbra, and in extreme situations such as Pentex HQs and poison swamps.

                                    Used offensively, RAW you can grab a foe and jump into lava, out the airlock, and so on. This Gift is amazing both defensively and aggressively. It's also pretty great when used deceptively; you won't be drowned or killed by an airless environment, or poison, but playing dead could be very strong indeed.

                                    The ability to put poisons and diseases on pause is pretty amazing against Banes and Fomori as well. The cost and roll is a little annoying, but if this Gift were fully passive it would be far too good!

                                    If you're being chased, or are able to set the scene for a fight, this is pretty great. Talk to your ST about the difference between a hazardous environment (toxic air) and a hazardous situation (falling) though!

                                    Great Leap: Spend a Willpower, roll Strength + Athletics, leap 100 feet per success. Any form, any situation really. Moving 300 feet in 3 seconds is 68 miles per hour, so with a pretty conservative result on the roll you'd catch up to almost anything short of a vehicle moving at maximum speed.

                                    The mobility granted by this Gift, even in Homid, is amazing. A Lupus Garou can get 21 feet per success if they use Hare's Leap to its fullest extent, and the roll is difficulty 3; they'd need 5+ successes to have any chance of matching this Gift.

                                    Let's compare a Lupus with Hare's Leap and 11 dice on their roll with a Silent Strider with this Gift and 11 dice. The Lupus averages 7.7 successes, which is potentially 168 feet. If the Strider rolls even 2 successes, and indeed they average 4.6, they will leap further. Furthermore the Strider could be in Crinos form, and also they aren't doing a regular jump; this power doesn't demand you run up and move athletically, it's magic (my logic there being that Hare's Leap interacts with the regular jumping roll, whereas the Strength + Athletics roll for this Gift is the Gift's activation roll).

                                    The HL user does also need to pass a difficulty 7 check to use their Gift in the first place. But interestingly these Gifts do combine! You can double the distance covered by Great Leap to 200 feet per success!

                                    Mark of the Death-Wolf*: Spend a Gnosis point, and one turn, carving the mark with your claw. Roll Wits + Occult (difficulty 7). For one week per success Ghosts are attracted to the mark, causing the area to become "badly haunted".

                                    I wish this one were less vague, or gave examples of what might happen as a result. Making someone's house badly haunted could wind up killing them, sure, but it could also just make their house a hangout for Ghosts who play pranks. It's a bit like a much, much, much weaker version of the Fianna's fae-summoning Gift. You're calling upon another splat, inherently variable (depending on your ST's knowledge of the splat, their interest in involving them, how powerful they think they are, and the precise NPCs who show up), and letting things unfold as they may. At least with Fae they're showing up immediately and feel compelled by the Dreaming to help you. Here it's just... putting up a lure for ghosts, who may well hate you for bothering them!

                                    Potential uses include luring the cause of a haunting to a particular location to make it easier to fight, messing with a rival pack, harassing a Pentex executive's home, and much more. But the results are so ST-dependent (I know next to nothing about Arcanoi and Wraith mechanics, some STs know a lot) that it's basically impossible to rate. Assuming a basically competent haunting, I'll make this an Amber.

                                    "Angered Wraiths can be very dangerous" "this Gift causes Ghosts to become very angry with nearby humans, blaming them for sins which are not their own", "this Gift draws in the worst kinds of Ghosts" etc. would make it so much more clear what you can expect. Personally I'd love it if this Gift had another option to it; the sign you carve draws Wraiths in, and you can fill the sign with positive or negative energies. That way you could draw in regular old sane Wraiths to make them a hangout, where you can gossip with them and so on, or you can draw in the worst of the worst, at your own option.

                                    Sense the Unnatural: See the Lupus entry. Great for hunting down ghostly and vampiric influences!

                                    ================================================== =================

                                    Adaptation is worth it on any Garou, I'd argue. Ditto with Great Leap.

                                        Rank 3 Silver Fang Gifts

                                        Burning Blade: Pretty disappointing, despite being conceptually very cool.

                                        Roll your Gnosis, difficulty 7, and your weapon is set on fire for one turn per success. This means you deal 2 additional dice of Aggravated fire damage - pretty good at ignoring some kinds of armour, setting things alight, making ancient vampires flee in terror, and so on. What's annoying is how wimpy the duration is; rolling Gnosis at a difficulty of 7 may reliably give 1-2 successes, and that means the Gift will run out. It also makes it harder to use as a setup in advance; Silver Claws lasts for a whole scene, while if you use this a turn before combat starts it might elapse too swiftly.

                                        You then have to deal with the fact that melee simply isn't the best possible option for Garou - though this would combine well with difficulty 5 Fetish weapons and the hand blade Gift.

                                        I'm marking this as Amber - if you're a melee focused Garou there's no reason not to have this, but you'll find it elapses mid fight in many cases. Being able to start fires is handy, sure, but as a rank 3 Garou you aren't hankering for a way to start small fires by setting a knife ablaze.

                                        Silver Claws: See the Ahroun entry. This Gift is potentially more risky on a non-Ahroun, it should be noted; an Ahroun can burn off excess Rage produced by this Gift very swiftly, while a character with a Rage of 1 or 2 may struggle if they fall behind for some reason; on a turn where you spend or roll Gnosis you won't be able to burn off the Rage given by this Gift, and it could accumulate over time. Regardless of your Rage, however, it's a great Gift.

                                        Talons of the Falcon*: Spend a Gnosis, and make a Dexterity + Brawl attack with your claws. The attack deals 3 additional LEVELS of damage, and all the damage from the attack can't be regenerated for the rest of the scene. Though note that it says regenerated, not healed. As we've seen with the Glass Walker force lightning Gift, reliable damage is pretty good, though of course you do have to hit your opponent, and soak is still a thing. Coup de Grace is better, in my opinion, as it costs no Gnosis, applies to your next successful attack, and DOUBLES damage output.

                                        This is great on a character with a Rage of 1 or 2, so a Ragabash or Theurge who has left their Rage at starting levels. Ultimately a single powerful strike may be much better than two wimpy ones. On a more dedicated combat character with a Rage of 3+, the tradeoff is worse.

                                        I'm marking this as a Amber, but with potential to be Green, because its potential as a finishing move against a foe on their last legs is pretty great! I personally wouldn't bother with it, because my Ahroun's action economy is very Rage focused (and they'll be regenerating Rage with Silver Claws at this Rank), but on many, many characters it would be very strong, even for 9xp. Would I suggest buying it out of list? Probably not. Overall the fact that you need to consider whether you should take it or not is, to me, a sign that it should be Amber as a baseline.

                                        Wrath of Gaia: This is a good execution of a terror power. Only creatures of the Wyrm are targeted. It lasts for a whole scene. You spend a Gnosis and roll Charisma + Intimidation (IMO Appearance would work better), and any creature of the Wyrm who looks upon you must get equal or greater successes on a Willpower roll, difficulty 7, or flee from you. Given how much of a threat huge swarms of weak Banes and Fomori can be, the power to make an army of them flee from you is pretty great!

                                        The only reason not to take this would be if you have poor social stats for the roll, so I'm not even sticking a * on it - it'll prove worthy even if you only use it as a prep power before ambushes and similar.

                                        ================================================== ===============

                                        Wrath of Gaia is great to buy on anyone.

                                        Talons of the Falcon... perhaps. Possibly. But it's a tricky one - if you'd benefit from a decisive damage boost of +3 levels of damage (equivalent to +6 dice of damage!), it might be worth it, especially if you're a surgical assassin type fighter with lower Rage, focused on a single big hit.

                                            Rank 3 Stargazer Gifts

                                            Clarity*: You may roll for this Gift (Perception + Enigmas difficulty 7) to ignore things which impede your vision, such as fog, rain and pitch darkness.

                                            Against illusions, you can see past them if you get more successes on the check than the creator did on the illusion.

                                            Given that this has no cost, and the roll is doable, there's no reason not to activate this constantly, in every single scene, which is a problem some costless Gifts have. Still, it's definitely usable, and the ability to see past illusions could be very handy. There are other Gifts that let you see in the dark or ignore visual penalties, but this is a pretty usable one. I don't have anything against it, it isn't particularly exciting or particularly bad.

                                            This could be Green in a game with many enemies wielding illusions, from Gifts to Arts to Magick.

                                            Merciful Blow: This is an odd one. Of course it's annoyingly similar to Mercy, the Level 1 CoG Gift, but this is different in some ways.

                                            Spend a Gnosis, attune yourself to your foe, and your attacks don't apply any lasting injury. They are not downgraded to Bashing damage; you can still dish out Aggravated, and avoid soak and regeneration, but you know your foe won't die.

                                            A target you defeat with this power regains all health lost to your attacks and remains unconscious for the rest of the scene; this is 100% guaranteed.

                                            There are a few things worth keeping in mind here:
                                            • You are guaranteed a scene's worth of unconsciousness, which is hard to achieve with some kinds of enemies (such as spirits); RAW it's very hard to defeat a spirit without disincorporating it
                                            • This Gift is not explicitly restricted to your fangs, claws and punches; RAW it works with bullets, Klaives and so on
                                            • The target heals the damage you apply instantly, whereas in Mercy they have to take time to heal the Bashing damage

                                            It's worth getting if you're a pacifist Stargazer, and it's better/worse than Mercy in some weird niche ways. You might consider spending 5xp on Mercy, and getting it at the start of the game, instead of spending 9xp on this when you're deep into it.

                                            Sense Balance*: See the Philodox entry.

                                            Wind's Returning Favor: This Gift provides a defensive action which disarms foes and takes their weapon. When attacked with a melee weapon you may spend 1 WP, roll Dexterity + Athletics (difficulty of the target's Wits + Melee), and if you get more successes than the target you take their weapon. You also subtract successes from their attack, making this similar to a dodge/parry.

                                            Stealing a foe's Klaive is neat, though any self-respecting Garou will have a Wits of 2+ and if they're swinging a melee weapon they'll have a Melee of 3+. A difficulty as low as 5 is great, but as high as 9 or 10 is pretty bad. Spirit enemies, some of whom clearly use some kind of weapon, will have a difficulty of their Gnosis as it replaces Wits, so often 3-6ish, but sometimes as high as 9-10.

                                            Unfortunately the Gift has a pretty mean curve; the higher your foe's Dexterity + Melee, the more successes they will have on their attack. The higher their Wits + Melee, the higher your difficulty to fight back. If they're stabbing you at a difficulty of 5-7, with 8-10 dice, and you're resisting with 8-10 dice at a difficulty of 8+, then you're in trouble as the Gift will almost never trigger.

                                            And that's the real problem here; if your foe is so dangerous with a melee weapon you want to disarm them in a single defensive move, that you NEED this Gift, the Gift is ultimately at its weakest. Because the mighty Grand Klaive won't, typically, be in the hands of a fool with little training in wielding it. It could happen, sure, Silver Fang Failson holding a legendary Klaive is certainly a thing, but even then.

                                            If you're mugged by a drunken fool, this Gift will trigger. But you hardly need that, do you? What you need is to take a dangerous Aggravated damage weapon off someone, and that's where this Gift is at its worst. With a fixed difficulty of 6 it would be solid, but as it stands the variable difficulty works against it.

                                            Why is this almost Red? Because ultimately this Gift could prove detrimental, it could cause you harm. When 4 successes with a Grand Klaive are headed your way, attempting a difficulty 6 dodge is far superior to attempting a difficulty 9 disarm. The Gift is basically a trap; you waste 9xp on it, you feel good disarming some idiot who couldn't have hurt you anyways, you spend your precious WP, and when you truly need it you get sliced in half because the probability was never in your favour against a real threat.

                                            The saving grace of this Gift is that you can choose to activate it when your foe fails or botches, or gets 1 success on their swing. Even then it's a niche of a niche.

                                            ================================================== ========

                                            Clarity might be worth 15xp if you desperately need it; if illusions will be a major theme of the chronicle then go for it. Wind's Returning Favor could be useful to Klaive Duelists as a way of stunting on weaker foes or as a way of capitalizing on blunders.

                                                    Honestly I think they needed to have Gifts repeated and reflavoured more often, and use more variants/upgrades of Gifts. A Tribe can have a unique combination of 3-4 Gifts at each rank without having an endless list of annoyingly similar entries. For instance both the Children of Gaia and Stargazers could have Mercy at the first level without stepping on eachother's toes; that's only an overlap of a single Gift. But my biggest complaint is that there are too many "destroy/disable an object" Gifts. With the endless possibilities of infinite spirits of animals and plants and places and concepts and mythical beasts, we get a lot of the same repeated. Where are the Gifts where you're nourished by sunlight like a plant, or can hide Gnosis for later like a squirrel burying nuts, or can recover Willpower by stealing something shiny like a magpie.

                                                    At the same time, idiosyncrasies can be cool and good if presented correctly; one Tribe might have a relatively simple flight Gift, another a Gift which gives you huge Pegasus wings, and if you acknowledge that this is weird it takes some of the stupidity out of it. Not all Gifts are equally good and useful; they're the blessings of spirits, some are better suited to the modern world. These idiosyncrasies should be the exception and not the rule however, since good game design and a fun set of balanced powers should come first.
