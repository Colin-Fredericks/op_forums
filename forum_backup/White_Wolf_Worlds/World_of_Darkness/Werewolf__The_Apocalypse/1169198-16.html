                        To the trans-garou topic, I think it the most annoys me how being a transperson and a garou is treated as a rarity. In a species that changes shape and is tied to spirits. Mostly, because it reflects how modern people view being trans and not really how being trans is hardly an unknown thing in spiritualism. For example, if we look at why Furies keep male metis from 1st ed onwards, it is most likely in refrence to galli (eunuchs in the service of the goddess Cybele). Given Furies in 1st ed were written as priestesses, the concept matches.

                        Now, I already noted how the idea of being trans is hardly new in myths. But even Book of Auspices (Page 52, section Theurges and Gender) notes that garou DO in fact have a tradition of playing with gender roles, at least theurges and ragabash. A theurge who is a transwoman can draw spiritual power from her crossing that boundary that society had set for her.

                        In less flowery terms, WtA borrows heavily from actual mystic traditions, the book only took contepolar concept of being trans into consideration.

                        The same is actual true with how the metis section discussed homosexuality and how different tribes saw the metis breed. The constant "Hey, gay sex is A-OK" is refreshing but also not really very deep. Having sex with another garou even if it doesn't produce a metis has been multiple times noted as still incest. Maybe there is a push to move away from that, which I get. But I always felt with garou, it made sense.

                        As for the tribes hating metis, I didn't mind that. But the reasonings were very shallow in some. The Wendigo only accept metis who are wendigoxwendigo, due to the lack of white blood. That says nothing but the very surface level of the tribe. Especially a tribe that would also maybe not use the term metis of the breed. Going deeper and explaining cultural reasons of metis hate would have given a more complete picture.
