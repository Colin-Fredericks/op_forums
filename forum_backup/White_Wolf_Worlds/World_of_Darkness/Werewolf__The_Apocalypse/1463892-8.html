                Remembering how fraught comparing things to Disney's run with Star Wars has been:

                I don't see how the comparison works. Star Wars had, long before Disney, a hierarchy of canon. The movies were always at the top. When the prequels came out, they contradicted lots of EU stuff which was immediately rendered non-canon because that's how Lucas decided things would work. This is, generally speaking, a very strange set up in large media franchises (any that grow close to this sort of size opt for parallel canons like the Marvel comics vs. the MCU). Disney just opted to take advantage of the existing way Star Wars handled canon status to proactively put a bunch of the lower canon status EU works into the Legends category of non-canon.

                Whatever W5 does is going to be an apples-to-oranges situation to that because whatever happens with W5, it's not how Lucas setup canon to work in SW.

                This actually makes the situation worse; as we've seen with V5. In SW, there was a long standing public facing way to figure out what to do if setting material contradicts in different sources by labeling certain sources inherently more authoritative. In V5, there are some serious continuity issues because it is very fuzzy about how the past canon and the current canon interact. The editions are supposed to be in a single continuity, but there's a lot of unaddressed contradictions and no real guide in how to navigate them.

                SW fans might be pissed off that Disney decided that G-canon remains canon, T-canon is canon unless stated otherwise, and C-, S-, and D-canons are all rendered N-canon under the Legends header... but the very fact that sentence can be parsed to mean anything sensible undercuts a lot of outrage that isn't just a difference in preference over the direction of the franchise.

                WoD fans are already trying to navigate how to deal with all of the gray areas where V5 has changed things without explaining if these are retroactive changes, changes that have actually occurred due to metaplot events, or some other option.
