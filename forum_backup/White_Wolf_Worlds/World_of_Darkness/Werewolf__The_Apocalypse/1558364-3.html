                        I would like to cycle back to cover a few points in the OP that I've been ruminating over.

                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                        One complaint I'm seeing a lot of is that 5th Edition is much harder on the Garou and nothing but how much they suck.

                        However, I remember 1st Edition Werewolf and early 2nd Edition. In fact, that's "my" W:TA because I didn't follow up on Revised and other works.
                        If you were that familiar with it you'd also know that, being an early WoD first edition, it was 90% vibes being thrown at the wall to see what stuck. People tend to assemble these through rose-colored glasses, not unlike "Only 3025" BattleTech players. RIP to the suggested Superhero theme and play mode and Wolf-Headed Bikini Babes.

                        The point was that the Garou had failed and the Apocalypse was imminent...because of all the reasons given in the 5th Edition book about them being too violent, unable to get over their differences tribally, and the Children of Gaia being ignored. It was why Evan-White-Boy-Saves-Us ( :kidding, kidding: ) existed as his whole thing. The Garou needed to make serious systemic change or everyone was screwed.
                        And 2e starting with Legacy Rite didn't suggest that they were doing something fairly different? 2e was also where the game declared its theme - Savage Horror, which the Storyteller's Handbook clarified was splatter. Gross-out horror. You were fighting Troma mutants and painting the walls with them before they painted the walls with you, not unlike Splatterhouse. And speaking of said Storyteller's Handbook, it was knee-deep in Joseph Campbell and applying mythic cycle storytelling to W:tA (also a thing that didn't exactly age well, much like the dedication to Neil Gaiman in the front of the 1e W:tA core).

                        Also, both 1e and 2e had miniatures game rules in the back. Honestly, it's a wonder that Trinity: Battleground became WW's first hero-based miniatures combat game, because W:tA had a lot of potential in that arena.

                        I'm not a big fan of 5E's take but I am curious why this specific complaint is coming up because it seems like it was just following up from the original presentation that all of the War of Rage, Impergium, and other things had come to roost.

                        Change or die and all that.

                        I wonder if this is related to the editions I missed and the fact that 5E was going back to the original versions.
                        5e is stripping things down just like it did to Vampire. If it had actually gone back to its roots, there would be more superheroic Garou and wolf-headed bikini babes. Instead, it's the flattening of everything into the One True Way, with a heavy serving of how the creative director hates the game, and also you for liking it, and the stunt-casted developer kind of agrees with him because it wasn't enough like Vampire. And the OTW here isn't splatter or a emphasis on the visceral and chilling nature of violence, but an attempt to force V:tM's standard of "personal horror" onto a setting that does not give a shit about it.

                        That is the problem. Throwbacks to the 90s getting control, preaching about making a "modern version" of games, and engaging in finger-wagging about character optimization that wouldn't be out of place in a usenet post from 1993. It doesn't understand how the concept of Loresheets from Weapons of the Gods are supposed to work and wastes all potential that they would have. It doesn't even have time for consideration of wolves and wolf behavior, in a game where a notable character type is born as a wolf and lives as one for a (very harrowing and traumatizing) year or two.

                                As far as the "back to the roots"-argument for W5 is concerned, I never found that one to be plausible. There's way too much antipathy for Garou in this edition. In that regard, the pre-gen-characters in the adventures have been particularly illuminating. You often get the sense that in W5 the way a potential Garou-PC-concept works is to imagine a human who has some motivation to oppose the forces of the Wyrm - and then all the Garou/werewolf-stuff is just an afterthought. It's an extreme example but there's this one pre-gen-character in the adventures Crude Earth and Rotten Home that's a great example of just that:

                                Deputy Sheriff Maya Chamberlain (Humanborn Silver Fang Philodox): Raised by her grandmother in Mulligan, Maya has no other family in town. She also has an Iron-Clad sense of right and wrong that brooks little argument. Fortunately for the people who come through Mulligan, listening is high on her list of right, and jumping to conclusions is high on her list of wrong.
                                She’s currently not welcome on Caern grounds because when the elder put her in a situation of choosing between her job and the Caern, she chose her job as she believes losing her job would make the Caern less safe.​
                                This one is quite extreme by highlighting the idea that when this character was in a situation where she had to choose between protecting the Caern and making sure she would keep her human job, she ended up choosing the latter. At that point, I honestly wonder why this character even should be a W5-PC. Just let her do her human job and help that way. Clearly, she thinks that's more important than being a Garou in a Pack that protects the local Caern.

                                But if you read this backstory with the idea that she's right and the Garou at the local Caern are wrong, then you get an idea of what W5 considers to be a "good Garou". What W5 imagines "good Garou" to do is to focus on establishing a balanced human life (job, family, money etc.) and you try to help the local Caern/Sept as much as possible. But the Garou who want you to treat being a Garou like a full-time-job and prioritize protecting the Caern and fighting the Wyrm are the bad guys. Furthermore, the mechanics of W5 support that idea. Going into the Umbra has been discouraged, shapeshifting is costly (especially the Crinos Form), Rage-management is hard (as in when you want to have a lot of dots and when you want to have few) and Harano/Hauglosk are these "inevitable doom"-mechanics.

                                There's a section in the 1st edition WtA corebook in Chapter Three called "Heroics". W5 is pretty much the antithesis of this idea. In 1st edition you would get a line from the Heroics-section like "Stories in WtA should present the characters with the opportunity to be heroes. Through the characters players can right the wrongs they do not have the power to correct in real life." (pg. 63). I could quote more but you get the idea, it's a whole section about how thinking of the Garou as heroes is important in this 1st edition framework.

                                Now let's see a section from the W5 corebook adding its thoughts on this subject: "Note, too, that this difference means that the players’ characters are the protagonists, but by no means are they the “heroes” or the “good guys.” The Garou are, by definition, flawed individually, and Garou society is… well, it’s a fucking trainwreck. The players’ characters are agents of change, not necessarily improvement. The proof of the change they bring lies in the outcomes they effect. Werewolf assumes that most players’ characters do seek some sort of improvement to the world, but the game doesn’t insist on such linearity. Also, characters may be limited by their Garou nature or other things outside their control.​" The undertone of pessimism is pretty obvious, I think.

                                I mean, what a contrast! First edition clearly had a setting in mind that's close to Apocalypse and a grimdark world but the PCs were meant to be heroes who struggled against it. In W5 meanwhile the writers are describing an apocalyptic setting and they've also already lost any faith in Garou (and the PCs must be Garou, of course) to make a difference.
