    Originally posted by vicky_molokh View Post
    I have two thoughts on this matter:
    1. Just because you're not the first to do something shouldn't make your choice to do it be treated that much worse. Sure, someone was the first to write Drizzt, that doesn't mean that others should not be given the opportunity to have all the fun.
    2. Are you sure that the stereotypes are based on truth? If 19 out of 20 tribe members turn out to mismatch their stereotypes, maybe the problem is not that they mismatch the stereotypes, but rather that the onlooker making the observation is a misinformed tribe-ist? After all, how many people had stereotypes about Jews, or USAians, or Japanese, only to find that their expectations don't match their actual experience with actual living members of those groups? Given that Garou tribes are in many ways reminiscent of nations, I think that's fitting.
    Yeah, I mean, my issue -isn't- that there are open minded Fangs or Fenrir or arsehole Children of Gaia. My issue is that often those are the -only- characters found in the games and as such it doesn't bring up a good conversation on the nature of the tribe.

    As for the stereotypes, minus Black Furies, they to me always felt like how america saw the various ethnic groups/sub-cultures. Fenrir as neo-nazi, Fianna as irish drunks, Bone Gnawers as drunk bums,etc. At least in the 1st edition and 2nd ed.

    Thing in play is, though, that some stereotypes are true. Silver Fang culture DOES support insane amount of separation between them and the rest of the Nation because they are the leaders. Red Talons do kill humans. Some Children of Gaia do lack the moral backbone to be true warriors and some Black Furies sacrifice male babies in rituals. Point is, tribes aren't good or bad, they are both. And without there being a good balance of both, a lot of the good stuff that could be explored by the game is lost.

    This is one of the reason I play metis from traditional tribes. Because they bring up discussions of the Nation's wrongs, or believe in very disturbing traditions. Yet are still victims of the system.

        Originally posted by Maris Streck View Post

        Nope, you tell them to play another tribe. Crafting a white fly is the most abused and trite narrative trick to craft a "different" character, because it's so easy that everyone can think about it. Make a Get of Fenris and have him not acting like a GoF, here's a cool character for you! It's such a cliché that from a literary standpoint it marks the author as "bad" just for using it, and I discourage my players every time they try to create this kind of character. If you want to play a pacifist then you play a Child of Gaia, not a Fenris.
        Of course, some storyteller may feel perfectly fine with white flies, especially when they raise the banner of some particular cause they believe in (like equalitarists Silver Fangs). But it's your campaign, so if it doesn't bother you or your players I see no issue in playing it however you like. In a tabletop game isn't even that harmful, since it's 4 players against a world of stereotypes.

        Thing is, though, that this kind of "versus" character is horribly harmful in a multiplayer online game. Partly because they break the game lore (potentially ruining the setting to other players), but also because they are the kind of character that lives on the shoulder of the others. When your toon's personality is to be against the status quo then he doesn't have a personality, he's just leeching some from the action of others by disrupting their gameplay.
        This may lead to some fun when controlled: the single thief in a city can offer a lot. But when half of the city is made by thieves they become harmful to the game world.
        Yeah, I'm going to say no for that. At least at my table.

        If someone wants to play a Silver Fang then they should be able to. If you have a room full of nonasshole Silver Fang then you should play that as the ST.

        "You are all people who share the same beliefs but NO ONE ELSE IN THE TRIBE" shares them.

        The fact they're a group of such believers should be incorporated into the narrative unless the players are uncomfortable with the fact they're not special snowflakes with the others around.

        Just my opinion.
