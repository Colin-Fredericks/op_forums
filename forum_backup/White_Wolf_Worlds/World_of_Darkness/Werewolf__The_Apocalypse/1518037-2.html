                        Originally posted by Doktor von Zwitter View Post
                        Like they basically didn't want to do a Werewolf: The Apocalypse game, but it was the assignment that they were given.
                        A LOT of how WoD5 has been going seems like Paradox want to use the World of Darkness license to make money, but resent everything they inherited about the games. They want to exploit the brand, but not anything contained therein.

                        It's why H5 dispensed with anything having to do with the Imbued. Or why V5 fixated so much on getting back to "the game's roots". You know, back when Vampire was mostly undefined and mysterious. Where all the maps said "Here Be Dragons". A setting where all the established politics and players were swept away, so nothing could get in the way of the New Vision^TM.

                        Now we see W5, where established relationships and lore are freely ignored. As demonstrated in the thread by the writer from the Hunters Entertainment attempt at W5, where the editor basically said (paraphrasing) "the Sword of Heimdal were purged? I'll pretend I didn't hear that. We should ignore the lore, I want my Nazi Fenrir."

                        Over and over, we see the folks at Paradox seem to come into the projects with their own ideas about what WoD should be. Anything that interferes with this is burned to the ground, to make room for THEIR contributions. A slash-and-burn approach to stewarding a beloved franchise.

                                Originally posted by Shadeprowler View Post

                                Strangely enough, Vampire: The Masquarade was very clear about the origin of vampires, their powers and weaknesses
                                Caine, 2nd, 3rd Generation to present day fledgelings, Camarilla and we are all good
                                In first edition, Golconda was portrayed as an unadulterated good, and relatively easy to achieve (to the point, Chicago by Night has at least two characters in Golconda, one of whom achieved it in less than a century).
                                And the "good guy" vampires, aka. Heroes with Fangs was the common, not to mention the whole Crossover-friendlyness
                                With the second and revised editions World of Darkness became progressively... darker and the various clan myths, origins, etc slowly replaced all those things
                                I wouldn't be upset if [anything]5 would have gone back to those roots, but where they went is NOT "the game's roots" in my humble opinion
                                What I meant was that most of the VtM part of the World of Darkness hadn't been fleshed out yet. We didn't know who all ran the various cities, how the Camarilla/Anarchs/Sabbat was structured, who the great Methuselahs were and what their plans are, etc. We had the possible origin with Caine, we had Golconda, we have the conflict between the young and the old, and that was about it. A lot of what VtM fans see as staples of the franchise hadn't been written yet.

                                THIS is what Paradox wanted to emulate about early VtM. The "blank slate" nature of it.

                                Because if it's a blank slate, that means V5's writers could inject all of their ideas into VtM. All they had to do was burn the setting to the ground. Clear away all the brush, as it were. In so doing, making the world seem bigger and more mysterious.

                                But they killed so many characters - under the pretense of letting PCs fill the void effortlessly - it made the world seem empty and small. They folded so many options into one another - under the pretense of making it easier for new players - they deprived you of meaningful choices (and watered down the choices you did get). Pruning Bloodlines and Disciplines in a militant crusade against "redundancy", when that was never a problem.

                                Paradox doesn't make the World of Darkness more interesting. Instead it flattens the setting. Makes it more shallow. In the name of making the game approachable, it removes so much messy nuance that could be used to attract people. A fully generic game about supernaturals living in an urban fantasy setting is less captivating that the Classic WoD's fractal specificity.

                                You can play a vampire in a lot of roleplaying games now. You can't play a flesh-crafting mystic seeking visceral enlightenment anywhere but VtM.

                                You can play a werewolf in a lot of roleplaying games now. You can't play a Garou Viking fighting for Gaia against Entropy and Capitalism anywhere but WtA.
