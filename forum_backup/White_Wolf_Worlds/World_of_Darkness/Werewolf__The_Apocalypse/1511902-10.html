            Rank 3 Elder Brother Gifts

            Banish Totem*

            A few problems with this Gift:
            • You need to know the name of the Totem.
            • A difficulty of Totem score will often be difficulty 10 when dealing with a mid-to-large pack of PCs.
            • Costs 1 Gnosis and 1 Willpower.
            • Rolling Gnosis isn't amazing.
            Still, if you get it right, and have the right targets, this Gift could prove pretty effective... in the niche situation of your enemies having a pretty powerful magical link. If you're fighting a pack who are all psychically linked by a spirit which is giving them powerful combat buffs, sure, nice. But -1 Dexterity, or -2 Strength, and turning off telepathy isn't THAT amazing. It's situational, and also not THAT good in the situations where it comes up.

            For this to be better, the mechanical benefit of being coordinated by a totem spirit would need to be stronger, or more important, or being suddenly robbed of it would need to be more harmful.

            Anyways, the * is for abnormal games where you're constantly fighting packs with powerful spirit patrons, and get to watch a whole group of Garou fall to their death after you cut them off from Owl in the Umbra.

            Chains of Mist

            Concentrating for a turn is bad. Spending Gnosis is bad. A difficulty 7 activation is bad. And your reward is that you hit 1 target per success with a weak, if broad, debuff. Their WP, Rage and Gnosis are treated as being 1 lower for the purpose of rolls (which arguably includes rolls where their WP, Rage or Gnosis are the difficulty). The issue is that this is just "-1 dice to all rolls" in most situations. You can't apply multiple chains to one target either.

            It's pricey, difficult, and doesn't do much. It also doesn't stop your target from running away or disappearing. Very similar to a disappointing Theurge Gift with similar mechanics.

            The edge case for this is that 10 Elder Brothers of this rank or higher could bind any conventional Spirit (only exceptionally powerful ones have stats of 11+). But there are better ways to do that, and it doesn't matter to a PC. Even in a game with 5 Elder Brother PCs, which is a push, it comes online far too late.

            Invisibility

            Between the Gnosis cost, halving your walking speed, being broken by actions which draw attention to yourself, having an activation roll, being beatable with a mundane roll, and having a very high difficulty in a situation where you'd actually want invisibility... well it's just invisibility. You're a Garou. Scent, sound, these are huge. It's not like you can fight with this turned on. It's not like you can track; you're moving too slowly for most quarry. It's not like you can carry out a half decent investigation or lay down the base of a ritual. It's good for escaping, thieving, some forms of spying. If you want to escape, Lupus, or fly. If you want to spy, Scry.

            All in all this is just much too conservative for a 3rd level Gift. But it's definitely not red.

            Rending the Craft

            Ignoring this Gift contradicting itself (the Armour Charm is disabled, apparently, but the passive strength from Potence isn't, despite those both being... the same), this is amazing. After a successful claw attack you need only spend a point of WP to turn off all active magical effects enhancing the target. Sadly this does mean you can't claw away curses and so on, but that's probably for the best. Given that the most dangerous of enemies will be carrying around a number of magical enhancements, this is amazing. It's even more amazing in crossover games, too, but it'll shine just fine against Banes, Fomori, BSDs, mad Fera and rival Garou.

            Scrying

            A remote viewing power, keep in mind that RAW this grants sight only. IMO that makes sense; you're looking at a reflection of distant events in a reflective surface.

            Perception + Occult, difficulty 10 if you lack a link to your target, difficulty 8 if you have something tied to the person or location.

            I'm not going to list a bunch of creative applications because it's so broad, but if you're going to get this Gift it doesn't hurt to be a magpie. A shiny rock from the Caern, a spoon from that fancy Kinfolk manor, a pen from the experimental gene clinic, and so on.

            Absolutely invest in the necessary Language merit / spec in an Ability / secondary Ability to lip-read, whatever that is at your table. The Gnosis cost is a little annoying but well worth it, and helps to make this power less un-fun for the table; you have to carefully pick where you view!

                                Rank 3 Younger Brother Gifts

                                Blood of the North*: I like the permanence. This lets you ignore all penalties from the cold, gain +5 soak against cold-based attacks, and reduce the difficulty of all Survival rolls (including activating Gifts?) in cold environments.

                                Naturally this gets a * because if you're playing a game set in Alaska or an Ice Age, it's amazing! In such a setting spirits and fomori will be far more likely to have icy attacks, and such penalties and survival rolls will come up more often.

                                Typically this will get an Amber. Nice to have, but a bit weak for a level 3 Gift. Better if you can lure your enemies into a freezing cold environment, or expose the entire scene to a source of cold-based damage (ultra-cold air or water which inflicts lethal damage each turn, tanks of liquid nitrogen exploding, the breath of a freezing cold spirit filling the battlefield etc.). Like with fire immunity for rank 1 homids, make this gift work for you.

                                Bloody Feast*: Neat flavour, weird mechanics.

                                You must inflict at least 1 lethal or agg with a bite on a creature which bleeds, but doesn't have toxic blood. So... Garou and Fera, excluding Ratkin. Spirits don't have blood, by and large. Fomori do, but often have toxic blood of some kind, and in some cases won't have blood at all. Humans work with this Gift, but you don't need bonus Strength to rip them apart with your teeth.

                                Assuming the target is valid, and worth using this on, you roll Gnosis (hopefully 3+ dice) against a difficulty of the target's Stamina + 3. So against a proper Werewolf enemy, the kind you will struggle against and need bonus strength to help with, chances are it'll be a difficulty of 10. You then gain +1 strength for every TWO levels of damage you've inflicted. So if they soak, if you roll poorly on strength...

                                This bonus Strength lasts for two turns per success on the Gnosis roll.

                                Ultimately this bonus strength doesn't last for long enough, it is too swingy, and is best used against a chicken. Or innocent bystander. After all with them the Gnosis roll will have a difficulty of 4-5, and you'll deal a bunch of damage for +2-5 strength.

                                Given that rolling Gnosis means no bonus actions, and you have to test for Frenzy after using this, meaning you're bound to wind up in Frenzy or even Thrall if you rely on it, this Gift is just bad. If your ST lets you feast on rats you keep in your pockets, sure, so this can get an asterisk to account for the possibility your ST is vulnerable to "my rogue has a pet rat who helps him always get sneak attack" level shenanigans. It's also worth remembering that generally speaking claws are better than bites, if you're a dedicated build with combat gifts, such as an Ahroun.

                                If you want to fix this, make it last for the whole scene, and make it grant a fixed bonus such as gaining additional Strength equal to your Rank. The Frenzy risk is cool, and flavourful, so keep that so long as the Gift is good and worth using.

                                Sky Running: Much faster than Spirit of the Bird. Doesn't cost Gnosis. Willpower, typically, is easier to replenish and more plentiful. Works in any form, so hilariously you can run through the air leaving a trail of fire in Homid. Doesn't debuff you.

                                The main drawback of this power is that you must keep moving or it fails, and furthermore you leave a trail of fire which is very conspicuous and also Veil-risking.

                                All in all this is strong in the Umbra, great in high octane werewolf fights, amazing for fleeing and chasing, but should be used carefully if you require any subtlety at all. I'm going Green because I struggle to imagine passing on gaining the ability to fly in exchange for 1WP for 4 hours, even if it has some strings attached.

                                Wisdom of Ancient Ways*: See the Philodox entry.

                                    Not arguing the rating on Bloody Feast... the mechanics alone make it a overwrought mess that near impossible to use and is never, ever worth it (It's a Gnosis roll, no Rage actions on the turn you activate it, basically cutting off one turn off it's real duration). If the mechanics were better, I don't think the bite vs. claw issue would be a consideration. The small hit of not being able to pile on a bunch of claw boosting Gifts to activate some bonus strength via a bite that you can then boost your claws with even more is well worth it in theory. Garou bites are plenty dangerous.

                                    The whole blood thing is less of an issue for me on enemies that count (mages and changelings are valid targets too, and in a pure WtA game you can make vampires a valid target on interpretation grounds), but rather it doesn't make any sense to have a blood-based restriction at all. It's a Gift taught by a cannibal spirit... cannibals aren't just blood suckers. Get someone hungry enough and they'll eat tainted meat to survive now even if they know they'll be sick later. Garou can even "eat" spirits in a fashion, while being part spirit themselves.

                                    It would be way better to say that you need to swallow something while biting the enemy. Then you're taking a tactical risk when it comes to toxic enemies, vampire blood, and so on. That also makes the Frenzy check more sensible.

                                            Rank 4 Homid Gifts

                                            Body Shift: I don't get the logic behind this being a Homid Gift, but the Gift fills a useful niche. Unfortunately it does so terribly.

                                            A difficulty 9 Stamina + Primal-Urge is horrible. Even with a large dice pool, you'll often be botching, failing, or getting 1-2 successes. And the Gift only lets you transfer dots from one Attribute to another for a Scene, and only one dot per two successes!

                                            Essentially this Gift is far too hard to use, doesn't last long enough, and doesn't do enough. Also, it doesn't explicitly allow you to increase an Attribute above 5, though arguably that's implied. All in all this one is awful.

                                            My change to this Gift would be to make it so that on a success you can freely rearrange your base dots in physical Attributes, with duration of one scene per success. Yes, it would let people just shift all their Strength and Stamina into Dexterity to be a glass stiletto of ultra-precise doom, but they're going to suffer from their low strength and stamina. It would also only let you move around the regular dots, not the bonus dots from the Forms.

                                            Bury the Wolf*: Spend a Gnosis, roll Gnosis, difficulty of your own Willpower. Since your own Willpower should be as high as you can get it or justify, from a char-op perspective, this isn't ideal.

                                            On a success you overcome The Curse, and hide away your Rage, and all your other forms. You are stuck in Homid, you seem human, and you pass as human to all forms of supernatural detection. That's pretty neat! The duration can be up to a lunar month as well.

                                            For all that's bad about this Gift, it provides something very important which is difficult to acquire otherwise. For a character with a Rage of 5 or higher, The Curse can be exceptionally debilitating. I'm giving this a ranking of Green, on the assumption you have a decent Gnosis pool, and a Rage which is worth hiding. The asterisk is for those who don't need this Gift. Unfortunately this Gift becomes semi-useless, at at least pretty risky, if you have a Willpower of 10.

                                            Cocoon: This 100% feels like a Metis Gift. Taking bizarre, sometimes creepy aspects of a long list of critters is more their deal.

                                            You spend a Gnosis to generate the cocoon, and while it exists enemies must deal damage equal to your Stamina + Rituals to harm you. Since your Stamina could be as high as 8, and your Rituals could be as high as 5, this could be very effective! I'd say a Stamina + Rituals of 7 is the bare minimum to want to use this Gift, and even then 7 might be risky with powerful enemies. Given a potential "soak" of 13, you could be nigh-invincible with this cocoon, however if it is pierced it is instantly destroyed.

                                            This Gift is a good way to survive against exposure, the elements, disease, corruption, dangerous crowds with weak attacks, but of course it is a Veil risk, best used in Crinos for highest soak, and is a last resort. You shouldn't really be planning on getting into a situation where you need to use this Gift, but it's handy to have. The duration is good, and it can be extended with more Gnosis.

                                            Decent, but not a must-have, especially at a cost of 12/20 XP.

                                            One pretty nasty use of this is as a suicide bomb, minus the suicide. Bring along a very dangerous wide area weapon, set it off, and cocoon yourself. Do keep in mind that this cocoon could be carried places, thrown into acid, buried deep under the earth, and you gain no protections against starvation and thirst. This is an emergency defense which is meant for short term use.

                                            If you don't mind a bad reputation, this could cleverly win you a lot of Challenges. Say someone is tasked with taking something from you; they won't be able to do that if you're inside a cocoon which is far too strong for them to break.

                                            Spirit Ward: This feels like a Lupus Gift, but that's alright.

                                            You roll Manipulation + Rituals, difficulty 7, and for every success you subtract one die from the dice pools of all spirits within 100 feet. Spirits within 50 feet, except for Caern Spirits and your own pack spirit, lose 1 point of Essence each turn per success. So for example, with 4 successes, you'd be subtracting 4 dice from the dice pools of all spirits within 100 feet, and any spirit within 50 feet would lose 4 points of Essence (which is both health and fuel) per turn. That's pretty great! The Gnosis cost is annoying, but you can set this up at the start of a fight to debilitate foes. There are even some dangerous spirits who have very wonky stats, such as a Rage of 4-5 or Willpower of 4-5, and so subtracting a few dice will seriously hamper them.

                                            A good example are spirits with Rage as high as 7-8 but very, very low Willpower; they're heavy hitters who typically miss, and with this ward they'll almost always miss, or lose the ability to attack at all.

                                            Assuming a good dice pool and some Gnosis to spend, this is a great Gift.

                                            One good use of this Gift would be to place it, with other Garou defending you as you do so, in a place of great corruption. Even if you die, there's nothing to indicate the ward would stop. Presumably the ward could be erased or destroyed, and thus it will need defending to do its work, but there are worse ways to deal with huge crowds of spirits. A cheeky combo would be to go into a cocoon and then use this, but since you're "immobilized" in the cocoon that might be impossible.

                                                    Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post
                                                    Shadows of the Impergium: If you're regularly encountering regular humans (remember that supernaturals are all Delerium immune!) with WP 9 and 10... something weird is going on. Delirium is a double edged sword, but I'm gonna say that overall it's beneficial to you in a lot of ways. Making foes panic, lose coordination, flee, attack wildly, forget you were ever there... and this Gift grants it (if weaker) to Hispo, which may be your preferred fighting form, and also eliminates the risk of humans who react in clever ways when you go Crinos. The ability to repress this also lets you avoid collateral damage, and means the Hispo form doesn't get permanently altered in a way which removes utility.

                                                    All in all this Gift has some handy utility, but it's more like a Quality of Life improvement than a good buff. Regular Delirium does the job fine against humans. Humans who have WP of 7+ and are not supernatural in some way (Bane, Kinfolk, psychic etc.) will be rare in most games. This might be handy if you're regularly being hunted by badass hunters and mercenaries and First Teams.

                                                    If you prefer Crinos form to Hispo in fights, and you aren't going to be facing high-WP mundane human enemies, this becomes red.

                                                    There is a niche combo (requires Rank 4), but very VERY powerful:


                                                    Terror of the Dire Wolf (Rank 4), which can inflict Delirium on partly or formerly human creatures (practically anything other than garou and fera, spirits and the like) including mages, vampires, and wraiths.

                                                    Combine that with Shadows of the Impergium, always active with no test required, which causes those affected by your Delirium to be treated as though their Willpower is five lower when determining the effects.

                                                    RAW, basically anything that isn't another changer or a spirit is probably helpless if you manage to activate Terror of the Dire Wolf. Per the Delirium chart, once the -5 is applied (WP 6 or less), 86% of affected enemies curl into a fetal position and do nothing. Even those with 10 WP forget.​

                                                        Another combo for Red Talons, again requires Rank 4:
