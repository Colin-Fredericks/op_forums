                            I agree to some extent. The problems werewolves have with vampires go further than "wilderness vs. cities", but they can certainly have the same goals on a small, more concrete, scale.

                            BSD are enemies of both, Vampires are enemies of both (that is, "other vampires" are enemies of both, if you get what I mean), and surely there can be other coincidences (Gangrel protecting the same forest against the same Pentex subsidiary, albeit for unrelated reasons it's a classic)

                            Garou, as a Nation have vampires as both "public enemy" and unholly abominations. But the more "micro" one gets, the more nuances to this enemity one can find. As has been said in the thread previously, to say that Garou reaction to vampires it's "kill on sigth" it's an oversimplification. Most of the time Garou have other things on their plate, and leave vampires be.
                            Also, as I stated previously, Garou are no hive mind (albeit they ARE a close knit tribal society). Clans don't hate all with equal strength, nor have all Garou the same approaches to such universal enemity. There's various hues of black to that universal loathing, so to speak, and individual Garou may subvert expectations even more based on their personal experiences or reasons (very rare in such a closed culture, but not unheard of).

                            From a metagaming POV, while the "Garou vs the world in general and vampires in particular" it's a staple of the game that's very dear to it's fans, crossover stories with Garou among other splats aren't going away because there's also a lot of people who like to do that. Older editions toyed with the idea of vampires with A Garou ally, Garou with A vampire ally, pacts of non-agression, etc. And "The enemy of my enemy" it's a staple of ALL WoD.

                            The setting leaves less room to wriggle a Garou into "the party" on a crossover game compared with vampires or mages (that are much more individualistic creatures, and thus more free to act outside the norm or believe whatever they want), but there are places to find exeptions to the general rule. There's plenty of Merits and written lore to make it happen, if one wants it to happen.

                            And that's where this thread comes so baffling to me. It isn't clear to me if the complains are about Garou morality as part of the setting, or about the game design making crossover hard (in which case it's nonsense to say Garou "are too villanous and hipocrite". There's no ethical pressure here, it's just a choice of "game design")

                                I'll try to throw out some points to further the discussion, but in large parts I agree with exactly what Aleph just said. The setting exists on a spectrum when one considers specific scenarios and opportunity is laid for stories of either style or leaning, especially when you add the Golden Rule into the mix.

                                Anyway though, I'll hop back up to where I left off yesterday.

                                •Loathing All Vampires Is Hypocritical:
                                -Sure, maybe. It's not so much hypocritical in the sense that they say one thing and do another large scale about their vampiric policy, but I can see the argument that the Garou have no right to be the moral judges of anything. Sure. Fair enough. Yet due to their nature, they need to serve that role. If they stop, the world ends. As far as the Garou are concerned, they are fighting an eternal battle against Stagnation and Corruption incarnate and every wasted moment further's their causes. The vast majority of vampires, to some degree, are indirectly serving these concepts and are probably feeding into them more then the average bane or pattern spider and definitely more so then the average person. The garou's animosity towards kindred is very much grounded in their core motivations, which despite all the atrocity the Garou have caused, their ultimate goals and ideals are undeniably noble.

                                •Changelings & Mages
                                -Yeah, not all changelings and mages are trying to actively murder vampires. Hell, they may even work with kindred if it benefits them. But that's a false equivalency. The goals of the Fae & Mages are, by and large, radically departed from those of the Garou Nation. The Garou are literally divinely appointed spiritual warriors. This task is their duty, not that of some random mage or fae.

                                Furthermore, while you're certainly right that mages (and to a lesser extent) fae are capable of working with vampires, outside of Nephandi, Thalliain, and the truly corrupt; I sincerely doubt that you'd be able to find many who know of vampires who wouldn't agree that humanity as a whole would be better off if they were wiped from the face of the earth. Individuals may be fine, but as a breed vampires are a parasite and a cancer.

                                The more motivated sorts of factions such as House Scathach and the Order of Hermes have said this since forever. The main reason why other supernaturals don't seek to destroy vampires in is pure pragmatism generally, not any particular sympathy towards this nocturnal breed of monster.

                                •Working Together
                                -Yet what we've discussed here is largely the rule and every rule has exceptions. Like Vancouver's Compact in canon, truces are a common conceit where mutual benefit or some other local circumstance can necessitate it and those can certainly be interesting. Plenty of material posits examples of such alliances and they are genuinely interesting.

                                Yet in the case of Vampires-Werewolves specifically it's important to note that for even the tribes most likely to go for it, Glass Walkers or Bone Gnawers, it's generally a case of ends justifying the means and frying bigger fish. Any truce with leeches in the eyes of the Garou is temporary. They've been betrayed too many times to rest easy and they have a saved duty. Whether now or centuries from now, vampires too must be eradicated to complete their holy service to Gaia.

                                            Vampires are objectively of the Wyrm, so Garou have a religious obligation to oppose them. In addition to this, the Garou oppose vampires for all the same reasons ordinary humans do - vampires view everyone else as prey. Not only do Garou need to worry about themselves in this regard, they have more reason to fear for their kinfolk.

                                            Having said that, Garou - just like humanity - have their shirkers, their criminals, and their ambitious who will make deals with the devil to get what they want. So we can expect that there is a not insignificant number of Garou who interact with vampires on an ongoing basis, There was more prevalent in earlier editions which portrayed various Garou, even prominent ones, of having various motivations and dedication to the Litany. In Revised, the setting was increasingly portrayed in heroic fantasy lights, and these kinds of Garou either weren't mentioned anymore or were outright killed.

                                            Of course, there is differences between the tribes on their frequency of interaction with vampires.

                                            The Bone Gnawers had the most interaction, with various books stating outright the tribe had allies among Nosferatu and the Sabbat. The tribe overall did not really follow the Litany anymore, and half of its "members" were essentially AWOL. The typical refrain of "Garou wouldn't do that" just didn't apply to that tribe. Bone Gnawer run septs would likely to be viewed by other tribes as corrupt hellholes. (again, this was heavily changed in Revised in a deliberate attempt to make them more heroic).

                                            The Glass Walkers, by virtue of living in the cities, also had to make accommodation with vampires. If they didn't, they'd have to be at war constantly with them. While perhaps not formal treaties, they'd likely have informal agreements with the vampires in their city overall (vampires know they should avoid Washington Park, while Glass Walkers know to keep out of certain night clubs, etc.) and specific agreements between individuals. Certainly, the larger the city, the more likely the vampires got the better end of the deal. Perhaps only in small cities - of a few hundred thousand population - which had a caern that attracted one or more dozen Garou, would the Glass Walkers attempt to outright destroy any vampires and make it a hostile environment. But once the human population was large enough to support a significant number of vampires, especially relative to the # of Garou there, anything other than sporadic violence would be more likely to be their disadvantage. Glass Walkers would be much more likely than the Bone Gnawers to avoid the appearance of scandal - they are trying to position themselves as the next leaders of the Garou, needed in the urban future. So such deals would be kept hush hush and out of sight. But there's likely to be many ongoing deals which the tribe doesn't look down on - provided people keep them discrete.

                                            Then you have the political tribes that publicly denounce any interactions with vampires, and privately police it as well, yet have various individual members, packs, and camps which do make deals with them for various reasons and motives. Shadow Lords fit this most of all, but Uktena, Children of Gaia, and even Silver Fangs have more than a few such members. Shadow Lords are more likely to do it as part of their intrigues. Uktena in order to gain occult knowledge. Children of Gaia in order to "redeem" high humanity vampire or because of naive views of love. And Silver Fangs as a distasteful necessity. But here above all the rule is "don't get caught," because there will be no mercy if you do. But if discovered by friends and allies, they have a chance it will either not be mentioned, or they will stage a private intervention rather than be exposed immediately.

                                            The other tribes are much less likely to interact with them, but very small numbers of individuals still do for the exact same reasons as above. But any discovery of their acts will probably result in immediate trial and punishment. There would simply be less understanding of the "need" to make compromises.

                                            And of course, ronin and Lost Cubs exist outside Garou society and would do whatever they wanted.

                                            One thing I do object to is the use of the term "racism" to describe Garou (or indeed anyone's) dislike for vampires. It is a totally inappropriate term. It is like saying humans dislike of the smallpox virus is because of racism.
