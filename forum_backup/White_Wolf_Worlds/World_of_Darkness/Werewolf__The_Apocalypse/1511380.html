    African werewolves.

    05-11-2023, 01:14 PM

    Subsaharan African werewolves don't seem to be mentioned anywhere. It is said that the local Red Talons became hunting dogs (who range in a relatively small part of Africa) and the Ajaba, werehyenas, take the role of Gaia's warriors on the continent. Africa has wolves, but they're smaller breeds, perhaps they were unfitting to be Gaia's warriors for that region?
    ​

    Can we assume Africa's werewolves lost the war of rage? If not for that, given the geographic seperation of everything south of the Sahara, I would assume there would be at minimum another five fully fledged Africa-specific tribes bound to major linguistic groups/mythologies, like in Europe, or types of wolves, alongside the usual more globally oriented tribe like the gnawers, glass walkers, silver fangs, Red talons and children of Gaia. Best explanation: the lions unalived them.

    Thoughts?

                  Werewolves are present where wolves are present, and there aren't really any wolves in sub-Saharan Africa. There certainly are not any gray wolves.

                  And even if you count African wolves and Ethiopian wolves (and both species until very recently were considered to be jackals, not wolves) as wolves, there is only limited overlap with sub-Saharan Africa. And insofar that the African wolf (formerly the African golden jackal) is the basis for the lupus members of the Silent Striders, then there already is an African tribe.

                  Africa does have additional species of jackals. These could have potentially been the basis of additional tribes, though at the risk of watering down the concept of "werewolf" into a more generic "were-canid". But the people who live in those areas don't seem to have werejackal legends. They have legends of werehyenas and werefelines (lions, leopards, etc.). In traditional folklore, people tend to pick the largest predator animals of the areas as the animal that bad sorcerers turn into as an explanation of animal predation of livestock and humans. And the jackals in these areas just did not make the cut. I think it is for the best that the game usually (not always) restricted the Changing Breeds to actual folklore and legends, otherwise we'd have Changing Breeds for every single animal species or genus out there, and that'd be ridiculous.

                          Um, by definition there are sub-Saharan wolves, since African wolves have been found in the costal countries of western Africa, and the Ethiopian wolf is found in... Ethiopia which is sub-Saharan. The range of both doesn't go very far south, but Nigerians are considered sub-Saharan people and Nigerian doesn't reach down to Angola.

                          The populations of both in, in modern times, tiny... but you can say that about wolves all over the globe.

                          And why would we not consider African and Ethiopian wolves as wolves? Because humans misidentified them for a long time? We certainly should want to consider them as wolves for the game since, as you note, we need wolves to be the natural breeding population for lupus Striders. Scientifically the evidence supports both being off-shoots of Canis lupus, it's just the continual debate on speciation with canids because canids are a nightmare for taxonomists.

                          Do we need a new Tribe? No. The Silent Striders Red Talons, Bone Gnawers, and Silver Fangs all have reasons to be there. We can just expand the existing Tribes into spaces the authors didn't consider.

                          The idea that "people tend to pick the largest predator animals," does not hold up in any broad sense in African folklore. Were-hyenas are far more common than any of the were-felines, because hyenas have always been more willing to live near human settlements than the large cats. African folklore has a very wide range of mythological shape-shifters or mixed-human-something-else creatures. Sticking to actual folklore and legends would flood Changing Breeds with lots of additional shifters.

                              I think fleshing out African Werewolves more is a great idea - the Silent Striders are clearly kin to Canids who are wolves, but used to be mistaken for non-wolves, given how their Lupus forms appear with Pure Breed. And there is HUGE genetic drift between Canid species it seems; if you look at the wikipedia article for practically any canid there will be a subsection where it's like "yeah so 20% of their DNA seems to be from mixing with other canid species".

                              IMO the only two things that should be "off limits" for the Garou are domesticated dogs and coyotes. The former because it's a well-established area they're 'barred' from (and you can come up with a lot of flavourful reasons for the observable lack of weredogs, such as Weaver stifling the Wyld spirit through domestication, Garou bloodlines growing too thin etc.) and the latter because the Coyotes have their own Breed already.

                              Though a Garou Werecoyote would be kind of a funny event; they're not a Nuwisha, they're a Garou, but their Lupus form is DEFINITELY a coyote. According to human science the coyotes are far closer in evolutionary history to grey wolves than african hunting dogs are, but of course that's not a fact spirits are bound by. Is it possible RAW? No, but if it happens at your table I'll find it amusing and like it.

                              Between the Silent Striders and Red Talons you have a good amount of coverage. And the ranges not covered? Garou can go there, even if they lack Animal Kin there. They'll never be at their strongest there, but Garou are powerful in Ireland despite there only being a handful of wolves in the whole island, and none in the ROI (the only wolves are semi-captive in a wild enclosure in a Northern Ireland peninsula), unless you say there are some secret wolf populations deep in Fianna forests of course. Regardless, Garou can rule in places with no wolves.

                              And of course in the areas the Garou can't cover you do indeed have more Bastet and more Ajaba. The cause of this geographical relationship is likely a mixture of things; wolves thrived there less, so the Garou were less effective during the War of Rage in those regions, but also the regions where they lost the most would have had any wolves living there wiped out... and so on.

                              Basically you can piece together a pretty neat Garou story relating to the canids of Africa using canon tools, and I like that.

                                      Africa has gotten some coverage here and there, starting with some of the early 1st edition material and probably culminating with the chapter on Africa (and the Middle East) in A World of Rage. (And, obviously, the WW20 material that was extrapolated from all of that.)

                                      The biggest issue has always been the wolf population. However, Rage Across Australia set the precedent of Garou being able to still claim territory in wolf-free areas, including that the Tasmanian Shadow Lords maintained a small breeding pack of wolves imported from Europe. (Personally, I've always taken this as justification for placing small groups of Garou, especially European descended ones, in places like Argentina or South Africa.)

                                      There was a thing in both the 1st and 2nd editions about a mythical lost Garou Caern of great power hidden within the Congo, but no one who has gone looking for it has returned.

                                      The first edition of the Black Furies tribe book mentions the possibility of Black Fury Caerns in the Congo dating back centuries, but it's an unconfirmed rumor. However, the Freebooters, Amazons of Diana, and Order of the Merciful Mother do operate in the continent, with the revised tribe book mentioning the Freebooter founded Blessed Home Caern and Dark River Caern being established around 1900. The first edition also has a (brief) write up of Julisha of the Thousand Masks. She is rumored to be a possible member of the Inner Calyx, so is probably the highest ranked Fury in the region. She uses miniature Zulu war masks as her calling card, so may originally be of South African extraction.

                                      The first edition Bone Gnawers tribe book mentions members living in Cape Town fighting against the apartheid regime. The first edition Fianna book also mentions tribal members living throughout the former British Empire, including South Africa and other parts of the continent. The first edition Get of Fenris also mentions the tribe being active in Africa, especially after WW2.

                                      My personal head canon is the the Silent Striders have always been around the entire Nile River area, even as far south as Uganda and Lake Victoria, as well as into Ethiopia and the Horn of Africa. (Obviously, they are of a lower density that they are in North Africa, the Middle East, and probably even India.) Obviously this requires strict adherence of the tribal policy of Don't Be An Asshole, especially where the African Mokolé are concerned. They've also been around the northern parts of the Niger River crescent since their diaspora.

                                      And, again as head canon, my South Africa has a population of Afrikaner Get of Fenris descended originally from the Dutch, who probably keep a very tiny wolf pack for breeding efforts. (Some of these Get are probably mixed race, especially since the 90s.) Cape Town and the other more English influenced areas have a small group of Silver Fangs of House Austere Howl, as well as some Fianna, Bone Gnawers, Children of Gaia, Glass Walkers, and Black Furies. But that's mainly just because I always felt that South Africa, especially in the 80s and 90s, was a good place for an interesting Werewolf chronicle.

                                                      The Ahadi should reek of idealism... it's an entirely idealism fueled endeavor that's only existed a few decades (at most) that's still heavily relying on emotional appeals based on current events to convince members to get along and try something that may or may not work, and may or may not even matter if it could work because it might not have time to grow into something before the Apocalypse hits.

                                                      The "genocide was a good idea," is a very strange take away. Europe didn't become the global metric for living standards because of wars from thousands of years ago, let along the Wars of Rage. That's an extremely recent change over the last ~125 years. Europe's current peace and prosperity is also inherently fueled by the destabilization and exploitation of Asian and African labor and natural resources. It's only "good" in a very Wyrm-ish sort of way were some of the richest and most powerful countries on Earth spread corruption, violence, and disease, just far enough away that they can normally ignore it back home and people can blissfully not consider themselves part of the problem.

                                                      Yes, the WoD is exceedingly guilty of exoticising non-Western cultures, as it got lost in satirizing European colonialism and imperialism. But at the same time, the more cooperative spirit in Africa and Asia makes sense:

                                                      1) In WtA lore, the Garou tend to be the ones that push disagreements between the Fera into large scale violence capable of wiping out whole breeds and so on. The rest of the Fera are generally more cooperative, and even more so after the Wars of Rage with the Garou as a common threat. The Beast Courts, the Amazon alliance, and the Ahadi are what the books say the status quo was before the Garou screwed things up.

                                                      2) Modern day Garou generally acknowledge that the wars with other servants of Gaia was a colossal mistake and many Tribes have been actively seeking to regain the trust of the Fera and reclaim spots in a more diverse coalition for generations.

                                                      3) Africa and Asia didn't have a "dark ages" comparable to the fall of Rome, leading to much larger swathes of history of relative stability, with commensurate stable trade (you know things like the Silk Road) and cultural exchange that follows. The idea that this greater stability translated into better cooperation between the Fera is a fairly easy leap to make.
