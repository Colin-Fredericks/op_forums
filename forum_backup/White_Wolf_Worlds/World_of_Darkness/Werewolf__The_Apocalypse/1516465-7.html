    Perhaps

    You bank your WP and when you have to choose between spending WP or entering Frenzy in Crinos, for example, you always choose the latter. Because Frenzy is a two-step-process, first you go after what triggered Frenzy, then after dealing with that, you roll whether you will go after everything in sight. That's where you need your WP-track undamaged. If you don't do that, you're not getting these advantages or have a lack of resources to use for shifting, regenerating etc. and Brutal Outcome turns into a hurdle instead. And Frenzy can still happen regardless.

            Honestly, James_Willoughby's house rule example really made me realise something; Why IS the Rage put onto the character all at once? While I understand the point about it being distracting, one of the things with the garou is that Rage can be channelled like a scalpel and used when needed. Clumsy and careless garou dump it all at once. But Rage Dice treat the whole thing as all or nothing, you cannot choose to use only one Rage Die to help you, they completely take over your regular dice.

            Which means that any garou with even 1 Rage Dice can just randomly smash what they are working on. For GWs, it means that they better not have any phones or laptops nearby since every hacking roll with Rage 2 might result in it being torn asunder and the precious willpower they need gets cut SEVERLY next session. Red Talons might have an easier time with technology than GWs, ironically.

            And yes, it is amazing how punishing all these supposed 'powers' are. These are things that are supposed to HELP you, yet the umbra hurts you, crinos is uncontrollable, Rage makes the (stated by the fluff) regular life hard.

            In older editions, the trap of Rage was how useful it was. It could save your character from death! Extra combat actions! Intimidation is easier! But too much Rage and you become basically unable to exist in the very little amount of human/wolf life you have. Heck, other GAROU start to see you as a loose cannon. Nevermind ANY Frenzy roll. Rage was like candy, delicious until you ate too much. The most important part of this was, that it was the player's own actions that often caused this trap to spring.

            But Rage Dice is not allowing the player to hang themselves with their actions, instead it places the noose on their neck from the outset. Which then means the Rage is treated with kid gloves all the time, which kills the whole point of it.

            Maybe the 5th editions are meant to just be mostly diceless or they are trying to match the movie werewolf style. Either way, if you make something that is supposed to be a strength of the character unpleasant to use? Then it will not get used because most players will hedge their bets in safer things.

            My gallery.

                    Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
                    Why IS the Rage put onto the character all at once? While I understand the point about it being distracting, one of the things with the garou is that Rage can be channelled like a scalpel and used when needed. Clumsy and careless garou dump it all at once. But Rage Dice treat the whole thing as all or nothing, you cannot choose to use only one Rage Die to help you, they completely take over your regular dice.
                    Yeah, Brutal Outcome is such an overly blunt thing as a game-mechanic, I feel like. Even compared to V5 because there you have Messy Criticals and Bestial Failure:
                    A critical win in which one or more 10s appears on a Hunger die is a messy critical. The character succeeds as in a regular critical – but like an animal would, not a being capable of foresight or self-control. The Beast scored the critical, perhaps, not you.​
                    Okay, so there's two conditions: You need to roll a critical success and there needs to be one or more 10s on a Hunger-Die.
                    A failed roll (not enough successes to reach the Difficulty or to beat an opposing contestant’s number of successes) in which one or more Hunger dice come up a 1 is a bestial failure.​
                    Again, two conditions: You failed the roll and one or more Hunger-Dice have a 1.

                    But W5 took this as inspiration and Brutal Outcome is this:
                    On its own, a Brutal result (1s and 2s) is treated as a failure, but two or more on a single test yields a Brutal Outcome. A Brutal Outcome causes a test to fail (usually with something being wrecked, harmed or destroyed) unless the aim of the test was to cause damage or injury, in which case a Brutal Outcome yields four automatic successes.
                    First, "Unless the aim was to cause damage or injury" covers a huge spectrum of rolls you normally make in a session, I think. That plus how here you just need two or more Rage-Dice with 1s or 2s to trigger things... it doesn't even matter what you actually rolled (unlike in V5).

                    It's like somebody looked at Hunger-Dice, Messy Criticals and Bestial Failure and thought: How can we make this mechanic more intrusive...? Also, with V5 you get multiple different examples of what to do with Messy Criticals and Bestial Failure. With Brutal Outcomes it's just "Your roll fails and your character wrecks/destroys shit".


                    Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
                    Which means that any garou with even 1 Rage Dice can just randomly smash what they are working on. For GWs, it means that they better not have any phones or laptops nearby since every hacking roll with Rage 2 might result in it being torn asunder and the precious willpower they need gets cut SEVERLY next session. Red Talons might have an easier time with technology than GWs, ironically.
                    Technically you can't get a Brutal Outcome with Rage 1, so there's that... but don't worry, there's still punishment awaiting the PC because if you have need of any ability (like a Gift or shifting into Glabro/Crinos/Hispo or regenerating health), a Rage-check can push you back to Rage 0 which then triggers "losing the wolf". With "losing the wolf", I think there you could also get an easy fix by just looking at W20 as there "losing the wolf" occurs when you have depleted all your Rage and WP-points. Same could be done here. "Losing the wolf" only happens when both your Rage and your WP are at 0. And just having Rage 0 means you can easily go back to Rage 1 through normal means without having to wait till night and do the whole "howling at the moon"-bit.

                    Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
                    But Rage Dice is not allowing the player to hang themselves with their actions, instead it places the noose on their neck from the outset. Which then means the Rage is treated with kid gloves all the time, which kills the whole point of it.
                    The fluff is all about how bad the Get of Fenris are for embracing Rage but Brutal Outcomes is written with the idea of "You either act out the Rage voluntarily - or the dice make you do it." The former gets you automatic successes, the latter makes you a failure.

                    Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
                    Maybe the 5th editions are meant to just be mostly diceless or they are trying to match the movie werewolf style. Either way, if you make something that is supposed to be a strength of the character unpleasant to use? Then it will not get used because most players will hedge their bets in safer things.
                    With H5 there certainly was more of an emphasis on the idea of "rolling dice is optional". There was that difference between V5 and H5 in its approach to "Automatic Wins". In V5 the text said:
                    If a character’s dice pool is twice the task’s Difficulty, the Storyteller may opt to rule that the character wins automatically without a dice roll.Automatic wins streamline play and reduce distracting rules interludes. Apply them vigorously, especially outside of combat or for tests where character failure is boring: information-gathering tests, conversation-openers, or gambits that open up the scene or let it move forward dramatically.​​
                    (Emphasis mine)

                    In H5 the text slightly changed:
                    If a character’s dice pool is twice the task’s Difficulty, the Storyteller may opt to rule that the character wins automatically without a dice roll. Automatic wins streamline play and reduce distracting rules interludes.

                    Apply automatic wins as much as possible, especially outside of combat or for tests where character failure creates no interesting outcome: information-gathering tests, conversation-openers, or gambits that open up the scene or let it move forward dramatically.​
                    (Emphasis mine)

                    And Renegade put out aids for playing H5. This is how dealing with dice-rolls is depicted there:

                    I assume the same attitude holds true for W5. I guess that could be why the punishing elements of the Rage-track were considered less of a problem by the developers when the assumption is that any dice-pool twice as big as the difficulty is never rolled and just assumed to be a success.
