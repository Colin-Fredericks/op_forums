        I find the writeup concise but informative, in the line of V5 Clan writeups: it tells you what Glass Walkers do, their relation with Garou Society at large, and gives you some concrete ideas how to play it.

        It also quite clearly states that Walkers are distrusted because other Wolves think they supported Humies during the Impergium.

        The fact is that the expectations of many are set towards another concept of Glass Walkers, I think, and another format of presentation.

        Presentation issue first.

        If we go back to V3 or V2 or V1 Clan writeups (I do not own other Werewolf corebooks besides W20, but I suppose the tone would be similar) , they weren't frontloaded with much setting info. That, was reserved for general fluff chapter and later supplements.

        V20 changed that format, due to the unique conditions of its birth: problem is, as we saw that kind of format was good for agnostic omnibus editions but difficult to maintain when you had a whole editorial line to support.

        What would you write in later supplements? If we look at many X20 supplements, they are either more omnibus formats (Lore of the X, Rites of Blood etc.) or far smaller books.

        At one point, they had to think of another edition to go on with publishing that kind of content, the space for agnostic stand alone supplements had ran out.

        Also, those kind of books were aimed at an expert fanbase: we long time fans CRAVE lots of information densely packed in one place.

        BJD and Apocalyptic Records are like Sunday happening on Monday with Ice Cream pouring from the Sky.

        When you are creating a new edition of a game, aimed at newbies, frontloading the splat page with setting details is risky, in terms of accessibility.

        Second point, we are looking at these Glass Walkers thinking of Legacy Glass Walkers.

        Taking into account this is a reboot, everything from imagery to archetypes to practical declination of verbs says that the "new" Glass Walkers are not Cyberpunk Corpos, but NeoNoir Hackers and Social Planners. They are close to Humanity, fixated with tech and want to navigate society and its many facets.

        A mix of Nosferatu and Toreadors, possibly, to stick with Vampire references. Some of their space surely is overlapping with OLD Gnawers and COGs but the important thing to look at is what the NEW tribes will be up to, confronted with the NEW Walkers.

        For instance, what few things we have learned about the COGs seems to make them much less diplomats and social activitsts, and much more priests and lorekeepers. As for Gnawers, if they and the Walkers are not distinguished by class, I'm curious to see where the difference will be.

        I actually like what I see, but I'm probably more in target of W5 than most of the forumites: I never went into old Werewolf because it had a very distinct terminology and complex metaplot.

        Which probably was part of its appeal and beauty, as I'm very fond of Vampire's own byzantine jargon and metaplot.

        However, we legacy players have the X20 editions to use for our philological needs: when they are trying to make a version of the game "as written in these times", aiming to a, new fanbase, I fully expect enough familiarity for old time players to jump on ship, but enough changes to justify the adaptation and a presentation of the materials which is simple enough to immediately grasp it.

            Ultimately though, they chose to make this the WoD 5th Edition, not the new new World of Darkness. This is Werewolf: the Apocalypse, not Werewolf: the Extinction. These are the Glass Walkers, not the Humanists.

            If they want to make a game where people aren't going to look at how the old and new look next to each other, they should have made something far more distinct. Plenty of people involved with V5 and W5 saw how much people did this to the cWoD vs. nWoD which were explicitly different universes with different settings and different takes on similar concepts. Of course it's going to be worse when they're keeping the original 13 Tribes, and then heavily redefining them (something V5 largely did not do).

            If they want to claim they're honoring the spirit of WtA 1e? This isn't it. If they're trying to make this easier to digest for new players? There's plenty of jargon that makes no sense unless you've internalized the lexicon.

            Like I said, the big problem with just this small snippet, is that 11 Tribes feel like too many for what they're doing and they needed to keep 11 Tribes because they want to keep the legacy of WtA attached. Maybe I'm wrong and after some more previews we'll see that 11 didn't overload the game's new direction, but this isn't a good start to convincing me otherwise.

                    I think what you are both saying is absolutely logical and understandable.

                    Broadly speaking, WOD5 has apparently pivoted away from the "bring back 1st edition" of Team Ericsson and embraced the idea of rebooting the game entirely, bringing parts of the old WOD and COFD together in the process not just in terms of mechanics but also of tone and presentation.

                    It's going into unsafe waters, and naturally invites comparison: perhaps it will pay off nonetheless, as it will allow them to draw on familiar brands but with greater creative freedom. Let's hope we do not end up in Star Wars Sequel territory.

                    Regarding the writeups, I agree we shall have to see if they can manage them without falling into redundancy: I'm a bit more optimistic after this one, as they are reworking concepts from the scratch, basically.

                    That said, the writeup is quite simple, it draws on two-three lore concepts presented before, but it stays into the right thematic territory, IMHO. Wolf is not particularly there, but urban animism and a certain urban fantasy theme are there. If anything, it is a bit mageish.

                    As for urban dogs, very true, I wonder if we will see something about them with the Gnawers. Contrast with Walkers might also be that Gnawers have accepted to "dilute their wolfishness" to adapt? Perhaps they will be more crime-connected or community-oriented, reflecting their embrace of domesticated canines? I'm curious.
