    Ajaba (Mechanical Deep Dive)

    03-15-2025, 01:02 PM
    Werehyenas! The Ajaba ruled over much of Africa given the success of their animal stock. The Spotted Hyena is usually seen as a lower animal, a scavenger, but they hunt and kill over 90% of their prey themselves. Closer to cats in evolution and closer to dogs in shape and behaviour (though both of these statements are simplifications!), spotted hyenas are highly social, distinguishing them from their closest cousins who are more solitary.

    According to legend the role given to the Ajaba when Gaia created them was to pick off the weak so that the strong could thrive. Eventually they were driven into conflict with the Bastet, most especially the Simba, leading to a genocidal war. Depending on the metaplot of a given game the Ajaba may be the proud rulers of Africa, or scattered to the four corners of the world, or fighting back in Africa as the core of the loose Ahadi alliance, or they might be returning to their old power after the defeat of the Simba.

    I recently finished a thread where I wrote about the Gifts available to Garou. Instead of doing a giant thread for the Fera, I decided instead to break it into a number of smaller projects. So if someone decides to look into discussions of Ajaba here in the WTA forums they'll find a post all about them rather than them having a section in a huge thread about Gifts.

    Green Gifts are often must-haves, or have applications which are incredibly game-changing, story-impacting or overall powerful. Amber Gifts are solid, their utility is often determined by how likely you are to need the given effect or whether it's important to your character concept. Red Gifts are, sadly, too weak to typically be a good investment. A Red Gift might have an insane cost for its effect, or have too hard a roll to reliably pass, or be entirely redundant, and so on.

    For example Persuasion is amazing for any character, whether socially focused or not, for 3xp it is an incredible power which changes your odds of passing social checks hundreds of times in a single chronicle. Master of Fire is neat, and if you build traps based around your relative fire immunity you can gain a great advantage over your enemy, but you might find it doesn't come up as often as you'd hope. Solid ability, worth buying, but you could live without it. Prey Mind is often difficulty 9 to activate and is made redundant by Hare's Leap and the speed of Lupus form in most cases; the Gift simply isn't very good at all, and you'd be better off investing in options which help you to fight whilst also helping you to run away if necessary, like buying more Athletics.

    Yava

    The Yava are supernatural weaknesses of certain Fera, the Bastet and the Ajaba. If you know all of the Yava a group suffers under then you know how to exploit their weaknesses to the utmost; supernatural abilities against a target whose Yava you know are at -2 difficulty, and using supernatural abilities against someone who knows your Yava gains a +2 difficulty.

    So, for example, an Ajaba trying to use a Gift such as Pulse of the Prey against a Simba would most likely suffer +2 difficulty. Meanwhile a Simba would very likely have a -2 difficulty to use the same Gift against an Ajaba, as most of them have been taught the Yava of the Ajaba. Thankfully they have no incentive to share these secrets; revealing their victories over the Werehyenas were the result of a supernatural curse and not raw strength would make the Simba look weak.

    The Yava of the Ajaba are that they will die if a specific spot at the back of their skull is struck, that pouring white wine into their footsteps causes them to become drunk, and that they are incapable of eating the flesh of a baby who is younger than a year old. The last one might be a tad confusing, but keep in mind that the job Gaia gave the Ajaba was to cull the weak; being unable to eat any human who is under a year of age was likely a factor in the rise of humanity. The fatal flaw in the skull is highly concerning of course, and we are given no precise rules for what striking that point would involve (+3 difficulty on the attack? Does the attack have to deal damage, or a certain amount of damage, to destroy the brain?). Reassuringly we are told that Ajaba often wear protective gear, presumably including Fetishes, which protect the spot.

    Forms

    This post isn't a substitute for reading W20 Changing Breeds, so I won't get into the full details of the forms. The most important thing in my book is that the Ajaba Crinos is arguably superior to the Garou Crinos; they have less Strength, but more Dexterity and Stamina.

    Ajaba have the normal Breeds, and their Forms are very close equivalents to the Garou. The only noteworthy difference is that Ajaba Metis often have a 'Metis Deformity' of being intersex, which speaks to myths about hyenas from prior to scientific investigation of the species; historically it was believed that all hyenas were part male and part female.

    General Ajaba Gifts

    These are available to all Ajaba, regardless of their Breed or Aspect. Garou Gifts are listed first, followed by writeups for Ajaba Gifts. See my Garou deep dive for discussions of the various Garou Gifts.

    Eye of the Hunter, Gift of Terror (Wolf at the Door), Infectious Laughter, Primal Anger, Sense Prey
    Curse of Hatred, Gender Shift (Man's Skin), Odious Aroma, Pulse of the Prey
    Clenched Jaw, Gift of the Skunk, Laugh of the Hyena
    Gnaw, Gorge (Willpower), Gorge (Rage), Gorge (Gnosis)
    Strength of Kilimanjaro (Endurance of Heimdall), Survivor

    Feral Grin (1): Passive Intimidation buff. +2 to Intimidation against targets with Willpower equal to or higher than yours, +2 or add your Primal-Urge (so 2-5 typically) to Intimidation against targets with Willpower lower than yours. With a Primal-Urge of 5, or perhaps higher with the right Totem, you'd boost your Intimidation by +5 against many foes.

    So, how good is +2-5 Intimidation? The bonus applies to Staredown, Exorcism, True Fear, Snarl of the Predator, Wrath of Gaia and various other Gifts, but sadly you're unlikely to have most of these. The biggest impact would be on the Facedown mechanic, which is available to all characters. +5 dice on Facedown would make you a great backup face for handling unruly third parties who might get in your way. The Taunt mechanic also makes use of Intimidation.

    A solid Amber, possibly a Green with the right Gifts and so on. Naturally far stronger when you have a high Willpower and maximum Primal-Urge.

    Crushing Jaws (2): Spend 1 Willpower after a successful bite to 'lock' your jaw. Once you've locked on, only death can end the lock against your will. Every turn you are locked on the foe takes 1 level of unsoakable Aggravated damage. While locked in, you can't do anything but stay locked.

    This is a dangerous Gift to use, but with a pack or similar backing you up... it's actually pretty decent. It's mechanically very similar to another Gift, Clenched Jaw, an Ahroun Level 4 Gift, which Ajaba get at Level 3. So, how do the two Gifts compare?

    Being able to make a difficulty 3 bite every turn is probably better than inflicting one unsoakable level of damage every turn. Meanwhile Crushing Jaws makes it impossible to get you off, while Clenched Jaw makes you very very hard to get off. Ultimately there should only be one Gift for this in a single list, and comparing them makes my head hurt.

    Against a foe with impossibly high soak, like an Incarna Spirit with 20+ Willpower, the unsoakable damage is far better than a normal attack. So it really depends on preference. I'm going to say that this Gift is Amber, much like Clenched Jaw, and that you should discuss the similarities between the two Gifts with your ST and how they can, or can't, be combined.

    Laughter of the Soul (3): Your laughter makes a single target who can hear you jumpy, terrified, or so scared they flee in a panic.

    Manipulation + Intimidation, difficulty of the target's Willpower, with 1 success causing unease, 3 causing panic attacks, and 5 causing the target to flee screaming. Keep in mind that to everyone else, all you did was laugh. This Gift might be a hyena laughing in the dark of night and the leader of your Pentex team running off into the night without a word, or it might be the opposing council in a court case laughing at a piece of evidence, which coincidentally happens a split second before the defendant starts sobbing with terror and fighting to escape.

    Worth noting that Feral Grin applies to this Gift!

    If the target is capable of a 'fear frenzy', which we see in most Fera, and in Vampires, then they will enter such a state if you get 3+ successes, unless they spend 2 Willpower to resist. In that case they become immune to being forced to flee by the power, but they remain very nervous and jumpy. Great power, well written, interesting mechanics, solid Green.

    Culling the Weak (4): Anyone who has yet to damage you in a given scene loses 1 Willpower and 1 Rage every time they try to attack you but fail to inflict damage (they missed, or failed to roll any successes for damage, or you soaked all the damage, or you dodged). This is fully passive.

    Incredible defensive/offensive power which is great for wearing down practically any enemy in the World of Darkness, as well as rewarding having a good defensive build. Worth remembering that Ajaba have great Soak in their Crinos form too.

    Withering Stare (5): Stare at someone, roll Rage, difficulty of the target's Willpower, and each success deals 1 level of Aggravated damage. This is a pretty subtle way of killing targets, or giving an invisible "warning" when someone is doing something you'd rather they weren't. A Garou attacks a spirit you don't want them to? Well, let them get a single hit in and then hit them with this; there's every chance they'll think that it's some kind of retribution or punishment.

    Given that any enemy you really, REALLY will struggle to fight will have decent Willpower, this isn't incredible, and the Gnosis cost for every single attack is kinda bad, but overall it's a handy tool to add to your arsenal. Just remember that too many successes on a Rage roll will send you into a Frenzy! That makes the Gift quite awkward to use, and potentially more of a detriment than a benefit. It doesn't help that Ajaba Metis can get access to Lash of Rage, which costs Rage instead of Gnosis, and doesn't have a difficulty of the target's Willpower.

    If Lash of Rage didn't exist this might be an Amber, but ultimately it comes out as a Red unfortunately. Clever uses exist, but this delivers very little for 25xp.

      Dawn Aspect

      These warriors were historically more likely to be female, but in recent nights the gender balance is equal. Dawn Ajaba have high Rage (5) and low Gnosis (1). Their Gifts are akin to the Ahroun. See the Garou Gifts thread for details on the pre-existing Gifts. Given the low Gnosis score of this Auspice, their Gifts are surprisingly spirit-oriented, perhaps reflecting that they are often leaders.

      Beat of the Heart-Drum, Lightning Reflexes, Pack Tactics, Razor Claws, Resist Pain, Sense Wyrm
      Command Spirit      , Dancing with the Dawn (Flame Dance), Shield of Rage, Spirit of the Fray
      Bloody Feast, Redirect Pain, Sense the Unnatural
      Grasp the Beyond, Stoking Fury's Furnace
      Bone Cracker (Fenris' Bite), Strength of Will

      Finishing Blow (1): If a foe already has a -2 wound penalty then you may spend a point of Rage (before attacking). This doubles your Strength for the purposes of the attack's damage. For example a Strength of 8 would become 16. A base of 16 damage dice, at Rank 1, under special circumstances yes, but 16 dice all the same, is amazing. The cost being Rage is good, the flavour is good, it may be a tad too strong but I love the Gift all the same.

      There's no reason this can't stack with other Gifts that double damage, for what it's worth. Coup de Grace, a Rank 3 Black Furies Gift, is much more complicated, but does double the dice pool instead of the Strength, so it's arguably superior, but the setup required is annoying. If you could get both you could kill God, so long as God has stats.

      Teeth at Dawn (2): On a turn where you go first, gain +2 dice to every attack you make. Fully passive, as more Gifts should be. Amazing Gift, suits the flavour of the 'Dawn' aspect being warrior-themed, love it. If they lacked Spirit of the Fray this Gift would be okay, but with that Gift guaranteed to be on your list... this is great. Garou would benefit greatly from having this Gift.

      Silver Jaws (3): Silver Claws, see the Ahroun Rank 3 post in the Garou thread, but this variant applies to your fangs instead. That makes the Gift worse if you prefer claws, but better if you prefer bites. I prefer claws... but if you're playing an Ajaba, you should be biting stuff most likely! This Gift remains Green for all the reasons Silver Claws is Green. If you're a bite-loving Garou with access to Silver Claws, try to contrive a reason to discover that this Gift exists and ask very nicely to copy it.

      Battle Fury Focus (4): Spend 1 Gnosis, roll Dexterity + Athletics (difficulty 7), and for the rest of the scene you get a free 3 dice dodge against all incoming attacks.

      In a typical fight, Dancing with the Dawn (Flame Dance) has you covered, since it lets you reflexively spend Rage to get a dodge when you need one. But your Rage expenditure is limited, and you might want to save Rage for attacking. This Gift is great when you are facing loads and loads and LOADS of attacks, and when you have a difficulty reduction to dodging, or if you have anything which adds extra dice to your dodge rolls passively.

      This is a great defensive Gift for the kinds of problems you face as a Rank 4 Warrior in WTA. Even if the 3 dice of dodge you get against the 20 attacks aren't enough to dodge them, it will reduce the damage of the incoming attacks significantly. If 3 successes becomes 1 success that might save you from some Aggravated damage you really can't be dealing with.

          Midnight Aspect

          The more balanced of the three Aspects (Gnosis 3, Rage 3), the Midnight Aspect used to be mostly male, which led to it being considered the least powerful. Traditionally Midnight Ajaba took on the roles which weren't considered glamorous and would not be snapped up by the other Aspects. Their Gifts focus on lorekeeping, storytelling, healing, and essentially being the 'support' for the warriors (Dawn) and assassins (Dusk). Interestingly they're closer to Galliards than Philodoxes all in all; the main 'balancing' aspect is that they have to balance a bunch of different roles and that they have equal Gnosis and Rage.

          I must give a disclaimer that I am not an expert on Africa, on African geography, on the cultures and languages and folklore, so this is just my best guess; there's a LOT of emphasis on rain in these Gifts. We're blessing the rain, we're turning the rain into bitter tears, we're bringing doom using rain. Very rain-heavy Gift list.

          Beast Speech, Mindspeak, Mother's Touch, Perfect Recall, Persuasion
          Distractions, Dreamspeak
          Exorcism, Song of Heroes, Wisdom of the Ancient Ways
          Gift of Dreams, Phantasm
          Fabric of the Mind

          Burning the Vision (1): By touching someone you can force them to remember the current scene, every tiny detail, every scent and sound and vivid scrap of colour. This costs 1 Willpower and requires no roll.

          This Gift could be used to ensure that an ally will remember every detail of something that is about to happen, like a meeting with a powerful spirit or a lifechanging battle. You could also use it during happy times to help someone to hold onto everything, especially a person with memory problems. But the primary intended use seems to be punishment or bearing witness. At a terrible site of an atrocity you burn the memory into the perpetrator, or a witness, so that the crime is never forgotten.

          How to rate this? If there were a roll, or if the cost were Gnosis, or if there were a maximum duration or any caveats this would immediately get a Red. But it's so absolute and does its job, even if that job is incredibly niche, so well that I feel an Amber is in order.

          Tears of the Heavens (1)*: Create rainfall in a radius of (Obligation) miles. At character creation we're not explicitly told how to split up an Ajaba's starting Renown; to be Rank 1 you need 3 points of permanent Renown, most likely 1 point in each. Increasing Obligation means prioritizing the needs of the group, so increasing it in a team game (mixed Breed, Beast Courts, Ahadi etc.) is likely relatively easy.

          This Gift does cost Gnosis, and the roll is difficulty 8. Unfortunately that really pushes it down. What's more, the resulting rain only lasts for 10 minutes per success! This would normally get Red, but Ajaba Gifts are very rain-dependent, and there are ways of reduction the difficulty of the roll (Wits + Expression), so we'll call it Amber. Outside of the context of the Midnight Gift list, this would be much weaker.

          Bless the Rains (2): Spend 1 Gnosis, roll Wits + Expression (difficulty 7), on a success you can enchant rainfall to heal wounds. Everyone within the radius of the rain (if the rain is natural then this could be a vast area, covering a massive region) recovers a level of Bashing or Lethal. This healing doesn't apply to creatures who are tainted by the Wyrm.

          Given that the specific instance of rain can only be blessed once, and given that you can't spend more Gnosis for more healing, and that more successes don't grant more healing... this is a tad limited. In a giant battle you could heal 500 allies, sure, but how many giant battles are you in. And unfortunately the Gift won't distinguish between Ajaba and Bastet, unless the Bastet are deemed to be Wyrm-tainted while engaged in genocidal activities.

          A side benefit of the Gift is that it makes the ground in the area unusually fertile. An Ajaba using this Gift during the heaviest and widest-reaching rainstorm of a season could restore a great deal of power to Gaia, healing a giant ecosystem and making it as bountiful as it was in centuries gone by. Naturally this bounty will only help Gaia if it isn't torn down excessively; using this in an area full of poaching, vanity hunting, poor farming practices and illegal forestry may do more harm than good.

          My main thought on this Gift is to imagine playing a group of Garou fighting for their lives; they're trying to save some precious cub from Fomori in the middle of a giant safari park. Halfway through the battle it randomly starts raining with no explanation, alright. The next turn every member of the pack restores a point of Lethal damage. That could be absolutely pivotal to the battle (everyone has taken some Lethal from gunshots and they haven't had time to Regenerate it yet), or it could do nothing at all (all the damage being done is Aggravated).

          Ultimately this is going to get Amber simply because the benefits you could bring with this Gift will be worth Renown, and because it will ingratiate you into almost any rural shapechanger community in the world.

          Scavenge (2): This Gift lets you destroy anything you can fit in your mouth. No questions asked, no roll, no cost. If the thing you're trying to destroy is actively dangerous (radioactive waste) then you have to spend 1 Gnosis.

          Shockingly useful, when you really think about it. Obliterate a Wyrm fetish before its owner can snatch it back. If you can get the blade of a Klaive inside your mouth for just a moment, gone forever. An evil ghost's Fetter is some nasty metal chain, well you can just swallow that like a giant spaghetto. It's also important to note that this Gift does let you digest what you eat, and safely. You can eat your fill of rotting meat without any issues... well beyond disgust and the horrid taste of course.

          This gets a Green simply because the potential for mayhem is amazing. In your various forms you have a nice big mouth and you're likely a biting expert; this Gift basically gives you carte blanche to inflict an insane amount of piercing damage on anything which will fit inside your mouth. This won't let you chew through a steel wall, no, but it would let you chew through a steel wall with a nice big dent in it.

          Shadows of the Serengeti (2): Spend 1 Gnosis, roll your Obligation (1-10, likely ~3 at Rank 2) against a difficulty of 8, for every success you summon a shadowy hyena. They have regular hyena stats but they have only three health levels.

          The Gnosis cost, rolling a type of Renown, and the difficulty being 8, make this pretty bad already. And then you find out you're summoning 1-2 ordinary hyenas, but with less health than normal. Red, which is a shame. The simple fix is to make it so that spending 1 Gnosis lets you automatically summon shadow-hyenas equal to your Obligation.

          Calling the Eshu (3): This is identical to Faerie Kin; see the Fianna Rank 3 post. The noteworthy change is that this Gift is typically used to summon 'tale-telling spirits'. The Eshu, one of the Kiths from CTD, have their origins in Africa and place great value on storytelling. That said there are a great many African Kiths explored in C20; this Gift could attract the aid of an Eloko, Okubili, Djedi, Obambo or Kuino.

          I'm marking this as Green, but if your ST has an attitude about Changelings (I've met some WoD players who have a serious hatred for CTD and the Dreaming), or is adversarial, it could be Red or Amber. If you find that this Gift consistently summons "help" which is inappropriate for the situation at hand, actively antagonistic or a huge Veil risk, creates endless complications or is just plain weak, consider asking for a refund on the 9xp (or switching ST).
          Bitter Tears (4): This Gift is also rain-dependent. While standing in the rain, spend 1 Gnosis and roll Manipulation + Expression (difficulty 7). On a success, every single human touched by the rain whose main home is in a place with a Gauntlet of 7 or higher becomes filled with a 'great listless melancholy'. This inflicts a -3 dice penalty to all dice pools while the rain lasts.

          Say you used this on some Pentex goons. It's almost certain they'll live in a town or a city, so great, the Gift works. -3 dice to all rolls. But the problem is the word 'human'. I'm going to assume that this includes all creatures you could reasonably call human. Fomori who are a human host. Ghouls. Mages. Kinfolk. Sorcerers, and so on. I wish the Gift's wording were 'characters lacking a Gnosis rating, excluding animals' or something to that effect. Regardless, given that this is Level 4, requires a niche setup, and harms niche targets, without the ability to choose who you effect... this is Red.

          Rain of Doom (5): Requires rain. Spend 3 Rage and roll Wits + Primal-Urge, difficulty 8. The Gift destroys manmade structure; 1 success would be enough for a cabin, 3 for a large house, 5 for an office block. The destruction is caused by the rain itself which begins to melt it away like acid.

          If a Level 5 Gift is going to cause disasters, it needs to be Veil-compliant, usually. This Gift could have a big impact, sure, but it's also a nightmare to use without exposing mundane targets to the supernatural. The cost is also very excessive. Where does this idea that a big Gift needs a huge cost come from?

              Dusk Aspect

              Traditionally female, the Dusk Ajaba have high Gnosis (5) and low Rage (1). They tend to work as assassins to fulfil their duty to Gaia. A number of their Gifts also help them deal with spiritual matters and the Umbra.

              Blur of the Milky Eye, Fatal Flaw, Open Seal, Scent of Running Water, Spirit Snare, Spirit Speech
              Blissful Ignorance, Luna's Armour, Sight From Beyond, Taking the Forgotten
              Paralyzing Stare, Pulse of the Invisible
              Open Wounds
              Reach the Umbra, Shadow Pack

              Laughter in the Night (1): +1 Stealth at night. Not terrible, and hard to justify not taking it (3xp for +1 to Stealth is a good deal vs buying more Stealth with xp), but hardly great either. +2 Stealth at night would probably be more fitting, or a difficulty modifier, but whatever, it's fine. Given that a number of Dusk Gifts use Stealth the +1 die will get a lot of use.

              Whispers in the Grass (1): Much better. Roll Wits + Stealth (difficulty 6), on a success you reduce the difficulty of all Stealth rolls in natural environments by 3. That includes, for example, the roll to activate Blur of the Milky Eye. The main downside of that Gift, and the only reason it's Amber, is a difficulty 8 Stealth roll. While this Gift has no effect in manmade environments, this will be endlessly useful in the wild and in the Umbra.

              Ambush (2): This, on its own, would make the Dusk Aspect pretty cool. So long as you're making a surprise attack you can spend a point of Rage, after you roll damage, to double your damage before soak. Say you're ambushing with a rifle, with a silver bullet.

              Dexterity 6 (4+2), Firearms 4 (spec in Sniping), Perception 4, you spend 4 turns aiming ideally, that's 14 dice to hit. And let's say you've grabbed Ogun's Blessing (see the Ahadi Gifts) for 4xp, and that you're a Homid so you have Apecraft's Blessings. Now say you're rolling 15 dice against a difficulty of 3 to hit. You'll average 12 successes. That's 20 dice of Aggravated, assuming you're shooting something with a Silver allergy which isn't in its Breed form. None of this is special, exactly, a starting Garou could do this, perhaps minus Ogun's Blessing, and perhaps with -1 Dexterity since Garou forms are stronger and less nimble.

              The thing is that Ambush will turn the 10 Aggravated hit, which could reasonably be survived by many Garou and Fera, into a 20 Aggravated hit. Even with the Rage roll to Remain Active, a Garou suffering this kind of hit is going to be in trouble unless they can get into cover or step sideways immediately. Then there's spirits, of course. No need for Silver, it's all the same to them. 20 Lethal is a pretty grievous wound to inflict at Rank 2, so not bad at all.

              Smoky Passage (3): Go through a solid wall at your full movement speed, so long as you spend a point of Gnosis. That wall can't be thicker than your Ferocity in feet, but even a reinforced panic room or safe won't have walls thicker than a foot wide. Given that if you're Dusk you have 5+ Gnosis, this Gift is a great way to handle escaping an attack on your enemy, or indeed to help you get in in the first place. If you have a Ferocity of 5, consider building yourself a little base which is completely inaccessible, 5 foot thick walls with no doors, no possible way in even in the Umbra, but you can access it using this Gift.

              As far as I can tell there's no reason this shouldn't work on floors and ceilings too. Ask your Storyteller.

              Umbral Strike (3): Also known as Backbite. Disappear and reappear behind a foe of your choice within 50 feet, then immediately make a -2 difficulty attack on them. Combines very well with Ambush, if you prefer a melee attack!

              Becoming the Night Jester (4): +1 to the physical stats of your 'Crocas' form at night. This pushes your Hispo equivalent from +3/2/3 to +4/3/4, which is very enviable.

              Echoes of Asamando (4)*: Ghosts can hear you, and you can hear them, for a full scene. There are two requirements; you must kill something (an animal will do) and you must spend 1 Gnosis at some point.

              A bit weak for a Level 4 Gift, and the requirements are annoying... but ghosts can be useful. This one is hard to rate given how much Wraiths differ between chronicles. Going to call it a Red, but could easily be an Amber at the right table.

                  Miscellaneous mechanical details.

                  By paying 3, 4 or 5 Freebie points you can free yourself of 1, 2 or 3 of your Yava via a Merit. Given that one of the Yava of the Ajaba is utterly lethal, I think it's worth getting rid of at least that one. The explanations offered are a blessing from a spirit (good plot hook) or simply that you're a 'strange prodigy' which I like less. An advantage to the 5 point version is that if you are totally free of your Yava, that means enemy Simba won't be able to take advantage of the mystic benefits of knowing your enemy's Yava. This manifests as -2 difficulty on supernatural rolls in one direction and +2 in the other direction, which is pretty significant!

                  Ajaba can have the Totem Background much the way a Garou would, tending to bond in Packs (all Ajaba, or more commonly in modern nights, mixed with other Fera). They can also use Pure Breed, but it's been decimated by genocide for the most part. Generally speaking they have all the typical Garou backgrounds including Ancestors and so on.

                  For Frenzying, Ajaba are much like the Garou but use a fixed difficulty of 6.

                  Renown-wise they utilize Ferocity, Cunning and Obligation; see W20 Changing Breeds for full details of course.

                  Finally, the Ajaba use Rites very similar to those of the Garou, with one noteworthy addition. The Song of Home is a Rite, Level 3, Accord, which restores all of the missing Gnosis, Rage or Willpower in a group. No special circumstances, no special requirements, it just completely restores missing resources with a single success. I would permit this Gift so long as there was a requirement or cost. A sacrifice of some sort would be a good start!

                              Final Verdict

                              Using Garou as a baseline...

                              +++ The Crinos form of the Ajaba is a significant step up in my book with its superior Dexterity and Stamina; Strength is great and all, but Dexterity is just sexier Strength
                              +++ If for some reason you use the Song of Home as it is written, without any changes, the Ajaba basically have access to totally unlimited Willpower, Rage and Gnosis
                              ++ On top of using the Garou Homid/Metis/Lupus lists, the Ajaba get a pretty good General list
                              ++ Access to Ahadi Gifts, and to the Ahadi alliance, is pretty excellent
                              ++ The Dawn and Dusk lists are superb, and compare pretty favourably with the Auspices; Dawn is basically just Ahroun + bonus stuff, Dusk is basically just Ragabash + bonus stuff
                              ++ Gnosis is determined by Aspect, not Breed, freeing you up to play a Homid or Metis with a starting Gnosis of 5 if you wish, gaining access to all of the benefits of those Breeds without sacrificing starting Gnosis
                              + Reaching Rank 4/5 is relatively easy given that any combination of Renown will do and the totals needed for the highest Ranks aren't that high compared to the other Changing Breeds

                              - The Yava can be a real issue if you don't overcome them with a Fetish or Merit
                              - Your Yava are known to your greatest enemies
                              - The Ajaba are currently in a terrible state due to the recent genocide and you're unlikely to receive much support

                              You're probably thinking "11twig, Garou are faster!" Spotted Hyenas are, in fact, slightly faster than wolves, but not a big enough difference to impact a TTRPG. Simply put, their various forms will be moving just as fast per dot of Dexterity, though annoying W20 Changing Breeds doesn't give a table for how fast the various forms move, or which forms get perception bonuses and so on.

                              All in all, in my personal view, all things being equal, a typical Ajaba will be slightly better than a typical Garou in a 1v1 confrontation, or a pack vs pack confrontation. Breed + General + Aspect will leave you with more of the core Gifts you need, on average (though Midnight isn't very good!), and you can cheaply cover a lot of ground. The higher Dexterity and Stamina in Crinos also really edges out the competition.
