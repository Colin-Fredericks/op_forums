            You're already engaging in this so-called revisionism if you consider anything from Greek mythology a "werewolf" when that word didn't exist yet. You can't say that Demaenetus was a werewolf unless you accept retroactively assigning of the concept of werewolf onto cultures that didn't use that word and even predated it. Or you're going to have to cite Pliny the Elder actually calling the boxer a werewolf, not some word that could idiomatically be translated as a werewolf.

            What it is, is classification. Anthropologists and related fields looked for common features in myth and folklore and gave them names if they felt like they represented something that goes beyond a small specific group. These labels frequently used words from specific cultural expressions of some larger concept, but when they got turned into labels they stopped being about those specific cultural expressions to the point of exclusion or there was no point in the label in the first place.

            The second you acknowledge that something is a "werewolf" despite coming from a culture that never used the word until interaction with Europeans that did use the word, you accept that it is a retroactive categorization of a type of cultural expression.

                                                    I think the question is less if avoiding tragedy at a First Change is doable or not and more that it isn't reliable either way.

                                                    There are a lot of things the Garou can do to ensure an inconsequential First Change, and provoke the cub into it to have it happen at a prepared setting. Heck, if the cub knows about them this can be Summer Camp-like, a pack take him to a secluded area to spend the days until First Change where nothing can go wrong (probably).

                                                    But every single option can also go wrong for a number of reasons in practice. Calm the Savage Beast and others depend on the Sept having the right person for the task and this person being available at the time. Certainly doable for the big ones, less so for the small ones, and in no Sept you can have someone with such resources that does nothing else of importance. Much less have an entire pack on stand-by for this task alone that can guarantee being able to intervene without killing.

                                                    A Baptism of Fire Rite has the same problems added to the roll itself. The best pool can still result in poor or no successes, and then the cub has years ahead to test those successes, so even a really good roll won't necessarily result in the spirit being there when it is needed.

                                                    Provoking a frenzy in the cub isn't guaranteed, either, because First Changes aren't normal frenzies. The cub has a tough time before it, but you can hardly say that they're just succeeding at Willpower rolls until the First Change. The actual First Change isn't predictable, so provoking it may not be a thing.

                                                    Of course, all that goes the other way around. too. The right people with the right skills may be available without issue. Even a relatively unskilled performer may have good successes by luck, and even with a single success the spirit may be there no problem when the time comes. With the Curse kicking in and the general bad mood of the cub they may very well be without targets at the Change even if there's no werewolf around.

                                                    So it is not a "per definition" thing that a tragedy will occur. But it is a possibility that can't ever be ignored. For a single character it either happened or not and that's it, but for the horror aspect the simple fact that you can't completely be sure is terrifying. Imagine this for a regular Garou parent: you can't live with your child until the First Change, under the risk of messing things up with the Curse or a frenzy end up with dead children. But your child will become a risk for the rest of your family, and you may not be able to stop it. Until the time comes it is inconsequential if your fears will be founded or not, the fear and uncertainty are there. They're always there.

                                                    Like Evan will always be haunted by the uncertainty. Did he killed his parents? He can't say, he can't know, he'll never know. The truth is irrelevant to the drama.
