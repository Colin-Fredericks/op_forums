            One of the disappointing elements of W5 is that it's way too matter-of-fact in how political it is. Sure, WtA always was political. Environmentalism was only the most obvious angle in that regard. But the spiritualism and animism of WtA should be seen as an asset in approaching these political issues from a different perspective. What W5 offers instead is far more worldly and grounded in present-day activism. Even though, the religious right in the US is an interesting topic for a game like WtA. What allowed things like Mormonism to establish itself? Or since one of the core-elements of W5 is the Cult of Fenris, why not take a look at cults in the US like the Manson family?

            Or to take a WtA-specific thing: How about exploring the switch from Gaia-worship to Luna-worship by drawing parallels to the switch from polytheistic believers to Christianity, like what the Vikings went through, for example? And of course, it wasn't just about religion and a matter of faith. There's plenty to explore here that goes beyond just having the Garou be environmental activists and the bad Cult of Fenris be right-wing extremists.

                        Originally posted by Damian May View Post

                        And a very centrist take on RL issues at that...
                        My definition of centrist tends more towards "lacking any party identification and therefore takes heterodox positions on a variety of issues" (To quote Chris Rock: "I'm conservative about taxes. I'm liberal about strippers!"). Which, as a political organizer, is less then ideal but at least something I can work with.

                        What is so frustrating about W5 is that its ideology and rhetoric isn't even centrist. Its moderate. What I call "the mushball moderates". Which are actually center-right leaning lovers of the status-quo. David Brooks being the perfect example. To their (upper middle class) eyes everything is fine the way it is: Nothing should change too much or too quickly. The left wing should be quickly upbraided for everything their fringe utters. Meanwhile they demand the angry fringes of the right be understood, spoken to, compromised with, and catered to. Societal others (women, POC, queers) are free to make the case for their humanity, but only if they do it the right way. Politely and in a calm measured voice (while a boot stands on their neck). Fascism is a historical boogeyman we defeated in WWII so why are you bringing it up now?

                        Whatever your politics (and I obviously wear mine on my sleeve) we can agree that OG W:tA was possessed a deeply radical politics. Radical as in "relating to or addressing the the root (causes)". Eco-Terrorism and/or spiritual warfare against the wyrm are deeply radical projects. Even the spirituality and social context is radical, weather it be an exploration of animism (often disparaged to as a "primitive" religion or worldview) or communal living in an (idealized) tribal arrangement.

                        And W5 just seems uninterested in any of that. Some of the toning down is understandable at least. The Chechnya kerfuffle surely had caused some harsh edges to be sanded off. And its understandable now in 2024 the responsible position is to be a LOT less cavalier about political violence. But I still expected some sort of motive force, some sort of directionality or position that could frame the action. Like, you can retain an agnostic position in the game text regarding "Garou must return to the old was VS the old ways must be left behind or destroyed" and just present it as a conflict in the setting. But we don't get that because no one remembers the lore, the elders are all dead, and oral history degrades SUPER fast evidently. Or a conflict like "KILL PENTEX REND SHRED BITE VS "actually some people who work in that Megadon front office are nice normal people who just want a paycheck and also volunteer at a homeless shelter on the weekends". Except no, because all the subsidiaries are gone and incremental change is the best you can hope for.

                        This is the definition of a "me" problem but I do not want to play "Union Organizer: The Card Checking" in my free time.

                        So you have a game that incorporates drama. Both games and drama almost require conflict (or at least competition, a form of conflict). When the ideology of your games presupposes the primacy of the status quo and the shushing of anything more radical than another cut in the capital gains tax you're left with a game without a conflict. So you're left with something that not only feels apolitical but devoid of stimulus to act in any direction. Nothing is incentivized, everything is punished. Action is a sin and ambition is unseemly. And that's why I think even those who don't share my politics find something lacking in W5

                                The lack of commitment to any political perspective is part of a bigger issue that has been plaguing W5 as a whole: It's shallow. And it becomes very apparent whenever the rubber meets the road in terms of worldbuilding, setting-description and any other sort of detail that tries to make sense of what is being described.

                                To focus on one part in particular: diversity. W5 has this idea of a fallen Garou Nation where regional traditions and beliefs are very important to Garou as references for their Rites and norms. Like when it says on pg 7 "Modern communications technology means that Garou can talk to each other easier than ever before, and so have realized that their immutable traditions and culture are often actually entirely local. Other Garou in other places do things differently.". But W5 is having a hard time putting this into action and follow up on this premise in a meaningful way.

                                The reason: W5 seems to be deathly afraid of paying attention to any tradition, people, culture or region outside of North America and Europe for more than a nanosecond. (Then again, W5's relationship with Native American culture isn't faring that much better either...)

                                Take a description like the one for Princess Mari from the Sept of the North Country: "An eighteen-year-old Lebanese teenager from New York found by a pack still loyal to Albrecht. She used to have another name and another family, but after her First Change, she was brought here and renamed after an old packmate of the King.​" Why Lebanese...? It often feels like these references like someone being from somewhere outside North America and Europe are made to simply make it seem like this book cares about any setting outside of Europe and North America. You get things like a Chinese Taikonaut, someone from Nigeria or India or you have a Sept that is rescuing Libyan refugees in the Mediterranean. All of these references are presented with the depth of a puddle, though. Scratch the surface and the references come off as almost random for how little they seem to matter in the writing. There's never any deeper meaning or consideration here. The country-names get invoked as "other" and "not-Europe/North-America" but that's about it. In the case of the section for the Sept of the Water's Birth Libya might as well be just a black hole that people are fleeing from. Or take a look at how you have this one Loresheet for this one Chinese Taikonaut who has a special relationship with Luna. There's no depth to making that character Chinese. It might as well be just a random name of space-faring nations drawn out of a hat.

                                And to further highlight W5's absolute focus on North America and Europe, let's just ponder why this book felt the need to include this sentence on Liu Aihan's character-sheet: "When off-duty from the space program, she travels the world, teaching other Garou about Luna and how they can shed the limits of their auspices.​" Could it maybe be because there's an unwritten rule in W5 that adventures outside of North America and Europe are somehow unimaginable for W5's official products...?

                                The closest this book gets to having something that isn't about Europe or North America is the Legendary Rite about Ross the She-Wolf taking on Union Bromide. But the writing is so bland that there's little reason for why the setting couldn't just be "Insert random country with a Supreme Court here". The descriptions and worldbuilding are simply so uninvolved and meaningless that the "where" of the whole scenario could pretty much be characterized as "anywhere".

                                And this shallowness is such a big problem because it has now repeatedly led to writing where it feels like you're getting a half-formed idea - and right there the thinking starts and ends. It never feels like the ideas are explored to a degree where you get something that inspires the imagination or illuminates a particular setting in a meaningful way. It's just this series of signposts gesturing at various things. Saving Libyan refugees from the Mediterranean? Sure, why not? Protecting environmental activists from bad guys? Okay, that sounds right. Sabotaging an Amazon warehouse? Certainly sucks to work there! Add it to the list! But all of this... It's about as meaningful as McDonalds tweeting "BlackLivesMatter". It's shallow. W5 is more concerned with what activism looks like than delving into the why of it all.

                                But of course, that's hard to do when W5 still falls into tropes like making Europe and North America the center of attention while everything else on the globe is just a sideshow...
