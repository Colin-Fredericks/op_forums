    About W6...

    07-16-2026, 06:36 AM
    Now Apparently from what little we know about the at the time V6 project salted from to be revealed at gencon is going to be closer to legacy.

    Now assuming that 6E being closer to legacy isn't pure crpo speak on Parawolves part and that theoretical "legacy mandate" is applied Consistently across game lines, What would it for a Possible 6th edition of WtA?

    Like V5 is theoretically easiest line to pivot legacy. Just simply end the Beckoning and make the Sabbat playable, of course would be some challengers to like the writers having to come up with definitive answers on what the Beckoning is and what happened in the middle east. The Former would probably be disappointing compered to the mystique built up around it and the latter is obviously stepping into a Political MineField on the best of days.And the emergence of the Imbued and the Orgs reaction to them would make for good myth arc for a theoretical H6.

    And of course the main subject of this thread W6 and how it would follow up to W5.

    Now what is said about W5 in this Forum would not bear repeating. It's a distinct game from the past edition for (arguably little) better or (mostly) worse. Now if White Wolf is serious about returning to legacy and not just simply continue W5's current trajectory then what would it mean for W6? Like would the theoretical 6th edition just ignore W5 and start from scratch again, bend the little metaplot of the line has into a pretzel to try and probably fail to justify the shift into legacy lore and tone, or most dire scenario there wont be a W6 in the first place either because v6 fails spectacularly, Paradox shifting funding away from White wolf or management declares WtA as a whole too controversial to publish.

    The Future of WtA and WoD as a whole is uncertain until Gencon. But there little harm speculate.

      The problem is that we have nothing to work with right now.

      W5 is a problematic line, but it's not complete financial failure. The criticism is intense, but the sales are good and supplements are still being shipped.

      It also has a point that has some traction: while the lore in general is shallow, there is praise on the changes to the tribes, not everything, but the basic concepts.

      A lot of people also don't hate the new lore, just find it, as I said, shallow. Which means they are not all demanding the old lore if the new evolve enough.

      My guess is that ditching W5 lore is safe, as it doesn't have buy in, but elements of it are likely to inform the new direction. I think it will be a return to Legacy, but expect still as much of a change as V20 to V5.

      On the upside, W5 selling well with the shitty reception it has proved one important thing: WtA sells and Paradox noticed. This in itself is a strong sign that they're going to make a W6.

      But we also have the frequency problem. Since books aren't really their thing, but being control freaks is, Paradox is slow as fuck at publishing, doing in 10 years what White Wolf could do in 1 good year. If the new new White Wolf don't change things considerably, it will be another 9 years before W6 sees the light of day.

                              Well whether it’s a full-blown Sixth Edition or just WEREWOLF 5.5, I think it’s not unreasonable to suggest that Paradox & White Wolf alike work out how to best integrate the spirit of older editions (Which is what won a fan base to start with) and the surprising number of ideas from the much-maligned (and sometimes very fairly maligned) Fifth Edition.

                              To be honest, I sometimes wonder if the key failing of Fifth Edition was tone - too often projecting the impression that this was not an Apocalyptic, but a Post-Apocalyptic setting, which rather defeats the whole purpose of the game, pitching characters into action as the die-hards determined to cancel the Apocalypse or die trying (In cases “… and die trying” might be more apt).

                              This is, admittedly, written without reference to behind-the-scenes dramas of the sort I take no particular pleasure in reading about, beyond the understanding that Paradox have done themselves little credit by their treatment of creative staff.


                              To be honest, I sometimes wonder if it would be more fun to pitch WEREWOLF: THE APOCALYPSE as something more like WEREWOLF: THE WYLD WEST (With players thrown into a fictionalised historic era and allowed to enjoy all the adventures possible therein whilst writers get to enjoy the benefit of hindsight as they put together their setting), not least because doing so allows one to very consciously structure things in a way that allows sourcebooks to start as ‘Trenchcoat Nineties mayhem’ and count down to all-out Armageddon over a period of years.

                              Say with the core sourcebook starting in AD 1992 and the last one launching the no ifs, buts or candied nuts Battle of Armageddon circa 2012, (Yes yes, the ‘Mayan Apocalypse’ was not a thing, but twenty years is a nice round number) with the books published more or less in real time?

                              This is, admittedly, driven by my suspicion that WEREWOLF: THE APOCALYPSE is a title that rather demands a Grand Finale, so it makes sense to build up to it from the start rather than leave it a ‘Maybe’.


                              (I admit that a pastiche of The World of Darkness - and other very 20th/early 21st century Urban Fantasy/Horror stuff) called THE SHADOW MILLENNIUM has been a notion tickling my fancy for a number of years now, so one might regard this as retrofitting the idea to the original).

                                  Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                                  To be honest, I sometimes wonder if the key failing of Fifth Edition was tone - too often projecting the impression that this was not an Apocalyptic, but a Post-Apocalyptic setting, which rather defeats the whole purpose of the game, pitching characters into action as the die-hards determined to cancel the Apocalypse or die trying (In cases “… and die trying” might be more apt).
                                  Tone was not the problem. It is indeed a Post-Apocalyptic setting from werewolf POV and it's intentional and publicly known, and that surely alienates some fans.

                                  Old fans have a too strong tendency to overestimate their own value both as fans and as representatives of the public. Being a new setting with a new proposal wasn't in the least a problem for general reception.

                                  What it is, and that's a very consistent depiction by the people who actually plays it and reads every book, is bland and shallow.

                                  Supplements have been being released and bought, but the strong consensus is that they mostly do work that should have been done in the core book and are still lacking.

                                  Likewise it's not the spirit of Legacy that's being missed, is its depth. Core WtA, any edition, gave you enough to explore Garou politics, Sept disputes, fight the Wyrm in many fronts, face Pentex, explore the Umbra and so on, all while having a strong grasp of how your Breed and Tribe inform your identity, relations, cultural expectations and questions. That's before everything the supplements added. Core W5 gives you character creation rules, a rough idea of party line and immediate threats, and a declaration that there's nothing else to know. Oh, there's a spirit realm, but isn't important.

                                  And that's why the simplest solution is to just bring Legacy back. Not because it has better tone or "spirit", but because being deep is a lot of work, using Legacy the work is done.

                                  Remember those people had the audacity to re-release a Second Edition book exactly as it was, not even a new cover, and charge full price. For Vampire, Revelations of the Dark Mother.

                                  Your idea is interesting, but we already have the Apocalypse book from 2004 IIRC and fixing one Apocalypse would counter the role of a TTRPG book. Overall the idea of covering the history of a period makes for games with very limited usefulness, as happened with Orpheus (though it was made for this and succeeded at this, still even fans of Orpheus rarely play Orpheus).
