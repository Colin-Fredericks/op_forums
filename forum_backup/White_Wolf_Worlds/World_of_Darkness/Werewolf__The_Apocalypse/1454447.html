    W20: Tips for "Fighting Dirty" or how to make combat more interesting for oneself?

    07-07-2021, 09:19 AM
    Hello everybody,

    I am a player in a Chronicle that as of now goes on for more than half a year and every time a prolonged combat scene comes up I always dread it.

    Maybe I am exagerating a bit with my choice of words, but combat in WTA is very boring to me.

    So I occasionally try to spice up things for myself by asking for Perception Checks that maybe allow me to notice things in the evironment to use to my characters advantage or try to use special moves from the Corebook like "letting someone eat the pavement". While this is fun the payoff seems often times not worth over just doing the standart "Claw", "Bite" or "Weapon"-Attack supported by Gift usage. We were in talks to use "Pack tactics" more often, maybe even come up with our own, but often these are very very specific.

    I am in no way some min/maxer who is always searching for the most effective solution but "Rule of Cool" has it's limits when your beloved characters life is on the line every intiative role, so you better "kill or be killed". Especially with the "Stun"-Ruling and my characters low stamina.

    Combat is occuring rather often in our Chronicle. At least once or twice per 2 sessions. And it can last very long.

    As the player of the packs Theurge I am admittedly more at home doing all the spirit dealing stuff and umbra mysterie riddle shenaningans, which I enjoy very much. I tried playing on that angle for combat situations but it seems silly to me trying to conduct the Rite of Summoning in the midst of combat. Technically it is possible rules-wise (I think?) but it seems out of place. And having the correct items or offerings for proper chiminage is also difficult. Since combat is, at least in our chronicle, more than often started by ambush, preparation is also not always possible.

    Having played a Shadowlord Homid Philodox before who was very much into conducting a "shadow war" against the enemy with indirect attacks through contacts and other deals I am a little bit lost now playing a Red Talon Theurge. I love playing this character any other time than combat.

    Do you have any tips or tricks for me to spice things up for myself in combat situations? Especially as a Theurge. I am asking this strictly for myself because my friends are very content with the way combat is conductet and I don't want to impose anything on them that told them how to play the game.

    Homebrewing advise is appreciated but since I am not the ST I would have to pitch that to him and my fellow players first. We are playing a very raw version of W20 with very light homebrewing.

    Thank you in advance

      Combat in WoD is very abstract. You can storytell what your character is doing, but in the end, it comes down to the roll. You could incorporate that very cool, over-the-top, anime-style combat actions that are fun to listen to grant bonus dice. I think that is what Exalted does (or was it just my ST, not sure).

      If you want combat done differently, the 2nd Edition had "WoD: Combat" - possibly adapting parts of White Wolf's licensed Street Fighter RPG. It basically had lots of manoeuvrers, each making slight changes to initiative, dice pool and difficulty, for bonus damage or additional effects, like pushing an enemy back or so. It also worked well with the lowest initiative has to reveal manoeuvrers first, since one would have home-made cards which one would place on the table and everyone could see, who is doing what and so on.

      If that is too much, there is always the possibility to remove Rage actions from the rules and use Rage for benefits to attack rolls, damage rolls in addition to ignoring wound penalties and such things. Additional actions were "cool" in the 1990s but since then most games got rid of them or made hard caps. V20DarkAge and V5, are WoD examples, but SR5 and SR6, as well as D&D3.5 got rid of those. A mechanic, where the Ahroun may spend two Rage to get, for example, half permanent Rage to an attack and a dodge roll might bring about a situation, where splitting dice pools makes sense and the ahroun still remains a position as the best fighter auspice.

      If that is too much diversion from the rules, then do something different. Rites used to need about ten minutes per Rite level, if no explicit rule was given - to the rule of thumb would prohibit usage of Rite of Summoning in battle. Doesn't mean that you cant make pacts beforehand and get a spirit to help you in combat. The Rite of Binding is used to create Talens, but it ca very generally bind spirits to objects. So, binding the spirit to some task is something that needs a lot of fine tuning and deliberation between players and ST - for the whole of game balancing might be at stake. Then again, WtA isn't really balanced and game mechanics are considered less important than the stories told, so it works well as long as players and ST agree that any exploits that might become apparent will not be abused at the expanse of the story.
      Then again, the W20 is not an encyclopedia like V20, so it isn't a complete list of all spirit charms ever published in it. For for a greater variety consult older WtA or MtA or MtSC books. Same holds true for Gifts, actually....
      Last edited by heinrich; 07-07-2021, 02:12 PM. Reason: Changed "WoD Combat" to "Combat in WoD" so it isn't mistaken for "WoD: Combat" (the book).

          Just to get to a simple point: the Summoning Rite isn't functional in combat (rites usually take a couple minutes per level). It part of the reason Gifts that let you summon stuff are highly valued in the rules.

          I think part of the problem is... Red Talon Theurge isn't really a a combo for a character that's going to be able to build towards some more off the wall combat options. "Bite and claw until the enemy is dead," being the only really effective tactic for a Red Talon is kind of what the Red Talon's want as a Tribe; being clever is something you do as you stalk the enemy, not once the fight breaks out. Theurges might be the least flexible Auspice in combat because their so oriented towards spirit-combat that even Ahrouns get more "neat trick" stuff to do.

          My big suggestion would be to focus on that prep work. Lean into the Theurge role and get some spirit friends lined up before combat if possible. For your sake, go for ones that create tactical fun instead of raw might. The rest of the group - with warning of course - are going to enjoy fights just as much if you're busy organizing a bunch of rust-spirits to destroy the enemy's weapons rather than attacking directly. Consider doing a bunch of Talen crafting (and maybe some Fetish crafting) to bring in more lateral solutions to combat problems. While a Revised supplement, Hammer & Klaive should work fine for a guide to homebrewing more than the example ones in the book; also take a look at the spirit Charm section for what sort of tricks a Talen might do if you stuff the right spirit in it.

              Don't roll for damage or soak, just take half. Speeds things right along. This drastic reduction on the amount of rolls you do will open you up for dramatic action.
              Another good rule is to reduce the penalty for locational damage so that you're more likely to want to target a limb.
              You can choose to just.. do normal strikes... but phrase them cool.

              Personally, I also hold the view that only a Galiard seeks an even fight, and Galiards are the worst auspice. You want to win overwhelmingly and decisively in the first round, if not before your opponents even have a chance to retaliate. You should always use ambushes. At worst (say, a duel) you should be prepared with weapons that are garanteed to kill or cripple within the first round. If combat enters a third round and you're not obviously winning, you should run. Your enemies should be into this too; littering your forces around the metaphorical dungeon is not going to work against werewolves.

                  Galliards have no specific reason to seek "even fights." They're the storytellers of the Garou. They want to have tales to take back to the Sept and regale with at Moots. If you look at the Renown charts you get a good idea of what sort of things the Garou value socially, and thus what the Galliards are going to want to sing about. Your pack using smarts to swiftly destroy a Wyrm threat without taking any serious injuries is worth a lot of Renown, which means its good fodder for the Auspice's duties. Even when it comes to formal challenges they're going to just spin the situation that makes for the best campfire telling. An evenly matched duel is an tense back and forth to keep the audience guessing who wins, but an uneven match could be a great little underdog victory to encourage people when things look bleak, or a smack down as a warning to keep people humble.

                  Galliards do get underwhelming mechanics (the Renown awards for their specialty is peanuts compared to the others, their high starting Rage makes a bunch of concepts hard to pull off, and like a D&D bard they can easily feel like they're always second fiddle to a more focused Auspice thanks to their Gifts).

                                                      Originally posted by Spacedigger View Post
                                                      Maybe I am exagerating a bit with my choice of words, but combat in WTA is very boring to me.
                                                      As Heavy Arms said, what you needs more is lateral thinking. Some things come to mind:

                                                      Combat is frequent and you are usually ambushed, right? Then take to yourself the role of outsmarting your enemies before you're ambushed. As a wolf it is totally in character for a Red Talon to be a smart predator and wary of their environment. Have spirits doing surveillance and scouting for you.

                                                      Once you get used to anticipating combat instead of waiting for it to begin, then prep time will be easier.

                                                      Finally, get the group and develop your own Pack Tactics. You only need a couple, for the more pressing matters. I'd recommend something for when you're ambushed and/or to keep you safe and using whatever Gift you can pull to give support.
