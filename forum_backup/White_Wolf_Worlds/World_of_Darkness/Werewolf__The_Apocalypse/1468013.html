      I believe that reduced difficulty does apply to Gifts as well as mundane actions. If people know better information, I'd love to hear it.

      The standard rule in the game is that if your dice pool equals to the difficulty level, then you can choose not to roll and gain 1 success (and 1 success only). So if their dice pool is at least 3, and the character only needs 1 success, then yeah, it can happen automatically. However, if the Gift (or any other action) is something that requires more than one success, or is done better the more successes you have, then the character should still roll.

                          The book let's you make the damage roll without an attack roll because the scene may still have a number of complications that make it relevant. Like the enemy that can get back on their feet.

                          Yet, this same example is also one where number of successes is key: attack success affects damage pool. If you need this bonus, roll for it. You're trying to get a precise strike instead of just a strike, in a limited time scale (a turn) instead of taking your time.

                          So, there's drama. If there's drama, there's no reason to use average results and call it a day, rolling matters. If you don't think that rolling matters, then you already decided that even the theoretical possibility of this enemy getting up is a foregone option and you can just end the scene.

                          It's the difference between the player choosing a standard success and the ST choosing to not ask a roll in the first place. An ST don't have to ask every roll, or any roll for that matter. The ST may decide the outcome in any number of ways without a roll.

                          As a rule of thumb, if you really have a situation where, as an ST, you want to know the effectiveness of an action without asking for a roll, then sure you can simply run the average of the pool and call it a day, but that's not an automatic success rule, instead it is the ST choosing to not ask for a roll because it adds nothing to the drama.

                                  For 10+ difficulty I personally like to apply the Mage rule. If the action is indeed still possible, cap the difficulty at 9 and demand an extra success for each point it would have above 9. So instead of dif 10, you have dif 9 but the first success is "lost", and so on. You can add that those "lost" successes don't count towards avoiding a botch if you prefer.

                                  In the same vein, you can decide that below 3 the character gets extra successes if the roll itself is successful (zero successes in the roll alone are still a failure). You can theoretically cap at 2, but I agree that 3 works better. This option is untested, though, try it by your own risk if you want.
