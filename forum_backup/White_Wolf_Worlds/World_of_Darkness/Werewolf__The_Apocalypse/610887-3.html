                                                            This. So much this. Whatever wolf-killing humanity did in the World of Darkness was (unconsciously/subconciously) in response to the Impergium. Humanity's desire to pave over nature is a direct result of humanity's desire to make the world safe. That instinctual desire comes from the Delirium. The Garou created the Impergium, which makes the Garou responsible the Delirium and everything that resulted from the Delirium, including humanity's pogrom against wolves.                                                             Except that, again, we know how things turned out without an Impergium. It wasn't much better. If anything, the fear that made humans cluster closer together allowed the Red Talons to survive just a bit longer by making more territory into virgin wilderness where humans have never ventured. An explicit part of the atmosphere of the WoD is fewer small settlements and more people in the city, with more dark forests and foreboding stretches of plains and desert. What was actually wrong about the Impergium is that all the other Changers didn't put the Ratkin in charge of it, and that the Garou thought that they had done well by stopping the killing after committing genocide against the Fera that were meant to cull humans.

                                                            Adaptation is a law of Gaia, but what kind of adaptation are we talking about here? The narrative of progress sounds nice and all, but if humans are going to spread far beyond their original ranges, kill fauna that would have controlled their numbers using tools, turn wetlands into cities with drainage problems while simultaneously trying to grow flat and level lawns in the desert... that's not something that adaptation can really help, especially when the lack of care exhibited by highway traffic for most living things cuts every habitat into tiny, marginal zones with the threat of death for ranging around. I guess that's an ironic twist benefitting the Mokole that don't need as much open territory, but it isn't a condition to which wide-ranging wolves can adapt. Even dogs would almost entirely be dead in no time if they had to run around without direct human support, and the Red Talons view domestication as an unthinkable crime, since it made wolves into a slobbery, obedient, puppy-like not-wolf. They get a massive uncanny valley effect just thinking about dogs, and they have for at least thirty millennia or so.
