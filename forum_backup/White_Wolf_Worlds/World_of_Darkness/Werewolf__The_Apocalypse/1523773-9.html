    I think there are two separate concepts combined into one character. There's nothing bad about that. Mixing different ideas can lead to stronger, more interesting characters. But sometimes multiple ideas can work against each other, diluting each concept instead of making a stronger one.

    The hairlessness is the metis concept as is any social outcast as a result. By itself, fairly standard metis. Hairless is even one of the ten examples of malformities given in the 1e corebook. This aspect for this character is very harsh and negative, but other early 1e metis characters don't have it so bad and are portrayed more noble and compassionate and are even looked out for by certain other Garou.

    Then there is the spirit whisperer concept that I don't think is based on the character's breed. It's because of their tribe. 1e WtA emphasized the Uktena as being dark and mysterious. Their members were unfriendly to other tribes, being sullen and introverted. They dealt with malevolent spirits of the Deep Umbra, and jealously guarded their sacred sites. It's part of 1e Werewolf being much less epic heroic fantasy and more dark fantasy gothic horror oriented with many Garou having darker edges to them. Later editions would deemphasize that, and the Uktena being a tribe that included other peoples was more emphasized and the tribe portrayed more positively.

    I will also say that while the 1e corebook obviously mentions the homid ethnic heritage of these Garou tribes, that I think it is really the upcoming tribebooks that heavily concentrated on the human ethnic groups as a means to develop each tribe. That came at the expense of a more broader view of the tribe as cultic devotees to their tribal totems. The first approaching emphasized the side of their human kinfolk and homids. The latter would emphasize more about the Garou as not really being human, not even the homids raised in human culture.

    In this template's case, the writer wanted to combine the two concepts for a tormented soul concept. On one level, I think this is totally fine. Lots of players could enjoy playing this concept. It's totally valid. Could this concept be disruptive or prove too difficult or not fun for some players? Of course. I don't think having such a concept here is wrong though. I think the writers are showing the diversity of concepts that are possible by mixing tribes, auspices, and breeds. My only real complaint is that so many of these templates use the obvious stereotypical combinations (a Get of Fenris Ahroun, a Children of Gaia Philodox, an Uktena theurge). Players will already naturally combine those aspects and come up with obvious character concepts for them. More unusual combinations would be better to showcasing options.

    I've never actually known anyone who actually played any template in any game. It was mostly people reviewing concepts before creating their own character. The only time I ever saw someone play a template is when someone's friend unexpectedly showed up to play so the ST quickly grabbed a book and told them to run a character based on this (with the assumption the player could either continue to play a more personalized version next time, or create their own PC with this one becoming an NPC). So I don't think a possibly disruptive template could doom the game. I doubt any gaming group ever used these templates, certainly not all of them, for a PC pack.

    I will repeat that my earlier post that as characters meant for the Sept of the Green / Rage Across New York setting, there is insufficient local knowledge to allow the ST to actually explain the setting. The ST would need to do some work to integrate the character with the actual setting. Not a dealbreaker, but there really should be a one page note for the ST with advice on how to do so. In RANY only the Sept of the White Water (at Niagara Falls) has Uktena. It's a mixed sept with Fianna (because of that tribe's aid in regaining this caern from the Wyrm) but still ruled by Uktena. As written there is no connection between the Sept of the White Water and the Sept of the Green. That can be justified because in the sourcebook's lore because Frederick Law Olmstead was a Fianna kinfolk, and he was involved in both the creation of Central Park and making Niagara a state park. So there's an obvious connection for the ST to build on. But I think that should be spelled out. In general, there should have been explicit information about the other nearby caerns sending at least some of their cubs to the Sept of the Green for their Rite of Passage. These templates just aren't concepts for a Central Park caern. That's the only way to justify the diversity of these templates and whatever concepts the players will create and putting all of them at the Sept of the Green.

            Curtis Worrell / Lay About (Homid Glass Walker Ragabash)

            So, Curtis is apparently a fairly normal young man, I presume from NYC or the surrounding area (Jersey, Conn., Long Island, etc.). In his own way, he's as much a delinquent as Janet Crowley was. Skipping school, stealing stuff from the local convenience store (I presume shoplifting rather than armed robbery), hacking into his school's computer system, and even the odd drug dealing.
            In the 10th grade, his computer started talking to him. Then a car. And other things. I really do like this detail. Once again, it's one of those reasons I wish we'd got a second edition of this book that went into a lot more detail about how Homids grow up and the weird stuff that often happens as the First Change nears and they eventually enter a whole new world.
            Some heavies in suits and shades rolled up on him on the sidewalk one day. He tried to run, but streets kept dead-ending or being blocked with trash. They caught him and tossed him in the back of a limo with a talking dog.
            While I do appreciate these interesting details about the First Change, I do somewhat lament that we aren't learning more about Curtis as a character. But at the very least, it does help understand the idea of a guy who still feels a little bit like he's in over his head and isn't happy about it.

            At least he likes the easy access to money and influence that comes from being one of the Glass Walkers, as well as the physical power of being a werewolf. But he fully lives up to the Tribe's stereotype of being totally urban oriented and hating the wilderness. He's participating with this pack Rite mainly out of fear of his elders, but will want to bail as soon as the going gets tough.

            There seemed to be a constant theme in 1st ed about Ragabash being cowardly, perhaps almost more so than being irksome troublemakers. If I had to guess, I'd presume that this is somehow based on them having the lowest starting Rage. Again, this was back when Rage was still kind of just a power stat rather than something that had an impact on your personality. But between this guy and the Ragabash template in Ways of the Wolf, it's really kind of noticeable. And, at least personally, mildly annoying. So I'm really glad it's something the game moved away from by the time the Tribebooks started coming out.

            The image is of a young man in slacks and a suit jacket. There's absolutely nothing remarkable about it, and you could just as easily use it as an image for a Vampire template. Not that there's anything wrong with this. I think it fits the character.

            I will mention that I'm not really a fan of the concept of "Lay About". It's similar to how I didn't really care for those Vampire templates who had any concept involving the term "wannabe". It kind of feels like the character is being set up to be thought of as a bit of a loser. I would really prefer something like "Young Hustler", which would kind of refocus him into someone who is out to get ahead rather than just sort of coast.

            For Attributes, Physicals are tertiary with one additional dot from freebies, for 2/3/2. Socials are primary, with 3/4/3. Mental is secondary with 2/2/4. I feel like maybe his Intelligence should be a little higher, but other than that, these are fine.

            With Abilities, Talents are primary with one extra dot from freebies. Expression, Streetwise, and Subterfuge are 3 each, a 2 in Athletics, and 1 each in Brawl, Dodge, and Empathy.
            Skills are secondary with one extra dot (again), with 3 each in Stealth and Firearms, a 2 in Drive, and 1 each in Etiquette and Repair.
            That leaves Knowledge as tertiary, which is perhaps a little unusual for a Homid Glass Walker. A 2 in Computer, and 1 each in Linguistics, Politics, and Science.
            These aren't bad, but I feel like they could use a little tweaking. I'd probably move that one dot in Linguistics to Computers, to better reflect what the write up says about his hacking. I'm not really sure about the Firearms. I can't help but feel like this is one of those "well, he's the Glass Walker, so he's the one who uses a gun" kind of thought processes. His write up doesn't really imply the kind of kid who'd routinely carry a gun, so maybe drop that a level and either use the freebies for something else or bump Repair to 2. Maybe also take one of the Athletics dots and move it to Alertness.

            Glass Walkers got 4 Background dots in 1st ed, and he has an extra from freebies. 2 are in Resources, and 1 each in Pack Totem, Contacts, and Fetish (Harmony Flute). I'm not sure why he needs a Harmony Flute. If it's needed for the story, it feels like Linda would be a more suitable candidate to have such a thing. The rest are fine. If doing this with 2nd ed Rules, I'd probably go with Resources and Contacts at 2 and Pack Totem at 1.

            Rage and Gnosis are both at 2, with Willpower at 4. I've not idea why he and Janet have such low WP compared to the other three characters. He also seems to be missing a freebie point. I'd spend it on WP. I think that dot in Gnosis could be better spent on another Ability dot. Perhaps Dodge or Alertness.

            Overall, this isn't a bad character. I think there's enough to work with here, but it could benefit from a little fine tuning and some moving away from the idea of Ragabash as cowardly.

            So, that was the group from Rite of Passage. Overall, they were fairly solid, with the exception of Whisperer (who suffers from both a lack of character depth and a bad case of not playing well with others). This was the first sourcebook released for the game, so they all lean pretty heavily into their Tribal and Auspice stereotypes. I don't think this is necessarily bad for something this early in the game, especially for new players who might be looking for the easiest character hooks to play. But I think all of these would benefit from some fine tuning based on how the game evolved over the years.

            Next, I'll be doing Bone Gnawers.

                            I think Curtis Worrell is a decent start of a character, but once again the character sheet doesn't seem too match the actual description of the character. No One of Consequence already mentioned the issues. Streetwise, Subterfuge, Firearms, and Stealth all seem too high at 3. Easily justified if the description made mention he built up these skills during his life as a cub though. But is this just a delinquent kid, or is he now more of a street criminal? Mention of "that Lockheed stunt" implies a much higher ability in Computer though. But an emphasis on hacking doesn't mesh with the more street criminal aspects of the character. That just seems to happen because of a general sense of "Glass Walkers should have this stuff". I find the high ability of Expression and a fetish of a Harmony Flute to just be bizarre. The one obviously is for the other, but they don't make sense. 1e corebook was very limited in the types of fetishes and talens given, and none really work for this character. Better to just select another background and other abilities.

                            This is all very "generic Glass Walker". He wears a suit like a businessman. He has an ability set best used by criminals. He has resources for no explainable reason. He has computer skills. It is very unfocused. Building on the most likely portrayal of the character, I'd be more explicit that he's progressing from delinquent kid to minor hood and that his old high school friends are now minor local criminals and he is trying to make use of them. That would solve almost all the problems.

                            Being an urban based character, he can easily slot into the Sept of the Green with little explanation.

                            One thing that some of these templates assume is that Garou begin developing certain Gifts as part of the First Change or early life. Spirit Speech comes obviously to Whisper like Control Simple Machines comes to Curtis. There's no sense that Gifts just don't happen, but require teaching or blessing by a spirit. They just develop those abilities. I don't think that is a problem per se, but it would be better if the game somehow acknowledged that unusual things do happen to Garou undergoing their First Change that are not normally part of the rules. Similarly, 1e WtA often made use of metis cubs spontaneously changing to a homid or lupus form soon after birth to better protect them and seemingly "locking" them in that form for the duration of their cub. I don't think that is a bad idea either. It gives more options to STs and PCs, but I'd prefer such things to be made explicit.

                                        Now I don't mind some depictions of abuse. It makes sense. But there is a big difference between "there is a lot of social stigma and some abuse does happen" versus "all metis are always and constantly abused". Not only does it unnecessarily limit how STs and PCs can portray those characters, it can lead into your earlier point of metis not really having any reason to enjoy being Garou or proud of their people and falling into a cycle where concerns that metis are of the Wyrm become a self-fulfilling prophecy. I recall a few that were much more positive and upbeat, acknowledging the stigma but presenting more optimistic characters.

                                        What is a bit surprising was that the 1e corebook would go out of its way to mention potential good aspects of the metis breed is that the 1e portrayal of the setting included a lot more negative aspects of the Garou. Every tribe had an outright Wyrmish camp, and often had another camp that while not outright Wyrmish should be controversial. The Silver Fangs were obviously failing as providing leadership to the Garou Nation and there were many conspiracies against them. There was a strong generational conflict. In other words, lots of internal enemies when Bill Bridges was the Developer. Those would be downplayed, ignored, or even outright eliminated after Ethan Skemp took over. So in a way, it's a bit surprising if this was the sole element that became worse. That might have been because it's such a prominent and unique aspect of WtA's take on werewolves so writers kept coming back to it.
