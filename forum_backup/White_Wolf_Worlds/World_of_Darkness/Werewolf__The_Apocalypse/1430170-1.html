    If an ST is going to include Hakaken cults in some capacity in her chronicle, I think a brief biography would be in order. The basics of Hakaken's life would be known to some degree, not only among the Shadow Lords but also the Garou Nation. We're given some very basic information (he was a great ahroun, a leader, a member of the Masks camp, and was active for over forty years), but not much else. I think we can determine some other basic information.

    1) He likely postdates the Black Spiral Dancers. Without the example of the White Howlers falling first, I don't see why Hakaken would want to prove he could overcome the Labyrinth.

    2) He seems to postdate the Dark Ages game era. We don't get much information on the Black Spiral Dancers of this era, but no mention is made of Hakaken. While this might not have been intended in the sense a theoretical Dark Ages Book of the Wyrm or Dark Ages tribebook might have listed him, I'm going to use the absence of evidence to be evidence of absence.

    3) I also don't think he's mentioned in any Wild West or Victorian Age sourcebook. However, I personally think this is rather late for an era when Hakaken was still around as a Gaian Garou or yet to be born. Forty years is a fairly long period, and he would have had an immense reputation. So despite the absence of evidence here as well, I'm going to say it makes more sense for him to have already lived and fallen by this time.

    4) In the Chronicle of the Black Labyrinth, Hakaken is mentioned in a document written by the Laird of Demborough in the year of 1923. He is definitely an Incarna by then.

    I'm not sure if anything else can be stated to be definitive. Therefore, it seems Hakaken likely lived sometime between 1300-1800. That allows for a lot of possibilities. Knowing there are many options and none are better or worse than mine, I'll use in my chronicles that Hakaken was active during the time of the Great Turkish War (1683-1699). War raged all over Central Europe and the Balkans during this time (plus a lot of wars before and after), and wars can cover a lot of Garou activities that would normally be Veil breaking. It would provide a lot of opportunity for Hakaken to lead Shadow Lords to strike at their vampire enemies and other foes. A good time to earn a lot of Renown. It's also not a time and place combination covered by existing Shadow Lord history summaries in the tribebooks. So it's a good place to put something that won't seem to contradict any known canon.

    Finally, what type of name is Hakaken? It doesn't sound like a Garou name. And Hakaken seemed to immediately become an Incarna upon dancing the last spiral, so he technically never spent time as a Black Spiral Dancer. Is it the name of some great Wyrm servant he slew, and he became known by that? Is it some kind of occult puzzle or lore discovered in the Labyrnith? Was it an alias he used while in the Masks to generate fear amongst his enemies? I'll probably lean to the latter for now.

                                                            My take was that Hakaken would have to do more than just dance the Black Spiral to become a Wyrmish demigod (Unless he had been secretly corrupt all throughout his career, which strikes me as less dramatically interesting than some sort of Nietzschean descent into the Abyss), hence my suggestion that he would need some sort of major triumph to become more than just another fallen hero (Especially where the Black Spiral Dancers were concerned).

                                                            Quite frankly I’m suggesting that it was the sense of a major Wyrmish threat growing at the same time that mortal society was tearing lumps out of itself (presumably inspiring or reflecting divisions within Garou society that mirrored and quite possibly fuelled divisions & paranoia in mortal society) that drove King Aaron Everstone to seek one of the most powerful relics in Garou legend and much the same fears that moved him to make such poor use of it’s power & prestige by trying to FORCE the British Isles to be a model of Garou civilisation (Resulting in actions so shameful they drove his own House into mass Harano).

                                                            Inspiring such Dread that the hero seeking to end your reign of terror becomes just another villain definitely strikes me as very much in keeping a character apparently characterised as a demigod of psychological warfare.
