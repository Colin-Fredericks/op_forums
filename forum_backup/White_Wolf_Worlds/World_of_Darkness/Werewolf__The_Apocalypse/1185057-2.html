                            Originally posted by Lian View Post
                            If culture can be completely hidden and have no wider human culture to exist off of why are Wendigo "almost extinct" why can't they have large secret bastions all over the damned place?​
                            Well, the simple answer is, that the books say, they are.

                            The longer answer would be, that, yes, there could be, in theory, native Americans who secretly migrated to Africa, maintained secretly their worship of a Ice Spirit and hid that fact from the world.

                            The question is, if you want that in your game world. I wouldn't. I see the drama and appeal of the Setting where the Wendigo as a people were to proud to go into hiding and therefore were murdered. It is closer to real live and therefore their struggle for survival as a culture is more worthwhile as a game topic.

                            If just anybody who feels the Wendigo ideals are cool, or who has only the slightest amount of native american blood but no regard for their culture could actually become a Wendigo, then their struggle, and by extension their existence in the Setting as a whole, would be pointless.


                            Originally posted by Lian View Post
                            And why would they be more prone to do so in Poland? If they are a culture set apart that they do in secret that has real tangible supernatural capacity why would they assimilate.. ever?​
                            The difference between the Wendigo and Uktena, compared to the Western tribes is that they did never go into hiding. Possibly now would be the time, but that is another topic.

                            The other tribes moved into hiding with the rise of the Church and the Inquisition. The older Dark Age Werewolf stated, that the Veil was added to the Litany at this point in time. Even if you don't believe that part, the general population in Europe by that time is estimated to be around 100 million people. So, probably not many of those kinfolk, therefore garou and their kin started hiding their "pagan" practices, or integrated them into Christianity.

                            Uktena, Croatan and Wendigo on the other hand lived openly. Even if you assume that they had the Veil and hid their true selves from any non-kin native Americans, if their were any, they and their kin still weren't hunted for their believes until 500 or so years later. However, even then, the disease from Europe and Africa are the main reason for the death toll among native Americans in the early years. Wendigo, Croatan and Uktena couldn't have hidden their culture to prevent this.

                            So, my take is, that the Wendigo and Uktena never started with hiding their culture from the general public and neither did their kinfolk. There was simply no point to it. They were persecuted and murdered for their skin color anyway. Uktena eventually opted to bring in other minorities (and their occasionally hidden cultural practices), Wendigo opted not to.

                            Originally posted by Lian View Post
                            One would assume that a sufficiently large culture would have an external footprint, you seem to have no issue with completely hidden and secret ones though.

                            I'm still going to question how Silent striders exist in your world at all.​
                            Yes, one would assume that a sufficiently large culture would leave signs of their existence. And possibly spawns of the Wyrm or Hunters try to find werewolf septs by searching for such a footprint.

                            What do you mean by "my world"? What aspect of my statements is it, that you think is flawed?

                            As for Silent Striders, they have a culture, it is based on a nomadic lifestyle, with plenty of kin that has settled, too. Their travels and (ir-)regular contact with others of their culture kept their culture alive and protected them from being assimilated into any other host culture. That is the benefit of traveling. People tend to not impose the same desire for conformity on a guest, then they do on the new neighbor.
