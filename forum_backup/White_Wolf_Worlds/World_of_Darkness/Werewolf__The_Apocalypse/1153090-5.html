                                                            Let's approach it metatextually - Werewolf was created in what seems to be significant part because of outrage at destruction and degradation of the world. One would therefore expect it to concentrate on this planet here that we we live on as the most important part of the setting. Which it does, because no one's going to other planets and no one's finding places to actually escape the dissolution. You stay and fight, because anything else is self-betrayal, and you at least have a chance. Run, and you'll either get eaten, or live to see everything frozen in place, unchanging, as it fades from complete spiritual disconnection. Perhaps even both.

                                                            If we except there's a wider universe and A.) gaia is the universal spirit-god then earth should have no bearing on gaia's well being and matter little to any but the incarnae of earth's natural world. If we except the wider universe and B.) accept that gaia is damaged/dieing to any significant degree do to the damage to earth's nature, then gaia Cannot be a universal god.                                                             Or the existence of life on Earth isn't insignificant, and makes the Earth important accordingly. Mage doesn't enter into it, and what it has to say doesn't matter for anything having to do with Werewolf. Werewolf is about getting angry about having everything taken from you and riding the anger toward change and solving the problems that caused it (ideally). Esoteric field trips to realms that may or may not be divine and holding prisons in deep space that keep Harry, Ron, and Hermione in maximum lockup don't really play well with this setting assumption, so one can assume that they aren't a thing if you're playing Werewolf.

                                                            You can have an intense and far-reaching understanding and still be brought lower than you imagined because of hubris. This is why the world is in the situation it is in, as presented in the text of the game, and metatextually speaking, because the creators presumably didn't want the players handed completely blameless characters with no personal ties to the sordid business of the ages.

                                                            Also, the game isn't too fond of handing out cosmic slaps on the wrist, per the essay in the 2nd edition player's guide. The consequences are more punishment than any deity could ever hope to hand out for the Wars of Rage and other rampages of the past. It would also be terribly counter-productive, given that the main chance for solving the problem without more drastic methods by more powerful beings that might cause even worse problems is for the Garou to get their act together and humble themselves by not exalting the deeds of their ancestors quite so much, particularly where the atrocities that wounded the chances of existence continuing occurred. They have everything else they need but said humility; they are not "primitive" or behind the times; they are acutely aware of what surrounds them.
