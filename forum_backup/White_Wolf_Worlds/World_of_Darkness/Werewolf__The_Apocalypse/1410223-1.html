    Let's start with the Dragon Kings. I must admit I am not fond of that name. The term dragon has a lot of connotations I don't think truly represents these spirits. And I disagree with a lot of the backstory and description created for the Mokole (there is a certain kind of people who thinks weredinosaurs are very cool – I am not one of them). At the very least, I think the Garou would use a different name even if the Mokole and Nagah don't. I personally would like something else that could be a placeholder for a lord of reptiles. Maybe the Great Reptile/Lizard/Serpent. Or the King of Snakes mentioned in the Bastet book. Or perhaps a mythic name like Draco, whose constellation is that of a serpent, but it isn't clear what mythological creature it is supposed to be so we can use that ambiguity. If I had to pick an actual animal, my preference would definitely be Crocodile. I don't know what I'll pick for my own chronicles right now (though leaning to just Crocodile, at least for the purposes of the name the Garou use for it). But for the purposes of this thread, I'll stick to the Dragon Kings for convenience.

    The Dragon Kings are clearly the patrons of the Mokole and Nagah. They are associated with most reptiles, the sun, longevity and memory, and venomous creatures. The Nagah ONLY have associations with spirits of the Dragon Kings because they will keep their secrets, and eschew connections with other spirits. And Mokole generally don't have mammalian totems, but do share many totems with Garou that aren't mammals. Though some spirit totems aligned with Mokole won't have anything to do with Garou. So off the bat, we know we should exclude mammal spirits and the like here. But we could still pick spirits of birds, maybe some insects, and perhaps even sea creatures as well.

    There's actually a very long list of existing Garou totems we can include. They are Chameleon, Adder, Anaconda, Rattlesnake, Cobra, and Crocodile. Mokole book mentions Quetzal as a common totem as well. And the same book mentions a lot of Mokole specific totems like Dragon, Finch, Scarab, Snake Doctor (a dragonfly – the term comes from some American Indians), Whiptail Lizard, the Last Ones, and Horseshoe Crab (though this last one could fit into several broods). This is a fairly substantial list, and most of these spirits don't have obvious homes in other broods. Bastet also mentions Old Snapjaw which is another Crocodile spirit, and Uktena lists both Gila Monster and Dragonfly. Though these two may not be part of Uktena's brood, but totems open to Uktena because of that totem's affinity with the Dragon Kings.

    Collectively, this is a very risky brood for Garou to deal with. Some are available as pack totems, but others will refuse to have anything to do with Garou. I think most of these will not know the Garou Tongue at all, and those that do would only use it with their own pack children. That puts a barrier between them and Garou, and would force Garou to utilize Theurges with Spirit Speech. We could say that even those pack totems might treat their Garou children well, but might be more hostile to other Garou. They are likely to not reveal certain secrets to the Garou at all (not even their pack children)... except maybe to the Uktena because of the nature of that tribal totem. That actually works well with that tribe's mystique. And the Silent Striders may also have some better look because of their association with Crocodile.

        Turtle's brood also offers us some distinction. While most reptiles will go with the Dragon Kings, Turtle probably has the amphibians as well as many amphibious animals that aren't reptiles. Turtle is associated with stability, patience, longevity, and armor. Turtles are also liminal animals that cross the boundary between land and water. Shelled creatures also have a natural home here – so while I can't find printed spirits like armadillo or snail (or any shelled gastropod like abalone and conches), we can include them here. Croatan Song gives us Earth Burrowers, Trout, and Corn Maiden. Trout's fine, and we can probably include some other freshwater fish (though these are also found elsewhere). Earth Burrowers seems to fill some of the category we assigned to Mole and his Tillers of the Soil, but there's no problem in at least some of those spirits being here (or you may want to ditch that group entirely and dump all of them here). Clams – which are shelled, aquatic and which burrow in the ground – would be particularly appropriate. As would the nautilus – another aquatic shelled mollusc. Corn Maiden could be an issue if you want to keep that spirit separate into a Plant based brood, or it could just be a special version of the spirit – there's lot of examples like that in the game.

        Are there other good candidates? I like the idea of putting Beaver here. I really like its totem description, and it's an iconic animal. I think it would have a good home with turtle. It's a semiaquatic animal, and I think it's instinct at building dams fits in well too. Another semiaquatic animal is Otter. Different Otter totems are mentioned in several places in the game (Children of Gaia and Wendigo), but there's no reason another can't be here. Some animal spirits seem to like having multiple brood homes. And as a Garou totem, turtle should have a some more diversity than the Dragon Kings. Salamander (from Rage Across Appalachia) and the various Frog and Toad totems also have a natural home here, but I think at least some of these creatures could be found in other broods as well (I previously mentioned some of these – especially those associated with Healing – might be found among Bear's brood).

        I also think Gull from the Silent Striders tribebook best fits here. While I think many of the totems in the tribebooks best fit as brood members of the tribal incarna, that is not a universal rule. Some spirits are mentioned in multiple places, or obviously belong elsewhere. I would say that while Gull (and many other seabirds) belongs to Turtle, that Gull is also specifically friendly to the Striders as well. And while we have Crab as its own Incarna and brood in out post on sea totems, I don't mind putting hermit crabs here – they aren't true crabs and their taking over of mollusc shells as a portable home puts them closer to Turtle.

        We might also want to include any of the aquatic insects among Turtle – those that live in water for part of their lifecycle, especially as eggs. These include dragonflies (we might say that Uktena and Mokole have access to them as totem as a result of their ties to the Croatan or Turtle), but there are lots of others like water beetles, whirligigs, springtails, etc.. Maybe the more friendly or newer species are part of Turtle, while the less friendly or older species are part of the Dragon Kings. Some might even be part of Uktena's brood, especially if they have myths or legends about them as mysterious or magical creatures. So STs could stick them in several broods depending on what makes sense to them. And there's no reason why these spirits can't be in multiple broods.

        Another group would be a select group of semiaquatic plants like mangroves, pond cypress, salt marsh grasses, reeds, and of course the iconic lilypad. Not all turtles are amphibious. Many tortoises live solely on land and even in the desert (though here they burrow to escape the heat during the day), so perhaps some desert shrubs and such thorny plants could also fit.

        Roleplaying Turtle's brood is a different challenge that the Dragon Kings. Turtle was once a strong ally of the Garou, but the lost of the Croatan seem to have caused it to withdraw favor (from them, but perhaps not from other Breeds like the Mokole). But that can't have applied to most of its brood who seem to still act as pack totems. But Turtle's withdraw nature, disappointment, and possible harano should be indicated somehow through its spirit brood. Perhaps they notice their Incarna protection's absence, or are more likely to criticize their Garou children in their failing to act. This breed likely knows the Garou Tongue, but might refuse to speak it in general as a result of Turtle's disappointment (again, excepting a pack totem's children, and individual Garou who have established a good rapport/chiminage with individual spirits). As an individual Garou builds his specific reputation/affinity with the brood and his reputation spreads to other spirits in the brood, the spirit brood would become more friendly with that specific Garou and begin using the Garou Tongue.

        Like always, I wish I had the creativity to come up with truly novel spirits like some of those found in Axis Mundi to populate the broods, but that's just something I'll need to work on.

        I'm sure if I tried harder, I would find more candidates for reptile based major incarna and broods, but for now I'll stick with two.

        At some point, my intention is to come up a list of major incarna and spirit broods, each with a brief description and roleplay guide. Something that can fit on one typed paged as an easy and quick reference to help me roleplay these spirits. Right now I have lot more than when I first started this thread. So I am hoping some people out there are finding this as useful as I am.

            Now I come to the Bastet looking for information as there clearly needs to be one or more major incarna based on the big cats. However, just like I originally conceived that both Nagah and Mokole would lead me to two different reptile spirit broods, I thought I'd probably have one spirit brood based on each kind of Bastet. However, after reading through the Bastet tribebook and their original description in the Players Guide, I now think one spirit brood covers it.

            The game's lore does a very good job of tying these various cat breeds together with its own set of mythology and legends (I think the Bastet book is by far the best of all breedbooks. It's excellent.). Each breed is supposed to be descended from the same progenitors, and they all have a common mythology. Just need to figure out who the Major Incarna should be. The obvious choices would be either the King of Cats, Mistress of Catkind, or the King of Beasts.

            I finally settled on King of Beasts because it had the highest totem cost in the Jamak section, and the Jamark description was very clear that each level of rank indicated a more powerful spirit type. Level 5 was for “totem spirit”. And King of Beasts was level 6 (indicating, I presume, that it was powerful enough to be a pride/pack totem, not just an individual Bastet). It is clearly a Lion spirit. The description clearly says Garou packs are some allies of the totem, indicating this is the Lion totem in some fashion.

            “Lion” has a lot of baggage in Werewolf. It was its own Garou totem for the White Howlers, and that totem is now said to be part of Griffin's brood. Obviously that spirit can't be a major incarna with its own brood. So regardless whether one says the Garou Lion is actually “cave lion” or merely an “aspect” of Lion but not the true Lion, we need to be careful.

            From a Bastet perspective, I am perfectly fine with calling this Incarna King of Beasts. But I doubt the Garou would do so. Such a name would imply Lion is superior to Wolf, and Garou myth (which may not be shared by other Changing Breeds) puts Wolf as Gaia's favorite and all other spirits owe him reverence (this is why the Garou alone have tribes with other spirits as their patrons). So instead I will use the term Wildcat in my chronicles – the lord of all cats. It is generic enough name and his brood can include all the spirits of the individual cat breeds of the Bastet. The Garou totem “lion” just has a very peculiar history/mythology as originally a spirit under Wildcat/King of Beasts and then a major incarna in its own right as patron of the White Howlers, and now under Griffin. (And somehow there is a separate Lion spirit under Wildcat as well. Either Lion is a big enough concept that it has two aspects, or the Garou Lion is actually a separate spirit of the extinct Cave Lions. Use whichever one you like best.)

            Appropriately, there are not many pack totems obviously associated with Wildcat. I could only find two big cat totems – Panther in Black Furies tribebook and Jaguar in Rage Across Australia. And both of them make references to the War of Rage and troubles between the totem and most Garou. (Jaguar's ban even states its children must fight other Garou.) And an interesting thing about “Panther” is that panther is actually a “legendary creature” like Griffin or Unicorn, not an actual species of big cat! It might be that Furies have access to this totem precisely because it isn't the spirit of a real breed, but a legendary spirit more open to allying with certain Garou despite its allegiance to Wildcat. To these we could add any number of other cat spirits representing the tribes of Bastet and other felines. These are all obvious choices.

            Other spirits easy to slot here would be other feliforms (catlike animals) in Carnivora – hyenas and mongoose are likely well known, but also animals in the viverridae, eupleridae and more rare orders. Incidentally, placing Hyena as one of the spirit brood of Wildcat solves the issue of the Ajaba being involved with the Bastet despite not being a cat, just like sticking Jackal or Thylacine under Wolf can explain how werejackals and weremarsupials are part of the Garou Nation. This is an unexpected benefit of approaching things from a spirit brood perspective.

            Another group of spirits found here are the Jamak – the special spirit allies of the Bastet that are defined as “minor Gafflings of larger totem Incarna” in the Bastet tribebook. This is categorically different than pack totems who are called “jaggling avatars” of the tribal incarna. Jamak include a wide variety of spirits – vultures, elephants, butterflies, and various cat spirits are all included. Although not detailed, the book also includes a list of notable Jamak of snakes, spiders, rhinos, hippos, cranes, hawks, and more. Most have unique “personal names” rather than universal titles (Bonyscrap rather than Vulture), so it may be a clue they are distinct (category of) spirits rather than true universal spirits of those animals. So rather than these spirits being normal types that are part of Wildcat's brood, I think it's best to just say Jamak are their own special category which include many diverse spirits.

            There are lots of African animals not obviously part of an existing brood – elephants, hippos, giraffes antelopes, zebras, rhinos (if they are not part of Unicorn's or Bear's brood), lemurs, aardvarks, water buffalo, monkeys, apes, ostriches, and so on. They have to fit somewhere. But none scream out to me that they belong under Wildcat. Some of them could be. But others might fit under a theoretical brood under Elephant (any number of large animals) or under another totem (like antelopes under Stag). Many of the Bastet breeds are represented in Africa, so this is a disappointment I can't find some to put under Wildcat. But if you have suggestions, let me know.

            Besides cats and related animals, Wildcat has correspondences with curiosity, nine-lives, secrets, hiding, gracefulness, and hunting prowess (usually solitary). In theory, this provides a good basis to assign or create additional spirits of the brood. Unfortunately, I can't think of any right now. The sheer number of other broods have taken up a lot of spirits. I'll have to be much more creative later to come up with them. A legendary spirit like manticore could be one of them. In the meantime, feel free to list your choices for inclusion.

            However, this does lead me to an insight. One reason the Garou are so dominant, perhaps why they won the War of Rage, is the sheer number of spirit allies they have as a result of their tribes. LOTS of spirits are prone to be favorable to them, and this gives them a great advantage that other Changing Breeds don't have.

            Wildcat's brood is fairly powerful, but especially targeted towards helping the Bastet. Less useful to other Fera. They are not favorable towards Garou, but some are willing to help certain packs provided they pay a price. These should not know the Garou Tongue and be initially unfriendly, perhaps even hostile. This is a good roleplaying hook for STs when running spirits here. Many spirit patrons of other Changing Breeds like Boar and Bear don't seem to be hostile to Garou despite what happened to their children. So it's good to have one Gaian brood that is on the hostile end.

                Let's take a moment to consider Axis Mundi which did more than any other book to develop the idea of spirit broods and showcase examples of them. The book makes clear a brood is composed of gafflings, more powerful jagglings, and even lesser incarna. Generic spirits are gafflings while individually named spirits are either jagglings or incarna. As we'll see, there are also groups of jagglings described in the broods.

                Each of the broods were written by different authors, and it shows. Some broods are clearly better written than others.

                I think a comparison to the theoretical broods I constructed would be beneficial. It could point out places where I may have been wrong, and when it seems to fit. I can't do all broods at once, so I'll take them one at a time.

                Chimera is defined simply as a totem of dreams and whatever spirits she wants in her brood, mostly dealing with wisdom and enlightenment. Some Epiphlings are in her brood, but appear only in dreams while other spirits can appear in both dreams and the normal umbra.

                We're only given four spirits – one is a unique jaggling, another lists a jaggling leader and his gaffling servants, one a group of jagglings, and the last gafflings. They're sufficiently different and evocative, but unlikely to come into play unless a chronicle is heavy into dreams. I think they best serve as creative examples of spirits an ST can create herself for broods. Beltsu and Aralin are just OK. The Patchwork Wolf is a good example of a unique jaggling that could play a role in a spirit quest. But I think the Dream Ravens have the most potential to be used in chronicles.

                These are good spirits, but four is just too little. We need some generic dream spirits as well, though I assume we can take some Chimmerlings from the corebook. However, it does a good job letting us know Chimera is primarily a spirit of dreams, with little concrete correspondences in the material world. This is a tight focus, but there's nothing wrong with Chimera being primarily a totem of dreams, one of the Oneiri.

                (Outside of spirit brood issues, the description of the Dream Ravens establishes there in fact very rare “independent” Stargazers (Garou devoted to Chimera) in the Americas along with the Three Brothers. Like a lot of things mentioned once in the game in a minor way, I believe this never got developed. It opens the possibility of members of various tribes being presented in the "wrong" hemisphere, but isolated from their brethren and not realizing the have many more members.)

                            Cockroach recruits spirits for their cunning and survival instincts, and his brood are primarily about protecting Cockroach's influence. That's a very utilitarian perspective and one centered around Cockroach's power – not the defense of Gaia. That's my interpretation of what is written, but it gives good RP guidance to STs. It distinguishes Cockroach from the other Garou totems. All totems want power, but that is generally not their primary motivation. Then again they also aren't a small insect that is despised and hated by everyone.

                            The brood is a mixed brood of naturae and triatic spirits that help Cockroach. Five spirits are described - 2 jagglings and 3 gafflings. However, several other spirits of Cockroch are name dropped which an ST could easily expand on. One annoying thing here is that a lot of nonsense words are used to name these spirits. It gets confusing as you forget whether this spirit is the kilakac'n or mula'krante, and whether it was the kir'kakay who created one of them or vice versa or if that was the mu'jabber instead. The writer would have done better to have a more pronounceable and understandable name. They just get in the way. Some of the name dropped brood spirits – like Spider-Wasps – sound more interesting than what was published. The easiest to remember are the Scab Birds - spirits of birds that live in cities (the scabs per most Garou) like pigeons.

                            It's a diverse brood with utilitarian use. Any city based chronicle should be making use of these spirits. But unfortunately few of these spirits really scream Cockroach to me. Instead of being the definitive spirits of the brood, they seem more like interesting flavor. The Glass Walkers seem to me a strong concept in general, but the specific ideas behind them never seem to quite come together. This brood is the same way. Except for the nonsense names, the brood is not bad. It just seems incomplete and does not cover things it should.

                            (Incidentally, I think the key concept Cockroach needs to give its Garou tribes are the traits of commensalism (animals living in long term symbiosis where one group sgain benefits while the other is neither benefited nor harmed) and hiding among humans. This is critical for any group of Garou who interact with large groups of humans like the Walkers. These traits would have been immediately useful even in the earliest times. Connections to the Weaver would have come later as an emergent property dealing with the strange umbra that developed around human communities.)

                                Falcon is next, and we're told is the totem of “victory, freedom, and insight” and hates the Wyrm most of all totems. The freedom part comes from members of the spirit brood Falcon liberated from the Wyrm, but is worded in such a way that implies Falcon himself was once enslaved. That is probably unintended His brood are strong warriors so the Garou have always respected the spirit and his brood. We're also told that great speed, power, and knowledge are part of his spirit realm, and ties to Helios are mentioned. The main description is disjointed with one thing added after another. It lacks a focus that could better tie everything together. Something like “Falcon is the kind of the birds of prey, and his keen eye is like the sun overlooking the land, searching for his enemy the Wyrm.” Then build on your thesis.

                                We're only given three spirits. They're evocative, but very limited. The Talons of Horus are a tremendous flock of falcon spirits – but they're rarely seen and the Great Flock left for the deep umbra and hasn't come back since the 14th century. The Children of Karnak are spirit guardians of the temples of Helios. Firebirds are order oriented solar spirits of the Deep Umbra that keep Falcon's lore. These are more like story hooks than spirits one would normally interact with.

                                Garou in most chronicles are very unlikely to run into any of them. Again we don't get a real sense of what Falcon's brood comprises of. We're not even told birds of prey are part of his domain! That should be obvious, but still make it explicit. The tribebooks give us a lot of such spirits as totems. And we should have other kinds of spirits appropriate for Falcon's several roles. The three spirits are fine as is, but massively incomplete in the sense of giving tools for an ST to use in an ordinary game. There is a lot of text for these three spirits. Less text and more spirits would have improved things. I mark this as Incomplete.

                                        I like the idea of Swan - it makes a lot of sense for the reasons you've given. Thumbs up.

                                        I also like your addition of "purity" to Falcon. One issue with the Silver Fangs is their need for a minimum amount of Pure Breed, and whether that is merely a cultural thing or part of the nature of its totem. By incorporating spirits associated with "purity" in Falcon's brood, we build a case for that spiritual component.

                                        And it just sparked an idea. Werewolf has a Raven legend where the bird was once white, but his coat turned to black when it got too close to Helios. Falcon is also associated with Helios, but he did not have a similar story of his coat of feathers changing color. Unlike Raven, Falcon did not become "burned" or tarnished, soiled, discolored, etc. In terms of myth, this is like saying Falcon retained his purity - perhaps as a blessing by Helios or reflecting Falcon's essence as a spirit of respect/honor. So that would make a great explanation why Silver Fangs need a minimal amount of Pure Breed to be accepted by Falcon - because he is a symbol of Purity and his children must reflect that. Also creates some interesting conundrums in regards to Metis, but myths need not be consistent or even coherent. And there's certain things an ST might do - for example maybe the only Metis kept by the tribe are those that appear "normal", but any Metis showing too obvious deformity is not inducted into the tribe. There's a lot of ways to handle that, and people's tastes will vary.

                                        It'll be a while before I get to Bunyip and/or Rainbow Serpent (the Bunyip's tribal totem was retroactively changed between Rage Across Australia and W20). But I think that brood will be mostly limited to all the unique animals of the Australian continent. As you say, black swan could fit there or be under Falcon. A lot of this is personal taste as there aren't clear distinctions, and some broods have spirits that are very similar. But there's no problem for broods to be in conflict or rivalrous. In fact, it adds a lot of flavor and helps an ST in roleplaying spirits differently so that the umbra can be more interesting.

                                        I agree that more solar spirits could be tied to Falcon. All three of those listed are technically a spirit associated with Helios.                                             The description of Fenris is a harsh and unforgiving master demanding endless sacrifice, and his brood does the same. None will associate with cowards, but Fenris offers rewards – bounty and wealth – for those with courage.

                                            We're given eight spirits! This is more like it. Unfortunately, the spirits are mostly defined as anything associated with Norse myth. These spirits aren't bad and cover a lot of ground. But it misses the opportunity to think of Fenris as part of a Gaian mythology and not just a Norse one. For example, Fenris is probably best known for biting off the hand of Heimdall for violating an oath, which has the opportunity for some kind of oath keeping spirit. But it's not found here. One thing I do like is the strange inclusion of Cuckoo bird spirits – simply because as their numbers dwindled the spirits choose Fenris as the totem to protect them and in exchange offered to sing his praises. It's still not great as it is a trifle generic distinguished by their obvious Norse inspirations, but this is the first brood we're given that seems “complete” as opposed to just a few spirit ideas.

                                                    Griffin's brood are spirits that are independent and capable, bonded together by mutual respect but never coddled by Griffin. They can be divided in three parts – ancient hunters, modern predators, and the last group a varied collection of spirits that choose to follow Griffin – aspects of him, scavengers that follow the hunt, and extinct species seeking his protection to not fall into Slumber or fading away entirely. It's an alright description, but I would prefer a more “nature red in tooth and claw” focus.

                                                    There are six spirits. Sabertooth is either an incarna or jaggling depending on low powered he's gotten. Same with Lion, the White Howler totem. Simurgh and Sphinx are likely the same, though as a wisdom aspect of the totem. They could be both a lesser incarna and a class of jaggling servants The we have mammoth gafflings and scavengers. The Scavenger spirits are exceedingly generic, and are probably a class of spirits derived from more specific spirits of actual scavenger creatures that now represent the act of scavenging than actual animals. But they make for a nice variety.

                                                    This is another very incomplete brood that raises a lot of questions. At the time, it probably did a lot to expand Griffin as a totem, but the brood itself seems very incomplete. Besides spirits of extinct animals and legendary hybrids similar to Griffin, we don't have much. This seems like another case where the approach was to throw a bunch of separate concepts at the brood instead of picking a distinct focus and tying spirits to that theme.

                                                    I'd emphasize the idea of hunting and “predator kings”. Have spirits that represent the instinct or urge to hunt, a kind of spirit of Rage. Spirits of solitary ambush hunting, or pack tactics. What about non-extinct animals? Are any part of Griffin's brood? Or is he an abstract lord of the hunt? Griffin can work as a more abstract figure dealing with celestial messengers, a protector of designated sacred spaces or taboos, an alternate concept of kingship based on it being a hybrid of eagles and lions – the respective kingly animals of birds and beasts. Those are part of real world lore about griffins. But we need more spirits to fill that out, as well as some kind of thematic unity that is the core of Griffin. We can have ancillary concepts, but it's best is if there is a solid core. Like “guardian of the world as it was before Man.”
