    Artificer Build for Mage

    11-01-2025, 11:13 PM
    I have an idea for a somewhat unconventional Mage character. They’d be closer to an Artificer (from D&D 3.5) than a typical Awakened Mage. Alternately, they could be a pseudo-Technomancer of sorts, depending on whether I go with a “Magick Items” or “Hypertech Devices” approach. The latter would probably attract less Paradox.

    The general gist is to combine the Sanctum Background with the Phylactery Flaw. In this case the Phylactery is either the location itself or something like an altar or statue whose presence makes the area around it into a Sanctum; it’s big and awkward to move, so you can’t run around town with it.

    The Benefit: I never have to deal with Paradox. Outside of my Sanctum, Paradox can affect my items, but not me.

    The Drawback: Loss of flexibility. I can’t just whip out spells whenever and however I please.

    One idea I’d also like to pursue is from M20 The Book of Secret, p. 161:
    “Technocratic Union facilities produce Devices and Principiae without the Willpower or personal Quintessence requirements. Individual technomancers and Technocrats, however, must invest Willpower and personal Quintessence into their Devices.”


    Now, as far I’m concerned, you don’t get to just throw that out there and not include rules for it. The idea seems to be that you substitute enough Quintessence to make up for not sacrificing Willpower points. The questions are then how many and how do you set up your personal facilities to be able to do this? So, I’d be looking to get my workshop up and going with that ability as well. Invest some points in the Node Background and I should have a pretty good magical items production facility.

    Thoughts or suggestions?

      About TU facilities, AFAIK, "just throwing that out there and not include rules for it" it's exactly what they did. You would need a house rule for that.

      The only thing that's explicitly ruled in BoS it's that the facility allows to ignore those requisites, and that this is a property of the facility itself, not the mage (an "individual" Technocrat, in spite of having the correct Focus and affiliation, can't benefit from this advantage).

      I joke that, as far as we know, TU could be kidnapping orphan children to drain their willpower for use in their Devices - not that I think this is what happens, but the point it's that there's no proof, AFAIK, that it does not happen. MECHA worked a bit like that, after all :P

      It could be something akin to Do, something intrinsec to their Focus that requires a special preparation. Or it could be a special "procedure" to create a Device-Factory (probably involving high level spheres) that only the Technocracy knows about, a bit like how Hermetics have their Parma Magica, or how only Technocrats, Etherites and Children of Knowledge know how to make Primium

      Not sure where you've read that having "enough Quintessence" can replace Willpower, but I think such a glaring exception should require an explicit ruling, because a mage can have, individually, lots of Quintessence. If it was something you can have with just a Node, something you can get at char-gen, I think that an explicit ruling it's a must.

      But ST can rule that this is the case, after all, AFAIK, it's not ruled.

          First of all, I don't think this is unconventional. It's basically most Prime Mages with a Sanctum.

          And indeed you get the benefit with Sanctum alone, all that Phylactery does here is to prevent you from even trying Magic on the run. And even then, Phylactery was specifically worded with this option in mind.

          Wonder creation requires both willpower and Quintessence, so I think you misread something here. There's nothing about using Quint to bypass the Will cost.

          The rules for Technocratic facilities ignoring costs aren't given because they're not meant to be replicated. Setting-as-intended, any Mage who finds out how to replicate the single greatest asset of the Union has in their hands a world-changing secret of epic proportions.

          It is, for all intents and purposes, for them and them only. Unless, of course, the ST deliberately chooses to run things differently.

          So there's no rule for it. As Aleph said it's a property of their laboratories and factories, not their Agents, but other than that you'll have to homebrew with no starting point.

          By the way: if anyone knows how the Technocracy gets to run things like that, it's someone extremely high in their hierarchy. Depending on how the current setting is in your chronicle, there may not even be anyone alive that knows how to do it, and the Union be left with whatever production lines survived the Avatar Storm or were retaken since. It may or may not require direct work from an Archmage to make it, or at least someone with Arete/Enlightenment 8+.

                      The rules that are needed are: how does one set up a TU construction facility? The rules for using one are already there: enter the facility, go through the usual Wonder-crafting process, and skip the steps involving Personal Quintessence in order to make rolls and expending Willpower in order to finish it. (Note that it doesn't remove the usual requirement to incorporate Quintessence into the Device being made; only the "surcharge" that's needed to make the rolls.) What's involved in setting it up? That's a proprietary TU secret; which, to me, means that it's something that you need the Primal Utility Sphere variant to do; probably at 5 dots, or more. It's not something that player characters can do; not even Technocratic protagonists.

                      One possibility would be to say that setting up the facility works a bit like the Terranorming system found in the Operative's Dossier, and/or involves incorporating a Node into the process. Given the TU, that Node stands a good chance of being a Primal Venture. Basically, you have to rewrite the local Reality Zone to permit mass production of Wonders.

                                                          Originally posted by Arcanum Scholar View Post
                                                          Yes, I'm well aware they didn't include rules for this. That was my point - they should have.
                                                          I understand your anger, but there are other considerations to make when writing an RPG book.

                                                          Outside any desire to make this NPC-exclusive (which is kinda lame, and isn't what they're doing), there are a few reasons to not make PC-oriented rules in the book:

                                                          1- Sometimes the concept is more important than making rules for how it happens to begin with. Making rules for how to establish a Technocratic Production Line that work as a universal guideline for every Mage game was certainly harder to do than it was worth;

                                                          2- There are things that must be a secret for setting reasons, like it or not. The ST is free to come up how Constructs are made, but almost no character knowing it was the point, or rather a part of it. Not even Union Agents know how those things work, this is setting design. In fact, to be clear, it's as true for NPCs as it is for PCs;

                                                          3- Defining how some things truly work impacts the kind of chronicle you're playing. We're talking something that directly impacts the main conflict of the setting, and that could have a million answers that each have a different effect on the game. Each answer opens the way to tell a different story, including the story of how the PCs will get their hands on this so important secret;

                                                          4- Another important consideration is at which point in the game will be a character able to get something if they have rules to do it. Is it really something a starting character should have been able to build themselves? Should they have it so soon with no strings attached? If not, when should they be able to make or procure such resource by themselves?

                                                          You're also greatly ignoring that the game is built around the conflict between two organizations with their own advantages and disadvantages. The Technocracy is not "the NPCs", and they don't have everything the Traditions have. They have a whole context in which they get access to those production lines, as they're not personal assets, they're special and tightly controlled. You can just make a Technocratic Agent, and in fact you can just have an abandoned production line you managed to make it work without having set it up yourself.

                                                          No, I don't think they should have included those rules. They're unnecessary, and wouldn't easily make for the setting as they planned it. And you may want the setting differently or whatever, there are many ways to implement rules for this that help a particular chronicle, but that's beyond the scope of that book. I would personally prefer rules tailored to the particular game and story to be told rather than a standard for how to build an asset that should be virtually impossible to build.

                                                          Originally posted by Arcanum Scholar View Post
                                                          Well, its role in magic item production is fairly critical, and at mastery level you're rolling in all the Quintessence you could ever ask for and can even nullify Paradox. As the foundational Sphere of magic, I've always prioritized it.
                                                          And all that is completely irrelevant to Affinity Spheres, that's what Heavy Arms is trying to say.

                                                          What an Affinity Sphere does is to slightly cut XP cost. Period. That's all. Your Prime 3 will be exactly the same be it Affinity or not.

                                                          Since all you get out of it is a small XP cost reduction, and your starting points don't cost anything, your best return will come from having as Affinity a Sphere you start at 1.

                                                          Even then, the difference is 3 XP at best. You'll save up 10 if you start at 1, 9 if you start at 2 and 7 if you start at three. Do with it what you will.

                                                          Making your preferred Sphere an Affinity Sphere will only make it marginally faster to increase.
