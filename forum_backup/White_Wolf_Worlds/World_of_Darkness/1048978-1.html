            Originally posted by Aleph View Post
            It's important to understand that Technocracy isn't the scientific community. It's far, far, above the scientific community.

            Haha, I mean in quite a meta sense. I feel like phones only working Because Consensus is a tad... so Sorcery and Magick rely on personal views of the world, and it doesn't devalue them to talk about the nature of belief. A regular Joe with no supernatural powers who invents something or deepens our scientific understanding (Lovelace for example, however as the first computer programmer and the daughter of Lord Byron I doubt you could call her an "average Joe") does so from a place of rational thought and hard work, and there's something... special about that. A Universe which undermines the achievement of basically every great person of science is a bit... less fun almost?

            Like Religion, Science, Mysticism... they're all beautiful for different reasons. It would be nice to have an approach to Mage which made them very different as Paradigms, not the same Paradigm with a different aesthetic and varying Reality Zone Bonuses. Like Religious paradigms don't rely on beliefs being true. Two members of the Celestial Choir could think they were chosen by God to fight for justice and peace and love and truth... and they could end up on opposing sides of a bloody and meaningless conflict which God has nothing to do with. Their belief is very human, very flawed, and its flaws are its greatest assets. The person who thinks God chose them to kill every last Vampire/Demon/Werewolf is beautiful not because they're right, but because of the precise way they are wrong.

            Science, on the other hand is Correct. And... Correct in the most boring way possible. Read the introduction to Lore of the Clans if you want to get an idea of what I'm getting at; Facts vs Truths. Facts are dead, dry, boring things you can pin down. Truth is fluid and beautiful and dangerous and terrible. It might not be a fact that the Prince embraces and Diablerises humans on a weekly basis, but for the Neonates who tear down his haven and tear him to shreds it is certainly True in the moment. That, in my opinion, should be the underpinning difference between Scientific Paradigms and other Paradigms. You can explore why truth matters, and the value in things which aren't true but are useful (Parables for example aren't true, they make a point about the nature of the world however).

            "We're right and we have proof" is the cry of any Technocrat who happens to speak to a member of the Traditions... and the scary thing is that the Traditions don't care, nor should they. The scary thing is that the people who are technically right are BY FAR the most wrong. Such a system would focus a bit more on why being Rational and Correct aren't always good things, why humanity and warmth and wonder and spiritual belief matter even in the face of Cold Science. Imagine Dawkins as a Technocrat. Correct on Science, wrong on practically EVERYTHING else. The Orphan kid asking the Earth Mother to heal some injured protesters who are fighting to protect their native land is completely wrong about the true nature of reality, but that doesn't matter, nor should it. What matters is that she has power and she uses it for something that is Right to her. The Progenitor who sees such a scene and does nothing (why would they?) can go home and comfort themselves about how rational they are, but the overarching narrative I would hope to engender is that being the Winning Side in one sense can leave you the loser in every other.

                    I, personally, like to believe that you can invent/discover new stuff in Mage without being Awakened. That Technocracy (or Mages, for what matters) don't rule the world to such a deep extent.

                    I, however, don't explain this as "Science it's 100% true", but rather as normal people working within the constraints of a Paradigm, expanding said Paradigm and thus slowly shaping the world. What normal people do it's also magic, it's just that it's paved within the limitations of what's already believed to be possible or plausible. Anyone that reads sci-fi knows that this it's a big leeway.

                    Sorcerers can do stuff that isn't quite accepted by the Consensus by exploiting fringe aspects of the Consensus. They can go further than any normal person with a lot of willpower and dedication (note that I don't put supernaturals in this same bag).

                    Mages think outside the box, they don't need to respect the Consensus. Ironically, I see Mages as closer that normal people to the true rules of Reality, the Universal Constants set there by the Pures and other primeval beings.
                    Opening their minds to the full potential of humans, Mages understand more clearly what's truly subjective and what isn't (No one can radically change Gravity without a lot of effort, so it must be real, at least to some extent. Germs?, those may be relative, perhaps they're bad spirits that got into your body). They don't know everything instantly, and are limited to their preconceptions, but they can probe further than anyone else, and whatever they can't change, it's probably there to stay.

                    This way I recognize the labor of the Technocracy pushing their Paradigm and manipulating the advancement of science according to the Time Table, but also recognize that a mundane scientist may gave them a surprise once in a blue moon.

                    Also, I mantain that all Paradigms are equally valid AND invalid. Reality exists, but only as underlying principles that sometimes agree with science, and sometimes resist it's methods but are more agreeable to different approaches to knowledge

                    Of course, this it's just an idea. Plenty of stuff in the bibliography to go for a "harder" or "softer" reality.
