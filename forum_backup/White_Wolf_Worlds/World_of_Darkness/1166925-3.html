    Personally, no. I never liked the ascension war except as a setting element that informs some of the choices of character. I prefer Revised when the Ascension War was done and nobody cared anymore about the consensus.
    I like Revised more exactly because the Masters were cut out from the Earth: this gives the newbie players more weight and responsibilities on the events taking place, removing at the same time the hassle of having elders born in the 15th century shaping your life from Doissetep. Even if I never like the new Discorporation rule, even that helps keeping the plot on Earth rather than removing the mages from the world altogether.

    Of course, having rules and options to choose which campaign to run is better than a straight line, but it wasn't honestly that hard to cut those extras off Revised if you didn't like them.


    I dislike the the disparates because on one hand the way are presented as the "is the traditions guys, just not problematic".
    Are they? I think they're just poorly written.
    The Sisters of Hippolita are almost a joke: the same philosophy of the Verbena and the same structure of the Black Furies (but without their background): it seems they were only created to mock Feminism. The Templars do the same thing of the Choristers, just with an extra spin on fanaticism... and by the way, how the hell can they get along with the Hippolitai, or Voodoo practictioner?
    The Taftani seem to have lost their whole point of existence with the passage into M20: they were a group of mages iron-bent on fighting the Consensus through public Vulgar magick displays and they despised the Traditions for submitting to the Technocracy through the use of coincidental magic; now they're using coincidental effects, technomagic and still despising the Traditions enough to not join them? What the hell?
    The Hollow Ones murdered hundreds of Tradition mages with their betrayal of Concordia, so much for not causing problems; I'm surprised they're still around, they probably won't for much longer if the Euthanatoi manage to get back in shape. Also, isn't Goth Magick a bit out of fashion now? And the Orphans, yeah, they shouldn't really mingle with the Disparates any more than with theTraditions.

    My overall idea about the Disparate is that either they're poor copies of the Traditions or mage societies that were downgraded to make them fit with the others. Really, I may use a couple of them but as an alliance they're horrible.


    Let's go to Greek mytology. Try to make a "Circe" like character, she was a "wizard" type after all: You need a Master of Life and Paradox may not like it. As a play stile, it sucks, Mage rewards being subtle. Thus, Mage it's bad at playing greek monsters too.
    Every fantasy wizard topos needs to change a bit to fit Mage, though, because the Paradox is neither in the Odyssey (and it wouldn't be, morphing people was coincidental back then) nor any other fantasy book. Also it's not like you can't mass morph people, you just need to spend some extra effort and make it vulgar... but it's not like a diff 8 Life effect is going to be an impossible task.

    Btw, Circe wasn't really a "monster"... her story is a bit more than turning people in pigs. =P

                                But the execution is abyssmal and, ultimately, unworkable. Even taking into account the "This is how these groups CAN work together. See, here is the "justice system" that irons all that out" from the Book of Secrets, it's just not going to fly based on how groups of people can, do and have worked together, or not, historically and prehistorically speaking. Some of these groups WILL NOT play well with each other (e.g. the Hippolytans, the Templars, the Kopa Loei, the Taftani) without compromising themselves to the point that the thing which defines them (for the most part, that self-same NOT-compromising to the eccumenicism of the Traditions) will be lost. The various groups that came to Convocation had a pressing reason to TRY to work together. More than one, even. The hegemony of the Order of Reason via the Age of Exploration/Colonialism was coming (and pretty much all the groups had diviners and prognosticators of some point. That's a common "thing" in the vast majority of cultures' mysticism). Plus there was an ongoing change in the way the fundamental base code of "How Magic Works" that had reached a tipping point. The only real reasons for the Disparates to come together that seems to be presented in M20 is "The Nephandi are EVERYWHERE and we're the only uncompromised" with a tinge, a very slight tinge and only with a few of the groups, that together is stronger/safer/easier to hide that apart. Plus some centuries old grousing about how things went down with the rise of the Traditions and the Order (and the ideological combination of the two... thanks Batini) and a dash of willful ignorance.

                                They may be the Traditions without all the baggage, but, boy, do they carry a TON of baggage of their own and it's certainly not really dealt with at all. Whatever various "original sins" and past bad acts the Tradtitions are lugging around, the Disparates are just as encumbered with modern issues. Specifically rabid ethnocentrism and it's cousin, ideological purity.

                                Maybe if the whole thing was a web manipulated by the Batini who were approaching each group as a sort of anti-Nephandi and using them as operational fronts, I might be able to buy it better. "No the Templars and the Sisters don't have to work together. The Templars are great at X. The Sisters are great at Y. That's how we use them and that's how they should be used." Perhaps with some "greater plan" than just rooting out Nephandi (which is, admittedly, effectively one of the Seven Pillars of Batini-dom) they might be trying to manipulate them enough that they could work together some day. (I was toying with the idea that some of the groups (Ngoma?) might be "in on it" but, after thinking about it, I doubt it. The Subtle Ones would be being less than subtle. working against their strengths and throwing a show into the works.)
