        Gypsies:

        I have to start with the worst offender of the bunch and get it out of the way. In many ways, this is the precursor to Sorcerer, with most Paths available for starting PCs, most of which have been renamed and re-inserted into new material. Some as Sorcery Paths. (Fortune, Alchemy, Summoning, etc.) Some as Psychic abilities (Psychic Hypnosis) The main difference is that the powers were either reflexive (like Fortitude) or the roll was Attribute + Power rating (rather than ability). This might actually help with rolls for other Paths as well, as a simplification. IE, not having to figure out if a certain ability really fits the Paradigm.

        The exceptions:
        Dance of the Knives. AKA, Celerity light. Basically, the first dot does nothing but prep for the second, and the skill is only good on knife attacks, dodging, punches, or kicks. No fast movement from point B to point B. Nothing unbalanced, nothing really great though. The effect could probably be better served with an Alchemy potion, though not entirely innapproriate for a martial arts based character or a contestant on Hell's Kitchen.

        Other items:

        Elemental Affinities: These actually seem pretty neat, like a strong merit, which at 7 points, they pretty much are.

        Magical items that could just be picked up and used by Sorcerers as is or with cosmetic alterations.

        When utilizing Mage the Ascenscion, there was a rule sidebar indicating that Awakened characters could use these abilities, and were subject to Paradox as if the abilities were Coincidental. Not a bad basis for Mages that know Sorcery Paths. (Or Sorcerers that Awaken in game.)

        And for Vampire, it states that during the Embrace, a Vampires loose access to these abilities, unless they drink from someone that currently has them, and it lasts until they spend that specific blood point. (Blood being used in the order it was taken.)

        The concept of a magical bloodline that creeps out non-magic folk has it's merits (and is, in fact, a major point for WtA), but Blood Purity would have to be reworked significantly to not hobble a character

        Families: Oh look, more powers. No, don't look at the backgrounds. Cringeworthy.

        Lupine Family; Spirit of the Wolf: Parts of this are covered by Spirit Chasing (if you choose a matching predatory animal), except the level that allows the Kin to learn a level 1 or 2 Tribe or Homid Gift, and that isn't loosing much since Kin CAN learn level 1 Gifts.

        Ravnos: Meh

        Urmen; Faerie Threads: One special power related to Fae (keeping in mind, ones listed are really early edition) that can be used (path level) times per story. I don't really know enough about Changeling to make a good comparison.

        Phuri Dae; Patteran: Seems like a random assortment of other powers. Some like Clairvoyance, Precognition, and Astral Travel

        Tsurara; Blood Sense: Early levels like Scent of True Form or 'the Sight'. Starts with 'Sense eachother, sense almost like us (Mages), sense others (vampires, werewolves), then goes into tracking and ambush. Good power for magical hunters like Shih or Inquisition.

                    Originally posted by EvilTyger View Post
                    The concept of a magical bloodline that creeps out non-magic folk has it's merits (and is, in fact, a major point for WtA), but Blood Purity would have to be reworked significantly to not hobble a character
                    Were it me, I would use a kind of "Magical Blood" merit, to denote a person having potent mystical heritage. This doesn't by any means need to be limited to Romani, but can be applied broadly to any sort of imaginable legacy a character could carry in their veins. Descendants of great magicians, saints, angels or devils, dragons, some sort of blessed peoples (like a priestly caste). Whatever it is, the character inherits potency, that scales with how strong that inheritance is.

                    Mechanically, I'm imagining that in Sorcerers or Psychics, it adds dice to Attribute + Ability rolls when casting Effects, either one die per one point of merit, or on die per two points of merit. If the character becomes a Mage, the bonus should definitely not scale 1 to 1, since it's an easy way for players to game the system into doubling or tripling their Arete dice pool. Other options would be to make the merit lower casting difficulties, or waive the Willpower costs of effects cast by a variable number of points per day.

                    The advantage here, of course, being that it makes having Blood Purity a nice bonus, but not essential to running a character at all.

                                    One problem I see with Sorcerers standing on their own it's the lack of a purpose.

                                    cWoD it's characterized for giving it's splats a profound background that helps build the story. When you play Vampire, you know that there's this world of intrigue and vendetta in which your monster thrives, Werewolf makes clear your role in Gaia's plan, in Mage you know that you fight for reality on one faction.
                                    Sorcerer was created as a supplement, and it shows it. Sorcerers can be usefull alies and good enemy goons, but they aren't given a lot to do on their own. Sorcerer expands a little on this by giving them some factions (that I found to be mostly uninspired imitations of the Traditions, with one or two exeptions), but it isn't much.

                                    In a way, I preffer Sorcerers when they're part of the Traditions/Conventions, because then you know what they're up to: They will want to further the goals of their group, which authomatically makes them antagonists of the other sides of the Ascencion War.
                                    What does Sorcerers want to do? They want to learn cool powers, of course, but everyone in the WoD can say that that. I think we need to give them something more, something to do when they aren't helping or antagonizing more interesting and conflicted groups of supers.

                                    My idea:

                                    Let's take a rule from Mage: The Awakening. The rule that gives you extra experience points each time you discover something supernatural, that can be used to buy magic. I propose to give this capability to Sorceres. Awakening mages have much in common with sorcerers, in that they tap on power that exists in the world, I think that their shtick of "researching the weird and magical" could be just what Sorcerers need to "do stuff". Now you have a real mechanical motivation to go out there and get exposed to stories, if you want to learn.

                                    I also propose to reflect in what are the Mythic Treads that give sorcerers their powers. If it's true than Sorcerers draw their power by tapping on existing hidden laws of the world, not their belief, then this power has to be something that can be researched or understood. The way it's now, it's just background noise to grant sorcerers the power to do whatever they want.

                                    Now, we don't want here to make the Threads to be part of the Consensus that it's ingrained into the universal subconscious, because that would make them part of Mage.

                                    Related to the rule I proposed: Mythic Threads could be a general name that englobes supernatural forces that are active in the world. Vampiric sorcery employs some, Garou rituals others, and there are countless more (everything from magical bloodlines to ancient spirits). Sorcerers, in turn, are mortals who have learned, through hard training, to tap on these existing wheels of elderich power and general weirdnes to build their own brand of power, the Paths.
                                    Sorcerers would be a special kind of Hunter that search the paranormal expecting to gain power or understanding from it. "The paranormal" aren't just the supernatural splats, but also relics of every kind, other Sorcerers who practice yet unknown Paths (or their doings), magical lore, alien sights, even weird reality zones. Dependant on how hard you want the concept of 'Practice' to be, there may be limitations on what you can use to gain the extra xp (or how).

                                    The idea would be that, say, the Star Council trully got their Paths thanks to what they perceived as alien techology. They aren't a bunch of weirdoes that wrongly believe that their mojo comes from alien techology, when in truth it's an undefined hidden law manipulated by "totally not belief". I think that "Mythic Threads" should mean something.

                                                System:

                                                Roll Path Rating + Something (an Attribute or Ability), Difficulty is 3 + Level of Highest Aspect. Dice pools are always effected by Wound Penalties (which can be ignored for a turn with Willpower, remember).

                                                [I'm borrowing from DA:F with that first bit, always liked the Dominion + Something thing] [I like the idea of Sorcerers being wound-adverse]

                                                Casting Time: 1 turn for Spells, 15 minutes per level of Ritual. Spells can be made extended rolls. Some Paths demand extended rolls, and in some Paths the "Spells" can last hours as a balancing factor (changing the weather of a whole country can't take less than a few hours).

                                                [Casting taking 5 turns for level 5 was dumb. Let us have fun]

                                                Cost: All Sorcerers have a Mana pool. This starts off at 10 and is steadily increased as the Sorcerer masters the magical arts (when upgrading a Path, learning a Ritual or increasing an Attribute or Ability linked to their style of Sorcery, a Sorcerer can spend an extra 3 EXP to increase their Mana pool by 1 as well, refining their art while gaining in reserve magical power); the Mana pool maxes out at 20 (however those with mastery of the Path of Mana Manipulation can increase the limit to 30). Mana is spent to power a Spell or Ritual; failure to do so increases the Difficulty by 2 (a sacrifice Sorcerers will often make when they must, or feel the difficulty hike is no problem). Spending additional Mana reduces the difficulty of a Spell or Ritual by 1 per point, but only 3 points of Mana can be spent by a Sorcerer on a single spell or ritual. If a Group of Sorcerers work together on a Spell or Ritual, the difficulty reduction from Mana expenditure only applies to one of them, and all of them must spend it as though they were casting alone. Mana is restored over time naturally, at a rate of 3 dots per day. Mana Manipulation can effect this positively or negatively.

                                                [I think that having something akin to a Blood Pool is nice, it's simple and fun. Maybe my system is a bit odd here? Happy to change it]

                                                Paths: Every Path needs to have Aspects, and the system needs to be a totally unified one. Aspects are typically Duration, Potency, Area of Effect or Number of Targets, some Paths have more Aspects. In each Path the user favours 1 Aspect; the favoured Aspect is given a free pass if it's not the Difficulty-determining Aspect. If it is the difficulty-determining aspect, the difficulty is reduced by 1. So if Susan is an expert in making Potions with the Shelf Life Aspect, and attempts to make a potion which stays good for a year (level 5), and the potency and duration are lower than or equal to 5, she rolls at difficulty 7. If Susan is making a Potency 5 potion and only wants it to stay good for three days (let's say that's level 2) since she'll need it in 2 days time, she doesn't need to get a success for Shelf Life, it just happens since she's adept at this.

                                                [Seriously, half the Sorcerer paths are fluffy bullshit. We have no idea what Path of Shadows does. None. Give them all Aspects and Rules]

                                                EXP: Raising a Path's level is 5 x Current Level. A Free Ritual is granted with each level of a Path, and new Rituals can be learnt at a cost of their Level. If a Path is challenging to a Sorcerer since they have a rating of 1 in a closely associated Attribute, or 0 in a closely associated Ability, the cost of raising it will be increased to 7x Current Level until they rectify this. Beginning a new Path costs 10 EXP and always requires a teacher of some kind.

                                                [Yes, Sorcerers don't need to be everyone's bitch]

                                                Successes are split between Aspects. The Aspect chosen to reflect the difficulty is fulfilled by 1 success, but for every other Aspect which has been Named an additional success must be added. Potency 5, Duration 1 and Area 1 needs 1 success at difficulty 8, Potency 5, Duration 5 and Area 5 needs 3 successes at difficulty 8.

                                                [I'm pretty sure this is how it works right now]

                                                EDIT: Forgot about Tools. Every Sorcerer has a Tool for each Path (multiple Paths can have the same tool). Working without a Tool forces a +1 difficulty modifier on magic for the relevant Paths.

                                                        You know how Vampire's mortals have Humanity, while its ghouls also have Vitae, Clan associations, and limited access to Disciplines, making them vampire-like? So too with Kinain vis-a-vis changelings. For the purpose of this thread, here's what matters: Kinain get up to five Fae Gifts (roughly analogous to the benefits granted by a changeling's Kith) based on the strength of their faerie blood, and can learn changeling Arts if they have changeling mentors. The Cantrips of Changeling's Arts are as close as anyone in the gameline gets to sorcery; and frankly, they're more similar to Mage Spheres than they are to Sorcerer Paths. This will be especially true once C20 is out, and the notion of Unleashing the Arts joins the game.

                                                        I think you'd be better off treating Fae heritage as a Mythic Thread as described in my earlier post, providing a framework for sorcerers with fae-inspired Practices and Instruments for their Paths. Perhaps the Mage merit that says the mage has Fae blood could be adapted (or completely rewritten) for use by sorcerers; or perhaps sorcerers who practice Fae magic simply use Glamour instead of Mana much like your suggestion for blood sorcerers using Vitae instead of Mana. I don't know that a Merit or Flaw would be justified.
