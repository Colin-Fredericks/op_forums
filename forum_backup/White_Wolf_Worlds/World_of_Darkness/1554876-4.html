            Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
            But I'm not much versed into Baali lore, why the ones in the Clanbook fight infernalists?
            The short answer is internal schisms on how to use infernalism. Nergal and his line wanted to use it to awaken Namtaru and thus spread the use as much as possible, Moloch and his line would rather use it to keep the Children asleep and keep it under wraps. Both lines thought using it to enslave yourself to demons was pretty stupid. Dark Ages Baali thought themselves above infernal powers, in contrast to their modern kin. It's pretty fun contrasting the Dark Ages version of Daimonion and the modern version with that in mind.

                To answer your first question, though, see TC4, p. 44:

                Originally posted by Transylvania Chronicles: The Dragon Ascendant, p. 44
                In the wake of the Anarch Revolt and the Council of Thorns, Tremere rouses from torpor long enough to assist in the ritual that curses the Assamites. He also magically brands those Tremere who do not follow the clan’s dictates with a telltale sigil that only true Tremere can see.
                A strict reading of this implies he did both at or near the same time (so probably 1496). "He also..." does this, rather than "He later" does this. It's possible he stayed awake for at least 4 years, though, and did the Rite of the Betrayers in 1500 or thereafter, making the 16th century date given elsewhere accurate. This is a bit clumsy, but technically keeps both statements true. So, it's up to you whether you list this as one date of "~1500" or two dates of "

                That Tremere performed the curse himself also aligns somewhat with the original Clanbook: Tremere, p. 20:

                Originally posted by Clanbook: Tremere, p. 20
                By the mid-18th century, it was noticed that a number of younger Tremere had inexplicably gone missing. Only through diligent effort was it learned that Goratrix, hidden deep in the mountains of Eastern Europe, had joined with the Sabbat and was perhaps responsible for these defections. He is the head of his own covenant, House Goratrix, and his loyal antitribu followers now act as counsels and advisors to Sabbat leaders in-both the Old and New Worlds. Since the discovery of House Goratrix, the Tremere Councilors, led by Tremere himself, have branded the followers of Goratrix with a sigil marked on their foreheads, readily visible to any Tremere magus,
                A strict reading here implies it happened in or after the "mid-18th century", since it says "Since the discovery of House Goratrix" (i.e., afterwards). This fits with a 1750 date. It also says Tremere led the 18th-century rite. Maybe he woke up again?

                The mid-18th century date also aligns with the date given in TC3, p. 17:

                Originally posted by Transylvania Chronicles III: Ill Omens, p. 17
                Nobody knows what sect you are part of just by looking at you (the Tremere antitribu won’t have to deal with that problem until the mid-1700s), and nobody is forcing you to tell them.
                And again on p. 103:

                Originally posted by Transylvania Chronicles III: Ill Omens, p. 103
                1750: The Tremere, after three centuries of compiling a list of suspected traitors to the clan, ritually curse the antitribu with a symbol that all Tremere can see.
                You've already seen this date, but note that this says the ritual was performed "after three centuries of compiling a list", suggesting they knew about the defections from around 1450 (likely the 1465 date when the antitribu joined the anarchs). Again, this all fits with the 1750 date.

                Yet, as you rightly point out in your timeline, TC2, p. 112 puts the Rite of the Betrayers in the 16th century:

                Originally posted by Transylvania Chronicles II: Son of the Dragon, p. 112
                A rogue branch of the Tremere within the anarch ranks make its presence known. Descendants from Goratrix, these rogues seek protection from the Tremere within the ranks of the anarchs. In the 16th century, when the Sabbat forms, the Tremere antitribu are cursed (much like their sire Goratrix) with the mark of the traitor magically branded on their foreheads. Though invisible to normal sight, the mark is instantly noticeable by any non-Sabbat Tremere.
                The Sabbat usually isn't considered officially "formed" until the mid-16th century by most sources, which complicates the picture. But if we assume they're merely referring to the time when a bunch of anarchs refused to kowtow to the Camarilla and kept on going, then the early 16th century is still plausible. Either way, it potentially creates a third date for the curse.

                Note the second bit of bolded text, though. Why say this if Goratrix was cursed at the same time? This implies he was cursed earlier. The best way I've found to reconcile the dates is to assume Goratrix (and maybe a handful of others) was cursed at the same time as the Assamites, in 1496, by Tremere himself. Over the next few decades, those in-the-know within Clan Tremere use the same (or a similar) ritual to curse any other known defectors, one by one. These are probably all descendants of Goratrix, since these are the easiest "missing" Cainites to identify.

                But in 1750, the clan learns that the defectors aren't just small pockets of resistance, or even only direct childer of Goratrix, but a wide network calling itself "House Goratrix". So they do the only rational thing for such a bunch of control freaks: they alter the curse they'd been using to target specific defectors (or replace it) to instead target any Tremere who partakes of the Vaulderie. Maybe Tremere woke up for this ritual, too, or maybe it was just Etrius. They probably only acted because this was the point at which the rest of the clan learned of the antitribu, and they had to make a big show of smacking them down.

                This is somewhat supported by the Players Guide to the Sabbat (p. 81) and the previously mentioned text in the first clanbook, which both suggest the 18th century was when the main clan, as a whole, learned about the antitribu (the Inner Council and select others probably knew the whole time).

                PGttS technically suggests the 18th century is when the Tremere antitribu first joined the Sabbat, not when they were discovered, but that appears to have been retconned with TC2. Best guess? That's just when one particularly prominent wave of defectors joined in the New World, the others mostly being defectors from the Old World who joined the Sabbat during the 14th and 15th centuries. It was this second, probably larger, defection, occurring during the Camarilla–Sabbat war, that tipped off the "grunts" of Clan Tremere and therefore required immediate action. They didn't have the time or energy to fight their own clanmates as well as the rest of the Sabbat, so tried to put a stop to it once and for all.

                            A couple more additions:
                            • Caine's Chosen (p. 9) puts the start of the First Sabbat Civil War as 1767, with its peak in 1785. This is backed up by MCbN (p. 21). MCbN says it started winding down in 1793 ("one pack at a time"), so it's a low ramping down until the Purchase Pact.
                            • GC3 (p.13) suggests it started with Shay's Rebellion in 1786. The wording is vague enough that it could just be when the civil war spread from the Americas to Europe. The same page also implies there is a Sabbat Civil War still raging by 1848, but I think this is the Second Sabbat Civil War (see further down). 2e books sometimes behave as if there was one Sabbat Civil War, even when they've said there were multiple in the same book.
                            • Caine's Chosen (p. 18) says Dastur Anosh went to America to help stop the war and then disappeared during it. V20 CotR (p. 64) has him come to America much earlier and disappear in 1766. The First Sabbat Civil War is said to have broken out "shortly thereafter", which seems to confirm 1767.
                            • Clanbook: Lasombra Revised (p. 35) suggests it started in the late 1790s, which is ludicrous, much like the other dates in the book.
                            • Caine's Chosen (p. 20) says the Sabbat Inquisition complained about the Black Hand before the First Civil War "started in earnest", but attributes this organisation to Monçada, Nahir, and others. This is interesting because, as you note, CotN says Black Wallace joins the Inquisition in 1780—well before its official founding in 1804. But the same page of Caine's Chosen also says no Inquisitors set foot in the Americas until 1808, which implies there was a Sabbat Inquisition in Europe and that the 1804 founding was only for the American branch. Presumably they went to Europe to train up between 1804 and 1808, and then arrived in 1808 in Peru with Charles Delmare per Caine's Chosen.
                            • GC3 has a whole adventure based in 1848, and the Sabbat internecine squabbling is a big part of that. So, the Second Sabbat Civil War has either already started or is beginning to start.
                            • MCbN (p. 24) puts the first "rumblings" of the Second Sabbat Civil War around the time of Santa Anna's capture (22 April 1836) and his signing of the Treaties of Velasco on (14 May 1836). It states the Lasombra and Tzimisce began "drawing lines in the sand" around the time of the Mexico Cessation (2 February 1848), which fits GC3. In GC3 (pp. 29, 80–82), Doctor Streck leads the attacks on Paris and London in 1848 (Lady Vadislava having attacked London Toreador earlier). This was part of a campaign that also targeted Vienna, Milan, and Berlin, but which was unpopular among Lady Astor of London, who was set to become archbishop but wanted to take things much slower. This establishes the intense rivalries between elders prior to the over violence of the Second Sabbat Civil War.
                            • MCbN (still p. 24) also states that Gorchist pulls the fighting back from the brink several times, with the aid of the Black Hand, but keeps referring to it as a localised conflict. So arguably, this is still only the precursor to the war proper.
                            • The same page gives us another fixed date: Gorchist is assassinated on the eve of the French invasion of Mexico City, which dates it to 9 June 1863. After this, the war begins "in earnest".
                            • Montreal by Night (p. 84) says the Conference of Caine in 1910 was a major step towards ending the Second Sabbat Civil War, so this fits. That conference took part at the same time as the Eucharistic Conference (7–11 September 1910). The book says the Litany is reopened "every summer" as a result, but apparently Canada's summer does include early September (June Solstice to the September Equinox), so that also works out as a regular anniversary event.
                            • The sources all agree the Second Civil War ended in 1933 with the signing of the revised Core of Milan, helmed by Galbraith per MCbN (26–27), but MCbN (p. 6) makes a goof and suggests Galbraith claimed the regency at the end of the Third Sabbat Civil War, which is clearly a mistake.
                            • Of course, the revised Code of Milan still carries Gorchist's name, despite him having met Final Death 70 years prior. This is clearly an error, but one that's sort of been retconned by saying the Code published in 1933 was only a revised version. This implies Gorchist signed the original version, or perhaps drafted the revised version before he died, but only Galbraith got it over the line. A final possibility is that it invokes Gorchist's name as the last legitimate regent of the sect, so it's like a blessing ("In the name of...", etc.).
                            So, I think we can safely say the dates are:
                            • First Sabbat Civil War: 1767 for the start (1785/1786 for the peak); 1803 for the end.
                            • Second Sabbat Civil War: Between 1836 and 1848 for the early conflicts (which may or may not count as part of the war); 9 June 1863 for the "official" start date; 1910 for when things start to calm down; 1933 for the end.
                            • Third Sabbat Civil War: Latter half of 1957.

                                    Another one:
                                    • ~1550: Lucita meets Lambach Ruthven in Lenapehoking (modern day New York City) before the Code of Milan went out and learns of the tale in which the Tzimisce founder survived his diablerie. [LT2-S, pg. 126]
                                    This can't be accurate. If you turn the next page, if says:
                                    • "I laughed at him, in fact, and asked if he expected the founder to show up on the next train, since we were visiting in one of the subway tunnels." [LT2-S, Pg. 126]
                                    But the NYC subway wasn't opened to the public until 27 October 1904. Even if we factor in the four years required to build it, she can't have met Lambach earlier than 1900. Clearly, when she says she "met him just once, in New York City, shortly before the original Code of Milan went out", she must be talking about the revised Code of Milan, which was drafted in NYC. This puts their meeting somewhere between 1904 and 1933.

                                    The lore of The Code of Milan is another of those tangled messes. According to the Players Guide to the Sabbat (p. 97), the Code was penned "during the great Camarilla–Sabbat Jyhads of the early years", when "a civil war split the Sabbat" (presumably the First Sabbat Civil War, but it could potentially just be a smaller precursor). It was signed in Milan in order to end the conflict, meaning it occupies the same narrative space as the Purchase Pact (which first appeared in GttS, AFAICS). Frankly, adding the Purchase Pact is probably the thing that messed it up the most. It also says those on the Path of Honourable Accord follow a version of this Code that was massively expanded between the First and Second Sabbat Civil Wars, and that version is likely no longer the same Code people mean when they talk about the original.

                                    According to the Storytellers Handbook to be Sabbat (p. 43), it's existed since the Sabbat began in one form another, but only "gained acceptance" after "the Sabbat Civil War" (it doesn't say which). Like MCbN and GttS, however, it says the Code was "revised" in New York, so this presumably refers to the revised Code published in 1933 (hence, at the end of the Second Sabbat Civil War). It also says this version is shorter than the original, was revised because of the efforts of the Black Hand (there's no separation of powers here!), and that many packs recite it at the start of their esbats, making it "a creed now than a set of laws".

                                    Montreal by Night (p. 73) only mentions it once, as the (clearly misspelled) "Code of Milian". But it honestly would've been better to have two different codes with different names.

                                    Clanbook: Lasombra Revised says the revised Code probably isn't actually reflective of the original Code at all (p. 35), but is rather a "consensus statement" between the Black Angels and everyone else. Giangaleazzo apparently burned the original, so I would look to the date of his "coronation" as Prince for the date of the original draft. He may have allowed the document to be written there prior to his public defection.

                                    According to Chaining the Beast (pp. 56–59)​, the tenets of the Path of Honourable Accord go back to the Path of Chivalry, with its principles dating to 1150 or earlier, but the tarot motif in the Code dates its final compilation to much later. The tarot was invented in Italy between 1425 and 1442, but their use in divination originates in the 18th century. That gives us 1425 as the earliest date for the longer Code.

                                    I think those are the main references I can find. I guess it's feasible that the Purchase Pact came first, ending the war, and Gorchist promised to document the Code of Milan afterwards as a sop to the traditionalists. Alternatively, maybe the name was simply recycled from the original, apocryphal text to give it more respect.

                                                I love that discussion.                                                 As you can tell, I'm a little obsessed with the lore right now. I've been developing a Sabbat guide for V20 (mostly for myself; maybe for STV one day), which involved creating a whole timeline of the sect from the first stirrings of the Anarch Revolt and a little before. (I basically start during the War of Princes, since that's when the Inquisition begins being active.)

                                                After fleshing it out with every detail I could find about the Sabbat, I then began filling in the gaps for things to add plot hooks, explain things that didn't make sense, or give more specifics for individual events. E.g., there's lots written about the Sabbat Civil Wars in Mexico, and sometimes the US, but there's practically nothing about what happened in Europe during these times.

                                                The messiness of the lore really does make it feel like you're studying real-world history, though, because even that is widely disagreed upon in many cases. Not just in terms of whether event x happened on date a or b, but also in terms of what event x even means and whether event y was also an influence.

                                                Filling the gaps in can create new storytelling opportunities if you like that sort of thing. E.g., Melinda Galbraith (no given surname at the time) is said to be "a fourth generation Lasombra" in the Players Guide to the Sabbat (1992), where it describes her as "a ruthless manipulator who used assassination and blackmail to gain control of the sect; however, she has proven time and time again to be an outstanding leader" (p. 19). It also says there have been four regents (further up on p. 19) and "all but one of the Regents" have been Lasombra (p. 61).

                                                The Storytellers Handbook to the Sabbat (1993) finally gives her a surname, and says there are two archbishops beneath her in Mexico City, since the city is so huge (p. 35). It's said Mexico City is rivalled only by NYC for the power of its vampire inhabitants. The book also says Ventrue antitribu Loyalists dislike the revised Code of Milan, since they don't think the regent responsible for it had the authority.

                                                In hindsight, that little line his actually makes the apparent contradictions gel slightly better, since Gorchist's death in 1863 (per MCbN, p. 24) means he couldn't possibly have been the one signing the Code in 1933, even though it bears his name and says it is his decree (its opening line states "by the solemn word of Regent Gorchist"). Since MCbN established that Galbraith unveiled the signed Code in 1933 and then declared herself regent, this prior bit of information retroactively suggests she was laying claim to Gorchist's authority a full 70 years after his Final Death. She really didn't have the authority to make it official, and Gorchist was dead, so he didn't either! What was an error created by later books (Gorchist signing a document after his death) can become plausible again when you comb back over the old details—to get things done, Galbraith had to invoke the dead regent to authorise herself as the new regent. This is perfect fuel for Loyalists to continue to challenge the Status Quo.

                                                In The Chaos Factor (1994), Melinda Galbraith is retconned to be the childe of Helena, and is only the cardinal of Mexico City (later books ignore her demotion, but you can assume she's regent and cardinal if it helps). This is when she becomes a fifth generation Toreador antitribu. This ostensibly creates another problem, since prior books established her as Lasombra, except that MCbN also reveals that the Lasombra kept out all the other clans from Mexico, Hispaniola, and most of the New World until at least 1519 (p. 16). Meaning Melinda and Helena may simply have posed as Lasombra to allow them to travel with Cortés to Hispaniola in 1504. Once again, with a bit of quick thinking, a retcon can make a little bit more sense. (Her generation is still out by one, but if she doesn't tell anyone who her sire is, it makes sense that they might assume she's fourth generation. If you like, it could alternatively mean she's committed diablerie. Mexico City by Night, p. 17, says Huitzilopochtli was diablerised, so what if that's when Galbraith became fourth generation? Wait, she ate Shaitan?! Plot twist!)

                                                It does, however, create other issues, in that her backstory now makes her Spanish (Galbraith is a Scottish surname), and her first name comes from the 19th century, not the 12th when she was sired. Maybe she made the name up, though, to distance herself from her past. This is sort of supported by a typo in CF, where they twice call her "Marie" instead of Melinda (both on p. 17). So maybe Marie was her original name (or more likely Maria)? A Spanish Cainite called Maria could plausibly fit in with the Lasombra, especially if she and her sire have bucketloads of Presence and Dominate. To also fit her portrayal as Lasombra, Galbraith has Dominate 8, Obtenebration 5, and Potence 7 in CF, so that sells the lie.

                                                There's still the issue of the regents. Once Gorchist is said to be Tzimisce, it means all three of the other regents must be Lasombra. But now that we've established how she snuck into New Spain, it could just be the case that most simply believe Galbraith to be Lasombra. New York by Night addresses this by repeatedly suggesting most Sabbat Cainites (and even Camarilla Kindred) assume whoever is in charge is Lasombra, and the Lasombra don't correct them because it suits them to have everyone believe they're in charge (pp. 17, 18, 25, and indirectly on pp. 20 and 21).

                                                And now I immediately want to start writing up the other two regents and who they were! Do they still have loyal followers who blame their successors for their Final Deaths? PGttS (p. 19) and Guide to the Sabbat (p. 152) imply that's how most regents got their position, after all...

                                                Of course, since the books often contradict themselves, it creates an implicit invitation to do that ourselves. So you don't even need to try and make it make sense—you can just pick the bits you like and ignore the rest.
