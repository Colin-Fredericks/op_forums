                      I can't point to any specific canon examples, but small villages of all-kinfolk sound entirely reasonable (for certain values of village). There's tons of them, they're easily guardable, they don't have any need to be around other people. Villages with large changeling populations don't seem reasonable because it's not lineaged in quite same way, and changelings thrive on having large numbers of human dreamers around. Villages of all vampires aren't do-able at all for reasons that should be obvious, and you can bet your fangs that the Garou will not tolerate them in their villages of kinfolk. Mages seem like they would get along alright, maybe even with other supers, but there's hardly enough mages to fill out a population.

                                  . . .and I thought about how much fun a game centered around magical beings, preferably magi since I am a fan, holed up in their own little corner of an otherwise mundane world, occasionally leaving it and bringing a bit of wonder with them into the regular world would be.
                                  You're looking for Dudleytown, Connecticut (detailed in Freeholds and Hidden Glades). Known to the fae as "Graymere Glen", it was originally a nunnehi (Native American fae) site. But they abandoned it due to certain . . . "difficulties". Later, Polish immigrants found tin in the area, and built Dudleytown . . . only to experience the same "issues". Although, to their credit, they did hold on until the mid-Twentieth Century.

                                  You see, it takes a special kind of place to accommodate both the fae and mundane humans. And most places can't do it - too hard to balance banality against glamour. But Dudleytown wasn't most places.

                                  From the epic Beowulf to the Friday the 13th movies, people have told tales of the "dark lake in the woods". A place where the willows walk . . . and something terrible dwells in the water. They're all talking about the same place. They're talking about Graymere Glen. A wise man would have stayed away. But the humans wanted the tin . . . and the lake wanted to hear them scream.

                                  Some of the newly arrived sidhe discovered the area during the Accordance War. The perfect place for a summer home, after all. Old growth forest, beautiful lake, natural source of glamour . . . the perfect retreat.

                                  The lake didn't like that idea.

                                  Some of the sidhe actually had a chance to scream.

                                  * * * *
                                  If you're looking for somewhere to set your campaign, and want to use Dudleytown, you have two options. You can go with Dudleytown during its prime (i.e. pre-1950s), before the tin ran out. Plenty of humans living and working in the area, all desperately trying to ignore what's going on around them. Not the easiest feat, considering that many of the local chimera can step into "mundane" reality, at least for a moment or two . . .

                                  Or, in the modern day, you can look at Cornwall, the neighboring town. Nowadays, Dudleytown is almost a ghost town (inhabited by a couple changelings and chimera born of an entire town gone mad), but its so etched in the local imagination, things can occasionally wander through the woods to "visit". Even most of the changelings of Graymere Glen actually live in Cornwall. Not that they're afraid of their own freehold, you understand. . . .
