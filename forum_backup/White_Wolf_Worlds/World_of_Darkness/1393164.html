    Catalog: Mannikins (2nd Sample)

    05-31-2020, 02:45 PM
    A second sample of my lost Project, "Catalog: Mannikins"

    5th floor: The Unblinking People.

    Character Creation:
    Follow the steps in “Inanimae: the Secret Way”.
    Step One: Character Concept.
    Choose concept, Court, Legacy, Jeu, Style and Salon (optional)
    Step Two: Select Attributes.
    Prioritize the three categories: Physical, Social and Mental (7/5/3)
    Or
    Divide 15 points between the nine Attributes.
    Mannikins begin with 1 point in each attribute, like all other characters.
    Step Three: Select Abilities.
    Divide 27 points between all the Abilities you posses.
    Mannikins do not usually prioritize their Ability Categories (check with your Storyteller this step!).
    Step Four: Select Advantages.
    Choose Backgrounds (5) and Realms (5). Mannikins may choose both Inanimae Realms and Kithain Ones, but they rarely mix both of them.
    Choose Slivers and/or Arts (as determined by your Jeu)
    Step Five: Finishing Touches.
    Record beginning Glamour, Willpower and Banality as determined by your Jeu (check Inanimae: the Secret Way)
    Record Birthrights and Frailties, and Style’s Boon and Flaw.
    Spend freebie points (15)
    Choose Merits and Flaws.

    Mannikins’ Birthrights and Frailties (Obsolete)
    The mannikins’ Birthrights and Frailties were dropped from “Inanimae: the Secret Way” and appeared in MET format in the “Shining Host Player’s Guide”. This tabletop version includes the Styles, and is the one I use in my Changeling campaigns.

    Birthrights:
    • Human Touch: Forged from ideals of human perfection, all mannikins get
      three free dots in the Husk Background. Mannikins of the Jouet Style gain an additional Charisma point. Mannikins of all other Styles gain an additional Appearance point.
    • Jack-of-All-Trades: Can learn Kithain Arts and Realms, and may learn Inanimae Slivers and Realms. They may also take any Backgrounds, including those normally reserved for other Inanimae.

    Frailties:
    • Master of None: Cannot master any Art or Sliver (they cannot attain level 5). Also, mannikins do not posses an Affinity.
    • Spark of Life: While they are a good imitation of human life, there is always something odd or alien about a mannikin appearance. They get a +1 difficulty in all Social rolls against mortals who know their true nature, even if they have managed to get their trust (i.e. Allies). Most mannikins are quite sensitive about this flaw, and will become resentful of those who insist on reminding them of it.
    • Velveteen Dreams: All mannikins are interested in the Heart Riddle, and all would love to become “real” humans. They must make a Willpower roll to avoid exploring any possibility or chance to become real when presented with one, no matter how remote.
    Styles & Salons

    Styles are both like and unlike the Sidhe Houses; all mannikins belong to one, but the choice is irreversible. You can only change your Style if you adopt a new Anchor, and that is something that most mannikins would prefer not to think about, ever. Nevertheless, they are also unlike the Houses; Styles are not fraternities, families or secret societies… they are more like “outlooks” or “lifestyles”. Mannikins bound to similar Anchors usually possess similar outlooks, tastes, preferences… and they tend to stick together. Salons, on the other hand, are like political impulses, or private clubs. A mannikin does not have to belong to a Salon, but all mannikins belong to a Style.
    If an Anchor is destroyed, mannikins usually bind themselves to a new one from the same Style. (You should read more about this in Inanimae: the Secret Way, pg. 75)

    If a mannikin changes his Style, he will enter a dazzling world of changing-alliances and mixed reactions from both the members of his new Style and his former friends. If the Anchor of a Natural is Krofted, of course, the Natural becomes a Krofted mannikin (most likely a Top Model, a Supplicant or a Phidian), and he is sure to find himself surrounded by new faces.

    All Styles posses a cost in Freebie points, reflecting their relative rarity in the modern world. Finally, all members of a Style receive both a Boon and a Ban.

    While it is unusual for mannikins to change their Salon’s affiliation, it can and does happen. Mannikins of the Automata Style are highly vocal about their membership in a given Craft or Factory Salon, and anyone hosting a meeting of Automata mannikins is careful not to invite members of opposed Salons, unless a verbal (and sometimes physical) confrontation is the desired goal.
    Most Supplicants are ideologically, if not formally, members of a Craft Salon. On the other hand, most Top Models agree with the Factory theories, but there are still some Fashion Victims allied with the Craft Salons. There are more Factory Toys than Craft Toys, but as members of this Style tend to be gentle and the Style itself lacks the venomous internal-politics that plague others, the Craft-Factory conflict is seen as a quite boring and banal matter.
    The Manzolini mannikins aligned with the Craft Salons form a kind of gerontocracy, but one that is willing to listen to the arguments of the Factory thinkers; all in all, argue the Manzolini, all human beings are quite similar to each other if you stop to look under their skin, so why should mannikins not follow their lead? Naturals, being Gladeling mannikins, are outside the bounds of the Craft/Factory debate, and form something of a “Salon without members”. If hot-tempered debates arise, Naturals tend to align with the Free-thought Salons. Finally, Statues affirm that their style does not posses any Factory members, and that the concept itself is ludicrous, so nobody has yet heard about a Phidian being member of a Factory Salon.

    Salons:
    It is important to remember that Salons are much more important in the Dreaming than in Earth (specially when you are in Capecia!). Mannikins dominate the Empire of Dolls because they are both smarter and more numerous than all the other chimerical doll races together, but there are many, many members of the Salons that are not mannikins. For instance, there are many bronze talking heads, like those built by Greeks and Romans, belonging to the Copelli Salon. They are not mannikins, because they lack a human-like body.

    - Čapek:
    The members of this Salon, called Bots, are spread along a spectrum of Factory ideologies, from the hard-core radicals of the Removers of Identity to the moderates of the League for a Better Tomorrow. The Mechs dominate numerically the Čapek Salon; its Top Model members write many of its ideological treatises and propaganda.
    Members of this Salon advocate the homogeneity of Husks as the most desirable quality of mannikins, and reason that differences between individual Husks are the seeds of social unrest and disorder. Young Kithain tend to call Bots “fascists”, but this term is meaningless for the Inanimae; those of the mannikins who have studied the history of the Autumn World are quick to pinpoint ideological differences between the Čapek Salon and the Italian and German Fascist Parties of the 30’s.
    The radical thinkers and academics of this Salon, on the other hand, tend to dismiss these critics with the all-too-often-heard phrase (first heard in the Capecia Senate, addressed by a Čapek Senator to a Sidhe Ambassador, or so it is said): “You meat won’t understand”.
    There are other Factory Salons, mainly the radical Cyberdreams Salon (sponsored by some filthy rich citizens of the Cybernetic Quarter of Capecia). The Cyberdreams Salon advocates the destruction of all non-homogenous Husks and the proclamation of a decree forbidding the creation of such Husks. Their presence in the Capecian Senate is purely symbolic. The long, tedious speeches of the Senators of the Cyberdreams Salon are usually embarrassing for the Bots, because even the hard-core advocates of Factory know that it is technically impossible to craft 100% identical Husks without losing them to Banality.
    • Copelli:
    The Copelli Salon did not exist before the implement of mass-production by Henry Ford in the beginning of the XX century. Previously, Craft had been the dominant movement in the theoretical and philosophical landscape of Capecia, and advocates of Factory had been rare. Many “guilds” and “craft schools” existed that defended the creation of Husks and the superiority of hand-made Anchors, but there was not a common line of thought, a theoretical fundament or a manifest. All of this became a need after the founding of the Čapek Salon.
    Members of the Copelli Salon, like their counterparts in the Čapek Salon, are spread along the whole spectrum of Craft philosophy: from the moderates of Manufactures of Glamour Inc. to the hard-line radicals of the Thousand Toy-Makers.
    To the Copelli, as each faerie spirit is an individual, each Husk should be different from all others. They see mass-produced Husks and Anchors as abominations, or at least as poor substitutes for the hand-made, traditional ones. They point to the dangers of Quickmadness, the variety of Bedlam that affects mannikins of the Factory Salons. Moreover, they look at the impersonal look of the Factory Husk, and label it “ugly”.
    To Bots (and many Kithain), the Copelli are elitists and sickly refined in their tastes and mannerisms. Their mechanical movements (the Copelli Salon is numerically dominated by the Alberti and the Manzolini) are frightening and creepy in their beauty.
    There are other Craft Salons, but their membership is low, and their participation in the Senate is minimal. The most radical of these is the Luddite Salon: some of these Inanimae are so set in their hatred for the Factory that have resorted to terrorist acts, such as the use of Engine Poison against Mechs and Factory Manzolini, and the use of explosives in the corridors of Capecia controlled by the Factory Salons.
    • Rotwang:
    No single Style dominates the Rotwang Salon. There are as many Top Models as Supplicants in this menagerie of “liberal”, “democratic”, “anarchistic” mannikins. If only, there is a slight majority of Statues, but that is probably due to the fact that Statues resent any political impulse not led by themselves, and that many of them love to talk endlessly about the glorious, bygone days of the Greeks and the Romans.
    Rotwang philosophy treads a middle ground between Copellian idealism and the stultifying pragmatism of the Čapek Salon. Members of this Salon recognize the individuality of a given mannikin, and support the right to posses a hand-made body. However, they doubt the desirability of the mass-produced Husks recommended by the hard-core members of the Factory Salons.
    Moreover, members of the Rotwang Salon support the right of individual mannikins to use Factory-produced Husks, unlike the more orthodox members of the Copelli Salon.
    Rotwang are not very strong currently at the Capecian Senate, but they are slowly increasing their numbers as the two varieties of Bedlam that affect the Inanimae, Dumbmadness and Quickmadness, are claiming the minds of more and more citizens of Capecia. In addition, they are progressively gaining the support of the few Naturals who are members of the Senate. Soon, they could ally with the more liberal members of the Copelli and force the abdication of the High Dux.
    There are no other significant Free-Thought Salons. There is, however, a terrorist group (“Cog Boyz”) formed by a renegade, splinter bunch of Mechs that grew disfranchised with the cautious, moderate policy of the Rotwang Salon. These “Cog Boyz” claim the Senate is an outdated institution, and that what Capecia needs is a consensual, anarchistic government. Passersby have spotted Members of this group fighting both the Luddite Salon and the Capecian Ducal Guard.

    Styles:
    • Automata (freebie cost: 1)
      • Nickname: Alberti (Craft) / Mechs (Factory)
      • Boon: Automata mannikins are expert crafters and artisans. They gain a free dot in the Crafts Skill (usually Clockwork or Electronics), and cannot botch Crafts rolls.
      • Ban: Members of this Style tend to be cold and detached. Their Empathy Talent cannot be higher than half their Glamour score (rounding up); this Style is deeply split between Craft and Factory, and Automata members of a Salon suffer a +1 difficulty in all Social rolls against Automata members of an opposed Salon.
    • Ceremonial (freebie cost: 2)
      • Nickname: Supplicants & Sepulchrals
      • Boon: Ceremonial mannikins are solemn and formal. They gain a free dot in the Etiquette Skill, and cannot botch Etiquette rolls. All Ceremonials posses 1 free dot in the Relevance Background.
      • Ban: Stone Supplicants are disliked by Glomes, and Wood Supplicants are disliked by Kuberas; both phyla see them as agents of the Krofted, and they suffer a +1 difficulty penalty to all their Social rolls against members of the pertinent Phylum. Supplicants whose Anchors are made not of stone or wood suffer this penalty with both Kuberas and Glomes (they think they are cover-agents).
    • Fashion (freebie cost: 0)
      • Nickname: Top Models & Fashion Victims
      • Boon: Fashion mannikins are the most common, and the most used to deal with humans. They begin the game with a free dot in the Empathy talent, and posses a –1 difficulty to all Empathy rolls.
      • Ban: Most mortals (and some supernaturals) tend to feel betrayed after discovering that the Top Models are not “alive”. Fashion mannikins suffer a +2 difficulty to all Social rolls (instead of the usual +1) with people who know their true nature, unless they manage to gain their trust again.
    • Jouet (freebie cost: 0)
      • Nickname: Toys & Game Partners
      • Boon: Toys are always willing to play with children, and are used to their ways and manners. Toys gain a free dot in the Empathy talent and posses a –2 difficulty to all Empathy rolls with children only.
      • Ban: Toys must buy the Flaw: Small, but receive no free points for it. Their Husks always look like children (when weeks pass, they look like wizened, gnarled children!), and their Dream forms are always small. Moreover, they are not taken seriously by other mannikins, and cannot posses more than 3 points in the Relevance Background.
    • Manzolini (freebie cost: 2)
      • Nickname: Plastic Corpses & Waxworks
      • Boon: Waxworks are experts in the activities that surround their Anchors: Anatomy and Medicine. They gain a free dot in the Medicine or Science (Anatomy) Knowledge, and cannot botch Medicine rolls. All Manzolini Husks possess internal organs; due to this fact, Manzolini gain an additional dot in the Husk Background (up to a maximum of 5).
      • Ban: Manzolini are creepy. Mortals and other un-enchanted beings tend to feel uncomfortable around these mannikins, and they suffer a +1 difficulty on most Social rolls (except Intimidation). When building a Husk, Manzolini cannot help creating removable skin-plaques; if these are retired, the veins, arteries, tendons, intestines, etc… are fully visible, and the Manzolini’s Appearance drops to 0 (however, Manzolini without their skin-plaques gain a –2 difficulty to their Intimidation rolls)
    • Natural (freebie cost: 0)
      • Nickname: Naturals
      • Boon: Despite their instincts, Gladeling Inanimae trust Naturals. Naturals gain a free dot in the Survival talent, and posses a –1 difficulty to all Social rolls with Gladeling Inanimae.
      • Ban: Naturals are looked down by other mannikins, and suffer a +1 difficulty to all Social rolls with non-Natural mannikins.
    • Phidian (freebie cost: 2)
      • Nickname: Statues & Golems.
      • Boon: Statues are sages and keepers of lore. They gain a free dot in Academics, Gremayre, Occult or Lore (Dreaming) Knowledge, and cannot botch rolls in their field of study (if the player chooses a free dot in Academics, the character cannot botch Academics rolls). Moreover, they are solid and stoic, and posses a free additional Bruised Health Level.
      • Ban: Statues are haughty and patronizing, and tend to disdain the comments of those they perceive as inferior to themselves. As a result, they suffer a +1 difficulty to all Social rolls (including Intimidation). They are also vulnerable to flattery, and suffer a +1 difficulty to all Empathy rolls while being flattered.


    New Background: Relevance
    Mannikin society lacks the stiff, feudal rigidity of Kithain society, and possesses instead a more flexible social structure. The mannikins call this curious mix of modernism, personal qualities, castes and status “Relevance”, and it measures a given mannikin’s importance and position in the society of the Unblinking People. It measures also the degree other mannikins have heard about the character.
    Mannikins, like all other Inanimae, cannot be affected by Sovereign, regardless their Relevance rating, unless they have accepted a formal title in Kithain society.
    Relevance correlates sometimes, not always, with Status (which measures the position of an Inanimae among all other Inanimae). (i.e. Black Robes (who must posses Status 5, tend also to posses Relevance 4-5). However, this is not obligatory, because you could be very famous among the Inanimae (high Status) but not very known among the mannikins (low Relevance).
    All Supplicants possess at least Relevance 1, reflecting their prestige and influence in mannikin society.
    x Nobody knows your face. You are a second-class citizen.
    ● You are recognized as a citizen, but granted no special privileges: Toy worker, upcoming Top Model, unimportant Supplicant.
    ●● You are a member of the low-middle class: successful novice Top Models, artisan Alberti and Mechs, Manzolini herbalists and apothecaries.
    ●●● You are a successful middle-class citizen of the Empire of Dolls: Toy magistrates and senators, ambitious Top Models, looked-down Supplicants & Statues.
    ●●●● You are one of the movers and shakers of Imperial policy: Natural & Top Model senators, Manzolini anatomists, Alberti & Mech Master Artisans.
    ●●●●● You are one of the most important citizens of the Empire, and you are respected everywhere: Supplicant, Manzolini, Alberti & Mech senators, Salon Leader, Top Model couturier (yes!).
