    Originally posted by beccatoria View Post

    So what should the price be?
    Determined by character creation, you buy up or buy down Banality from a base point make it a simple matter of character creation than some sort of optional special snowflake merit system.


    The more I've thought about this thread I think Banality in general has been done wrong. Its treated generally as an active thing, something you generally get in play, much like humanity loss in vampire. However they are sort of inverted situations really, a vampire creates a stagnant routine because that won't risk their morality. Going out to "Adventure" is when they risk change they risk their carefully built morality in the face of the beast. Conversely for the changeling the risk isn't when they go out and adventure the risk for banality is when they fall into routines, that perhaps there should be some sort of mechanic for determining background Banality the Pc picks for interacting with the mundane world. I think this might cover both my side and your side of the issue. As this would more readily cover much of the fluff dangers of grumpdom that just never showed up in the mechanics, but also allow those who cheat the system.

        That's an idea I could get behind in a lot of ways. As I said I wouldn't be against it as a Merit/Flaw situation, so a point buy could work. Though as I've said my view is that there needs to be a mechanical correlation between growing older and gaining banality to establish underlying themes, so I think it'd need to be a point-buy from differing baselines. Like as a Grump your default banality is 5, but with an option to buy it down with freebies. Or if you want to be a Childling with a higher banality, there could be an "increased banality" flaw based a more difficult pre-chronicle life. I think it should be a reasonably expensive one though because banality should be a big deal and buying it off - or accepting extra - should be a big deal too.

        That way you give options to players who want to play against type without undercutting the setting.

                            I go back and forth on this just about every time I think about it.                                                     This sort of threads really do take me back to the "Good Ol' Days" of the old changeling forum.
                                                    One of the things i most enjoyed about this game was that there were so many gaps in the rules, and that the fans took it upon themselves to come up with house-rules to fill in such gaps. Some of those house rules were amazing. There were others that were merely adecuate. Of course there were also the awful ideas, but lets not dwell on that. In a way, the gaps in the rules were an integral part of the game.a

                                                    So what if we didn't get a Rev. Edition, we just came up with fun ideas to cope.

                                                    Now, back to the topic at hand... At first, when i saw the Wonder/Seeker/Keeper bit i was surprised. A new mechanic that would affect the game at the most basic of levels. At first I was against it... but then i changed my mind (i do that. A lot) We need to wait until further teasers reveal a bit more about this new mechanic. I will most likely come to love the idea once my troupe has had a chance to play the game... But even if i don't, there's nothing to worry about. I will simply hit it with the old "house-ruling-stick" until it fits into something my players will enjoy.

                                                    That's the beauty of Changeling, that Glamour and Creativity are not only for our characters to enjoy and use.
