    Why would the Chakravanti need to brand themselves in the first place? They're not a club thirsting for members, they're an ancient religious tradition with a very deep and involved philosophy. They have some Greek and Celtic members who have religious leanings toward chthonic deities and a similar practice, but they're relatively few and far between. The bulk of the Tradition has always been about the Wheel.

    The Chakravanti isn't any more or less a "culturally exclusive" name than the Euthanatos. It's Indian rather than Greek, which makes it a better representation of the Tradition's history as a whole. Besides, if they're looking for new members: there's a LOT of people in India, who already may have compatible philosophical leanings and a familiarity with the culture. They don't exactly need European or American recruits any more than the Akashayana do.

                    No I mean...

                    Lets say you want to offer your services for fun and profit to other mages that don't quite have the skills you do.
                    You are a welsh mage of the Chakravanti tradition.

                    Your magic stems from Celtic belief by the way. You got lumped in with the Chakravanti lot because of similarities. Since the Indian Chakravants can claim their magic older, and they've had the entire tradition change names, many people have began to assume that your magic is some kind of derivative. (At least with the Euthanatoi, you could blame the greek-roman connection and how that stuff conquered europe. India has had little impact on your celtic stuff to your knowledge) Maybe some scholar can point out that your tradition is a devolvment of the indian belief system, maybe it was an independent development, it doesn't matter. You are a proud welsh mage that practices a belief several-thousand years independent from india.

                    If you used a welsh name, people would have to think about it and translate it within their heads and go 'oh, you're with that indian tradition' which hurts your all-important mage pride.
                    If you said you're Chakravanti you would belittle your all-important beliefs and you'd get weird looks for being the whitest guy claiming to be indian.

                    These options undermine your bargaining power. If there are two entropic mages offering services to others, one person seems all together, and the other person is a white welshman playing the indian, the former has an advantage.
                    (Note: if you did things the other way around, India is enough of a bubble that it wouldn't matter what the europeans named the tradition)

                    If you went with something culturally neutral (The circle, The tapestry, the Cycle) Then you don't have any identity issues regardless of you being Welsh, Greek or Bhurmese. You can move to any country and market your Magick to other mages who need it evenly. Your Welsh magic does not have the connotations of being a culturally appropriated Indian magic.

                    No european mage would be happy with that connotation. It's not an issue of racism (It could be) it's that their magic works with their own, very old, cultural practices, and it's an attack on their very being to have their beliefs play second fiddle to a foreign one, even if it the big beats are similar, the details aren't, and mages go all in on details.

                    Catholics and Protestants have a lot in common, and they were burning eachother a few hundred years ago. Today, If you started calling ardent protestants catholic, two groups would be at the least annoyed, and if you started calling them both jewish (Let's pretend there's no baggage to that, and it's just a matter of thinking all the Abrahamic religions should be named after their original faith) You'd be causing quite the frustration with three.
                    While I'm sure some Indian Chakravanti would be absolutely delighted to impose their name on everyone in their tradition, a good few would sensible, and a good few would be loath that a welshman, with his beliefs that only match up to theirs from a shallow outside perspective, would be included in the Chakravanti label. Some people want a big brand, others want a selective brand.



                    It's just not good, Ok? What would be wrong with an all-encompassing name that doesn't make anyone's beliefs secondary?

                        Alrighty so I think I get what you mean to say here.

                        Going along with your example, I believe what you are describing is a very intentional quality of the traditions. The Council of Traditions is made up of broad umbrellas of loosely associated groups that do not all entirely agree despite the similarities of their beliefs. That tension goes back to the arrogance of the tradition founders and it's one of the big reasons why there is both so much tension within and between traditions but also between tradition and craft mages.

                        The hypothetical welsh mage has a few options. They can accept membership in a tradition founded by Indian mages whose paradigm mos resembles his own or they can go another way. The Verbena provide a more closely associated culture but neither the terms and associated cultures of the Euthanoi or Chakravanti really suit his culture. So he must choose between being a fringe member of this tradition or refusing membership and joining/forming a gaelic craft that suits his paradigm.

                        It facilitates the punk aspects of Mage as a gothic punk setting that one must choose between being on the in or on the out of the faction imo and it's something that could be fodder for a good story. It also highlights the fact that the traditions are a highly imperfect system of organization and one which arguably causes more problems then benefits for the Council Magi.

                                                The factions within the Euthanatoi, as of the Revised Edition, are the Chakravanti, the Madzimbabwe (focus on sub-Saharan ancestor veneration), the Hierochthonoi (Greek mages who deal with the more “earthy” chthonic gods of their mythology, and particularly with the Fates), the Aided (celtic bards who are masters of the Geasa), and the Vrati (defined less by culture and more by proficiency, these are the “Special Forces” of the Tradition). Those are the main factions, with the Indian mages easily being the majority and referenced as the ones who “define the Tradition's identity”. Then you've got a smattering of others such as Yggdrasil's Keepers (Nordic mages who use runes to reveal and preserve Wyrd), the Pallotino (Italian death-mages), Yum Cimil (Mayan mages), and so on.

                                                Note that the Aided fit into the Tradition not so much because they're about death, but rather because they're about fate.
