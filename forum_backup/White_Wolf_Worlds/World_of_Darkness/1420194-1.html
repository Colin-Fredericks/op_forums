    The technocracy got around the willpower problem by discovering a way to make gadgets without expending willpower to do so. I'm a bit more leery of the idea of the Order of Hermes duplicating that feat: I tend to think that it was The Syndicate and their money magic that discovered the secret, though it may have been iteration X and their notion of mass production.

    If the loophole is based on Mass production, there may be no way to deal with it. The predecessors to iteration X May well be the only group that would have thought of such a thing.

    But if the loophole is based on the Syndicate's money magic, it turns out that the Celestial Chorus has a surprisingly similar view. I could see their predecessors developing something not unlike Primal Utility, but focused through faith rather than economics. With that, an alliance between the Order of Hermes and the Celestial Chorus could produce the massive amount of Wonders needed. Certainly, the Syndicate's ability to harvest Primal Energy from Primal Ventures was a major contributing factor to the Technocracy's massive surplus of Quintessence needed to construct so many Wonders.

        I think that it's as simple as making Effects so accepted in the Consensus - so hard-coded into what people think is possible - that is ceases to be "Magic" in any form, and starts just being "Thing You Do".

        You know, like driving a car. Cars used to be Rotes/Wonders by technomancers, and/or dedicated Instruments for casting Path of Conveyance spells. Now it's entirely a function of the Drive ability. No Willpower expenditure, Awakening, or instruction in linear Sorcery required. (Even the creation of a car is more a function of the Technology ability, albeit one so complicated no single person can do it all themselves).

        There's an argument that can be made (because I've done so in the past) that large swaths of what people consider "normal human feats" were once True Magick, that just became so normalized that Reality doesn't recognize them as "magic" anymore. Building a fire from rubbing sticks together is Forces/Hellfire, until everyone was doing it. Talking is functionally a method of telepathy, using grunts and inflections to put ideas into other people's heads.

        All the Order of Reason did was introduce new technologies and techniques, popularizing and supporting them long enough for the "kinks" to be worked out (read: make it stop invoking Paradox or require Willpower to use). Academic, media, and economic factors helped, as did mass production, but they were means to an end. That end being turning it Coincidental.

        If the Order of Hermes were inclined to do the same thing, we would see mass adoption of things like golems or invisible conjured workers tilling the fields or doing manual labor, instead of tractors or forklifts. If people really believe in the spells to build or conjure workers (either spirits or programmed masses of invisible force), they could be taught to do it themselves in more concentrated ways. (I recommend looking into the OSR book Magical Industrial Revolution, which illustrates how wizardry could be brought to industrial proportions.)

        Same with things like magic brooms or flying carpets to convey people around, or magic mirrors to facilitate long-distance communication.

        One could argue that a Hermetic industrial society would require skilled magician-artisans to keep going. I would argue that we already have that kind of thing in the real world. Our society would grind to a halt without the legions of engineers, mechanics, technicians, programmers, and experts that maintain our machines and systems. In a Hermetic industrial society, it would be the same, except with more wand waving and arcane sigils.

            We've got some guidelines as to what it takes to make something Coincidental; and I could definitely see uses of Magick to make Procedures available to Extraordinary Citizens (see my thread about Spellbooks for some thoughts on how the Wonder Crafting rules could be adapted to allow Extraordinary Citizens to learn and use Procedures). I mean, that goes beyond making it Coincidental, and beyond creating “Sleeper Magic” (Sorcery/Hedge Magic, if you wish; or Acolyte Magic, if you don't want to use a whole separate system to handle Sleepers with magic). Once you've got those factors in place, what does it take to remove the need to spend experience points on arcane powers and fully integrated them into the natural order of things?
