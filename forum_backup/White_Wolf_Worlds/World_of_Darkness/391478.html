              A fair few times - consciously and unconsciously.

              The obvious thing to note is that a story generally has a set beginning, middle and end, and doesn't give you any ideas for what to do if the player does something different than the original protagonist, so it's a good idea to think about alternative outcomes in advance. In this example, is there a way to save Astra?

              The other risk is that the players know the story as well and recognise it. Changing names and locations can help, but only to a certain extent.

                  Have used other universes for inspiration and influence on my games and characters? Yes.

                  Have I run stories through RPGs? At most, sort of but again its more akin to the above. When the Warhammer 40K RPG line, Only War came out I ran a game where they were a squad in the Valhallan 597th, the regiment of famed Commissar Ciaphas Cain.                                           In my Hunter: the Reckoning game, the PCs investigated a deadly fungus that was spreading through the Los Angeles sewers. They discovered it was tied into a series of blonde women being kidnapped, fungus-riddled mutants, and a massive mutated turtle who had sufficient intelligence to declare himself the king of the sewers. Further investigation determined that the "mushroom men" had a prophecy of a mustachioed hero in red who would defeat the sewer king, and so they had been kidnapping blonde women in hopes of luring the hero to help them.

                                          It took my players a surprisingly long time to figure out that the whole thing was (in-universe and out) an extended Super Mario Bros. reference. Once they got it, though, they figured out just where the hell this "prophecy" came from, and with great effort managed to... get all the mutants killed by the Technocracy. Yeah, they kind of screwed that one up.

                                                      Originally posted by Alucard View Post
                                                      Did White Walkers exist? Dragons? Did magic come back into the world as the story progressed?
                                                      As I said it was ten years before Robert's Rebellion so neither White Walkers, Dragons or magic (beyond the Rites and Gifts of the Garou and Spirits) featured in our game. But they did exist.

                                                      The short version of the backstory was that several of the Tribes lived openly among the First Men as they came to Westeros. These tribes were the White Howlers, the Fianna, the Children of Gaia, the Red Talons and the Black Furies. They fought alongside their human kin against the Children of the Forest and further claim that it was one of their own, a CoG, that brokered the peace between them and came to serve as the keepers of that peace, ensuring that both sides honored the peace. When the Long Night and the White Walkers came, they were on the frontlines. Unfortunately their dead made for some of the most terrible wights around.

                                                      However once the Long Night ended and the Wall was being built things began to fall apart among the tribes, with the Wall cutting the lands belonging to the White Howlers in half. Needless to say they weren't happy about this, fighting happened and the Howlers and their Wildling kin were pushed beyond the Wall and in the present the Howlers are seen as feral monsters that want nothing more to burn everything south of the Wall. Basically they see them as a non-corrupt, non-corrupting BSD.

                                                      As I said, at that time the Garou lived openly with the First Men, with many of their noble families breeding with Garou, seeing it as a great honor to produce a Garou child. Indeed there were even Garou Kings from time to time that ruled both races.

                                                      Then the Andals and those tribes that lived among them came. The Andal Tribes, the Silver Fangs, the Shadow Lords, the Gets of Fenris, the Bone Gnawers, the Warders of Man and the Silent Striders lived much as canon Garou with the Veil between them and humans. They also brought silver weapons, something the tribes of the First Men never encountered before and proved to be quite effective against them. In the end, even though the North never fell to the Andals they were forced to adopt the Veil as well, though much looser than in other parts of Westeros with several Stark Kings that were also Garou long after the Andal Invasion and even in the present every major Northern House still carries the blood of Garou. In fact the pack briefly met Roose Bolton, the new Lord of the Dreadfort and a Shadow Lord after he and his pack saved them.

                                                      Yeah, the moment he revealed himself as Roose Bolton the rest of the table freaked out.

                                                      There is only one place in all of Westeros that Garou have no presence, the Iron Islands. Ever since they first came to Westeros they felt a powerful but dormant, sinister presence beneath the islands, something that unnerves them. They believe it to be what the Ironborn revere as the Drown God and that first BSD came into existence because they went there. And when the Ironborn invaded the Riverlands they came with the largest number of BSD ever seen.

                                                      When Aegon invaded the Garou at first did nothing, seeing it as merely another dispute between human kings and were much more busy with the BSD the Ironborn brought with them. It wasn't until Aegon torched Harrenhal that they realized just how dangerous he and his dragons were but before they could mount any proper kind of attack or defense, the Field of Fire happened and afterwards they decided to continue as they had so long as House Targaryen did not pose a treat to them. Though they had always been a little leery of the Targaryens and their dragons.

                                                      Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. They're indomitable. Indomitable.
