                Now, specifically about someone who would look upon others as "uncultured rabble"?                 The thing is though, Wan Kuei at the core are intended to be a cross cultural splat. India, China and Japan are all very different cultures, but when it comes to Wan Kuei society, it doesn't matter. They're all viewed as equals and all treated the same. There are certain bigots who will look down on one group or another because of their mortal culture, but as a whole Wan Kuei society and their Courts are above and beyond mortal culture.

                It's no different than the Camarilla. When a Kindred enters a domain and asks the Prince to stay in his city, it doesn't matter what race, or gender or ethnicity the vampire is. It doesn't matter where they grew up or what religious beliefs they have. It doesn't matter if they can't quote the Bible or Shakespeare. It doesn't matter if that Kindred was born and Embraced in Africa. What matters is that they're a Kindred and that they're familiar with the Camarilla's Traditions. The Camarilla's culture is much more important than any particular mortal culture.

                And this goes for pretty much every World of Darkness splat altogether. For werewolves, one's Tribe is generally much more important than one's human culture. The same thing is true for Mage and their Traditions. A Celestial Chorister isn't going to care if a member of their Tradition was born in Asia or South America.

                Of course you do have individual exceptions. Everyone has prejudices. Some Kindred will look down on other vampires because of their mortal stock - because that vampire is black, or Irish or came from Asia. But as a whole, the Camarilla doesn't operate that way, and it's the same for the Wan Kuei. Mortal culture becomes a lot less important after someone has taken the Second Breath. And no matter how much a Kindred or Werewolf or Mage studies the words of the Dharmas or listens to stories about escaping from Hell or being chosen by the Jade Emperor, none of that is equal to actually living those things, which is where the Wan Kuei's sense of pride comes from.

                        Originally posted by Kharnov View Post

                        I've generally spent less time on specific locations and characters within the setting than I have on general aspects (e.g. the Umbrae), but this doesn't quite jive with my personal experience of the World of Darkness as a setting. Groups like the Camarilla aren't concerned with issues of race and mortal culture in the same way modern North American society is "colour-blind" or "doesn't see race." It's absolutely still present and important in the experiences of characters in the setting, it's just less often to be explicit or formalized/legalized.
                        At the same time though, these are not institutionalized policies. A prince might be misogynistic and look down on women, but he's going to run into problems pretty quickly if he starts talking down to a thousand year old Methuselah just because she's a woman. The same is true for vampires who bring other mortal prejudices with them. The Traditions are pretty clearly the most important part of Kindred society and any vampire who starts breaking those because of mortal prejudices is going to get a reality check fairly quickly.

                        That's not to say that there aren't any vampires who engage in obvious actions based on mortal prejudices when dealing with other vampires, but rather that those tend to be the exceptions. You certainly have vampires who look down on women or black people, but at the same time Camarilla culture would not condone those racist or sexist vampires actions.

                        This is also true for with Wan Kuei. There may be some members of the Quincunx who look down on non-Chinese Wan Kuei, but at the same time Wan Kuei culture recognizes that Arhats come from all regions and all groups, so even the most racist members would have to at least give lip service to the equality of Wan Kuei from India or Southeast Asia. In fact, with Wan Kuei, even a fairly racist Thousand Crane from China will likely be more respectful towards a Thousand Crane from India than to a young Devil Tiger from China.

                        Again, in both cases you absolutely have individuals who are racist or sexist, but those don't really reflect the rules of the society that they're a part of. Both with Wan Kuei or the Camarilla the central idea is one of equality between members of their splat type. Further, when you do have issues, those tend to be more reflected by supernatural standing - members of clans in the Camarilla look down on one another, or members of different Paths will treat each other differently. As a general rule, within supernatural communities, the supernatural differences between individuals tend to be more important than mortal differences. That's where you start to run into semi-insitutionalized rules, like the treatment of Malkavians or Ravnos.

                        So it's not so much a matter of being color blind, but rather that the colors that get the most focus are not skin color but supernatural color, especially as vampires get older and more and more divorced from their mortal lifetimes

                                            I think one of the biggest problems discussing the Cainite v KoE emnity is that you are looking at it from the modern lense here, especially in regards to nationality and culture. This emnity isn't something -new- there's references to it in the in-lore ancient texts and stories. The Ercyes Fragments hint at it with its prophecies which could be as far as Second City era, since they would be written before Brujah/Ilyes' diablerie by Troile. The history of the Clans presented in their clan books show some of the wars; mainly Tzimisce / Kartarirya, Ravnos / Chandraputra, Nosferatu / Unknown Methulselah, settling in Harappa area and fighting wars with KoE in Mahenjo-Daro. Clan Book Lasombra Revised mentions broods before 1300 BC and 1100 BC in the Asianic areas. Overall the emnity between groups goes back at least 4-5000 years.

                                            On to gender/race. Id like to refer to Clanbook Lasombra Revised Pg 16's aside "What Manner of Creature" as an example of the Game/Devs/Setting explicitly stating that in general older Kindred don't care about such things. Are there Sexist or Racist Kindred? Sure are! Critias for example is a Pedophile and only feeds off young men, but these kinds of character's are outliers for the setting as a whole. This is also the reason that when read through the core books, the mention of gender/race doesn't appear till the Clan/Bloodline sections where they are talking about background. It really isn't all that important to the game as a whole, unless the players want to make it important. Don't forget this -is- a game.
