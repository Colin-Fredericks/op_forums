    ​​​​​​I had originally considered posing it as a variant of Violence as a Focus: make a Magick roll to temporarily imbue your Ability with a Magickal use, then (in the same turn) make an Ability roll to use it.

    I rejected this idea as being too clunky, and not something that you'd need Prime to do: Violence as a Focus doesn't require Prime. But that's what I was using as the baseline to decide whether my mechanism was overpowered.

    While I described what I finally decided on as adding Ability dice to the Arete pool, it would be equally fair to describe it as using Arete as the Attribute in a traditional Attribute+Ability pool; and that is, in fact, my preferred way of looking at it: instead of making an Arete roll followed by an Attribute+Ability roll, the inclusion of Prime •• lets you combine them into a single dice pool. Looking at it that way, you're not really getting more dice for the Magickal use of the Ability than you'd get for a mundane use of the Ability; you'd just be changing the Attribute from a mundane one to the Magickal one.

Which means no chance of outright failure, or a Paradox-inducing botch.                 I would use a variation of the Dividing Successes optional rule on M20 p.538: roll the dice as usual, then split the successes between the enabling Feat and the use of the Ability. If you're that worried about the mismatch in pool sizes, you can insist that no more than half of the successes can go to the enabling Magick, and the rest must go to the Ability roll.

                It's largely a moot point, though, because the enabling Effect is almost always accomplished with a single success: M537 says that “Personal Effects that make minor changes in your character's own reality require only one success. These include sensory perception enhancements, divinations, self-healing, temporary trait boosts, minor cosmetic changes (hair color, skin color, height, etc ) and the like. Extreme or long-lasting alterations — radical shape changes, multiple selves, transformations into elemental forces, and so forth — require three successes or more.”

                • The Chi-Fire Punch is a trivial example of this: you enchant yourself so that your next strike does aggravated damage.
                • Chi Flight is this, too: instead of actually flying, you run along surfaces that normally wouldn't support you; or you move through the air as if you were in low gravity instead of as if it was zero gravity. As well, the bulk of what Chi Flight does (in this view of things) is accomplished by your Athletics, not your Magick per se: you'd be rolling the likes of Chase, Climbing, or Jumping (M20 p.403); all that the Magick does is to alter how you interact with your environment, allowing you to move as if there were wires supporting your weight and pulling you around.
                • Chi-Healing can be thought of this as well: conceptually, it's like being able to use the “Patch 'Em Up” optional rule on M20 p.409 without the downsides such as needing a lengthy recuperation afterward: the removal of the downsides is “all” that the Magick does. One, maybe two successes to the Effect; the rest to the Medicine task.
                • The Spirit-Strike merely imbues the Chi user's next strike with the ability to treat appropriate insubstantial targets as if they were material; the actual attack mechanics do the rest.
                • The War Power Chi Shout explicitly references the Kiaijutsu Iron Shout technique (M20 p.429), which doesn't require the Magickal casting rules to use; the Life 3/Mind 2/Prime 2 Effect described on HDYDT pp.65–66 basically serve as an alternate prerequisite for the technique, letting the Chi Magician pull it off without needing to invest dots into Do. In this case, and indeed any other Special Do Techniques that the Storyteller decides should be accessible using Chi Magick instead of actually studying Do, I'd go with Attribute+Arete rolls instead of Arete+Ability rolls: substitute Arete for Do.

                The only Chi Effects from HDYDT that I'd require multiple successes be dedicated to the empowerment part would be the Chi-Field Push (unless you reinterpret that as a variation of the Hurricane Throw Do technique, M20 pp.428–429), the Chi-Vampire Strike, and Elemental Fury Chi; and even there, you're still using the Martial Arts Ability to do the actual attack: all that the empowering Effect of Chi-Vampire Strike does is to change damage inflicted into Quintessence drained; and Elemental Fury Chi, arguably the only one of these that might require more than two successes going into the Magick, adds appropriate elemental damage to the mundane damage of your regular Martial Arts strike.


                Bottom line, you're rarely going to be putting more than one or two successes into the empowering Effect; the rest goes straight into performing the Ability's task. So there's a slight drain on the “mundane” effectiveness of the task being performed; but that's easily compensated for by the Magickal benefits added to it. Which is why a Quintessence cost is appropriate in most cases: otherwise, it might be a bit too good of a deal.

                Chi Magick mostly isn't about performing Mighty Feats with Magick; it's about using a touch of Magick (mostly Simple Feats, occasionally Standard Feats) to leverage what your Abilities can do.

                                                Originally posted by travisjhall View Post

                                                Well, I think it’s really useful to have examples in the context of their paradigms. That’s among the things I’d most want to show to new players (or even old players coming into my game, as experienced players can have wildly varying expectations and I want to manage those expectations) so that they can see how it all fits together.

                                                It’s too bad that some fans don’t have a supplement that gives them what they want, but I can see why HDTDT followed fairly quickly after the publication of a new core book.

                                                (And while many effects are written up for specific foci, I don’t agree that there’s any implication that similar can’t be done, tailored for a different focus.)
                                                The problem is that this goes against much of how mage has been set up to function.

                                                There is a clear distinction between set between Focus, and the Spheres. The Mage's spheres demonstrate what it is that a mage is capable of producing theoretically. These are the depths of their effects, what elements of reality they have within the purview of their manipulation. These are stratified in a way which is not context dependent, for example, if you do not have Time 3, you cannot reverse time, if you do not have Prime 1, you cannot hold more than your avatar's level of quintessence, and you cannot cross the gauntlet by sidestepping without Spirit. It is true that you can achieve similar ends via alternative spheres, but that is not what I am describing here. The point is that each rank of a spheres enables you access to specific acts of manipulation.

                                                When it comes to conjunctive effects, this property holds true. Effects that your mage casts that call on multiple spheres do so because each element of that effect requires that level of the sphere to pull off. If you wish to transform a flower into a in to a crystalline flower, for example, this would require Life 2 for manipulation of plants, and Matter 2 for transmutation of substances into other substances. If you wish to make a portal into an Horizon world chantry, then you would need Spirit 4 to make the portal that goes in to the umbra, and Correspondence 4 to make the portal link to locations that are otherwise no where near each other.

                                                Each sphere in a conjunctive effect contributes something to the original effect which that sphere alone would otherwise be too limited to do.

                                                Focus instead isn't a question of what the spheres allow, but on what the mage believes is possible within the boundaries of their spheres. Let us take Forces as an example. Depending on what the mage believes Forces to be will influence the kinds of effects that they believe Forces can do. Let us begin with Forces 2 for minor influences.

                                                A Verbena style mage may see forces as the elements, Fire, Earth, Water, and Wind. For them, Forces 2 would be about moving fire, influencing the flow of water, Causing termmors in the earth or enforcing great gusts of wind. Their magic would be very much based around the interaction of these elemental forces and as such the effects they work on would be tied in some way to these for areas. Flight for them would be a means of imbuing themselves with wind, Gravity may be a product of Earth, Heat is a force of Fire, cold if a force of Water, but they are inherently linked such that the mage would be able to draw heat from fire, draw cold from water, draw flight from wind, and it would not be seen as a transmutation for them, which would otherwise require Forces 3. However, turning heat in to cold would indeed be a form of Transmutation, they are changing one elemental force in to another elemental force, and so they would not see it possible at that level of the sphere.

                                                A hermetic mage instead may see each Force as a specific Form from the astral plain, given concrete physical properties via perception. There is a Form of light, a Form of darkness, a Form of Attraction, a Form of Repulsion, a Form of heat, a Form of fire, a Form of cold, a form of movement... All of these forms are distinct and separate Force's patterns, and as such each one is subject to manipulations via Forces 2 on their own terms and independently from one another. It makes sense to the Hermetic that they can move shadow about as this is just manipulating the Form of darkness, while the above mentioned Verbena would have trouble seeing how this would be possible. They might instead consider that darkness is something that is imbued within water, and so the properties of darkness can be manipulated directly by making it flow more like water. However, to the Hermetic, the forms are absolute unto themselves, and as such to make darkness flow like water would be to transform one form in to another form, so they would require Forces 3 to attain a similar feet.

                                                A Etherite instead may theorise that all forces are simply manifestations of energy, and that this energy is come in a number of difference Qualia. They might recognise a Qualia of Heat, where high energy is warm and low energy is cold. A Qualia of light where darkness is the absence, a Qualia of Plasmic, where high energy plasmic is Electricity, and low energy plasmic if friction, etc. To them Changing the qualia up and down the scale of high to low is about either introducing or removing more of the correct qualia of energy to the equation. And so for them all Forces 2 effects is about manipulating the energy levels within qualia. But Forces 3 would be require to convert one qualia into another qualia.

                                                But the point here is that these are all still Forces effects. The Verbena does not need Matter to affect water, wind, or earth associated Forces, the Hermetic does not need Mind to focus on the Forms of the astral plane, and the Etherite does not need Prime in order to influence energy levels of the various Qualia.

                                                Forces covers this, and it is something that the writeup for Chi magic seems to disregard. Each sphere within a conjunctional effect must contribute something to the effect itself in a manner which makes sense for what that sphere is known to do. If the only reason a sphere is used is because it better fits in with their belief and does not actually change the effect itself, then this conflates Focus and Spheres in a way many find contradictory to the established logic.

                                                This is why I have come up with this interpretation, it demonstrates a role that Prime 2 actually provides for Chi effects in a way that not only makes the sphere use less arbitrary, but also opens the door for Chi using mages to better emulate the areas of the genre from which the practice was inspired.



                                                To sum up: The spheres should dictate what effects are possible from a gameplay persepctive, the Focus should dictates which effects within those spheres the mage believes they can do, and how they must go about them to achieve them.

                                                Focus should not make certain spheres mandatory, but they can enable more options that better fit the "genre" of the mage who has said Focus.
