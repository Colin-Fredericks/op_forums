                                        I agree with this.

                                        People seem to think the setting is much more hardcore than it is - partly a reaction to some ill conceived crossover in the early days, both canonical and by over rambunctious players and STs. Having a foe like the Wyrm - basically Satan - and setting it in the End Days, does much to clarify the mind and set players to kill the bad guys.

                                        In reality, the Garou live in shades of gray like everyone else. Garou definitely fight the Wyrm, but that is not all they do. And not all Wyrm creatures are equally a threat. In real life, people have to prioritize goals and sacrifice some things in order to get others. Some people are criminals. Some people are apathethic.

                                        A common problem in fantasy roleplaying is that certain sourcebooks and many gaming groups seem to assume "adventurers" are the common character types and the world is based around people like them. In the real world, "adventurers" (mercenaries, freebooters, hired thugs, adrenalin junkies) are few in number, sometimes despised, and live on the fringes of society. It distorts the setting to assume the world's important characters are similar to PCs or that what they do is normal. But that is what is scaled because that's what the game is about.

                                        A similar issue affects many portrayals of Werewolf. PCs want to be heroes, so we assume that's what the Garou are overall. In reality, Garou are just as self-serving or heroic as people in real life, which means the PCs actually are probably the most idealistic of the bunch. You'll have some septs that are very traditionalist and very sincere at obeying the Litany. You will have others that almost ignore it completely.

                                        The Litany is only enforced as much as ordinary Garou take it upon themselves to enforce it. There is no "state" with cops that do it. It's up to personal interpretation and how much one is willing to fight for it in a sept with people who hold different values.

                                        In the case of other magical creatures, simply killing everyone outright tends to lead to war. I believe the Garou have too many enemies to fight all of them at once. They have to select their battles carefully. A lot of enemies probably escape notice provided they don't threaten major Garou interests like the sanctity of caerns, important kinfolk, etc.
