                    So, the expanded-rules supplement for WOD5E just dropped. And there's one section that seems very relevant to H5 called "Preparedness":

                    Nothing slows a story down more than a lengthy inventory scene in which players list every conceivable item or object they may need for a night’s activities. The preparedness test avoids this scenario by allowing players to test during the session to see if they remembered to bring something along or pick it up earlier.

                    Characters need not roll for signature items: Of course the Martial Hunter brought their pistol, the sneaky Nosferatu brought their lockpicks, and so forth. Everybody has the normal sorts of things people might have in their pockets: cellphone, pocket knife, pencil, or what-have-you. This test covers slightly outré or unusual things: rope, a shovel or other larger tools, extra burner phones. The less likely the item, the higher the Difficulty the Storyteller should set.

                    The Attribute for a preparedness test is Intelligence; the Skill should relate to the item or its use in some way. Roll Intelligence + Firearms to have brought a silencer, or Intelligence + Craft to remember a wrench, or Intelligence + Occult to produce a tarot deck from the folds of your trench coat. Storytellers should ask for rolls only if failure is interesting, and preparedness is no exception. Is it worth a roll to see if the characters remembered to bring their tickets to the concert? Maybe not if they had nothing distracting them, but if they fled an overturned car after a high speed chase, did they grab them from the glove box? Would not having the tickets bring the story to an unsatisfying halt?
                    Okay, let's remind ourselves how H5 portrayed its Asset-Edges in the book with this example:
                    Amineh possesses the Arsenal Edge, together with Team Requisition and Special Features Perks. At the start of a scene she wants to provide as many large-caliber handguns as possible to her cell, together with concealed cases. The test is a win with a margin of three. One success in the margin is required for the case, and the remaining two provide two additional handguns, each with their own case.
                    Doesn't it feel like Preparedness is simply a better-written version of what the Asset-Edges in H5 are? But if you use Preparedness-rules, why would any player ever pick Asset-Edges...?

                                                The basic premise reminds me of the "horror story of the week vs. procedural mystery detectives" early seasons of Supernatural. It's not all that alien a framing for the monster hunter section of the urban fantasy genre. Dracula is in many ways a technothriller, and I've taken far more WoD inspiration from crime fiction and other indirect genre touchstones than I have for horror or urban fantasy directly, TBH.

                                                When I think of hunter character concepts, I think Vic Mackey, Mike Ehrmentraut, Parker, Dexter Morgan, Carl Kolchak, the Continental Op, etc. The core loop of a monster hunter game is basically either a mystery procedural or a heist, in terms of structure, usually both. This was true of Hunter classic; you had some shortcuts provided by Edges (especially in terms of monster detection) but you were basically doing the same work - investigation, planning, takedown, extract - as you do in H5. Which is the heist format.

                                                The rules do basically nothing to support this, though.
