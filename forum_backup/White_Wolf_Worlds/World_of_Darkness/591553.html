    The Wyrm / Grandmother?

    07-15-2015, 02:15 PM
    Okay.... are the Wyrm (W:TA) and Grandmother (W:TO) one and the same?

    The Wyrm lies imprisoned beneath the Black Spiral Labyrinth deep within Malfeas.
    Grandmother lay sleeping beneath the Labyrinth above Oblivion... until a dumbass mage nuked it and woke her.
    Many suspect these are the same labyrinth.

    The Wyrm creates bane spirits loyal to it which are bent on destruction.
    Grandmother made the neverborn spectres, which... kinda want the same thing.

    People/spirits can become corrupted by banes, and in the end will assist the eventual destruction the wyrm desires.
    Wraiths can slowly accrue shadow, their essence becoming corrupted as part of them desires oblivion and destruction until they become spectres.
    Couldn't spectres be a form of bane spirit?

    The Wyrm wants to destroy all of reality.
    Grandmother wants to do that too. She's literally EATING it! Consuming the labyrinth as she pulls herself out and up, to eat everything else as she goes...

    Anyone see any holes in this interpretation? 'Cause I'm pretty sure this one holds together under scrutiny.

              Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post
              No, because the Wyrm is explicitly stated to not be active in the Dark Umbra. Whiffs of taint on spectres are from coincidental relationships, read because spectres are such shitty people that they give off similar vibes. They aren't banes, though, because they lack the capacity to turn living beings into fomori when they possess them.
              The wyrm is explicitly stated to be imprisoned in Malfeas, which is in the Deep Umbra. But Malfeas is also a Near Realm (as is the Abyss). Placing Malfeas in, around, or near the Abyss and the Oblivion would not make the Wyrm active in the rest of the Dark Umbra.

              Every shitty person doesn't sporadically light up as wyrm-tainted. It takes significantly more than that. Though I didn't know that werewolves would actively detect wyrm-taint on spectres, it was only a guess (thanks for confirming that).

              And I don't know what happens when a spectre attempts to possess a person! But I know that spectres can become pretty warped and unnatural... I suspect that's something that would be up to a Storyteller's discretion. Seeing as "Banes are evil spirits in service to the Wyrm," if Grandmother can be established as associated with the Wyrm in any capacity then spectres would, in a sense, be banes.

              I'm not saying that Grandmother, and only Grandmother, is the Wyrm. I expect it's much more complex than that, but I would pin her as one of the Wyrm's most powerful manifestations.

              This entire thing you're going off on has been done before, and it doesn't hold up because all of the games are designed to disagree so strongly that you'll never shave off those ill-fitting ends to make them fit without losing something from the game whose ends were just shaven off. It isn't an interconnected picture like Exalted, it's a bunch of different pictures painted by each game, which all have things that are important to them that other games either don't care about or downright just disdain. It won't add up.
              I don't feel like I'm shaving anything off... especially not when you consider that some of the published materials have suggested that the earliest eras in the WoD were practically free of all banality, that would leave a much more fluid world (especially with regard to the passage of time, existence of multiple realities in the same place, etc) more like the Flux which would allow multiple explanations of events to be true simultaneously. Speaking of multiple right answers.... the minds behind World of Darkness frequently wrote multiple answers or paths for their stories so that storytellers could pick the best fitting one for their game.

              As for the Weaver and Lucifer, let's go back and review about the Triat being building blocks of the universe in addition to vast spiritual presences. As Supernals rather than mere Celestines, they carry far more of the cosmos on their backs, and each is absolutely necessary. You could probably scrub Lucifer out from his own game and not actually end up losing a third of what makes the universe the universe, so that one fallen angel is way, way too small to be a part of the Triat.
              The triat is a mythology. A bad retelling of creation, as telephoned down over generations of fera tribes. The Triat themselves are more conceptualizations, or cosmic forces, than specific entities; it's only through the garou mythology that they can properly explain them. So I'll concede that the comparisons are not basic one-for-one equivalencies. But nothing is lost from either Werewolf or Wraith by saying the Wyrm and Grandmother overlap, all that happens is that the two groups perceive their enemy differently. Which makes total sense when you consider that perception is pretty fluid in the umbra. As I suggested Grandmother is a facet or incarnation of the Wyrm, I'd pose that Lucifer too had a major role of a similar nature regarding the Weaver.

              And no, you could not remove Lucifer from his own game without losing a tremendous aspect of demon and the WoD's history. Yes, you could write something to replace him. But by that logic you could remove almost anything from almost anywhere, and this test becomes trivial.

              I appreciate the input, but I enjoy doing stuff like this. So I'm going to keep doing it.

By being able to connect the disparate threads of different books' stories, you can find shared motivations that many of the different denizens of this world could unite behind. Werewolves could side with Stygians against Grandmother as a greater representation of the Wyrm. Black Spiral Dancers and Lasombra or Baali clan vampires could be serving as thralls to Earthbound intent on advancing the Wyrm's destruction of the world.

                          I like being able to unite the different groups. For a game about the end of all things, it's satisfying to be able to draw together as many different threads as you can for the final climactic battle.
