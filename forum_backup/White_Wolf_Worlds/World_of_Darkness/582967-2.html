    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    I'm not sure that's a good case to justify the Technocracy as inherently banal. It's blaming basic human nature on them.

    The Technocracy pumps out "miracles" even if not on the Glamour-generating-level as the moon landing all the time. But the same thing happens, as always happens with humans, and new and amazing turns into mundane and ordinary.
    I think you're all forgetting the Technocracy's underlying motivations, though: for the most part, they DON'T want people to think. The Technocracy is about controlling people and dominating the consensus. The only free will is for the Enlightened.

    They may have had noble origins in the beginning, but they've always represented humanity's worst tendencies to oppress in the name of progress.

    It's about more than rote and ritual making things less wonderful. Those things are inherent to humanity, as you say. It's more about making things mundane so that they become the ONLY way the world works.

    The Dreaming is about potential, dynamism and things that don't make sense. As an organisation that ultimately tries to make everything make sense, and on their terms, the Technocracy is the exact opposite of the Dreaming. The Dreaming tells you there might be monsters under your bed, while the Technocracy not only shows you there isn't but proves to you why you were wrong and silly to be scared in the first place (literally killing the dream of the monster under the bed).

    To return to the space travel analogy, they made it mundane not so that we could all fly among the stars but so that we could only ever fly into space on their terms.

                        I did. Sorry. You were just the last person to say the Technocracy shouldn't be banal because of whatever. I think as an organisation, though, the Technocracy should be banal, even if individual members aren't. But I meant I wasn't specifically just disagreeing with you. In Britain we have a weird way of apologising for disagreeing with people by starting along the lines of, 'I'm not just disagreeing with you...' to make it clear our disagreements aren't personal. I'm not sure if this translates that well? I wasn't very clear.

                        This really has less to do with when these details started to emerge in the setting, but a lack of communication between the game lines as they were being written.
                        This is true, and that table in the book is written from a CTD perspective, not a Mage one. If I were playing a Mage game where Banality became an issue, I'd use a much more nuanced system for allocating individual Banality ratings.

                        Making sense is hard when the books don't provide a consistent explanation of what makes something have a higher Banality rating. The books are exceedingly unclear about what they want Banality to represent.
                        This is also true, and I wouldn't disagree here. Banality has been described differently in different sourcebooks and most CTD players regularly state that they'd like a clearer, functional definition that is more nuanced than 'magic good, science bad' (which, incidentally, can be seen as the original duality between Traditions/Technocracy in MTA too).

                        I think it's best to see the Dreaming as the last manifestation of the Mythic in the human consciousness. The Technocracy played a huge part in the end of the Mythic Age, and as such, that's why they're anathema. The Dreaming is full of wonder, but the 'logic' of the Dreaming is one of the Mythic (fairytales, legends, oaths, quests, etc). The 'logic' of the Technocratic paradigm (and yes, I understand there are several, but let's say the 'official' paradigm of the organisation as a whole) is very un-Mythic, even if it can inspire wonder. It's rational, and imperial in a scientific and cultural sense. The Dreaming is the right brain, and the Technocracy is the left brain. They see things differently, even if they sometimes come up with the same results.

                        So, for the purposes of brevity, I think it's fine that the table in CTD lists the Technocrats as 8-10 Banality. But for individual characters, obviously this will fluctuate.

                        I would probably use Resonance here. Static Resonances would make the character more banal, while Dynamic Resonances would make the character less banal. Stasis (Weaver) is often linked to Banality, while Dynamism (Wyld) is linked to the Dreaming. In MTA, Technocrats are considered the more Static group, while Marauders are considered the more Dynamic. Hence, Technocrats are at the top of the Banality chart, while Marauders are at the bottom, and Mysticks fill the gaps in between.

                        You have to remember that most adult humans have Banality 7 anyway, so the range for mages isn't really that shocking. Changelings are basically interacting with a world of make-believe that most adults simply won't believe is real.
