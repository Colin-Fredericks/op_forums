        No, the goalpost was can the Nephandi 'be' human, can they have and maintain loving relationships, not 'act' human.                                     Except that's not the way that works?

                                    To borrow from Pact, there's a difference between death and destruction.

                                    To go back to Mage, let's say there is a Celestial Chorister. Pillar of his community, gives to the poor and fights back the darkness in his community.... But he has two enemies. One the Technocratic Amalgam of his city, and two a Widderslainte who happens to be in the area.

                                    The Technocratic Amalgam could break down the door, and make him disappear. He would be dead, but his memory would live on. People would still think back and go "That guy was a swell dude." Or at worst, wouldn't remember him at all.

                                    The Widderslainte would not be so merciful. Not only would Joe Celestial die in an agonizing and degrading way, but everyone would look back and see his works tainted. Every thing he once stood for would be seen as a lie, as a cover for some depravity. Joe Celestial, as he existed before would be utterly gone. And all that would be left would be a twisted mockery of that, teetering on the edge of an abyss, only standing so long as to draw others closer.

                                    To go back to Pact, in Pact there is an equivalent to the Nephandi.

                                    they're called Diabolists, and they summon Demons. What's so bad about that you ask? Demons destroy. They do not merely kill you, they unmake what you are. And they do it in such a manner that the destruction is contagious. Things close to you inch that much closer to your fate. And so every victory for a demon takes all of reality just that one step closer to oblivion.

                                    And, as a counter-example, there are Scourges. These people summon Bogeymen, which are, long story short, every sadistic horror movie trope made living. They are terrifying, they are murderous, they are unclean.... they feed on fear, death and injury. but ultimately, they aren't causing destruction so much as they cause change. The change is unpleasant, but a living person dying doesn't mean what they were is gone, anymore than them trimming their fingernails does.

                                    It's a fine line. But still.

                                                        I doubt that there is any particular angle that Mage intends. The primary metaplot is Tradition versus Technocracy, but that is only one angle of the game that people can explore. As for PCs being exceptional, they are Mages. Mages are exceptional because they have the ability to defy reality, so I do not really think that they need to be more exceptional than that.

                                                        I think that it cheapens the horror of the Nephandi to make them anything but inhuman. The inversion of the Avatar and the resulting corruption of the soul is an attack against everything that most people consider to be human. Nephandi, in many ways, prove that humanity is a concept that is measured in terms other than biology and genetics.

                                                        I think that it also cheapens the horror of the Nephandi to make them so common that one is discovered every week by the PCs. In the Mage games that I used to run, I would use a single Nephandi as the mastermind, one that would be confronted at the end of the chronicle. The PCs would find that their normal enemies were criminals, cultists, formori, and sorcerers.
