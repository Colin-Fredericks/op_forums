          The Operative's Dossier
          Throughout the book, there are Disputed Data boxes designed to facilitate different takes on the content, depending on what metaplot elements you do or don't feature in your chronicle. They're very much into “it's your game; do as you wish”.

          Chapter one: Constructs and Symposiums
          This chapter has five sections in it: the Arcology X Project (Sejong, Republic of Korea), the Multinational Military Development Site (Northern Territory, Australia), Terranorming Research and Application (Lake Delton, Wisconsin), Ultima Thule, and the Experimental Station Symposium. Despite the being five of them, they are not aligned with individual Conventions. That said, they do have leanings: Arcology X is very much the story of things the Progenitors would like; MMDS has a very Iteration X vibe; Terranorming Research and Assocation is very much an NWO thing; Ultima Thule lands into Void Engineers interests; and and the Experimental Station Symposium is a Syndicate operation.

          Chapter two: Unlikely Allies
          Five sections there, too: the Council of Nine Mystick Traditions, Dissident Permutations, the Disparate Alliance, Orphans and the Unaligned, and Anomalous Allies.

          • The first section is a deep dive into the Ascension Truce metaplot option from Technocracy Reloaded.
          • Dissident Permutations deals with factions within the Union who are at odds with the Union as a whole: several Cross-Convention Initiatives (the Challenge Fate Foundation, the Department of Metahuman Studies, the Existential Threats Directorate, and Panopticon), the Friends of Courage, Project Invictus, and the Special Information Security Division (a spiritual successor to the Special Projects Division). The Disputed Data for the SISD includes an option near and dear to my heart: rather than being part of the corruption of Pentex, it suggests that the SISD might “merely” be monitoring, collecting, and studying deviant technology, such as those created by the likes of Etherites and VAs.
          • The Disparate Alliance section begins with the Black Daggers (what, you thought that the Ksirafai abandoned the Union?). It then goes into further detail about the Challenge Fate Foundation, and how the Disparates (and others) may have trouble believing that there's an honest-to-goodness good guys faction within the Union. The Cassandra Complex, meanwhile, is aiding the Disparate Alliance in its efforts to purge the Union of its corruption. The Friends of Courage also get revisited, in terms of how they're looking at the Alliance as a possible new option for Ascension. Both of the Enlightened Science factions being courted by the Alliance (Navalon and Mokteshaf Al Nour) are addressed. Finally, Project Invictus is also looking for allies among the Disparates.
          • Orphans and the Unaligned: these terms are synonyms, though “Orphans” carries pejorative connotations that “Unaligned” doesn't. As well, the point is made that as much as the Union likes to think that it has a monopoly on Enlightened Science, it doesn't: not only are there Etherites and VAs who aren't so far gone into “deviant technology” that they still might qualify as Scientists, but there are Unaligned Enlightened Scientists as well. The section goes through various stages of life for an Unaligned Scientist and how he or she might slip through the cracks and manage not to be recruited by the Union.
          • Finally, the section on Anomalous Allies provides three sample characters — a vampire, a werewolf, and a ghost(?).

          Chapter 3: Digital Web 3.0
          The big new thing that DW3.0 brings to the fore is Augmented Reality. This is followed by a rundown on how the Digital Web works, Social Media and the Agent, and the Dark (Digital) Web.

          Chapter 4: Technomancer's Toybox
          Adjustments: deviance potential evaluation, distribution management, ecological harmonization, generate emotional field array, predictive analysis, remote access, save file index, Schrodinger's parcel, system havoc, white out.
          Alternate Perceptions: Augmented Reality, Near Infrared, Pulse Mapping, Thermal.
          Procedures: Anti-Hemophagic Saline, Atomic Override, Force Compliance, Multipartite Host Infection, Pen Lance, Thanatos.
          Gadjets: 30 second start, adaptive combat analytics interface, brown paper bag, conversation emulator, field medical kit, nanite tags, Urban navigation tool, augmented reality smart glasses, pocket drone modifications, portable perimeter fence, DOUG upgrade, reverse temporal projector, automated construction, isolated strand, turbulent displacement field, wormhole generator, and tropic perception field alteration, node denial device.
          MMDS Prototype Technology: paradox sink, surveillance nanites, Quantum State ion weapon mark 1.

          Also in this section is a subsection about creating constructs and chantries. And a section on Terranorming.

          Chapter 5: Mission Statements
          Mission One: Hope
          Mission Two: The Miracle of Creation
          Mission Three: Progress is a Process
          Mission Four: Cats in the Dox Box
          Mission Five: This Equipment Has Not Been Tested
          Mission Six: Wounds of War

                  I have submitted some errata concerning the Terranorming section. As written, it's a six-step process:
                  1. Declaration: side what change you want to make (the Thoughtspace) and where you want to make it.
                  2. Recon: identify any deviations from the desired Thoughtspace. Essentially, this involves identifying reality Deviants who would actively opposed the change.
                  3. Repriming: dedicate a Node or a Venture to the Terranorming. The racing should reflect the size of the era that you are attempting to Terranorm.
                  4. Clearing: remove the obstacles identified in step two.
                  5. Holding: maintain the desired status quo for a length of time determined by the aforementioned node rating, from one month for a 1 Dot node to 50 years for a Five Dot node.
                  6. Capping: seal the change, so that it no longer needs the node or venture to persist.

                  There's also a box to the effect that this process was formalized by the technocracy, but is informally followed by everyone who engages in what the technocracy would call Terranorming.

                  The errata that I suggested involves a couple of things:

                  first, add a step to actually implement the change. This should include a page reference to M20 pp.616–617, which discusses the process of Paradigm shifting a reality zone. As written, the sequence doesn't include anything proactive; just making plans and clearing obstacles.

                  Second, make the repriming step optional. I can see it being harder to Terranorm without a node or Venture backing you up; which I would represent in game terms by having the holding time take longer (my suggestion is to calculate the time as one dot higher if you don't actually have any repriming); but it shouldn't be impossible.

                  My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
