        When Rome fell, most of the Ventrue elite abandoned the ruined city to establish new centers of power elsewhere.
        During the rise of the Merovingian dynasty, the Ventrue began infiltrating existing government as feudal lords, thus taking credit for the creation and expansion of the medieval feudal system.
        When the Holy Roman Empire was dispersed, the Ventrue tried to move their center of power to Italy again
        However, Rome was dominated by the Lasombra (as was much of the Church) so they had to establish centers of power elsewhere in Italy, in Venice, Milan, Genoa, and Firenze.
        Here, the Ventrue constantly opposed the Lasombra (and their proxies in the church and in Rome) in their quest for power, as their own early efforts had not succeeded.
        Posing as kings and merchant princes, the Ventrue expanded their influence across the Mediterranean through trade routes and crusades, thus becoming again a force to be reckoned with. During these times, many Ventrue were influenced by the laws of chivalry and personal honor.

        The Borgias' allies included powerful families like the Sforza and Colonna, foreign powers like Spain, and figures such as Niccolò Machiavelli, who advised Cesare; their influence also extended through papal connections, notably Pope Callixtus III and Pope Alexander VI, - they were basically Team Lasombra

                Originally posted by Shadeprowler View Post
                Vampire easly could have include the Givoanni Family as a Major player, rivaling the Borgias and the Medici - and possibly could have ended in a "triumvirate" of sort, and the area could have been rulled by the Borgia (Lasombra) - Medici (Venture)-Giovanni alliance
                The original Clanbook: Giovanni was excellent in many ways, but it really screwed up popular understanding of the clan. Their original description in the Players Handbook made it very clear they were a rich merchant family from Venice, the Most Serene Republic, and one of the classic maritime republics of Italy. They were northern Italians who had a lot of inspiration from the legends of the Borgias (who were in fact Spanish nobles and not originally from Italy). "Modern Day Borgias" (as said by someone on this board once) is a cool concept.

                The clanbook, however, depicted them as mainly a southern Italian organized crime family. There was no real understanding of Italian history, and how the different families would fit into that. Huge fail. It really needed a different main narrator who didn't sound like he was a casting call reject from Goodfellas, and instead used him as a secondary narrator reflecting the perspective of the Putanesca family instead. It's OK if they sound like a goomba from New Jersey. No Venetian patrician family member is going to sound like that.

                There are huge differences between northern and southern Italians. Between the Venetian Republic and the rest of Italy. Between rich merchant families and crime families that come from humble origins.

                It's not that Clan Giovanni doesn't have some ties to organized crime. It does - that is what the Putanesca family is for. It's just that the vast majority of mortal stock is from "respectable" society (with lots of scandalous skeletons in the closet).

                In the chronicles I run, I have the "Putanesca crime family" be one of the Five Families of New York, and they are responsible for handling anything illegal that the Clan needs done. However, they are looked down upon by all the other families in the clan.

                Each of the mortal families of Clan Giovanni obviously served some kind of purpose. The Giovanni themselves were obviously an important family of Venetian patricians. They were rich and powerful within the Republic, but as Venetians they could only have limited influence outside of the Republic. The Rossellini were an aristocratic family of the Papal States like the Orisini, Collona, Borghese, and Conti. They were papal nobility and were influential in electing the popes. They obviously provided Clan Giovanni with a different kind of influence than the Giovanni mortal family could do. The Della Passaglia and Ghiberti provided the means for the clan to spread west and east during the Age of Discovery (and were probably families from other Italian cities) just like bringing in the non-Italian minor families allowed the Clan to expand away from the Mediterranean. The Putanesca probably weren't even important enough to embrace until the late 19th century after Italy was united, and a lot of previously brought in mortal families were no longer as useful as they had been when Italy was divided. They may have been long term pawns for a while, but probably weren't brought into the clan as vampires until relatively late. There's a lot less difference between most of the Italian families of Clan Giovanni now than there would have been before Italian Unification.
