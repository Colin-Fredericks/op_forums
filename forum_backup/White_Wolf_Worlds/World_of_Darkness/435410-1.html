    Chakravanti? Sure, but remember that they're one of many groups during the DA period. The Euthanatos Tradition has not been created yet. In Europe in the period covered by the line, there are:
    # The Knights of St. Gregory of Nyssa (a recognized knightly order that serves as a cover for the worshippers off Radamanthys)
    # The Ixoi (they have not yet split into the Golden Chalice and Ksrafai)
    # The Pomegranate Deme and the The Aided (probably both Old Faith right now, but with a slightly different Foundation. The Aided are actually outcast from the main Old Faith, because they violate the pagan virtues. The Pomegranate Deme are protected by the Gregorian Knights).
    # The Valdaermen (part of the Fellowship is incorporated into the Euthanatos Tradition as the Gallowsmen)
    # The Pollottino family (a small family of "white necromancers who use Etruscan rituals," and enemies of the Giovanni family)
    The Chakravanti comprise the:
    # Lahksmists (followers of the divine principle of fortune)
    # Sapindya Sadananda (the Fellowship's warriors and assassins -- also known as the Consanguinity of Eternal Joy. Currently ruled by the necromancer Helekar).
    # Natatapas (your "average" Tantrist Chakravanti, somewhat matrirachal in outlook).
    We can give them all the same Foundation and Pillars, though.
    Foundation: Duty (Dharma)
    The Chakravanti believe that mystical power and enlightenment come through adherence to one's karmically ordained duty. Average people perform the duties mandated by their caste; Chakravanti have the onerous duty of correcting people and groups when they fall astray of that duty through corruption or by clinging to a distraction. When performed correctly, the Chakravat Duty is equated to the positions of the gods and beyond that, the uncreated power that exists beyond the gods. Thus, proper performance of Duty gives godlike attributes.
    Pillars: Lokha
    Lokha (worlds) are layers of existence that are both states of being and actual places that the soul (Atman) incarnates. There are six Lokha in total:
    # The demonic Lohka (Naraka)
    # The ghostly Lokha (Preta)
    # The animal Lokha (Triyagoni)
    # The human Lokha (Manu)
    # The Lokha of the warring demigods (Asura)
    # The divine Lokha (Deva)
    The human Lokha is already within the mage. It is practiced through adherence to Dharma. The Asura Lokha is a false path practiced by other supernatural beings. So the Pillars are:
    # Naraka: Punishment and destruction, cursing.
    # Preta: Necromancy
    # Triyagoni: Healing the body and controlling base desires.
    # Deva: Manipulating the fundamental forces of the universe (like Quintessence), taking in divine characteristics, blessings.

    for anyone interested in pillars for the modern euthanatos you could start with this and make what is missing

                        Originally posted by Possessed View Post

                        Not necessarily (and, in some cases, not at all). It is entirely possible for a given mage to have, say, the teleportation effect of Correspondence:4 *without* possessing the other aspects of the previous levels (individualized teleportation, mulit-vector spatial awareness -- hell, spatial awareness of any kind, for that matter -- the ability to scry locations, or any of several others) -- it just makes the Effect more dangerous. It also side-steps the point I'm trying to make, which I'll illustrate via other examples as -- I suspect -- my "oft-cited SoE" example (which was an ongoing topic for a number of people back in the 90's, on this forums-then incarnation, as well as several WoD-related conventions) is not illustrating the point very well, due perhaps to its (my) dated reference...

                        A specific example brought up at several conventions was how to simulate Star Trek-style transporters, as they most emphatically don't operate on the Correspondence:4 principles. I provided the definitive answer at two of the conventions: Life:4 (transforming/altering simple-to-complex living patterns), Matter:4 (ditto for non-living materials), Forces:5 (the energy transformation of said targets, as well as the force-fields involved that have demonstrated as being able to mitigate extreme outside energy interference on a number -- not all, but a number -- of occasions, including matter-anti-matter explosions) and Correspondence:2 (targeting a given location via sensors and micro-"pricking" of subspace to allow matter-energy stream transmission). That isn't Correspondence:4, not by a mile, even with the sliver-cut piece provided by Correspondence:2. I cite that because *that* fit the conceptual idea to a T, as well as fitting the broader paradigm of not only a great number of Technocratic paradigms, but a number of SoE characters, as well.

                        It's not a question of whether or not the Spheres, as presented, make metaphysical sense -- they do, absolutely. No question. The issue more comes about that as presented, they are just another paradigm-expression for how the concepts listed can be expressed or delineated. Correspondence, at lower levels, provides scrying of localities (C-2). Not all mages will have that in their paradigm (Messianic Voices, for example, in any direct form), but they may have teleportation (Gavri-El:4, and applying to the character, himself) anyway, via a different metaphysical means. Depending on the fluff, this may make the teleportation more dangerous, and/or require certain circumstances (use of sad ability in the service of God, as opposed to more base uses), or conversely simply "work" via spiritual guidance, therefore safe-guarding against mishap (and, again, not requiring previous levels/abilities indicated by Correspondence). On a related note, it can also be that the ability to teleport, say, others (something doable with C-4) also isn't included, as presented, in someone's paradigm (as is the case, again, with the Voices), but rather requires additional support from another pillar, based on a metaphysical concept which, again, is *not* related to C-4 (in this case, the Messianic would apply Gavri-El:4s teleportation to another via Mika-El:2, for a willing/unconscious target, or Mika-El:4 to compel the target's presence -- to or away; in that regard, it's the underlying ability to apply conceptual command to a situation that allows Gavri-El:4 to teleport another, *not* any additional principle as laid out in Correspondence:4...). The result may be the same, but the means-and-methods are quite different, both by fluff and by game mechanics (the crunch).

                        That's the point.

                        Ascensions Spheres operate on the underlying principle that *every* aspect of *every* Sphere is discernible and achievable in each Traditions paradigm(s), and that they are ultimately achieved (by relation) by way of the same "degree" and effectiveness (relative to how the Spheres are presented/delineated).

                        Bullshit.

                        Complete and utter bullshit.

                        *That* conceptual error is why so many questions came up so often in the 90s (and since) relative to "but my mage's magic doesn't work that way... and it doesn't work that way in [fill in blank of movie/book/tv show/what-have-you] that I am trying to simulate... yet this whole "Paradigm" thing keeps getting pushed hard... but I can't seem to do "xyz" with the Spheres, as presented...", which I can personally vouch for as having shown up on the forums at the top and bottom of the hour. But you *can* simulate [ ] much more closely by making a set of Pillars to reflect more accurately the metaphysical paradigm in the character's/player's mind(s). *Much* more closely.

                        Some aspects, for some mages, simply aren't as easy as the Spheres indicate (a proto-SoE from the *proper* Steampunk Era -- which wouldn't be the late Victorian, as some misperceive, but from the late 1600's thru the 1700's, during Enlightenment -- would, per my write-up for a friend, use Dynamics, Odyllics and Synergies to simulate teleportation, as such would truly be more difficult that via just a single Sphere/Pillar, alone, for them). On the flip (and more on-point for many mages), there are a number of Effects that are *easier* to work than indicated via the Spheres : my character, Andreas, has a natural penchant for perceptual/divinatory magics, and his Clairsentience Pillar has a range from "just a touch" to "a nice slice" of aspects that would require a number of Spheres to simulate... but he doesn't have any of the other aspects associated with those Spheres, which would be "out-of-paradigm" for him, contextually speaking, so for example if he wanted to work a divination that let him know mystical powers were at work (Prime:1) at several of his/his friends homes (Correspondence:3), whether or not those forces game from this or the other side of the Gauntlet (Spirit:1-2, depending on areas), and whether those forces were going to have a detrimental influence on him/them (Time:2 w/ Entropy:1, the latter helping make clearer his visions of the future) and what their intentions were (Mind:3), that would require 12 dots in Spheres (and, within the context of Ascension's designed paradigm, that would be correct. *However*, for Andreas, he can accomplish that with Clairsentience:3. (Of course, said Pillar doesn't let him "do" anything, actively, except in rarer instances... but then, that's the point of the Pillar: it's utility, in Andreas' paradigm, is the considerable awareness and understanding it provides/he achieves through it, not whether it lets him "do" anything, actively, as such...).

                        *THAT* is how the concepts in Ascensions Spheres are *properly* manifested through the various paradigms: the overall concepts/Effects are recognizable, but their implementation (including the "color of methodology" actually resulting in a "different game mechanic", in a number of Paradigms) is often very, very different. The Pillars go a long, long way towards having both fluff *and* game mechanics reflect this.

                                    The long and short of it is that Foundations and Pillars are really great if you can keep down the number of different options open as character choices down. Then you can plumb the depths of a paradigm and intricately and specifically tailor the rules and the paradigm together.

                                    But, when you try to have Foundations and Pillars replace Spheres, if you look at the constituency in any Tradition or Convention, you're going to need multiple different combinations of multiple different Foundations and multiple different Pillars to accurately portray the membership of these trans-ethnic global sodalities that were brought together by some spectrum of shared interests, a few similarities in outlook and a lot of politics and accidents of history. Moreover, some Foundations and some Pillars are going to cross-over between sub-groups that parallel each other in completely different Traditions and Conventions.

                                    Or else you just need to pull out the spackle gun, aim at all the different subgroups and twist the setting dial over to "Euthanatos" or "Verbena" or "Iteration X" and then glom together a wide variety of very different groups together under one onerous Foundation/Pillar system. Why onerous? Because you are going to be erasing all the important distinctions (i.e. paradigms) between the sub-groups by doing so and, thereby, loosing the thing that the Fellowship Foundation/Pillar system does SO WELL, integrating paradigms and mechanics.

                                    There are some things that the Foundation/Pillar system does VERY WELL, but there are some things the Sphere system does better. Not being able to access every possible power mentioned in each and every dot of a Sphere because you can't manage a way to wrap your paradigm about it isn't a weakness of the Sphere system. It's a part of dealing with the limitations inherent in a character and every character is going to have limitations. It's just not a thing written into the Sphere rules as explicitly as it is in the Foundation/Pillar rules. With Spheres, that sort of limitation has to be negotiated on a case-by-case basis by the gaming group in question. Which also happens with the Foundation/Pillar system. (As we have CERTAINLY seen threads about "How do I do this?" in DA Mage magic). And, in a larger sense, is the thing that really sets all flavors of Mage apart from other most games.

                                                            My own approach to this issue is to riff off of the Techcratic Sphere variants. Yes, the Traditions have a set of nine Spheres that, in theory, any mage can learn and use; but as I see it, many (most?) of these Spheres are so widely applicable because they’re compromises, designed by committee to more or less fit as many of the Traditions’ various paradigms as possible. However, each Tradition has its own ideas of what a given Sphere ought to be like, which doesn’t always line up with how that Sphere is normally taught. Those Traditions have developed customized variants of those Spheres where they think that the “generic” version missed the point. That’s where the Machine Sphere came from: the notion that the Sons of Ether and Iteration X needed something that was less about chemistry and more about mechanics.

                                                            I tend to avoid completely restructuring the organizing principle of the Nine Spheres, at least as far as the Traditions go; every Sphere variant stands in for one of the existing Spheres. And I try to limit each Tradition to two or three Sphere variants, while having them use regular Spheres for everything else. Heck, even their custom Spheres aren’t universally used by their membership; though the extent to which the custom spheres replace the generic ones varies from Tradition to Tradition. My rationale is that the Traditions are a sect that was founded on finding common ground; and the Nine Spheres form that common ground. The Technocracy likewise uses a very similar set of Spheres, but with their own technomantic tweaks to them.

                                                            OTOH, I’m all for different organizing principles when it comes to the Disparates. For instance, I have no problem with the notion that the Sisters of Hypollyta study the Seasons instead of the Spheres; and I’d be perfectly fine with the Templars calling on Archangels instead of wielding Spheres. I suspect that with a little effort, every set of Pillars described in Dark Ages: Mage could find a home with a modern Disparate faction. They are, after all, Disparates; being at odds with the way the Traditions do things is what they do. And Disparates that don’t practice DAMage Pillars may well practice their own Pillars. One wonders what the Wu Lung Pillars might look like…

                                                            I’ve also toyed with the notion of replacing Arete with Foundations, with most Traditions and Crafts getting thier own Foundation. (In keeping with their “One True Way” mentality, Technocrats all share a common Foundation: Enlightenment.) For this notion, I scrap the “Tenth Sphere” talk as a setting element and instead look to what the published material has to say about Tenth Spheres as inspiration for devising the various Traditions’ Foundations — in a manner of speaking, the Foundation a mage uses is his “Tenth Sphere”.
