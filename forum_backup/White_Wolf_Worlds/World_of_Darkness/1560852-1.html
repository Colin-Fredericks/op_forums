                    Originally posted by Tjalei View Post
                    Some Nosferatu never go to the surface. An isolated community may see some language drift over time. Though they live indefinitely so I'd be very slow. Perhaps some of them speak really old versions of modern languages though, including long forgotten regional dialects
                    Quite a lot of language drift can happen, they're not an isolated community: that's defined by a lack of contact with others, but the Nosferatu have such contact by nature and necessity, with other vampires and with mortals.

                    The fact some never leave doesn't change this status: they may be isolated individuals, but the community isn't, and they still talk with their peers.

                    But the notion holds, not despite that, because of that. As their own communities, Nosferatu is the most likely clan to produce an independent, internal language spoken in the warrens. This would borrow freely from other languages, but adapt to its own system. Exchange don't make languages necessarily more mutually intelligible, nor necessarily kills old words.

                    In this scenario, the Aranta-Shadur could as a word have developed at any point in time, from any combination of languages, and still be part of a stable lexicon unique to the Nos.

                    For such a scenario, I think the cultural spread would be similar to modern Arabic: you have a Standard language centered around important written records that are kept untranslated for this very reason, and local variants that may be barely mutually intelligible, but preserve a core lexicon and structure.

                    More importantly, the untranslated texts mean that everyone is formally educated in Standard form and have ample written use for it, so it do work as a Lingua Franca.

                        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                        Whatever people spoke this language in the past, if they're even human, the Nosferatu met them because of living in caves and the like.
                        That's an interesting idea. We even have canonical candidates (like Vhujunka) in the larger WoD setting although their identity i's certainly not limited to those candidates. Of course, none of that would ever come into play unless the ST decided on a very unusual chronicle. But as flavor text, being told that the term supposedly came from "little people", "morlocks", "dwellers from the earth," or whatever term is used for the underground people would have a very different feel than that is was some dead language spoken north of Sumeria.

                        Of course, if the term does come from some underground creatures/people, I feel the ST should create some additional terms for aspects of Nosferatu specific culture as I think such an encounter would unlikely to have left just this one term.

                        It also provides the option for the ST to create additional evidence of the language or the underground people that the Nosferatu clan would be interested in collecting. As unlike a dead language thousands of years long gone, the underground people could still exist, and the Nosferatu might have reason to try to resume communication.

                        I don't know if I would personally go down this route for my chronicles, but it is an intriguing option.

                            - A breached website user/password list from the dark web (computer use)
                            - The security pass of a low-level employee (impersonation)

                            - Blueprints of public Building X (resource access)

                            - A random package from the customs office (interception)

                            - Live video stream of an Elysium's parking lot (surveillance)
                            - A trichobezoar (rare item acquisition)
                            - A metal item from a magpie's nest (Animalism)
                            - A flaked-off piece of flag blue pool coating (trolling)
                            - Spare high voltage resistor from a substation (sabotage)
                            - Recording of a number station transmission this night (coms)
                            - Screenshot of comments/reposts on a planted ridiculous fake news article (misinfo)
                            - A prepared specimen from a museum collection (artifact extraction)

                            I think placing the items in places a Nosferatu would need access to semi-regularly makes sense as a means of maintenance; public registries, the morgue, hospital, etc.
                            Reading back on my list, it's very dry. There is probably a way to add more fun twists while keeping the practicality.

                                    More good suggestions for the list. Thanks very much!

                                    Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
                                    I also think the examples of the Clan Book are really tall orders, especially infiltrating the Havens of (possibly) multiple kindred twice
                                    I thought so as well. I do think that is something much easier with fairly young fledglings and neonates than older Kindred. Havens tend to be fairly simple at first, but get more elabaorate as characters (and players) gain more experience and resources. I imagine most Kindred targets of the scavenger hunts are these much younger, less powerful, and less experienced Kindred.

                                    Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
                                    Reading back on my list, it's very dry. There is probably a way to add more fun twists while keeping the practicality.
                                    I think most of the items for the scavenger hunt should probably be dry. After all, almost every brood in the world will be having such hunts at least once a year. Sheer repetition will result in a lot of mundane items. However, the ST will want some some more interesting/tailored items that appear on a list to enliven the experience overall, or to serve as an interesting scene or plothook.

                                    I think even having a large list of mundane, dry items will help people craft more interesting variations.

                                    For example, I think a lot can be done with extracting a museum piece in storage (not on display since that would be too quickly noticed). Lots of variations can be done with the type of artifact, the organization which has it, and its actual physical location in storage. Likewise a specific kind of animal which may not be native to region or domesticated. You can involve zoos, exotic pet stores, invasive species, farms, mansions that have exotic pets, etc. If the clan elders knoww there is only one boa constrictor over ten feet long in the city, not only is such a target interesting, it'll create competition.

                                    Another easy variant is to assign different points to certain items than others. It would call out their perceived rarity or importance of developing that skill. There might even be some kind of handicap system so less skilled Nosferatu are encouraged to participate (and thus gain practical experience). Thus even the newly released neonate has a chance against a skilled aniclla.

                                    Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
                                    From an in-game perspective though, I like the 'deep ones language' idea.
                                    I just realized that the Clanbooks also detail the level six Discipline power Song of the Dark which can summon forgotten or mysterious underground creatures that tend towards fantasy/horror/legend. Tying in the term aranta-shadur to creatures that get occasionally summoned to warrens by use of that elder power preserves a sense of continuity.

                                                    Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                                                    wait a minute, what if it’s something of a tradition for Nosferatu elders to create ‘time capsules’ towards which they can point future participants in the aranta-shadur? (In a generation or three).
                                                    This is another interesting idea, and I like monteparnas comments on it.

                                                    However, I am having a hard time developing it into good specifics for play. Details matter because they turn interesting ideas into either exciting or disappointing scenes during actual play.

                                                    First, if this is just "find the time capsule" then is it a matter of figuring out clues and riddles to its location? Is that the challenge? (Presumably the clan elders know their precise location and monitor them to make sure they can be found in 20 or 30 or 50 years time or whatever the time length is.)


                                                    Second, what should the the time capsule be carrying? I'm of the firm opinion that must time capsules (in real life) just have useless junk in them. What contents could they have that would be of any interest to the Nosferatu? Historical information isn't likely to be of use to the Nosferatu. If there is useful information in it, it should probably be preserved in a way that is always accessible. So these would probably just be curiosity items that might have some relevance in understanding the history of the warren, but not in any way that may jeopardize the Masquerade or warren security. So tokens of mementos of previous (and possibly still existing) Nosferatu of the warren. And remember that a long standing member of the warren will see these same items multiple times over their existence as a vampire. These mementos should be something they enjoy perusing again and again, but not so much that they'd prefer to keep it with them instead of in a capsule. In this scenario, while one Nosferatu (or group in a team effort style hunt) will get bragging rights to having found it, going over the capsule is probably meant as a bonding ritual for the local Brood as ancillae and elders explain what the various bits mean.

                                                    At least that is how I am currently thinking about it. Interested in other opinions.

                                                        I think the mementos is the way to go. The objects are just curious at best, but going through the capsule the elders tell their history, which is more valuable.

                                                        They could include mementos from members of the Warren who met Final Death, or who left for somewhere else. Collective mementos from events or periods that affected everyone. Mementos brought, along with their history, from other Warrens. And things from other Vampires as an excuse to talk about them and their secrets.

                                                        Finding them could involve some clue or riddle, but the real challenge is to find out what happened to the capsule: was something built atop it? Is it now a military base? Who lives in that house today? Was it moved? Was it found? Is it now in a museum? What if it is out of immediate reach? The Nos may take care to avoid this, sure, but they may also want to see how the participants figure out ways to bring it back or present an alternative that counts. It would be entirely within the spirit of Vampire that the Elders expect the younger vampire to manipulate events and arrange things so the bearer of the time capsule will be in town with it by the time of the Aranta-Shadur.
