    The True apocalipse in WOD

    07-10-2023, 07:50 PM
    I think it's even strange to talk about a "true apocalypse" in WOD considering that in the books several scenarios are offered for each game, however I believe that these stories all happen in the same passage of time in the same world and despite everything these events must collide at some point, I mean, one doesn't necessarily stop the other. What I mean is that if an apocalypse were to happen as a canonical event, what scenario(s) do you believe would happen and what would each kind of supernatural look like after all that. I can only think of the most tragic, but optimistic ending for this last one actually. Anyway, how do you think the world of WOD ended?

                      Originally posted by Psicose View Post

                      If you're okay with theories, let's start with a question, what's the difference between the Garou and Mages views of Umbra?
                      W:tA Umbra: Penumbra extends throughout the universe as a mirror of physical reality. The Deep Umbra refers to another, stranger place beyond a second Gauntlet that's kind of like physical reality in some ways, but way, way weirder.

                      M:tAs Umbra: Penumbra is more of an Earth-local thing. The Deep Umbra is everything after the asteroid belt. Nothing beyond that is outside of the spirit world. The Horizon is also there, somewhere. Aliens are spirits and vice versa, depending on your point of view, never mind that the structure of the Umbra has some bits that don't change by vidare (you always need Entropy and Spirit to enter the Dark Umbra, even if you believe that any kind of Umbral travel is through "the Underworld").

                      Both fall out of basic assumptions of the games. In Mage, humans are the most important things in the universe and the structure of the Umbra pretty much has a limited physical reality around the Earth as a security blanket to humanity. In Werewolf, Gaia is the most important thing in the universe, and spans farther than human imagination can really reach, hence the fully physical universe with the Penumbra of the Middle Umbra as the soul of the world and the Deep Umbra as a reflection of what's really going on (Malfeas, the snarl where the Wyrm was trapped in the pattern web by the Weaver, is in the Deep Umbra rather than between it and the Penumbra as other "Near Realms" are).

                      These two perspectives are not reconcilable. Mage is too tangled up even on its own terms to offer a means to it, though since M20, there have been efforts to try and build more bridges for crossover, such as with the "Mythic Threads" explanation for why disbelief kind of just bounces off Garou and Fera but can really mess up a Sphere effect.

                                                  If I was going to do a coherent singular apocalypse for a long-running campaign, it'd probably have to be one that is entirely homebrew. I don't use any of the metaplot at all. It's just declared non-canon at my game table. In other words, if you play in a game I'm ST'ing, assume that WoD5 and most of Revised just flat out didn't happen at all. It's a lot closer to the 2e version of WoD in all its gonzo glory, with some of the hindsight from the X20 lines thrown in. Yeah, I'm a sucker for Classic 90's WoD and I liked how V20 had that perfect balance of being a metaplot-agnostic toolkit.

                                                  BUT if I were to run an apocalyptic event, then there's nothing that says I couldn't crib elements from the various Time of Judgment books that I do like.

                                                  Vampire will probably require a custom scenario that's not in the Gehenna book at all, but maybe it can borrow a few specific moments from each of the scenarios like the Shaper (or any other "Caitiff Antediluvian" who never sired) or the Gangrel Antediluvian trying to meld with the Earth.

                                                  With Werewolf, I'd go with a mix between A Tribe Falls and my own custom scenario ideas that involve one last grand battle against the Wyrm and the Weaver. The entirety of the Gaian Fera finally reunite and put aside their differences for the very last battle. The Triat is defeated (even the Wyld falls in the chaos of battle) but Gaia is alive and on top. All Fera go extinct but the souls of the Gaian Garou are reincarnated in the Summer Country of the Umbra, forever protected and cared for by Gaia.

                                                  Now, Mage is the most straightforward of the main three. I'd just use the Judgment scenario from the Ascension book with only a few minor tweaks.

                                                  When we get into the other gamelines and the Time of Judgment book, then it gets a little tricky....

                                                  Changeling: I'm just gonna go with a variant of Starlight Exodus. The Kithain and all the other Fae all retreat to the Dreaming at the same time all of humanity awakens during the events of Judgment. Mage and Changeling get their scenarios happening simultaneously and they also happen to be the very last ones to happen.

                                                  Kindred of the East: I'm gonna be honest, I never liked KOTE all that much. Or at least not the Kuei-Jin in and of themselves. There's a lot of elements within the line I liked but retconning the Kuei-Jin was probably the one good call V5 actually made. I'm not even going to comment on the unfortunate levels of Orientalism within the game as a whole.

                                                  But if I had to pick a scenario to "canonize", I'm gonna go with The Trumpet of Mount Meru and have a similar scenario not only apply to the Shih, but also Strike Force Zero and all of the Western hunter groups such as the Society of Leopold, the FBI Special Affairs Department, the Arcanum, the Orphic Circle, et cetera. Hell, I'd even allow the less prominent mortal hunter groups like the Young Bloods MC or the Mafia be eligible for these grand endowments of power. Basically, all these mortal hunter groups get what can best be described as "Numina on Steroids, Meth, and PCP", flip the fuck out, and start hunting down every single Kindred, Risen, Fera (be it Gaian or Wyrmish), Demon, Mage, Mummy, what have you.

                                                  This would be the very first scenario to kick off the Time of Judgment and every single event would follow from that.

                                                  Hunter: I liked Hunter: The Reckoning and even got to play a one-shot of it a couple of years ago, but Imbued only make sense with the Sixth Great Maelstrom taken into account with the rest of the WoD setting. Unlike the original five WoD games, Reckoning is tied with the metaplot from the very get-go. Just stick to Hunters Hunted and the other Mortals books and invoke the reimagined "Trumpet of Mount Meru" scenario, though if you do want the Imbued thrown in the mix, have that same event give all Imbued five-dot Edges and maximum Conviction as part of the last battles, joining the more organized hunters in battle. I could cook up an alternate HTR that doesn't involve the Sixth Great Maelstrom but that's gonna be on the backburner for a while.

                                                  Mummy: Never played Mummy nor read it, so I can't comment.

                                                  Demon: I have read Demon, have not played it, and I don't really want to. Much like how the Kuei-Jin are kind of squicky with their Orientalism, Demon is very squicky on a religious level and this applies to damn near every religion past or present once you factor in the infamous Earthbound book. Even when you account for the more revisionist Milton-esque view of Lucifer presented in Demon, I still ain't touching that shit with a ten foot pole so I am just gonna move on...

                                                  Wraith: I don't know much about Wraith, have never played it, but unlike Demon, Reckoning, or Mummy, I'd like to actually try it out one day and the concept itself is interesting. I've never read Ends of Empire but I do know that it's the book that kicks off the Sixth Great Maelstrom which gives us Demon: The Fallen and Hunter: The Reckoning. Once I get a better feel for Wraith and learn more about its setting, I'll give Ends of Empire a look and see if I can cook up a modified version of Ends of Empire or an entirely new scenario altogether.
