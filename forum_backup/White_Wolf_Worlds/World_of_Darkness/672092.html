              Actually played through most of the Sam Haight-related adventures (with the Sabbat pack of when we "started" at A Quest Beyond Death, to boot) and honestly never felt like he was a heavy-hitter so much as a enterprising - and sometimes amazingly crap throwing - adventurer/troublemaker.                       I've recently used some major Vampire: The Masquerade NPCs in passing in my current game (Smiling Jack taking part in an arms deal with some anarchs, Tremere appearing in a vision) and the players had a solid "oh crap" reaction to them - but they also do that to the local DIY NPC elders. It's probably a healthy response either way.

                      I've also played the W20 adventure The Skinner (as run by Stew Wilson at our local convention) and the "oh crap, Sam Haight could come back" effect really worked. Sure we made some jokes, but the adventure's a great example of selling such a character as a credible threat, emphasising how dangerous and horrible he could be without showing off the OTT power set.

                              I used vykos and some of the big cammies like hardestat in Tranaylvania by Night Dark ages chronicle. Gave them each big speeches and counter arguments during the Convention of Thorns. Had Hardestat and the Anarchs in a heated face off where H threw down an open challenge to the upstarts to do anything that he couldnt defeat of do better.

                              To which Vykos cut off his own genitalia and threw it at Hardestat, hit him in the face too. The players cheered just as assassins then shot H in the chest and ran as he got up.

                              It went very loud to very quiet.

                              I've used God45 in a hunter campaign. You lnow the players havent read the books when he's telling them what's going down and they think he knows what is going on and has a solid grip on what needs doing.

                              They all started to get a little worried, which increased further and higher with them actually asking him for things to do to help and then seeing the aftermath. You could almost see in the players eyes the doubt as their minds drowned in the uncertainty of ethics and beliefs in the wake of their acts.

                              it was finally a quiet calm end as one player read through a book and asked mid session and asked "is God, God45?...this God45 right here...our God?!" I said yes and the players all looked at each other..as their pcs decided to walk away... and never trust anyone else ever again...

                              I cant think why?

                              Albrect has been used. I made him and burnt out who only rose the occassion in the last moment possible. PCs were like 'this is the cost of being Hero?... whoa!' Which was unexpected but I liked it.

                              Carpenter I've used as a creepy messenger. Often a public telephone will ring just as a pc will walk past it and its always the same wheezing maniacal misery stricken bipolar wraith with news on what is about to happen somewhere. Giving the pcs a conundrum or needs for action or a warning thats a set up.

                                  The heaviest of 'heavy hitters' that I've used in just a story (that is, not a specific ramping-up-to-Gehenna story) was... Günter Dorn, author of Das Ungeheuer Darin. However, I decided that such a character, being a complete cypher without anything attributed to him beyond that book, needed more than just a name. So, I gave him more, a lot more in fact.

                                  In my game, Dorn was the nom de plume of Caine himself. The player characters in our Dark Ages game wound up wandering across Transylvania with this vampire, not thinking anything about him, good or bad. That aura of perfect non-threatening, absolutely what he looks like, nothing weird at all around 'Dorn' is how I imagine the Mark of Cain(e) working, anyway.

                                  ...Oh, we've had methuselae, even one or two possible Antediluvians show up, too, but those tales were supposed to be BIG affairs. The players in my troupe still don't know that they met Caine.

                                      I never use any of the signature NPC's from Revised or even a lot of the 2e ones either, preferring instead to make my own NPC's (I don't like the metaplot and the problem is that characters like Vykos, Beckett, Lucita, Anatole, and the like are often heavily tied to the metaplot, so it's hard to use them in a chronicle that does not use said metaplot), although I have used some of the 1e heavy-hitters from Chicago by Night before. It surprisingly went well for me. The players ended up supporting Capone and Annabelle, and were surprisingly respectful to Prince Lodin (before betraying him and setting up Annabelle as the new Prince). One of the players, a Malkavian played by my youngest brother even convinced Capone to turn his back on Lodin and support Annabelle and the players' plot to depose Lodin and set Annabelle up as Prince.
