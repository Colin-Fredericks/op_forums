                    Honestly this is going to dip into a bit of my personal philosophy on World of Darkness game theory, so, this won't apply to everyone and sorry if it seems somewhat off-topic but recent discussions with the Metis and how-to WtA made me think of it.

                    •The first thing that I would say is that if my players weren't comfortable with the Metis' Thrall of The Wyrm (ToTW) for any reason I would probably end up giving all garou some mix of Homid-Lupus ToTW - unthinking ferocity, bloodlust, and cannibalism.

                    We're all here to play a game and I think it's an easy thing to tweak and if it helps my players enjoy the game then that's what matters most and I think that Session 0 discussions of limits and expectations are useful for the hobby and it's community.

                    •The second thing and this is more about WoD and WtA games specifically then just the matter of Session 0-game theory and the Thrall of the Wyrm is this: I see two major styles of running games/stories in the World of Darkness setting although there are certainly a vast number of nuances.

                    The first method and the one which I feel MyWifeIsScary illustrates with his post above is what I'll call 'The Gothic Story'. This style of game embraces the monstrosity and horror of the setting and seeks to embody the horror of the setting. These are the stories in which you play the sort of things that the lore tells us are the status quo of the setting. Scheming vampires, ultra-conservative werewolves, arrogant and schismatic mages; etc. This is an exploration of the WoD setting as it it, its standard portrayal, and I think it's a very interesting method.

                    It let's the players truly explore depths of monstrosity and the psychology of horror from within as well as a look at the World of Darkness setting as it is. I think it's a valid style of play and one that can be extremely fulfilling.

                    It's obviously not the only one however.

                    The second method is what I'll call 'The Punk Story'. These stories take the World of Darkness setting as the status quo and then proceed to draw the players in to flip that on its head.

                    These are the stories of punkish rebellion. These are the stories of radicals, idealists, marytrs, and revolutionaries striving to make the world a better place. These are vampires seeking change from within or calling for revolution. These are werewolves adapting to modernity and overturning harmful systems. These are the mages who cast aside partisanship in order to enlighten humanity and changelings who choose co-existence over tyranny. This is exploration of the WoD setting in perhaps a more archetypical narrative model but still a very compelling and fulfilling one, in which we see the possibility (and importantly) the realization of change for the better.

                    This allows a game in which the player characters truly are heroes, however flawed. A story that allows the players an opportunity to change the setting and embrace all the consequences of that change, good and bad. Furthermore it illustrates a strong message for use in our own lives about the power people do have to bring about change in the world.

                    The Punk Method is just as valid as The Gothic Method. I say that neither can be called objectively superior then the other and that as every player and troupe is different, every table will take these methods their own way and use them with their own spin.

                    But I think that it's important to consider both avenues from a meta-perspective as valid ways for how to play the game and his consideration of either approach can help us encourage others and enrich not only their experiences, but our own.

                        (for clarity, I do ask players if there's shit they really don't want to deal with in the game. I'm not that much of an asshole)

                        I think players should rage against the machine, but the punk, for me, works best when you first establish just how shit everything is. In the session 0 i'll usually go over a little of some things they know and some things they would've been taught to try to help . In a book i was writing i put in a little tidbit advising players that they try to roll with it for the first few sessions whilst they get their bearings.

                        If players just roll into the game guns blazing, fighting the power, as if they'd read up on how Caitiff/Metis/orphans were secretly the best and are unjustly oppressed by jealous conservatives, it doesn't have anywhere near the weight of actually experiencing the world as one of it's denizens before railing against it; Turning on the Camarilla is much cooler than starting the game as an open anarch, also PCs who bide their time within the system tend to fair way better than characters who start without it.

                        (also to me part of the punk experience is having the option of going full fascist and playing the boot for the face. Nothing is more anti-establishment than being the establishment and a blood sucking vampire/Rageaholic/Technocrat/Sidhe)
