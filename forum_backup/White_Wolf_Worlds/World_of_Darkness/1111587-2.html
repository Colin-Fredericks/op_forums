    If I'm running a game then my focus is on that game. However. I will take elements from other games to use as I require, so there is a Camarilla and a Sabbat and Anarchs when I'm running Werewolf or Mage but odds are I give them little consideration unless it impacts on the story or character actions. The same in any other combination too, my Technocracy is monolithic and controls much but is far from as omniescent as my players feel when they are playing Mage than when they are Camarilla neonates. I also tend to reuse a few settings like the fictional cities of Severnford and Hecates Peace with some reoccurring NPCs and events of previous stories and chronicles also colouring the setting in later games. Actual crossover happens infrequently and then in the form of antagonists.

        I run games in a unfied WoD but as the different supernaturals naturally tend to avoid each other crossings are not necessarily that common but they do happen. There are several more examples of such rather official connections between the lines so I feel any claim for the lines to be seperate requires a huge amount of suspencion of disbelief to work. But like it often is with these things YMMV.

                        Certainly not in Revised. The 20th Anniversary editions didn't walk back all that much from it, either.

                        And not just because I say so, but because White Wolf, who own the creative rights on these IPs has decided so. You can disagree with them, but since they own the IP rights it's them that must choose what is true and what is not true, and they decided the the lines are merged in one world.
                        The Storyteller's Handbooks for Revised disagree flat out; for Werewolf's piece on why it doesn't get along with Mage, see page 199 of its respective ST Handbook title, and keep reading. It quite plainly uses the term "in strictest Werewolf cosmology", implying that, line by line, they're each separate settings unto their own. The crossover section in general starts on page 195 and also touches on having to change a lot of things if you're going for different moods from different games, like reducing the chances of Garou frenzying and killing their friends in a somewhat lighter (or, at the very least, less brutal) Changeling game

                        Also, nuWW is making a setting that probably isn't going to resemble what was in past books very much. They've stated their intention to break apart canon and declare past events as "unreliable narration", and that's ultimately what you have to do to weld disparate games together - break apart or ignore anything that contradicts, make choices about what is and isn't right. That does not end up as a single, intelligible canon setting. That's a collection of elements with some assembly required. Not as much as GURPS, perhaps, but the more you add on, the more time it takes to construct a group's setting, and the less you'll find yourself actually using. Even nuWW can't fit consensus reality with animistic reality together, no matter what magic words of god it tries to invoke.
