    questions about V20, W20 and M20

    09-29-2015, 11:18 PM
    I know the 20th Anniversary product line is a version of cWOD that is set in the current decade, but the events of Time of Judgment never happened. Some people have said the 20th Anniversary books have no metaplot, but that can't be entirely true since the Mage books introduce a new faction (Threat Null) and one of the Werewolf books brings back Samuel Haight.

    Are any of the following metaplot events still part of V20/W20/M20 history: the Sixth Great Maelstrom, the Avatar Storm, the death of the Ravnos Antediluvian, the decimation of the Ravnos clan, the appearance of the Red Star, the death of Baba Yaga, the lifting of the Assamite's curse, the Gangrel leaving the Camarilla, the Stargazers leaving the Garou Nation, the destruction of the Tal'Mahe'Ra, the birth of the Perfect Metis, the defeat of the Sabbat in New York, the Kuei-jin invasion of America and the destruction of the Anarch Free State?

    If the Sixth Great Maelstrom never happened in V20, do any of the books explain where the Harbingers of Skulls came from?

    Do any of the books mention the Imbued? Hunter the Reckoning has always been my favorite cWOD game and I've always wanted to know what they've been up to since 2004.

    Which characters from the old books get mentioned in the new books (besides Samuel Haight)?

    I've heard that By Night Studio's MET Vampire and Werewolf books take a different approach to continuity than the 20th Anniversary series. Which metaplot events are part of its history and which characters have been mentioned in these books?

          Like Nist said, the *20 books are very good about either making something multiple choice or settling on one as default and giving you the options in a sidebar or something. Let me do a quick dive in my copies to try to find some specific answers so you get a sense of how the books treat them.. From just the core books:

          Originally posted by VoidMaster View Post
          the Avatar Storm
          Full page and a half of options, nothing really nailed down as default.
          the appearance of the Red Star
          Not mentioned in any of the books.
          the Sixth Great Maelstrom
          M20 defaults to it didn't happen but talks about options where it did happen and they rebuilt, it's still happening, etc.
          the death of the Ravnos Antediluvian
          the decimation of the Ravnos clan
          the lifting of the Assamite's curse
          the Gangrel leaving the Camarilla
          All four of these get a sidebar called "canonical changes" with Assamites and Ravnos getting a fuller description and Gangrel (and Malkavians) getting a mention for having changed over time.
          the destruction of the Tal'Mahe'Ra
          They're still around, though hardly mentioned. They have an entire book on the way, IIRC.
          the defeat of the Sabbat in New York
          A cursory mention in an unrelated section defaults to New York being Sabbat controlled. Otherwise not mentioned.
          the destruction of the Anarch Free State?
          V20 mentions the Free State once, doesn't mention the destruction of it.
          the Stargazers leaving the Garou Nation
          Stargazers default to being in the Garou Nation in W20
          the Kuei-jin invasion of America
          the death of Baba Yaga
          the birth of the Perfect Metis
          Nothing about these three are mentioned.
          If the Sixth Great Maelstrom never happened in V20, do any of the books explain where the Harbingers of Skulls came from?
          V20 plays up their origin as a complete mystery.

          I hope you find these examples illuminating. The books have had to nail -some- stuff down as the default in that edition, and some of them might be slightly anachronistic from a metaplot standpoint - Malkavians using Dementation instead of Dominate while Assamites still being under their curse being the prime example I remember - but all of it is easily changeable and the books usually go out of their way to illustrate the different possibilities if it's an important story point.

                          M20 includes huge amounts of metaplot, but it's largely confined to sidebars ("Future Fates"). So the Avatar Storm is optional, for example, with various options on incorporating it into your game. These options are one or more versions of "These things will happen", "These things might happen", and "These things may never happen". Future Fates sidebars cover things like the Ascension War, Doissietep's destruction, Heylel's return, the victory of the Technocratic paradigm, post-millennial developments like 9/11, the collapse of the Horizon realms, the Reckoning and the Week of Nightmares, the Avatar Storm, the Spirit-Nuke, the spread of cell-phones, the collapse of the Council of the Nine Traditions, the Sphinx and the Rogue Council, the reformed Technocracy, Nephandic infiltration of the Union, Threat Null, and the treachery of the Hollow Ones. These are covered in the first couple of hundred pages of the book. As you'll see, most of Mage's major metaplot developments are optional in M20, allowing the ST to set the dials to suit their own needs and desires .

                                      That said, some *20 books introduce their own completely new plot developments, though these seem to also be optional. V20 Rites of Blood, for instance, introduced the Montmartre Pact, which altered the established history of Clan Tremere so that, in order to enter the Camarilla in the first place, they had to free all their Gargoyles and swear off the creation of any more. This book also presented the first real write-up for the Inconnu, albeit stating flat-out that it was only one potential way of portraying them. The same chapter also presented an interpretation of the Tal'Mahe'Ra, with the same consideration.

                                      M20 is where a lot of developments happened...potentially. The transition from Disparates as disaffected Crafts to their own Alliance. The formal renaming of many Traditions, such as the Sons of Ether becoming the Society of Ether, etc. The Taftani now adapting to the modern day despite every other book on the subject making it clear they would rather die than accommodate the Consensus. The shifting of the Digital Web from Tron-esque to a fully realized HD world. Just a whole lot of little things that you can take or leave.

                                      While the 20th Anniversary editions all retain a fundamental metaplot agnosticism, each new book has the people at Onyx Path looking towards what sort of new things they can do with the systems and settings.

                                              V20 mentions the Free State once, doesn't mention the destruction of it.

                                              Actually, the Anarchs and where they are in the present are talked about in great length and detail in the V20 supplement, Anarchs Unbound.

                                              The default setting that book uses is that Gehenna and the Kuei-Jin invasion has not happened. In this version of the setting, a new sub-faction of the Anarchs emerges called "The Red Question", a hacktivist group of Anarchs (in the write-up, so pretty obvious allusions and parallels made to group like Anonymous). There existence was made known when they released a digital document called, well, "Anarchs Unbound". Essentially meant to be a new and updated version of the original Anarch Manifesto. It also mentions how the Red Question was very instrumental in bringing Kindred society into the digital age, mostly due to the Camarilla and the Sabbat out of necessity so they don't keep getting thrashed by digital attacks. And because of that, it's said that the Thamauturgy Path of Technomancy originated and was developed by the Red Question.

                                              And (more uncomfortably, at least for me) it's stated that the global 2008 recession was actually the doing of the Red Question, as a way to completely cripple the Camarilla's assets, and as a clear message telling the elders to back off from them.

                                              ...yeah, needless to say I've chosen to ignore that particular plot point in my own Anarch V20 game.

                                              There is a sidebar called "Looking to the Future" those essentially summarizes all the Revised events, specifically the ones that impacted the Anarchs in some way (including the aforementioned Kuei-Jin invasion, Tara's betrayal in San Diego, Justicar Petrodon's fate, and Baba Yaga). It doesn't really provide any suggestions on how to use or alter these events like Future Fates does in M20, save for just plugging them back in wholesale.
