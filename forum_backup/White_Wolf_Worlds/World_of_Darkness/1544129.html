                          If I'm adapting the M:t Ascension setting to Cyberpunk I first ask what does that do to the core of each group. I know you were asking the original poster Person of Great Importance and worth, but I'm tackling his question.

                          Between Gothpunk and Cyberpunk I don't think the meaning/value of the Trads change. The Technocoracy is probably much more smug. The Desparates are already bitter anti-Post-Colonial types. But the advanced tech doesn't change the themes. A Space Opera game would bring far greater change because of possibilities of exploration and new worlds.

                          A Cyberpunk setting, because implants and Cyber gear are things you see discussed in Discovery or Popular Science not edgy sci fi wouldn't risk paradox. The implant limitation would likely involve some character trait some attribute plus willpower divided by something. I'm sure Anima has an interesting idea about that.

                              If we're talking Shadowrun, the Order of Hermes probably has a place in the Technocracy, since hermetic study is a major, mainstream magical tradition that the megacorps love, and most frequently use hermetic mages to ward their high security areas from... ahem, deniable, magic-using assets. The TU might have also gritted its teeth and made overtures to the VAs and Etherites to get them back in so that they could manage more control over the new frontiers of the cyberpunk world. It probably wouldn't convince everyone in those traditions, but it might convinced enough to have also won back what are effectively two new branches (Magic-Tech workers and the Grid Overwatch Division/megacorp construction and control over the Matrix), plus the OoH.

                                                      For this, I would strongly recommend the Storyteller's Vault supplement Bleeding Edge 2.0. While it's Chronicles of Darkness and not World of Darkness, it's similar enough that you can draw a lot of useful material out of it.

                                                      The Virtual Adepts' worldview would be central here: it is, quite literally, what they were made for. The paradox is that in their worldview, they're the plucky underdogs standing up against the Man; so although their worldview is ascendant, they are not. On the other hand, much of their technology will have become mainstream; meaning that things that would today be VA Wonders are Instead mundane technology. This includes the netrunning decks of the Nexplorers, of course; but also the cybernetics of the aptly named Cyberpunks. Of the other Traditions, I'd expect to see the Cult of Ecstasy playing a strong secondary influence, as sex, drugs, and rock and roll are a big part of the "punk" part of cyberpunk.

                                                      On the Technocracy's side of things, it would be the Syndicate that's ascendant, followed by Iteration X and then the Progenitors. The New World Order is surprisingly rather low in standing, as the cyberpunk genre tends to assume that formal governments have become fragmented and largely ineffective, leading to megacorps filling in the power vacuum. And given the mostly Earth-centric nature of the typical cyberpunk setting, the Void Engineers are the least important of the Conventions. Heck, not even the wilderness explorers in the Convention have much to do, as cyberpunk tends to deal primarily with urban sprawls.

                                                      In terms of the Digital Web, memophores or Digital Web Entities (M20 pp.638–639) should be a more significant thing, with more of them being like the ISOs of Tron Legacy: digital intelligences in their own right, not merely programs created by and serving humans; viable characters. The long sought Artifical General Intelligence turns out not to be artificial at all, but merely a natural part of the digital world.

                                                      My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                          Originally posted by quelsec View Post

                                                          Wouldnt changelings just be shadowrun elves, trolls etc?
                                                          No, not quite. Elves and Dwarves and Trolls and the rest are basically sub-species of humans in Shadowrun. In the game lore itself its as if some humans had the "recessive genes" of these sub-species for several millennia, and when the Moon got ascendant over Saturn under the age of Aquarius and then Magic went BOOM!, these genes that were dormant became active. People who were normal humans, became Elves or even Halflings overnight.

                                                          Changelings however are human beings with a sort of "alien parasite" Fae Soul. The process of a Changeling being born is a spiritual one, the process for Shadowrun's other species of fantasy hominidae is magic-genetic.

                                                          That's a consideration one would have to make for such crossovers thou. Are the Changelings the Dreams of the collective unconscious about the mythical past upon which these races roamed the Earth, and these dreams came to "infect" the dreamers, creating the Changelings? But what happens now that the "real deal" is back?
