              The biggest change I've made has been in my mage games. I run that starting in the early 2000s, the Technocratic paradigm started breaking down. The technocratic paradigm is entirely based on the idea of science logically explaining how and why something unexplainable is happening, which in the 90s and earlier was a big thing. But really since the 2000s, the population's mentality has shifted. It's all about convenience again. People don't WANT to know how their computer works, they just want it to work. If it breaks, they don't read the manual and try to figure out how to fix it. They try a couple of half-baked basically arcane rituals (turn it off and turn it on again as the solution to everything), or they send it in to the company to "just fix it". In short, the idea of explaining something logically is no longer the protection from paradox that it once was because that isn't the sort of explanation consensus reality is looking for anymore. Consensus has gotten really good at ignoring facts and more and more insists on ignoring them in favor of their original assumption. This is death for the technocratic paradigm. In response to this I run a much more even footing in the Ascension War with the Progenitors and Iteration X leaving the Technocratic Union and joining the Traditions as they could no longer justify to themselves that what they were doing wasn't magic as more and more of them kept getting hit with paradox for things that had made perfectly logical sense to them, and the Void Engineers just going off into the aether and vanishing.

                  Wraith and Demon. Consider the Sixth Great Maelstrom never happened (as a necessary condition for a Wraith chronicle set after 1999). It also means the storm never cracked the walls of the Abyss (so, no Demon the Fallen). The only compromise I could work out between the two game lines is assuming the Sixth Great Maelstrom affected the Underworld as much as the preceding five (with the difference it was not triggered by anything happening this side of the Shroud).

                  Now, with all that in mind, consider DtF's particular plot, culminating with Lucifer's visage in LA. It would make the WoD a far more superstitious place. Of course, many wouldn't believe it was a angel or a demon, and many would try to find a rational explanation. However, many would, at least temporarily, renew their faith, and considering the WoD, I don't know how good it would be. I'd say a general atmosphere of superstition and obscurantism covers the world, specially the U.S. Also, all the scientific truths are shattered by Lucifer's Revelation, and the resulting insecurity impacts the cultural zeitgeist.

                  If Hurricane Katrina impacted America's psyche, a "natural" tragedy such as the Devil's Night earthquake would impact it on a deeper level. The world suddenly becomes a even more hostile place, and most start wondering what other catastrophes await in the future? Guess people in the WoD would respond to that by adopting a even more inconsequential attitude.

                      Saying the Sixth Great Maelstrom never happened is not "a necessary condition for a Wraith chronicle set after 1999". There's a very simple hack that has worked quite well for us. Just treat the Sixth Great Maelstroms as exactly like all the other Maelstroms and even the previous 5 Great Maelstroms that have occurred. Eventually it died down. I usually run the Underworld as an absolute wreck, saying that the Sixth Great Maelstrom didn't completely die down until 2003 (the release of Demon the Fallen) but that it didn't rage forever. This is necessary, not just for Wraith, but for any vampire chronicle in Revised as well (or the Giovanni really have nothing do do anymore). What has worked well in addition is to say that in most places, the Shroud is still the Shroud, but that in other places due to the unpredictable power of the Storm it did harden into the Stormwall, thus allowing Orpheus to exist as well.
