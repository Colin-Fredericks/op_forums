              The pre-Columbian Americas is where the Mage worldview has trouble. Mage says that consensus shapes reality. In a Euro-centric view, they've explained how that would result in a world largely like the real world at most points in history. In the Americas, it would be a big divergence.

              For example - the Order of Reason had to slowly build firearms into the consensus in Europe. Sorcerer's Crusade covers a time when very basic firearms were in the consensus and could be used by anyone, but more reliable/powerful ones were not - so they only show up in that setting as a focus for a High Artisan to work his Enlightened Will with, or as a Wonder/Device imbued with its own ability to warp reality. If that's true, when Europeans landed in America and tried to use their firearms, shouldn't they have been Paradoxical again? Nobody in the Americas, aside from the other guys on the ship, believed in them, so they should have only worked for Enlightened folks who could warp reality.

              If the Europeans land and their guns don't work, then no conquering is really going to happen, and we diverge from real-world history too much. You'll have to come up with a reason why technology accepted by Europeans but not by Americans worked in America. For the guns example:
              • There weren't a lot of guns on the landing ships - mostly swords and other simpler things. The guns they did have were actually operated by High Artisans or were Devices donated to the expedition by supportive Artisans back home.
              • The guns they used were much crappier than in the real world - arquebuses, hand-cannons, etc. Those were barely consensual enough to not explode most of the time, but still allowed them to take over natives via shock and awe.
              • They didn't bring guns at all, and conquered native cultures not with technology but by pitting them against each other and spreading disease.
              For the spiritual landscape - yes, I think having a generally lower Gauntlet in the Americas and a more common spiritual presence makes sense. Note that the Uktena took it as their job to find all the bad Banes and bind them, so aside from these areas of binding, the spiritual landscape should be in pretty good shape.

                              The big difficulty with Pre-Columbian America as a setting is that before there was time to understand it it had all but been destroyed. We are ferociously ignorant of how life actually worked, especially on the larger scale. It's something that historians are busy revising and learning to this day. A recent study I read suggests that the population of the Americas before Europeans arrived was 70,000,000 with vibrant inter-cultural exchange and more languages and traditions than we can differentiate. Partially due to war and enslavement, though mostly due to diseases like influenze and smallpox, a century later the population of the Americas was less than 4,000,000. Two continents of humans almost totally collapsed. So much was lost that we can never truly get back that you'd have to be very careful and thorough with your research and still acknowledge you were likely to be wrong about 1001 things.

                                                      Like to see some sources to back that up, because it doesn't really match up with anything I've ever seen. It ignores Baal, both in the singular and plural sense, utterly, and pretty much all archaeological evidence points to the importance of Ba'al in pretty much every way, everywhere the Canaaites went. Including the Phoencian era expansion across the Mediterranean where they established nearly complete hegemony across North Africa and up into the Iberian peninsula. Textual sources, both Hebrew and otherwise, indicate the importance of Ba'al, as well. There are even depictions of the two together in artistic formats by Hebrew and other Canaanites, where Ba'al and Yahweh are depicted together, with Ba'al always depicted as the higher status deity (and, incidentally with some female deity indicated to be at the same "level" as Yahweh and subordinate to Ba'al). Not to diminish the importance of Asherah, Astarte & Anat (no matter how you slice those three/two/one, oh yeah, and Tanit....), but to put them at the top of the pile doesn't seem to be backed up by the majority of the evidence. Co-equal, maybe, but not at the top. And even then, it's Ba'al who is the King. It's El who can give away kingship/the right to have a palace. Anat and Astarte may have a lot to do with Ba'al becoming king (including beating the crap out of El for dithering), but it's not she who rules.

                                                      And Yahweh doesn't seem to be much of a volcano god. There aren't any volcanoes in the relevant area, for one. He also seems to be associated with atmospheric phenomena, which is one of the reasons for the syncretization with El (and, sometimes, much to the chagrin of the Hebrew priestly caste, with Ba'al as well). He MIGHT have some sea/dragon aspects and have something to do with Yam.... but that is really weak, especially since some of the oldest Yahweh stories depict him as a dragon fighter (which is probably borrowed from Ba'al battling with various dragon-ish things, including Yam and with syncretization with Marduk during the Babylonian exile).
