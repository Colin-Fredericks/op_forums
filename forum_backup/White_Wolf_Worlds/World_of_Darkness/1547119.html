          So, just as a note, I'm going to assume we're still talking about mundane stuff, and not getting into what supernaturals could to do make these things even more nasty.

          Exploding rounds: mainly inspired by Aliens these are supposed to give much more firepower to your gun, mainly to deal with big bad monsters or damaging heavy armored machines. Considering the effect of fang dagger that "bites" deeper in the wounds by doubling the damage dealt would it be fair to use this same system for a bullet exploding inside the wound or should we adding bonus damage dice for the weapon?
          Explosive rounds is a broad category that is used for a number of different purposes. One problem across the board though, is that it's very hard to do anything with explosive rounds for small arms. The standard rounds in assault rifles simply aren't large enough to carry an explosive charge that can do anything effective in combat (making the Alien's rifle a thing of science fiction). The most common explosive rounds are HEAP (high explosive, armor piercing) which are only really effective against hardened plates used in combat vehicles (anything softer and they don't explode and are just big bullets with less mass than normal ones of that size), fused flak (basic anti-aircraft rounds designed to explode near more delicate vehicles and spray shrapnel around, various fuse types have been designed over the decades to maximize the chance of going off near targets), and anti-personnel (basically just modern grapeshot cannon fire, they explode and spray pellets over a wide range to hit as any human targets as possible). All of these are things you see traditionally out of mounted guns, or maybe large team based infantry units, not stuff people just walk around with as combat weapons.

          So, while these certainly can be more powerful weapons to bring to bare, it's probably best to start with higher powered vehicle weapons before trying to figure out different types of ammunition that's meant to be fired from those.

          Incendiary bullets: i think converting the weapon's lethal damage to aggravated should do the trick but what about the burn on next round? How many damage per turn (1 or 2 maybe?), how many turn (1 or 2 maybe) and what difficulty to soak them (7 or 8 maybe)?
          As I've lamented around here before, incendiary bullets don't work on human shaped targets. You're not going to light a human on fire with one. They're designed for shooting at readily flammable material caches, and even then don't actually have high success rates in the field (so you fire a burst of them from a machine gun, not on perfectly placed shot like many action movies). While they are extremely hot from the chemicals used (though bullets are in general very hot) they don't burn for very long, and have a significant chance of passing through humans instead of lodging in them for any extra damage.

          As best, if a creature is supernaturally weak to fire, it could be worth upgrading that first damage per attack to Agg. Otherwise they're far more dangerous when used for their intended purpose.

          Slugs: i really think it should add around 2 or 4 additionnal damage dice to the shotgun since all the power is concentrated on a single huge bullet that will crush anything it hit (especially when you think a 12 gauge shell is .72 caliber and a Warhammer 40K Bolt is .75, without the exploding part here of course)
          Funnily, slugs are popular among hunters because they're worse weapons than shot: they're harder to aim, and do less damage. The less damage is actually the good part, because it means less meat and other useful materials are wasted, where shot makes butchering much less efficient because stray pellets get into good meat, rupture organs that spoil meat or fur/hide, etc.

          It's important to remember something that RPGs generally overlook with shotguns: choke. Shotguns use a choke (most sporting shotguns have a set solid choke, though adjustable ones exist as well) to control the spread of shot to help maximize the results for the type of prey and expected distance from the target. This is accomplished by narrowing the barrel at the end in various configurations. Slugs have to pass through the choke, which means there's frequently a significant gap between the walls of the barrel and the slug as it's being fired. This leads to reduced accuracy, and less energy as more of the explosive gases can pass around the slug through that gap.

          In combat, a normal rifle is better than a shotgun regardless of shot vs. slugs. And even when combat units use shotguns, shot and slugs are produced because the better option is situational.

          There are a number of more exotic shotgun projectiles but most are either very niche or very ineffective compared to existing options. Someone invented a 12 gauge grenade so you can turn a shotgun into a grenade launcher... but it's worse in every way than a proper grenade launcher. If your only option is a shotgun in the first place, the odds of you having access to such ammunition is probably nil.

          Armor piercing rounds: maybe divide by 2 the soaking pool of the target work well enough in that case
          As I started to mention above, AP depends a lot on the target. Rounds designed to defeat heavy military armors can actually be less effective against unarmored humans because the physics get funky. What to do against things that can soak lethal would really depend on the kind of round and why it can soak lethal.

          Electric darts: the effect is mainly to paralyse the target, so a die penalty or difficulty increase for any physical action seems appropriate, but how many die would you loose or how much would you increase the difficulty (or set it whatever is the actions is)
          Tasers are already in the game (though I'm not sure how many books actually cover them).

          --------------

          So, broadly speaking, specialty ammunition exists for... specialty applications. When we want to do more damage... we just build bigger guns that can hold/withstand bigger cartridges (both bigger bullets, and more grains of powder). While there are some pressures and ammunition manufactures (international treaties have not been kept up to the times and now ban rounds that would reduce suffering in war because they originally banned rounds that used to be used in increase suffering), broadly speaking the best way to deal as much damage as possible is just a bigger rifle. If you want to get into explosives, we have bigger and better platforms specifically designed to deliver those because guns really aren't good at it outside of a few key applications.

          There are, of course, people trying to advance things. Lots of research has gone into better aerodynamics and stabilization than just the rotational force of a rifled barrel. The more the shape of a bullet does work to maintain trajectory and velocity, the more energy it has on impact from the same size of bullet and initial charge. But in the end... bigger is better when it comes to guns and doing as much damage as possible.

                  As I prefaced, my reply was presuming not taking into account the influence of the supernatural. While I'm not sure which precise Pentex example you're thinking of (the closest in that section of W20 core are the Phantasmi, which are more like particularly nasty poltergeists than Terminators, and Pentex doesn't know the exact process of creating them, just that they happen every so often if enough Garou kill enough normal fomori), the bigger issue is such things are not something a normal mortal in the WoD can just go buy in a gun store or print from a digital file if they resort to ghost guns given local laws. You need supernatural oomph that's currently not treated as something even Pentex can mass produce to get the kind of specialty ammo you're seeking. And at that point, the rules of the given game-line take over more than the real world issues of firearms. Any WtA character type that can use the Rite of Binding can make Talen bullets to various effects (including more damage), vampires are very limited it being able to do much to make guns more powerful, mages can do a scarily large amount without even risking vulgarity, etc.

                  As for war conventions: there's two main concepts within international war treaties that influence this: weapons that cause cruel death to enemy combatants, and weapons with high rates of non-combatant casualties. The first category is the bigger deal for practical modern warfare because it covers frangible rounds; that is, bullets designed to deform and break up on contact with the target. Early frangible rounds were considered very cruel because the rate of immediate death from being shot wasn't "better" because the extra fragments spreading internally didn't actually lead to a serious increase in fast bleed outs from severed blood vessels and the like, but massively increased mortal wounds that could take hours or days to kill from things like sepsis. Modern frangible rounds are actually very common in the form of hollow points, where they "mushroom" more than splinter into a dozen pieces. Hollow points are very popular among police forces because (probably a bit more perception than reality) they have more "stopping power," and more importantly they have a much smaller risk of overpenetration and hitting unintended targets behind the intended target. As modern warfare has shifted from large army on army fights in open battlefields towards small unit engagements near or in residential areas, there is considerable debate over whether the potential cruelty of modern frangible rounds is really so bad compared to the risk to non-combatants from normal high powered rifle bullets punching through things.

                  The second category has certainly deterred things like trying to construct more practical small arms explosive rounds, but even what has been developed (such as the 12 gauge grenade shell) still doesn't demonstrate much reason to push the envelope anyway (unlike frangible rounds since lots of countries use them on their own populace these days and think that's better, so there's more development). HEAP rounds don't really make sense in human-on-human conflicts, as we have non-convention violating munitions for punching through modern combat body armor already. Why spend millions trying to make a micro-HEAP to do what non-HEAP armor piercing rounds can already do?

                  On incendiary bullets, keep in mind that even small ones are generally decently powerful rifle rounds, which have a much lower rate of lodging in people compared to all the pistol and sub-machine gun rounds that absolutely do lodge in people a lot. A 9mm (one of the most common pistol and sub-machine gun calibers currently) is just too small to fit an incendiary in. You can combine frangible rounds with the large rifle bullets that can fit incendiaries in (though yeah, war conventions make it so there's minimal desire for anyone to try), but there's an issue of the incendiary material losing impact as it spreads out as well. Consider the difference of applying a butane torch to a small piece of wood for 10 seconds, vs. the same amount of fuel spread out over a log 10x as big. When you want to burn something, and you have a limited amount of fuel, you want to concentrate, not expand (heck that's the whole point of how HEAP works). If you want to melt a candle with a single match, you want to catch the wick, not wave the match along the candle's wax until it goes out. Also keep in mind that we have things like grenades. While most certainly not cool by international convention, a napalm grenade is much easier to make than a practical incendiary bullet for fighting vampires/etc. and if you're fighting vampires do you care about the Geneva Conventions or do you toss an explosive that sprays sticky fire all over the place at one?

                  On slugs vs. shot: a lot of what you're getting at is complicated because it's all very situational. A big point though, is that this is why hunters tend to have lots of guns. Not because they just want to collect all the guns (there is of course plenty of overlap with collectors), but because different tools for different jobs. You don't just use different ammunition for hunting birds vs. deer vs. boars vs. elephants (though, sorry for the editorializing, but fuck anyone shooting elephants). You use entirely different guns. There is no one-sized fits all gun here. The 12 gauge shotgun is popular because it's versatile and can be used in a lot of circumstances, but if you have options, you're going to take a different gun for hunting boar rather than doing the best you can by getting different shells.

                  Even the Kevlar armor example is questionable. Range in particular is going to matter. At longer ranges where the buckshot can spread out, it might not penetrate Kevlar any better, but the odds of hitting unprotected areas goes up significantly. And a simple Kevlar vest really isn't designed to save you from either. Body armor meant to protect from long guns, including shotguns, is going to at least include trauma plates, which slugs don't do well against (not that shot does do well either, but it turns into more of a wash). Yes, there are absolutely situations where you want a slug over shot. But the same is true in the other direction. And both are still less deadly if your goal is killing human-scale things than a decently powerful hunting rifle that's the same size at the shotgun is.
