          There is no clarification anywhere about what the Abyss of Demon resides in comparison to everything else known about the Umbra. Actually, it isn't even clear if the several Abyss-named realms are really several distinct realms, linked somehow, or actually the same saw by distinct parties.

          In the end, the answer is yes. From a MTA standpoint, there is no place you can't go with the right magic and a degree of effort. But the practicality of it depends on a lot of questions that the ST will have to answer alone.

          Some options: you can try to access it through Middle Umbra Abyss. That would need you to find some way to brace yourself against both the undoing effect of the place and its depressive effect. I guess that you got reasonable chances with Prime, Spirit, Life and Mind. It would be, of course, a guess try, very dangerous and without guarantees.

          You can try to go through Obtenebration's Abyss. To find how to access the damn place would be another thing entirely, but you probably can do if you put your hands over a high level user of Obtenebration and lets it experiment with the damn thing. In this case, what is highly dangerous and hard to pull is to find this Abyss, the actual journey can still be ripe on dangers, but nothing as the insta-kill of the previous expedition.

          As already presented, you can try the Labyrinth. It is probably the most dangerous of the three. But you can run out of options.

          Finally, all of those can be false paths, and the true one be something else entirely not yet known. Judging by the descriptions of the Demons, though, I assume that it is a place that can't attain visual representation, a realm of minds without the facsimile of space and matter that most realms have. So it could be either a very alien realm in the far reaches of the Umbra, or it could simply be a truly inconspicuous piece of the Astral Umbra, even one very near to Earth.

          Without a clear entrance, you can try to make amends with Correspondence. Coupled with Spirit and enough ranks, you can trace your way there by some other means. You could try to use the Correspondent link between another Abyss and "true Hell" to force one of the first trips to actually work in a way. You could use it on a Demon to trace it back to Hell, this would probably be the simplest way to find the Abyss' location, though it wouldn't necessarily give you means to reach it.

          Of course, all of that isn't enough to answer the final two questions: what would you find nearing the Abyss, and what effects you presence there would have on you. Whatever the Creator placed at the boundaries of Hell could keep Magi from entering it for all of Creation's history until now, as far as we know. And the "place" itself isn't meant for the presence of any other kind of being aside from Angels. Not even other spirits can dwell there, and it don't have space, time or matter references not even as an impression, distinguishing it from the rest of the known Umbra.

          Good luck on the trip.

                  It can either be, be not, or exist both.

                  The books never went clear on what infernalists were actually dealing with, much less if they were dealing with the same beings all the time. Instead, you have only in-character descriptions, and those are non-reliable.

                  As a whole, the Umbra have at least half a dozen distinct ways of generating spirits that can be dubbed "demons", and realms that can be described as hell. So probably most infernalists are not actually dealing with the Fallen, but with other kinds of demons (no less demonic). Some could be dealing with the Fallen, but on the course of history it probably wasn't all that common.

                  Even then, Devil's Due demonstrates this point. Demons had been reaching Earth since dawn of civilization, so some are indeed Thralls. But the powers of those groups and their ritae and beliefs preclude the Fallen, and can work perfectly well with several other things. If you put Fallen in their midst, it is more probable that some will be Demon Thralls, either by the rules on DTF or DD.

                                                      No, that's just one way of looking at it. Who said a local umbrood can't be a demon? On a lot of views demons are local spirits of some sort. On most of Europe and Middle East demons were saw as elemental beings with specific alignment, like the Ifrit and other Jinn. Some on the medieval Catholic Church believed Demons to be on the same class as God, but others thought of it as blasphemy. "Near absolute power" is such a loose term that a Nexus Crawler can easily fit in, and lesser spirits can do with some effort. And faith being a requirement is pretty much a recent school of thought.

                                                      The setting isn't less muddy on this than real life. Lots of spirits describe and comprehend themselves as Demons, they have a huge variety of powers, origins, natures. And the very word "Demon" do not have strict meaning. Also, the setting is heavily defined by particular unreliable narrators. The Fallen have just a narrative of what happened, and few proof of such narrative. The Baali are just denizens of the world, so they calling their patrons "demons" means nearly nothing outside that the Baali themselves believed so. And they could as much miss something as disagree with any given definition for Demon.

                                                      You should first define clearly what is a Demon before you could distinguish objectively what is and what is not a demon. And even then, that would be just the beginning of the discussion.
