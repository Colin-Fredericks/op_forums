    Story-games?

    07-30-2016, 10:43 PM
    I re-posted this on the WoD forum, since i'm interested in both community's opinions and not everyone reads both.

    So, it's something I'm thinking about recently, namely modern story-games and their mechanics. Admittedly, I haven't played any so far, but read the CofD corebook and the Requiem 2e book and getting interested in the Dresden Files rpg, because I love the novels. However as I read about the core assumptions of the FATE system, I've been getting the picture that there is an emphasis on mechanics that allows the player to directly influence the story and I have a problem with that, because it somewhat breaks the immersion and suspension of disbelief for me. It feels a little too game-y, if I'm not interacting with the story through my character's actions, but directly. I'm totally into collaborative world-building between the ST and players, I think it adds tremendously to the game and the player's interest in the story and their characters, but altering the story and events out of character with a game-mechnaic during actual play feels somewhat off to me.

    Now, I think I understand the appeal of that type of gaming, it could be a wonderful experience of collective story-telling, but somehow it feels to me a little different thing than what I picture as role-playing. Not worse just different.

    I think it's relevant for WoD, because the new WW guys are coming from nordic larp, so I get the vibe that the new edition of the WoD games might be moving toward the story-game style from old-school rpg and these type of games seems to be the hype of nowadays.

    What do you think? Are you enjoying story-games and mechanics? How do you feel and think about them? Is there something about them that I missed? How these mechanics playing out in practice in CofD games?

    If nothing worked, then let's think!

Mind, it's entirely a different thing for me, than integrating the player's ideas into the game and setting and things like WoD backgrounds. On the other side, NOT doing things which my character'd totaly do, but it'd be bad for him, because I know it'd be bad for him is also metagaming.

It's not wrong, actually, I think I'd enjoy it time-to-time and the more I'm thinking about it, the more I think it suits better one-shots, or short chronicles with that special focus, but it's still a bit contradictory with what I'm expecting from 'default' role-playing.
