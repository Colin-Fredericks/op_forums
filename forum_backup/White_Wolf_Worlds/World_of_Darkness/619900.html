    Classic World of Darkness - IV Edition

    08-08-2015, 06:15 AM
    So, with VtM IV Edition announced I was wondering what people hope for, in regards to drastic changes in the game world mechanics. I named the topic "Classic World of Darkness IV" since I hope for some streamlining among all games in the cWoD (and I'm confident it won't stop at VtM).

    So, for me, there are a couple of things.
    1. Change the combat. I'd like to see a Defense score - like in nWoD - and possibly variable total health levels. Attack, possibly Dodge, Damage, Soak was to many rolls for one action in combat, a system that reduces this would be very much appreciated.
    2. When the Merits from nWoD are brought over, I hope it is revamped. Foreign Languages and stuff like that, especially if it can be learned during play, should be a Background (if it isn't a knowledge anymore).
    3. I hope the Morality from nWoD make it into VtM and possibly the cWoD as a whole.
    4. I like alternating difficulties, but, the system should have caps. Any difficulty raise above 9 (due to circumstances) should reduce the dice pool, every difficulty lowered below 5 should give bonus dice. Difficulties below 5 or above 10 simply break the probabilities too much.

    That is, from the top of my head, things I'd like to see changed in a new version of the cWoD mechanics.
    Any one else who would want a drastic overhaul of the mechanics ?

                              I've been toying with it in various incarnations for a bit now. One of the reasons I like greater or equal to lowest trait, compared to half or dice-pool from SR, is it's more likely to actually happen with d10s, and provides a bit of a mechanical distinction between high/low and even trait characters.

                              With the half dice-pool method, Someone with Dexterity 5/Medicine 1, Dex 1/Med 5, and Dex 3/Med 3 have the same issues with risking complications (and the same odds with the cWOD Rule of 1 methods).

                              Using the lowest trait means even is preferable. A character that's got a surgeon's hands, but barely passed some first-aid training is very likely to mess something up, even if they do more good than harm. A character that can't hold their hand's steady, but it an expert doctor can easily slip a bit during an emergency surgery. Meanwhile someone that's above average in both is much more solid if not causing complications.

                              It also avoids the need for making exceptions to botches for things like WP, Soak, and Damage rolls (if you even keep those last two rolls). Any single Trait roll needs to come up all 1s, which is rare enough to be worth a spectacular botch.

                              Lastly, I've found it pretty nice for adding another widget for blessings/drawbacks. Weapons that are prone to being dangerous to their users can add 1s to the results (so that chainsaw might do a lot of damage, but it's really easy to bust the chain or worse). Instead of, "your character can never botch rolls with X Ability," you can say, "Ignore the first X number of 1s rolled for Ability X."

                                  Originally posted by heinrich View Post
                                  3. I hope the Morality from nWoD make it into VtM and possibly the cWoD as a whole.
                                  The Humanity score that mortals have in VtM essentially is nWoD's Morality.

                                  Originally posted by heinrich View Post
                                  4. I like alternating difficulties, but, the system should have caps. Any difficulty raise above 9 (due to circumstances) should reduce the dice pool, every difficulty lowered below 5 should give bonus dice. Difficulties below 5 or above 10 simply break the probabilities too much.
                                  There are two related issues here: botch mechanics, and overdriving the difficulty. Here's my thoughts:
                                  I'd balance the botch mechanics by introducing the concept of the “boost” — the notion that 10s are special in a manner that mirrors the way 1s are special. Think of boosts as being a bit like the Enhancements being developed for the Sardonyx System: each boost lets you do a little something extra. Boosts and botches would cancel each other out: if you roll two botches and a boost, that's the same as rolling one botch; conversely, rolling three boosts and a botch is the same as rolling two boosts. Boosts and botches would be independent of success and failure: that is, botches don't cancel successes; and even when a botch from a 1 cancels the boost normally granted by a 10, it doesn't cancel the success granted by the 10. Conversely, successes don't negate botches: you can succeed and "botch" at the same sime, leading to a “messy success” where you achieve your goal but something still goes wrong.

                                  Difficulty has a maximum and a minimum; the numbers are open for debate, though I’d strongly recommend not setting the maximum higher than 9 or the minimum lower than 3; there should always be a chance to roll a regular success or failure on any given die. Driving the difficulty above the maximum starts negating successes instead of raising the target number (e.g., if you’re three over the maximum, ignore the first three successes rolled), and driving the difficulty below the minimum starts granting bonus dice instead of lowering the target number (e.g., if you're three under the minimum, you get three additional dice in your pool).

                                  My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                          08-10-2015, 03:37 PM
                                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                          With a system sensible to the number of successes, I think the Boost rule is kinda redundant. Unless they make the system works with the idea of one success being enough, I would keep to Heavy Arms' idea. Having enough successes already counts for a boosted effect.
                                          I never defined the specifics of what a Boost would do for you, other than the fact that each boost cancels out one botch (and vice versa). That is, in fact, my primary motivation for introducing boosts: to provide an equal and opposite counterweight to botches. 1s and 10s cancel, and 10s are good in a way similar to how 1s are bad; so increasing the size of the dice pool doesn’t mean that more bad stuff will happen to you, the way that it does when there's nothing to cancel out your botches (e.g., 1e and 2e dice mechanics). Conversely, it’s not as harsh as the Revised-era notion that a single success trumps all botches rolled, which resulted in botches very quickly becoming vanishingly rare as the pool size increased (which largely negates the point of having botch mechanics in the first place).

                                          The mechanical benefits of a boost could be as boring as “each boost increases the effective number of successes you generate by one”, which would be similar to the “10s count double toward success” mechanic that I believe some of the non-WoD Storyteller games have used save for the fact that these extra successes would be vulnerable to being canceled out by botches. But that’s not the only way to do it; you could also say that boosts enhance the results in ways that additional successes don't, either by specifying what multiple successes do for you and using boosts for anything else or by saying that boosts grant non-mechanical benefits much like botches cause non-mechanical complications.
                                          If boosts are a no-go, another possibility would be to say that 10s don't provide any sort of boost, per se, but that a single 10 (not a single success) negates all 1s. I'd have to run the numbers to know for sure; but my instincts say that this would drive the probability of a botch down as the dice pool grows much like the Revised rules do, but not as quickly as the Revised rules do. Heck, you could even keep the 1e/2e notion that uncanceled 1s subtract from successes. This could provide a middle ground between early editions' out-of-control botches and later editions' stifled botches.

                                          My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                      I've been thinking this a lot since the annoncement of V4, but what is the purpose of the rules ?
                                                      Why having rules ? Is having rules can/should enforce the theme/mood of game, or not ?

                                                      I'll try to explain : for me, the Rule of 1 is the most important rule of the cWod, because is emphasize the theme "There will always be complications/you'll never truly win". This theme is the core of the "gothic-punkness" of the cWod. Remove this and what will distinguish the cWod from DnD, thematically speaking ? The dramatic failure add flavor to the game.

                                                      Combat is another exemple : I've always think that combat rules where needlessly complicated to enforce the point that "violence has a price". That's the great irony of W:tA : you play the greatest warriors, but violence will not be the solution against the Wyrm. Or this is supported by the fact that the Sabbat wins battle, but will ultimatly lose the war. Make combat a lot more simple and the whole "violence has a price" theme will be undermined (even if violence is necessary sometime, ence the drama).

                                                      As a counterpoint, look at DnD. The theme of the game is "Bang the door, Kill the monster, Loot the treasure" (thanks Munchkins!) Yes, a lot of us have roleplay heavy games, but most of the rules are geared toward combat and are adept at covering combat situations. The levels (the character growth) is developped by combat almost exclusively. A "1" isn't a botch, but just "you fail, no matter the bonus".

                                                      It is not that rules can't be smoothered, improved (as V1 to V2 to VRev to V20), but the purpose of the rules is what should guide the changes.
                                                      My only problem is I can't find something to improve. Except maybe don't give the Point-Blank bonus to Full-Auto shoot action. So many deads
