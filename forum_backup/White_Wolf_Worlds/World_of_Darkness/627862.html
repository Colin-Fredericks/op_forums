    Revising Multiple Actions

    08-15-2015, 04:36 PM
    There has long been a lot of debate over powers that provide multiple actions, especially Celerity, not only with balance but game play. I generally like splitting dice pools to perform multiple actions, but I think that it doesn't go very well with Celerity or Rage spending for multiple actions.

    My thought, then, was that dice splitting to attack more than once could be limited - you can only split those dice pools under special circumstances. These could include having an automatic or semiautomatic firearm, having a weapon in each hand, or using a special multi action power - like Celerity.

    Celerity could allow you to take as many extra actions each turn equal to your Celerity rating and to also add that score to your dice pool for multiple actions. For example, if you had a Dexterity of 3, a Melee of 3, and a Celerity of 2 - you would get 8 dice each turn that you could split among up to 3 attacks if you were using a single melee weapon. Then Celerity would require no activation and would always function.

    Something similar could be done with Rage or other multi action powers.

              Are you aware of the Dark Ages Rules to Celerity from the 20th Anniversary Dark Ages book?

              If not. I just fetched it from the cupboard, so:
              1. Celerity is added to all Dexterity based dice pools, including initiative.
              2. It is added as one yard or meter per level to her movement.
              3. If a blood is spend, the vampire may change the way multiple actions are used.
              4. Also, the vampire my spend a blood instead to gain 1 + celerity times his normal movement as his speed this turn (lasts one turn in combat, or a scene out of combat)

              That said: how are multiple actions used:
              1. You must decide that you want to take multiple actions, before taking the first action.
              2. The first action is at a -1 dice pool penalty and a +1 difficulty. Each action there after is at an additional -1 dice pool penalty and +1 difficulty. If the difficulty would be 10 you can't take more actions. Also, only one action my be an attack.

              Celerity changes this rules if a blood is spend:
              1. the +1 to difficulty is not enacted, until you have had as many actions as you have Celerity. After that, the difficulty is +1 per additional action.
              2. The number of attacks may not be 1 + half the Celerity rating (round up).


              So, while this certainly is a much better approach to additional actions, than every edition before, I find the system still overly complicated, and it leads to certain strange situations, where the order of actions that are supposed to be happening at once are actually making a difference.



              MY APPROACH FOR A COMBAT SYSTEM
              I would allow a character actions, and reactions. Reactions are Dexterity + either Athletics (Dodge), Brawl (Unarmed Parry) or Melee (Armed Parray). Whenever the character is first attacked in a turn, he rolls the complete dice pool of his Reaction. For every additional time the character is attacked, the dicepool is reduced by one. Obviously not all attacks can be parried and so on.
              The dice pool refreshes whenever a new turn starts.

              Actions happen all at once. Each action has the normal dice pool, minus the additional number of actions that one takes. So if someone wants to do three things, the dice pool for all actions is -2. Movement is an action in this regard, as is the activation of powers that don't require rolls, and concentration to keep powers active, if the description state such a concentration is needed. Only one of these actions may be an attack, however (rules for double tap and the like not withstanding).

              Certain powers, like Celerity or Rage may allow for more than one attack, while other powers (like a certain Thauma Path) might allow for more than one power to be activated. I would keep the Celerity rules otherwise as in DA20 (with each Celerity counting as one Dexterity and the ability to spend blood to get an additional attack per uneven point).
              One could use Rage in a similar way, allowing the permanent rating as a bonus to attack rolls, but not to speed and dexterity rolls that aren't combat related. Also, spending one point would allow for additional attacks per uneven permanent dot.

              That said, I would go, as suggested by someone else, for a fixed damage based on strength, weapon used, and bonuses from gifts and the like. Damage would consist of three parts:
              Damage Value (the number of health lost, to be reduced by soak roll)
              Damage Power (a number of dice reduced from the soak pool before soak)
              Wound Type (if the wounds are considered fast healing, moderate healing or severe).

              So, combat would mean one attacks, the opponent has his reaction and if there are less successes scored, the damage is inflicted but may be soaked. The soak roll is modified by the Damage Power, but if dice remain in the dice pool, they can reduce the Damage Value.

              That said, Damage Values should be moderate, like Strength/2+2 for a sword or something like that. Powers, like disciplines and gifts, should only rarely change Damage Value, but on lower levels probably change Damage Power, or raise the Wound Type. Obviously, Silver should have a pretty high "Damage Power" against garou, but the mightiest would still have some Soak dice remain, and so on.

              I think such a system would all in all work well.

                      What we've done - and this was before DAV20 came out - was handle multiple actions and Celerity in this way:

                      1) the character may take as many actions as she has dots in Wits or Dexterity (not both), because sometimes you're not as quick as you want to be either physically or mentally;
                      2) the player chooses the smallest dice pool for the actions they want to take,
                      3) successes are split amongst the actions. If the player wanted to take three actions and only rolled three successes, they have the option of assigning one success to each action, or two(or three) successes to one action. An action with no assigned successes is an automatic failure,
                      4) Celerity and other powers that grant multiple actions allow the player to utilize the full dicepool for each action.

                      Of course, we've streamlined combat down to one roll with static everything, so combat isn't a huge time-suck for us.

                                              Personally I still prefer the Revised multiple action rules, where no dice pools were split, but you simply had a dice penalty of <total amount of actions> for your first action, with a dice being subtracted in every additional action.

                                              So if you did two actions in one round, you had -2 dice for the first, -3 for the second.

                                                          I was going to link to my whole house rules/hacks n' faqs, and then I was just going to cut out and post the relevant section on multiple actions, but then I realized that between my two previous posts, I basically explained it. To sum up:

                                                          Players may take a number of multiple actions equal to their Wits or Dexterity score, whichever is lower;
                                                          players must choose the smaller dice pool from the actions they would like to take;
                                                          players assign their successes among the actions, and every action must have a minimum of one success assigned;
                                                          any actions with no assigned successes is an automatic failure, as well as any action after that in the chain;
                                                          players may assign their successes in any way they like.
                                                          Powers that grant multiple actions (Celerity, Rage, Time Sphere, Pandemonium, whatever Changelings have) allow the player to utilize the appropriate full dice pool for each action (though the number of actions is still limited). The actions from these powers cannot be divided.

                                                          This has been working for us for about, what? Three years or so? It feels fair to the players at my table, barely takes up any more time than regular actions, and limits multiple action abuse (the largest complaint about Celerity).
