    The moral of the story is, every faction and each subgroup of Mages thinks they are right, every group thinks they are the 'good guys', and every (sub)group, even if they cooperate, ultimately would love for their point of view to become the norm.

    They are very human like that. And all of them are flawed in human ways. This is nothing special at all. That's the point.

    *Any* kind of human can awaken. The most gentle soul and the most deranged bastard. And when humans organize large groups, shit goes wrong on *some* level or other.

    Nobody actually thinks they are a 'bad guy'. This applies to both Mages and RL folks that do abhorrent things. They might lack empathy and understanding, but they do not think they are 'the bad guy'.
    Even those Mages that push some actions and consequences to extremes consider it essentially to be 'the end justifies the means'.

    In the end, Mage asks the personal question 'If you were a powerful reality-bending being, how far would you go for what you think is right?', to every character, as well as the followup question 'And how would you treat normal, unaware people? What if they get in the way? What if you want them to see things your way? Just what would you do to win this war?'


    The only exception are the Nephandi. Which is what differentiates them from simple, sociopath murder Mages that naturally also exist out there. The Nephandi went through the Cauls, the Avatar got inverted, and they absolutely know they are pure Evil. They simply think they are either a necessary evil, or really don't care.

                            Going back to the topic, a game about "Ze evil Technocrats" vs "the angelic oppressed Traditions" would not be a game I would like to play. And it's not because some "good vs evil" very black and white game wouldn't be fun - when I play D&D, it is strictly "boy scouts" heroic deeds (I absolutely dislike all of the disfunctional tables with "Chaotic Evil" Clerics shoehorned into it). But I think that Mage the Ascension can explore things with so much more depth to it than just that. And even if I want to make some non Nephandi as a clear cut and outright villain, that can be done with so much more depth than just "evil for evil's sake". My example of the Technocrat that "fights Things-men-were-not-meant-to-know" is because I once used such a villain at my tables. He was a war veteran from deep space, and there was no atrocities that he would not commit for the sake of the Union - but there was a purpose to it, a motif. And his reason was that, in face of what awaited on the other side should the Union fail, ANYTHING else was secondary. This was an extreme view of "trading safety for freedom". And that's the sort of moral analysis that I like my players to indulge into. It's not unlike primitive tribes of the Paleolithic exterminating one another over food. When pushed to the extremes for survival, moral considerations are thrown out the window. And that was the worldview of this villain, a "barbarians at the gate" mindset that is able to justify anything.

                            That's also the dangers of ideology. When you are certain that you are on "the right side of history", you can justify ANYTHING. Do you think that the Nazis though of themselves as villains? Of course not, in their lunatic worldview they were waging a spiritual war to save the world. The Chinese youth under Mao also thought that were on the "right side of history" when they were denouncing millions to die under the Cultural Revolution. But they were the "righteous ones". And when you believe yourself to be "righteous", it's very easy to become a tyrant and commit unspeakable atrocities.

                            Even when you believe to be standing against the "oppressors" on behalf of "the oppressed" (which by the way, both the Nazis and the Communists also firmly believed to be doing)

                                        The main drive, as I see it, for adding gray to the Technocracy is that they have a near-monopoly on Enlightened Science; and a lot of people love the idea of playing Enlightened Scientists. There are basically two possible solutions to this: make the Technocracy something that people can stomach playing when they don't want to play evil characters, or break the monopoly.

                                        Both have been tried. Guide to the Technocracy and the Revised Convention Books try for a “kinder, gentler Union”, and M20 introduces the possibility of groups like Navalon, which practice Enlightened Science but loathe technocracy (lower-case “t”). The former approach operates on the premise that the Dimensional Singularity and the subsequent loss of Control was a shock to the system for the Technocracy and made them take stock and get introspective, cleaning up their act to an extent. The latter approach takes a faction that was inside the Union but was always uneasy about what the Union was doing, and recently decided to jump ship.

                                        But this thread isn't really about the Technocracy; it's about the Traditions. And even if the Technocracy was as bad as the Nephandi, it doesn't mean that the Traditions are good guys.

                                        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
