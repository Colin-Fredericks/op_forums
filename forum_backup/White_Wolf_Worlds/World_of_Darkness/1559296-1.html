                            There are two things that commonly get muddled in comparisons of the Syndicate and the NWO. For the Syndicate, they run afoul common disconnect present in most casual discussions of economics: because people often use the same terms differently. Some people use the term capitalism in a very general sense to mean any economic system with people trading based on wants and needs. Others use it to refer to very specific applications of it, commonly the ones we see in the modern west right now. The more specifically we define capitalism it and the more we tie the Syndicate to the specific modern economic system, the more difficult it is to change them without it seeming like a dramatic break. On the flip side, if they are tied to capitalist systems in general and able to switch away from the current one, they can change quite a bit while still being the same Convention.

                            That isn't an argument for which of those ways they should be depicted, by the way, just a statement of the options and how they relate to Syndicate reform.

                            For the NWO, the problem is the discussion tends to revolve around whether state regulation and control can be a force for good (itself a quite controversial topic) but misses that the NWO is not a state. They aren't elected, they have no Mandate of Heaven, no divine right of kings, they didn't get a sword from some watery tart, they don't have the right of conquest, and most importantly, they don't assert themselves as a government or an arm of a government. They are a shadowy conspiracy that has appointed itself to secretly manipulate the people whether they like it or not. A lot of mage groups (especially the Conventions) have that issue to one extent or another, but the NWO have it to a profound degree and character. They aren't the halls of power, they are the corruption in the halls of power: unknown and unaccountable. Redeeming that is considerably harder than just shifting their motivations. It requires major changes in justification and accountability.

                            Note, this is not an argument that the NWO are morally worse than the Syndicate, it is pointing out a specific Achilles' heel they have that makes reformation very difficult without changing their very nature. It is a problem most of the Conventions have, but one that is much more significant for the NWO because of their focus and how they relate to it.



                            Also, as a side note: I tend to get a lot of pushback on this, but it's been my headcanon that the Syndicate is the Convention of economic systems, not just capitalist ones. Indeed, if they are tied to the value of human endeavor rather than money itself, it makes sense that they would be. I think they've done a at least some experimenting around with planned and communist economies and they may do so again in the future. But as I said, that's mostly headcanon, so feel free to disregard.

                                        While I don't want to get too side tracked, the thing about arguments against "state regulation" are generally against the modern concept of a "state" far more than they are about the need for regulations (even really hardcore anti-regulation advocates don't mean no regulations, they just want less rules for the stuff they want to do). Humans need social rules once a group gets big enough. You can't get a thousand people to live cooperatively and in a mutually beneficial fashion without enforceable explicated rules. We're just not wired for it. To that end, when it comes at least to internal matters, regulation is the whole purpose of the state.

                                        Much like my point before about the relationship between the Syndicate and capitalism, "regulation" is a necessary component of any system of human governance. Anti-statist models change who makes the regulations and/or who enforces them; even if they want to call the societal rules that govern internal behaviors something different. The Neo-NWO needs to drop the emphasis on controlling the rules-making apparatus of society, and shift to pushing it to work as best as possible under its own terms. It doesn't really matter if whatever method humans use to organize themselves is the modern state concept or something else. Either there's a method to make and force rules on the people because it's necessary for groups of a large enough size to even function and thus there's something for the NWO to work with, or humanity has splintered into such small social units that most of the Technocracy loses its central purpose.

                                                        So, I'm going to question what corruption is and what redemption means. I'm also going to question whether Mage organizations, Technocracy or other, are personalities or offered frameworks for human existence.
                                                        Viewing the factions as personalities is the easiest conception to hold in our minds.They are this and those others are that. Then we can generalize behaviors and actions as a whole and judge them according to whatever criteria we see fit.
                                                        Viewing the factions as frameworks, however, gets more complicated in the mind. If the Syndicate and others are defined by their models or paradigms, then they are offering a sandbox for everyone to play in. That means, while the Technocracy is certainly making the rules for a lot of things, they are neither the only players nor the only shapers of their own systems and their implementations.
                                                        The text regularly conflates these two visions, often by design to muddy the eaters and also to allow variations of play. Each table has to make decisions, either conciously or unconsciously, about how much mages, and by extension, the Technocracy, control reality and how much they are just pushing ideas out as possibilities and trying to keep up with the myriad of variations Sleepers, or the Masses, turn those into.
                                                        Every opportunity creates a potential, even a need, for corruption. Once others are allowed to work with your udeas they are going to change and reinterpret your ideas. Where this is corruption where it is growth and where it is evolution are actually quite subjective, up to a point.
                                                        So, consider the corner shop keeper of whatever type you like. The opportunity to own and run a shop is an opening of the pattern to allow growth. Nice. However, when that same shopkeeper runs into financial trouble from mismanagement or outside pressures or environmental shifts (deportation, gentrification, general economic depression, whatever) there are some desperate choices to be made. Do they close the shop? Sell at a loss? Or maybe start allowing drugs to run through their shop or gambling or launder money for others to keep things going?
                                                        The latter choices are obviously corruption. Is that the Syndicate causing that? The Nephandi? Some Tradition or Craft mages? Or is it the shop keeper's choice to go simpler routes rather than foresee events and make more positive changes? Is this because the Syndicate system is evil or just a lack of predictive soothsayers who could have read the stars fir rhem to help them out? Should we need to consult astrologers to run a business or us it better to be enabled with the self determinism to fail or succeed by our own choices?
                                                        So the creation of opportunities, which is a big part of the Syndicate model, is also the opening to failure, hardship and corruption. Is the Syndicate corrupt for promoting this model, or just imperfect? Are they dominating these choices at the micro level or painting with broad strokes and trying to keep up with the consequences?
                                                        How each of us thinks about this at our tables will create the "objective" truths about how good or bad each faction is and how much we care, based on each faction's impact to our characters and their passions and interests.
