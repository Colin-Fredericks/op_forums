                      Depending on which side of the story one is on, the "bad guys" present a unified front because their selfish, greedy, manipulative desires and behaviors. They have no false veneer of civility to cover their actions, and every party knows the other is going to try to screw them over eventually - it just depends on how much mileage they can squeeze out of each other before that happens. When one is playing the more traditional "good guy" roles in the WoD, the antagonists appear to work together because their goals are nearly the same. This changes when ones plays the "bad guys" - the "good guys" don't work together (or work together well), and while they are fractured, they are individually stronger on a comparable level, and their attacks reflect this. For example: a "good guy" werewolf pack will face vampires, crazed mages, Wyrm spirits, fomori, myriad others, and take them all down, usually with some violence. But playing a "bad guy" Pentex-executive game, one has werewolves attacking warehouses, vampires attempting hostile corporate takeovers, mages stealing or destroying technology, wraiths haunting the actors of the dietary supplement commercials that led to their death, weird fairy-tale people who seem to have some undefined grudge, dudes with flaming baseball bats attacking your soldiers... All while still having to pay off government officials, balance the quarterly budget, and attend the ribbon-cutting ceremony for some low-income housing.

                      Basically, the bad guys work together because they have no morals or ethics.

                                                          Why shouldn't the "bad guys" be just as prone to fracturing? Why aren't they as petty and short sighted in their attempts at destruction as anyone else?
                                                          I think part of this has to do with these bad guys being controlled by powerful evil entities (the Wyrm, Oblivion, etc.) which are all more or less reflections of the same thing. *Humans* are a fractious lot. But when we talk about the *forces* that are at the core of these groups, we generally aren't talking about people anymore, so we can't ascribe human foibles to them. While it is hard to imagine a group such as the Nephandi being real (Let's destroy everything! Because... reasons!), the existence of the Caul and the dark forces of destruction that guide them not only make such a group possible, but also makes any alliance with similar groups believable as well. Their existence itself is already a 'gimme' as it is.

                                                          As for the protagonists, they are supposed to be human. And they are also supposed to be largely ignorant of one another, being at the bottom of the conspiracy ladder, whereas the baddies tend more toward the top of the pyramid. They don't have benefit of our knowledge either... "Did you see that? That looked like a changeling power! She must be a Sidhe or something!" Um, no. It's not like you went to "supernatural people night school" or something. It sort of makes the "super friends" scenario a little bit improbable, at least at first. But then again, we are playing a game about vampires and werewolves, and the whole point is to have fun, so whatever.
