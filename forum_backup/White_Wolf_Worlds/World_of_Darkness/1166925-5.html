    Originally posted by Aleph View Post
    Since being in the Technocracy it's a full time job that has everything to do with controling the world of humans and mages, as their name suggest, I would say that those would deserve a new terminology, similar to Roge but w/o the negative connotation. They're not Technocrats because they don't care about Control, but they do follow the rules set by the Technocracy and don't cause trouble. Sympathisers, perhaps?
    I don't agree with the criterion you gave in regard to a Mage being a Technocrat. Descriptions of the Technocracy mention or imply that it is possible - with calling upon friends and contacts, having and calling upon personal achievements, knowledge, and power, and/or through skillful politics, and also through navigating the Technocracy's rules and duties - for a Technocrat to get away with ignoring or bending seemingly obligatory standards and rules within the Technocracy ; and that it happens regularly.
    I think a Mage is a Technocrat when the Mage uses technocratic methods and Foci - that were and are developed by the Technocracy - for what they do as a Mage and as a person ; while also being an active member of the Technocracy.
    Former Technocrats - who are no longer in the Technocracy, but still use technocratic Foci and methods successfully - could be considered and called technocratic Mages.

    Sympathizers is the term for mundane humans who are agents or retainers of Technocracy and who are partially initiated into the Technocracy's ideas and mission statement ; or depending how you look at it, presented a derivative version of these, one that is comprehensible for them. So another word for this kind of Technocrat Mages would be needed ; or the word ' sympathizer ' would have to be taken away from it's current use, to use it as a term for this kind of Technocrats.

                                            In short, I do think, given a couple of years that a M5 would be a good thing. Not to denigrate the work done with M20 (or M4 as it will be), and I will still be holding on to my dear Quintessential Edition, but I'd like to see some aspects of V5 enter into a re-development of the game ground up. Specifically:

                                            1) A re-emphasis of 'Paradigm' over factions - that is, characters being based on their personal beliefs primarily, over and above any particular group affiliation. Politics can still be part of the game, but a character could study Hermetic magic without being part of an Order of Hermes, and so on. Following on, a lot of the various Traditions, Crafts and Conventions could be condensed down to a more manageable list (at least in the core-book). Something in the region of less than ten, inclusive of techno-magic types (so no need for the provision of additional Technocratic Union options). I'd actually reconsider the whole Technocratic War theme too, which may be a bit reminiscent of the Mage Revised era, but I honestly wonder whether its really a prominent theme in the current times. I think Nephandi could be more emphasised as the main big bad.

                                            2) I really like the idea of having a mechanic similar to Hunger Dice, but based on Paradox.

                                            3) Some of the concepts in the magic system could be tidied up a bit - Forces could be better defined, for example - where it's now loosely (and incorrectly) defined as 'elemental energies', it could be more correctly refer to the interactions and motions of physical things. A good review of the language and terminology of the game, generally, would be great.

                                            4) A general review of the look and feel of the game, to match V5's 21st century look would certainly be interesting.

                                                    In the game we had this week for example, one Toreador character rolled a 'Messy critical' while questioning a crime witness. She interpreted the roll as being highly successful - the witness gave up all the information she was guarding - due to her using vampiric sexiness, and ended up giving her a Lingering Kiss. Now, during what was a routine scene, my Toreador player has now accumulated an obsessive fan, who she will be able to draw blood from on occasion, but will also have to manage the obsessiveness - it's become a really good plot device and player complication. The player loved this challenge, it brought out the themes of playing a Vampire character with personal style and it wouldn't have happened without the Hunger dice interacting at the table. In practice, we are finding that the Hunger dice seem to add about two extra, unscripted but very thematic scenes to every session we play. It's a brilliant mechanism.

                                                            Originally posted by Aleph View Post

                                                            I've never seen someone forget about their Paradox in play. It's such a huge deal in Mage - you're either gaining it or trying to avoid it all the time. I find rather hard to forget about it.

                                                            Altrough I've seen people refusing to play Mage because in their opinion Paradox was too intrusive. I've never tought that myself, but my experience with other people it's that they overemphasise Paradox even beyond what the rules say on the matter. Who knows?, maybe the new edition will be fair to their expectations.
                                                            I have, insofar, that Paradox is often treated as a bit of a chore for some STs and players, rather than an opportunity to really delve into some interesting scenarios. Some players just try to burn it off as a damage roll, rather than anything interesting. I've played in some groups that just try to avoid it altogether. Paradox shouldn't be a chore, and making an elegent mechanic to handle it and provoke a bit of story creation with it is a good thing. I do acknowledge that this is the intent with the current rules, but I think the mechanics of it could be improved in a manner similar to V5 (and Werewolf too with 'Rage' dice).

                                                            A Hunger dice mechanic is pretty cool because it requires less book keeping (you would only have one track 1-5). Making a magical effect would generally have you rolling a Paradox check (6+ on a D10 roll for no consequence, and 1-5 accruing a Paradox point). Vulgar effects might have players roll more than one dice to check, so players would be encouraged to keep things low key and Coincidental. The accumulated Paradox points would be translated as Paradox Dice to be included in all dice pools, exchanging for a one for one basis (as with Hunger dice). If characters have a Paradoxical Failure (no successes rolled, and one or more Paradox dice showing '1's) or if they have a Paradoxical Critical (roll two '0's with one of them being a Paradox die), then they would incrue some sort of ironical backlash from the effect they cast previously.

                                                            For example, say they cast an effect with Mind to make themselves invisible - accruing some Paradox along the way - and then get a backlash doing some other task later on - say driving in a car chase - then a Paradoxical Critical could be that your car seems to go invisible momentarily, bamboozeling your competitor who then crashes. A Paradoxical Failure, however, could have the character failing to perceive another vehicle in front of them and crashing. It's just a simple example, but it's really easy to interpret - because the situation has provided parameters for developing the narrative. Other informative aspects could include the paradigm of the character or just the STs narrative development of the story to suit.
