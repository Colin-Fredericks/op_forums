                                                            We have first to be clear if we're talking pre-GttT or post-GttT Syndicate. The whole Union is a completely different thing in each. Old T is the Conspiracy Behind the System, the villains who deliberately keep the world being how it is.

                                                            From Guide to the Technocracy onward they're a conspiracy with a plan, but not with the whole picture or full control. This distinction is really important, because this Technocracy is NOT the reason why the world is the way it is, how much it's their doing is debatable, and each of the Conventions changes from Secret Masters to Ideals Gone Wrong.

                                                            Next, identify where we run a risk of conflating things, and define them clearly.

                                                            The Revised Syndicate os not about Capitalism but about Capital. An important distinction and yes, we'll use Marx's definitions because they are the scientific definitions we have:

                                                            Capital is not money. Capital is money as the engine of production. This is a very new thing in history, and is at the center of Capitalism, but isn't limited to Capitalism.

                                                            Question: what you need in the first place to start to make something with economic value? The answer to this question is the engine of production of a society.

                                                            Before the Industrial Revolution it changed with time and place, but the common denominator
                                                            was land control (it still plays a huge role). In the late Middle Ages you could be rich as fuck, without land you couldn't do shit and no one would sell you land just for money if they weren't in a desperate situation.

                                                            Money already existed. Commerce already existed. But Capital didn't. Nobles with control over land could lend or take this control as the primary means to make things happen. Give an artisan a shop, give the right places to those who produce what they need and so forth.

                                                            So the transformation brought by the Industry that both birthed Capitalism and the Syndicate proper was not the system of relationships and power we call Capitalism (it has additional roots), but Capital: production became so fast and distribution so easy that you can, in fact, just buy your way into establishing a business.

                                                            It's so powerful and complete that today we have this weird problem where we can't easily understand Capital because we can't wrap our minds over the notion of its absence to have a contrast to compare.

                                                            And I think this is our first point to define: is my proposal coherent? Is the Syndicate about the specific system, the mere idea of money, or the basic framework where money itself is the medium to establish production?
