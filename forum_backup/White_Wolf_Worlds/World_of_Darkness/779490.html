    Wanting to get into cWoD, where do I start?

    12-03-2015, 10:45 PM
    I've been hearing about stuff like Vampire: The Masquerade and Werewolf: The Apocalypse for some time now, and recently decided to do some research to see if it was something I wanted to run for my local gaming group. After quite a bit of looking around on various forums and wikis, I've decided that cWoD sounds like a better fit for my group than nWoD. The question I have now is, where do I start? I'd like to know which game line is the best for newcomers to the storyteller system, the sourcebooks that I'd need to run it, and which books to pick up to best start the metaplot. Any other general tips you have for World of Darkness newbies would also be helpful.

          Well, there's a very useful "book" in the Dark Ages Basic Rules. It's not going to help much with the setting, and the metaplot side of things, but it's a nice read for what the essential cWOD rules look like (as best as you can given the number of variations there are). It's a Revised era document, so it will be different from the newer 20th anniversary books, but it's enough to get a better idea of the basics.

          Otherwise nothing more to add than Dwight's points. You're going to want to settle on one of the game lines (Vampire, Werewolf, and Mage are the easiest right now since the 20th books, while massive slogs, cover a lot of things that are spread around multiple books previously) to start.

              If you like political intrigue and centuries-old antagonists, then Vampire is your best bet.

              If you like fighting against Lovecraftian entities who literally control aspects of reality, then check out Werewolf.

              If you like philosophical and social themes including discussions about potential vs safety, then Mage will serve you well.

                          If you want a game where literally nothing matters in the long run, and everything will be destroyed by pseudolovecraftian beings, or a game about searching for hope where it might not exist, you want Wraith.

                          If you want a game where science is silly and wrong because it destroys wonder and creativity, then you need a different person to write the pitch for Changeling because I think it will only become a good game if the C20 core is more respectful of the wonder from understanding.

                          If you want a game about trying to fit into a society you barely understand, both more and less advanced than you're used to, with a passtime of trying to make sure both the crazy and the 'a god am I' members of your species, look into Demon.

                                                  I'd avoid the metaplot if you're new to oWoD and then research it more after you've played a game or two to see if you like the metaplot. Not everyone is into the metaplot, but some people are. It really is all a matter of taste and what your ST is intending for their chronicle. The best place to start thematically would be the 1e corebook, but that is rare and the rules are wonky, so I'd say go with Vampire: The Masquerade 20th Anniversary Edition or V20 for short. It's the most complete of all the books, is metaplot-neutral (doesn't force the metaplot onto you but does have options for if you want to use the metaplot), and is the most mechanically superior of the Masquerade corebooks.

                                                      It sounds like you're looking at the metaplot as a source of adventure hooks - which it can be, but they're often embedded in books across the various lines. There are timeline guides for the various games that flag what happens when and where it appears. (The forthcoming Beckett's Jyhad Diary is a Vampire: The Masquerade 20th Anniversary Edition sourcebook about metaplot events and various ways to use them, while the rest of the V20 line largely avoids metaplot and references the changes it made to the setting over the years in sidebars.)

                                                      If you're looking for ready-to-use adventures and chronicles, they're available as standalone books and as part of sourcebooks, and there's a list here.

                                                          If you're into metaplot, pick up:

                                                          Nights of Prophecy
                                                          Time of Thin Blood
                                                          The 'by Night' books
                                                          The clanbook series

                                                          The first book has plenty of story ideas. The second details a lot of the Final Nights stuff. The 'by Night' books detail cities and often have much smaller metaplot developments, providing for plot hooks without causing huge setting changes. The clanbooks offer lots of clan secrets (and delusions) that shed light on the history of Cainites (or obfuscate it).

                                                          The Giovanni Chronicles and Transylvanian Chronicles have sweeping chronicle ideas that feed into the wider metaplot quite heavily.

                                                          But if you buy Vampire Revised, I only think you need the core book plus Guide to the Camarilla and Guide to the Sabbat. The ST's Companion and ST's Handbook are useful too. Everything else is just fluff (okay, not quite, but only the first three books are really 'essential' in my mind).
