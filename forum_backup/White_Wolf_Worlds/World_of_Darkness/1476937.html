    Time travel to wipe out the Technocracy

    03-22-2022, 05:39 AM
    I have long since fancied a rules and setting hack that lets the Ascension War turn Hot and engulf the other supernaturals as the Technocracy overreaches to expand and intensify the Pogrom, either out of its own hubris or from the covert instigation of Threat Null. Most Night-Folk reorganize into Triatic uber-factions that war for reality. A Technocracy civil war occurs between corrupted and uncorrupted factions that folds into the larger conflict. Consensus becomes accepting of low-key sci-fi, fantasy, and horror stuff. Banality and the Gauntlet considerably weaken and Tradition mages, shapeshifters, and changelings experience a major resurgence and population boost. The world becomes a war-torn science fantasy setting on the brink of Apocalypse.

    This setting change further enables my preferred rule hacks of no Vulgar Without Witnesses, rolling Arete + Sphere for magick, lethal soak for all supernaturals, no Avatar Storm, no or greatly weakened Acclimation or Disembodiment, etc. but they would exist even without the Reality War. The Consensus never became strong to weaken magick casting or cause Paradox in most cases without the active and present support of Sleeper disbelief or the mage bungling the spell. Awakening or getting a fae soul strengthens the body as much as being a vampire or shapeshifter, if in a different way. The Avatar Storm never existed, its destructive effects get partially replaced by the Reality War without restricting access to the Umbra.

    In this context, I got the idea to apply a classic sci-fi trope, time travel to change the past. A radical group of Tradition mages in the Dynamic faction gets desperate because they fear the Entropy faction may win (or theoretically the Stasis one, but they are the weakest side) or the war itself may destroy the world. They decide to travel to the past and prevent the rise of the Technocracy in the first place. They somehow harness the massive amount of Quintessence that is likely necessary to nullify the mind-boggling amount of Paradox that rewriting a millennium or so of history would cause. Therefore, Paradox cannot stop them. They are aware the timeline change would likely prevent the Council of Traditions and the technological and social benefits of modernity (unless mystical equivalents arise) from existing, but deem it an acceptable sacrifice to save the world. They could not care less if it means mages and other supernaturals get to keep lording it over humans as feudal masters, since they deem covert rule by the Technocracy and the Camarilla has been just as bad and it is still way better than the Apocalypse.

    Which would be the best focal point to prevent the Technocracy from existing? I have singled out two, and possibly another couple. The obvious possiblities seem intervening at Mistridge in 1210 to cause the defeat of the Craftmasons (which in all likelihood would cause the faction to be crushed or disband) or bomb the 997 Gathering of the Square that set up the Craftmasons and their nascent alliance with other proto-Order of Reason groups in the first place. Without the Craftmasons, chances are the proto-Technocracy never gets organized. A third obvious possibility is to destroy the Convention of the White Tower in 1325, although I am concerned by then the nascent Order of Reason had already got too momentum by then for its rise to be disrupted. Another possibility IMO is to kill the Tremere leaders in ca. 1000 when they turn themselves into vampires. Without the major distraction of the First Massassa War, chances are the Hermetics are able to nip the Craftmasons in the bud before they get too much momentum. Which do you think would be the best option?

    I suppose messing with the timeline this way is going to have major repercussions. Since Paradox has been defanged through massive Quintessence expenditure, I suppose we may expect rival time-travelers from the Static and/or Entropic factions to try and undo the change, or possibly even tinker with the timeline to their benefit (e.g. to prevent the Council of the Traditions and/or the Changeling Way from existing). The Reality War may well acquire a temporal frontline. Your ideas on this?

    In the case the Tradition/Dynamic radicals get successful, which kind of future are they going to create with no Technocracy? Broadly speaking, they are sincere in their commitment to save the world (even if they put the welfare of Tradition mages and other Dynamic supernaturals before the one of Sleepers) and their concerns are not without merit, although their fears might be exaggerated. Speaking from the ST perspective of an omniscient observer, the simplest and most effective solution to prevent an Entropic victory or too much damage to the world (a certain degree is likely unavoidable) may be a truce and alliance of convenience between the Dynamic and Static factions to fight the Entropics and set up a science fantasy Consensus, but this is far from simple or bound to occur.

    As it concerns the way time is going to work in this context, I waver between malleable time (think Back to the Future) or time travel causing alternate timelines (think Avengers: Endgame). Your suggestion?

      One difficulty for a time traveler is that the changes you've outlined for how Magick works now aren't going to apply then. Consider a more modest trip to explain this: a 20-year jump from 2022 to 2002. When your time traveling makes arrive, they're going to find themselves in a reality in magickal turmoil, where not only is Paradox not weaker, it's coming down like a sledgehammer: the MRev-era Paradox mechanics are in full effect.

      Travel back to the time of the Craftmasons' assault on Mistridge, and you'll find that the nature of magick will be even more different; one might even say alien to the time travelers. Their very presence is likely to be an ongoing “Paradox” effect (though back then, it behaved very differently). Even if they themselves aren't being rejected by reality, their Magicks likely will be: it would bea good way for them to discover that their supposedly authentic magickal beliefs aren't really as authentic as they thought, and Effects that they take for granted as being Coincidental will turn out to be Vulgar (even without Witnesses, because this is a time period before “Vulgar without Witnesses” ceased to be a thing).

      On the other hand, it's possible that the time travelers bring a bubble of modern-day reality with them to the Dark Ages, allowing them to continue to use their Magicks in the way that they're used to. Mage even had support for such a concept in its existing material: Marauders.

      My inclination would be to go with an Everett Volume, where by default changes to the past tend to cause an alternate timeline. That said, I could see Time mastery being used to set up an ongoing Effect that works against that, and attempts to integrate the changes made into the original timeline. The more drastic the change, the more likely it is to disrupt the Effect, spawning an Everett Volume in a manner not unlike a Paradox Realm and trapping the time travelers in it.

Broadly speaking, there is going to be Plan A): achieve a truce, an alliance of convenience, and a compromise deal for a syncretic science fantasy Consensus (kinda like your vanilla kitchen-sink comic-book universe) between the Dynamic and Static factions in the present.                                   By the way, I am already assuming the time travel effect is going to require truckloads of Quintessence to defuse Paradox since the timeline change is global, rewrites 700-1000 years of history (depending on the temporal flashpoint used), and massively changes the Consensus, the status of humanity, and the fate of most supernatural splats (most notably the mages, shapeshifters, and fae) and their Umbrood allies. The actors might get a little slack since they come from an Apocalyptic temporal flashpoint.where the Consensus is being shaked to the core and the Tellurian faces the very real possibility of the End Times if the Entropics get their way or the war escalates too much. I suppose the Everett Volume effect is going to take just as much effort in terms of successes and mystical juice since it has a similar impact. I am reminded of similar feats in the Ascension and Apocalypse books. But then again, that is the nature of the beast.
