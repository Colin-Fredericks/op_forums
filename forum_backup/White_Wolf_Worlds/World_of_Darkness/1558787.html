    V5 and W5 hard to kill aspect

    03-11-2026, 06:11 AM
    I've never played V5 or W5 (yet), but i have both books and the survivability of Vampires and Werewolves seems a bit extreme, no?
    According to W5 the only way to definitly kill a werewolf is fire, silver or complete destruction of his body (cutting his head off seems pretty definitive too)
    V5 say that the only way to kill a vampire is fire, daylight, decapitaton and body destruction (using acid as an exemple in the book)

    Aside from those any other aggr damage, upgraded from bashing or directly inflicted, can be healed over time. Of course the system is different than V20 edition, keeping only 2 types of damage but the general health idea is still the same, some wound are worst than others and need more time to heal.

    On one hand it make these creature scarier and more myth accurate, on the other hand it means that in game using fire and their specific weakness is the only way to kill them

    I'm just curious of how it is percieved in the community, do you think it's good/bad or just a specific mechanic that work because it is designed for V5 gaming rules?

      I don't think there's that much discussion about this specific aspect of things... or at least not in places I hang out (which means not places that are exclusively WoD5 focused).

      From back in the V5 playtest era, my impression was very much that there's a bit of a disconnect between the description and the rules. In the descriptions of how enduring they are, killing them seems hard. In the actual rules? Sure harder than squishy mortals, but not so hard.

      I think some of that came from the initial concept of tuning conflicts into 3-rounds of rolling. If two vampires are fighting to the final death, and after three rounds one is clearly outmatching their opponent, the idea is that you stop rolling and then move on to narrating the execution. So "doing massive damage" doesn't really need a lot of mechanical rigor to it, it's just meant to guide what describing that looks like.

          In W5, I would argue, one of the issues is that the situation is the complete opposite of "hard to kill". Especially in the beginning when players aren't familiar with all the systems. Some of the punishing mechanics can sneak up on you and disrupt things. For example, you gotta be careful in managing your character's Willpower and Rage. For example, using Gifts and Rites requires either Rage checks (you roll a die, 6 or better nothing happens, 5 or lower you use a dot of Rage) or spending Willpower. But if that lands you at Rage 0, you enter a state called "losing the wolf". In that state, you can't shapeshift into anything other than Homid or Lupus and you can't use Gifts and Rites. And the only way to get out of that is to howl at the moon at night. Unless you have a specific Rite... but of course you can't use the Rite when the PC with the Rite has "lost the wolf" themselves, for example. Also, it means that landing at Rage 0 during combat is really bad. The Willpower-cost is another tricky thing because once you enter the Umbra, you have to spend a point of Willpower at the start of every scene or you will take a point of Aggravated Damage. And the Umbra Agg-Damage is another thing that will kill Garou (well, it turns your character into a bad Spirit but technically you lose your character is what that means). This then is also compounded by Willpower being very important for when Frenzy occurs (if you fail two Willpower tests, your character is supposed to fight everything in sight until the Frenzied character is the only thing left standing).

          My experience as ST with W5 is that it's a tough balancing act. On one hand, in the few turns where a PC might use their Crinos Form they can dish out a lot of damage and if you have multiple PCs use their Crinos Form, it's tough to create a respectable foe. But on the other hand, if a player didn't manage their Rage right and ends up at Rage 0 during combat, then that character immediately becomes pretty useless and then any enemy that was created with the Crinos Form in mind will easily kill that PC. And even with the Crinos Form... since Regeneration triggers Rage checks, that is yet another way a character might end up at Rage 0 during combat.

                      I'm gonna focus on the W5-side of things here... In W5 the "losing the wolf"-status matters a lot. I had that happen in the oneshot I was running. Because W5 has so many punitive mechanics, things just pile up.

                      As it's described on pg. 134, you get this:
                      A Garou who has been incapacitated by Aggravated damage is taken out of action and falls unconscious, unless they’re able to regenerate at least one point of Aggravated damage. (This can be done even if unconscious, providing they have enough Rage.) If allowed to rest for a night they also regenerate one Aggravated health level automatically, as normal, but if they Sustain aggravated damage from fire or silver before resting, they die.
                      I highlighted the important bit here. Because here's the issue with W5. Yes, technically only silver, complete bodily destruction/decapitation, fire and being in the Umbra too long will kill a Garou. But here's what's going to happen if you get to this point with no Rage. Your tracker is filled up with Agg-Damage, the character falls unconscious, for whatever reason the enemy is prevented from giving the character the true death-blow, they're allowed to rest for the night and regenerate one health-level. BUT... since they have no Rage, they've "lost the wolf" which means until the next night when they can howl at the moon, the character can't use Regeneration, can't shift into the "supernatural Forms" and can't use Gifts or Rites. The character is just a normal human or normal wolf until that time. So, a character might not die but it will take quite a while before the character is back to being a functional Garou.

                      And with any WtA-game, getting into combat is far more common than with a VtM-game, I would say.

                      Overall, both a vampire in Torpor and a Garou who has "lost the wolf" are pretty easy targets unless there are others around who protect them in that weakened state.
