    Originally posted by Magus Maximus View Post
    I'm not sure that "Touched by Faith" is exactly the same as "Divine Revelation".

    A "Mage" that holds a "Touched by Fate" Paradigm could even be an atheist. Heck, traditional Buddhism is atheistic (or rather speaking, agnostic), but Buddha (the historical Siddhartha Gautama) could very well be "Touched by Fate", despite preaching an agnostic religion that is borderline atheistic.

    I think there's strong ground for a 4th axis on this.

    "Divine Revelation" is Gnosticism at its highest form (we are all the One-God) or any one Theistic, Dualistic or Politheistic faiths (I'm a servant of the One True God / The God of Light and Goodness / The Gods of Creation).

    A "Touched by Fate" (we need perhaps a better name, semantics actually help our thinking processes, there is REAL power in words after all) could be for example for a group of people that believe that they have a genetic mutation that gives them psionic powers...

    That's not scientific, that's not mystic but it's also not divine revelation.
    How is it not mystical insight?

        Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post

        In brief: too many moving parts, and too much emphasis on the mechanics as opposed to what the mechanics are supposed to represent.
        I don't think it's too many moving parts. It's 11 items in total divided in 4 different groups, with those 4 groups being combined to create any sort of Paradigm. This could be more like an easy table for blending in your favorite "Paradigm of the Week" in a more concise manner. It's just a way to facilitate things.

        Mechanics may or may not be added in order to better illustrate things. For instance, a Psychic that believes he only needs to contract his sphincter to use his psyonics; we could place some sort of game mechanics for it, maybe for flavor, maybe for game balance. But that's not an absolute necessity.

        Now that I thought about it, maybe on item C. Openess of Paradigm, maybe I should add item 4 - Unrelated: essentially, you believe that what you (and your little group) do is one thing, but what others do is completely unrelated. This would be a good Paradigm for Texhnocrats: we are Enlightened Scientists, we do a higher form of Science; there's no "Magic" BS behind our strict scientific methods. Those Trads are just a bunch of poor sods infected by trans dimensional (dangerous) aliens with reality warping capabilities; don't fall for their "Awakened" BS.

        The other thing that I mentioned "mechanics" about is in how they could help better construct the setting and its cosmology. For instance, having this item C. 4 -, in relation to the openess of the Paradigm, which means that your Paradigm dictates that what you do and what others do is completely unrelated - it would be like comparing the Gifts of Werewolves with the Disciplines of vampires. Well then, in such a case, we could have the game mechanics of "No Avatar", meaning that you don't hear strange voices or see strange things.

        These mechanics could indeed help, instead of hinder, the more subjective view of the world. And no matter what people say, the mechanics influence A LOT of how either how things actually work, or at least how the players perceive them, and that's highly important.

        Imagine for instance having mechanics for PSYONIC POWERS that don't follow the mechanics for "Magik". Then, a "Psy" could actually claim that he's a mutant with psyonic powers rather than a self deluded Dr Strange.

        Certain specific Mechanics, for instance, could give more credibility towards "Divine Revelation". If you just simply have a mechanic that "You must adhere to certain Disciplines of Faith" otherwise you "lose your divine favor", then you are ACTUALLY given credit to the idea that your "cleric class" is "getting his spells" from a God or Gods, rather than just being a self delusional fool lying to yourself.

        The idea is that "willworking" is one possibility... But that's all. Maybe all Mages are either blessed by God or (willingly or unknowingly) servants of Satan; maybe all "Mages" actually are just a bunch of psyonic mutants, or maybe they all just draw from "The Mists", or are infected by Aliens; or, it's all willworking. Or maybe, we actually have "Psys" and "Mages" and "Clerics" and "Shamans" and they are as distinct "splats" as werewolves and vampires are and their power sources actually have distinct origins. ALL of these possibilities SHOULD be viable, but if you don't have adequate mechanics to support that, it all falls apart. That's the only reason why I advocate for mechanics, not for simply adding modifiers to piles of dice rolls.

            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post

            How is it not mystical insight?
            Yeah well... We'd have to better define what mystical insight is. In a way, it's a sort of "pseudoscientific" approach of existence, in the sense that it obviously don't use the Scientific Method, but also it isn't just "God wills it". Mystical insight considers that there's some rational to the world, some order that can be understood by reason (not necessarily the classical Greek kind of "reason", but what I mean is that it isn't just the whims of some entities, while at the same time being beyond just a Materialistic or Mechanicist view).

            What "Fate" or more appropriately speaking, the "Unknown" or the UNKOWABLE, is not that. Paradigms of that kind don't believe in "Mystical Insight", as in, the capability to discover the truths about the universe by will and rational thought; instead, you are a "Convergence Point", a "Universal Confluence", a "Reality Abomination" or whatever else.

            Whatever it is, you CANNOT achieve knowledge by your own will or effort. The source of your powers is unknown - maybe even Unknowable. Maybe they are a curse from Cthulhu, maybe "Da Force" simply erupted inside of you, or you have mutant genes that ignite your psyonic powers - but there's no explanation on how they work. They simply do. Or maybe they are the results of alien parasites from other dimensions.

            Be that as it may, you now are a "reality warper" for some inscrutable reason.

            I agree thou, the example of Buddha was a bad one. Buddha absolutely is the result of Mystical Insight. An Unknown source of power for "Mages" however is absolutely not that; Buddha was very aware that the world is Samsara, an endless cicle of suffering, and the only sensible way to escape it is to achieve Nirvana by not participating in this cicle (Buddhism is basically a death cult, or at the very least, a life denying cult).

            That's mystical insight.

            Unknown source has none of that. Why do you "warp reality"? Who knows? What's the purpose of it all? Nobody knows, and that's probably not even relevant at all.
