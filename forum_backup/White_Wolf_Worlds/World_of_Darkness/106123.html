    What are the main critisisms of Demon: the Fallen?

    04-05-2014, 04:07 AM
    As the tagline says, I'm interested in what the big critisisms were of Demon: the Fallen. When the game was released I had quit White Wolf for a time, so I basically missed all the arguments at the time, I just keep hearing bits about how it invalidated other gamelines and stuff. Care to enlighten me?

      Well, first off, it does have some annoying mechanical issues. Nothing too extreme by cWOD standards, and it deserves a bit of a pass for only having one edition so there was no chance to iron out a lot of the kinks later. Though it also deserves a bit less of a pass because it was so late in the cWOD plenty of those problems shouldn't have been hard to spot from experience two 2-3 editions of multiple other games. Demons also had a slightly annoying set of, "we scoff at your supernatural powers," traits. All demons were completely immune to supernatural mind control and fear effects. All demons could pierce any supernatural illusion made by a non-demon with a basic roll that did not factor in sort of power level in the creation of the illusion; busting through a starting level mage's Forces illusion is as easy as seeing past a 4th Gen vampire's elder Discipline based illusions. Demons sensory range for detecting supernatural effects is measured in miles, and they have an unlimited (including cross dimensional) ability to hear people talking about them if one of their two special names - so not any name or alias - is used.

      The fluff side of it was largely that Demon basically went out of its way to try to present a unified WOD, but forced all the other game lines into Demon's point of view in the process. It didn't necessarily invalidate other game lines, but it did frequently make it look like the fluff of the other game lines was just false history of what the Demon books really said happened. There were multiple game-lines that Demon basically said demons were responsible for directly. Demon also chose to be explicitly Abrahamic. While Vampire's main fluff is clearly Abrahamic in nature, there's plenty of reason to doubt that whatever happened actually hewed so closely to those stories. For example Kindred of the Ebony Kingdom presents a completely different take on the origin of vampires that works just as well to explain how they came to be and work the way they do. Demon said, "yeah, we're fallen angels from the Bible." No attempt to make the demons in the game more of a cross-cultural phenomenon. No attempt to at least call into question whether the default Biblical take was really what happened or not.

          Mechanically, the game is a bit of a mess. As Heavy pointed, I suppose that can be forgiven considering it only had one edition. I have no issue re-working things for my groups’ use, so this isn’t a huge deal for me. Personally, I like the idea of Demon’s having innate uber-powers. I never got to test this with other Spalts, but I think it would be interesting to play up the summoning/binding aspects of a Demon’s nature. [My take, mechanically, was Demons would be very powerful, but the other Splats would have avenues to control them. This idea is still on my “To-Do-Test-Play” list.]

          For me, the Fluff is where the game fell apart. Heavy already summed it perfectly: Demon “…went out of its way to try to present a unified WOD.” I could almost forgive this if they offered some explanation about what is REALLY going on with (for example) Neverborn, Umbrood, Banes, Middle-Kingdom-Demony-Thing, etc., but it doesn’t. The absolute certainty that the background was presentenced with felt very shoe-horned.

          In VtM, characters use their Generation to determine how far removed they are from the First Vampire. In the Euro-centric outlook that many of the Clans have, this First Vampire is Caine, but there is still some mythological ambiguity surrounding the “Dark Father.” In Demon, not really the case. They ARE fallen angels. THIS IS what happened. Half-way through the fluff of Demon, I kept expecting to see a little sidebar that suggested that the returned Fallen are remembering history flavored by their hosts’ knowledge and memories. Never happened. [Oddly, the same lack of ambiguity that I found so jilting in the Corebook fit perfectly with the material in Devils Due. It worked better in the DA setting.]

          Demon was so dependent on WOD metaplot, I think it should have been a post-Splat for WrtO, not an entirely new game. Demon feels like it should have been what Orpheus was.

          Finally, the thing I found most off-putting. I bought Demon: The Fallen, expecting to read Demon: The Fallen and instead I ended up reading Angel: The Prophecy. I can't pick up the book without imagining Christopher Walken...

                  DtF was (and still is) great. The main reason people didn't like it is because they felt, like Heavy Arms said, that other lines were blurred away by Demon the Fallen's Abrahamic nature. And since someone who holds dear to his/her heart Werewolf's Triat and Mage's wide variety paradigms, it can be frustrating. The only problem I have with that reasoning is the fact that it is meant to be its own gameline - not an attempt to unify and retcon everything. Should Vampire players be mad because of Werewolf's main mythology that doesn't fit with Caine's? Or vice versa? Of course not. Some elements of the metaplot and the World of Darkness' universe have been adapted (very well I must say, at least to my tastes) to fit a DtF universe. Just like most other lines do.

                  There's something for pretty much any kind of play. From the very mundane to plain epic, depending on what a group wants to play. And even after all these years I am still amazed at how great Creation was described, the different feel and touch for each of the Houses of demons, the factions, etc.

                  Great game. One of my favorite lines, ever.

                                      Originally posted by Nazfool View Post

                                      George R.R. Martin's vampires in Fevre Dream self-identified as the descendants of Caine long before Vampire the Masquerade was a twinkling in Mark Rein DOT Hagen's eye. Grendel is also explicitly picked out as a descendant of Caine in Beowulf.

                                      Milton suffered greatly for his "positive" portrayal of Lucifer in Paradise Lost. "He shook the pillars of Heaven" could mean either that, hey, look, big rebellion and that's ALL he managed or it could mean, this guy managed to SHAKE the foundation of God's supposedly immutable firmament with his audacity. Also, seemingly had a good reason for the whole rebellion thing.

                                      Demon's foundation may be cliche, but it's one of the most awesome and pervasive cliche's in Abrahamic culture. Which is a significant part of the world. It's a stronger and richer core myth than the assembled Vampire traditions as made sexy through modernist literature or than the various werewolf myths scattered through the world. I guess it treads a fine line though. In religious based societies, it's dead on blasphemy. In secular societies it's a cliche and possibly in poor taste.

                                      I think there is an element of "Fuck you!" to the whole game. Coming from the angle of someone who played RPG's back when they were Satanist traps to guide my soul to Hell. Back when demons and devils in D&D had to become "fiends". This game would NEVER have hit the shelves. I think the designers have a bit of that going on.

                                      It recovers the same ground In Nomine did. Not sure it did so better. In fact, if pressed, it probably didn't. (You also got to play un-Fallen angels there too, that's a plus). One of it's strengths however is exactly the problem people had with it. It was part of larger universe (even if they are all superstring alternates or difficult to manage integrations). There were all the other supernaturals out there and you could bring them in. It did suffer from, "We have all the answers. We know the Truth. The rest of you are silly, ill informed and got it wrong. Bless your little souls... (and can we have those now please? We're HUNGRY.". It suffered from that A LOT. I've been able to accept it to a degree and the rest I can put down to hubris. There is no splat that suffers more from that than Demon. And that's the way it should be. Also, (too many points), this game line probably knew from the start that it was doomed. The New World was coming. So there was a sense in which they were trying to wrap things up.

                                      That being said, this is the one OWOD game I have never gotten to play. For that alone, I am interested.
