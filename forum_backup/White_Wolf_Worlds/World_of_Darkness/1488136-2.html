    I am curious to see how the upcoming "advanced rules," book that's been talked about by the Paradox team shapes up. It seems, to me, very necessary to build on the V5 core system as the much more action heavy W5 looms on the horizon. Considering we also have the more whimsical quests of Dreaming, and high concept of Mage, expanded rules seem like they're going to get really important really fast. V5's condescended combat might work for vampires overwhelming mortals, or the Driven quickly finding out if their plans were perfect or trash, but I get the feeling that they're not going to hold up to a PC pack of Garou taking on a pack of BSDs in terms of capturing the feel of WtA's more splatterpunk happy history. Likewise the systems seem like they'll cut struggles short before the creative twists of fae cantrips or awakening magic can really come into things.

    While it is positive to see signs that Paradox is trying to course correct here, it's still a big question as to if it's too late for V5 and H5 being as good as they could have been to push the WoD5 line to it's full potential.

        There's something else I want to get out of my chest.

        Why does hunter the reckoning 5th hate hunter organizations so much?

        I get the impression that they want to force player characters to play in small groups with no support (tier 1 playstyle in hunter the vigil). I'm not against this type of game, it goes very well if you want to emphasize the feelings of paranoia, isolation and the fact that you're alone against the horror, but why make this the only option? I don't see why being driven and being a part of a larger group has to be incompatible, and their explanation for it is pretty lame. Likewise, I just don't get why they felt like they had to make every hunter organization downright evil at worst and selfish at best. I would really like to see more nuanced hunter organizations.

            I don't think it is the only option. It is in HtR5, but my guess is they will build upon the "orgs" in future supplements and possibly VtM5 books. Not sure how in detail "2nd Inquisition" details these orgs as playable. Hunter's Hunted II for 20th-Edition also isn't that seasoned by today's standards, so this customers might already be satisfied.

            And, I feel, a this are non-org Hunters with this mechanics and this are org-hunters with those mechanics would have made the book a mess. I already hated the "this are vampires with disciplines, and these are quasi-vampires with this mechanics"-approach in VtM5.


            And sure, 5th editions are continuations of the games lines, but they are not that compatible anymore, due to changes in setting and mechanics. So, with the Storyteller's Vault, they might go a similar way as D&D3.5 did with Pathfinder 1. Sure, the license agreement of the Storyteller's Vault is by far not the Open Game License early version - but still, if you are interested into particular game, you might find good additional material there. And sometimes stuff that seamlessly fits with the printed material, since it was material cut from books due to editorial decisions, time or word count constrains. And sometimes it is home breed material of lesser quality or material that doesn't fit your head canon. So, Storyteller's Vault, for me, is always a bit of grain.
