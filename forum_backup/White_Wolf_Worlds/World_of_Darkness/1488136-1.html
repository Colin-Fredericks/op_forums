        I think the biggest killer to any success with the "my 2nd TTRPG" crowd? Almost $40 for the pdf.

        While, in general, I think RPG pdfs are undervalued and the industry should normalize higher price points for them, that's a huge bar for entry compared to other options. A few bucks more than your main competitors is one thing. This is going to be $10-15 more than most similar products (many of which are giving you more bang for your buck like more thorough antagonist creation systems), and there's no discount for a PoD + pdf combo to help entice people to accept a higher base price or something similar.

        Considering the lack of audience among older games already familiar with the breadth of options in the hunter genre, who are also more likely to be financially well off and would be more like to spend if you tickled their nostalgia a bit more (making the Driven capable of a true Imbued style of play, or not creating an artificial divide between the Driven and orgs so HH could feel appealed to), this feels like a very large issue for a product that's going to rely so much of first time buyers.

                            Having looked at the book and given it a quick overview I personally mostly like what I see. But I also understand why some people are annoyed or at least confused. The challenge here is what the new edition is intended to be. I think with V5 and what we have seen about W5 the philosophy of the new editions seems to be to streamline the rules and setting, reset the metaplot in a way that makes it easier for new players to get into the setting without all the accumulated story and lore from 30 years of the game, and to borrow elements from the NwoD/CoD line. Some people seem to just not like that at all but I think if you want a full-on continuation of the original game then WoD20 is for you and not the WoD5 line. The issue here, for what it's worth, is that there is no H20 it's not as clear what this game is an update of. It's not an update of Hunter the Reckoning because it's about mortal hunters and not Imbued as was the original. And it's not really anupdate of Hunters Hunted because it focuses exclusively from a player perspective on non-org hunters. So on it's own terms I think there's a lot of good in this game but I also think they missed the opportunity to either provide a new take on the Imbued or to provide a broader set of options for mortal hunters including more detailed options for playing as part of orgs (a did Hunter the Vigil). Honestly I think it would have been better if they'd genuinely taken the idea of HtV and ported it over to WoD5 ie rules for playing mortal hunters from street level to hunter orgs like the Inquisition, Arcanum etc.

                                Originally posted by scifirabbi View Post
                                The challenge here is what the new edition is intended to be. I think with V5 and what we have seen about W5 the philosophy of the new editions seems to be to streamline the rules and setting, reset the metaplot in a way that makes it easier for new players to get into the setting without all the accumulated story and lore from 30 years of the game, and to borrow elements from the NwoD/CoD line.
                                I think there's a disconnect because the nWW and Paradox teams have never stated most of this.

                                Streamlining is certainly a goal, but there's been plenty of debate over how much streamlining has really happened vs. just shifting where the systems are clunky (or just streamline the rules and put more work on the ST to make up for it instead of actually having a more streamlined process).

                                The metaplot is explicitly not reset, but continued. Sure there's a bit of retconning (just like every other edition). However V5 assumes in broadstrokes that everything in the previous editions happened as the books say that they happened. Just more stuff happened between 2003 and 2021. Nothing indicates that W5 is having a reset either instead of using the massive gap in time to put in all sorts of new metaplot material to get from 2003 to the new "now" Paradox wants. This point is particularly galling because the Driven are a brand new thing, the Imbued are ignored, and the orgs are pushed aside to make room for the Driven after formerly being treated as underdog mortals fighting against much more powerful supernatural forces and humanity's unknowing submission to supernatural control instead of being jobbers.

                                That then impedes new players coming into the games. It's a fifth edition of a long running game line. People, even brand new players, are going to expect there to be a lot of stuff fans of the last four editions know about the game that they don't.... it would be kinda weird if that wasn't the case (even in D&D once you're playing in existing settings like Forgotten Realms there's lore and metaplot to know). There's a big difference between trying to make the lore more accessible (which V5 has some good moves on doing) and just shunting off lots of lore so the new players know it exists but don't know what it is (something V5 also does, and H5 does instead of trying to have lore at all).

                                And, while WoD5 does seem to be borrowing liberally from the CofD for design ideas, lets not leave out the other games they borrow from that they don't own. Which isn't a bad thing (that's how RPGs evolve) but it is generally good form to acknowledge when systems are lifted from other games rather than make it sound like the newest game to use them made them up themselves.

                                Some people seem to just not like that at all but I think if you want a full-on continuation of the original game then WoD20 is for you and not the WoD5 line.
                                This is unfair. The WoD5 line is a continuation of the previous editions. The 20th books are largely just omnibus collections with a bit of refinement of the previous three editions. The 20th books didn't continue the setting or plots. They're adamantly compatible with being used with any of the eras of the original run of the games by including rules for before and after various rules-impacting metaplot events. V20 covers things like Clans changing Disciplines (Dementation vs. Dominate Malks) and weaknesses (Banu Hakim and the Curse) so you can play before an event and through it with the attendant changes, ignore the event and keep the older method, or after the event with the post-event rules.

                                If I want to see what happened to vampires after 2003, my options are V5, fan works on STV, or making it up myself. V20 doesn't do that.

                                As I frequently point out, a lot of us that where around when Rich and Eddy announced their own new edition for VtM before the Paradox acquisition of the IP, were all really excited about it. VtM needed a proper new edition badly. Why a lot of us don't like V5 is because of what V5 turned out to be, not because V20 is what we really wanted all along.

                                The issue here, for what it's worth, is that there is no H20 it's not as clear what this game is an update of.
                                It's not an update of anything. It's a new thing using an old name.

                                            It's worth noting that open development isn't the same thing as doing large playtests or Onyx Path's method of using Kickstarter backers as additional feedback sources via early manuscript releases and early backer pdfs.

                                            Open dev was on it's way out before all the CofD 2e cores used it, as the devs that used it the most either left the CofD/OPP or soured on how useful it was as a tool compared to more traditional dev with open playtests and backer feedback.

                                            Something that has frustrated people a lot with V5 is that there was a massive playtest and it seemed like a lot of feedback from it was simply ignored (including things some V5 writers that have come on since seem to feel shouldn't have been ignored), on top of the last round of playtests being dropped to get the book out for Gen Con (extremely old fashioned thinking as "releasing at Gen Con" simply isn't the event it was back before digital distribution became common place. It seems like Paradox learned the exact wrong lesson from how V5's playtesting went and opted to simply avoid early feedback in any sense rather than find a way to get some of the benefits of open dev in a still close dev process.

                                                            So what I wanted from V5 was a similar engagement process to the one suggested by OPP, though a CoD-style process would have been the best. Another round of playtesting would also have gone a long way.

                                                            E.g., I feel like making Hunger more like Drive/Desperation, thereby allowing it to cover bonuses like Blood Surge and Discipline dice pool bonuses as well as things like losing control, would have gone a long way.
