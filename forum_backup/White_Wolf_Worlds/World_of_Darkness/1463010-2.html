    Leaving star wars Aside because I don't see the point.

    Honestly, I don't like the Disparate alliance. Largely because the Taftani are one of my favorite factions, and while some of the changes in M20 are good. The vast majority of what made the Taftani awesome was gutted.

    And honestly that kind of seems to be the norm. Much of the unique flavor of these crafts was trimmed away to make them into pale imitations of the Traditions. And that to me just... Kind of sucks.

    Like, to a degree it feels like they wanted to simplify the crafts and bring them together as a 'big alliance' like the Traditions and Technocracy, but a large part of their appeal was their ability to stand on their own, refusing to compromise in the process.

                Originally posted by Astromancer View Post
                the Disparates don't really add diversity. Even the most seemingly West-centric Trad are shown to be multicultural.
                The Disparate don't add diversity, they add self-advocacy, the idea that a minority group don't need a white knight to protect it from the big bad world.

                And the Trad only accept other cultures when those accept to adopt the trappings of their "parent" Trad and the hierarchy of the Council.

                Originally posted by Michael View Post
                the point of the Traditions working together is that they don't want to impose their paradigms on the world
                Not exactly true, either. It's more that doing so isn't feasible in the foreseeable future, but many of them were fully engaged in trying this when it was.

                Originally posted by False Epiphany View Post
                Because in my experience, the more aggressively authors push a social agenda, the worse their stories and worldbuilding in their settings tends to be. Such authors also tend to disrespect the works of authors that have come before them.
                Every story have a political facet, well written or not. Every author pushes a social agenda of some kind. The only thing you achieve by bashing the agenda instead of the story is to alienate ideas before ever really looking into the story.

                Curiously, you're doing it now with two settings, WoD and SW, that were blatantly political from the beginning and never tried to pretend otherwise. In my experience, "Tainting the franchise with politics" is the go-to argument when you have no other reason to dislike something except your own political agenda, and here it is being directed to an epic about the disenfranchised fighting an over-violent terrorist state and a setting about social criticism and ideological empowerment.

                Originally posted by False Epiphany View Post
                My "fancy metaphysics" are the axis around which the setting turns.
                No, it isn't. By the lore itself this fancy metaphysics is specifically the Order of Hermes magical theory that they imposed over everyone else, with extreme prejudice, and this was a big deal.

                The axis around which the setting turns is that belief shapes magic and will is power.

                Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                The DA are just Traditions 2, electric boogaloo.
                From a Doylist perspective the DA corrects some of the assumptions of WW in the 1st edition, and this isn't actually a new movement in the setting.

                When 1st edition was made, in a time when WW thought Gipsies was a good idea and respectful depiction, the Traditions were meant, indeed, to be champions of diversity as the authors understood it.

                But their concept still left a lot of things unrepresented, and the authors picked up this. We started to get a history of the Traditions that explains why they have the structure they have, and we got to see the disgruntled unrepresented factions on the edges, along with equally disgruntled underrepresented factions inside that have joined out of necessity but are far from happy with the arrangement.

                This served the purpose of explaining out the Traditions by casting a not-so-good light on them, saving the setting from having to be rebuilt from scratch, but had the collateral effect of harming the idea that the Traditions really stand for diversity as much as they profess. Not only the Hermetics forced their hand more than once to make it into 9 traditions, keep most seats of power in Europe, force-feed their own Paradigm down everyone's throats (Spheres, 9 Traditions, 4 groups of Magi), but the individual Traditions, being built of not-so-similar groups with only one standing as their representative (because they ARE named after specific "crafts"), ended up repeating the same process down the line as they got to indoctrinate their newcomers.

                Nothing here is a new development, it was taking form as soon as 2nd edition, with seeds already on 1st.

                It only makes sense for the Disparate Alliance to appear. It addresses both Watsonian (those disgruntled Magi would organize sooner or later) and Doylist (players have a group that offers an organized alliance while being even more free on character and Paradigm concept) perspectives.

                Keep in mind that saying the Crafts already existed doesn't address the fact that lacking a larger political body to belong to is too limiting a downside in the game as it is. And it is also fun to rub your existence in the face of the OoH beloved tarot analogy.

                                                            Um... *gestures at the whole Sorcerer's Crusade era* there is a distinct period in Mage history where the global Consensus shattered and the world was plunged into a jigsaw of Reality Zones.

                                                            That's when a lot of the regressive parts of the Traditions actually set into their culture because for a few generations of mages, Reality in their home turf was what they believed. Before that magic was just magic, and technology was just technology. Outside of personal magical spaces, a Hermetic's fireball worked the same anywhere on map, and so did gunpowder. During that time, you could cross invisible lines and the rules of Reality changed. On one side of a line praying to Jesus was the best cure for disease, on the other side of the line ancient herbal remedies were, and both were true in their Zones, but false in the other's. This is when the Ascension War became defined as a war over Reality because the Order of Reason was trying to recombine all the Reality Zones into a new one that suited their goals instead of returning it to the old one or letting it stay as is. And at the time, the Traditions wanted things to stay like they were.

                                                            However, once the Order of Reason won and reunified Consensus... the Traditions had to come to grips with the idea they were never getting the Reality Zones for Everyone era back. And thus they started pushing for going farther back: to when the global Consensus was just more flexible accepting of lots of different things. That push is also what kept the strongest Crafts of the day out of the Traditions because they didn't want to accept that solution; they wanted to stay lords of their personal domains (mystically or literally).

                                                            In modern times, the Traditions have spend centuries in the stance of fighting for a new old Reality as epitomized by Horizon. There's a scant handful of mages left that remember the Sorcerer's Crusade era and what it was like to actually have whole countries of Sleepers living under your Reality Zone's rules. It's questionable if anyone is really left from before that (even if some might exist as spirits or Examplars or etc.). Despite the internal fractures in the Traditions, Horizon is dogma that everyone is indoctrinated into. It's part of the identity now and it's far to ingrained to expunge from the faction.

                                                            And that's, ultimately, where their conflict with the Crafts is still rooted: The Crafts don't want Horizon (except the Batini, but they Watsonianly want a Traditions take 2 because they like the idea but they felt the Council was broken from the get go and they're finally trying again). They want everyone to have their own Realm again.
