Instead of marking someone's individual skills, why don't we just make solid Attribute rolls for anything and everything?

                            It's not that I don't agree that the Abilities have become bloated. But the entire reason we have them in the first place is to let us play characters with varied and specific skill sets. Do we really need Computer when we have Science? Strictly speaking no, but we have it as a separate skill regardless because in real life it is its own discipline. And when you devote time to learning this specific skill, that's time you're not devoting to learning something else. If we go too far with collapsing Abilities into other Abilities, we'll prune the system to oblivion and be stuck with characters that are generic and boring. Characters who can all do everything because their Abilities do everything. And we can't rely on Specialties, because some skills are broad enough to have many areas of expertise within them. We'd have Specialties within Specialties!

                            I'm sorry for ranting.

                                    Sorry, time issues forced me to cut this into two:

                                    Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                                    Here's another point, if we're saying that one Ability is "basically" like another, why don't we go further with that. Why not fold Brawl into Athletics? Or Science and Technology into Academics? Or hell, why don't we just roll all Abilities into their appropriate Attributes, since we're simplifying? Instead of marking someone's individual skills, why don't we just make solid Attribute rolls for anything and everything?
                                    Slippery slopes require more than just saying they could happen to be valid points.

                                    And yeah, I've seen people hack the ST system to completely remove Abilities. The nWOD presented this in one of their system hacking focused books as well. So that's not a bad thing, or inherently wrong; it also doesn't prove one must go to that extreme.

                                    It is very simple to look at the Ability list and set up a series of heuristics that end up reducing the total Ability count with a reasoned approach that doesn't sacrifice the role Abilities play in describing your character. For example:

                                    1) If two Abilities have nearly identical functions and any differences can be maintained using existing other rules, or there is not a significant risk in dropping the differences, combine them (ex: Brawl and Martial Arts, Esoterica and Hypertech).
                                    2) If one Ability's practical applications accomplish everything another Ability's uses accomplish (or worse and more) combine them (ex: Do and Martial Arts, Expression and Leadership), or if one Ability's practical applications are covered completely by two (or more) other Abilities combine it into those (ex: Computers into Technology and Science, Research into appropriate Knowledges governing that topic).
                                    3) Only maintain a separation of "theory focused" Abilities and "application focused" Abilities (ex: Occult vs. Esoterica) if both Abilities have significant impact in play.
                                    4) When it doubt, keep Abilities that have a strong conceptual niche (ex: Rules 1-3 could make a case to eliminate Medicine in Investigation for diagnostics, Science for theory/lab work, and Empathy or Technology for treatment depending on the nature of the condition, but Medicine is a strong enough niche role for healer-types to keep).
                                    5) Maintain Ability list symmetry; all categories should have the same number of core traits. If Knowledges has 11 Abilities and Talents only has 8, it has a negative impact on prioritization during character creation.

                                    Yes, there's plenty of subjective calls in there, but you can follow that, cull out 1 or 2 Abilities per category, and not negatively impact characterization.

                                    But the entire reason we have them in the first place is to let us play characters with varied and specific skill sets.
                                    Multiple variations of the ST systems have set Ability/Skill lists, often with less of them (Exalted has 25, nWOD has 24), and no built in assumption of expansion-as-needed (Secondaries or the Hobby/Professional/Expert traits), and still manage to accomplish this goal.

                                    Do we really need Computer when we have Science? Strictly speaking no, but we have it as a separate skill regardless because in real life it is its own discipline.
                                    You can say that about any science... why aren't biology, chemistry, physics, and mathematics at a minimum separate Abilities, as all are their own real life disciplines? Computers isn't there because information theory and programming design isn't a science, but because of (1) legacy issues regarding the relative specialization of computer use from 20+ years ago and (2) as something the follows #4 on my list above in the game creator's minds.

                                    And when you devote time to learning this specific skill, that's time you're not devoting to learning something else.
                                    True, but ultimately this doesn't make a solid point. Training up Science instead of Technology is time spent on one specific thing over another regardless of there being 24 Abilities, 33 Abilities, or 60 Abilities. This has no baring on the number of them. It's also not spending time improving Attributes that could likely increase your facility with multiple Abilities.

                                    If we go too far with collapsing Abilities into other Abilities, we'll prune the system to oblivion and be stuck with characters that are generic and boring.
                                    Conversely, if we bloat Abilities too far, they simply become unwieldy and frustrating for trying to make sheets that reflect specific concepts because more dots get tied up in basic proficiencies for the concept and don't leave room for personalizing them. It does not take large numbers to accomplish lots of combinations. Just think for a moment how many different permutations you can actually get out of V20's Ability options at creation without freebies. So that's 13/9/5 in to three categories of 10. Just for the tertiary alone, there are five spreads (all1s, 1/1/1/2, 1/2/2, 1/1/3, 2/3). Which works out to over 2,500 different unique combinations of your tertiary Abilities alone. You can make 2,500 characters and never repeat the same tertiary Ability assignments once with 30 Abilities. The idea that we're at risk of losing uniqueness by cutting out a few doesn't work; and M20 starts at 33.

                                    If I want to make an Akashic that started as a street brawler making their money in underground fights and was good, but came to an epiphany that their life style was destructive and sought to find a positive outlet for their passion through studying formal martial arts with a strong philosophic bent, before Awakening and becoming an Akashic...

                                    In the current rules I should take Brawl, Martial Arts, Do (and probably a few other things), and the first two should have at least a reasonable investment for the concept (lets say 3/3/2). Of course, I'm never going to use Brawl or MA, because I have Do and Do is far superior even if I start with a bit less of it.

                                    If we use my house rule presented earlier, I can just take Brawl (with an appropriate MA specialy via Well-Skilled Craftmen) at 3, and Esoterica with a Do specialty at 2 for the same level of competence. Now I have three more dots to make sure that I can flush out my character with other traits to be less cookie-cutter Martial Arts fighter.

                                    Or to reverse your slippery slope on you: Why should the game stop at 33 Abilities, why not 66? or 330? The more Abilities, the more ways to make unique combinations, the less generic and boring, right?
