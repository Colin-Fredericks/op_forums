                                          I had PC Mages do the "time travel to the Wild West" thing as well. I was lucky enough that it was a setting based game, specifically St. Louis, so I didn't have to make up the rest of the world (except by implication). The PCs were getting over a soiree in the near future (which ten years away, in 2017!) with a foray to the further future and overshot the present on their way home. We had just lost a PC to an Epic Destiny (which unfortunately didn't include hanging out with the rest of them), so the player generated Wild West mage character to join up. Because of that, I had to hammer out the whole "This is what the Traditions are like" document, with some codicils about what was going on with the Technocratic Union as well. Well, actually the Union and the remnants of the Order of Reason. Part of the concept was the ongoing conversion from the Order to the Union and the strange bedfellows it was producing. Order of Reason mages who didn't want to convert to the "new orthodoxy". The Traditions had their own internal disconnect between the European groups and the non-Europeans and between the mystical haves and have-nots based on the relevant prevalence and position of the various ideologies involved. (The PC ended up being a Doc Holliday-type/snake oil salesmen with working snake out, with both Cosian and Verbena leanings).

                                          It worked out REALLY well. We spend a good long chunk of the game back in the 1870s. It was great because I could show the genesis of issues they had been dealing with regularly since the game had started. I definitely put all the games into play in an adjunct fashion. The group had interacted with wraiths in modern St. Louis and I had the opportunity for them to meet some of them when they were still alive. I went light on the changelings. They had played a huge role in the wrap-up of the escape from dystopia (yup, that's the dystopia of 2017, so take that for what it's worth). Since I had all my Werewolf Wild West books, I replaced them with some significant werewolf interactions. They had minimial dealings with the vamps back in 2007 and they didn't meddle much in vampire business back in the 19th Century (though I did have it ready in case they decided to go play in that part of the sandbox). Again, it was not to hard as I had set up the "current" St. Louis vampires with a conflicted history, so I just rolled it back. The Prince that had been defeated was the head honcho still, etc.

                                          Oh and a LOT of historical research. A LOT. Since it was on one location, it wasn't too bad. There was some really intricate and tricky stuff that ended up in a 10 page write up of a cursed family and their haunted homes and breweries and how that all tied in with the family curse and history of one of the PCs.

                                          It was, overall, a ton of fun. I loved it so much I often contemplate just running a game in that sub-chronicle setting. Or just porting over the basic structure of that version of the World of Darkness Wild/Wyld/Savage West and using it for a game set somewhere else.
