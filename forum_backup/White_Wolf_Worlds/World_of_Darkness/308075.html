    Botches in your game

    11-10-2014, 08:15 AM
    I used a recently a botch in an entertaining way. I ran V20 session, players got their hands on a mysterious plant they found during investigation, and asked semi-NPC with Green Path to check on the plant. Willpower roll went too good, so to not make it easy I demanded Intelligent+Occult roll to interpret visions gleaned. And... a one big mofo of a botch happened. Being cruel and feeling inspired by my Pathfinder GM who had my PC nearly drown in magic slime and temporarily lose half Constitution, I decided that the visions overwhelmed the Tremere and he fell into one month torpor basing on his Humanity score.

    Everyone joke it must have been some good shit. And the reveal is saved from leaking too early.

      Rite of Summoning; The Pack were heading into what was expected to be a big ruck with a Wyrm Infested location and had a few days to prepare, so they set to making Talens and Learning some Gifts. I forget what they were doing this summoning for, but they wanted a Spirit of Rage or War ( a big one) for some aid in the upcoming fray. The pack is gathered on the roof of the Urban Caern's building and perform the Rite... and Botches, big. The Heavens of the Umbra literally split and Nerigal appears before the pack! An incarna is well above their ability to try and deal with, especially a pissed off Celestine of Battle and War. The Ahroun tries to dive between the Theurge and the Mars Incarna and gets his guts ripped out on the barbed spear thrust by Nerigal. The Philodox and Ragabash are tossed aside like leaves caught in a storm, by which point the Theurge has recovered enough from the shock to try and treat with the War God. He manages to speak with the angered Spirit to get it to keep from slaughtering the pack and Nerigal agrees to the bargain, with one more cost. The Celestine of Mars reaches out with his mighty hands and rips the Theurge's arm from his body (Battlescar) with a ban against healing. Thus he earned the named "Wounded by Mars".

          Well, a month of torpor might be nothing in some games and very harsh in others. Depends extremely on the playstyle so can't really evaluate that.

          However I had a Character using Auspex3 somewhere on a Graveyard to investigate. The character botched really hard (I do measure the severeness of botches by the number of 1s rolled) and I decided that in the same spot she used her discipline a summoning of a dark entity took place some time ago. The botch lead to her forming a connection to the energies of this ritual and the essence of a dark being started to flow into her.

          The Player reacted quickly as she felt weird and saw her arm beginning to transform into something monstrous that she couldn't control. She had another player cut off that arm instantly and killed it with fire .
          To bad those damn Tremere tend to have high willpower other characters might have hesitated longer for even more fun.

                                                      The one that leaps to mind was in a Mage game. The PCs were driving on an icy mountain highway in a van (the VA in the group was wheelchair-bound). The driver botched a simple, normal, mundane Drive roll. So the van went off the road, meaning over the edge of the steep slope..

                                                      So, of course, every character in the van immediately starts up some effect to salvage the situation (e.g. "not die"). EVERY effect attempted either failed or botched. It was spectacular, in a completely awful way.

                                                      The van went rolling down the slope, off the edge of cliff, through free fall while vulgar effect after effect was fast cast inside. The did manage to live through it. They were mages after all, but the minor inconvenience of the trip through the mountains became an entire session of white-knuckled terror that could have ended in a TPK...

                                                      The same group also played an all SoE game set in interplanetary space. They would keep trying to short cut travel times by dropping into adjacent dimensions where they could cross the distances more quickly. And they kept botching when they did it. They ended up in some vary interesting places. Where, of course, they did marginally sane things to try to escape. Which led to further difficulties later one (invasions of planets made of crystalized evil, making a solipsistic universe aware of things "not-it" leading to the foundation of a religion, etc. etc.)
