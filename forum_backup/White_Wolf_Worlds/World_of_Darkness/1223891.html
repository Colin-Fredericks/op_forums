    Creating an antagonistic Mage

    06-01-2018, 08:39 AM
    Hi all, I am the ST of a Werewolf game and the main antagonist of the campaign is a Mage. I would like your help in defining him. I didn't know if posting here or in the Werewolf forum but in the end you are the experts in Mages. If you feel this does not belong here just tell me and I will move it.

    I am currently reading the M20 book but it is dense and taking so damm long, by the time I've finished a chapter I seem to have forgotten what there was in the previous one.

    I know that I could just create a custom enemy with made up powers and call it a day, but it would feel like I'm cheating (maybe this is stupid) and my players may sense I am pulling things out of my ass, so while this guy does not have to have a character sheet I would like to make him as close to the Mage system as possible. And because they might encounter him before I have finished the book it seems logical to have something prepared, can you help me?

    I have a basic understanding of the system by reading this forum and some wiki pages + first chapters of the M20 book but if I butcher any term or concept please let me know.

    This guy, lets name him Steve, is at his core a specialist in spirits, he can cross the Gaunlet, pull people to and from the Umbra, command spirits and trap them (willingly or not) into objects e.g. Genie jars. His objective is thinning the Gaunlet which has put him at odds with the Garou as his experiments may use some (a lot) of kinfolk blood.
    He is not a flashy guy throwing fireballs or kinking people into the moon but he needs some kind of offense and defense, for offense he lets the spirits he has trapped deal with the problems and for defense I was thinking on some kind of wards, statics if they need to be with maybe some silver for dealing with Garous in particular.
    The wards won't last forever so in my mind he has the rote "I will just use my secret exit" which enables him to pull from a book in a bookshelf, lamp statue or any other object typically associated with secret doors and open a door which didn't exists before.
    He is also fond of using a projection to deal with other parties to avoid the possible risk to his health. This projection is incorporeal but looks solid to the sight.

    I want this mage to have the upper hand during the duration of the campaign, always being able to scape or just being 1 step further than my players but being beatable in some form (knowing my players, by killing him) when they have enough intel about him.

    My questions in the end are:
    • Is there some kind of "level" system such as rank in Werewolf and generation in Vampire that allows me to say, a mage with this many sphere dots would be a "archmage" or at very least he is very well known in the country? I want him to be kind of obscure and not too powerful.
    • For the spirits powers I describe, are they possible? and in case they are, how many dots does he need in the Spirit sphere?
    • For the wardings I have no idea what to use.
    • For the secret doors at first I thought Correspondence but you are moving matter so you need Matter and once you use Matter why would you need Correspondence?
    • For the secrets door would it make it harder/easier and more/less vulgar if the door mechanism last or if someone that tries to replicate it cannot produce the same result?
    • For the experiments with the kinfolk blood that in the end produce a consumable that can be eaten and used by sleepers, Matter again?
    • For the projection power, Correspondence for the distance, Forces for the image and sound?, Matter if he wants to interact
    • Do Garou count as witnesses for vulgar magic?
    • From reading this forum and the book it seems that a mage in his "Sanctum Sanctorum" (chantries?) has his powers amplified and it is impervious to paradox backslashes. If my players walk into his house, are they done for?


    What I want is an enemy that feels consistent with the rules even if it deviates a little.

    PD: His paradigm is a mix of Vodou and Christianity, he believing that all his powers come from his ability to command Spirits, even the ones that are not affected by the Spirit sphere.

    Thanks in advance

              Theme question: Vodou is already syncretized with Christianity. Is this just a case where there's an extra helping of Christianity?

              Mages can easily have non-magical cohorts such as a family, congregation, civic group, community and such that can fill in whatever holes you encounter. This also allows the Mage to have more firepower of whatever type you need without throwing in an extra dot. Mages also have access to chiminage to barter with spirits and, if doing work with Vodou, maybe even the undead so you have a lot of knobs to work with. Whether your character is a Houngan, a Mambo, or bokor, you have a lot of options for invoking powers from any of the main families augmented with the Christian saints either as a focus or through their spirit agents.

              Also, when you're throwing in another system, there's nothing to say can't just have a list of vague powers that you want to work with. The full system for Wraith is a bit daunting but thumbing through Arcanos gives you ideas.

                  Originally posted by Hares666 View Post
                  [*]Is there some kind of "level" system such as rank in Werewolf and generation in Vampire that allows me to say, a mage with this many sphere dots would be a "archmage" or at very least he is very well known in the country? I want him to be kind of obscure and not too powerful.
                  menos125 answer covers the mechanical quirks that make a potent mage. I'm going to focus in the social/metaplot aspect of your question, that has also a mechanical component: Mages, roughly, identify themselves by the level of mastery reached in one (probably several) Spheres. For instance:
                  With one Sphere at 3 you're a Disciple of that Sphere. Disciple it's also a rank within the Traditions, directly above Apprentice and below Adept - it'sthe rank and file of mages. However inside of character nobody knows how many dots you have, all that people knows it's that you're capable of things, perhaps things that an Apprentice can't do.
                  Now, I'm oversimplifying stuff here: each mage group has it's own rules about when a mage can be considered part of the rank and file. Somethimes it has little to do with Spheres or Arete (in the Technocracy being good at your job matters more among the lower ranks). Most groups of mages would require you to have more than one sphere at 3 to be regarded as a Disciple or equivalent, but lvl 3 it's a very common staple.

                  When you reach 5 in a Sphere you're a Master. Again, no Master worthy of the title will have only one Sphere at 5, but having 5 it's mandatory - even in the Technocracy you can't be the big boss without one Sphere at 5. These mages are the ones that rule the world of mages, they have the potential of being very well known unless they have voluntarily choosen a life of anonimate.

                  Now, for your character, judging for the things you want him to accomplish and the fact you don't want him to be too powerful I would suggest to make either an Adept or a Disciple with a powerful Mentor.

                  As an Adept, Steeve it's a powerful mage but not so powerful as to be a luminary. It's the social equivalent of an Ancilla - big enough to take care for himself but he ain't going to become the Prince if there's a Master in town (so to speak, in truth Masters tend to live of world and Adepts are the most potent dudes you may find). He may have 12-20 points in Spheres without being too flashy. Arete 5 should be OK

                  As a Disciple he would fill the bill of being "obscure and not too powerful", but won't be able to do the stuff you want him to do because the effects you want require at least Spirit 4.
                  However, a powerful Mentor may have provided Steve with Artifacts capable of doing the feat. Perhaps Steve's Mentor it's the one that suggested the experiments that Steve it's doing. Perhaps Steve's Mentor wants to lower the Gauntlet because perhaps he got traped in the Umbra during the Avatar Storm, and now he needs a hole in the Gauntlet to pass safely. Or perhaps he has other reasons.
                  The advantage of this route it's that you have more stories to tell (what will Steve's Mentor do when he notices his apprentice has been ripped apart?). The disadvantage it's that a pack of Garou may be more than what Steve can handle - but if you're going with the assumption that Steve it's very inteligent and resourcefull, he may be able to do the job. For this build, I would recommend someone slightly more powerful than chargen, perhaps with 9 points in Spheres and Arete 4

                  This guy, lets name him Steve, is at his core a specialist in spirits, he can cross the Gaunlet, pull people to and from the Umbra, command spirits and trap them (willingly or not) into objects e.g. Genie jars.[*]For the spirits powers I describe, are they possible? and in case they are, how many dots does he need in the Spirit sphere?
                  menos125 nailed it, you need Spirit 4 for most of those effects.

                  If you want to go with Disciple Steve, then he would need a level 4 Wonder - could easily be a Genie Jar - that traps spirits and allows to command them afterwards. You could make several "one use" genie jars that allow to capture spirits instead - these would also count as a 4 level Wonder. He would also need a Wonder to pull people from and to the Umbra.

                  [*]For the wardings I have no idea what to use.
                  Wards that block passage are the ones that would hinder Garou the most (because they so love to sharpen their claws on other people's faces). A Correspondence 4/Spirit 2/Life 1 Ward can prevent Garou from getting too close. Correspondence 4 creates a Major Ward, a distortion in space that prevents passage and can't be broken by pure brute force, while Spirit + Life are needed to detect Garou so the ward can block them, Spirit 2 it's for the ward to affect the Umbra.
                  Such a Ward could protect a house (Garou can't enter the house), an item, or could be worn as an amulet by Steve (so he can't be atacked in mele with claws or theet, but swords or really any other weapon would be OK - as long as he hasn't wards against those)

                  Disicple Steve hasn't access to such potent Wards, however he can manage. A Correspondence 2/Life 1/Spirit 2 Minnor Ward can alert Steve that Garou are entering his house. Add Mind 3 and the ward can cause potent feelings of "don't enter this house, leave now" (that can be resisted as any mental influence).

                  Forces 2 can conjure forcefields, that may be all the ward you need. Unlike Correspondence Wards, enough brute force should be able to overpower a forcefield.
                  In M20 you need Matter 2 if you want to put a forcefiled in a building or another material object without concentrating on the spell, if you don't want to use this whole "pattern locking" rules fell free to house-rule them away, nothing breaks.

                  [*]From reading this forum and the book it seems that a mage in his "Sanctum Sanctorum" (chantries?) has his powers amplified and it is impervious to paradox backslashes. If my players walk into his house, are they done for?
                  [*]For the secret doors at first I thought Correspondence but you are moving matter so you need Matter and once you use Matter why would you need Correspondence?
                  You need Matter 4 to create a real secret door (one that can be opened by other people, at least theoretically) .

                  You need Correspondence 3 when you are truly vanishing into thin air.

                  It's worth to note that under M20 you can't "conjure a secret door" to make your magick Coincident using Correspondence (and to do so with Matter would require you to also craft the passageway, wich would require a ton of successes). Only 1rst edition allowed this kind of benefitial "artistic licency" when using magick (the in-famous conjured taxicab)

                  [*]For the secrets door would it make it harder/easier and more/less vulgar if the door mechanism last or if someone that tries to replicate it cannot produce the same result?
                  The axis of Coincidence it's determined at the moment of the casting - people trying to replicate your feat has no relevance, all that matters it's if what you did would be considered magical or impossible by a bystander.

                  Note that teleportation it's almost always Vulgar, and it's supposed to be. A price that mages would pay gladly if it means to not confront the Garou. Vanishing behind a secret door...It's still, most likely, Vulgar (what if someone was in the secret passage?) - there may be ways to make this Coincident, but not without falling in edge cases.

                  [*]For the experiments with the kinfolk blood that in the end produce a consumable that can be eaten and used by sleepers, Matter again?
                  As menos125 says, it depends...

                  If you want it to have a magical effect, you're probably thinking in Prime. Prime 3 can enchant a substane into a consumables that causes magical effects, called Charms. Prime 2 it's enough if you use Tass with a ressonance appropiate to the spell the object it's going to cast - which you could rule kinfolk blood it's (this would be a house rule - kinfolk usually don't count as magical creatures, albeit kinfolk with Gnosis could be the exception). Note that the mage needs to be able to cast the spell he's imbuing into the charm.

                  [*]For the projection power, Correspondence for the distance, Forces for the image and sound?, Matter if he wants to interact
                  Correspondence 2/Forces 2 it's enough. Forces can't just project light and sound, but also Kinetic force - you can touch stuff with it. It's higly Vulgar, but also beats having Garou sharpening their claws in your back. Note that with Corr 3 you can also cast spells where your projection is, and with Corr 2/Matter 2 you could teleport small stuff there (like, say, a grenade)
                  [*]Do Garou count as witnesses for vulgar magic?
                  Never, ever.

                  Kinfolk count as witnesses, but only if they aren't so jaded by their experience with garou as to wholeheartedly believe in anything supernatural

                  From reading this forum and the book it seems that a mage in his "Sanctum Sanctorum" (chantries?) has his powers amplified and it is impervious to paradox backslashes. If my players walk into his house, are they done for?
                  Not quite.

                  See, a Sanctum doesn't make you immune to Paradox, it makes your magick Coincident as long as it stays there. Thus, you can't get Paradox unless you Botch and the difficulty it's reduced so you can get more successes. But if your magick was already Coincident, casting it in a sanctum hasn't much benefit. Also if you, say, want to teleport outside the Sanctum to escape, it's Vulgar (unless you teleport into ANOTHER Sanctum).
                  But you still have to roll.
                  A fireball casted by an Arete 3 mage within a Sanctum it's still going to cause a maximum of 8 (10 if Willpower it's expended) levels of aggravated damage, the only advantage it's that getting the maximum amount of successes it's going to be easier and you won't get 'dox.

                  The real danger of the Sanctum for Garou it's the amount of milacres Steve may have stored there without fear of Paradox. Vulgar traps and items are very common in Sanctums, since you don't have to worry about them being crushed by reality. However, again, that depends on Steve's capabilities - a Disciple ain't likely to have a dragon in his Sanctum. Since Steve it's a Spirit mage, he could have a bound guardian spirit

                  PD: His paradigm is a mix of Vodou and Christianity, he believing that all his powers come from his ability to command Spirits, even the ones that are not affected by the Spirit sphere.

                      With regards to a Sanctum; if a Garou enters the Sanctum of a powerful Mage, alone (assuming they can find it; they have an Arcane rating which shields them) they may be destroyed. However they have an advantage; the Sanctum does not impact their powers at all. If a rival Mage enters your sanctum, and their magick doesn't fit your practice, then they will struggle to use their powers. Garou will function as normal, just against a powerful enemy.

                      The sanctum will likely have a very thin Gauntlet, so these Garou PCs might be able to enter through the Umbra. If I were this Mage I would have wards which detect inbound Spirits and Living Creatures (Spirit/Correspondence and Life/Correspondence).

                      You should read the Fetish rules for Mages. A Mage with command over Spirits, who is pissing off Garou, could easily be forcing spirits into their instruments to gain power.

                      I think the best way to make a Mage an interesting antagonist in another game is to tie them very closely to the splat. For example, a Mage NPC in a Vampire game could be a Revenant or Ghoul, or be a Hermetic who works for Clan Tremere, or a Lych of the True Black Hand. If this Mage were in some way linked to the Garou, by cosmology or bloodlines, then it would make their presence feel less random.

                      Last of all, there's a Werewolf rite/gift (I believe) which commands Paradox Spirits. I think it's a Glass Walker / Werespider thing since it's weaver-related. Regardless, if a PC had the power to influence paradox spirits they could completely destroy this Mage with some good rolls.

                                                          Aleph M20 makes it clear that Esoterica: Traditional Healing can be used as a knowledge base for certain things, such as the basis for healing complex diseases with Chi (for example). Ancient Mages who wanted to create human beings "out of thin air" did so with detailed knowledge of the human body as understood by their paradigm.

If you're trying to create a Pattern with Prime and a Pattern Sphere, you have Level 1 in that Pattern Sphere as a prerequisite. Ancient masters of Life will have analysed the human body with Life 1 until they understood it well enough to replicate it. The same applies to all of the Pattern Spheres, honestly. Every Mage has a great justification for learning all of the Abilities they need. Now an ST could choose to hand-wave the Abilities requirements by letting a level 1 effect analyse what you want to replicate to get sufficient information... and you know what, that works. But it still doesn't help a person who is attempting to create something out of nowhere without an example to analyse of course.

                                                          The question of "Do you even know what you're doing?" is a *question* after all. All you need to be able to do is sincerely say "yes, I absolutely do" and point to some trait which would reasonably give you the necessary mental scaffolding. And that could be modern medicine, a thorough understanding of a traditional healing method, a specialisation of esoterica...

                                                          As for money vs. fire, this is again apples vs. oranges. Money is intentionally difficult to reproduce and create, even moreso in the WoD with the Syndicate's involvement. Currency isn't easy to replicate. Fire is so easy to replicate that it does so when we don't want it to. Conjuring up a single coherent substance, like water or air or a type of metal is very different to pulling out a roll of 20s.
