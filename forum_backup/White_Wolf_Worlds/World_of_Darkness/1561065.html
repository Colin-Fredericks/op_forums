      Explosives are covered on pgs 437-438. Basically, no, you don't add extra attack successes to the damage. They're just there to put its ground zero point where the attacker wants it to be. Other games usually have a Scatter mechanic to cover this; basically, the launch system has some inherent instability that put it a random number of movement measurements off-center, and each success removes one from the hex, square or what have you that it's off-target, as determined by a simple die roll where you just take the number, like with Initiative value.

              No.

                                          Originally posted by Cain Loup-Noir View Post

                                          There's still some strange stats, like the .50 machine gun dealing 16 damage and the 105mm canon dealing 20. Only 4 dice of difference? I'm no expert in weaponery but that feel a bit weak for a tank weapon
                                          Because that's not what the dice measure. A log - like progression is expected.

                                          What the system models isn't the joules or newtons of impact, it's the likelihood that it will impair or kill the target. Especially, in this system, a humanoid supernatural target.

                                          The 4 dice of difference means 4 dice more of effects a supernatural creature must amass on the absorption roll to survive. For Vampires, this jump from 8 Lethal average to 10 Lethal average means going from needing Stamina 4/5 to have a reasonable chance of not filling up on Bashing, to needing 8/9, which means you need both Fortitude and Stamina at high levels to deal with that. You change the kind of character that can take on it significantly.

                                          Even for "realism" there are several reasons for us to be entering the domain of the Law of Diminishing Returns. Usually the more you break, the more energy you would have needed to break even more at first. You're increasing power, but the outcome is also increasing everything that makes the result less predictable. Against things you kill, they're not getting deader, but against things you don't, you need way more to get a meaningful advantage.

                                          Finally, bloat usually makes the math go in weirder ways rather than realistic ways. The less dice you have to throw for something, the better it is.
