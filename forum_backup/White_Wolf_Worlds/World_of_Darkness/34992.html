      As far as I have figured, the fatsplats (V:tM, W:tA, M:tA, W:tO, C:tD, H:tR, D:tF, etc.) are often parallel, but different versions of the other lines in the WoD that sometimes cross, but don't always match up. In V:tM, the lupines* aren't necessarily Garou, and in W:tA, the vampires* aren't necessarily Cainites, etc.. What a Demon thinks of the fairies* might be quite different than what he might think of the Changelings IF the two fatsplats actually crossed, as well as what the Changelings may think of the demons* if they encountered them, etc.. Each game line might interpret the representation of the other fatsplats different than how they would be seen natively in their own systems.

      *note the lower-cased usage to denote a generalized name rather than the specific self-title of that group.

      So, to try to help you in your answer to your questions, it will partly depend on how that "other" concept is interpreted within the native Fatsplat (how fairies, the representation of Changeling: the Dreaming concepts, are portrayed within the Demon: the Fallen fatsplat, etc..)

      So, Changelings may view demons (or their representation of Demons within C:tD) as some sort of lost fae, or Dreaming creations, even though that wouldn't match up with what is Factual within D:tF.

      As for the specifics of what each line thinks of the others... well, I'm sure your issue comes from the fact that some books (like Changeling) didn't really get to address the new lines, but maybe there's something packed into a supplement somewhere that those afficianatos might know about.

          Yeah the thing is WtO and CtD never got a Revised Edition. WtO ended in 1999 with Ends of Empire which itself was the catalyst for the Revised Edition era and the later gamelines that sprung up, including DtF. The closest thing we have to a WtO book in the Rev Ed is the Orpheus gameline, the spiritual successor to WtO that kind of is but isn't a sequel to WtO.                   I recall Changeling 2nd Ed stating somewhere that Changelings are quite aware of other supernatural creatures, which is a bit atypical in WoD. They call these others "prodigals" and assume that they are fae or changelings who have forgotten their true nature. Usually, they are pitied and avoided, but changelings can be inquisitive and capricious at times so sticking one's nose in the business of other supernatural creatures isn't unheard of.

                      In earlier versions of all the core books and system (say back when there were just 5), all supernaturals were at least aware of each other. Some books gave examples for how to run NPCs of the others within that system, but actually recommended checking out the other system books if more detail was needed than their in-system template. As Revised came about, such encouragement for system to system reference seemed to be omitted. Since Hunter, Mummy and Demon came in around this time, they too omitted direct reference of association with the other systems, especially those that were being discontinued. Instead the references became as Torakhan stated. That Lupines were not necessarily Garou and Vampires not necessarily Cainites etc. I suppose if you really want to figure out what Demons are to Changelings, the closest might be in the tiny supplement book of Denizens of the Dreaming. (Which I originally thought would be more about the Dreaming and different Bygones and Chimera, but instead was more of a Misc. Fae collection.) While it might not have anything that equates to Demons, how the general fae view the other Denizens as alien outsiders might be how they would view Demons as well.
