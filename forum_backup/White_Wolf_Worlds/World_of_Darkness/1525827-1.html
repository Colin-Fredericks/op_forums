    Being a Gen Xer as well as a lawyer, I think I have my share of cynicism about the state of the world, but more than a few things give me hope for the future despite me being scared shitless by climate change and the new rise of fascism around the world and here at home. Rather than turn this into a political discussion, I'll just leave it at that.

    As for absurdity being played up in some troupes' WoD games, I was involved in this one Mage campaign where the ST was nice enough to let me play a Bubasti since I didn't feel near confident or competent enough to play Mage's magick rules. Two of the other PCs were an Orphan and an alcoholic Hermetic. And the last player's character was a defecting Void Engineer. A motley crew if ever there was one.

    At the start of the campaign, my Bubasti character was told to rendezvous with this VE inside the stairwell of a high-rise building (don't ask why there, I don't remember the details). So this VE botches his Correspondence roll when he teleports to the stairwell and winds up rolling down at least three floors of stairs only to land in a heap in front of my Bubasti (who has his pistol drawn because of all the noise). The relationship between the two PCs only went downhill from there. :-P

    Later on, the VE and my Bubasti were forced to share a two-bed hotel room. Because the two of them didn't get along at all, my Bubasti decide to wait until the VE was asleep in his bed, then shifted into Feline form and urinated on him. With my superb Stealth roll, I was able to pull it off without waking him up. It was way better than finding a horse's head in your bed! :-D

    As for the reason a Bubasti was running with a mage troupe? The justification for having a Bubasti run with these mages was that the kheper (the Bubasti tribe elders) were trying to establish goodwill relations with mages to find new allies in their war against the Followers of Set. My PC was chosen to be the first attempt at this ambassadorial role. Needless to say, it...did not go as planned. :-P

    Oh, and that VE player? He's been my best friend in RL ever since. :-D

                        Originally posted by Amethyst View Post

                        I understand. Not everyone is into sugary sweet happiness in art. I personally prefer optimism, which is I guess why lately I prefer D&D to the World of Darkness (the main reason I like WoD is because I like vampires and also the urban fantasy of Mage and Changeling).

But in D&D you don't just simply win the day all the time by the power of holding hands... I don't know if you get my meaning. Lord of the Rings is my favorite story of all times, and it is absolutely positive in every way, but it is not sugarcoated super boring sunshines. I utterly despise the utterly nihilistic and absolutely void of any meaning Game of Thrones (A Song of Ice and Fire), as the nihilistic garbage made by a wealthy hippie boomer. So it's not about being optimistic or pessimistic, but about doing it properly. WoD is over the top and it's satirical, which is why it is so fun.

                        Peter Quill from Guardians of the Galaxy is a quintessential Millennial. A rebel without a cause, but despite that still have a good heart and wanna do good, just without following social conventions to do so. And he also works well with others, because Millennials, despite being spoiled little brats, are very social and agreeable. He is also SPECIAL - let's not forget, he is the Scion of a god (with a low case "g" when his daddy is feeling humble).

                        The part of being "special" and winning the day by the power of holding hands is what I have a problem with. Likewise, Harry Potter wins the day not because he's smarter or stronger or more skilled or because he has greater moral fiber or stronger will, but because he's special... Again, another Millennial "trait" that makes me puck inside.

                        As a comparison, in Lord of the Rings, the heroes win because they acquired the skills to do so, because they are willing to self sacrifice for others and because of their moral strength. Frodo goes to Mount Doom because he knew it was the right thing to do; Sam helps him out of duty to his best friend. Aragorn spent decades as a Ranger to acquire all of the skills that he uses on behalf of his people. Except for Aragorn, none of them are particularly special, and even for Aragorn - or Boromir or even Legolas - even thou they are "special" due to their social statuses (they are basically aristocrats), their higher status is NOT a reason for merely living in luxury, but instead they have a duty to their people. Boromir leads Gondor as their captain, fighting in the front lines and commanding his men in front of them, as does Aragorn, Faramir or Legolas and Gimli. So, Aragorn is "Special" (he's destined to become the King), but this represents a duty for him, and he still had to prepare for decades to be able to lead his people.

                        It's vastly different from the Millennial "literature" upon which you are special simply from birth, because you're "mom and daddy little spoiled brat"
