    Agreed 100% , the intent would be about what determines the Spheres needed. If possible the effects should remain the same, with minimal differences.

    Here's another example of RBD vs. PBD:

    Let's say a VA want's to scry on a distant location using his computer. So, he describes as haking into cammeras to display the far image into his PC.

    Scrying on a distant location it's Corr 2, but a PDB ST would argue you also need Forces 2 to either hack into the cammeras (if the ST agrees in that there are cameras there in the first place), or to directly display the results of the Corr effect as a video in your computer (if the ST decides there aren't cammeras there, but still allows you to roll with it because "magick").

    Neither it's, strictly speaking, a Vulgar effect, but on PBD you need an additional Sphere.

    A PBD ST could say in his defense that you can totally do something like this with Corr 2 alone ... if you instead open a spatial window and superpose it to your computer screen. The result being an image in your computer imples additional things that Corr doesn't cover, and could be exploited (i.e. you can save a video, edit it with software).

    One could think that RBD never it's Vulgar, because of how the taxi-cab example works - but that's not really the case. Even that example could be Vulgar if the distance traveled it's too far, and the travel too quick, to be plausible. Even the best car at max speed, with all the trafick playing to it, takes some time to go from A to B

    The reason why it "reduces" Vulgarity it's because RBD reduces the Spheres needed to do otherwsie complex effects to play with plausibility. A taxi it's already faster than someone on foot, so you can travel long distances faster w/o straining belief

    More so if the ST agrees it's plausible to skip some parts of the taxi's travel entirely w/o incurring Vulgarity (here's where the axis of HOO vs HAP enters, some HAP believes on "the batman" disappearing after jumping trough a window in the night, may as well miss a car if the conditions are right)
    RBD doesn't mesh well with players seeking exploits. But then, arguably, Mage doesn't mesh well with players seeking exploits.

    It does mesh really well with Paradigms that don't have easy explanation for things just happening, and rather relly on complex processes to do stuf. Such as the Syndicate

    In fact: If you want more good examples on RBD, I would dare say ... Search stuff about for how the Syndicate does magick. Even on M20.

    Like how they summon stuff via calling a helicopter or drone to drop the stuff near them.

    Supposedly M20 doesn't use RBD, but the Syndicate kinda does

                            Do you NEED Forces 2 to put the results of a Corr 2 effect on a screen? I argue no. That's what the software in the machine is for. Corr 2 is establishing the connection to the other location (with or without a camera at the other end). Whereas, presumably, the Virtual Adept has programs on their computer to interpret that feed and turn it into a window that pops up on the screen. Magick isn't needed for that part, because Computer is already a skill well worn into the Consensus at this point. It's the tool working as intended.

                            Presumably, the Syndicate rep HAS helicopters or drones on standby, or because of their wealth have connections within an existing supply chain. Their use of Magick bypasses the normal particulars of how those supply chains operate, getting what they need to them slightly faster than it would on its own. They "know the system", and can thus finesse it in a way that a normal "user" couldn't. Same thing with using Human Resources as an Instrument: the Syndic's agents and helpers do a great deal of things in a mundane way. But within the gaps between them, and due to their employer's Enlightened direction, they accomplish extraordinary things.

                            It's probably worth underlining that PBD and RBD are not hard binaries with concrete lines of demarcation. They are fluid and permeable, based on the suspension of disbelief of the ST and players involved.

                            It's also dependent on the Real Life technology involved. The classic Taxi example of using Corr alone would clearly be RBD if the Mage were summoning the taxi on the spot, from nowhere. Under PBD, they'd need all manner of Matter, Prime, and/or Life to justify the taxi appearing.

                            But nowadays, a Gutter Mage (or VA, or Syndic, or whatever) could "summon a taxi" using an APP. Using Corr to find one, the app to get the driver to come there, maybe more Corr to get the driver there faster ("huh, I made really good time. Thanks, GPS!"), and then using the same "Seven League Stride" principle to get the Mage where they wanted to go. All part of the same continuous Effect.

                            An Effect that seems RBD can become considerably more PBD, if the player puts more thought into how the Magick interplays with the Mundane. (Advances in technology beyond the early 90s also help immensely in this regard.)

                                    Originally posted by Bluecho View Post

                                    I would argue both the VA putting a camera feed on their computer and the Syndicate rep "summoning" via helicopter or drone isn't really doing RBD.

                                    They're just using their Instruments.
                                    Not exactly.

                                    You can't Magic your Instrument into existence just because it's an Instrument, doing so would be a Magical effect of its own. The computer is an Instrument regardless, sure.

                                    The question, though, is if there is a camera or not at the observed point, and if this camera is being actively used. Is this camera an Instrument, an Effect or a target?

                                    All three options can be used, but they are distinct effects.

                                    If there's no camera, then the spell has to either create the camera or to ignore it altogether. The camera is not an Instrument, because it doesn't exist to begin with. In this case, both methods can go ahead anyways with just Correspondence, but under RBD + HOO the effect is Vulgar since there's no real camera that could account for the image (there's either a fake camera or no camera at all, it doesn't really matter except for the unlikely occurrence of someone witnessing the camera appearing). Under PBD creating the camera would require higher Correspondence, Matter and Prime, but ignoring it would be simpler, and Coincident under HAP, so it's completely unnecessary.

                                    Only if the camera exist it can be a target or an Instrument. Even then, for it to be an Instrument you would have to first connect to the camera in some way under PBD, at which point it's possibly easier to just use the camera instead of Magic, but doing Magic can still convey many circumstantial benefits. The camera as a target would require added Spheres under PBD, but possibly with added benefits. Under RBD using the camera as an Instrument wouldn't require anything but Correspondence.

                                    Union paradigms usually favor, in this case, using the camera as either target or Instrument, not simply making images out of nothing, and the Data Sphere specifically calls for this (you can't scry with Data if there's no equipment at the end point). So while ignoring the camera may be easier under PBD, it may not be an option.

                                    Syndicate characters depend a lot on the player and how much forethought they put into it. I completely agree that preferably all those things should be Instruments. The Syndicate specializes into making human work, training and management into Instruments. But in practice RBD is a simpler solution to let players make Syndicate characters without demanding the forethought they should technically have. It's a grey area, but useful to think about nonetheless.

                                    But on the computer being able to record the image regardless, sorry, but this is false under RAW. More than once the rules alude to the fact that imprinting information into a computer system requires Forces. If you use the computer just as an Instrument, then the image isn't in the system at all, it is a Magical effect, regardless if it is conveyed through the computer. Without Forces this information will never arrive at the internal workings of the computer and the machine can't process the image in any real way (although your effect can still be able to).
