            I love when White Wolf tries to tangle with the big philosophical questions! Were the Fallen wrong to turn against Heaven, even though they believed they were acting to save lives? Was their oath of obedience more morally obligatory than their need to behave compassionately? Do you listen to your conscience or to authority? And, if you've made a mistake can you ever really make up for it? They're an exquisite tragic hero, continuing the gothic tradition in the best possible way.

            Originally posted by Camilla View Post
            The reason why I personally find Demon: The Fallen offensive is because it outright states that all religions other than Christianity are evil religions created by the Earthbound. This includes my religion as well. And then it manages to potentially piss off Christians by saying that Satan created Christianity.
            First, the World of Darkness isn't real. No statements made by characters in a game have any significance to your faith, which is related to the real world. Coincidentally, there are people in the real world who say your faith is bogus. I'd be more offended by them.

            Second, no it doesn't say that all other religions are created by Earthbound. This is patently false. It does say they maintain cults throughout the ages, but that's not the same thing. And the Earthbound didn't create the Garou faith, or the Catholic church (which also exists in WoD). So this statement is demonstrably false.

            Even if it were true, fictional Christianity in a fictional game is not your religion.

            Bear in mind that Demon was meant to be the very definite canon explanation to everything in the World of Darkness when it came out. Playing demons isn't the problem for me, it's the attacking of other people's religions by claiming every single religion but one are evil faiths created by eldritch abominations. And then insulting the one religion you don't directly disparage by stating that Lucifer, the being who is for lack of a better word, the main antagonist of said religion, invents it.
            Actually? No it wasn't. White Wolf has explicitly stated that none of the cosmologies is necessarily "more right" than any of the others, not even Demon's. They stated that because approving one over the other would would potentially undercut the narrative value of the other franchises. They spent an eternity in a pit of madness, and had their memories colored by spite, while now trying to recall the full breadth of Angelic memories through the miniscule space of a human brain. It's very unlikely their recollections are totally correct, or possibly not even partially correct.

            In the World of Darkness, no one is right. Or more accurately, everyone is wrong. One of the most interesting possible solutions is that ALL of the cosmologies are true all at once, because creation occurred at a time with little or no banality when both time and causality had different meanings than we understand now as we exist in the corrupted and dying world on the verge of apocalypse.

            Now can you see why I am offended by it?
            An imaginary world, set in the worst possible reality where everything is wrong and even the creator is capricious, leaving you abandoned in darkness managed to cross the fourth wall to offend your faith.

            The World of Darkness is meant to be a hopeless reality, that means nothing is pure and there are no true good guys. That's the whole idea. It's fantasy.

            In that context? No, I cannot see why you are offended.

            In any table top game, it's very important to understand the difference between players and characters. Between story and reality. And to ensure that characters (not players) are offended. It's just a game, and in it we've posed a hypothetical worst-case universe to explore. You can't do that without upsetting religious ideologies. They give hope. Hope must give way in the WoD.

                                                I'm willing to cut White Wolf some slack for their really early stuff. yes, Berlin by Night was godawful, but really the game was only just out of its "xerox copies held together with paper clips" phase, so we can't really demand perfection. or all that much quality from this period.

                                                Most of the usual suspects have been cited. Gypsies, Tribebook: CoG, Bloo-Dimmed Tides, etc. So I'm just going to avoid the "garbage" period of Vampire, and the social commentary stuff, and get right into the meat of bad books.

                                                Bastet was terrible. Okay, fine, your first shot at a breedbook, it's not going ot be stunning. But it's painfully clear Phil Brucato would have rather been writing a Mage supplement instead of this. In fact it reads like he was trying to turn it into one. And of course we a;; expect every splatbook to basically go "we're the best, everyone else sucks," but the bastet book just goes WAAAAAY overboard on the Mary Sue-ness. It creates a whole lot of jargon which exists solely to create the illusion that Bastet are like totally different - but they're really not, they're just more Jargon-y. Which is another problem. The Breed book didn't really make any efforts to make them more than "Garou who doink big cats." Most (not all) of the Breed Books create a fairly compelling new outlook unique to that breed, but the bastet are just like "pffft, Wyrm, Weaver and Wyld? That's dog nonsense! Come listen about Cahlash, Rajah, and Nala, the TRUTH!" - hurk, huuuurk! The artwork was uniformly terrible (save for most of the Ron Spencer - even he has some bad ones in this) and the design is not great.

                                                Nuwisha: if Bastet was too much fluff for too little interesting stuff, Nuwisha was the opposite. it's a book for an entire changing breed, and it's like ten pages smaller than any of the 2nd edition tribe books! I actually bought this one off the shelf for $12 - fifty cents a page, almost! They get Garou breed and Ragabash Gifts, then their own list... The Nuwisha came off less like an independent breed, and more like "The skinny Ragabash tribe." They could have gone places and instead we get half the information we need (what are the special maneuvers those totems give? What's the Nuwisha starting Willpower? What are these Gifts and rites that you are making errata for, they're not in this book!) and only a quarter of the information we want. The art is pretty generic middle-period WoD, and is frequently cartoony - which doesn't help the "goofball syndrome" this book alternately discourages and encourages.

                                                Havens of the damned simply did not need to exist. MTV Cribz for vampires. MAYBE if there had been more to it? Throw a module in the back. Put in some splat-by-splat general info beyond the specific havens. Outlines for how domain is handled in different situations, etc. It was never going to be a great book, but it could have been more than it was.

                                                War in Concordia is another "didn't need to exist" book. Changeling had so much basic stuff left undone. At least a REAL Boggan kithbook, guys! A 2nd edition player's guide wouldn't have killed anyone, honest! But no, we get this tome about how to set fire to your changeling game because White Wolf wasn't going to support it anymore.

                                                Destiny's Price is, hands-down, the goofiest, most inept book I've ever read. I'm sorry to the author but there is no more surefire way to sweep me up into a fit of giggles than someone writing about "da skreetz, yo" than when they are doing so in a microsoft word document in an air-conditioned office, to create a book for a game about Harry Potter vs. Agent Smith (I know mage is "more than that" so spare me, folks.) It is the HEIGHT of pretension, in a game line already famed for its pretension, from a company known for pretense. it is staggeringly bad and ludicrous. The specific iformation isn't bad game-wise, but the delivery and presentation is just mind-bogglingly lame (or as Destiny's Price itself would say, "Yo G homie-dawgs, dis here 'zine be trey whack yo dig for shizzle, no beef whuddup! Pssst shit fuck fuck shit hookers, wuuuuuuurd")

                                                            I've been rethinking this some. I think there is a difference in terms of religion and race in terms of a game line, because often in a game line, the storyteller has to decide what creation story is true for their game. Demon the Fallen is a bit more explicit about it, but really, any Vampire game that actually features Caine has made the same choice in terms of what is true for the particular story. Essentially, one religion (or no religion) will be "right" in terms of a game, the others are misguided somehow.

                                                            That said, given all of the negative experiences people have had regarding religious oppression and persecution, it's perfectly understandable that many folk will be upset or offended by the treatment of their religion. In the real world, it's not really enough to just say "we're only mocking an idea" when people have been tortured and killed based on those ideas. Demon is a great game, but it's a game that is based around Christian ideas (whether it upholds or deconstructs them) and not everyone is going to be okay with it.
