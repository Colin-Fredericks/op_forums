                Two things undermined any kind of mass popularity of the Hollow Ones over any of the Traditions.

                First, most players were not in fact Goths or wanted to play one. It was a particular musical sub-culture that had limited appeal, the popularity of the Cure not withstanding. Those players who did have an interest in that were often Goths themselves, and they usually wanted to roleplay something different.

                Second, the Traditions were simply much more interesting with some intellectual rigor behind them. They had fully thought out paradigms with extensive history and lore, and a distinctive type of enlightenment. In contrast, the Hollow Ones seemed weak, stunted, and limited. They lacked the "wow, that's so cool; that's what I want to be" compared to characters who knew super-kung fu, masters of Western hermeticism, spirit talking shamans, bad ass witches, mad scientists from the pulps, and so on. If those are the other members of your chantry, who wants to play the social misfit who listens to Bauhaus and Siouxsie Sioux while conducting a seance? That's a totally valid concept and could still be fun, but often seems lame compared to the alternatives for most people. Such characters could make interesting NPCs, but they usually lacked the appeal for people seeking to live out their power fantasies vicariously through their characters.

                Anarchs in Vampire are not analogous to Hollow Ones in Mage. The anarchs weren't actually a clan despite the Brujah being associated with them. Any character concept might find itself directed towards the anarch camp if she runs afoul of the prince. It's just a camp your character might fall into even if you aren't sympathetic to them. The Hollow Ones were just another splat who would already be on the same team as the Traditions except that they're so clueless and unimportant they often don't realize there are even people like the Marauders, Technocracy, or Nephandi.

The Hollow Ones were just another splat who would already be on the same team as the Traditions except that they're so clueless and unimportant they often don't realize there are even people like the Marauders, Technocracy, or Nephandi.
                        I do agree that the Traditions had more depth than the Hollow Ones, but all things considered, that was not a high bar to clear. The material with the Traditions themselves was sorely lacking; even back in 2e when they had more occultists on the writer's team, the ideologies and cultures of the Traditions all felt very shallow. Conceptually, it just made no sense that you had to belong to a faction with a globe-spanning culture in order to have your character use some of the most common magical traditions on the planet. If anything I think that the organizations in Mage should have been based on political or practical aims rather than philosophical ones. I would argue that the Traditions may have actually stunted the imagination of some of the newer players, since they provided an easy paradigm package that didn't require much thought. Ideally, if someone is coming into a Mage game they should really be invested in coming up with the paradigm and magical style of their character, and that isn't needed if they can pick the menu for the core archetypes. The Traditions felt too archetypal to really be useful outside of the utility of an aid for beginners.

                        On the topic of the Traditions' philosophical depth and lack thereof, I would also like to point out that the Traditions were always more about what media image for your wizard that you wanted. The Order of Hermes are plainly Ars Magicka wizards that don't even have much hermetic philosophy to them so much as trappings (to be fair, many magicians in Europe could not really be described as hermetic either; western ceremonial magic was never solely hermetic). Verbena are druids and also the Wicca player option. Sons of Ether are literally mad scientists (in reality, a lot of the theories of technomancers should be wildly different from one another, it's sort of handwaved in Ascension by stating that the Ether can also represent other hidden variables, but not a lot of effort was put into that). The Akashic Brotherhood are basically 90s kung-fu movie monks who rely more on the Southeast Asia trappings than the lore of sramanic traditions. The Cult of Ecstasy are the psychonaut faction (and I actually think that they were actually pulled off pretty decently compared to some of the other Traditions; it is hard to articulate why, but I think it has to do with how their ancient roots are often glossed over and the more recent spiritual movements of the 1960s onwards get more attention in their write-up, in a weird situation where being broad and generic was actually a benefit). The Celestial Chorus has the unlikely unitarian alliance and general early 1900s monistic theology going on (I have heard some people use the term Theosophy to describe them, but to be honest, they were always quite distinct from that movement). Euthanatoi don't really feel Vedantist in any of their incarnations, honestly. And I don't think much more needs to be said about the Dreamspeakers; needless to say, it is more than a bit funny to imagine that all shamanistic systems on the planet decided that they agreed on anything.

                            Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

                            Being possible to run such a game is different from such a game being the intended default for the setting.

                            I agree that M20 made it possible to run this sort of game. I agree that you can make the Hollow Ones the protagonists of your chronicle, or your version of the setting.

                            What I don't agree is that they've ever been intended as the protagonists of the setting as a whole, in the sense the Anarchs were meant as the protagonists of Vampire.
                            My point is that the idea that M20 is somehow less suitable than earlier editions to the sort of gameplay CTPhipps is advocating for is backwards. And as I said, the hypothetical new edition that Brucato outlined in Mage Made Easy would have embraced the independent mage as the intended protagonists of the setting; but in doing so, it would have rejected the idea of the Hollow Ones being the intended protagonists.

                            Because each edition has moved the Hollow Ones further from being "the faction of Orphans". 1e treated them as synonymous. 2e treated the Hollowers as a proto-Tradition composed of former Orphans looking for acceptance among the Traditions, and presented Orphans as a separate category. MRev presented the Hollow Ones as a Tradition featuring Romance as one of its defining features. Finally, M20 presented the Hollow Ones as a full-on Craft, complete with its own preferred Focus, with the Practices of Chaos Magick and Gutter Magick being "near-universal among Hollow Ones." By contrast, M20's entry for Orphans emphasizes the lack of any unifying theme: even Gutter Magick, while mentioned, is merely "especially prevalent in the technological world" as opposed to being "near-universal".
