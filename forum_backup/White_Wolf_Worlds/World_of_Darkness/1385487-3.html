                                They're both valid ways of handling the game but I think that we might have boiled down this debate to what is ultimately a difference of paradigm. X D

                                Regardless, for my take, I prefer and think it's more interesting to go with the revised philosophy of Awakening. An unlocking of latent potential yes but ultimately one's absolute conviction in their ideals and beliefs that allows them to bend reality and then the journey of self-discovery towards True Ascension where the Willworker must refine their beliefs and consider whether or not what they do is right or even what they think it is. If reality or life or anything is what they think it is.

                                I see the merits of running a story in the style of the Awakened all being aware of their ability to harness their Will to shape reality and having the conflict over which method of working will is best, but I ultimately find the personal storytelling potential of the revised philosophy more appealing so that's the one that I lean towards.

                                As far as Hedge Magic is concerned, like what others have said above, I generally consider that to be some sort of mystical knack or connection to manipulate existing cosmic mechanisms, mechanisms that the Awakened may or may not be able to create, alter or destroy but mechanisms that can theoretically be learned and used by anyone with the right potential, somewhat akin to the defined and 'linear' magic of most other gamelines.

                                                "No one formula or path can dictate Awakening; this awareness strikes where it will, without distinction." Mage Revised.

                                                Mages not truly understanding how Awakenings work is an underlying conceit of the game's logic. If Awakening was truly understood as a process, mages could purposefully Awaken people by forcing them through that process. But since the nature of Awakening is a mystery, it's impossible to reliably cause them.

                                                Moreover, the books are rather clear that Awakenings are as unique as the individual mage. Every mage experiences Awakening in their own way, from their own perspective, mixed up with their own Avatar.

                                                Some mages just Awaken. Some mages spend years struggling to figure out what's going on with them as they start to understand things that they shouldn't, and that the weird stuff happening around them is actually because of them. The very fact that some mid-Awakening characters will end up doing Awakened magic despite not having any sort of understanding about their ability to will the universe into compliance undercuts the idea that will is an inherent component instead of just a very powerful tool of magic.

                                                The Kitab al-Alacir does not describe magic via will-working at all. Everything outside the walls, metaphorically of your mind, are true, you bring them into the walls by exploration, and thus expand yourself if you explore with wisdom, or destroy yourself if you bring in things without properly examining them (aka the Trojan Horse).

                                                Just as all that talk about Euthanatos philosophy is rooted in their ideal that magic is fueled by duty, not will, the Etherites hold that magic isn't wiling something to exist that doesn't, but finding that with already exists but isn't "within the walls" of other people's minds, and bringing it back with you.
