                                Tribebook: CoG Revised has already been mentioned as being one of the worst supplements of the cWoD, and I agree that Gypsies is without doubt the worst WW book ever published. I haven't read it and never want to, so I can't comment on its quality directly. Even so, the entire concept of the book should never have gotten off the brainstorming page. And WoD: Mafia? Same thing. Way too many stereotypes involved in either book to make it a practical gaming book.

                                So those are my top three contenders for worst WW books ever published: 1. Gypsies, 2. CoG Revised and 3. Mafia.

                                Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand I remember enjoying when it first came out, but not long after that, I remember thinking how it was silly and over the top. And the stuff about Vicissitude being a sentient disease and the Souleater war I never liked at all. I'm interested to see how V20 will "redeem"(another poster's word, not mine) DSotBH in its future supplement, but that's for another thread and time.

                                One particular book that I absolutely cannot stand (regardless of any quality as a gaming book it might have) is the Werewolf supplement Chronicle of the Black Labyrinth. When I bought it, I was expecting something like a Werewolf version of the Book of Nod. What I got was first-hand witness accounts of Wyrm-ridden perversities and atrocities that began turning my stomach from page 1. Granted, that's what the book is supposed to do, but to this day and for the rest of my life I absolutely refuse to take that book off my shelf nor remove it from its plastic storage bag. I mean, holy jeebus, even Freak Legion didn't gross me out as much. (Granted, Freak Legion had its own irreverent quality which took out some of the sting, but we all know that it's by no means a pleasant book to read.)

                                People were actually hating on Blood-Dimmed Tides? I think that one is actually one of the best WW books of all. I wasn't much interested in the Changeling or Wraith parts of it, but the rest I liked. Hell, the blurb about the Arctic Ocean helped me find the Fifth Talon of the Wyrm!

                                I'm not a big fan of Wraith, but I think WW went above and beyond the call of duty in trying to make Shoah: Charnel Houses of Europe as respectful of the Holocaust as they could. Hell, the foreword of Shoah even describes how the authors approached Janet Berliner to do the foreword for the book and how she almost balked at the idea of a gaming book using the Holocaust as setting until she said to herself "We must teach them through the tools with which they are comfortable." Say what you will about the book, but the intentions of publishing it were honorable. I learned quite a bit about the Holocaust from this book, perhaps more than from any other source I've encountered in my life with the exception of the Holocaust Museum in DC. I'm just a short drive away from the Museum and my sister works there, too.

                                (If you've never been to the Holocaust Museum in DC and get the opportunity to visit it, I emphatically recommend that you do so. Be warned, though: it's a very intense experience and not for the faint-hearted.)

                                1e Clanbook: Malkavian...perhaps the book most responsible for the proliferation of Fishmalk PCs. I tried to read that book and...couldn't. It gave one of my Vampire players a noteworthy character concept: an elder Noddist scholar who could very well have been a Mnemosyne...but the PC was still essentially a Fishmalk. Ugh.

                                                We don't need to assume a single 4th gen needs to have a chance in a one-on-one, stand-up fight for the War of Ages to matter, because the War of Ages was never about Street Fighter-style battles to the death. It's about playing the long game to win. That's what the whole thing IS! Wheels within wheels, pawns moving pawns who move other pawns. Because when you are one of the thirteen blood gods, fighting any number of blood demigods you sired through the ages, you don't need to poke your head out there to win, but poking your head out there definitely allows your enemies to dog-pile on you.

                                                So no, we don't need to assume a single 4th gen can take on a 3rd gen. And coincidentally, we don't see that happening. We see three Bodhisattva are needed for one freshly awakened Ante. We see Tremere taking down a sleeping ante, and still bringing pals along just in case. We see a band of allies going after [Lasombra] and [Tzimisce], and only getting one of those two dead (and he might have even been willing, so yeah).

                                                The problem I do see, however, is if a single 4th gen can take on a 3rd gen in a straight fight and have even a "less than 50%, but better than 33%" chance of winning, then the War of Ages starts making no sense. Because there are a hell of a lot more 4th generation kindred than there are 3rd gen. How have so many antediluvians survived into the modern age if they are outnumbered by guys with a good chance of taking them down and a motivation to do so?

                                                            I would like to toss in "Holy War," from Hunter: the Reckoning. It had some really cool stuff in it (a Hunter-Net equivalent for regions with limited internet access, nifty new Edges, and a good... attempt... at a non-Western outlook on The Hunt), but it also had some very silly things (Middle-Eastern Imbued can't be Martyrs, because Islam encourages everyone to sacrifice themselves for the good of others).

                                                            Now, White Wolf isn't entirely to blame for the awkwardness of this book, considering that it was published one week before September 11th, 2001. Had the terrorist attacks not occurred, it would have just been one more RPG setting book that plays up outdated stereotypes for the sake of an exotic setting. But afterwards... the outdated stereotypes and politics just became uncomfortable and obviously inaccurate.

                                                            Although, if they had written and published it a year later, it might have been far worse...
