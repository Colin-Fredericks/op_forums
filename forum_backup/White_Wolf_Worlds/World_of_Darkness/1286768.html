    How Do You All Treat Forces Making a Force Field?

    02-11-2019, 01:24 PM
    This came up in a session I was running, and I wanted the opinion of others to see how they would handle it.

    One of my players has Forces 2 and Life 3, so he should be able to reliably make a Force Field around himself, as Force Fields seem to be specifically called out in the Forces 2 Sphere level. Since damage with Magick uses the Damage/Duration Chart, I figured a Force Field should as well for consistency. We're using the Divided Successes special rule, which means a Minor Ritual (Up to 5 Successes) gets to 4 Successes on the Damage side of the chart and 1 more to turn it from a "Turn" to a "Scene". The Force Field was against any sort of attack that used "Momentum" against him, so punches, kicks, and gunshots were all being reduced (Though it wouldn't stop a flame thrower or some sort of ice based attack). This ended up giving him 8 Levels of Soak against all of these things for the entire scene, which effectively made him immune to mundane attacks. Obviously the effect is Vulgar, but from the rules as a successful cast is just one point of Paradox.

    For it being a Minor Ritual, only needing at most five minutes to cast and only generating one point of Paradox, it seemed pretty nuts during the game since they were at the time fighting some more mundane threats.

    Has anyone else ever messed with creating a "Force Field" type spell, and if so how did you handle the effects of the spell? I'm certainly not against having a character able to achieve that sort of power, I just wanted to make sure I'm not missing anything else in the rules that better illustrates what a Force Field effect would be.

      Instead of adding Soak dice, I'd have ruled the force field to reduce successes on the attack rolls against the mage. Maybe I'd go even further, giving them only as many "repel" dice, as the player would invest into the strength of the effect. This way, it is not a perfect no-weight armor but the minor protection spell a Forces 2 effect should be.
      Forces 3 or higher could potentially repel entire attacks or even throw them back at the attackers (with 4, possibly - if the effect cancels all the successes of the attack).

      Edit: As a rule of thumb: A lasting effect shouldn't negate successes automatically, for balancing reasons. A minor sphere effect should rather have an indirect effect (modifying attack or defensive rolls) instead of direct effects (modifying damage).

          The way I do it may be less restrictive on balance than TheVarulfen's method. Maybe it's OP, but I haven't found it to be too bad.

          -I think it should only require Forces 2, because you're diverting an object's direction, not changing the kinetic energy into a different type of force.

          -If they have Life 2, it is normal difficulty. But my house rule for "locking" effects is that if you don't have the Pattern Sphere to lock, it only incurs a +1 difficulty. Without this allowance you need Matter 2 and Life 2 to do too many things.

          -I treat it as negating attack successes. I rule that you can negate up to four successes and still be Coincidental. Even if that provokes Samuel L. Jackson to say "WE WITNESSED A MIRACLE, AND I WANT YOU TO F*&%ING ACKNOWLEDGE IT!"

          -I make a base success threshold of 3 (4 if it is on another person), which negates 2 successes for all gunshots for a scene. Another success makes it negate 4 firearms successes per attack received. If they want to negate more successes, they have to declare as much from the beginning, and the entire effect is Vulgar.

          -If they want to block additional types of attacks, the success threshold is higher, and it is more likely to be Vulgar. Especially if it blocks hand-to-hand attacks.

          -I use 2E "sustained" rules, so a shield counts toward your two sustained Effects before you start taking penalties.

                          Originally posted by Faradn View Post
                          -I think it should only require Forces 2, because you're diverting an object's direction, not changing the kinetic energy into a different type of force.
                          I think this one depends a great deal on the effect behind the force field. There are deflecting force fields that change the direction of the kinetic energy, sapping force fields that just lower the kinetic energy in something, transference force fields that move the kinetic energy into something else, pseudo* frictional force fields that convert kinetic energy into heat energy, magnetic/kinetic force fields that create an actual repulsive field, windstorm force fields that increase air speeds to interfere with the object's path, etc.

                          *I say pseudo frictional because a real frictional force field would work by changing the properties of the air around the object, which would be Matter.

                          Some of these are clearly just changing direction or location of the force (Forces 2), while some are changing one force into another (Forces 3), and some are creating a new Force where none existed before (Forces 3, Prime 2). You could even make a Mass Effect style force field, though that either works by manipulating mass (Matter) or by manipulating gravity (Forces 4 or 5).

                                              How do you all rule "Arete Enhancing Abilities" when using Forces to enhance a punch/sword swipe/bullet? My group had been playing it as using the Damage chart just like any Forces attack, which add Levels of Damage to the attack
                                              The official rule is that any magical damage *replaces* the normal attack's damage - purely for balancing reasons, really.
                                              Magick damage can quickly get insanely high. An Arete 3 character can dash out 10 aggravated damage with fiery/electric forces very easily (spend 1 willpower for an extra success, spend ~2 Quintessence to lower diff -> probably 4 successes -> 8 damage + 1 'free' forces damage level -> 10 damage.). Remember that this is the damage result, not the hit result - those 10 damage don't get turned into dice and rolled.

                                              And 10 damage pretty much equals the average result of a 20-dice damage roll(!)

                                              Even a 3-success Arete roll with a non-forces sphere attack is already quite a wallop of 6 fixed (pre-soak) applied damage. With forces you just need 2 successes for that.
                                              And even *if* it is just a bashing kinetic-forces attack, if the average thug out there soaks 2 of those 6 bashing damage, that leaves them with taking 4. A result that has a very high chance of stunning them for the current *and* next round if the damage is equal or higher than their stamina. People always forget the stun rule.

                                                      I've been toying with the idea of a kinetic forces shield having a "Durability" as if it were an actual object (per Mage 20). At the end of the day, the projectile doesn't care whether it's hitting a wall, or an invisible barrier made of kinetic "force" that provides the same resistance against the projectile as a wall would. In essence I would subtract the durability from the projectiles' damage rating (it wouldn't make sense to factor in the ramp from the attack if you consider that a magical kinetic barrier is probably uniformly strong all around), if there's enough left over to make it through, you would tack the ramp back on and roll.

                                                          If the shield is essentially a constant 'push' that affects certain kinds of objects that try to hit you, it does not make sense that durability lowers - it is less of a barrier that wears down and more of a constant literal effect of physics around the mage.
