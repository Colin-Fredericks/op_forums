                                            In terms of fixing, it would already help a great deal to give the Creed-FIelds some consistency.

                                            Let's start with Faithful and Martial:
                                            • Faithful: Any direct conflict (physical, social, or mental) with the supernatural while on the Hunt.
                                            • Martial: Physical conflict while on the Hunt (which need not be with the quarry itself, or even the supernatural).

                                            Both are about conflict while on the Hunt and Faithful talks about any direct conflict with the supernatural (the bit about "physical, social or mental" can go because that's just a description of well, any roll, there are no rolls that are not physical, social or mental). But Martial restricts it to phyiscal conflict and calls out that the supernatural/Quarry don't need to be involved.
                                            But then you get Inquisitive:
                                            • Inquisitives: Gaining information while on the Hunt, such as research, breaking and entering, and interrogation.

                                            Here the supernatural/Quarry isn't mentioned at all. And rather than go with the "physical, social or mental" nomenclature, three example tasks that fall under mental (research), physical (breaking and entering) and social (interrogation) are listed. So Inquisitives can add Desperation-Dice to any roll as long as it fulfills the goal of gaining information. But gaining information about what...? In context with the previous two, it makes it sound like Inquisitives can add Desperation-Dice which is about gaining information in general even if it's unrelated to the supernatural/Quarry. And even then, with Martial this was called out but here, it isn't talked about at all.
                                            And once again, the formula changes with Underground:
                                            • Underground: Stealth and subterfuge in service of the Hunt.

                                            Physical, Social and Mental aren't mentioned, the relation to the supernatural/Quarry isn't mentioned but now it also isn't goal-oriented like Inquisitive, this time it's about methodology. And no examples like with Inquisitive are listed. Also, it's suddenly "in service of the Hunt" instead of "while on the Hunt".
                                            But the most confusing is Entrepreneurial:
                                            • Entrepreneurial: Building, inventing, augmenting, or repairing while on the Hunt.

                                            Okay, no mention of Physical, Mental or Social, no goal-oriented description, no examples of tasks... it's just verbs. Does this Creed allow to add Desperation-Dice to repair... relationships as well? Or to invent an excuse? Or to build a legal case against somebody? It's just verbs after all. Of course, in the context of the bigger write-up we can assume this is all in relation to technology but still...

                                            This is what I mean when I say that I personally have no desire to play this right now. The foundation of this game thanks to its shoddy writing is so flawed that you'll have to put in work to get this game to a working state.

                                                I think I agree with those saying that stripping out detail and context has been taken as simplifying the rules.                                                 For example:

                                                Contests: Use whenever two narratively important parties actively engage each other, such as in combat or a battle of wits. E.g., PC versus Big Bad.
                                                Take half: When two parties engage each other, but the opponent is a minor character and/or you want to speed up the resolution, take half of their dice pool. E.g., PC v trivial NPC.
                                                Passive: When one party is working against an unwitting or inactive opponent, use the opponent's Skill rating as the Difficulty instead of taking half. (You could also use Discipline, Advantage or Power stats for things too.) E.g., PC v an NPC who is unaware of their presence.

                                                Now you have a framework which helps you make rulings on the fly without needing to consult the book for which dice pool or difficulty to use each time.

                                                That seems to be the sort of thing WoD5 wants you to be able to do, but it doesn't give you the tools to actually do that.

                                                            What's so strange about this is that we're talking about basic stuff here. Also, the whole existence of the "Expanded Rules"-book as a "living document" (ie it gets regular updates) is a double-edged sword when looking at H5. When there's so little consideration in paying attention to the minutiae of the mechanics and how they impact the experience at the table, then what made H5 so bad will be an issue for all WOD5E games going forward. When there's an Expanded rules"-book in existence that is considered optional, the idea of how the game is supposed to work in the creators' minds becomes much more complicated - both for the writers and the people who will eventually play the game. And then it's natural for authors to write with a perspective where they either just assume you know what they're getting at without providing clarity within the text or they're unaware of an issue with how the game works (especially because now there's a version of the game with expanded rules applied and one without those).

                                                            Maybe it could work if Justin Achilli & co had a good framework for their version of the WOD5E Storyteller system. But H5 clearly wasn't that.

                                                            In regards to W5, since we're talking about the bad way H5 with rules... There's this part in the Discord-Q&A where I had flashbacks to when Justin Achilli talked about "sober play" and why H5 didn't need a morality-system (similar to Humanity in V5):

                                                            Will 'Chronicle Tenets' feature in W5? If yes, will they be a gameplay-element like in V5 or more of a mix of gameplay and 'Safety Tool' as in H5 (which I think has some problems)?
                                                            Still in development. Werewolf has a lot of "moving parts" systemically, and the more systems in place, the more of a challenge to uninfluenced play there are. Werewolves don't have Convictions, for example, for that very reason, while the tribes have Ban and Favor suggesting key attitudes and values.
                                                            I can't wait for W5 to have the same dissonance as H5 has... In H5 you're supposed to play the "good hunters", not those cheap knockoffs who work for organizations. And if your hunter-player-character cares absolutely nothing about collateral damage and starts with three Touchstones but only starts caring after two of them die, the system provided in the book is absolutely fine with that. Justin Achilli really seems to believe that game-systems are a hindrance to play. That doesn't mean all games need to be these huge tomes of rules and mechanics (like Shadowrun, for example) but in my opinion the big issue is that Justin Achilli wants a pure Story-game and the WOD5E-Storyteller-system as established by V5 isn't that. And since this is a game as a brand which still wants to uphold V5 as part of the brand, he can't just flip the table and start over completely. But he still has these nagging thoughts in his head like that systems are a hurdle to playing the game.

                                                            And that's the whole rationale for "sober play" and removing the "Humanity-morality-system: In Justin's mind Humanity is a system that makes it harder for players to do what they want - and that's bad. The solution: Don't have that system. I don't think that's a solution. Also, good story-games like good PbtA-games may be rules-light but what systems they have matters. The idea of rules-light isn't for the system to stay out of the way, it's for the system to emphasize and focus on what matters thematically to the game and the kind of storytelling it wants to emphasize.
