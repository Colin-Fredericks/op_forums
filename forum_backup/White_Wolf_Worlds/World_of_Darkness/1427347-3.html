                Personally, I prefer treating the various Avatar related merits and flaws as being at least correlated with the mage's Focus. There can be exceptions to this (the recently cited Technocracy-specific Flaw is by definition an exception to this, and one that I generalize as “your Avatar loathes your Focus”); but for the most part, I prefer the notion that the Avatar either tends to be compatible with the Mage's belief system (you're not going to have an Avatar providing access to Past Lives if the mage in question doesn't believe in Past Lives; or is it vice versa?) or is somehow constrained by the mage's beliefs (a mage with a belief system that doesn't allow for Past Lives supresses her Avatar's ability to impart visions of any past lives it happens to have).

                Again, there would be exceptions; but for the most part, Foci that involve the notion of reincarnation will tend to have the lion's share of Past Lives; and Technocratic Geniuses are more likely to be Circumspect than most Avatars. That sort of thing.

                My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                            My own personal headcanon is that Avatar and Soul are one and the same and the whole thing reincarnates.

                            A Wraith can’t work Magick for the same reason a vampire or Sleeper can’t; Magick requires the soul to be in a particular state of being. Specifically it seems the soul must be tied to a specific living pattern that quintessence flows freely through... versus a vampiric corpse which is a static pool of quintessence that is only refueled by sucking more out of living beings or a bodiless soul that only refuels off the memories and emotions of others. Werewolves and Fae are basically soul/spirit hybrids that result in their only being able to channel quintessence in accord with their spirit’s nature.

                            Sleepers have the same soul state as a Mage, just not conscious access to it so it exerts a general pressure against that which it doesn’t believe in. This is why their disbelief causes pushback against vulgar Magick while supernaturals whose souls aren’t linked up in the same way don’t.

                            As to what happens to a soul/avatar (Awake or Sleeping), I think the very nature of WoD’s cosmology is set up such that reincarnation is the default. That’s the whole triatic cycle; birth, life, death, birth again. As Orpheus posits; even Oblivion itself is a lie; it all gets recycled eventually.

                            Wraiths are just stuck on one part of the cycle and the main difference between Transcendence and Oblivion is how much you retain on your next spin through the cycle (those who transcend recycle with important aspects of themselves intact, key personality traits, desires, spiritual connections, submerged memories... Oblivion instead wipes all those away and results in having to start over from scratch).

                            So a Mage who becomes a Wraith isn’t going to see someone else pop up with their recycled avatar; their soul/avatar is busy being a Wraith. Only once they’ve Transcended (in which case their next life will have strong links to their previous ones) or met Oblivion (in which case their soul starts again fresh in the next life) can their soul/avatar be reborn in a new mortal body.

                            Ascension is leaving the whole cycle behind for whatever is beyond it. This might be what a Christian recognizes as Heaven/Union with God. It might instead be the ability to spawn your own new Creation in accord with your Will or the Transcendence of Nonbeing... regardless it’s something beyond even Awakened comprehension.*

                            * Heck, leave aside the concept of linear time and the entire WoD and everyone in it might be the countless journeys of a single soul through the cycles until it can finally know itself so completely that it can ascend/move on to its next thought experiment (i.e. not only are we all gods, we’re all the same God endeavoring to know Itself better).

                                    Originally posted by Erinys View Post

                                    It could be. I've not looked at End Game in quite a while, but here's a re-post from another thread that dealt with Oblivion and the larger WoD cosmology which sums up part of it;

                                    Originally posted by Chris24601
                                    The main way I dealt with it in my Mage campaign was to take one of the elements of Orpheus and run with it; Oblivion is a LIE. The idea that all of Creation can be undone is the Nephandic equivalent of a fairy tale. In one campaign the PCs were investigating and ended up on the edge of Oblivion itself... specifically at the spiritual embodiment of the Fallen Tower which was a Nephandic holy site that leaned out over the maw of Oblivion). What they learned through ancient writings on the walls is that the Oblivion was a myth created by the first Neverborn Malfeans... the first beings to reach its edge and falter in primal fear at the gateway to the unknown. This myth of eternal destruction filtered up to other spirits of the dead and the practices of Mages who associated with the dead and over time was corrupted by angry outcast mages into a nihilistic credo that became the first Nephandi.

                                    It filtered up to the Fera as well in the story that the Primeval Wyrm had gone insane and was now consuming everything.

                                    The Nephandic Cauls, the Black Spiral, the Banes and corruption spirits all grew from the power of belief in this lie and further hiding the truth.

                                    The Truth? Oblivion/the Wyrm is what it has always been... the portal to birth/rebirth and the transition from one life to the next. The primal fear of the Neverborn is the fear of an infant being born from the safety of a mother’s womb that is all it has ever known into a bright, cold uncertain world beyond.

                                    The Neverborn are literally the “never born.” Primeval miscarriages whose spirits were too afraid of birth and even more terrified of the unknowns of rebirth to pass through “Oblivion’s” maw.

                                    The greatest irony is that the corruption the werewolves are fighting isn’t actually the Wyrm at all. It’s just the natural side effect of the Weaver’s efforts to keep the current world from sliding into the maw of Rebirth.

                                    By defying the natural pull of entropy, the Wraith Hierarchy, the Weaver Spirits (embodying man’s primal desire for a stable and knowable world... a fear of chaos and the unknown), the static vampires and the Technocracy all must build ever more elaborate constructs and schemes to keep it from all tumbling down... and it is the imperfections in this ever more rickety structure and compromises needed to keep it all afloat that creates the corruption the werewolves sense as The Wyrm.

                                    What the werewolves are actually fighting is not the True Wyrm, but the corruption in the construct built by Man to protect itself from the very primal fears of corruption/oblivion placed in them by the Impergium. The Nephandi are lunatics who drive themselves insane because their exposure to “The Truth” instills in them not a desire to clear away the rot so there’s room for new growth, but a nihilistic desire to “tear it all down.”

                                    Both the Banes and the Nephandi are self-destructive messes because they are NOT actually any sort of cosmic force of oblivion.

                                    The banes the werewolves fight are the natural corruption of a system that has grown too stagnant (like papering over the dry rot or applying another layer of bondo and paint to the rust on a car), but corruption keeps growing because the wolves are so focused on the symptoms, they can’t see the disease (i.e. the stagnation of the world) and even more that THEY are actually the root cause of that disease (the world stagnates because of its overwhelming fear of the unknown... of the monsters that lurk there and prey upon them... in short, the werewolves from days past).

                                    The Nephandi are the people who react to a cancer diagnosis by trying to kill themselves to just get it over with instead of devoting the time and energy it would take to actually cleanse the body of the poisons.

                                    In other words, the actual means of restoring the cosmic balance is to stop fighting “Oblivion” (or to reach Transcendence, which is the same thing except you're able to bring a few important things with you to the next cycle) and allow the dead and corrupt to be consumed so they can be reborn. The Wyrm and Nephandi are just the two opposite extremes of dealing with it (“ignore it and hope it goes away” and “burn it all down” respectively).
