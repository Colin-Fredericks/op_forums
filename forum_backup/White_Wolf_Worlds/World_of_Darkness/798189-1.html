    Originally posted by KokoroWatari View Post

    Well, actually I am not indicating that bunks are making magic more mundane. Oppositely, it lowers the mundane eyes banality. Making casted magic more possible to mundane eye.
    Yes and no. Cantrips in the old system had to overcome the target's Banality, thus making mundane eyes see the magical. But Bunks themselves are more to do with the relationship between a changeling and the Dreaming than between a changeling and her target.

    Re-reading this, it looks like I hate Bunks, and I don't. I'll clarify: I love Bunks, I just think they're part of the problem with tone that Changeling sometimes had, and so they're a key part of what will fix that.

    I'm also hoping for Bunks that feel more 'fae' and less 'make believe'/'playground'. The latter is fine for some childlings, but it doesn't seem to fit with the folklore much.

                Cosmetically? They might be similar, they might be different. It really depends on how the player/ST describes what they're doing. I've been to a few tarot readers for fun in my time; not one of them has read the cards the same way as any other. Some readers chant over their cards. Others flip them in silence. Some have incense, others smoke cigarettes. There are dozens of types of decks, dozens of types of layouts, dozens of ways to shuffle and cut a deck, and even card interpretations are different and randomized. If a mage and a changeling are sitting side by side using their magic to read the future with tarot cards, that (in my opinion) is probably going to be where the similarity begins and ends.

                As for the "feeling": they're completely different systems from completely different sources of power. A changeling is not a mage, and a mage is not a changeling. They don't use the same magic. Magical similarities should be superficial at best, imo.

                        There are various differences between Bunks and Practices.

                        One difference was mentioned. They serve to pull different "magical strings", so to speak. Practices are to Focus the Will into an instrument in order to alter reality, while Bunks are there to ease the channeling of Glamour into a Banality laden world.

                        Other difference, more important to me, it's what they mean for the users.

                        As their name implies, Practices are more than a convenient way of casting. Practices are something that the Mage has to, well, practice. They're a discipline used to do magick and, many times, a way of living.
                        Mages believe that their focused Practice it's the cause of the alteration of Reality. Even after they leave their Instruments, Mages still believe in their Practices, and old Instruments are still capable of giving a Bonus.

                        Changelings, in contrast, use Bunks for convenience and flair. They don't believe that Bunks have the power, or that certain Bunks, used in certain ways, are a better way of releasing magic than others. To faes, Bunks are a way to ease the usage of magic, not a path to have acces to it.

                        If a certain Bunk seems appropiate for the scene and the fae, they have no problem on doing it. For a mage that would be unthinkable, you can't switch to a different Practice in the moment to do something that "seems glamorous".
                        In the same sense, faes would find ridiculous to defend a particular kind of Bunks over others, and fight over wich Bunk it's better for the world

                        The outside observer wouldn't be able to notice the difference if she hasn't supernatural means to do it, but the practitioners would do them with a very different intent
                        ​
                        But, more to the point, while a certain type of Bunks could be identical in its appearance to certain Practices, this can't always be the case. Glamour springs from histories and dreams and Practices spring from visionary ideas about how the world works, and many are tried and true ways to reach magic in human folklore.
                        Both can overlap a lot. However some Practices might be Banal, tied to Banal ways of understanding the world, and thus unappropiate to Changelings. Even if Weid Sciece it's "practiced" by some faes, certain, more "serious" ways of understanding might be anathema (like bureaucratic magick)

                        (and Mages might take more time reading the cards, small dicepools and all that ).

                            Mages are limited by their paradigm. Changelings are limited by their imagination and personal preference. A technomage isn't likely to write "speak friend and enter" in Sindarin on a wall to create a door because it's not likely her paradigm incorporates Tolkeinisms; she's more likely to write out code in a programming language to "change things" and hack a door into place. A changeling can write Sindarin, knock three times and mime turning a knob, click her heels and say "there's no place like inside", draw the outline of a door with chalk, attempt to Disapparate, stick a key in a hole in the mortar and wait for moonlight to fall on it, anything that suits her tastes.

                            Another example using Tolkein: an ascetic mage isn't going to burst out with Galadriel's "treacherous as the sea" speech to help him magically cow an opponent... but a changeling, any changeling, could find it useful as a Bunk for a couple of Arts.

                                                Originally posted by PookaKnight View Post
                                                It depends on the character. Sure, a sidhe noble is probably going to stick to certain themes, but it would diminish the game greatly if there wasn't room for random, sometimes off the wall improv.
                                                Improv is different to just doing something unrelated, though. You can improvise a Bunk that has resonance with the thing you want to achieve. That requires *more* creativity and *greater* wits than just saying, 'Oh, I'll just moon that passing car and hope for the best.'

                                                Of course, mooning a car might suit the cantrip being cast, but doing it for Omen, say, seems irrelevant. If a changeling still gets a difficulty reduction for doing something irrelevant just because it's silly, then you're not actually rewarding creativity but silliness. And it cheapens the system. People perform Bunks just to get a difficulty modifier, even though the Bunk itself doesn't require any real effort.

                                                People always complain that Changeling is silly, and its fans always defend it against that charge. But we can't if the game actively says, 'We'll make the game easier for you if you're randomly silly all the time.'
