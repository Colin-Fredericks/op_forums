    Originally posted by Alucard View Post
    Sounds like a pretty amusing scene, but aren't Ratkin way too crazy to consider something that relatively complex and abstract?

    And really, why bother when the panic from a global pandemic will crash the economy and achieve the number one goal of population reduction at the same time.

    Manipulating the economy by itself will atract attention they don't want, while not achieving the desired megadeaths.
    Madness doesn't hinder the ability to go for something complex or abstract. Quite the contrary, precisely for being crazy, they are likely to develop a lot of highly abstract and unnecessarily complex plans to do anything they want done. This is probably the reason they are not far more effective already on culling humanity.

    On numbers I won't say much. The books never stated how many Ratkin are out there precisely, just that they are more numerous than almost any other changer, with the possible exception of the Ananasi, for similar reasons: they keep out of sight, and their animal Kin are numerous, breed in great numbers, hide easily and thrived on the modern world, dominating "civilized" areas even in their savage states. If the proposed scenario demands a million Ratkins on total, it is still in the acceptable spectrum.

    Now, let's see:

    Garou
    Some Garou could get a hint of what is going on before it is too late. Mainly the Bone Gnawers, but also the Glass Walkers tribe would have a reasonable chance at it, along with some of the more urban members of other tribes. With their works, the Children of Gaia would be at a good place to get what is going on, while the Blood Talons would be the last to know anything without being directly informed by the Ratkin.

    The more Garou get on the know sooner, the more probable it is for the Nation to act more or less unified against the Ratkin on such a plan. But if information is spread in a more fractured manner, things could go far away from this. The Glass Walkers are the less likely to work with the Ratkin, but some could still see some benefit on guiding more surgical strikes with such bioweapons and still give the thumbs up. Among most of the other tribes there are still groups that would either embrace this as a return to Impergium, or accept it if their favored kinfolk are spared. Bone Gnawers can get on board for survival, Silver Fangs can want a world in turmoil to restore aristocracy, Black Furies can accept the death of men for a world of female supremacy, Silent Striders can want to exterminate the Followers of Set through any means. It is unlikely that all those scenarios happen at once, or at least in concert, and it wouldn't include all of a tribe, but just the members that are interested enough in the promised gains. Blood Talons would just collectively hide the Ratkins plans once they are sure that the diseases aren't going to kill wolves.

    Getting on it soon, the Nation would probably include the Ratkin in their list of Wyrm-Tainted-by-Default enemies and hunt their nests. The less visible the threat is, the more it will take a political toll for its similarity with Yet-Another-War-of-Rage. Too soon, the best moment for the Garou to act, the war would be mostly fought on propaganda and intelligence, wins the side that better convinces the bulk of the Nation. The later, the more open and desperate the war would be, and the more fractured the Nation would be as both the threat becomes more desperate and the potential to have individual agendas furthered by such extreme scenario would increase. Children of Gaia and Glass Walkers would be probably focused on developing cures and other countermeasures, while the rest would depend a lot on what is going on and who's in charge.

    Nagah
    It depends not only on when they learn about it, but also on their actual total resources to deal with it. Such a grandiose plan would require the efforts of all the Nagah to concentrate on the Ratkins. They can achieve to keep the Ratkins at bay through enough assassination, but that would require the Nagah to go all out and have enough resources. Even at the best-case scenario, they would make their presence clear enough to other changers. At the worst-case, they would need to not only make their presence known, but actually act as diplomats to get the resources of the other changers on their side. The only way for the Nagah to not have to show themselves is for them to see ways of putting other changers on the hunt for the Ratkins.

    Ananasi
    It isn't easy to imagine how Queen Ananasa would react, but that could bring the conflict to another level. Depending on how you play the Ananasi, they can be as numerous as the Ratkin, or even more. They serve a purpose similar to that of other changers as a whole, but by their own means and mentality. They can hear to pleas from anyone, and they can ignore anyone without apparent reason. Collectively they follow only one leader, the alien mind of Ananasa, while individually they are extremely fractured already and seem to deal well with this.

    And they supposedly did the War-of-Rage thing with far more efficacy and less guilty than the Garou. So their response will be unpredictable, and probably different for each Spider. But if they go for it collectively, they can be the deciding force on the field, be it by killing the rats, the other changers, the humans, or just manipulating people seemingly at random.

    Rokea
    Probably won't even notice something wrong. And when they notice, they will likely gather in numbers on the coastline just to munch on anything that falls. They want humanity dead, but they also want the Ratkin dead.

    Changers
    On a more general take, the other changers would act depending on how much they know about what is going on. At first, they would let the Ratkins be, or otherwise deal with them as they already did. Ratkins trying to kill humans are not a novelty. But most changers are still linked enough to humanity to still care, specially when their Kinfolk is threatened. Differently from Rokea, Ratkin and Ananasi, other changers can't simply ignore their human Kin, even when they have a healthy source of animal Kin. Most still have a lot invested in human society, but not enough to have the precise planning of the Glass Walkers. And there is simply the interests, goals and problems that won't be solved by killing humanity. They mostly have less of a reason to want such a thing in the first place, at most they would want to get rid of human presence at some locations. So they are likely to invest a good deal of resources in stopping the Wererats.

    But at the same time, most changers don't depend on the human society enough to put it all on the fight. Wyrm-Taint generated by such a slaughter can be a serious concern, but otherwise they can still try to rebuild by themselves on the ruins of human civilization. The Garou are warriors, and even if they don't go all out they still go war-style, but other Fera not so much. They will try for it while they think it is doable, but would retreat once they think they can't stop the rats, so they can keep going without civilization.
