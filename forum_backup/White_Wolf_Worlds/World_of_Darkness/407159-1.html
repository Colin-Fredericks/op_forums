    With a good player and an attentive Storyteller, it works just fine.

    My Hunter: the Reckoning game features several, including a Hunter who has been Imbued for over a decade (the Imbued only became "a thing" ONE year ago), an Inquisitor who follows the Sanbenito heresy (they think some monsters can be saved), and a Japanese dhampir (yep, the most cliched special snowflake you can get). And believe it or not, all the characters are cool, work well enough together, and their uniqueness serves as minor plot points without detracting from the attention given to other player-characters.

    Now, with a bad player or an inattentive/inexperienced/just-plain-dumb Storyteller? Yeah, those are the situations that leads to people automatically assuming a "special snowflake" is a bad thing.

    I've seen those, too, especially when it is the Storyteller's fault. Like the situations where one character is clearly the "main character" and everyone else is a sidekick. Or the one time where the ST allowed the PC to be something special, then became obviously vindictive about it, as though trying to punish the player for choosing something interesting and different.

    So remember: If you are a player, spare a thought for everyone else's fun, and be gracious if your character concept is turned down. If you are a Storyteller, give careful consideration to what the player is asking for, and see if you can suggest a way to make it work in your story.

                                Before anything else I have to say I want to point out that I feel that I don't think everyone here is on the same page as to the definition of "snowflakes". From reading what everyone had to say it seems like some people think that snowflakes are people who only create problem characters that detract from the experience for everyone. While others (myself as an example) see snowflakes as unique character concepts that stand out or break the mold, but do not break the game or experience. They add to it.

                                To those who really mean "Problem players who F up the game for everyone" I don't see snowflakes that way. Those "problem players" are "insert derogative word starting with A" and should be dealt with by the gaming group as a whole. People who want to play a White Howler, an Abomination, or a Mummy True Mage, etc... I am not defending these types of players. In my experience these players (not all, but usually) cause problems.

                                And as to what Heavy Arms said at the bottom of the first page, I totally agree. http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...674#post411674

                                All of that said I thought I'd mention the three PC's in the current group I play in. These represent "snowflakes" that I think are acceptable. I leave your personal opinions up to you guys and will take no insult. Feel free to skip what follows, it's not part of the argument. Just an example of what I think is fine in a game.

                                Aizen - Toreador

                                At the start of the new game I told my ST that I wanted to play a master swordsman and he knew I loved Tories. He suggested I play a Toreador whose "art" was sword fighting. It was a stretch, but opened up some interesting role playing. Resentful Sire who accidentally Embraced me and had to have her Primogen Sire cover everything all up for example. I started off as a veteran of the Setite Blood War in our city with a reputation for combat, but my ST noticed I was roleplaying more of a "Samurai" and not Humanity as it should be. We talked and checked the books. I was role playing the Path of Honorable Accord without even realizing it. So we just swapped it in. It made sense. Later through the game I ended up getting one dot in Vicissitude through plot device/magic item. However the ST was running it like the Parasite from the Umbra. After learning up to Bonecrafting I started weaking, my aura and flesh became even paler, and I was losing 2 blood a day instead of one. I had to seek out a Mage and bargain for him to get the parasite out of me. After it was finally over I was frustrated OOC because I really wanted Horrid Form.

                                I never planned on getting Vicissitude, the ST did it to me. But it changed my goals OOC. We talked for a while and I eventually brought up creating my own Discipline and Bloodline that would have one power that was similar to Horrid Form mechanically. But the how? The city has a City Mother spirit that is on the level of an Incarna. And one of her spirits (an information spirit) has access to incredibly powerful items, many cursed, that it sometimes will sell to people. Often times whether it will sell the item at all is more about the person it's selling it to. Very long story short after much debate, searching, researching, and burning my Resources down for the story (had 5 Resources) for $500K I was able to purchase a book from him that has lost secrets and could teach me how to create a new Discipline and Bloodline. But there's still more hitches.

                                So the further hurdels? As a running gag, my character has only 1 Int and we have locked it in place there. He allowed me to purchase a merit called "Flashes of Brilliance" (made up) to explain Aizen's sometimes amazing ideas. So now I had to buy up the Research skill and it's my only 5 dot skill. My combat skill is at 4 while everything else is 3 or less. And the book was also written in Latin so I had to buy the language as well. And after all of this and some downtime I will get my new Discipline. And I've already traded Boons for the right to create to childer and have the ghouls ready.

                                At the start of the game Aizen was just a Toreador with a miffed sire and a laughable art in the Toreador's eyes. In the end he's become a snowflake and the Progenitor of a Bloodline all because of how he ended up reacting and dealing with Plot as it hit him in the face.


                                Berny, aka. "Count Morgula" - Child of Osiris

                                At the start of the new game Rob wanted to play something unlike anything he'd ever played before. He also is a bit of a power gamer and wanted as many In Clan Disc as possible. We house ruled that without a teacher all Disc were restricted. You had to pay a Major Boon to learn 1-3 dots for vampire and a Life Boon to learn the 4-5 dots for anything out of clan. So he decided on a Child of Osiris so he'd have the 3 Clan Disc plus Bardo. Then he bought Extra Disc (5) merit and for a gag made his original clan Serpent of Light. The Setites in our city are a major villain group and the ST pulls from the lore in Mummy when setting them up. So Berny was grabbed by the Setites but he thought they were bat shit crazy and the devil incarnate. He went as far in the opposite direction from them as possible. It was an interesting gag that also allowed him to power game to get 5 In Clan to start while we all only had 3. We were OK with it. He also took Phobia: Snakes,heh.

                                He started off with 8 Humanity and was role playing it hard being super humane at all times and doing the right thing. It was surprising and out of character for him in real life. But we figured out that he actually really got into the character concept and that his vampire loved humans. He started a late night TV show called "Count Morgula Presents" which is a spoof off of a real life TV that was her in New Orleans called Morgus Presents (kind of like Elvira) to also be closer to them and make their days better by sharing entertainment with them.

                                As time went on our characters starting finding unique niches and roles growing into the upper echelon of the power structures in the city. However Berny was not. He wasn't pursuing any major personal goals or even leaning towards an obvious plot line. As I mentioned before the City Mother spirit for the city is on the power level of an Incarna. She's the main Protagonist for the city as the PC's strive to help save the city from the encroaching darkness as Formori, Demons, Sabbat, Specters,etc... try to warp and devour it. The ST took an interesting turn and made her take a personal interest in Berny and has even visited him to just talk to him. His care for the humans in her city, his humane nature, his endeavors to do things to provide jobs and entertainment, and his efforts in stopping the Setites and the Sabbat (we're in a Sabbat War atm) made him stand out. And now he has a personal relationship with an Incarna that has a few of the powerful mages and garou in the city very jealous.

                                At the start of the game Berny was just a rules raped character for extra Disciplines and a gag for humor value. In the end he's become a snowflake with one of the most powerful spirit connections any Garou or Mage player would kill for.


                                Trevor - Tremere

                                When the new campaign rolled out Doug was disappointed we were not doing a Zoo game because he plays Mages exclusively. So he picked Tremere of course. He's always run damage control in our games and role played good guys so he thought he'd go dark. He made an ex-Nazi who was there for WW2 as one of the doctors with a Humanity of 6. The one thing that set him apart is that the ST allowed him to take Necromancy. Wraiths are a MASSIVE part of the city and it was more to help out the ST by having someone capable of interacting with them rather than make something up later. But beyond that he was a standard Pyramid climber.

                                Time went on and he got obviously frustrated playing a vampire. He also was not enjoying being a darker more evil character. He likes being the good guy. As I mentioned before about the information spirit working for the City Mother, when I was (as Aizen) dealing with him for the book I investigated many of the other options and alternatives he had. It turned out that he supposedly could make someone become a Mage, a Changeling, or a Garou. Obviously all conversation stopped at that point. This was something our ST had never put in the game in over 20 years of running WoD. This spirit called Wyrdly doesn't sell everything he has to everyone. He picks and chooses. And what we found out was that this was something the forces loyal to City Mother had been working on with her direct investment for who knows how many centuries.

                                There were three items and they were one shot. Once used they would change you into one of the three. One specific item for just one creature type. The catch wasn't money or the like. Your Avatar would be permanently bonded to City Mother. You would always be in her service, you could never leave the city, and you'd always be reborn/reincarnated there in her service. Doug was willing to take the Flaws and plot hooks to get to be a Mage and also for just the cool points of being a Tremere that got their Avatar back. He agrees, uses the glowing blue crystal shard, and there is a very Highlander Quickening like effect.

                                The session ends and Doug says he's going to retire the character since it's a vampire game and the ST tells him no. Play it through. So the next session picks up there and I go and check his pulse. He's still a vampire. He doesn't seem remotely different. The ST opted to use this as the spark, or catalyst, for him becoming mortal again and regaining his Avatar. But he's still going to go through the process of learning and changing, exploring what has been done to him. Almost like a real life year long quest for Golconda that you hear about in other players' games. The ST didn't cheese it but made him an incredibly cool snowflake as he starts to learn what he is becoming. And he didn't make him a vampire mage which would have been stupid.

                                At this point he reveals in game that he was never a Nazi. He was a Jew who had infiltrated their ranks in order to hunt Nazi's down. It was obvious that this was him finding a way to not be an evil bastage, but it didn't upset any of us. We let it go through as an under the table retcon and it added additional role playing elements making Trevor more interesting as a person.

                                At the start of the game Trevor was vanilla with one ST trick so he could communicate with Wraiths. In the end he's the classic tale of a vampire seeking to regain mortality and their Avatar, but somehow actually succeeding.


                                Every one of our players shines in the spotlight in certain arenas and adds new dynamics to the game. No one detracts from it or overpowers the campaign by derailing anything. It's not the style everyone would like, I know that. But after gaming for over 25 years now the vanilla stuff is a bit boring to me. And the other guys have all been gaming for 30+ years at this point.
