        A great deal of fun may be had with villains. Off the top of my head:

        Wyrm Cults- Just as there are Totems allied to the Fianna and the fae, so are there dark totems who serve the Elder Dark. And such entities have their followers among twisted mortals, shapeshifters and dark fae (see below). A cult dedicated to G'lough (aka Shub Niggurath), Fachan (an elder Redcap god) or even Qyrl (aka Cthulhu) are all possible. Most of these cults could just be mortal creeps without powers or enchanted by a fae leader, mortal sorcerer or Black Spiral Dancer. Fomori may result from such cults.

        Night Hags- Called Nocturnae by the Garou, these awful Thallain are particularly nasty opponents. These evil fae are all about tormenting dreams and sucking the life out of their prey through dreams and night visits. And if the hag is experienced, they might be quite powerful. A whole chronicle might revolve around a Hag or group of them and chimera servitors preying on children, etc.

        Autumn People- Banal people looking to "clean up" the area by destroying a place of natural or artistic splendor. This will destroy a place of mystical significance and chase away any Glamour. The local fae and shapechangers must work together. Autumn people are VERY banal, and difficult for the fae to deal with directly, but going in there are ripping them to shreds is not a good idea either. So some finesse is needed.

        Banes, etc- Many banes overlap with Nightmare chimera, but are not always the same thing. They feed on negative emotions, negative fantasies and such, and so the difference could be splitting hairs. In my games I treat many Banes as corrupted Chimera, gaining a boost from linking with the Wyrm, but becoming increasingly foul because of it.

                                •Totem & Dreaming Synergy
                                -Yeah, I lean towards something along those lines myself. Something between the concept of a sort of Ur-Dream akin to the Proto-Indo European culture/mythology/language that some academics spend their careeets trying to reconstruct and a sort of 'white light through a prism' situation where these all originate from the same central cosmic Concept but have been refracted into several different wavelengths. (Also, thank ya Myth Advocate, your posts are cool as well!)

                                •Trods & Dreaming Roads
                                -I definitely agree that what many garou describe as umbral trods, particularly pocket realms tied to Dreaming individuals, are likely analogous to the various roads that changelings use to maneuver. To expand on tries though, some potentially interesting things:

                                --Trods each have their own unique methods of access. While the vast majority of freeholds contain trods that access the Near Dreaming and trods close to The Waking World (mundane reality) have less extreme codes, all trods have a code. The details are sky as the limit; some simply require an introductory poem to be recited, others only allow you to continue by walking back, others will have you accosted by riddling chimera; no trod is alike and refusal to play by The Dreaming's rules is a fast lane to an... interesting life.
                                --Trods entering the Far Dreaming tend to be even more difficult, requiring traveler's to observe ritualistic conditions and submit themselves to the esoteric logic of dreams. Access to the Deep Dreaming is even more difficult, often requiring either truly prodigious circumstances or an extreme sacrifice such as a significant treasure or a great tribute of glamour.
                                --Trods often simply appear as narratively appropriate (both in and out of universe) although the rise of banality weakens them and many were lost in great cataclysms. Other ways to access these roads are through the use of considerable Wayfarer, the services a proficient dream-sorcerer (Oneiromancy) capable of physically traveling via dreams, or having the sheer luck (or lack there of...) to randomly chance upon a Wild Portal. Along with all of this, one should note that trods are invaluable military assets within the Dreaming and treated as such by changelings and chimera, although fae military procedure is sometimes rather eccentric when compared to The Nation.
                                --There are also Three Roads specifically which are invaluable to safely travel the Dreaming. The Silver Road was established by the Tuatha and is of Dreams, with those of Nightmare pained by contact with it due to the ancient Silver Ban. The Paths of Balor are the elder equivalent of the Silver Road claimed by the children of the Elder Dark and with Paths reaching throughout the Dreaming, Shadowlands, and Autumn World. The Twilight Trods are fiercely contested because they can be used by either side, being largely rebuilt Post-Shattering.

                                •Nightmare & The Elder Dark
                                -Thallain, Nightmare Chimera, and other Children of the Elder Dark are those glamorous beings who declare fealty to the primordial terrors of the world's beginning, the Nightmare and Elder Dark. They feed from the worst aspects of human passion and their nightmares; ranging from reaping sorrow or provoking bloodshed to feed upon terror, hatred, and desperation. They are, by their nature, inherently opposed to the Children of the Dreaming. The two factions have fought since humanity achieved higher cognition, or even earlier. They were held distant by The Silver Ban for a long time but the Evanescence in the late 90s and early 2000s saw a massive influx of Nightmare glamour which saw a great surge in thallain numbers. They dream of returning humanity to its base and primordial state, welcoming the Long Winter as that promised return and the death of Dreams. They are, by and large, almost certainly tied to the Wyrm, particularly thallain who are the debased little siblings of the Dark Kin.

                                •Agents of Autumn
                                -Autumn People & Dauntain probably would register highly as normal people, being difficult to detect unless one is closely tied to glamour, but those with high gnosis or Sense Weaver would almost certainly sense such individuals. They represent the agents of Banality, the slow and crushing destruction of Dreams under the weight of sheer and unrelenting apathy, disappointment, and emotional numbing. They are arguably an even greater threat to the Fae of the Dreaming then the thallain, particularly since they are far more insidious in human society. Autumn People aren't cackling satanists or sadists. They're bitter school intendents who cut funding to the arts and aloof passerby who make a point of tipping the street busker but only to condescendingly tell him to 'get a real job'.

                                    Another question for the setting. What should Garou look like when they are in the Dreaming? Should they look as they normally do in whatever form they are in? Or should they look subtly changed so that it is obvious they are werewolves? For example, maybe in homid form they look hairier than normal with unkempt hair, more pronounced fangs, and perhaps even a change in eye color? And in lupus they'd appear bulkier as their mouths slaver, and their eyes have a demonic look to them? Something that obvious marks them as a Prodigal rather than an Enchanted human or trespassing Mage?


                                    I've been thinking on my own of the kinds of monsters to include for Garou to deal with. I don't want Garou packs of Stag to be seen as someone who deals with "ordinary" threats to the Fae, though they still could in any given situation. I want to keep the idea that they're a kind of military reserve to be called out when things get really bad or out of the ordinary. So I was thinking a good idea may be when the Fae are fighting something "not native to Earth's Dreaming" but something that wandered out of Lovecraft's Dreamlands or Dunsany's Pegana. It's fairy-like, but not a creature you'd expect the read about in a fairy tale.

                                    Examples would be creatures like Nightgaunts, Moonbeasts, Gugs, and Serpent Men although I'd need to file all the serial #s off and reimagine them in way that better fits the cosmology of the World of Darkness. Nightgaunts are easy because I can simply have them be heralds, messengers, and agents of the Elder Dark. The representatives they send to the Shadow Court and Thallain. In Lovecraft's Mythos, Serpent Men in the Dreamlands were once creatures of the real world who moved there through psychic or sorcerous powers to escape their race's decline on Earth. I usually use the Vhujunka as my serpent men analogue in Werewolf games, so I could do the same here. Despite including one Vhujunka in most of my chronicles as a sandbox element, I've never yet had PCs run into them. So this actually may be a better opportunity to do so.

                                    I have to do a lot more thinking on Moonbeasts though. They're actually one of the most malevolent races in the Dreamlands and sufficiently alien to make them interesting antagonists in the Dreaming. But I don't think I can tie them to the moon. Not only would that not make sense from a Garou perspective since Luna is an important patron of them, the moon's correspondence to Arcadia in Changeling also makes it bad fit. I think I need some other kind of celestial correspondence. Perhaps they are like an "Eclipse Beast"? Moonbeasts are sadists and slavers, and have agents among the Men from Leng. So they too would make great behind-the-scenes masterminds using secret agents among the Shadow Court.

                                            Aye, moonbeasts and alien dreams make great Changeling-style antagonists. The Dreaming is every dream and so it makes sense that the eldritch horrors that are dreamed by things we don't understand would be horrors there.

                                            As far as the chimerical aspect of werewolves, there will definitely be a great deal of diversity. In a way, I'd say that Breed-Auspice-Tribe can all serve as ways to provide unique factors to them alongside universal features akin to the Tells of Wolf-Blooded in WtF.

                                            I'd also say that the level of the Dreaming is important to recall. The Near Dreaming is similar to The Waking World, except that the mien gains prominence and stands upfront in reality. The Far Dreaming intensifies things as archetypes gain more and more potency. The Deep Dreaming is a realm defined by Archetype and Primal Dream, where Jumgian Archetypes, Mythic Legends, and The Heroes Journey reside as measurable foundations of reality.

                                            For example two random garou in the Near Dreaming might manifest greater body hair, bestial eyes, or attract animal followers. Similarly homids might see their fingers change sizes or lupus might be hulking big bad wolves or metis might be slick with birth blood and placenta. A Black Fury might find themselves clad in laurels or that chimerical steeds make a point of approaching them while a Fianna might find their voice gaining an ethereal quality and that the landscape itself wants to dance and sing with them.

                                            The same characters in the Far Dreaming might find the landscape shifting to resemble a graecian temple or hibernian glade. Ancestral relics akin to voile might manifest or the garou feel primordial instincts rising to the fore. Ragabash might literally vanish from sight while stepping into shadows or Ahroun might shine with a penumbra of moonlight.

                                            With The Dreaming, imagination and intent are really the only mitigating factors.

                                                    Heck the Dark Side of the Moon makes sense to me, that sounds pretty solid and interesting. Along with that might be things from beyond the stars or below in sea or stone where light never touches.

                                                    •Aliens & Fae
                                                    -Oh yeah definitely. Dreams are eternal but their images can take on plenty of different forms. Plus extraterrestrials have managed to insert a strong significance into certain sorts of dreams such as fear and fascination with the unknown so the idea of flying disks or chimerical grays wandering mysterious stretches of chimerical reality is perfectly plausible. Plus plenty of arts and enchanting 'abduction' can have solid analogues.

                                                    •Totems in the Dreaming
                                                    -I'm... genuinely uncertain about this. Because I can see the logic and get the use of having a pack totem join the pack entering the Dreaming. I guess my problem is, I think that the Dreaming and Umbra are on different wavelengths and for spirits of one to pass into the others... if it's possible, I don't think it should be an easy undertaking. Spirits are manifestations of that realm's foundational energies come to life and while the human world is a universal constant, realms built upon completely distinct energies can't be easy on outsiders.

                                                    Personally, I'd say that for totems such as Stag that do have ties to fae elements, I'd say that they probably have contacts and allies bound by old oaths in the Dreaming who could serve as guides or mentors to their packs, although they probably also have rivals and sworn enemies. Stag for instance might be allied with The Horned Lord, The White Hart and able to secure numerous chimerical deer and hunters to help their packs. Yet they might also constantly draw the ire of cruel hunters and ravenous nightmares, such as rabid bears or sadistic trappers or even simple grass-lings (tiny people) that begrudge Stag's proud hooves trodfing through their villages.

                                                    As for some of your other questions: I'd say that a totem does probably have some of its incarna's knowledge with its totem value and the points into it expanding its knowledge but suffer from some key misconceptions simply due to the difference in nature of umbral spirits and dreamt chimera and their minds. They likely recall past experiences and know basics such as the names of allies and enemies as well as how to pass through certain ways and the capabilities of certain chimera, but the intent and reasons of much of the Dreaming probably baffles them. Akin to that, aspects of the Dreaming can only interact with the spirit as far as they can understand and interpret it. Chimerical deer might mostly understand but not get why Stag allows its children to be hunted and killed. Alternatively chimerical hunters might happily hunt any Stag, regardless of its significance. Or as well a daydream might simply not understand in the slightest what any particular spirit is trying to say at all.

                                                    Most chimera and changelings probably don't have spirit speech or an equivalent. They have no reason to. Spirits are animist reflections of reality. Chimera are human dreams inspired by reality, sometimes very loosely. They interact on different levels. They might be able to comprehend each other's existence but it's... no sure thing.
