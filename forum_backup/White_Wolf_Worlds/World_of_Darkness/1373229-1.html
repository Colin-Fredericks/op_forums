    Originally posted by Aleph View Post

    That's what Vampire ST handbook says - it's very much part of crossoverland.

    For Vampire, it makes a lota sense to have ArchMages with Spheres going to 6+ levels, as Vampire measures (and compares) powers in a scale from 1 to 10



    It depends on what do you want Mages to be able to do. While Mage does sell magic(k) as being able to do everything, it never says it needs to be easy.

    If you just want ArchMages to get more "bang" for their successes, that could easily be modeled by giving special powers to Arete at the 6+ levels. Like to say that Mages with Arete 6 duplicate their successes. At 7 you can break the rule of targets and affect huge areas for free...and so on. etc.

    Archspheres (and even in 1e) represent a "ceiling" to what ordinary mages can do in a very similar vein as how Disciplines at 6+ represent in Vampire powers beyond the access of "normal" pc, but that can be reached in the course of play if the PC becomes an Elder. It allows you to build in the system "Elder Mages" of sorts.
    If you want to do that, it's a very convenient tool to have. But I think it stands to debate if one really needs "Elder Mages".

    I much prefer archspheres stopping at 6 actually.


    Level 7+ archspheres are just ridiculous
    For starters many of their higher ranked effects are not really "stronger" than the weaker archspheres. They just exist for the sake of filling out rank x



    In my opinion Level 6(and remember that in Horizon the costed around 120 xp i think) should basically be able to do everything EXCEPT the literally godlike
    Creating an entire universe or an entirely new force, or complete immortality come to mind....
    This should remain the province of the Exemplars or something like that.
    (Not oracles, since they are not supposed to be obsessed with the archspheres)

                    I personally agree that the Archsphere system in the Horizon book is better than the one in Masters Of The Art. A single new level 6 that enhances and boosts the sphere's normal effects by a great deal.
                    It would need some subtle rewriting for things like the Life sphere, because in M20 it allows full Metamorphosis on others at 5 already, and the Time sphere because M20 allows backward travel at 5 already as well - but for the most part the Horizon system just works, and it works fine in the way that it simply takes the previous sphere levels and turbocharges them by essentially allowing for the same extreme godlike feats, just with with less successes required.
                    It's simple, elegant, powerful, has a deserving cost, and just 'fits' into the normal sphere system without actually limiting the previous existing levels through its existence. Again, unlike the MOTA system.

                                            Traveling to an Everett Volume requires more magic(k) than just Time 5. You need Entropy 4 at least. More importantly, it causes less Paradox than using Time 5:
                                            Originally posted by VE
                                            Void Engineers might use them for a less risky form of time travel, albeit one with fewer practical benefits. Changing history in an Everett Volume doesn’t affect the main timeline, but it won’t invoke disastrous temporal paradoxes either
                                            Which strongly implies that *arch* Time Travel has *more practical benefits* changing history at the cost of epic Paradoxes

                                            That's not to say that the current timeline necessarily can be changed with Time 5 in M20.

                                            Since Revised Time 5 time travel kills you, stories were safe. No need to apply the same rules in 20th edition where this it's allowed for *regular* characters. Ultimately it depends on the ST.

                                            However...

                                            Consider a Mage that drops an anchor in their current time, travels back, does things, and travels forward again to the anchor - nothing will have changed! How strange!
