    How The Heck Does This Setting Hold Up?

    12-30-2017, 07:50 AM
    First of all, let me start by saying that I know almost nothing about classic Mage, aside from the fact the Technocracy rewrote reality so that mortals stopped believing in the supernatural. And this apparently had an impact on the way magic worked, and pretty much screwed the Fae over pretty hard.


    But what about the other splats? If mages and Changelings are having such a rough time of getting things to work for them, then why aren't Werewolves, Vampires, Ghosts and the like seeing a decline in their power, or even fading from existence? Tremere mages don't seem to have a problem, and even came up with a new type of magic when they lost access to their old ones.


    And what would happen if the Masquerade (I'm using that as a blanket term, by the way) were to be broken and existence of the supernatural was revealed to the world? Would the Technocracy's magic be undone by this revelation, or would it somehow find a way to reinstate itself, despite hard evidence to the contrary?

              There are some things that are part of the Tapestry intrinsically bound to the Rules of Reality, this is known primarily as Static Magic and is where Werewolves, Vampires, Sorceres and so forth occupy in the cosmology mythos of Mage the Ascension. The Consensus is a powerful thing dictating the flow of 'mundane reality' but there are still things that it doesn't get to out right change. Mages use True Magick however which instead of employing the little static laws that let other supernaturals do their thing they use secrets of Reality itself to reshape the tapestry to their whim, they literally remake the world as they see fit when they perform an act of magic, no matter how small. Changelings likewise occupy a special place as they do not remake reality so much as use the power of dreams given power and form and so suffer from the active disbelief in magic in that it makes Arts harder to perform. However because a Changeling isn't rewriting the Tapestry but actually sidestepping it whatever they do doesn't have consequences for the caster like it may for a Mage as the tapestry likes being a certain way and the snap back of meddling with it is known as Paradox which any Mage may suffer.

                              Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                              First of all, let me start by saying that I know almost nothing about classic Mage, aside from the fact the Technocracy rewrote reality so that mortals stopped believing in the supernatural.
                              OK, I'm going to go back to address this, because it's incorrect and that's a big disconnect here.

                              The Technocracy didn't "rewrite" anything. The Order of Reason (the precursor to the Technocracy, as the Technocratic Union is actually under 200 years old) made massive strides in understanding the way the world really worked (that is, reality is subjective and dominant 'truths' are based on observer consensus), and then set out to make the world work better for humanity by getting humans to see the world in a fashion that benefits them. The OoR's tools were rational thought, science, philosophy, and education, which took away special knowledge from those hording it. You don't need a priest to cast out a demon, you need a doctor to heal your sickness, and anyone could study medicine without needing to jump through a faith's loops.

                              Of course, mages are humans in many ways, and the altruistic intentions quickly bent from empowering human creativity to control. The OoR realized that they could eventually be superceded by the masses as they used the masses to push back at other mages (and supernaturals). So the OoR's more ambitious and power hungry members started taking control from the idealists (with multiple purges). Knowing the reality is ultimately subjective, the leaders of the OoR subverted their initial goals. They wrote rules for reality, and used their positions of influence over Sleeper thinkers to get them to "discover" those rules as parts of science and rational thought. As that made their Sleeper followers more technologically power, it also made the belief in what the OoR wanted to be truth stronger.

                              At first, this actually 'broke' reality (this would be the Sorcerer's Crusade era) into major Reality Zones. What was true varied wildly based on location. The OoR pushed for expansion and control (influencing the Age of Exploration and the expansion of European colonialism) to unify these Zones, and large succeeded over a very long time. Eventually, during the Victorian era, is when they reorganized as the Technocracy with the backers of the British Empire seeing the global unity possible as they abandoned the last vestiges of the OoR's original mission.

                              So, the Union never rewrote reality. They wrote their own rules for reality, and got people to believe in them. One of the Union's biggest problems though, is that humans are very capable of holding contradictory beliefs. Simply giving them new things to believe didn't get them to stop having old beliefs; even if those old beliefs aren't to any obvious benefit. The modern Consensus reality they created is imperfect because despite all their efforts to address this, it remains the case. The old realities still exist in that Consensus. Making one reality the strongest, didn't make it the only one.

                                  A lot of what keeps supernatural stuff running the World of Darkness, despite the Consensus, is Inertia. The Consensus allows hedge wizards to use Sorcery Paths, even according to ancient ways, because the Consensus has always allowed them. It's deeply ingrained in the fabric of human belief, so an individual can still use them. The Mythic Threads exist because, even if people wouldn't seriously say that magic or ghosts or monsters are real, those things still have a hold on our collective imaginations. A part of humans has always maintained, "Magic and monsters aren't real...but....what if...?"

                                  Even if we assume the Consensus doesn't support certain things, there are explanations given in the Mage books (like the Revised Storyteller's Handbook and others) for why vampires and werewolves et al are still around. For Vampires, mages assume that they WOULD be too Vulgar to exist...which is why they have to drink blood. Blood is the Life. It is the currency of the soul. And it carries mystic power. Specifically, vampires are able to derive Quintessence from blood - siphoning the energy that underpins a creature's existence to prop up its own. In the same way that other Bygones feed on Quintessence to remain in the mortal realm - like by sitting around a Node or entering into a pact with a mage as a Familiar - so too do vampires have their own form of Thaumivore lifestyle.

                                  Moreover, again, vampires are old beings. Their curse is ancient, and allegedly placed by God. If anyone's Will outranks that of the Consensus, it would be the Most High. Vampires continue to exist because a power higher than mere human belief proclaims as much.

                                  Which brings us to Werewolves, who are even more secure in their existence. The Fera have supposedly been around for millions of years. Additionally, shapeshifters aren't just biological beings. They are half-spirit. And one of the consistent details in the Mage cosmology is that spirits are unaffected by the whims of the Consensus. They just ARE. Spirit Charms never suffer Paradox, and may enter the material world and manifest physically at their choice, so long as they are individually powerful enough to do so. This is probably because spirits are the mechanisms behind Reality, and are inextricably tied to it. Shifters, being half spirit, share this innate tie to the essential flow of existence, and are therefore afforded broad tolerance by the Consensus in what they can do. Hell, the one way they seem to be at odds with human belief - the Delirium that panics and memory-alters people who see shifters in their full supernatural glory - is explicitly stated in Werewolf as having more to do with Humanity's deep, racial fear of shifters, and not Paradox.

                                                          It's worth noting that the Consensus was changing well before even the Craftmasons, the group of Enlightened Philosopher-scientists that were the major force behind the creation of the Order of Reason. The Fae have already been mentioned but there were also fall of House Tremere into Clan Tremere, specifically the reason the Tremere turned themselves into vampires. They found their Magic becoming harder to preform and feared the loss of their powers and Magic-based immortality/longevity, they didn't understand that the Consensus was changing. So they eventually hit on the idea of becoming vampires to preserve what they could.

                                                          To throw all the changing of the Consensus at the hands of the Order of Reason and Technocracy is an oversimplification of things. Certainly, they ensured it would go down the path it did and likely sped it up but it was still changing regardless.

                                                          And it's also worth noting that even despite everything that's happened to the world and the Consensus, it's not all doom and gloom for everyone that's not a Technocrat. Or that the Union or Science itself is some Wonder crushing, Banality generating monstrosity that will stamp out creativity and Wonder. Indeed, the Changelings, one of groups hit the hardest by the growth of Science received a large boost from the Space Race and Moon Landings to the point that group of Fae that had long abandoned Earth, returned. All because of sheer Wonder produced by the Masses looking up into space and dreaming of going there.

                                                          Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. They're indomitable. Indomitable.
