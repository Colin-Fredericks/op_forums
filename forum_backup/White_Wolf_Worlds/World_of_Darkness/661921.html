                  For crossover games I would let vampires play ghoul characters during the day. That allows the other players to do things during the day without the vampire player having to sit around and twiddle his thumbs. If I were running V:tM with W:tA I would give vampires a bit of a physical boost so that if a fight were to occur, they could participate without being quickly destroyed or being a useless. Letting them soak aggravated damage with Stamina would be one such boost.

                  I wouldn't address the topic of who is right or wrong in any cosmological sense. The is no need, the characters don't have access to the answers to big questions like that, the players don't need them either.

                      My favourite is to simply NOT do it... and while that's more me being cheeky than helpful, it has some merit in regards to classic World of Darkness. By that I mean the various 'splats' are explicitly written to be soley populated by that 'splat'. This means only that crossover is a decent bit of headache for a Storyteller, but it can be quite worth the effort!

                      The 'rule' I lean on most is to have fun, and I apply that to ALL members of the troupe (including the Storyteller!). Sometimes following that rule means throwing out the rules-as-written, but I find that it USUALLY expresses more as finding various forms of flexibility over slavish devotion to any 'canon' from a book. As Sergeant Brother suggests – and I outright state – the rules may need a tweak (if not an entire reinterpretation) to make any crossover work. Let's face it, a player character ghost is weak in our world when compared to a vampire, but even a puissant Kindred such as Augustus Giovanni can be smashed by the forces of the Underworld if he's not careful!

                      The fact of the matter is that the Storyteller needs to know what the players want, weigh that against what the Storyteller can do, and that's where negotiation comes in. Does the player want a mummy to hang out with vampires 'coz there's a great story to be told? Does the Storyteller think it can be done with a Setite instead? Why does it NEED to be one of the Amenti, anyway? It's a lot more nuanced than simple 'power gamer' versus the rest of us in most cases, but I know those things happen. Toe to toe, a recently awakened mage is no match for a Garou right after her first change, and while that's not the point of MY troupe's games, such 'white room combat' IS fun from time to time. The player who joined our Vampire: the Masquerade chronicle playing a Bastet pawn shoppe owner had legitimate reasons for wanting that character, and after some work, we made it happen without too much trouble. We DID work, and that made all the difference.

                              Since some time ago, my PnP Chronicles seem to be almost complete crossover.

                              It all began, when I wanted to play a mere mortal, and tried to look up the ruleset for them. Since the mortal-rules were spread over several books (including every core-book), I started writing them together, innocently beliving, this would take a few hours at most.
                              Since I was already going through each core book, I started adding the rules for Guhls, Kinfolk, Hedge-Magicians and those near mortals as well.
                              Let me just say it spiraled out of control since there, and I`ve been writing on this "book" for the better part of 6 years now.

                              I asked some of my friends, if they would meet with me for ocasional one-shots, trying out the rule-tweaks I`ve written together and testing how everything worked together.
                              It led to some of the most awsome Hunter-Chronicles we`d ever played together and some of them still go on to this day.

                              Once the rules for each "Sub-Species" (Guhles, Kinfolk etc.) including their potential magical abilities were finished, it was litterally a few hours of extra work to expand it into the full blown respective splatts.
                              Now we play even fully awakend Groups with this crossover rule-book, and so far everything works out quite well.

                              A month ago we started a Chronicle with an Imbuned Huntress, a Kami (think a Gaian-Fomori), a Changeling and an awakend Mage in brazil, with Pentex and a couple of Black Spiral Dancers as one of the main antagonists (they try to remove the village of the changeling and the kami to make way for an highway into the amazonas, and the huntress and the mage do have an old score to settle with the alpha Spiral, who is leading them).
                              I know it sounds like a mess, but it works surprisingly well. Combat is hardly the focus of the chronicle till now, and the different splats are working off each other quite well.

                              Going over every House-Rule or outright Rule-Change of this Crossover-Houserule-Book would take ages, but I am more than willing, on sending them per E-Mail, if there is interest for it.
                              One word of caution though, its all written in german, so take that into acount.

                                  For the purposes of cross-overs, I tend to favor certain cross-overs more than others (a Mage can fit into any game--Vampires, not so much). However, for the purposes of hacking together a system, I lean very heavily on the Mage Storyteller's Handbook (Rev.), because it goes through great pains to establish a robust cross-over system in one of the appendices. And while it's far from perfect, it's an incredibly helpful and useful starting point. I would also like to add that I go to this book even when I'm not dealing with mages at all--this is because, absent two systems that plainly interact with one another seamlessly (e.g., the interaction between Auspex 1 Heightened Senses, and the Rank 1 Ragabash gift Blur of the Milky Eye comes to mind as an example of two disparate systems that perfectly work against one another in a well-defined tug-of-war), I use the crossover rules in the aforesaid Mage book as a sort of Rosetta stone.

                                          Well, Asmodai, I think that as a starting point he's right. They're crappy rules, but the only ones available from an official POV.

                                          The you get this starting point and go beyond, to something that really can almost work.

                                          My main concern in Crossovers, though, are the basic traits on the sheet that aren't consistent from a gameline to the next. Glamour/Banality is a tough one, but at least can be figured out without affecting the other things in any given character sheet. Faith Potential have some clear rules about how it works, but they are poor rules. A Demon in a Mage game will need to find creative ways to waist the helluva lot of Faith she will get from the Mages if they do the Pact... and they are simply worth enough thralls for the Ravaging being almost a non-issue.

                                          Virtues are worst, since when they show up they DO override some basic rules, the ones governing Willpower above all. And Demon using Virtues in a way while Vampire does another didn't helped it. My personal solution until now was to make any mortal follow Hunters Hunted rules as default, and have the splats using their own rules, but it IS suboptimal. I need a way to get Vampires and Demons on the same level as everyone else or give Virtues to everyone... and then I'll have to figure out what to do with Willpower inconsistencies.

                                          Abilities are the worst issue by far. They are more basic, and so they are more pervasive, but for the hell of it, they got more inconsistencies than any other trait. Some are completely redundant or simply too narrow, whyle others are very important except for not being on every line. Not to talk about the ones that are only ever rolled because there are powers based on them.

                                          I'm trying hard now to work around it. For now I have done with some friends a new list of 30 habilities. It is a start, but far from what I want for crossovers:

                                                          I did a bunch of things, borrowing from NWoD copiously, both mechanically and setting wise. The primary thing was to use the mechanics in Kindred Of The East, since it is by far the most crossover friendly of the CWoD which is most crossover unfriendly. I also used the houserules on The Howling Void blog, by poster I Hate All Life(Hi Steven, your the man)

                                                          Everyone has a Hun, Po, Yin and Yang stat or equivalent. This means splats without these stats must get them assigned.

                                                          All of the above stats are either made equivalent with an existing stat, if no stat is suitable they must be assigned such a stat. Shifters have given me the most trouble here as well as changelings

                                                          Every splat must have a power stat, or have one assigned, Generation = Dharma = Rankx2 =Faith etc. Shifters give me trouble again as well as changelings.

                                                          Every splat must be given the ability to spontaneously cast minor effects of their disciplines/lores etc at an increased cost. Pick two attributes, subtract the rating of the effect, and pay power pts equal to the effect. Shifters are again an issue here, till my player suggested that totems, those spirits who teach specific gifts, could be assigned portfolios and ratings therein to get such spont abilities, i agreed to it, and he's doing all the homework so yay.

                                                          Every power must be resistible if cast on another splat. Characters can either use an existing power to defend, or make a Power Block. by rolling their power stat equivalent in extremis.

                                                          Mages get two successes for every one a non mage gets in an effect roll or defense, but this evens out since Non mages can have larger dice pools to counter.

                                                          Extra Damage Successes from an attack roll is capped at stat or 10, which ever is lower.

                                                          Theres a bunch of other stuff too but that the biggest part i think.
