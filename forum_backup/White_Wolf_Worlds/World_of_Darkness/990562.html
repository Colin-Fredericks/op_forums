    Dealing with PC's going too OP

    10-20-2016, 03:41 PM
    So, i got a rank 3 get of fenris. she is pretty buffed up right now. I throw her enemies but she keeps going higher and stronger (dealing massive ammounts of damage and soaking A LOT). which made me think..
    how do you guys deal with PC's going too high caliber? how do you make threats that they can't fight against or hold them against the ropes?

      An Adren combat focused Garou should should one of the most dangerous things walking. Also, if that's what your player made talk to them a bit. Do they want to have fights that are more dangerous/harder, or do they like some threat but like being the furry blender of doom?

      As for ways to make combat more challenging:

      1) Have fights in places where the Veil matters. This is probably one of the biggest things that can tamp down on Garou in fights. Even if they know and have plans, it's a giant complication to have to deal with.

      2) Numbers, numbers, numbers. It can be a pain as a ST, but swarms are inherently hard on PCs in the WoD system. Defense is hard to maintain, and random Soak means eventually damage gets through. Eventually a big enough group will wear down any Garou.

      3) Multi-stage fights. Even if it's just "one scene" for specific powers/Gifts, having to deal with waves without time to relax/recover resources is similar to the swarm issue. It creates a tactical choice of having to take more time on weaker enemies with the risk of them getting a lucky hit but saving up your Rage and Willpower for later, or tearing through the mooks and hopping you have enough left for the bigger fight.

      4) Combat threats that require lateral tactics. Make enemies that you don't take out with damage, but through some unique weakness you have to exploit. While a bit easy for a Garou, something like the Golem where you have to damage the inscription that gave it life or it doesn't stop. Give them something that the Get's combat abilities means she's helping by keeping it busy, while the rest of the pack is trying to hit the "off switch" instead.

          Non-combat situations force combat-focused characters to depend on their weaknesses. One scenario would be to have a few of her kinfolk get arrested by the FBI and require her to figure out how to get them out of an FBI building without backup. If she decides to use combat, let her suffer the consequences (while silver weapons are rare, I am sure that the FBI suspects enough about the supernatural world to make sure that there are a couple of specially marked automatic shotguns loaded with silver slugs for security to use against atypical threats). Otherwise, she will have to depend on her weaknesses, rather than her strengths to get through the scenario, which will allow her to grow as a character (which would also earn her a point or two of Wisdom during the next moot).

                  The big thing to be careful about with increasing the non-combat challenges, is that it can annoy the player because they made a warrior that focuses on fighting and now they don't get to do that. There's nothing wrong that having situations were non-combat options are vastly superior choices, or the only acceptable choice, but too much of it and you lose the combat focused characters.

                  In WtA this can be even more problematic because Glory Renown without combat is a minor trickle, and it puts any Glory reliant Auspice in the weeds for advancing in Rank.

                                  All the advice about non-combat encounters is pretty great, but lets be honest, Heavy Arms is right about a Get wanting to get their claws wet.

                                  Something tells me your combats involve Mockeries/Fomori, which exist to die to werewolves' claws and fangs. Black Spiral Dancers tend to get pureed when they face an experienced Get Ahroun too. Tearing apart mooks is all well and good, but challenging or even frightening opponents are necessary once in a while. Maybe the Wyrm's forces start to recognize that their plans are foiled by rampaging Garou, and so they unleash the big guns. This gives you as a GM to come up with some really spicy combat opportunities.

                                  If you are looking for imposing antagonists, I'd recommend spirits. The obvious ones are banes, although any of the Triat's servants can get uppity. Big spirits can have heaps of essence/power (whatever you want to call 'spirit hit points' depending on edition) and they get whatever crazy suite of charms you want them to have. They are also capable of infusing cherished mortal allies, twisting the environment, and corrupting/calcifying/mutating fetishes! Most also don't give a flying fuck about the Litany and, given enough power or the right charms, might just come crashing through the Gauntlet to cause a real mess.

                                  If you want to have the best of both worlds, have a powerful BSM perform the rite of the Flayed God on one of these beasts and then you can have a Dancer with charms (and I tend to give them extra health levels to represent all that delicious essence, but that's just me).

                                      I get that you're not advocating no combat, but ultimately the solution to "this character is so good at combat I'm struggling to make combat hard," is not, "less combat." That just makes the problem happen less, while also risking issues with both player fun in making a combat focused PC and potentially making a character that largely relies on combat for Renown/Rank.

                                      And while I don't disagree with how Renown should work, that's not the RAW and house rules on modifying Renown gains are an entirely different issue.

                                      Galliards and Ahrouns need a regular flow of Glory to progress. Combat is pretty much the only RAW way to get it, and combat also offers decent rewards in Honor and Wisdom; esp. for non-front-line characters who are more likely to be focused on those Renown types anyway. The only non-combat ways of getting Glory are solely in the realm of inter-Garou relations or Umbral quests, and those rewards aren't exactly a lot in comparison.

                                      I've been in multiple low-combat games, and those two Auspices regularly fell behind by 1-2 Ranks because of it. It's just the nature of WtA, and it's something that needs to be at least discussed with the group due to the potential impact on fun it can have if the change in combat frequency is sudden and/or ST driven.

                                      Garou already are pariahs to most of the population of the world. Their Rage means normal humans and animals shun them. This makes them insular and reliant on other Garou and kin to make up the majority of their social groups. And since they're a pre-historic warrior cult, violence and force are generally something that Garou are taught to embrace as positive traits within their isolated society. And the Garou have plenty of simple tools to keep brutish Garou in check in regards to their own social norms.

                                      One of the key elements of WtA and it's approach to consequences is that the Garou are exceedingly good at defeating immediate threats with the direct application of force and violence, but their main opponent can't really be beaten in that fashion. Garou struggle with the futility of violence in solving problems, but this is highlighted by making them really good at solving the simple problems in front of them with violence.

                                              Some ways to get around a garou combat monster
                                              • Guns, Lots of Guns: Garou do their deadliest damage when in close combat, try to keep that from happening as often. A number of enemies armed with guns, keeping separated will put the garou in less of a position to chew through them quickly.
                                              • Silver: Eventually the garou has to run into those who know their weakness, and many garou learn that the hard way when they are used to shrugging off bullets but find themselves with a gut full of silver shot.
                                              • Authorities (Police, FBI, etc): You did not mention where these fights were taking place, so I will assume outside of most mortal eyes. Try to set the fight in the city, and the garou have to worry about the police showing up (this is where the way humans process through delirium helps, since they dont see a werewolf but rather a gang member doped up on pcp assaulting people and could call the police). If your garou starts killing cops and those consequences are going to catch up quick, especially since the strange officer deaths are going to attract the attention of those 'in the know' (Pentex, FBI supernatural investigators, Hunters, Technocracy, etc).
                                              • Go after the Brain: If the garou soaks damage like a sponge, then go after the mind. Fomori powers like Mind Blast (which can immobilize a foe), Sanity Rending (which can inflict a mental disorder), Stasis Touch (prevents shapechanging). Vampiric powers like Dominate, and Dementation. Mages with the Mind Sphere. It doesn't matter how tough the body is if the garou in question is standing there drooling.
                                              • Go after the Family: Eventually chewing through enemies will lead to grudges, the bearers of the grudges are going to start investigating the garou. If they can trace connections such as kinfolk or other important points of interest and will begin moving against those.
                                              • Don't Fight: As mentioned above, while combat is important to WtA its important to stress the other aspects of garou responsibilities many of which require them to not be murder machines.
                                              • It's A Trap!: Lure the garou to a warehouse, foundry, or other structure that can be reinforced and sealed and light the place on fire. Not only does the garou have to worry about whatever foes are locked in there with them as well as any other nasty surprises, but also the ever present and growing danger of the flames and debris.

                                                  If you have an OP Garou, just take off the gloves and start throwing in threats that force them to avoid direct combat.
