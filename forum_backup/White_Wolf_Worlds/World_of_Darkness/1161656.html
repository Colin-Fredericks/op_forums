    How Many Origins Do The Baali Have?

    11-22-2017, 07:47 PM
    Hey everyone. I've a question for the experienced readers of masquerade books out there. How many origins of the Baali? I know they're supposed to mysterious and their origins are supposed to be unclear,so i ended up wondering how many versions of their story were published. I've bought Lore Of The Bloodlines,It stated the Progenitors of the bloodline were two brothers called Moloch and Nergal who become more powerful because they drank the blood of monsters created before humanity by god. I thought they were cool,so i bought Clanbook:Baali and it told a different story,about how three people were thrown into flesh pits and rose up as vampires.And the white wolf wiki states some suspect Saulot was the Baali progenitor,it was weird because i'm pretty sure saulot is a nice guy. Can anyone explain the Baali Origin story to me? Were those three separate origins,or were those just different viewpoints of the same event?

      Separate origins (with some similarities)
      • Clanbook Baali has Ashur (implied to be Saulot, but Cappadocius is also referred to as Ashur in other sources) finding the First Tribe, destroying them throwing the bodies into a pit and then dropping some blood into it. From this pit crawled the three Baali founders (Nergal, Moloch, the Unnamed).
      • Lore of the Bloodlines has the two brothers Moloch and Nergal.
      • DAV20 offers a few rumors of their creation - the Tzimisce due to Kupala and the Tremere say it was Saulot.
      • Dark Ages Companion has Shaitan (the Slave Boy origin) was embraced during the Second City and cast out and took twelve apostles from the other clans with him
      • V20 Black Hand Guide to the Tal'Mahe'Ra says that Ashur is the place of the First Tribe found the entity from which they gained their knowledge of demon power. An Antediluvian came and destroyed the city Nergal fled and Moloch took the other survivors and left.

      From these I figure the likeliest story is the following.

      One or more Antediluvians traveled from out of the Second City (either before or after its fall) and come upon Ashur the city of the First Tribe. This tribe had discovered a being (a Demon of some variety) from which they gained their infernal knowledge and the use of the Well of Sacrifice). The Antediluvian(s) came upon the tribe and were disgusted (either by the acts or the naivety of these mortals to try to be wicked) and destroyed the tribe. They took the remains and hurled them into their pit of sacrifice and dripped blood into it. From this emerged two or more embraced vampires (at least one of the variations of Nergal and Moloch). They began a rivalry based on how they dealt with the demons their tribe had found (Nergal being very open and evil, Moloch preferring to keep them asleep).

              Another theory that surfaced with V20 Black Hand is that all the theories are true. The Baali have been created several time, each time by a different antediluvian; the common denominator in all of these origin would be at this point not the Antediluvian but the Well, the pit that birthed the Baali. A Well that might have been used more than once.

              By the way the well for sure was used at least a second time after the creation of the Bali: Ur-Shulgi origin is linked to the well, since Ur-Shulgi was a boy whose soul was destined to be tainted, and Haqim embraced him and tossed him in the well so that his new child couldunite the Baali under one banner and guide them on a open-field battle, so that the armies of the Second City could finally wipe out the infernalist that were lurking in the shadows at that time.After the battle Haqim took his childe and cleansed his soul, or so it is said.
              It is even hinted that Ur-Shulgi might be the third of the baali founders, the Unnamed himself.

                      To throw a wrinkle in there, the answer really depends on what era the various origins were written, and which team wrote them.

                      For instance, Clanbook Salubri from Vampire: The Dark Ages (the first edition) pushed the rumor that it was Saulot who Embraced the Baali. Clanbook Baali hinted at the Cappadocian Antediluvian, but also hinted heavily at [Tzimisce], by way of a scene that smacked of Vicissitude. The same writing team did Clanbook Tzimisce, which in turn introduced the idea that the so-called Baali Wars went beyond fighting said Clan, and that Samiel (the founder of the Salubri Warrior Caste) actually died fighting [Tzimisce] after his own infernal pacts were discovered. Finally, the Gehenna book introduced a number of different endings, but the "canon" one cast Saulot as the founder of the Baali. Why did he do this? A definitive answer is not (to my knowledge) given, and trying to figure out his motives is a topic in and of itself.
