    Verra : You seem to have a very, very, wrong idea about what the capabilities of other supernaturals are (Garou losing their intellect and becoming unable to use weapons being the most glaring example). That necessarily colours your ideas about them and what they might bring up on the table.

    So

    Originally posted by Ambrosia
    You really need to start reading more Mage source material. Seriously. Because you are coming into this from a very, very, very one-sided perspective that makes it glaringly obvious you haven't. Well, as the main question of this thread shows.
    That.

    We also have this Forum, where you can find countless of threads with "vs debates" . These can't really end with a conclusion because there are plenty of arguments either way, because (as Ambrosia says) the rules aren't very consistent, and because who wins in the end will often depend more of external factors than sheer power.
    These debates often devolve on a flame war where everyone starts arguing in favor of "their guys" while painting the others as sitting ducks (like you were doing), but reading them helps understanding what different creatures could potentially bring to the table, considering different scenarios.
    I've had debates in both sides: Sometimes arguing that mages aren't just prey to other Supernaturals , sometimes figthing to explain that NO, the Technocracy can't just detect and eradicate all the supernaturals whenever they want.

    Research. It can be pretty fun if you're interested in this topic.

    To pick your curiosity, and note that I'm not saying that this is a way of ending vampires in a total war, of course not. It's just an example of what, RAW mages can bring up on the table:

    It has been suggested that a ritual spell of Prime 2/Forces 3/Correspondence 3 (something a starting character can have) can be used to:
    a) Detect all vampires on a city because vitae it's magical and has a very specific Ressonance, thus Corr 3/Prime 1 can detect all traces of it in an area (let's cast at daytime to minimise the potential to miss obfuscated vampires).
    b) Automagically cause a unspecified, but big, amount of LEVELS (not dice) of aggravated damage (let's say, "just" 30 for the sake of argument) to ALL of them. Impossible to dodge AND nearly impossible to counter because innate counterspelling can't counter direct damage (and you would have to counter a LOT of successes)

    This could cleanse a city of vampires w/o giving a figthing chance. Only extremely powerful vampires could survive that. And *that* it's a relatively basic spell. A cabal of "Neonate" mages can potentially cast it (wouldn't be easy, though, and that's why it isn't done. It's hardly worth it). Masters (Elder mages) and above bring MUCH more power to the table.

    And magick it's infinitely versatile. Damage on a big area isn't the only thing they can bring on the table (supernatural perception it's, probably, the place where mages excel the most). On the realm of social misdirection, Vampires are very smug with Dominate but they often forget that they themselves can be mentally controled or subtly influenced by the powers of other supernaturals. Much like Garou haven't a monopoly on fighting (as noted), vampires haven't a monopoly on infiltration and subversion.

    So, all in all, can mages just go and eradicate all the vampires? It's my opinion that NO, most likely they can't (at least not w/o having won the Ascension War first. And for realsies, not like the Technocracy who declared they've "won", yet can't end climate change w/o being slapped by Paradox). My point it's merely that they're not so defenseless as you seem to believe. Most certainly not "just prey" for vampires (at least no more than vampires themselves can be called "just prey" by Garou).

                                                            My general feeling has always been that there are enough studious/well-informed Kindred and Technocrats to know about one another, observing warily from a distance and understanding that they are most likely observed in turn.
