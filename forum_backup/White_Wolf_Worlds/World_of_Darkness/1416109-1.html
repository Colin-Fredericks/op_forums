    In the end, I think, it's one of those "up to the storyteller" things. If the storyteller likes a more poetic type universe, where belief is key, and therefore, the rules of the universe may have been very different before the Order of Reason and modern day, I think they could pull that off.

    I admit though, I prefer the idea of Earthly Constants too. Science doesn't develop in a vacuum. Someone, usually a number of people, has to make the observations, that lead to the calculations that lead to discovery. And most of these sorts of ideas: like the Earth being round, or the Earth not being the center of the universe are older than we think. A lot of the medieval belief that we talk about was strictly a European thing, and sometimes even THEY didn't completely believe what we think they believed.

    There's possibly a way to balance the concepts: Consensus is established by more than just one culture after all. Or by incorporating time elements into it: Consensus is defined not just by the people of the time, but people in the past and future. Or Consensus about particular observable phenomenon might be determined by other life forms as well. Animals likely wouldn't know or care about whether or not a human can cast a fireball, but they may be able to comprehend that the horizon has a curve to it, or that things fall when they're pushed off a table. That might be an explanation as to why gravity is an Earthly Constant, but the capabilities of mages could change so abruptly from the Mythic to the Modern age.

        In particular, one problem with the Consensus=Reality idea is that there's little reason why unintuitive notions would ever become reality. The belief that the Earth is round came about because of observations such as seeing ships sail past the horizon; but if the Consensus defines Reality and the Consensus was ever that the Earth is flat, then those observations would never take place.

        Originally posted by kalinara View Post
        There's possibly a way to balance the concepts: Consensus is established by more than just one culture after all. That might be an explanation as to why gravity is an Earthly Constant, but the capabilities of mages could change so abruptly from the Mythic to the Modern age.
        That's actually at the heart of the essay that I linked to at the start of this thread: there is no universal Consensus, because things like Correspondence Ranges, Gauntlet ratings, and separation in Time limit who can influence what. The asymmetric nature of time means that past beliefs influence the present far more than future ones do, and that present beliefs influence the future far more easily than they influence the past (this is what MRev called Historical Inertia); and the Gauntlet that isolates the Earth from the Umbra means that humans have little say about the nature of the Umbra; but also that any Willworking spirits that might reside in the other side of the Gauntlet have a greatly diminished influence on what Earth is like.

        But this rule of separation? That's an objective rule about how the world works, and isn't subject to the whims of the Consensus. More generally, there are object's rules about how belief influences reality; and those rules aren't subject to the Consensus. Those are examples of what MRev's Cosmological Constants and M20's Earthly Foundations are for: you literally cannot use the Consensus to change the rules of the game.

                                                        My point about climate change wasn't about how it came to be. It was about how best to combat it. In a Consensus=Reality setup, the best way to combat it is to deny it. More generally, this applies to any problem that confronts you: convince the Consensus to deny it, and the problem goes away.

                                                        Which, as others have pointed out, is logically consistent — or would be, of the various groups in-setting actually favored that approach. But there's a general assumption in the setting that the proper way to address these issues is that denial is a bad thing — that the first thing to do when dealing with a problem is to raise awareness of it. Exactly the opposite of what the Consensus=Reality model suggests. Thus, the model doesn't make sense.

                                                        And then there are all of the other inconsistencies that have been pointed out, such as things existing that the Consensus doesn't acknowledge.

                                                        I'll agree that there's a certain thematic purity to the Belief=Reality position, of which Consensus=Reality is a large part. And I'm not saying that it's wrong to play the game that way; it's your game, play it however you want. But that doesn't mean that it makes sense, or that acknowledging objective elements of the setting that marr that purity doesn't make sense.
