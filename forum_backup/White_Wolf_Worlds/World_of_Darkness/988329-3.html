    Well there's your first problem. They chose an Orphan.

    The reason why other posters advocated starting with a specific Tradition is because it creates an archetype the player can work with as a base. From there, it becomes easier to work the character into their own person. A Hermetic character is a wizard; now the player decides what kind of wizard they want to play.     Orphans are difficult for new players because they need to build their concept completely from scratch. And that IS hard.

    If character generation isn't too far gone, you might want to talk to the player about switching to a non-Orphan, and onto a more established Mage archetype. The nine Traditions offer a wealth of potential types to choose from. Witches, shamans, mad scientists, Sudhus and yogis, miracle workers, super hackers, etc. It'll be a lot easier to figure out what Instruments the character would use to perform a particular Effect when popular culture and legend have paved the way so thoroughly.

        Elements-as-Instruments (as opposed to Elements-as-Forces-controlled) are employed in ways relevant to the style of the Mage, obviously. But it involves getting a certain amount of the Element together and doing something with it.

        Fire: Candles lit in a circular pattern for spirit summoning. A torch wielded as a weapon (that the magick enhances). A bonfire whipped up until the smoke rises into the air, promoting the formation of storm clouds. A match lit and thrown at a person or object, to set them on fire. Holding a lit candle in one hand, in order to drive away cold. Waving hands in front of a fire in order to manipulate shadows. Enhancing a butane torch so it cuts through steel like butter. Lighting a cigarette to influence minds into thinking you're cool. Staring into a fire and reading the future in the flickering flames. Bearing a lamp to banish the darkness of ignorance. Casting sacrifices into a fire, to propitiate spirits and/or gods of an area relevant to your desires.

        Water: Holy water harming demons and vampires. A vial of water from one's home town, so that the town may protect you from harm. Sprinkling water around an area to purify it. Drinking fresh water to nourish the body and heal wounds. A still pool used for scrying. Diving into a body of water in one location, and surfacing in a different body of water entirely. Draining a bottle into a dry patch of dirt into order to cause a spring to bubble up. Using a spray bottle to create a thick, obscuring mist. Adding thrice distilled water to an alchemical concoction. Raising a glass of water to promote good cheer. Dumping a bucket on a blaze, causing the fire to rapidly die. Drenching oneself with sea water, so as to transform partially into an ocean animal. Promoting a plant's growth along a specific path by watering it in an unusual way.

        Air: Holding a bundle of balloons, in order to float through the air, weightless. Walking with the wind in order to drastically shorten travel time. Predicting the future in the clouds. Piloting a hot air balloon so high, you float into the Umbra. Blowing a handful of sand out in front of you, to summon a gale. Climbing to a high altitude, using the thinner air to promote an altered mental state. Performing a rite in a large, open area.

        Earth: Surrounding oneself with a circle of stones, to ward against spirits. Keeping a rock in one's pocket, to channel its strength and endurance. Bearing a pebble of turquois, to manipulate the sea and sky. Erecting stone columns, Stonehenge style, in order to establish a Node. Chucking a rock at a set of scaffolding, so that it breaks just the way you want it to. Surrounding your bed with dirt from your birthplace, to promote sound sleep. Looking through a hunk of crystal, to see into the world of spirits. Rubbing sand into a sturdy surface, until it begins to crumble. Covering yourself with mud, so as to cancel your heat signature from thermal detection devices. Breaking a building's cornerstone, that it will eventually collapse into ruin. Meditating on a pebble, to achieve insight.


        I can keep going with more unconventional elements, like Electricity, Glass, Plastic, Metal, Wood, etc.

            Although, one thing I have to add - all my players at the moment are almost done with the character creation BUT all of them struggle with FOCUS part of the creation - that's literaly the last thing they have to do and none of them understand how to work it out.. :-/ I'm still struggling with explanations for every single paradigmatic choice of theirs. Basically they don't know how to use their paradigm to cast spells. As an example, one person chose Everything is an Illusion, Prison, or Mistake -> The Art of Desire, and asked me - how do I make magic happen with this? From my point every mage desires to hurl magic, so ..'I meditate and want to unlock this door with matter, therefore it's open'?? This thing is really harder for us to overcome than we thought it will!
            The first thing I've noticed is that the player in question only has 2 of the 3 pieces of their focus Paradigm (Everything is an Illusion, Prison, or Mistake) and Art of Desire (Practice) but Im not seeing any Instruments and that last part is the closest to the actual how in terms of what the Mages does to cast their spells. However lets look at the first two and see if we can't piece it together.

            Paradigm (Everything is an Illusion, Prison or Mistake) - The question your player has to answer here is how they view the world through the lens of this paradigm. Some possible interpretations below (note this doesnt have to be true your Mage just has to believe it).
            • The world is a mistake, the rounding error of an immensely powerful being and it there was no significance in its creation (a very Lovecraftian perspective)
            • The world is a creation of a (or many) evil entities (malfeans, the devil, etc) to act as an experiment on lesser beings
            • The world is a prison either for humanity or for something else and humanity is just caught in the crossfire
            • The world is a test, an exam from a powerful entity (god, angels, incarnae, etc) that humanity must 'pass' before they can ascend beyond the mortal state

            No matter which of these (or some other permutation I didn't come up with, that list is no mean exhaustive) there is some deeper truth which the Mage is trying to see to and use to make their will manifest. Depending on which of these they choose will add flavor to their casting and magic. The Mage that thinks the world is a prison for humanity has a much darker and depressing view of the truth than someone who thinks the world being an illusion is a test for humanity.

            Practice (Art of Desire) and Instruments (None chosen)- These two are closely related. The Practice is the overall binding element for the Mages instruments it also gives practical form and methodology to the Mage's Paradigm. The Art of Desire is all about finding out other peoples desires and using that to enact your own desires. Its a very 'big business' view of doing magick, as the believer of Ars Cupidae is coming up with a grand plan than looks at those who would buy into that plan and how to sell it to them to reach their goals. The Instruments are the bits and pieces which make the Mages Practice function and the actual 'stuff' they do to make the magick happen. Remember that until a Mage becomes very powerful these are things they must do to cast their spells or they take penalties for fast casting or working without instruments. The problem is I don't know whether your mage views the reality behind the illusion as benevolent or not so it makes it harder to flavor the Practice and Instruments, but Ill try to provide thoughts on both.

            Art of Desire (The truth about reality is benevolent or neutral)
            People want things because deep down they know that this isn't all there is, there is something more. Your Mage has started to see what lies beyond the material world we live in and has started to manipulate theirs and others desires to pierce the illusion and make their will manifest. Looking at some of the common instruments, note that the Sphere(s) you use may change how you use the instruments to cast the spell. When casting a spell your Mage will often use multiple Instruments to make it happen, combining them to get the effect they want. I also put the Sphere(s) I think work best with a particular instrument at the end of my description.
            • Cards and dice - Fate and probability manipulators, your Mage has a goal and you want to push the outcomes to what you expect versus what may be likely to occur. As you are casting the spell you might have to roll the dice (maybe multiple times) or play a game of solitaire as you try to push the outcome of the rolls/game to match what you want to happen. They might also function as a representation of resources (the cards remaining unused in Solitaire or the deck in a game of poker for example). Sphere(s): Entropy, Prime.
            • Cosmetics - Its about using makeup and prostheses to make yourself or others look the way you want them to change to be or trying to draw a particular reaction from a target. The casting of the spell involves applying the makeup and prostheses until the look matches what you want. Sphere(s): Life, Mind
            • (Very refined) Dance and Gestures - Dancing may be about drawing a particular person a certain way or getting them into a certain move. Gestures can be anything from kissing a ladies hand to slapping someone in the face with a glove. Its about provoking reactions leading to your goal or watching and manipulating the flow of a dance to get what you want. When casting you dance or perform the appropriate gestures necessary for what you want. Sphere(s) Correspondence, Mind
            • Eye contact - To try to figure out what someone wants you have to look at them. Additionally there is the adage about the eyes being the windows to the soul and by looking someone directly in the eyes you are forging a connection with them. How you make eye contact can also alter the vibe of the contact as making bedroom eyes at someone is a different feel than looking at someone with an angry deathglare. Eye contact will almost always be part of casting a spell involving a particular person. Sphere(s): Mind
            • Fashion - The 'power' suit, the little black dress, the white tie tuxedo, the face hiding hoodie, military uniforms all these are particular looks which inspire an image and idea about the person wearing them. There is the adage 'dress for the job you want not the job you have' the masters of the Art of Desire have taken this to heart in regards to casting spells. If your character wants to cast a spell to wow someone to get them to do what they want they might dress to impress; where as if they wanted to cast a spell to protect themselves in combat they will wear body armor to inspire fear and respect. Casting the spell will involve choosing and putting on the outfit or showing it off in the way to produce the desired effect. Sphere(s) Life, Matter (altering the materials for protection)
            • Gadgets - The catch all for the various items the Mage uses to represent the effects and devices needed for their spells. Its broad to cover the areas the other instruments might not, and are especially important to Technocracy and technology based Mages. Specifically to the Art of Desire a couple that come to mind - the cellphone/little black book with all the contacts the Mage might need to call, online appraisal/ebay lookup to determine the value (or value the mage sets) on an item or location. Sphere(s) All
            • Mass media - Using the power of TV, Radio, Print, Online media to gauge what people want or make them want or not want something. The product jingle comes to mind, the little song that sticks in your head and makes sure you remember the product when you go to the store. Another example is the fear mongering attack ad, if you vote for them the terrorists win. Useful when used in conjunction with larger effects that affect multiple people. Sphere(s): Correspondence, Mind
            • Money and wealth - Money is power and buying happiness and what you want, paying people to do what you want, a physical representation of value. Money can be used when casting a spell to affect a particular person. It can also be useful for applying value to a certain object or thing. Practitioners of the Art of Desire are likely to view quintessence and tass in terms of wealth, a weak node might be called a fixer-upper where a powerful one might be called a lucrative investment. When casting the spell you might be paying the person or transferring the funds, even something as simple as looking up values or counting money can work too. Sphere(s) Mind, Prime
            • Sex - Without getting into the racy details, sex is about manipulating or providing someone one of the base desires of a majority of people. Tantric sex or the quickie could also fall under the purview of an Art of Desire Mage. Sphere(s) Life, Mind
            • Social domination - Celebrities, the Diss/Insult, the secret tape, the dirty secret. Using a greater social standing to get people to do what you want or making sure people help or hinder a particular target or goal. Casting the spell will involve crafting the tweet, mass mail, or insult/praise to get the desired reaction. Sphere(s): Mind
            • Vehicles - What you drive can be as important as how you drive it. A limo or high end luxury car is going to send a much different message than a black SUV or a beat up station wagon and a wise user of the Art of Desire can make great use of this advantage. Vehicles are also symbolic of travel and distances and can be used to manipulate such (your flight just happens to be early or your rivals flight gets delayed. Casting may involve driving to a location and getting out or booking the flight or looking up a person's flight plans. Sphere(s): Correspondence, Mind
            • Voice - Its how you articulate what you want; whether its a question, statement, or threat. The right words in the right place or ear can make all the difference between going forward with an idea or one being left on the cutting room floor. Casting the spell might be asking someone what they want, shouting and insulting an opponent, or just making a statement regarding something (I'm reminded of the Doctor Who moment where the Doctor says "Doesn't she look tired" which ends up leading to her defeat from politics). Sphere(s): All
            • Weapons - The various tools of war which the Mage will likely use when casting combat effects or intimidate or reassure. Sphere(s): Forces, Mind

                Everything above is perfectly applicable and certainly worth consideration. But just as a counterpoint (as well as a small challenge for myself) I'm going to try and think up a Focus with the criteria provided.

                They are an Orphan
                They have two dots in Mind, Matter, Prime, and Time (wow that's a lot for a starting mage!)
                Paradigm is Everything is an Illusion
                Practice is Art of Desire
                Instruments are the Elements

                Step one, the Paradigm: As Cynic01 correctly states, you need to understand from your player what they actually consider the world to be in terms of illusion. But as long as it's established that the world we see is not the world that is, then we're good to go.

                Step two, the Practice: Art of Desire. This actually fits in to the above Paradigm pretty well. Perhaps the Orphan mage thinks that the world is only what people want to see in it. So if they're negative and enjoy bashing how nothing works, then their desire for pulling things apart literally manifests in systems falling apart all around them, the bus is always late, the printer always jams, nepotism will always keep you away from your promotion, and going to the doctor will always cause bad news and terrible ailments to manifest.
                Conversely, perhaps they think a positive outlook and a desire to see the good in the world will cause positive things to manifest around them. A kind man helps a lost child reunite with their searching mother, the sun comes out just before a party in the park, you meet someone at a bus stop and you just seem to click.
                This viewpoint is somewhat a combination of the "rule of 3" from Wicca, and the Karmic cycle, where what you expect colours what you desire to see, and what you desire shapes what the world provides for you. When you're a sleeper, you let what the world provides for you colour your expectations, creating a closed loop of self perpetuation. But when you're awakened, you're able to break out of this loop, and focus your desires properly to create the outcome of events you actually want.

                So most people expect matter to be solid, and so subconsciously desire it to be solid, therefor matter is solid. Your mage, however, knows that if they can truly focus their desire for the matter to be less solid, reality will abide and change.

                Step three: the Focus: Here's how we can get the elements to fit into all of this: Think about the minor arcana of the Tarot. There are four suits each corresponding to one of the four elements.

                Wands: Fire
                Cups: Water
                Swords: Air
                Coins: Earth

                However, they also correspond to different ways of being.

                Wands/Fire: Action & Creation
                Cups/Water: Emotion & Passion
                Swords/Air: Intellect & Reasoning
                Coins/Earth: Instinct & Intuition

                These different modes of thought can be used to shape the way one enacts their desire on the world, and thus creates the effect they wish to see. The focus itself can be a symbol for them to focus that area of their desire into being.

                So if they need to create something, perhaps it comes out of the burning embers of something else. Burning a log in order to find a number of coins within it (Matter 2), or using the flickering light of a flame and its constant dance to focus their desire upon another's mind, creating an impulse within them (Mind 2).
                The cold feeling of water on their skin may allow them to tap into the emotional side of their desire, and create a connection so powerful with whatever they are looking at that they can look into their future (Time 2).
                Maybe they need to go outside into an evening breeze to clear their head enough to think rationally, allowing them to properly move Quintessential energy into their own body (Prime 2) or provide greater strength to their weapons (also Prime 2).
                Maybe they feel the need to ground themselves, literally, burying their feat in the earth so they can tap into that instinctual side of themselves, allowing them to intuitively feel out time (Time 1) or simply know what something is made of (Matter 1).

                These are just some examples, if your player likes them, then they are free to think any others that would fit in this system.

                Additionally, they aren't limited to the elements in their "raw" form, but could use icons as representations of them. This could be as simple as using the tarot symbols themselves (wands, cups, swords, and coins) or objects that are associated with these elements (Candle, Bottle, Balloon, Plant pot), or even objects that resemble these elements, such as gems (Jasper, Sodalite, Opal, Jet) or Nature (Fang, Shell, Feather, Claw). As long as it makes sense to the orphan why these particular objects can be used, and how they can help in focusing their desire in specific ways. It can't be arbitrary.

                The key here is that the element used puts them in the correct frame of mind to amplify their desire on to reality, breaking the illusion and resulting in real physical change.7

                How's that?

                                            If a rule is getting in the way of having fun, then the rule isn't worth the hassle. At the end of the day, it's about doing what's best for the group.
                                            That being said, I would agree that to ignore the paradigm aspect of mage is a real shame as it's one of the most interesting sources of conflict there is.

                                            My current crop of players were completely new to Mage as well. I'm not sure if this will be of any use to you at this stage, but here's what I did for them.

                                            At Character Creation, I worked on them with their character individually. As in, I invited one of the players over and the day was dedicated to only that person's Character.
                                            I asked them if they had an idea for a character in mind, and listened to what they said.
                                            If they didn't, we'd start from scratch, and either begin with Roleplay or Stats depending on what they felt more comfortable with.

                                            Stats is pretty self explanatory, we chose the Concept, Nature, Demeanour and Essence, shown them the list of Traditions, I give the numbers and ran through what each stat actually did. Everything right up until the Advantages and Spheres section.

                                            Roleplay first means I ask them a bunch of questions to try and figure out what we know about this guy. How old are they? What's their dayjob? Do they have any family... That sort of thing.

                                            Either the stats help inform these answers, or these answers help inform the stats, either way works.

                                            There are, however, three questions I always ask my players, and I do not accept anything less than a well thought out answer.

                                            How did your mage Awaken?
                                            How does your mage think the universe works?
                                            When your mage does magic, what do they think they are doing?

                                            These three questions are key to how I got my Players to work out their paradigm.
                                            The first allows them to put their character in the context of the setting. it forces the player to consider the difference between their Sleeper player and what changed. This change is how they became aware of Magic, but not only that, but on how they realised that reality wasn't as static as they had once thought before. It also forces the player to contextualise the tradition they associated with, and may even give allegiances towards a particular faction within the tradition.

                                            The second is the key point of paradigm. I never showed my players the M20 book on Focus. Sure it's a nice source which lays things out pretty nicely, but it made the whole thing feel more like a flowchart than a personal field of view. If they were going to understand their paradigm properly, it would have to come from them.
                                            I do not give too much guidance on this, I let them look through the tradition's various beliefs and encourage them to ask me questions on how to go about this, but at the end of the day, this answer has to come from them.

                                            The third question is how to get their Practice sorted out. Now that the player has a concrete idea of how they think reality works, they can consider what the mage is able to do to alter it.
                                            Here are my players as examples.

                                            - One thinks the universe is basically all constructed out of data and code, so if they can hack into the values that structure reality, they can change said reality, much like one would mod a videogame.
                                            - One believes that the universe is nothing but a swirling maelstrom of chaos, and any semblance of consistency is just an illusion created by the observer to make themselves feel better. Because they are aware of this, they are able dispel this illusion and return the natural chaos to them, or even take control of this chaos and force a specific kind of order into it, In this way they can make changes in the way reality appears to work, and in a reality that is nothing but illusions anyway, that's exactly the same thing as makings things change.
                                            - One thinks that the rhythm of life is made on the backbone of language, and that all things are part of an ongoing narrative. If then, they can place specific portions of narrative, words, or rhythms into the right place at the right time, they can force a level of change to take place in the story that plays out before them.
                                            - One does not believe that magic is inherent to humans, but is actually a form of divine intervention. When he does magic, he is actually acting as a conduit for the Will of the Divine.

                                            They all came up with those themselves, no guide necessary.

                                            Anyway, once this is all thought up, it is then and only then that we go through things like the Spheres. We finish up their character sheets, and before going on to Merits and Flaws, we look at "Tools". Now each player knows exactly what they are doing with magic, and which spheres are under their influence.
                                            The next question is "How dose you character actually do magic then?"

                                            This is the only time I let them look at the list of tool's to give them idea of what is available, and remind them that this list is not exclusive. They are allowed to use any tool they like so long as they are able to explain to me how it fits in with the paradigm and practice they though about earlier.

                                            For the Computer guy, it only makes sense that his tools be various pieces of software for his computer, smart phone, or tablet.
                                            For Maelstrom guy, he needs to get his mind into focus to deal with the chaos, so he turns to alcohol. If he can loosen himself up a bit, getting his head on the same track as the chaos around him, then things get a lot easier.
                                            For Language guy, it's all about the words, poetry, and language. He has a number of wooden Runes which he uses to spell ancient words of forgotten lore, he can also recite poems in a rhythmic process to impose specific kinds of narratives to take place around him.
                                            For Religious guy, he is simply paying tribute to his Deity, and in so doing is able to garner their favour. A Prayer for help gives help. A request for the tools needed to vanquish foes is provided. Holding his cross in hand allows him to commune with a divine sense allowing him to feel out what he is searching for.

                                            Then we cover everything else, go through Merits and Flaws, and that's it, all done.

                                            Another question I might ask them is "How aware if your mage of his Avatar?" and then try to figure out what that relationship is like. This can be done as early as talking about Essence, or as late as the last thing you do. But it's an important part of Mage. How aware they are, as well as how their avatar interacts with them shouldn't be glossed over.


                                            The important things are as follows:
                                            1. It's a one-on-one meeting with the ST and the player. This allows them to create a very individualised idea of how magic functions. If they are all in the same room, then it becomes all too easy to laps into a shared belief system, and the ever present Purple Paradigm.
                                            2. You can't look at tools without knowing how they affect reality, and you can't know how to affect reality unless you know what reality is and how it functions. This must be done in this order, and they have an internal logic to them. If they don't, then the paradigm doesn't work. Be strict about this, if something doesn't make sense to the player, then it can't possibly function for the character.
                                            3. Do your utmost best not to lead the player to a paradigm. The more it comes from them, the more willing they will be to defend it like their Mage character would do.
                                            4. Think about their avatar. It is the best ST tool in the whole game.

                                            One thing I might suggest is, if your Players are really having a hard time getting their head around Paradigm, and it's gone to the point that you can't do a thing about it. But they are still interesting in playing a free-form magic game. Why not try out Mage: The Awakening?

                                            That system has every mage have more or less the same paradigm, they just bicker about the "best" or most "efficient" means of producing magic.
                                            Sure, there's no technocracy, and very little lore by comparison, but it might work as an alternative route into the system.
