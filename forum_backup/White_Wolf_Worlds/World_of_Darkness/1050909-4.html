                        Originally posted by Aya Tari View Post
                        I do not think that anyone is arguing against the Consensus, just what the Consensus effects. In crossovers, it pretty much only effects Mages and their creations (otherwise it is just a Mage game with superfriends).
                        As I said on the other crossover thread, some people are just hell-bent on picking one gameline's mythology as "truth". I never got that, for me they're compatible and became incompatible just in the case of taking them to the extreme. Vampire's God can be many things that could fit into the other line's worldview. The consensus could work with the other lines if approaching it with the earthly foundations and mythic threads in mind.

                        Same with the organizations.

                        Honestly, having as many crossover elements as we have, crossover is canon to me, period. Constantly saying that "oooh, but the critters in this book (even when that book is intentionally a crossover book) aren't the critters from the other game, really" is getting old after a point. That the authors themselves had varied opinions on crossovers, I'm okay with that and with thematic purity (although sometimes it's starting to look as "get off from my lawn" joke), but honestly, it's also somewhat lazy referring to that again and again instead of writing more compatible rules and taking into mind the other games when writing a supplement, with more interactions between the splats, etc.

                        I might have been read too much urban fantasy, but in my mind it's always better to having a diverse setting with multiple splats (with the possibility of focusing on just one if I want) than an entirely "purist" setting and honestly, WoD started that whole genre back in the day. As far as I'm aware, WoD was the first setting with vamps, (multiple) werecreatures, fairies, mages, etc. in it that countless authors copied afterward to some extent.


                        If nothing worked, then let's think!
