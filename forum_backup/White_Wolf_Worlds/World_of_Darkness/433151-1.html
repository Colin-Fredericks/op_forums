                    Which works just find if your are playing Werewolf, Mage or Vampire.

                    But Mark seems to be asking about an opponent book for DA Vampire that draws on some of the concepts of DtF with questions that directly bear on issues straight out of DtF, specifically the archdukes of Hell. So the cosmology of Demon is germane to the discussion, probably much more germane than the cosmology of Werewolf.

                    Though DtF is pretty heavy handed, (to the point of OMG did they REALLY just denigrate the figures of an actual religion that is being practiced), it's cosmology doesn't necessarily cross-over in a non-mutually exclusive manner with Vampire or Wraith or Reckoning (or with a bit more stretching KoE).

                    If you take Mage, tilt it at the right angle and look through these glasses (gives strange looking glasses) and hum along with this song (hands over iPod with ear buds) that might work too.

                    Changeling neither integrates or fails to integrate with other lines on a case by case basis, but usually they are so off tripping in their own shared headspace that it doesn't matter overmuch.

                    The one cosmology which seems like it has to be absolutely true for the integral pieces of that game to function as it is presented is Werewolf. For Werewolf to work, it really ALL has to be about what's going down with the Triat. So everyone else has to fit into one of these three categories or some mash-up of them... When other games are handled with Werewolf, they have to take into account, usually take on, this absolutist view. I haven't seen nearly as many people trying to shoehorn Werewolf into the other games, it's always seemed like it's trying to take Werewolf's version of things and shoehorn in the other games. (How is Oblivion at part of the Wyrm? Mage Essences are just reflective of the Triat, etc. etc.) It's very seldom the other way around. (So the Wyrm is just a renegade Neverborn or something that crawled out of the Deep Umbra and got stuck in the Middle Umbra or that the werewolves are just obsessed with a trio of aspected powerful Middle Umbra Spirits that seem to line up with the different types of Avatar, etc.)

                    DtF just says the Werewolf is wrong, that the Garou are limited and don't know what they're talking about. Like when Garou tell Kindred that Caine story is BS and they are all Wyrm tainted animated corpses.

                    It would be simpler if all the game lines were really separated out, but there are so many crossover products, like this one, that sometimes you have to try to figure out what to do when worlds collide.

                            That's not... Do I actually have to type this again? Wyrm is a cosmic principle. It simply is. It always will be and it will be the force of ending, renewal and making the way for new things. If the Wyrm was not actually fulfilling its main purpose, everything on the planet would not exist... there would be no death, no circle of life and the reality would look like a horrifying calcified fixture with pockets of random chaos that would splurt out.

                            In addition to this, a facet of the Wyrm is deeply and horribly messed up. It was caused either by Weaver's encroachment or its own attempts at control, but either way there is a part of the Wyrm as a cosmic principle that has a raging hard on to murder, debase and horrifyingly destroy everything in its path. This part is the "evil" that we encunter in Werewolf and why the shifters have issues with the Wyrm. You will note that a lot of shifters have positive things to say about the Wyrm, and that there are even factions who actually serve the sane part of the Wyrm. This gets drowned out in the simplification of everything Wyrmish being evil, which is often conflated by people who are new at Werewolf.

                            Also, everything in Werewolf is NOT about the Triat. The Triat simply is. Not every element of the setting stems from the Triat and the countless umbral worlds, spirits, entities and strangeness (like the Dreaming, Shadowlands, Oblivion, Astral Realms, Abyss, the planets and even the Thousand Hells) with their own agendas and plans, and where the concepts and ideas of the Triat never really come into play outside the most general ones.

                            Whenever the factions of the WoD encounter the servants of the insane Wyrm in action, they all pretty unequivocally agree that these things are evil, horrible and should be killed and fought (Even the Devil Tigers know that there is no profound wisdom in crazy monsters, so they kill them or force them to serve them instead). What they disagree and discuss philosophically is what can be done with the Wyrm... Is it Evil? Are its servants evil? Can it be healed?                                                 Crossover discussion threads seem to wind and went their way here all the time don't they?

                                                Mark,

                                                My answer to your OP is that DD uses Torment in a simplified way compared to DtF, because, why not? It's an antagonist book and it's not really designed to make DA devils playable characters. So, the reason that DD has a lid on Torment at 10 is that, even though it's being used as a "power pool" stat, it's really more of a "power stat" sensu stricto, like Pathos, Gnosis, Glamour, etc. It determines the value of that pool an limits it, but, not all Torment 10 are created equal, just like all Wraiths have max Pathos 10: a Deathlord and a wraith that was Reaped yesterday could both have Pathos 10. Joe the Demon From Hell who is possessing my buddy Adelbard can have Torment 10. (Poor, poor Joe.) Belial, the Great Beast, also has Torment 10. Belial can almost certainly use his Torment 10 more effectively than Joe, just like the Deathlord can use his Pathos and Angst more effectively than the newbie wraith.

                                                Even though DtF Earthbound explicitly does not handle the Archdukes (more handwaving that they're too strong for stats), it does give a really good idea of exactly how powerful Earthbound can get. They all have Torment 10, but their Torment 10 ends up being a different beastie.

                                                Obviously, DD is just using DtF terms where they work out, and the meanings are not precisely the same between the two, but, as the DtF is informing DD, it seems like the answers are there. With the caveat that DtF is in no way as fully developed a line as the DA, so there are going to be lacunae there as well. Things they just didn't get to. (Like actual rules about Demons and the other two Umbrae, aka how, exactly Demon will elect to stomp all over Werewolf. Like Werewold stomps all over everyone else in a brute force kinda way and Mage does so in an erudite and lawyerly way.)
