      I think... you're interpreting the dual nature of the changeling-human bond a bit too... disparate. While a changeling is technically a fae entity that joins with a human host, they exist as one being; even if what "world" they're interacting with can cause them to bend farther towards one of the two components more strongly.

      The changelings as a whole don't have much dealings with the Traditions. One of the founding Houses of the Order of Hermes was known for interacting with the fae of old, but they have long since fallen into obscurity. The Hollow Ones are probably the group with the most regular dealings with changelings, simply because the Hollow Ones have a thing for getting involved with other supernaturals.

      If the human host dies, commoner kiths reincarnate into a new host. Sidhe die; they don't like this.

      There's nothing stopping a changeling from enchanting a human, and then conceiving a child with them while they're interacting with the changeling side of things.

      The last question... who knows sometimes.

              if the changeling dies, the human host forgets Everything about the dreaming
              First of all this would imply either a Chimerical death which is more akin to a coma, for the Fae part of the changeling that is, which is temporary only meaning that after a set time depending on the Changelings Banality the Fae soul once again awakens with all the memories it previously had or you mean the Fae half has been undone by Banality which is more permanent, though arguably depending on the exact nature of this may not be that permanent meaning that a mortal death may release the Fae soul free to reincarnate, but in both of these cases yes the mortal half will not remember anything about the Dreaming thanks to the Mists.

              what happends if the human host dies?
              Well first of all host is a bad wording on the matter. Changelings, other than the Sidhe who for the most part aren't "proper" Changelings, have become what they are through a Changeling Way ritual that has entwined their Fae soul with that of a mortal meaning that it is not as much a possession as it is one fused soul that has been born the way it is, making the Changeling a half-mortal half-Fea being instead of fully either. So taking this into account if the human part dies, as in the Changeling faces non-Chimerical death, the Fae part dies with it and moves on to reincarnate with the next suitable human soul.

              can a changeling in his real form actually have a child with a human?
              Yes he can and the child is called Kinain, though thanks to the Mists the human partner won't really remember much of the Changeling unless Enchanted, but the result is the same if the Changeling has a child with a human without showing her/him it's Fae side, which also leaves the mortal with normal recollection of event's.

              it needs to be with the human host?
              Like mentioned earlier there is no separation between a Changeling and it's human side so even when in their Fae likeness, that is the Changeling has either called upon the Wyrd or Enchanted the human, they are still also their human half thus like said above the results are the same no matter whether the mortal knows s/he is sleeping with a Changeling or not.

              There are only two Fae beings that are fully Fae beings without being tied to a human, and without going into the classification of True Fae for which we have no actual stats unless using DA:F Fae as the True Fae, and for the most part only the other one of them is capable of Possession. These two are the Chimera and the Adhene, the latter of which are very much like Changelings except they have never gone through the Changeling Way ritual and they do have the ability to possess a mortal to shield themselves from Banality but it isn't easy and the mortal must usually be asleep and should it awaken the possessor is generally driven out. You can find more information about the Adhene from Denizens of the Dreaming.

              does the Age of a Changeling shows on its human host or the human host can be very young while the actual changeling be ancient?
              Usually they are one and the same, like said it's not possession it's fusion of the two souls and beings that has been as it is since birth. But there are arguably exceptions especially depending on ST's take on the matter. For example there are certain Sidhe who age considerably faster unless they regularly practice certain abhorrent ways of attaining Glamour, which again depending on ST can either or even both prevent their fast aging or reverse it altogether, and some might rule this aging to affect both their human sides and their Fae sides equally some might not. I'd err for the side of caution and say that unlike me most ST's probably keep the aging in such cases purely Chimerical though the source material might not be quite so clear cut.

              Then again if you are trying to avoid the ageism trap of CtD then you can unlink the Childing/Wilder/Grump states from Changelings physical Age which could mean that the Fae half can be of seemingly different age than the human side. But this latter part is not in any form canon.

              mainly on the relationships between the Changelings and some magical traditions (the hermetic mainly)
              I'll start this by saying that the Changeling relations with other supernaturals are complicated considerably by the fact that the Mists affect the other supernaturals as easily as they affect mortals that is unless Enchanted the other Supernaturals cannot usually remember the Changelings except as fleeting dreams depending upon their Banality ratings of course. That being said the Hermetics do acknowledge the existence of the Fae and one of their minor Houses that has been absorbed into Ex Miscellania in the modern day, though they are likely to hold on to their own House moniker even within Ex Miscellania as they are separated from the rest by blood, and that is House Merinita which had strong ties to the Fae and their magic many if not most members having been Kinain themselves. I do think that either Guide to the Traditions or Mage Storytellers Handbook, revised, mentions them and has some suggestions on how to use them in a Mage game. But in any case House Merinita has traditionally had strong ties with the Fae, and I would think the modern remnants of the House keep up those relations as best they can. As for hte Hermetics at large the Code of Hermes still holds a commandment about not angering the Fae but for most of the Order the time of the Fae has passed and while they once did wield powers to make even the Masters quake in their boots they are mostly believed to be lost now, again see the Mists.

              Some Verbena, again like those with some Fae blood in them, might hold relations similar to the Merinita with the Changelings and the same is true of a some Dreamspeakers perhaps but like said thanks to the Mists even the Mages tend to not know much of the fate of the Fae or even of hte existence of Changelings.

              As a final thing, and this is mentioned both in the TB: OoH and in either the GttT or MSTH regarding House Merinita and that is that their decline in the Order is the result of majority of them retreating into the otherworlds, ie the Dreaming, sometime in the 1300's and that they might still be present there gathering their strength. Similar rumors claim that the Dreaming might act as a refuge for the last surviving members of House Diedne as well which may not bode well for the Order at large.

              So there ya go hope these answers/ramblings are of some use to you.

                                  Originally posted by Crowley View Post
                                  . . . if the changeling dies, the human host forgets Everything about the dreaming. but what happends if the human host dies? what can the changeling Do?.
                                  Commoner (i.e. non-sidhe) changelings and the sidhe of House Scathach went through a ritual around the time of the Shattering (ca. the early Renaissance), making them more like mummies or Celestial Exalted. If the human body dies, the faerie "soul" reincarnates (at some point), bonding with the soul of a newborn human child (often one related to its last incarnation) to create a new changeling. Theoretically, there could be some memories retained from life to life, but in practice, that's pretty rare - so while technically its the same being, in reality its effectively a whole new entity.

                                  The non-Scathach sidhe, on the other hand . . . well, they're closer to demonic possession. The sidhe "soul" kicks the human soul out of the body, which is (believed to be) sent to Arcadia. However, when that human body dies . . . well, no one really knows what happens to the sidhe soul. In Arcadia, the death of a sidhe was temporary. On Earth . . . no one's really sure. Many believe the non-Scathach sidhe truly die (although at least one appears to have become a banshee)

                                  can a changeling in his real form actually have a child with a human? or it needs to be with the human host?
                                  Of course a changeling can have kids. There's a living, breathing human body in there, underneath all the glamour, perfectly capable of being impregnated (or getting someone pregnant).

                                  The catch is the child will either be a normal human, or kinain (a human with some fae "blood") - not another fae. The impression I got (others may disagree) is that the number of faerie souls is fixed - at least on Earth. Maybe its a consequence of the changeling ritual, maybe of the increased banality of the world - but the changelings are literally a dying breed (although thanks to the reincarnation thing, they can stretch that out for millennia or more)

                                  does the Age of a Changeling shows on its human host or the human host can be very young while the actual changeling be ancient?
                                  The age of the human body is reflected in the faerie "seeming" (i.e. form). So a thousand year old faerie soul awakening in a six-year-old's body will look (and act) like a six-year-old faerie. As others have mentioned, with the commoner and Scathach sidhe, its a bonding, a sharing - not a possession.

                                  There is a catch to this, though. Inside a freehold (the changeling equivalent to a node/caern/haunt), a changeling (or enchanted human) does not age, either physically or mentally. Sure, they can learn new skills . . . but they don't mature or "grow up". There are sample NPCs in some of the Changeling supplements that have been frozen in time for decades (or more). So its theoretically possible to have a changeling who's chronologically over a thousand years old, but still looks/talks/acts like an eight-year-old.

                                      Originally posted by Crowley View Post
                                      One last question.

                                      a friend told me that Nightmares are quite brutal when it comes to Changeling. that they are particularly nasty when it comes to beings that use Glamour but they are not much of a threat against anything else..how can make a Nightmare that can actually be a threat against mages/werewolves and other supernaturals as well humans?
                                      Picking up on Moirdryd's explanation:

                                      A NIghtmare is a type of chimera, a being made of pure glamour, spawned either by a particularly potent dream or certain forms of changeling magic. However, they have no mundane/"physical" body. As such, most chimera can't directly affect mundane reality - although they can (and do) interact with 'dual nature" entities like changelings or enchanted humans. Some are glorious wonders, some are just nuisances, and some are quite freaky. And, needless to say, chimera spawned of nightmares aren't the nicest beings - some are merely creepy (like monsters-under-the-bed), while others are virtually demonic.

                                      So, as a rule, most humans (or other supernaturals) are immune to the machinations of Nightmares and other chimeras. They can't fly on a chimerical pegasus . . . but they also don't have to worry about a three-headed xenomorph-dragon using them as a chew toy, either. Unless a changeling enchants them, of course.

                                      However, certain mage rotes et al allow the user to perceive, and even interact with the denizens of the Dreaming . . . which makes them fair game for chimera (Nightmare or otherwise). And some places have special rules. Graymere Glen, for example, a changeling freehold, was the site of a human town for generations. The idea of one particular type of Nightmare (the Schnorrfleurs) became deeply ingrained in the residents, etched into their imagination generation after generation . . . resulting in the Schnorrfleurs actually being able to step into mundane reality in the area around the freehold.
