      My darkest chronicle ever was a Wraith game, hands down.

      Most of the characters in it were regular people on the surface with terrible secrets that were often buried in their subconscious minds a-la Silent Hill, such as a character who discovered that he was the responsible for the death of her teenage girlfriend, now turned a spectre, when he pushed her to have an illegal abortion years before his own demise. At the end of the chronicle, all but one player decided his character would walk right into the Maelstrom to be torn apart into Oblivion, as they deserved it from their sins.

      Funny thing is, though most of the players enjoyed the game, they wouldn't ever play Wraith again, as it was too grimdark for their taste.

          Led my players in the solemn enactment of a satanic ritual.

          So, this is a Hunter game, and as a group of PC's we had spent the last 3 games chasing down a brutal cult, and gathering clues that they quickly discovered were elements of a ritual that the cult was going to perform. One of the PC's was blackmailed, and he gave up all of the things that the group had gathered to the cult. And over the course of those games, they had created a pretty wordy and intricate ritual. All the details, and occult symbology, and the spooky words and all. They were a bunch of philosophy/theology geeks who love to chew scenery, so you can imagine how far they ran with it.

          The next game, I switched it up on them, and had each of them play a member of the cult. I took the role of the high priest. They already knew the ritual. So, we busted out the black robes(I had 6 of them, matching, made for a con long ago. We were Sith.) and we did exactly what our parents always feared we would do one day, and summoned Satan in the livingroom. I -was- planning to have them switch back to their PC's before the ritual could be completed, but they all decided that they didn't want to change back until it was over, and they could drink the kool-aid I had made for them.

          So, the ritual went off without a hitch, and the next game the regular PC's found the mass suicide that the ritual had left behind, and ominous hints that the End could not be stopped.

                                  The game started before revised edition, so when it came out and said the main caern was going to fall and the gazers were going to basically go to the beast courts they wanted to be involved in that and there pack thought it would be awesome. So I did a couple of scenes that lead to them being there during the time of the fall. Basically Chimera gave one of them a vision of something bad going to happen and they went on a quest to warn them. The pack was made up of 2 stargazers, an Uktena, a Glasswalker, and a Silverfang. To make a long story short the Silverfang thought it would be a great idea to have the glasswalker set up explosives in the escape canyon so that if they needed to retreat they could blow the path on the way out and protect the Stargazers.

                                  Everyone agreed, the fight happened, they stayed in the battle to long trying to rack up as much renown as they could until the Stargazers called it quits.As they are running out glasswalker goes to trip the first set of explosives with control simple machine and botches hardcore (by hardcore, i mean on 7 dice he gets 4 1's and no successes). Ends up setting off all of the charges. 75% of the tribe is buried in the canyon with the BSD. The ones that ran into Umbra were slaughtered by Wyrm minions on the other end. At the time the only packs outside of the caern were an envoy to the Beast Courts and the Nation asking for aid and a few stragglers who hadn't made it home yet.

                                  Up until this point they had never failed a mission, the dice loved them. This was an epic failure that changed the whole direction of the pack. The rest of the chronicle was them trying to save as many of the Stargazers left in the world as they could and get them to the Beast Courts safely. There would about 15 stragglers + the 2 envoys which were about 5 wolves each so 25 NPC Stargazers left in the world at that point. 1 pack didn't make it, ended up dying saving the PCs.

                                  Throughout this the Glasswalker and Silverfang ended up losing their shit. Glasswalker started giving in more and more to the weaver. More upgrades to make him better so he could redeem himself. The silverfang fell the other way, he and the Uktena ended up dabbling with banes. He lost his mind and nearly got the pack killed because of the whisper of a chance at more power (this is what cost them the other 5 gazers).

                                          I was running a Vampire the Masquerade game that I named "Broken Choices" none of the players ever caught on to the fact that the name of the chronicle was also the theme. So:

                                          When they felt they could get away with Diablerie because they were attacking a Sabbat City, rather then spending their time trying to infiltrate or track down members for assassination, the Coterie leader, (a Malkavian who was built like an Assamite who basicly had Megalomania) felt that the best solutions would always involve pipe bombs and the kind of explosives that flattened buildings.

                                          After they successfully brought the city of Portland to it's knees in an assumed terrorist attack that had successfully destroyed a large number of key infrastructures they decided to go after the sabbat leaders! Long story short: they attacked the Harbingers of the skulls and successfully diablerized two of them. In a feeding frenzy and diablerie-off one of our newest players succeeded, and there was a huge difference between the two generations so he was very happy to get his extra blood expenditures but wondered why he didn't get some kind of cool discipline, I told him that it didn't make sense to just learn some diciplines out of the blue so that his powers would have to develop.

                                          When they returned to their home city he decided to feed from a woman he picked up at a bar and get a little nookie in the process. He accidentally Thanatosis'd the woman as I asked him for a willpower roll and oh my did he botch. The look of horror as the player realized not only did his character kill his date but that she had become a mummified corpse wraped around his body in flagrante delicto was priceless. It took him three more games of accidentally activating his bonus dots of Thanatosis before he got full control.

                                                  When I was in college, I once played a Red Talon Philodox who successfully sterilized hundreds of homid Garou (mostly Glass Walkers), over a thousand human kin and something like 85% of the populations of Montreal, Toronto, Vancouver and Edmonton. Including one of his own pack mates (though that was inadvertant). . During an infiltration of a Magadon facility in New Jersey (Wyrm-tainted Pentex corporation's pharmaceutical arm, for those not familiar with Werewolf), my character got separated from the pack. While on his own, he found an R&D lab where they were working on spiritually active bioweapons. He tortured the researcher who was present to find out what everything did, then took the one that interested him- a strain that was virtually undetectable and caused irreversible sterility in humans and homid Garou. Magadon considered it a failure they were trying to only target the Garou and couldn't make the virus selective enough. He destroyed the rest, then secretly started introducing it to the water supply at select caerns and in the aforementioned cities. The pack was nomadic and ended up spending a lot of time in Canada because of a quest we were doing for the Wendigo (I forget the details), and he figured using it in Canadian cities would be more effective than using it in the US since it would take less effort to depopulate Canada. The punchline-he got away with it! Suspicion was deflected onto Magadon and Pentex, and when they finally realized that it had to be a Garou accessing these caerns, the Wendigo pack member got blamed because none of the stuff had ever been released in any Wendigo caern or First Nations population. After the Wendigo pack member was killed for it, my character stopped using it and passed his remaining supply to some Red Talon elders to decide what to do with it (he only had enough left for maybe two more cities at that point anyway).

                                                          I played this DA:M game years ago where after Mistridge and some other clashes between the Craftmasons and the Order of Hermes brought the two Fellowships to the brink of war between them. But there were members of both trying prevent this. That's where we came in. Our cabal consisted of two Craftmasons (myself and another) and two members of the OoH working together to broker a peace before things spiral out of control.

                                                          Long story short, our cabal traveled across Europe, negotiating with various leaders among both groups, carrying little quests to appease some, prevented an assassination attempt on one and removed another. All the while the ST was selling us on the idea that depending on our actions we could change history, perhaps even preventing the rise of the Order of Reason and the Traditions leading to a whole different future. Ultimately we were successful, our masters coming together at the Tower of Languedoc (currently a Heremtic chantry) in France to iron out an accord. First night there a big glorious feast and everything seemed to be going so well, a happy conclusion to a long but excellent chronicle.

                                                          But it was... well it was the Red Wedding* with the Heremtics blindsiding the Masons and those Heremtics that sought peace.

                                                          Worse it was started by the Heremtic players who privately approached the ST early on about this and help craft this whole betrayal. Virtually the entire time we were going about trying to bring together the two Fellowships they were plotting our downfall. Needless to say we were pissed off when the reveal happened and the killing began.

                                                          Of course some of the Masons did manage to escape (us included) and in the Epilogue, set a decade later, we got our revenge on them, destroying a major Heremtic chantry in the process.

                                                          *If Game of Thrones the TV series existed back then I suspect that the ST would have played The Rains of Castamere just before the big reveal, just to have that "Oh shit..." moment.
