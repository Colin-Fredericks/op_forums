    Originally posted by PookaKnight View Post
    I'm not sure I should be wading into this conversation, so I'll leave the rest of it alone. I do want to say something about one thing though.



    Humans are incredibly social creatures. We are pretty much hardwired to form groups. Part of that is empathy and supporting people outside of the individual - the basis of duty.

    Glamour comes from the higher order human thoughts and emotions, both positive and negative. Honor, duty, and standing among one's peers is part of glamour, so is rebellion, shirking responsibility, and forging one's own path.

    The two courts embody the extremes of human psychology, so while death before dishonor and passion before duty are polar opposites and almost no individual will ever always embody one to the exclusion of the other, they are both valid psychologically and are equally glamorous.

    Death before dishonor, is crazy.You'd never get that as advice from someone. You'd never get "Never forget a debt' as advice. But "Follow your heart" that you'll get. And that's all Passion before Duty is but made "Edgy". Fairies are creatures of cosmic madness, they are stories given form. Safe, reasonable, sane these are and should be the buzzwords the call of banality. So I think ultimately Passion before Duty needs to be ramped up, it needs to be something more something insane,

    :Let your Passions consume you.

    If Seelie are willing to die over honor, the Unseelie should be willing to die for their passions. When the Joker tries to make Batman break to the point he would let himself die just to "win" that's what should be here. When Malificent takes a slight social faux pas as an excuse to lay down a curse on an entire kingdom? Even to the point that it ultimately destroys her, even to the point that the sane thing would be to ignore it, that should be there.

    That's my vision of the Unseelie. Not as cynical counter point to the Seelie. Not as fifth column for banality or the Formorians but a grand and gloriously dark part of the Dream. Something necissary, something important and something that has been betrayed by the Seelie.

                        oocly but I think it might be best to start from some basic premises.

                        1. Seelie and Unseelie should be valid ways to "Good" guys.

                        I find this is undercut by setting points that treat them either as 5th column for banality(as they dwell in it more readily have no problem using iron etc) and for the Formorians(Thalain, House balor), I find this is undercut by the willingness to Ravage when the setting basically says "Yep that kills the dreaming".

                        2. I feel that banality should have its lure for changelings. The Dreaming is about narrative logic and tropes it wants you to do elaborate death traps then walk away. Banality is about cutting through that and sometimes just wanting to machine gun a person down. The seductive quality of banality is its easy, its sane, its smart.

                        3. With number 2 as a premise I don't feel the Unseelie should be the fail state of the Seelie. Failing to live up to the Code shouldn't be a "Fall" to the Unseelie. Too much of the Unseelie code reads to me as a fail state for the Seelie. I think rather being a dishonorable person who shits on beauty and love should be a Seelie embracing banality rather than unseelie nature.

                        4. Currently there's no real lure for an Unseelie fae to "fall" to their seelie nature.

                        This is why I don't feel the Unseelie Code works currently. It seems to be just completely at odds with basic premises of the setting. Both in its pro Ravaging stance, to in my mind being almost an active addherent to banality. I want something that pushes rather accepts whatever action may come.
