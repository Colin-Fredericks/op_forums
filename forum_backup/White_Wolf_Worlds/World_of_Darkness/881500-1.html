                                                    Censorship in the US in the late 80s/early 90s is kind of impossible to explain, because it was a complete mess. There was little inherent logic to it; even in more "regulated" industries like the music business (see: the PMRC). Nobody wanted to be the next circus paraded in front of Congress and forced to hammer out some sort of weird self-regulation agreement to keep moral-panic-happy censorship movements off their backs. Slapping a label on your "worst" offenders - pretty much regardless of how you defined them - was the general compromise. If you had some way of saying, "this is our most 'view discretion advised,'" material, it generally shielded you (and your distributors) from moral-panic backlash (when a kid decides he's actually a vampire and uses that to justify killing people VtM is the first to blame; and yes that did happen). To a large extent, nobody really paid enough attention to where a given company set its lines as long as it was setting lines at all.

                                                    Like I said, it was a complete mess.

                                                    The fact that putting stickers that say, "adult material," on your product is like say, "Hey kids, buy me!" was something the entertainment industry seemed just fine with as a whole, and completely lost on the, "don't sell stuff to our kids we don't like," movements.
