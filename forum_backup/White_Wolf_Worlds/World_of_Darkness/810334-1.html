                                        I agree with this. While I've never been the biggest fan of CoD/nWoD, there are books among its lines that I do recommend even for purely WoD groups because they contain useful and interesting things. Mirrors is one of them.

                                        Originally posted by Gryffon15 View Post
                                        I think it would be a interesting idea that before the veil goes down for everyone you could start by having an X-files-esqe story, where stuff like project twilight and FBI hunters go into full conspiracy mode to try and weaponise the night folk and keep them from public eye as long as possible and then have humaniy freak out.

                                        Yeah Gehenna and the other ToJ books did have plenty of good pieces in them. The aforementioned Crucible of God is my favorite scenario partly because the setup which is brilliant and works wonderfully in any chronicle* but more because of how a lot of it is up to the players and focused on their survival and their struggles. The Antes are off doing their own thing and the groups not being railroaded into some greater plot, they're just trying to survive. Minus the Withering, the return of the Antes and the rest of the Gehenna-based stuff, it would make a great jumping off point for breaking the Masquerade from the Vampire PoV.

                                        Other splats are a little trickery for various reasons though I am reminded of Dark Ages: Darkening Sky. For those unfamiliar with it, it was an adventure book for the five Dark Ages games, with a standalone adventure for each line, all focused on an eclipse that happened at the time with all kinds of crazy events happening around it. In the Inquisitor adventure, the eclipse herald several days were the veil over the supernatural was gone and the various denizens of the World of Darkness could not hide themselves from mortals as they had.

                                        Something like that could deal with those splats that have a built-in masquerade like Werewolves and Delirium.

                                        *Though I do admit I like Nightshade's opening just a little more for the sheer awesomeness and WTF-ness of fighting monstrous, frenzying body of [Tzimisce] in New York City. If you're gonna end the world as we know it, go big or go home.

                                        Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. They're indomitable. Indomitable.

                                                As a starting point for homebrewing a post-Masquerade chronicle, I'd suggest putting some thought into precisely what influence the various supernatural factions had in whichever governments are going to be significant to the story on the day that the Masquerade broke. Some US Senators basically inherit their seat in the US Legislature from their fathers in real life, and many of them serve for decades. How many of them are blood-bound, and to whom? How many Cabinet members? For that matter, how many lobbyists? Does that influence come out in the open when the Masquerade breaks, or in the age of dark money and unaccountable SuperPACs, does trying to keep one's retainers and allies a protected secret constitute a second Masquerade?
