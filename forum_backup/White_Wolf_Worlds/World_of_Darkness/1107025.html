    What if the Technocracy had Never Existed?

    06-23-2017, 11:49 AM
    I don't really know a lot about Mage's metaplot, but for the purposes of one my summer chronicles, I'm rewriting how cWoD history unfolded. One of my necessary assumptions is that the Technocracy nor it's precursors (the Order of Reason, right?) were ever founded. Thus, I have the following questions:

    Is it necessary, for human technological development, to have the Order of Reason or the Technocracy? Why not say humans just discovered the scientific method as an reaction to the Dark Ages and its creatures (vampires, werewolves, mages etc.)?

    The council of traditions would never be created, right?

    What other events and developments could be entailed by that?

      A lot of this depends on just how influential you think the Technocracy and Traditions are. History, the way Mage tells it, is very closely tied to the spread of the Technocracy. It is not specifically necessary for the Technocracy to exist for the scientific method to arise, but would it be as effective without their backing? Depending on how flexible reality is (and I go with the interpretation that it's really flexible) they are responsible for much of modern technology. Even if they aren't it's hard to say they weren't integral to its spread. I'd say that without some major faction trying to unify everyone in a single paradigm the world would be more balkanized, magically.

      Which is not to say that other stabilizing or conquering groups couldn't have arisen. The Order of Hermes was in a position to spread their ideas to the world if the Order of Reason didn't, for instance. Not many of them would push a technological paradigm, though.

          The scientific method was a construct of the Order of Reason designed to make reality a lot more stable and to bring the power of the awakened into the hands of the unawakened. Because reality in Ascension is fluid, nothing is discovered so much as created and enforced. Without the order of reason it's possible that a construct of belief similar in essence to the scientific method would come about, but without the strength of numbers and organisation that the assembled Order of Reason would have provided, there is no real way it would become the prevailing paradigm, at best it would approach another craft, perhaps a disparate form within a larger whole like the Order of Hermes of the Solificati, at worse it would be a niche idea completely void of all the trappings we associate with technology today.

          The Order of Reason was assembled primarily as a way to combat the threat of the Order of Hermes and their increasing reaches of power and tyranny. Without the Order of Reason, the Hermetics would be able to continue rising unopposed. In this alternative history it's possible that other forces would try to assemble to combat this group, otherwise the future would be very Hermetic, with awakened magic being an elite art that only the most learned and fearsom of mages would even be allowed to consider. Power would define control.

          In effect, the Hermetics would become all the worst aspects of the Technocracy, and what's worse is that as their arts have been around much longer than Science, they may not ever need to deal with paradox.

                  Originally posted by Primal Flame View Post
                  Is it necessary, for human technological development, to have the Order of Reason or the Technocracy? Why not say humans just discovered the scientific method as an reaction to the Dark Ages and its creatures (vampires, werewolves, mages etc.)?

                  The council of traditions would never be created, right?
                  The over all rule of thumb for the entire WoD back in the day was that supernaturals don't control or direct human culture and development so much as they latch on to it and exploit it for their own purposes. One of Mage's main themes was always the problem of hubris among the Awakened, and part of that was the Technocracy's belief that they were responsible for all of human technological development.

                  The Scientific Method goes back as far as Aristotle, if not further.

                  And, yes, without the threat of the Order of Reason, then the Council of Traditions, and even some of the Traditions themselves, would never have formed.

                  If I had to guess, the mundane world would largely continue to look a lot like it does in real life, just a little darker and grittier owing to the various supernatural influences in general (dark spirits, the sense that there are bad things and conspiracies in the shadows, an increased level of xenophobia and somewhat justified paranoia on the part of everyone, a somewhat starker divide between the realms of control and anarchy, as well as between mainstream culture and counter culture).

                  From a mystical standpoint, instead of Traditions and an Ascension War, you've probably got a bunch of small fellowships/cabals/conspiracies all competing with each other to protect their own turf and advance their own agendas, sometimes working together against mutual threats like the Nephandi or the like. Groups like the Order of Hermes (or perhaps just a few of their Houses if the group collapsed in some sort of civil war infighting at some point in the past), the Old Faith, the Wu Lung, the Knights Templar, the Children of Knowledge, the Joybringers, the Bata'a, an independent Syndicate that looks more like the Hermetic House Fortunae, and so on. This is probably the sort of setting where the Hollow Ones would shine and be a good default starting place for PCs.

                  What is tolerance? It is the consequence of humanity. We are all formed of frailty and error; let us pardon reciprocally each other's folly. That is the first law of nature.
                  Voltaire, "Tolerance" (1764)

                                                  If science developed without the guiding hand of the OoR, then it's likely to be the product of one or more unenlightened societies that originally formed arround the ideas of ancient philosophers and improved uppon them with pure human ingenuity.
