                                    I would add that the Union has a f few tricks at its disposal to blur the lines between “what Enlightened Scientists can do” and “what the Masses can do”:

                                    1. Devices that feature Adjustments (Coincidental Effects) can be used by anyone who accepts the general Focus; and as the opening paragraphs concerning Technocratic Reality Zones in M20 point out, that's practically everyone where Technocratic Reality is concerned. Note that the first part of the “Technocratic Reality Zone” entry in M20 points out that Enlightened Science has the Coincidental Edge — meaning that a number of Effects that would be considered Vulgar if performed by others are considered Coincidental when performed using Technocratic Practices.

                                    2. Devices that feature Procedures (Vulgar Effects) can be used by Extraordinary Citizens. Extraordinary Citizens are a subset of the Masses, and it's somewhat unclear where the line between “Ordinary Citizens” and “Extraordinary Citizens” lies, in-setting; but it appears to have something to do with how steeped they are in the metaphysics: it could be argued that Extraordinary Citizens are individuals with enough Enlightened Will to have a Focus the way Enlightened Scientists do, even of they lack the discernable Genius and Enlightenment that Enlightened Scientists display. (Terminology is a little muddy here, as “Enlightenment” — the term Technocrats use as a substitute for “Arete” — isn't the same as the “Enlightened” part of “Enlightened Will” for this purpose.)

                                    3. A Technocrat using Management and Human Resources can work an Adjustment through the activities of a team of Ordinary Citizens. The Scientist needs to be directing the activities (though not necessarily micromanaging it); but the people being directed, or outsiders observing them, could easily come to the conclusion that they're the ones working the Effect, through their teamwork. And again, there's the Coincidental Edge, making a wider range of Effects count as Adjustments that thus can be performed through Management and Human Resources.

                                    4. Extraordinary Citizens can also learn techno-sorcery, picking up Paths that let them work the equivalent of individual Effects without needing Devices or the direction of Enlightened Scientists to make it possible. And recall that the line between “Ordinary Citizen” and “Extraordinary Citizen” is blurry.

                                    Of course, this is mainly most relevant in the World of Darkness. As mentioned above, there's a very real possibility that the metaphysics of Magick simply don't work in the Chronicles of Darkness, rendering this whole issue moot.

                                    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                Just to clarify, my points are:

                                                1. A piece of technology "really works" when it only requires the correct action. Understanding or buy in aren't necessary to operate it. We can summarize a test as:
                                                • The right set of actions performed in the right conditions will work
                                                • Knowledge isn't required
                                                • Belief isn't required
                                                • Knowledge is possible without belief
                                                • Belief is possible without knowledge
                                                • If operation is highly complex, technical qualification can be required
                                                • Technical qualification can be objective and independently tested

                                                2. The theory of "Mages are just really smart" (which isn't exclusive to the Union or universal in the Union, it's just their party line) demand that their creations (Devices or Instruments) "really work". It doesn't mean there can't be pieces whose operation really is that complex, but at some level they'd really work.

                                                        To probably no one's surprise, Mage actually isn't clear about the exact nature of an Extraordinary Citizen. You speak about them as if that was a metaphysically distinct category; but is it?

                                                        What's the difference between a hedge magician (such as those described in Hunters Hunted 2) and a sorcerer (as described in the various Sorcerer books)? The former are described as perfectly ordinary people who have stumbled across a bit of magic that they don't really understand; the latter are sometimes described as being somehow vaguely set apart from ordinary people. Especially after Sorcerer Revised added mechanical complexity in the form of Aspects to its Paths, it might be possible to argue that HH2 hedge magicians and sorcerers are two different things, practicing two similar but distinct arts that just do happen to resemble each other, and that hedge wizardry is something that anyone can pick up whereas sorcery is only available to a select few who have some undefinable quality that lets them use it. But it's equally possible that hedge wizardry and sorcery are the same thing; that the hedge wizardry of HH2 is a subset of the sorcery of M20 Sorcerer that preselects a Focus (along essentially Hermetic lines) and makes assumptions about how the Paths will be used; assumptions that M20 Sorcerer doesn't make. If so, then either the hedge magicians of HH2 are unknowingly special, or the sorcerers of M20 Sorcerer aren't special.

                                                        I mentioned before that, in my view, an Extraordinary Citizen has a Focus while an “Ordinary Citizen” doesn't. But while that might sound like a sharp distinction, it may actually just be a difference of degree rather than a difference of type. How do we know that your Specialized Examiner doesn't qualify as an Extraordinary Citizen by virtue of being a Specialized Examiner? Maybe the technical know-how that your engineer has defines a Focus; so, far from being a neutral observer who can objectively draw a line between science and techno-sorcery, by virtue of being an engineer he is definitionally already an Extraordinary Citizen. Techno-sorcery is merely a game construct for representing an Extraordinary Citizen who has learned a few secrets that are ahead of his time. And anyone who puts in the effort to learn that stuff will end up becoming a techno-sorcerer too (or perhaps the “hedge magician” counterpart to a techno-sorcerer).
