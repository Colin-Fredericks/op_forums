    Consequences vs. Fun (Emerald City Chronicles Sabbat players stay out)

    03-13-2014, 05:09 AM
    So I'm looking for some general advice here, and maybe some stories of your own. When is it right to enforce the logical consequences of your players' really stupid decisions (i.e. massive character death or often worse) and when is it better to give them false outs of their situations? I usually come down harder on the side of consequences, but lately have been finding myself giving way more easy outs than I normally do, and think my game is suffering for it.

    To put this in context, I'm the Head Storyteller of a Sabbat LARP for about 15-20 regular players. When I started this game, I put a bunch of plot landmines around the city that would activate when the PCs looked into them. One was a Hive of Black Spiral Dancers who were there to be potential allies against the problems they were having with Wendigo and Red Talons coming down from the mountains to harass them, or at least a neutral force (you do your thing, we'll do ours, no need for conflict) based on how the PCs dealt with them. This is a reasonably high powered game (average xp level is around 300-500 with a couple of 7th gens in play) so I set this up as a Rank 5 Hive with commensurately powerful inhabitants. The Hive was respectful and generally neutral at every turn, even offering to work as "contractors" with the PCs if/when situations arose that needed them. However the PCs were having none of it, and from the get-go were determined to wipe out the Hive, despite warnings from all their allies many times saying this was a bad idea, and even going so far as to attempt to enlist the aid of the Gaian Garou against the Spirals (a far less likely alliance).

    When it became clear that the inhabitants of this city were going to go to war here, I was left with the problem of what do I do? I had built the NPCs at that Hive, including the Rank 5 Silver Spiral Grand Elder and knew that if they went as they were, they would all just be slaughtered (Spirit Knife attacks from the Umbra are a bitch). What is more, due to a traitor the Spirals knew they were coming and had time to prepare. It was a rather contested ST meeting that week as we had this debate, but eventually came down on the side of throwing them a bone in the form of a magical spike put in their path by Pentex (who were trying to get access to that lovely Tainted Cairn) that would summon a localized Avatar storm wiping out all the Spirals who were in the Umbra at the time it was activated, forcing them to fight in the material plane, wiping out 2/3 of their number, and removing all their spirit allies, thus letting the PCs have their victory, with only one casualty (and only that against the final boss Rank 5 because Paws of the Newborn Cub is stupid OP, and even worse in W20 when they changed the dif to current Willpower rather than Stamina +3).

    Realistically they should have lost this fight and most of them died. I caved because everyone was having fun and they really wanted to go to war on someone, and unfortunately were dead set on this Hive as their first target. This is not my normal play style, which usually comes down like a sack of bricks on anyone being stupid and slaps them in the face with all the consequences of their stupid choices. It kind of made me feel dirty.

    Anyway, this (as well as a few other fudged consequences) resulted in a false sense of confidence on the part of the PCs and it's starting to show. They are really believing themselves invincible and I greatly want to disavow them of this notion. How do I correct this problem without it being a godhammer? Or is a godhammer mandated in this situation? I have a few in my back pocket available if need be (never leave home without at least one in your pocket). What scale of loss should I be going for? I think someone needs to die (we've yet to see any final PC death in this game; the one who died in the werewolf attack successfully managed to become a Wraith under our rather difficult system for doing so and was recently reinserted in a new body and is back to her old self again), but is even one death enough here?

    The current system I have to capitalize on here is a Pander (actually the childe of Joseph Pander) has managed to acquire Countermagick. He was being watched by an elder Tremere for other reasons and was overheard speaking of a plan to spread his knowledge far and wide across the Sabbat, teaching as many as he could and enlisting others to do the same. Obviously the Tremere could not abide this. So the elder Tremere arranged a covert mission to get a sample of this young Pander's Blood by impersonating his sire. This was done successfully, but the question is, now what? Via Blood Walk (and using the blood sample to embrace a childe, then bloodwalking that childe for those of you with a very literal interpretation of Blood Walk), they now have his True Name, and under the rules for True Names presented in Blood Magic: Secrets of Thaumaturgy, this means they can hit him with any magical effect at any range. Now the smart thing for them to do is just kill him. They could do it easily by repeatedly hitting him with some mystically damaging effect while he can't resist in any way. However, once again, my staff is trying to convince me to cave and run some elaborate plan that gives him an easy out of his situation and lets him win via a lost art that can change his True Name while the Tremere prep their master plan to make him Wight via Return of the Heart rather than just killing him. I'm personally torn again, because while I want to give the character a cool end if possible, he really should die from this.

    So TL;DR: What is the right balance between enforcing the logical consequences of the world and keeping the game fun for everyone involved (because however well the player takes it, character death sucks)? It's also especially problematic given that this is a LARP where each character is equally important and just pieces in a grander story rather than a tabletop where the PCs are the main characters of their own story.

    Any thoughts or insights or personal anecdotes that might help me with this one?

      This is a tough situation, and one I've found myself in on a few occasions. Often times it comes down to picking your battles and finding a middle solution that nobody is really happy with but nobody is unfairly burned by. As far as this specific scenario: I'm never in favor of killing a character by fiat, which is what the collect-call Tremere gank would amount to. The Return of the Heart tactic is an interesting and much more thematic idea, which I'd personally be more inclined towards as long you make sure you stand firm on it after giving a reprieve on the True Name.

      As to their political situation, in the sudden absence of the Spiral hive they find themselves stuck between Pentex and the Garou. They owe Pentex for saving their bacon with the Spirals, and if they suddenly get recalcitrant about returning that favor then Pentex isn't going to want to help them much when the Garou decide to come down on them now that their attention isn't divided between the Leeches and the BSD's. If they do play ball with Pentex then the Garou won't be as much of a problem, but then Pentex will really have them over a barrel. If they want to go to town with Pentex the company has the ability to come at them obliquely with influences to make their unlives difficult in addition to pulling out whatever Bane/Fomori/Bullshit needs to be plucked from an obscure supplement or constructed from whole cloth to put them on an even footing with the Elder Sabbat.

                  I would have let the BSDs wipe them out, or at least giving them a really hard lesson. It might have let some of the players down, and some might have left the game, but all in all it would have been a valuable lesson for the players as well as their characters.

                  I once had a player (in a tabletop RPG game) who had a Rank 4 Qualmi who took on a legendary Rank 6 badass in an attempt to gain his fifth Rank, even though he was warned both by other NPCs and several Spirits that he would definitely lose the fight, and that the elder Qualmi would kill him. He did it anyway, fought a tense and brutal fight, and was killed in a quite bloody manner. I did not find that he deserved to survive it, and in the end his player agreed. That fight is still something that comes up as a memorable experience, and also as a reminder to keep one's head, so to speak. It might not compare well to wiping out an entire LARP group, but in your case I would have made the same decision for sure. Stupidly attackin a powerful enemy for no reason other than arrogance should be rewarded with a foul fate.

                          I'd say actions always have to have consequences but that it's possible to soften the blow a bit. It feels like the Spiral blow was over-softened and lost its punch.

                          Has it emerged that the Black Spirals in the Umbra were killed? Or is that behind the ST veil. If so, I'd change the outcome. Have the 2/3rds of the Spirals who were hit by the Avatar storm be mostly scattered rather than destroyed, and have them find their way back from the Umbra in dribs and drabs. This gives you the option of having long term consequences for their actions that aren't a larp-wipe. The returning Spirals will be inclined to hold a grudge - and less inclined to face them in a straight up battle. Hit and run would give you the option of having the limited PC death consequences that you're looking for.

                          As for the Tremere, doomstrike from afar is pretty awful to bestow on someone. I'd take a slightly different tack - the Tremere mostly want to discredit him if at all possible to discourage others from attempting to duplicate his success in learning Countermagick. With the ability to work at range and effectively undetectably, the Tremere have their leisure to twist and derail his reputation. While it falls into the same pitfalls as using any hand-forcing discipline (Dominate, Presence etc) in Larp, mechanically something like path of corruption/blood's curse/curses/father's vengeance would all be possibilities for softer discrediting vengeances that would create a better story for the character to go out in. Mix/match as desired.

                          Hell, they could have him hate everyone he's had vinculum with, be despised by all, addicted to sabbat-unacceptable behaviour and cause him to frenzy at the worst possible moment. Isolating him and seeing can they tip the Sabbat into killing him themselves basically.
