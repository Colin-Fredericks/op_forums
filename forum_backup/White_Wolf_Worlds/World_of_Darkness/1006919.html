                          The tone and themes of your chronicle should also influence what is possible or how easy it is to achieve. Time travel that doesn't involve absurd degrees of risk is a great way to rob a story of its weight. Don't plan on having a "who's the secret Nephandi" plot if you allow some combination of Mind/Time/Spirit/Prime/Entropy to sense evil. Right now I'm running a pulp WW2 game where a character made a bunch of APCs able to burrow through the earth. In a modern, gritty game about escaping the NWO, should that be possible? Technically yes, but as an ST I'd ask players to stick to ideas not featured in Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles.

                              Also, it is stated quite clearly in most of the books. Especially when rotes are described. It is clear that those rotes are paradigm dependent. That does not mean that the effects are not possible in other paradigms that the one described by the practice of a specific rote. However, it does imply that a specific rote is linked to a specific practice. Moreover, several rotes have drawbacks or limitations (some spell only applies on women, some other needs you to cut of your arm or eat a living heart,...). Mage: the Ascension (as opposed to the Awakening) falls within the post-modern philosophy which emphasizes the personalization and the subjective construction of reality. Postmodern philosophers deny that an objective reality exist. This is the core of the game, so putting it aside is misunderstanding the game in my opinion.

                              That was to clarify my point: Something being game-mechanically possible should not necessary being feasible by any Mage since his practice depends on his own specific belief and paradigm.

                              That said, if your Mage can find a way to fold a specific effect in his paradigm, it's okay of course.
