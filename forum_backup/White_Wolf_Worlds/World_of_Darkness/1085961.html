    System for healing non-magical diseases and illnesses (whether physical or mental)?

    04-30-2017, 08:37 PM
    Specifically I am interested in the possibility of, and means by which a mage would go about healing diseases of non-magical origin of whatever kind, whether caused by bacteria, viruses, genetic abnormalities, whatever: AIDS/HIV, ebola, bubonic plague, Crohn's, cancer (any/all cancer), Rhett syndrome (look it up and be horrified), Parkinson's, ALS, Alzheimer's, etc. On the mental side, I'd like to know how to cure things like PTSD, phobias, bipolar disorder, and other derangements of non-magical origin such as people acquire and live with in the real world. For the purposes of this conversation, let us assume the truth of the mind/body distinction.

    Diseases/Illnesses of the "Body"

    I am familiar with the oft-cited chart regarding "Toxin"-rating and what have you, which is used to determine the game system effects of being inflicted with one or more of these conditions, and certainly I would imagine that any system that involves utterly ridding a person's body of such a diseasewould ideally involve that chart (because it represents the authors' creation of a metric by which to gauge the relative "badness" of each malady)--but I have yet to find concrete system for permanently treating these things and ridding them from the patient for good. For reference, I have looked at every edition of the main core book (first edition through M20, as well as Sorcerer's Crusade; though admittedly I skipped Dark Ages: Mage on the grounds that its magic system is so different from the other editions that it probably wouldn't be instructive or useful to me); both editions of the Verbena tradition book (the Rev. edition of which I believe has one the best and deepest treatment of the systems associated with the use of Life for various purposes including healing and permanently altering a subject in a variety of ways); The Guide to the Traditions; The Guide to the Technocracy; both editions of the Progenitors convention book; The Book of Shadows; Master's of the Arts; and How Do You Do That?. And for all that searching I've have little or nothing. Zero. Nada. At best I know it's tough, and probably involves an extended roll (maybe?), and depending on how you read different sources together, I could make arguments of various strengths for different diseases potentially being curable at Life 2, Life 3, or Life 4 depending on a variety of factors ranging from how "bad" they are, to what causes them--e.g., I would potentially argue that curing a bacterial infection is easier than curing a genetic defect, in that curing a bacterial infection merely requires ridding the host of the harmful microorganisms in question, whereas a genetic disease like Rhett or Huntington's may well constitute a permanent alteration to someone's DNA/pattern.

    And yet there's no guidance on how long it would take to cure someone of any one these. Sure something things are gimmees: structural problems for instance. Verbena (Rev.) talks about correcting birth defects as being treated as a "permanent enhancement" and refers the reader to that system and section of that book accordingly. With that in mind, I would obviously modify that stated system such that, in the instance of correcting a congenital defect, I would not require the beneficiary of the healing/modification to endure a permanent point of paradox because, ultimately, there is nothing inherently vulgar about not having a cleft palate, or polydactyly, or not having a bicuspid aorta (we're supposed to have tri-cuspid aortic valves). These are structural alterations not dissimilar from adding fangs, a tail, or claws... except that you're going from one state of coincidental existence to another (rather than turning from a human into a wombat-inspired crinos for or whatever).

    But can a Mage with Life 3 or 4 just wave their hand and cure Parkinson's? or AIDS/HIV? I am reminded of the scene from Star Trek IV when they're in the hospital and Dr. Bones is continually confronted with situations in which he basically has a pill or relatively quick fix for everything. Without getting into deeper meta-gaming discussions about, "well, if they can just wave their hand, why don't they blah blah blah", what's a fair, and internally systematically consistent way of handling these sorts of workings?

    Diseases/Illnesses of the "Mind"

    The same question goes for various levels of Mind (and potentially Life as well I suppose), and the means by which we could cure people of PTSD (which specifically M20 says is possible, though hints at it being not easy), being bipolar schizophrenic, or any one of a number of derangements? Keep in mind I'm not trying to heal Malkavians, metises, Silver Fangs, other Garou in harano--nobody who has a mental, psychological, or emotional condition by dint of a supernatural force acting upon them (such as a curse or whatever). I'm talking about conditions such as the ones mentioned that occur naturally, via the processes and mechanism by which such things typically occur.

    Any help of guidance people would like to provide would be helpful--though I would point out that I'm not looking for avoidances--i.e., responses to the question that avoid the problem presented by declaring that perhaps the question is invalid in the first place because these games don't have to/shouldn't be reduced down to dice/rolling/whate-have-you. I can appreciate that some people have a jolly ol' time coming up with the how hard something is to do on the fly, with every instance of a thing being different to the last according to the narrative needs of the particular situation--but for the purposes of this quandry, we should assume that that approach has been examined, considered, and specifically rejected in favor of having a concrete system that operates in a consistent manner regardless of its tendency to either enhance or diffuse the drama of a given story.

      The major reason that mages can't (in the Modern Era) go around healing people willy-nilly is because of Paradox and Consensus. While people "want" to be cured/healed, Reality(tm) is still structured around much of their negativity and self-deceit. Also, it's important to keep in mind that in the WoD, things are darker/bleaker than in our Real World(tm). While many/most(?) STs likely just use the real-world elements as-is, unlike in our world the WoD has *active* agents (mages, spirits, etc.) who *promote* disease/pain/suffering. HIV -- just to use that as an example -- isn't "simply" HIV in the WoD -- it's a disease which has spirits (mostly Middle Umbral, but some who are Low Umbral and a few High Umbral as well) that *support* its existence, and take active steps (when and where they can, which isn't always but often enough) to perpetuate the disease (having it adapt/resist treatment in a way that is beyond what occurs in real life). Same goes for a number of other things, too.

      A given mage with the right paradigm can heal any given *individual* of, say, HIV or severe PTSD (using Life:3 for the former, and Mind:3-4 for the latter, depending on certain salient aspects), but said mage (or even group of mages) would be hard-pressed to heal a hospital ward of afflicted individuals, and finding an as-yet-"unaccepted" cure for those things on a global scale is to face Paradox, directly. Mages lose. Period. Only the gradual, slow shifting of Paradigm/Consensus would allow for such things to be more globally treated... and that element is tied to the very underpinnings of what Mage/the WoD is literally all about.

      (Sidenote: I've got a character I've played on-and-off for many years now -- since the old, original forum days of the 90s -- who has been trying to slowly work towards a cure for HIV via gradual Consensus-shifting and attacking "Paradox" via having multiple papers published by different doctors/research groups in hopes of shifting the world paradigm enough to allow is already-long-existing "cure" (Life:3, which for him is the Pillar -- Biometabolics:3) to be more globally accepted. It's been two decades, both in real life and in-game. Still working on it...)

      So, much like with shooting a fireball from their magic wand or hypnotizing a given individual with a pendant or crystal, any given mage *can* just "waive their hand", as it were, and do "x"; but just like with said fireball or mind control/alteration, Paradox may have something to say about it... and even if not in the short term, then in the long-run via Disbelief and other forces working to sustain the negative forces in play.

                              There's very little nitty-gritty when it comes to this, and there never was really relative to the different editions of WoD/Mage.                               Examples: a faith healer might be able to rid a person of HIV within just a few brief moments of prayer (and a good number of successes/Q and WP spent); said healing may be "permanent", of it might merely temporarily suppress the disease/infection until if/when Disbelief or looming Paradox comes crashing down (which is only determinable by ST judgement -- how well documented/"known" is the victim's having of the disease? -- how aware is Reality(tm) of his having it? How many successes did the healer roll (representing the amount of faith/divine power was channeled through to make the healing "stick")? All of those things are factors. Alternately, a more science-minded mage may treat the patient with various chemicals and/or natural derivatives over a course of months or even years (building successes gradually over a period of time). Coupled with the patient perhaps changing his life -- moving, not necessarily being near/in contact with people who know/knew of the infection, creating effectively a "new life" for himself -- the "spell" may take quite some time to "cast" (much longer than the few moments of the faith healer), but because of (P)aradigm, technique, and over-arcing factors related to Consensus, the curing of the particular patient may in fact be permanent and perhaps even without looming Paradox floating about.

                              The solutions you are looking for -- in the manner in which you're asking -- aren't really what Mage(tm) is founded on, both mechanically, metaphysically and narratively. There are ways and ways and ways again in which "x" can often be accomplished, and in different ways with different means that can affect time involved, magic (read: Spheres) involved, as well as how Paradoxical said workings can be. There is no single (or simple) answer to what you're asking. And that's why Mage(tm) is more "magic(k)al" than most other game systems/settings: half of it exists outside the context of "mundane rules".

                                              Generally, healing a simple cold would likely only take 2 successes (relative to healing a given uncomplicated wound); healing a systemically harmful but otherwise vulnerable disease (like HIV, which is dangerous within a host body but comparatively vulnerable relative to many diseases when outside of one) could take as little as, say, 3 successes for a same-night infection to 5-10+ for a years-long infected (and having developed AIDS) target; healing a target of genetically-prone disease (such as cancer which is intrinsic to their Pattern vs. being induced from environmental conditions) is actually more difficult, comparatively, as the "healing" requires permanently altering the target's genes/Life Pattern... or else the disease will simply recur after being cured (10+ successes for a "permanent" cure, though in that context Life:3/Entropy:4 can cut the number of successes required down as Entropy helps mitigate the likelihood of the cancer recurring whether innately or by external sources).

                                              Those are ballpark figures, and they don't go into the nuances of when "x" can simply be handled with a few successes vs. when they require 20+ -- along with some Willpower and Quintessence -- because there are simply too many bases to cover. ST judgement goes a long way here, as the specifics will vary from target-to-target.
