        It was the idea that carried through. In Ars Magica, it was considered totally okay to make a generalist mage who wasn't great at big spells but was very versatile. And it was okay to make up a mage who wanted to be good at a particular thing to exclusion of everything else. The system under discussion privileges one of these to the exclusion of the other, and, in fact, to want to be specialized was at one point called "power gaming". Which, it may be, but, it's in the sense of getting a LOT of this restricted types of power at the expense of generalism. And it's not "being a power gamer" to want to be the one and not the other. if that's not the character you want to play.

        And, since Sorcerers and Static Magic haven't been addressed head on in M20, especially and particularly when dealing with 1) PCs and 2) issues of having high levels of power, and the past history has been all over the place, I'm not poking that pile of steaming deer guts with a stick. I like this stick and I don't want Sorcerer goo all over it. However, in Ars Magica that was it was entirely possible to be both a static and a dynamic mage at the same time in a way similar to, but more Ascension-like than Awakenings "Real" Rotes and Arcana are/were. The Flambeau probably had some skill in Creo and Ignam, even if the majority of his ooomph was going to Pilum of Fire.

                                    Okay. I disagree. Mage can be about either or both and is probably about something somewhere in the middle. Saying "You get 6 dots and you can only go up to 3, so you have to be a generalist" is not really the point. I can have a very tight character concept and take 2 Spheres at 3 dots. Or I can be Mr. Looky-loo and take 6 different Spheres at 1. Or I can do 3 at 2. Or 3/2/1. Or 2/2/1/1. Or 2/1/1/1/1. Or add some with Freebies and Flaws. None of that means the game promotes a particular style. It doesn't. And it doesn't make the specialist weaker. There are creativity workarounds to many of the pitfalls of not having "Sphere X" or, even just not trying to do everything, particularly the things you are not good at (as in, "Why I am a member of a cabal") A very very strong starting cabal concept could be 4 Mages with a 3/3 allocation of Spheres and no overlap (just ditch Entropy as everything it can do, some other Sphere can do as well and usually better). Sure, they won't be coordinating effects, but the game actually DOES discourage that with crunch, so it's not a great loss.

                                    In fact, in our game right now, most of the characters are specialists in a narrow range of things with a few generalists to round things out. We have a Forces 4/Prime 4 Hermetic who is also our Middle Umbra Specialist. A Mind/Time Cultist who has picked up Correspondence (so he's effectively everywhere). A Matter/Life Verbena. We're playtesting a different version of Data and a Matter Alternate Sphere "Machine" and we have one character with both of those. The party "leader" is a NWO is the generalist who overlaps with pretty much everyone else a bit and specializes in Entropy (which is slightly different in that is less of an overlap Sphere than it is about articulating and disarticulating relationships between different Spheres/aspects of reality than it is a hash of duplicating abilities from other Spheres in a herky-jerky stepwise fashion). We've added in a few new players who plug any gaps and play well with the specialists in their areas. And it works REALLY well. We've been playing this game for over two years now and are in the middle of the climatic ending.
