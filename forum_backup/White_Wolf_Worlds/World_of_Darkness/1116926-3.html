So, you seen it an appealing thing that certain mage groups are "obsessed with questioning rigid social norms, taboos etc...", but you were uncomfortable with the M20 corebook giving attention to "playing the others"? Could you extrapolate on that and what you consider "exploring in a mature way" the book fallen to convey, if you don't mind, because I'd rather seen it as a good thing, that it spoke about those things in the context of rpgs and mysticism and I'd like to understand why you found it bothering?

    Why do I like this? It makes the true origins of everything murky. It makes people who think Abrahamic religions are bullshit more comfortable inhabiting the world; it isn't all built on something they've rejected. It leaves room for outside perspectives. Have the myths be just that; myths. A murderer was the first Vampire; was he "Caine"? Who knows! Demons claim that he was, but their memories aren't exactly cast-iron. Maybe Caine inspired Cain. Maybe Cain was the origin of Caine. Maybe God actually exists! Or maybe he's just a potent spirit, like all human gods. It also gives legitimacy to Werewolves (and Fera) who refuse their role in existence; they aren't fighting reality, they're fighting a system.
    I'm staunchly not a Christian, or a religious person, but I never found it particularly irking. As you said, it's a myth. That it is an Abrahamic myth is totally fine and inevitable, since we're speaking about Western vampires, who grew up in a western culture and a lot of them during a time when Christianity was the truth. However, in the wast universe of WoD, I think even Caine doesn't know what cursed him, if there's a Caine and if he was cursed. That there's a thing as True faith and religious paraphernalia works for people with it? I'm okay with that, it's part of the whole vampire thing. I think too much people take the Book of Nod and other in-game references and mythology at face value.

                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                To summarize the issue, the problem with making Garou wrong about the Spirit World is that it guts them of all themes and story.

                The problem with doing the same to Demon is that it guts them of all their themes and story.
                Not wrong. Just not objectively, provably right.

                RAW WoD: "Gaia exists and has given me a purpose in life; fulfilling that purpose is a good thing. What I'm doing is right and will save the world from the Wyrm's madness."

                My WoD: "[Same]" However someone with an understanding of Spirits could point out that Spirits are very susceptible to being molded by group belief, and therefore the words of spirits are about as reliable as religious texts or oral tradition; unreliable, fallable and possibly deceitful people have altered the message at different stages. The Spirit Mage would say the same to a Setite who believed the Setite origin myth, or to a Demon who thinks she was the Angel associated with the concept of Redemption at the start of time.

                "Clara, I really do respect you, but you slumbered in that spiritual prison for aeons... don't you think that the power of Judeo-Abrahamic belief may have warped your memories as you slumbered? I'm not saying that it DEFINITELY didn't happen... but you shouldn't stake your existence on it."

                Memories of Vampires? Well when you're the only person who remembers an event 1000 years ago, gradually your own biases and wishful thinking creep in. Two Methesulahs who were both present at an event might have wildly different recollections; they've had centuries to lie to themselves in the meantime. The more you look at memory IRL the more you realise it's... mutable. We edit and tweak our memories regularly, we tell ourselves stories, and it's only social interaction with others who share those memories ("Nanny's bathroom was avacado!" "No, it was blue, ask your uncles") that allows us to maintain something cohesive. Ancient Vampires lack that, and have centuries more to remember than human minds are built to remember.

                SO at my table everything is mysterious, and all beliefs have a kernel of truth but I will not give an OBJECTIVE "you are correct" to anyone. One of my Malkavians has "ancestor blood", manifestations of the Malkavians who have died in her "bloodline", back many Generations... are they real? Are they part of Malkav's madness? Are they copies Malkav made inside the Madness Network to keep himself company? Are they real Malkavian spirits hanging around in the network? There are no Objective answers to these questions, and that's good. Everything is ambiguous.

                Maybe your Werewolves and your Demons are right. Maybe they're wrong. Maybe it doesn't matter whether they're right or wrong, or maybe it's the most important thing in the universe. Regardless, it is great for the table as it allows more STUFF to exist, as there isn't some Grand Cosmology that is 100% definitely behind the scenes.
