They spit and rant about their superiority to human beings, make a show of disdaining even the practice of keeping ghouls, but then they quietly adhere to their own version of the Masquerade. Sure, they want to go back to the dark ages, where vampires ruled openly over the Kine, but they're not really rushing to make that happen, in great part because they realize it's a pipe-dream - the burning times changed everything, and even if the Sabbat doesn't want to admit it, they can't deny it.

A single neonate vampire could easily destroy all of humanity by simple virtue of the fact that it can replicate near constantly with huge ease and enslave others ridiculously easily through use of his blood.     What I imagine generally keeps humanity from being gang-banged by an orgy of supernatural forces is that if any supernatural race ever actually did do something like this, they would be immediately targeted by all the other supernaturals.

    *Changelings are weak enough that humanity is a serious threat to them.

            Originally posted by RebelWithoutAClan View Post
            A single neonate vampire can be neutralized by a pool-cue snapped in two.
            It annoys me to no end to continually see staking spouted as a vampire weakness.

            If you try to stake a vampire, you will die.

            Do you have any idea just how hard it is to stake a vampire? It's a difficulty 9 roll on which you need to get no less than 5 successes, while also dealing a minimum of three levels of damage (vs a creature that can soak said damage with its stamina).

            Even spending willpower, the odds of succeeding that roll are so astonishingly low that it should never, ever be used in combat unless you have already lost the fight and are throwing a hail mary.

            Consider the very highest dice pool a mortal could reach for that roll - think a mortal ghoul with 5 points in Celerity who's blood buffed himself to a six in dexterity and has a 5 in melee. That's 16 dice. If he spends willpower for an automatic success, his average roll will have 2.92 successes (an average of 3), which is two short. Note again that this is with willpower expenditure.

            In the event of a normal mortal doing it (scratch the 5 in celerity and the blood buffing), the max dice pool would be 10. With a willpower expenditure this would result in an average of 2.1 successes, 1.1 without it. Note that these are the maximum values achievable by mortals - the very highest they are capable of reaching and thus the values that will only be possessed by a handful out of the 7.5 billion out there. Even they suck at staking vamps.

            Even all of this is worthless if they don't get the three damage - which, given how much they've focused on melee and dexterity, is unlikely as they'll be lacking the strength for real damage (which they'll need to beat out the lethal damage soaking vampire).

            All this for a far lesser effect than what would happen to basically anything else if it got a wooden stick through the heart (which would be instant, unrelenting death). I frankly don't count "something that would kill literally everything else but will just paralyze you" to be a weakness. That it can be considered one is a testament to the resilience of the Cainites.

            Staking is NOT something to be done in combat. It's how you neutralize vampires who have already lost the fight. Literally every single other method of combat (including the laughably pathetic True Faith, or even just a kid with a slingshot shooting balls of grass) is more likely to net you a legitimate result than trying to stake the vampire (which will most likely end with you failing and promptly being drained dry).

                The First Tradition: Vampires are very resourceful and capable of working together - the Camarilla is proof of that, and again, it came into being as a direct result of the threat posed by mortals - yes, the Church, but not every Inquisitor had true faith.

                Consider the opening pages of the Guide to the Camarilla:

                It (the Camarilla) exists to protect its members from the surging seas of humanity, which by dint of sheer numbers could wipe most of the Kindred off the face of the earth. The Camarilla's single greatest creation, the Masquerade, exists for precisely this purpose. A veil of misdirection and falsehood, the Masquerade hides the very fact of Kindred existence from the mortal world. What humanity can't see, it can't kill, and thus the Kindred are safe. The fact that the necessity of preserving the Masquerade cuts down somewhat on the casualties Camarilla vampires inflict on the mortal population is meerly a byproduct of the need to preserve the illusion.
                There was no need to keep the neonates under control in the Dark Ages - they were already well controlled, which was why the Elders were able to sacrifice them.

                Neonate vs. The World: This would make a fantastic V:tM story, actually, although it reminds me a bit of the plot from later on in Twilight. A Sabbat neonate out somewhere in the south west slaughtering an entire small town and turning them into a roving army of bloodsuckers. The Camarilla and Sabbat would go into damage control mode faster than you can say "Martial Law."
                Problem is, an army of Neonates won't last long against an airstrike, a napalm bath, or hell, a couple of snipers with phosphorous tracer rounds that deal aggravated damage. It would be terrifying for a short time, until a military-level response was initiated. And we're not even considering the vampire authorities that would come down on them harder than the sun at high-noon.

                The old Vampire 2nd Edition Player's guide had rules for artillery and military grade support weapons. A 120mm tank gun is doing 30 dice of damage. A LAW rocket is doing 15 dice.

                There's a reason they didn't include stats for artillery weapons in the Revised edition Storyteller's Guidebook - if you're going up against Stinger anti-aircraft missiles as a vampire, something has gone seriously off kilter in the chronicle. Even a Methuselah of Mithras's stature was slammed into Torpor by the bombing of London during WW2.

                Lets not even get started on flame throwers.

                Staking: Staking isn't as difficult as you think. Check out V20, pg. 280, or V:tM Revised 213, Staking a vampire requires a single success at difficulty 9 and must score at least 3 health levels of damage. Certainly not easy (especially if the vampire is not restrained and can dodge), but not as impossible as to make it tactically impractical.

                Battle Royale: I think you're sorely overstating the power of a Neonate vampire. You're not going to be able to Dread-gaze a score of people before you're beaten into Torpor. Obtenebration probably has the best crowd-control options with shroud of night - Arms of the Abyss won't cut it I'm afraid. Shroud of Night only covers 10 feet - the vampire's best tactic would be to shroud themselves and then try to pick off the mortals as they ran.

                I'm hard pressed to actually find any other discipline at all that would be as effective as Obliteration against 20 people.
                • Presence is worthless - Awe isn't going to stop them as combat constitutes a dangerous situation.
                • Dread gaze and Enchantment are both single-target powers.
                • Dominate and Dementation are also single target. Obfuscate only helps if you weren't seen to begin with, and then only to escape - it isn't going to let you disappear from view at that level.
                • You're not going to be able to fend all of them off with 3 dots in Celerity.
                • Fortitude 3 might be enough to save you if all your taking is Bashing damage, but...
                • With that many attackers remember that your defensive maneuvers and attacks will be made at Difficulty 10 (+1 per opponent beyond the first to a max of 10). You'll probably be down into Torpor before you inflict much damage in return, no matter what your Disciplines are. Again, I'll give you Obtenebration level 2, but one power from a specialty discipline you'd be hard pressed to find outside the Sabbat doesn't equal an easy victory for your average Neonate against a mob of angry mortals.


                Elder in the Sun: An elder fighting in direct sunlight is taking 3 Health levels of damage a turn. He can soak this with Fortitude at difficulty 10. That's not to mention having to check for Rötschreck. Even with a decent path rating (say 7) that's a serious handicap on dice pools. He's not long for the world in this situation.

                            I guess I'm more trying to say that among vampires, their sense of superiority took a major blow following the Inquisition. They'd never admit it, of course, but you can tell by their actions that they aren't quite as assured of it deep down as they used to be. By in large they certainly consider themselves superior to humans, but that perception is riddled with cracks and fissures now..

                            I'm not so much arguing with you as putting the view of Vampiric superiority into context. I think I misunderstood where you were coming from with the comment about zombies.

                            The point I'm making about the zombie analogy is that humans are scary to vampires precisely because their intellect makes them dangerous. Humans are, in the real world, the apex predator because our brains allow us to incorporate and build upon all of nature's aggression. We don't have claws and teeth, so we make them out of stone, bronze, iron, steel, titanium, carbon nanotubes. We learn how to launch those teeth attached to axes and spears, or fire them from bows, crossbows, and guns so that we can bite our prey from safety. We dip our artificial teeth in the poison of tree frogs to make them deadly. We make our artificial teeth burn with fire.

                            We don't have thick hide, so we take the hide of our prey. We learn to cure it to make it harder and more durable and protective. We learn to beat bronze, iron and steel into artificial hide, or to make it from ceramics and high durability plastics to make it lighter and more resilient, to the point where it can resist bullets.

                            We set traps for our prey. We domesticate animals to help us hunt, like dogs or falcons, because they have senses and capabilities we don't.

                            To make a long story short, humans adapt. It's like the book I am Legend (well worth the read - the movie doesn't do it justice).
