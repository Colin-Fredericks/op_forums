    About redemption

    10-11-2016, 04:42 PM
    Hi !

    Here again for a metaphysical question. What happens to the inverted Avatar of a Nephandus ? I read the Book of Madness and found very few things on the matter. It states though that Nephandi could die but would be reincarnated into Widderslaints. Is there no possibility for redemption then ?

    As far as I understand it, there is none. But I find it quite odd. The World of Darkness is a decaying world, tainted by corruption and depression. But Mages are the symbol of hope of that world. This is also a mystical world where the notions of fate and freewill are debated on a daily basis. I find it hard to believe that no hope of redemption is possible even in the most extreme cases. The World of Darkness seems balanced to me. Far from mitigated, but balanced nonetheless. Evil is more evil than in our world, and good is more good. Redemption is a whole theme of many chronicles of the WoD, even official ones such as the Red Sign for exemple.

    The choice of falling to the demon is a choice of the human being. It changes the Avatar but, why would it be impossible that in a next life, a Widderslainte choses to betray his demon to make his Avatar whole again ? After all, nor the Avatar nor the Widderslainte had a word about it in the first place. Betrayings could go either way don't you think ? Before the question is asked, I'm not sure I'm even talking about an actual Nephandus, but rather talking about the next lives of said Nephandus.

    What do you think about it ?

                      It might be a very interesting story hook, dropping the possibility of a ritual that could reverse Nephandism. Or, better yet, a kind of Anti-Caul.

                      Consider the functional opposite of a Nephandus labyrinth, at center of which stands a door plated in gold. Light pours out when it opens, and anyone who enters is surrounded by a swarm of angels, resplendent in their finery. An uncorrupted Mage is promptly and gently escorted back out if they wander in - there is nothing more that this host can do to help the Mage on their journey to Ascension. But if a Nephandus walks (or is forced) inside, they are immediately confronted, and the door slams shut.

                      Just as with a Caul, the Nephandus faces a choice. Indeed, the angels make the choice transparent, so that there is no misunderstanding (for unlike the demonic Cauls, this Anti-Caul is honest and forthright about its purpose). The dark Mage may do one of three things. One, they may stay still, moving neither forward or backward. For so long as they don't move, the angels will wait. Two, the Nephandus turns to run away, at which point they are promptly destroyed on the spot, burned to atoms by holy fire. But Three, the Nephandus may move forward, and accept the angel's blessing. This is the choice the angels encourage, which the whole host beckoning with sweet words, with promises of redemption and renewal.

                      Should the Nephandus step forward, the angels will carry them up (at this point, while the angel's hands are gentle as a whisper, there is no escaping them; the choice has been made). They carry the Mage forward into blinding light. Divine fire burns their soul - for indeed, pain is a necessary part of becoming better - until the taint is boiled away. Their flesh is unraveled, and purged of infection. And their mind is taken apart by delicate, expert hands. Their consciousness is flooded by visions of nobility, virtue, love, and hope. In that moment, the Nephandus is driven sane, their broken heart, soul, and/or Avatar is put back together, the right way. This process, all told, can take some time, depending on the severity of the Nephandus's sins. The angels are not hasty, but treat each transgression in turn. If it takes five hours or five hundred years, they will keep the soul to the purifying flame, until the world-hating malice is no more.

                      And when their body has been reassembled, they are annointed with sweet-smelling oils, and dressed in fine clothing. A fine costume, befitting a refined and positive version of their Paradigm. Their hunger is sated by sweet fruit, and their thirst by rich milk or wine. And songs of hope and good-tidings are sung into their ear. And at last, with their work complete, they usher the newly restored Mage through the threshold, to rejoin the world and contribute to its goodness. While what exactly is "good" is up to the Mage's Paradigm, they nonetheless are driven towards Ascension, rather than Descent.

                      Or, so it is rumored...

                      Magestuck: The Ascension
