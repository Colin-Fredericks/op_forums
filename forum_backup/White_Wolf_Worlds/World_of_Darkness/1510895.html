    Have you ever ignored the 7 points of flaws limit?

    05-06-2023, 02:18 PM
    Do you go RAW, tweak it a bit or ignore it?
    I tend to say you can take every flaw in the book if you like, but you'll only get 7 extra freebie points out of it. Although the temptation to just get, like, 100 freebie points by continuing to add flaws until I have all the dots I need-want is strong; I would create the most skilled and the single most cursed person on the planet. Juggling an infinite amount of flaws and merits is a headache though, so the limit is perfectly understandable.

          I've done the "max 7 freebies, but if you want more Flaws go for it," enough times that it's basically a standard house rule in my games unless I'm dealing with mostly fresh WoD players.

          Something else I do, is something I call "packaging" Backgrounds, Merits and Flaws; and only the net value counts towards limits. So if you have a Merit and a Flaw that are just inexplicably linked such that losing one without losing the other is narratively impossible, they count as one "trait" and if the net value is negative that goes to your 7 point limit, not the base value of the Flaw. Yes, this does allow for a bit more oomph for characters, but because they have to be packaged to count the benefit to the story has always been better than any headaches from characters having a bit more power and pitfalls at start.

              In my experience, Flaws work best when players actually want them as a roleplay tool. Whatever Flaw it is, for those players it's part of their concept for their character. They want to roleplay the Flaw. The extra build points are just a bonus. As a result, they rarely need seven points of Flaws, much less more.

              Flaws don't work when players use them simply as a means to generate more build points. Those players don't want to roleplay the Flaws and hope to ignore them as much as possible. Instead of a solid character, you get this strange hodge podge of defects. If the ST pushes the player into roleplaying their Flaws, their PC quickly becomes a character nobody wants in the game. Unfortunately, most players fall under this category.

              If the players really want to start play with more powerful vampires, it is probably just best for the group to ask the ST if they can have more Freebies or XP at character creation.

              Merits and Flaws work best as rare elements to spice up a character and highlight something the player wants as part of their core characteristics IMO.

                  While I like other design approaches than how the WoD works, I think it's worth remembering in a thread that's not game specific, that different games within the WoD can have wildly different lists, which can lead to wildly different player reactions. Mage, for example, works way better with a more liberal approach to Merits and Flaws because they're really good for adding lots of flavor to characters because Mage's core mechanics are more universal. Mage is absolutely the game where I've seen the most cases of players requesting more than 7 points worth of Flaws, even if they don't get more bonus points out of it. Changeling is a close second place.

                  I don't think it's as simple as "Merits and Flaws are best if rare." because different games benefit from what you can do with them in different ways.

                                              While it can be a fun narrative, keep in mind it can feel very off-putting to people with disabilities when games portray disabilities as, well, not disabling. It doesn't mean you have to copy real world impairments, but if disabilities give you a boost somewhere else (mechanically or narratively) - including being part of the price of an advantageous trait - it feels like you're not really thinking about how these things can very drastically negatively impact people's lives.

                                              People with hearing impairments don't just get the ability to read lips or learn sign language as a perk. Those are skills they had to learn on top of all the other skills they have to learn to cope in a world designed around people that hear within normal human average. It's less time and energy to put into other things, not more. Also, anyone can learn to sign or read lips. Having the deaf character miraculously always happen to be the one that eavesdrop on a conversation covertly because they don't need to hear it, or communicate near silently, is both a stretch and trying to hard to turn a hardship into a positive.

                                              There isn't necessarily a right or good answer to this issue. But if you're thinking about how to approach thing like Merits and Flaws, it's worth considering how someone with those traits would feel about how your house rules/etc. would react to your implementation.
