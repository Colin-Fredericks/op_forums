                          As I've said in other threads, I use a very slimmed down list of Abilities, with eight in each category.

                          Talents: Animal Ken, Athletics, Awareness, Enigmas, Melee1, Presence, Subterfuge
                          Skills: Crafts, Drive, Firearms, Larceny, Performance, Socialize, Survival, Weaponry
                          Knowledge: Academics, Bureaucracy2, Investigation, Lore3, Medicine, Occult3, Science, Technology

                          I prefer fewer skills because of the issue of "skill creep". This is a phenomenon that was, to my knowledge, first noticed with the GURPS system. Likely because so many books were written by people who were science and tech people, it was fairly common for every sourcebook to introduce newer and more specialized versions of existing skills. The problem with this is that the more skills there are, the more you have to spread out your skill points, and this tends to result in less competent starting characters. Conversely, the fewer skills, the further your starting points go.
                          Ironically, I soon found that with only 24 Abilities, I was able to cut back on the number of starting Ability points to 11/7/4. (Part of this is also because I made Arete 2 the default starting level for all characters, meaning no one had to spend those four bonus points, and so had more to spend on other things.)

                          During character creation, I specifically ask people to figure out just what each skill represents to the character. If you have Medicine, is it EMT training? Are you a surgeon? Or is it tradition cultural healing methods like herbalism, acupuncture, and the like? Fringe medical theories from the Victorian age? Or several of the above?

                          Additionally, I started using 1st ed Exalted's Specialty system, with everyone getting 3 Specialty dots/points to add to their Abilities at character creation, giving extra dice to role in situations that involved said specialty. I found this especially useful for things like Crafts, Academics and the like, where one could have Academics 1 (Law +2), Crafts 1 (Carpentry +3), or Medicine 1 (Herbalism +1).

                          1By Melee I mean hand to hand combat, as that is closer to the definition of the word.
                          2Bureaucracy in this case isn't just how well someone can navigate the modern world of forms, regulations, and agencies, but also one's own skill a organization and management.
                          3Lore became the skill that represents how much a character knows about his own supernatural subculture (For Mages, what they know about the Traditions, various rules and protocols, their own Tradition and its culture and history, various aspects of Awakened metaphysics, knowledge of their enemies, how not to make a muppet of yourself in front of Masters and Archmagi, and so forth.) Occult ended up being the measure of what one might know about other supernatural entities (including spirits and bygones).

                                                          I made mention of my starting Arete at level 2 by default. However, at some point, I started calling it Gnosis instead. A large part of this is how I tend to approach the game. I look at it as a game of Dark Gnosticism; Those who have Awakened have gained knowledge of the secret underpinnings of reality, and with it the dangers of knowing too much. This secret and sacred knowledge can provide one with the keys to unimaginable power and ultimate enlightenment, but it also thrusts one into a world absent of the comfortable illusions and full of justifiable paranoia.
                                                          (I use this as a generic term for Western Mages. Most Traditions have their own term for it, as do various cultures around the world.)
                                                          I also stopped using the Avatar and Arcane backgrounds, tying them to one's Gnosis/Arete level. The more one's Gnosis grows, the more powerful and vocal their Avatar gets. But the down side is that one also gets more divorced from mundane reality. You gradually start to "drop off the radar", so to speak, and eventually even mundane friends and family have trouble remembering you. This is even worse for Masters and especially Arch-mages, who can barely interact with mundane people and even have trouble with those of lesser Gnosis/Arete.
                                                          Their method of getting around this is something I partially borrowed from Unknown Armies and its Invisible Clergy. Arch-mages can take on Masks based on various mystical/cultural archetypes (The Mage, The Emperor, The High Priestess, The Hermit, The Engineer, The Paladin, The Trickster, etc.), where its easier to interact with people and have them remember you when you conform to certain archetypical patterns and activities. I used Porthos Fitz-Empress in a lot of my games as a mentor type for younger mages, and have him using the Merlin mask, which makes it easier for him to continue to operate in the world as long as he's acting as a mentor and teacher and behind the scenes mastermind the way Merlin did for King Arthur.
                                                          I started doing this because I keep things earth centric. Chantries are all on earth, though the use of various forms of magic allow them to be hidden in very interesting places. The Umbra/Otherworlds are full of wonders, knowledge, dangers, and ancient beings, but most Mages don't live there. But at the same time, I don't want Arch-mages overwhelming everything, so having it where they have a hard time actually interacting with others solves that problem for me, as they go off to be Exemplars, Oracles and eventually ascend.
