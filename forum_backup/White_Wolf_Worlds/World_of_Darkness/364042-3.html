                    Personally, I view HtR through the lens of Dark Ages: Inquisition, its counterpart in the Dark Ages line. That is, the Imbued are mortal agents of a higher power that's making a last-ditch effort to save the world. Outside the context of the latter days metaplot, I'm more inclined to say that the Messengers have always Imbued certain mortals to act as their agents on Earth; it's just that without an end-of-the-world crisis at hand, they tended to be more choosy as to who they Imbued, and limited themselves to Imbuing the religiously devout. That is, I treat the mechanics of HtR (with its Conviction, Creeds, Virtues, and Edges) as a reasonable substitute for True Faith: HtR is a game about what happens when the powers normally associated with true faith are instead bestowed on Joe Average.

                    I hate the notion of treating the Imbued as replacements for HH-style hunters; I vastly prefer to treat them as complementary things, each with its own focus and role. To the extent that I use HtR material for hunters, I tend to restrict myself to the Bystanders rules, with some slight tweaks (such as renaming Bystander Conviction as "Sanity").

                    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                        I am very biased here as Hunter: the Reckoning has always been my favorite White Wolf line...but let me tell you my experience with it:

                        The first time I read H:tR I was 15-16 years old, and played with some friends that were about the same age. Although we would like to see ourselves as "mature", we were not, and the core book didn't help: it failed to describe a proper "ecosystem": on the one hand it was full with illustrations that would make you think you could kill a werewolf with a paperclip and that you could be a BADASS MONSTER EXTERMINATOR that would call hunting "THE GAME" and on the other it told you that you would end up dead if you tried something as innocent as report your zombie neighbor to the police. At that time we obviously disregarded any creed outside Zeal, so we were basically all avengers or defenders: a creed based on trying to "heal" the monsters or wonder whether it's their fault to be monsters? "Yeah, right". We had a lot of fun with it though, although now I think of the chronicles we would play and can't help smiling. Every time I read people on the Internet bitching about the line, I assume they couldn't get past this first impression of the game, and I really feel sorry for them (not being condescending here, I promise!)

                        The second time was 7 months ago. I spent a couple months on a hospital bed and had nothing to read on my computer so I skimmed through 80% of the H:tR line. Now, being 10 years older than I was at that time, I could see a completely different side of the game. Some people in this thread don't like Reckoning because they're essentially "supernaturals", and that's not only true, but a basic foundation of the line: what would you do if something in your head told you you HAVE to do something with the supernatural? You meet people that hears/sees/feels similar things, although noone knows more than you. The only thing you know is that you'll probably end up dead very soon, but you can't turn your back on this new, terrifying world: the voices won't let you. As you start accepting your "fate" and facing the supernatural, another question arises: how are you not supernatural? You're being told by someone to face something you barely understand, something of which you could be a part of. Also, once you have learned what you can and can't do, and begin to act, you will encounter other hunters that will think that your way is not THE RIGHTEOUS ONE and they might try to frustrate your plans... or maybe even stalk you and kill you if they think you are collaborating with the supernatural.

                        Note that I am using the words "do something with/face the supernatural" here, as H:tR (for me) is not a game about killing monsters. It is a game about trying to survive from day to day first, trying to understand the monsters second, and trying to make a change in the world third. And the first and second are doomed purposes, as you will inevitably fail to succeed on the first one, either by dying at the hands of a monster or by experiencing the end of the world in first person.

                        To me, H:tR is a game about hopelessness. Call me a masochist, but that's what makes it very attractive to me.
