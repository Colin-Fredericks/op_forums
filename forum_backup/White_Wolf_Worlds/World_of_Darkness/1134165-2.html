    A good example of rules being relaxed would be when the Order accepted the use of Technomagic. Then again, Hermetic Technomancers do tend to stay on the lower ends of the foodchain...

    What you're propossing it's no small thing: I doubt that a single Master (or even a group of Masters) can opt that way without dire consequences. Hermetics are notoriously prideful and "by the book" kind of people. The ranks are there to be used by everyone (not just In-House). You will be trying to force other Masters and proud Disciples to aknowledge your Sorcerer's rank. What's going to happen when you force the cherished Disciple of another Master to obey your *lowly* Sorceror?. That's not something to scoff at. Even if no one takes legal actions, Certamen it's assured (both against the master and the student), treachery and scheeming are assured (against both, too). It seems like a dangerous business to me. If the whole of the House was in...well Flambeau IT'S the most powerful (well, at least the most numerous) house, If someone can pull this off with minimal resistence, they can do it. I'm not so sure about Verditus.
    Well, there are Paths that allow for the creation of magical doo-dads. If the Verditus tests are about what doo-dads you can create, then they might be more interested in the doo-dad than the dude that made the doo-dad.

    The distinction I'm trying to draw is that the materials we read are largely the "Way the Hermetics See Themselves". After spending time as both an anthropologist and a lawyer and an archaeologist, I know that for pretty much any aspect of any human culture we know about, "The Way People See Themselves" is only tangentially related to "What People Actually Do". In fact, there is a step in between, "The Way We See Ourselves (But Really, Everyone KNOWS That We Really Do This...)".

    So, when I see the brightline Rule, that Hermetics NEVER allow a mere Sorcerer past the third grade of initiation, there's no way I don't reflexively think "Yeah, right...." I'm not saying they do it all the time. It's going to be rare and it's not going to happen everywhere. I'm sure House Quaesitor and Bonisagus would NEVER do it. I'm sure that House Quaesitor is supposed to suss such things out, re-test, convene a Tribunal, Brand and/or Censure the person who was in charge of the test and, at the very least, demote the Sorcerer back to a No. 3 and Brand him (but probably worse). But, there is a political cost to doing so to, say, Flambeau (still the House I think it is likely to happen in most often), that in realpolitik the (very small) Quaesitori and the more wordly and less stick-up-their-asses Bonisagi realize isn't worth it. They are going to know that it's stone cold stupid to piss off what is effectively the (large) military branch of the Order who like to strap their grudges to their backs so their hands are free to cast spells of mighty destruction...

        This whole test thing feels really weird to me.

All this testing of what you can do is meaningless when you can basically scan someone for what you're looking for. Or at least put them in a Wonder that only works for people with Arete and Sphere ranks.

                            Added Caveat:

                            One of the key questions is asking WHO is saying Tremere is a sorcerer, crappy mage, sub par in most every way? WOD deliberately obfuscates who the narrator is by having their fluff often told in the first person by unreliable narrators, but as Brucato points out in some of the essays in BOM, even the 3rd person narrator is deliberately unreliable and varies between stating 'This and such is what people think or say" and "This is what is". And balance is skewed towards the first over the second. Witness how the information about the various Factions are very skewed depending on which Faction's you're reading. And so on. And so on. Fluff is WOD is always about the "perception" of the reporter, whether that reporter is explicit or implicit.

                            So, again, WHO is saying Tremere sucks? In M20, MRev, etc. it's the Order that has every reason to vilify him. And those opinions don't even stand up to the light of inspection as stated with regard to, say, how the Massassa War went down and what the results where in the acrimonious divorce of Clan and Order. (The Hermetics seem to think they fared way better than they did. Witness how the Tremere absconded with a lot of the important stuff.                                                 Important nitpick:

                                                You're talking about the Observer Effect. In scientific observation, interaction with the subject is vital, but inherently influences the natural behavior of the subject.

                                                The Uncertainty Principle is that certain aspects of wave and wave-like systems can't be measure precisely and simultaneously. Measuring an electron's speed and position results in inherent uncertainty, because the more precisely you measure one, the more imprecisely you measure the other.
