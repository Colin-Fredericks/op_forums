And yet... they are both members of the same Sect. SOMETHING ties them together. And it's not a mandated Specialty Sphere, since every group now has a few to choose from.     That has no name now and no formal place in the rules. But either it's still there and just being ignored in favor of Focus, or, really, there ARE no such things as Sects anymore. Just a vast sea of Orphans with membership cards to organizations of arguably less than immediate relevance or importance. As in, much less important than the Orders in Awakening or the Fellowships in DA..
    Well, sometimes, it may actually be a political decision strictly based on a wrong stereotype. Case of most Dreamspeakers, a bunch of dudes that deal with Umbrood that haven't more in common with each other than with the angel summoners of the OoH. A lot of them only accept to "belong" to their "tradition" when the Technocracy it's on their doorstep (Albeit even the Dreamspeakers have specific camps that have, at least, unified goals, if not belief).

    Other times, this "paradigm" implies a method or ideal that it's held by every member of the Tradition, which transcends its differences. Case of the Choristers, all of them believe that Faith it's what matters to do magick. Faith in what it's debatable to the point that an atheist with strong hopes for humanity qualifies (albeit it woud be very fringe), but faith it's important (to the point that it's even suggested to change True Magick for True Faith in their book, as a very extreme choice)

    There are Traditions more unified on common principles and Focuses than others. For a Hermetic, it's almost granted that your brother/sister will have a Focus compatible enough to cast rituals together, if your pal it's from another House (especially from Ex-Miscelanea) it may be necessary to make a traduction between the focuses, but normaly it will be easier than to work with another Tradition. For a Dreamspeaker this is far from granted, the sacred pipe of one may have very little to add to the high rituals of the other, if a Dreamspeaker can work in concert with another it has little to do to their belonging to the same Tradition.

    So, I would say that, sometimes, this "paradigms" are very real, to the point that they can manifest in rules like being able to "share" Artifacts or the beneffits of a Realm's Reality Zone, cast rituals together without fuss, and share magical knowledge. Other times are just there to unify a bunch of very different Crafts under the same flag.

Their mindsets are fundamentally different. It's not even that, over time, she can become a "magical" Orphan without having to relearn/rebuy her Spheres from the ground up. (Note the example of a cabal with which we are provided in the Quickstart) It would be "it's us against the world" until it wasn't survival on the line and the group would drift or, more likely tear itself apart. It's worse than a music band as these people don't even have that one thing, music, in common. (Just like every Chorister is their very own Chorister and doesn't have to have a thing in common with any of the others, and, in fact, doesn't and shouldn't) And that's even though we sitll have the baggage of a Nephandi is a Nephandi is a Nephandi. And we see that tension in the RAW now. In one place the Nephandi are irreparably damned and a redeemed Widderslainte story is "doing in the impossible", while, over here, we have a Flaw that presents Widderslainte as a lifestyle choice.
