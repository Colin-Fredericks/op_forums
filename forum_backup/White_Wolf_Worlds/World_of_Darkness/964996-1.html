                                Originally posted by Rabbit Pooka View Post
                                I'm not sure how I feel about that. I'm hoping for a revised and updated Changeling game that I recognize, not something wildly different. Though a bit of a boost in the power department would be welcome. I know that back in the day Changeling didn't get the revised edition that most of the other lines got, but I'm hoping that not too much has changed. The revised editions weren't wildly different than the base games, and none of the other 20th editions have been wildly different from their base games. Here's to hoping C20 follows that lead of modest changes at most.
                                It really depends on what you need to stay the same for it to be recognizable for you. I'm not being snarky with that. Different people have different things they emphasize.

                                Glamour and banality are being clarified, but not really changed. Banality is still the biggest threat to a changeling, but bedlam is still there awaiting the unwary. All the methods changelings had for gaining glamour are still there (plus a new one). All the core kiths are the same, but some birthrights and frailties are being tweaked (and a few kiths have been promoted to core kith status). There are still childlings, wilders, and grumps. There's still the Seelie and Unseelie Courts. There are the original 13 noble houses plus one more. Tensions are still there between the nobles and commoners. The 6 realms are still the same even if their individual levels have been tweaked (Nature and Time are usable now). Arts are probably the thing that has changed the most. They should all work with every realm now. Some levels of some arts are still the same. Others have been altered slightly. Still, others are going to be pretty different, hopefully much more usable.

                                As a long time Changeling fan, this feels like the definitive edition of Changeling - the game it's always wanted to be. I know not everyone will feel that way, it's impossible to please everybody. I hope you give it a try though, and approach it with an open mind. No matter what changes we made, we all made a very conscious effort to keep the heart of Changeling: The Dreaming intact.

                                                            Originally posted by Crusher-of-Cities View Post
                                                            (...) Which brings me to the question- what happens when Changelings meet or clash with other splats? If they spot a vampire, werewolf, or mage doing whatever it is they normally do, does an average Changeling come and say "Hi"? Attempt to murder or mess with them? Avoid and move on? Does Seelie vs. Unseelie make a difference in who enjoys whose company? (...)
                                                            From the perspective of most other splats, changelings are just normal humans . . . Right up until the point when the changeling drags them off to Neverland for a spot of tea. Most other splats have too much Banality to freely interact with the Dreaming, and most changelings aren't interested in cross-splat conflict. At least not unless they're in full Shakespearean melodrama mode . . .
                                                            • Once vampires hit the Elder level, they've generally acquired too much Banality for a changeling to even hang out with. Younger vampires are still an option, though - although I wouldn't recommend sampling a changeling's blood (think LSD laced with raw magic)
                                                            • There are mage rotes that allow them to see into the Dreaming, without needing to be enchanted. However, most of them involved enough hallucinogens that it would be surprising it they didn't see fairies all over the place . . . Many mages (particularly those who don't experiment with those rotes) tend to think of changelings as some sort of Marauder hedge mage
                                                            • Most changelings don't have any dealings with the Underworld. Occasionally, a human will get drawn into the "fun & games", and end up a wraith, and then there are the feared banshees, who prey on fae and wraith . . . But otherwise, not a lot of canon overlap
                                                            There are official examples of cross-splat projects involving changelings. Most of them are small scale/individual things though. There's a group of Verbena sharing a node/freehold with the local changelings in Santa Cruz, Elizabeth the Malkavian spies for the New Sons of Liberty in their campaign against their "evil" sidhe overlord in Boston. But most of these projects stay small, because the very concept of the Dreaming seems insane to most members of the other splats - and that members who get involved with the fae have simply gone mad.

                                                            The big exception, though, involves the Technocracy. In Nobles: the Shining Host, the sidhe were making preparations to smite the Technocrats, and destroy their anti-Dreaming paradigm. They were reaching out to possible allies, scouting potential targets . . . An idea for your campaign, if you're interested.


                                                            Edit: Changelings (and the Dreaming as a whole) are great for shaking up jaded characters (and players!), without changing the entire campaign. One minute, they're looking for an informant who hangs out at a seedy dive. The next, they're partying with the "wee folk", and have to challenge a leprechaun to a drinking contest for the needed information.

                                                            Or driving down a deserted highway, they see strange lights in the rearview mirror. And next thing they know, they're strapped to a table, surrounded by strange beings talking about an anal probe . . .

                                                            Since the Dreaming contains virtually everything humanity has ever imagined, and "normal" beings can't take any evidence out of the Dreaming, its a fun opportunity for a one-shot (or more) involving concepts that you normally don't get to use
