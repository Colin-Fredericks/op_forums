    Originally posted by adambeyoncelowe View Post
    A couple more comments:
    • Per p. 95 of CbARev, Jamal took the Black Throne in 1497. Izhim and his followers left two weeks after that, putting their exodus firmly in 1497. Elsewhere, the book says he ruled from 1494, but that's obviously a mistake as he diablerises the Amr in 1496 before taking the Black Throne.
    • Caine's Chosen merely says Izhim and Djuhah join the Black Hand after the Treaty of Tyre and before the lost colony at Roanoke was founded (1587). Putting a date span might be more accurate. With what we know about their exodus, 1497–1587 makes sense.
    • Also, we technically don't know how soon after they joined the Sabbat that it was before Vestgeir asked them to sever contact with their main clan. You could just soften the word ("Some time after...", etc.).
    • I can't find a particular date for Fariq's defection, but it's said he didn't go to the Convention of Thorns and was "immediately" branded antitribu by his clanmates (TC3, p. 80). Now, this could mean immediately after the Convention (1493) or, more likely, immediately after the Treaty of Tyre (1496). So 1496 makes a better date for that, but "after 1496" would also work.
    • Re: Karsh/Jalan-Aajav/Qarakh, the last of these is never actually confirmed to be 4th Generation as far as I've been able to confirm. He diablerised a Methuselah but was only 7th Generation at the time (and that's after diablerising his sire). In all likelihood, he would've been 5th or 6th Generation afterwards, not 4th. It's therefore more likely that it's just the same person flitting between the two sects (Jalan-Aajav in particular seems to vanish for large periods of time), or there's another solution. Not really a timeline thing, but worth bearing in mind.
    Jamal; He was first mentioned on Clanbook Assamite 2nd, then expanded on Revised meanwhile Dark Ages Revised mentioned Nakurtum became Old Man (Woman) of the Mountain during the Dark Ages after Sha'hiri (Thetmes' sire). I do believe it was Nakurtum who got ritually diablerized after the Treaty of Tyre but I keep that storyline to my own (non-published, though the first draft had a lot of "{me}" entries) timeline.

    Caine's Chosen, and much of Revised era books, storylines are written in 1st person testimonies, that make datelining the story really hard, so I try to stay true to those stories that are shared between all editions. Imagine that a single Conditioning (Dominate 4) would mess up everything I make an entry upon (obviously there's the "someone Dominated and made X vampire express X thing back in 2nd ed" and that would render all my effort pointless"). On the Lost Tribe specifically I carry things from 1st and 2nd ed all the way to Revised since almost before Gehenna we get the comprehensible book about them.

    Fariq: yeah, really not a lot specific on that one, but get used to it, I measure things like that there because I know I'll get moments when embracing dates will become impossible to date. Like Triglav embracing Velya, Djavakhi embracing Noriz (this one I wrote today). Have in mind that 2nd ed had the Second City dated back to 5000 BC (Chaos Factor -> Shaitan) aprox and on '94 archaeological evidence discovered Gobleki Tepe (very few linked to archaeology knew this back then and I doubt white-wolf linked to this), I had my theories back on 2nd ed published materials but that got off track really fast when current archaeology caught up.

    Vestgeir: there's only one sentence of him on PGttS, that's it, nothing more (perhaps there's an VtES entry). I really can't expand more of it without being completely ignored on Revised era.

    Karsh/Jalan: I give this on the old white-wolf forumite perspective. This is a thing we discussed a lot back in the day! I understand that the site closed after 2004 but those were really good times. Qarakh came a lot later than those theories (remember that Guide to the Camarilla haven't been published yet, and we still considered that Protean 9 was still "Dual Form"). When Children of the Night came out we had a lot of discussion about it on the forums. Remember that Qarakh came a lot later in Winds from the East and later even when he diablerized Alexander in Dark Ages Revised (Alexander's concept changed from VtDA to DA: V). Having that into consideration, Karsh got staked and disappeared in Brujah Trilogy Novels (I haven't write those ones yet) and Meonia (an elder/methuselah True Brujah) and his childer, Claudia and Rodrigo kills Xaviar. Jalan Aajav disappeared too when Lucita tries to contact him during Gehenna: Final Night. This storyline might have been amazing if Dark Ages: Italy and Brujah Chronicles got to be published since it explained the True Brujah involvment during the fall of Carthage (but those never came to be pubilshed).

    Remember I try to give as much from 1st ed, 2nd ed, and Revised primarily. I really can't take BJD to replace everything on all previous editions since it actually does that (Anatole dies in '99 after the "Leopold" incident in the clan novels, Theo Bell commits diablerie on a Tal'mahe'Ra Malkavian, Lucita, Giangalieazzo, Hardestadt and Okulos die in '04). Therefore I only take what can be retconned to old stuff.

    Let's do this, give me the sources (book and pages #) and I'll see how can I add it. Have in mind that stuff like Katherine de Monpellier from Cb-TR messes up everything so that might take a while to be redacted.

                                        Originally posted by DarkKing View Post
                                        Depends on which type of mythology we're dealing with.
                                        It doesn't, I wasn't talking about characters believing, I was talking about how we as readers should read the lore.

                                        First instinct is to read that if a given individual is named Odin in the setting, then he's in the setting the original Odin.

                                        But this is a wrong assumption.

                                        Actually there exists a myth of Odin, people that posed as the mythical Odin, spirits that act as Odin, and other, stranger possibilities, like an actual Godhead Odin.

                                        Without undeniable proof, which we'll never get, there's no way to say which came first and who's based on who. Any of them can be the original, any of them can be the origin of a given tale or aspect of the myth, and any of them can still have cults and/or answer prayers and rituals. Including the myth itself, the story.

                                        So, for us, readers, the claim is just a claim. It can't be verified outside one's own table.

                                        Originally posted by sathriel View Post
                                        In our world he was an actual person, I don't know what that has to do with the game tho.

                                        The bottom line with Rasputin and all the other historical/mythical/religious figures as supernaturals in World of Darkness is their true nature is up for debate.
                                        I'm not talking nature, I'm talking identity.

                                        We know who Rasputin were in the setting. We don't know what he was, we don't know what he is today, we don't know a lot of things about him.

                                        But the basic gist of this plebeian monk who had access to the Tsarina, walked Sankt-Peterburg as if he owned the place and died in a way that scared his assassins, we know the figure and his famous shenanigans as facts.

                                        Not so much for old gods. We don't know the original facts about any of them. We don't know what the first entity being identified as one of them looks like. We don't know any fact that can be used to say "this identity surely belonged to this entity at this time, this was the real one everyone's talking about".

                                        And it has nothing to do with the writers being careless. It's about the consistency we intuitively expect being actually bullshit.

                                        The writers aren't just taking cool names. They wove the original legend in, not making the character a faithful fit, but making a plausible candidate.

                                        On top of that, seriously: no one has to be "the real thing" to claim it. Even to think they are it. We have plenty of people through history that claimed to be Jesus either returned or immortal, in fact we still have some quite famous. I know about two at least. Those people believe what they say.

                                        That's how the world actually works. So what wouldn't actually make sense would be for any of those myths to have a fixed, undeniable origin and original identity.
