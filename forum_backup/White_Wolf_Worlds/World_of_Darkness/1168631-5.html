    Originally posted by Lian View Post
    That is true I do prefer generally a carrot based method of enagement with issues vs stick but stick has generally been how personal horror has been mechanically circled around as a loss of control by both the player and the character.
    Before even getting to carrots and sticks, there's the issue of player input. The alpha V5 rules are very much, "roll to see if being a vampire randomly sucks today." There's nothing you as the player, or your character can really do about this. You just randomly messy crit, or get blood hungry as the dice decide regardless of what you do or don't do besides killing people. When the only meaningful choice is, "do I kill humans when I need Hunger 0" or not, you're going to lose anything close to personal horror.

    If the keystone to personal horror is, "what have I become?" moments, than V5 loses the point because it starts you past the point of having those. V5 characters as they stand now are already psychologically accepting seeing humans as a resource to abuse for controlling their own supernatural hunger. Feeding on blood is no more horrific for them than my need to cook a meal. We're just in straight supernatural horror where people are playing the monsters.

    Carrots and sticks both have their place, but the important thing is that they work together towards creating the play experience the designers are aiming for. Hunger's carrots and sticks before even looking at how Humanity operates are all over the place. It doesn't guide players towards, well, anything, because the effects are too random to do much more than wait to see how mean/nice the dice are going to be tonight. It reminds you that your character is a vampire a lot, without reminding you that vampires are monsters much at all.

    Then the new Humanity rules get added to that, and fail to add meaningful guidance as well. So we get the two systems that are core to framing the horror of the game.. not really framing anything.

    The VtM Humanity system is annoying stick-only, and frustratingly written in any number of ways, but for the most part it keeps to the goal of what the mechanics are trying to reinforce. The biggest problem is that Virtues work in a counter-intuitive way towards their descriptions; which is to say they don't help you resist temptation like that say they do, but instead help you resist the consequences of giving in. So instead of a high Conscience character not avoiding doing bad things because they're compassionate and connected to their human empathy, they can do more bad things because their moral integrity resists Degeneration.

            That seems... really forced. It certainly not something the creators of the game or later developers have stated.

            There's also some holes in the theory as an emergent property. Dominate doesn't care if people see you as a superior or not. You don't have to spend blood on Dominate if someone thinks they're superior to you. A character doing the lord of the sewers or back alleys thing that looks like a bum can still Dominate cops and rich people that feel they have authority or social power over them. A neonate can Dominate a Prince if Generation doesn't get in the way.

            Presence, Dementation, and Chimestry all have blood costs whether you're using them to violate status lines or not. Obfuscate (esp. Mask of 1K Faces) can be used to cross status lines by convincing people you're someone of higher status. There isn't an optional blood cost in Mask of 1K Faces to impersonate a Primogen and try to violate social conventions and order people around. It's only for overcoming a difference in Appearance between you and your Mask.

            If this is all about mastery, why does mastery over the body (Vicissitude or Protean) cost blood? A Tzimisce lord over their ghouls still pays blood to assert that mastery physically. A Gangrel's mastery of their own body doesn't let them shapeshift without cost.

            Why does Necromancy sometimes cost blood and sometimes not... while Thaumaturgy always does in this framework?

            It seems there's as many counter points to this as there are supporting points.

            I can't really think of any consistent framework for in-character explanations of why some Disciplines are blood heavy and others aren't. It's not "physical vs mental," because a bunch of mental powers are blood heavy, and some of the physical orient ones aren't, and whatever you want to do with the more esoteric ones.

            Even the Nosferatu point is diminished by the fact that they need to spend at least one blood point to create a Mask that'll make them humanly ugly to walk around humans with even if the power doesn't usually cost blood.

            If the game designers for VtM want to make a serious commentary about human nature via what costs blood and what doesn't... the Disciplines need a huge re-write. I really can't see a case for anything other than the blood cost being much more game and much less purposefully commentary.
