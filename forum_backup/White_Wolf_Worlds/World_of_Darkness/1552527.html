    [Mummy the Ressurrection] Assorted questions

    07-19-2025, 08:52 AM
    Hello, everyone.

    I was reading the Mummy: the Resurrection core book (terrific setting, by the way. I loved the hopeful tone of the book). I've just read chapters 4 and 5, and now I have some questions regarding character creation, so maybe you might help me to understand the rules better.

    1. According to the Amenti Character Creation table, new Mummy PCs start with Balance 1 and Sekhem 1. The same table also says that "Characters cannot start with a Balance rating higher than three dots".

    So, my question is: I can raise both traits with freebie points during character creation, but a starting Mummy cannot have Balance or Sekhem higher than three, right?

    2. Later, the book states that "Your mummy’s starting Sekhem score equals his final Balance rating". Since initial Balance cannot be higher than three, I cannot spend freebies to raise my initial Sekhem trait above my initial Balance trait?

    3. The Experience Costs table on page 147 does not list an experience point cost to raise the Sekhem trait. Is it possible to raise Sekhem with experience? If yes, how much does it cost? Does it go up automatically whenever I raise my Balance? Or is it raised through the game, just like Backgrounds?

    4. Last question (for now): Balance is a Mummy's moral compass and the equivalent of a Vampire's Humanity (or Road). Just like Humanity, it also has a list of offenses / sins.

    However, as far as I remember, in vampire whenever a PC commits a sin he must make a Conscience Roll and if he fails he loses a point of Humanity.

    However, since mummies have no Virtues, how exactly does this works, rules wise, for the Amenti's offenses against Ma'at? Does the storyteller just remove a point of Balance after warning the player that her intended action might constitute an offense? Or is there a system described elsewhere in the book?

    Thanks in advance for your answers.

      Yes
      Even if you spend Freebie Points, those can't be raised above 3 during character creation.
      For beginning Amenti characters that is a hard cap so to speak​



      Originally posted by Greywolf76 View Post
      Hello, everyone.


      2. Later, the book states that "Your mummy’s starting Sekhem score equals his final Balance rating". Since initial Balance cannot be higher than three, I cannot spend freebies to raise my initial Sekhem trait above my initial Balance trait?
      ​
      You see it right!
      You cannot raise Sekhem separately at character creation — it always equals Balance and since Balance can't go above 3 at creation, Sekhem also can't exceed 3 but If you raise Balance to 2 or 3 with Freebie Points, Sekhem will automatically increase to match.
      Sekhem is directly tied to Balance at character creation​

      You are on a roll here! Right again!
      Sekhem is not raised with XP like normal Traits, instead, increases only when Balance increases, and cannot be directly bought with XP
      It can increase automatically with Balance... or as a result of in-game events

      This is the hardest one
      Balance is the measure of how well one of the Amenti understands the nature of Ma'at. It is somewhat analogous to a vampire's Humanity, but not quite like Humanity. Balance measures the strength of his understanding and relationship with universal justice, balance and rightness - the understanding of Ma'at (not the Goddess, the concept, tje oppostie the Isfet. Ma'at represents order, truth, justice, and balance, while Isfet embodies chaos, injustice, violence, and evil)
      The udnerstanding of this universal principle is the source of their power, the real soruce of their power (you can have Attribute ratings equal to his Balance score, so a character whose Balance goes above five can spend experience to increase his Attributes to superhuman levels. Similarly, a mummy's magical skill it limited by his adherence to the path of Ma'at. No Hekau can exceed his Balance score) and that is why it is limited at character creation. As you study understand more, as you prove yourself worthy in the eyes of the Judges you can strengthen and deepen his understanding , your Balance rises and thus your Sekhem rises too (there are great roleplay opportunities in how Sekhem awakening parallels moral ascension, just sayin')
      But when a mummy commits an offense against Ma’at (as listed in the book by Balance rating), the Storyteller makes a judgment call. There’s no dice roll involved - unless the Storyteller includes some kind of house made custom mechanic - and if the offense is serious enough — and deliberate — the Storyteller can rule that the character loses a point of Balance representing when a mummy strays from balance, they may be judged by the Judges of Ma'at, who will assess their actions and potentially impose penalties (of course, one may attempt to escape the judgment of Ma'at by severing ties with the Judges... good luck with that and with the consequances)
      Balance is, in essence, Spiritual Evolution
      You must prove yourself to the gods and the cosmic order and raising it's - or losing it - is not just a stat but a measure of a character’s spiritual alignment and udnerstanding with one of the universe' prime principle, the Ma’at.
      ​​Losing it and rising it is part of the story, part of your life and path as an Immortal - Osiris, Ma'at, the Gods and the Judges does not really tolerate the abuse of eternal life you got from them, your "second chance"; after all your a flawed human has been made "whole" by the fragment of an ancient egyptian Soul (the Egyptians believed a person was composed of nine different parts:
      Khat, the Physical Body - this one doesn not, for obvious reason -have a soul fragment, Ab, the Physical Heart, the seat of consciousness - again, no fragment, Khu, the physical and mystical representation of a being, akin to an Aura, that ties the spiritual and material parts together; this part makes a Sakhmu, Ba, the part of the soul that ventures into the Underworld, the "personality"; akin to a Wraith; this part makes a Khri-Habi, Khaibit, the Shadow, which represent the darker urges and emotions of the soul, akin to the Shadow of the Wraiths, P'o of the Kuei Jin and may or may not the Beast of the Vampires; it makes a Sefekhi, Ka, the part of the soul that remains close to the body and guards it from desecration "vital essence"; this part makes Kher-minu, Sahu, the indestructible part of the soul that will dwell in the fields of A'aru, akin to the Eidolon, pure intellect; it makes a Mesektet and the Ren, the True Name, a vital part of existence and the soul, a "brief" summary of what the person is. Between these parts flows Sekhem, the force of life itself that binds the other soul-parts together. )

      The Judges are part of the cosmic order that enforces of the Ma’at and they are watching you like a hawk through the eyes of your Storyteller, they are cosmic enforcers and arbiters, not just after death, but throughout your mummy’s immortal existence. The 42 Judges are divine beings who represent specific moral principles or sins. Each one questions the soul of the deceased, asking if they committed a specific offense. The Assessors of Maat are not explicitly named with a consistent list across all texts, but there is a list of them the Game based on:

      ​Some of them are actual gods, like Tutu, Master of the demons of Sekhmet and the wandering demons of Bastet, who is the Son of Neith, depictied as a lion with a human head, wearing the Double Feather crown, the Solar Disk and Ram Horns. As a guardian diety, originally the protector of tombs, Tutu later guarded the sleeping from danger or bad dreams. He was stated to have given protection from demons, nightmares, giving longer life and protecting people from the darkness of Duat, the Netherworld

      So, in short, Balance = Spiritual Evolution, Sekhem = Power Curve Control basically and they are tied together, the developers intentionally tied it to Balance. Preventing high Sekhem at start prevents characters from being supernatural demigods and similarly, as you lose Balance, your powers wither

      If your mummy falls below Balance 1, one of two possible outcomes occurs.
      The first possibility is that the Judges of Ma'at will consider the mummy unworthy of eternal life. They cast the mummy's soul to Amemait, and the mummy is no more.
      Now strange to mention, the game calls him Amemait, but usally this beast is know as Ammit or Ammut, a Guardian Demon, a personified monster of Egyptian mythology, the "devourer of the dead"​ that devours the hearts of those who stride too far from the Path of Ma'at
      In a sene she represents the "pure" concept of destruction that weeds out the wicked and enables the just to live without interference from evil
      In WoD she may represent an uncorrupted version of the Wyrm and/or Oblivion
      The second option and the only chance of the mummy's continued existence is for the Amenti to renounce Osiris and Ma'at and agree to serve Apep


      Balance is treated more like a supernatural ethical state — part of the cosmic order — than a human conscience so there's no need for Virtue rolls like in Vampire (that said, the severity of the act and whether the character feels remorse can influence the ST’s decision (and possibly redemption arcs or quests to restore Balance)).​​​

                  Originally posted by Shadeprowler View Post
                  The Assessors of Maat are not explicitly named with a consistent list across all texts, but there is a list of them the Game based on:​​
                  This is not to correct what Shadeprowler wrote in terms of MtR, but I want to provide some trivia on how some of these names have been used throughout the editions. I think some readers may find it interesting.

                  In MtR, it seems they used the names of the Assessors of Maat as actual deities.

                  However, in Mummy 2e (1997), they state there are 42 Shemsu-Heru, the mummies who follow Horus and obey the Code of Horus. While they state that this group being numbered 42 and the Assessors of Maat numbering the same are "coincidence", they provided versions of these names (along with the names of other Egyptian gods and Pharoanic royalty). I feel this may have been a nudge, nudge indicating that in the minds of the writers, the Shemsu-Heru were in fact the beings whom the mythological assessors were based on (much like many of the Egyptian gods themselves were actually mortals who became vampires, mummies, mages, etc.)

                  In turn, in Mummy 1e we are given the names of the Bane Mummies were based on the " traditional manifestations of Apophis, the Egyptian devil serpent" as found in E.A. Wallis Budge's Egyptian Magic. This list of "traditional manifestations of Apophis" included some of the names of the Assessors of Maat. However, since Budge was an early scholar of Egyptology, much of his scholarship has been superseded, and his books - while still popular - are seen as outdated with many errors by more recent experts.

                  Therefore, you are going to see several different creatures (Bane Mummies, Shemsu-Heru mummies, the Judges of MtR) having variants of the same name (like Hau-hra of the Backward Face (Bane Mummy), Hraf-haf (Shemsu-Heru), and the Hraf-haf ("Face Behind Him") of the Judges of Maat. It's just an artifact of the game's evolution and how different writers did not consider what was used before.

                  I did not know this myself until several people contributed to a discussion on the names of the Banes Mummies on this thread. It begins around post #20 so skip to that if you're interested. Special thanks to Elphilm who provided a lot of detail.

                  Greywolf76 did comment on that thread (as did others on here), so he likely remembers this. I just mention it for anyone else who may not be aware.
