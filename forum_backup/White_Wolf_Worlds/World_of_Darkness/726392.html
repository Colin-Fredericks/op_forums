                                              Originally posted by Tuch View Post
                                              How many of you use Perception + Alertness for everything visual? I know it's intended for tactical use primarily but we default to it for everything from detecting surprise to searching a room. Is this the common use for your games as well, or do you require the Search skill instead?
                                              Per another further up response, I think you are having a slightly skewed perspective of the Ability, hence the slightly weird nature of your question. Alertness is used for noticing anything immediate in the character's sensory range, not merely visual and certainly not just tactical. A character rolling Perception+Alertness could be doing so to notice: a shadow shifting in his peripheral vision; the slight scent of something burning in the kitchen/in a nearby yard; to feel something small (like a spider) crawling up his arm/back/what-have-you; to notice the slight shift in tone of the many screaming kids on the playground wherein one of the children is crying "for real" because they've been legitimately hurt; discerning that the slightly flat soda he just sipped has an usual -- if subtle -- acidity to it... possibly noting that he's been slipped something in his drink. It can be for any of those things and more, so long as said sensory input is within the normal range for his sense (which may, of course, be abnormal by human standards via certain Merits, magic, what-have-you).

                                              As for searching for things, a Perception+Alertness roll may be useful for general spotting of this, that and the other when searching a room (especially, and more specifically, when a noted thing in particular is being looked for). Investigation, however, is the ability to be used in order to key together aspects of what is perceived in a manner that provides information beyond the obvious (or even not-so-obvious). Examples: Alertness may let the character note that there is some dust on the floor that is out-of-place with what is around it; Investigation would reveal that the dust isn't from within the room, or likely even the house, and subtle patterning in part of the dust denotes it as probably having been left behind by someone's shoes/footsteps... shoes with soles that are considerably worn. Similarly, Alertness might let you find a particular book among hundreds/thousands that are scattered in a given study... but Investigation would help the character note (when paired with Intelligence) that *other* books *not* being searched for that are in the piles are a clue to the mindset of the owner in question, even as the searching character may not have been looking for "x" other books.

                                              It's a subtle distinction for some people, but Alertness, Awareness and Investigation are well-split in terms of what they cover/are used for. (Sure, some people go the much more specific route of using Search, Spot, Research and similar, and that's fine for those who want to be more specific about character qualities; I, though, and virtually everyone I've ever gamed with over the past 3 decades, rarely go that far as it creates needless point counting. WoD, in whatever incarnation, is generally supposed to be played more intuitively and on a comparatively "simpler" mechanical level than, say, DnD 3.0/3.5, which is stat'd-the-house-down. Still, though -- to each their own.)
