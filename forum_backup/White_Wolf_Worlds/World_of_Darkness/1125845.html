    "The author of this Grimoire goes by... my name?"

    08-07-2017, 07:50 AM
    So your character finds a Grimoire which perfectly matches their paradigm and has concepts written in it that really resonate with you. It's a Grimoire that's potent enough to push up your Arete and your favourite Spheres. The author? You, at least according to the book. Its resonance matches yours; if someone were to investigate with Prime it would match up with your personal Quintessence signature almost perfectly.

    How are you explaining this? What's your theory? How would you investigate it?

              Without nailing my temporary character down to a specific paradigm, I would have several explanations, depending;

              First, two premises:

              a) The Grimoire is just that - a Grimoire. It does what it's supposed to do

              b) It's a Trap! Custom-tailored for me, to be lured into safety by the resonance. And of course by somebody powerful. There is something nasty, hidden deep inside with double meanings and very carefully hidden effects. It might even do everything the Grimoire is promising to do, but there might be a..price..to pay, later. Or Instantly.

              Given those two, let's get to the origin:

              * It was indeed written by me in the future, and somehow made its way back. On purpose? No idea. Did I send it back? No idea.

              * The nature of the Grimoire's appearance might not involve Time at all. It might come from a parallel timeline/Everett Volume. How it got here? No idea. But a second me wrote it in a parallel plane.

              * The Grimoire was written by somebody else, but they took a LOT of time and investment to fake my resonance all over it, and mimic my writing.

              BONUS ENDING:
              * The Grimoire actually wasn't written by me, or any other Mage. Not really. Kind of. It comes from the High Umbra, one of the endless worlds where every concept that can exist, does exist - including the concept of a Grimoire written by me. How it got here? No idea.

                                      Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post
                                      (BTW does anyone know the rules for Grimoires?)
                                      Official M20 rules from the Book Of Secrets are, that a Grimoire can help you raise an ability (or several), A sphere (or several), or Arete. Or a combination of those.

                                      What it does is, indeed, cut down the costs.

                                      Abilities: Half cost, though usually Grimoires focus on knowledges and esoteric specialties, rarely Talents or the like.
                                      Sphere: Current Rating x7, minus the grimoire's level in XP Grimoire's level is the Sphere limit.
                                      Affinity: Current Rating x6, minus the grimoire's level in XP. Grimoire's level is the Sphere limit.
                                      Arete: As usual, but no seeking is required. Arete can't raise over the Grimoire's level this way.

                                      Also:

                                      "Individual Grimoires may have other benefits, depending
                                      upon the book in question. The reader may gain only one
                                      benefit per reading, and may expand her Arete only once with
                                      a given Grimoire. A reader can benefit only once per year from
                                      a given Grimoire."

Then you're removing all of the ones whose paradigms don't match the liberal interpretation of Catholic dogma and tradition layered over it. Each roll is one Downtime. The difficulty is the Grimoire's level + 4, but this can be reduced by 1-3 using Sphere effects on a roll by roll basis. The number of successes needed is determined by the level of what you're learning. You can't spend Willpower on these rolls.

                                                  - Abilities: 1 success per level
                                                  - Arete: 5 successes per level
                                                  - Affinity Sphere: 4 successes per level

Botches erase your progress and can inflict derangements, cost permanent Willpower etc. at storyteller discretion. More likely with a book of secrets stolen from demons than a book about friendly spirits.

                                                      The successes method would work okay too - I just don't like rolling dice that frequently. Easier to abstract it as a flat amount of time.

                                                      For the Grimoire - it sounds like you're assuming a Grimoire can only be used by those who completely align with the paradigm of the writer. I haven't heard that rule before, but you could certainly use it if you like. I will poke a few holes, though: language shouldn't be a barrier to anyone with Mind 3, and unless the Grimoire's creator is particularly annoying, it'd be easy for him/her to build that effect into it so it's universally readable. Also, if a Grimoire allows increasing Arete, in theory it grants insight into how paradigms are paradigms only and how a mage can appreciate multiple points of view / drop foci / etc. So it's at least partially a widening of perspective.

                                                      For me I'd boil it down to what role I want Grimoires to play in the story. Are they primarily a method by which organized Traditions get their new recruits up to snuff - e.g., the new Hermetic gets a Grimoire copy of the Corpus Hermeticum and spends a year in the library? Then you want short study times, relatively weak dot levels available, and at least somewhat wide allowance for paradigms; any paradigm based in Hermetic theory should be fine with that Grimoire, not just the House Flambeau guys who can speak French. On the other hand, are Grimoires primarily Ancient Powerful MacGuffins, that unlock heretofore unknown secrets and are pursued by all? Then the paradigm bit should be split wide open - if everyone wants it, everyone should be able to use it - and long study times are fine, because when you finish you'll be incredibly powerful.

                                                      Your approach of very powerful knowledge, very narrow paradigm, and very short study times just doesn't seem to align with any specific narrative role. It's not an object you'd compete with others over, since no one else can use it. It allows you to rapidly become very very powerful, which I'd only find useful to do offscreen as I transition from a story about neophytes to masters.
