    Originally posted by GaryO View Post

    My bad, I used a shortcut. Of course you can implement on specific discipline into the game, what I wanted to say is that these disciplines require a lot of work both in term of development and gameplay.
    If you have the following system: One Disciple Dot - One Spell as you describe above, you'll quickly have too many spell.
    You'll have to use a spell bar (like in Dragon Age or World of Warcraft), have something like a spell book and allow the user to bind/unbind his shortcut.
    This system, while perfectly viable for PC is hard to use on console because you have less shortcut available.
    You could decide to limit the size of the spell bar to 6, so PC and console player have the same number of spell and the portability for a system to another is easier but somehow frustrating because a player will never be able to use all his abilities.
    Or you could decide to create a PC-only game, but you're also losing an important part of the market.
    I have to admit I have never really played a console game, so I'm not sure how this would work; but the way I imagine it you would have each discipline connected to a certain hotkey, and then scroll the mouse wheel to select each "dot". Again, not sure how or if you could transfer that to console, but it's what I have in mind for a PC experience.

    Originally posted by GaryO View Post

    Moreover, when I said that Obtenebration was too complex, this is what I had in mind:

    The second ability, Shroud of Night create a gaseous darkness around the player (or its target if you prefer).
    In a game, objects are usually linked to a mesh, a 3D model with triangles.
    When you want to add an "effect", like fire or smoke, you create a particle system.
    Particle systems are quite complex so I'll not explain it here, but what you need to keep in mind is that it creates multiple small particles that moves together.
    Now, with Shroud of Night you allow the user to create a particle system almost anywhere.
    The particles should behave somehow like gas and be "stopped" by a wall for example.
    I'm sure you've already seen some buggy game physics with 3D-objects, well with particle it's far worse.
    Handling collision with particles is horrible, it put a huge stress on the GPU and can require days of optimization.
    And at the end what do you have? Well only the second ability of Obtenebration...
    The way I imagine it working would be somewhat like how the "Bat Swarm" in Castlevania: Lords of Shadow 2, go to about 9:00 of this video:


    Notice how the player must first lock-on to the guard and "fires" the bat swarm. Basically any time a player does this the targeted guard is imobilized and the player is able to sneak past. Basically it seems the guard plays an animation, he is unable to see the player, and a particle effect is "connected" to the guard in some way. I'm sure you know more about how that kind of system would be implemented the me though.

    Originally posted by GaryO View Post

    Additionally, you have to work on a shader for this "gaseous darkness" and shaders are also horribly complex.
    For example, the most famous shader for its complexity is the water shader.
    One could assume that, nowadays with the huge amount of game released, having a nice and smooth water effect would be pretty easy, but it's not the case.
    Here's an article explaining why and here is a tutorial to create a water shader (if you're curious).
    Moreover, shaders have a huge impact on the cost of the video game. They require highly skilled and experienced developer and, as you can imagine, they're not cheap.
    Does that also apply to, say, the spell effects in Skyrim? There are quiet a few of them.

    Originally posted by GaryO View Post

    So, when creating your video game, you need to ask yourself where do you want to put you money?
    Do you develop a very complex discipline like Obtenebration that'll be used only when the player chooses Lasombra as a clan or do you invest money into improving the rest of the game and remove the Lasombra clan from the game?
    If you want an example, The Witcher 3 had a development cost estimated at 15 million US dollars, and CD projekt used their own technology for the game engine, reducing the overall cost.

    If Paradox doesn't feel they have the resources to do the license proper justice they probably shouldn't have bought the rights.

            Originally posted by the_1990 View Post
            Does that also apply to, say, the spell effects in Skyrim? There are quiet a few of them.
            Yes it does, of course, shaders are used for almost everything. Most shaders are simple enough, but some of them are very complex that's why in some video games you can see that the water is quite ugly and marvellous in others. Also, there's a huge difference between a static meteorological effect (some fog have been added by hand by a designer at the exact location) and a dynamical system (you turn in fog and can move around). With an imperfect fog effect and some work from the designer, you can obtain a magnificent scene, but you can't use this trick with dynamical fog.
            More generally, shaders are omnipresents in video game, the texture on a character is applied by a shader, the glass effect is a shader, same for metal and everything that you can classify as a material. Shaders are a big part of game development and, while mostly unknown, the major factor determining the visual quality of a game.
            I may be a bit too zealous, but if you want to learn more about it, http://thebookofshaders.com/ is a good material.
            It's technical and you need to now programming, but there are many interactive examples that may help you to grasp the general idea of shaders.

                                            World of Darkness MMO
                                            So here is my answer to WOD MMO. Yes it can be done and done well but not the traditional way of MMO’s.
                                            First you must build a world which is total interactive. Many games out there have done this such has Skyrm and Red Faction. This would create a world which you can kick down any door and level any building. But with that comes the great part to a WOD MMO. Unlike all other MMO’s your actions are what generate quest/missions for other players. A vampire who feeds in a sloppy manor finds themselves in trouble in different ways. To start if an NPC watches it could report it to the local NPC law enforcement or spread a video on the WOD Web. This would alert Elder NPC’s to send someone out to bring in the character to have a discussion about their actions or maybe a Hunter gets an information he can use to move in on a Haven or even get his first sets of fangs. Player triggered missions would then start the ball rolling but it does not end there. Now you have those people who run amuck and act crazy well then you would have a system like in APB reloaded which would up the threat level and response to the point that the mad insane werewolf who is dry thumping the people in the park after killing them because he thinks it’s funny. Now he is the prey of something much stronger than him and its coming for him in the park and now he is on the run or dead. But wait can’t he just make another character and repeat this action. Sure he can but there a cool down of threat level, he will now have to lay low or quickly gain more threat which could repeat the same actions or attract player attention which would be also bad. Now outside of player actions you would have ST’s going around playing NPC characters who would be working towards a goal or taking over an area for their supernatural group or trying to take a group of players or NPC out of an area. Every time you succeed at a goal be it physical mental or social it would gain point which you could use to increase your character ability. Cities would be almost equal size to their counter parts to allow more room to grow power, and this would help bring new content to future book coming out. Picture you’re a lone Bone Gnawer working the docks and group of BSD players are guarding a shipment which is being put on a boat. Players would have to maintain control of the area until the shipment has left but you cannot turn the tides and let your Pack (guild) or Sept (whole city) know by making a single howl which those who are not garou would not understand. The WOD MMO would be a game of resources and reactions. This is your neighborhood are you going to let Pentext buy out all the businesses or the Technocracy invade the mortals fate, or will you let your neighbors be food for the Sabbat?
