      In Awakening, Entropy got split into the Death and Fate spheres. Other spheres are the same.

      Enlightened Grimoire by Charles Siegel has an optional rote system, including improvements to the rotes. You buy them with XP, though there is a system to give each character some rotes to begin with and starting characters with a high Library or Mentor rating get more points for rotes to reflect they had more options to learn them.

      Enlightened Grimoire - White Wolf | Storytellers Vault
      https://www.storytellersvault.com/product/249171/Enlightened-Grimoire?term=enlightened+grim
      Enlightened Grimoire - Twenty-Five Years of Magick Over the last twenty-five years of Mage: The Ascension, spells have been scattered like trea


      Also, Enlightened Grimoire is a compilation of rotes in published Mage books as of the time Charles finished it. It groups the rotes, gives you the original source with page numbers and summarizes the rotes. So, it's not particularly original, but it's well worth the effort for someone wanting to see the myriad rotes in Mage without going through all the books. PDF search works very nicely--I look up a rote in there regularly for my game.

          My own approach to Rotes is much more radical; but that's because I see them as a way to replace Hedge Magic with an all-Mage take on Sleepers having access to Magick: Rotes become something that anyone with the right Focus can learn and use — though there are drawbacks if you don't have sufficient Spheres or/and Arete to perform them naturally. I model their cost off of the cost of an equivalent Enhancement.

          Meanwhile, MRev declared that fast-casting was a penalty for not working Magick by Rote. I tend to turn that around: if you've learned a Rote, but have sufficient Spheres to perform the Effect without it, the Rote is good for a -1 Difficulty.

          My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                  Originally posted by Eldagusto View Post
                  I was thinking of keeping paths and rotes.
                  I don't object to Hedge Magic; I only object to considering it to be somehow linked to Awakened Magick. That is, I prefer to treat Hedge Magic and Awakened Magick as similar but distinct things, in much the same way that vampiric thaumaturgy or werewolf rites or mummy Hekau are similar in appearance to Awakened Magick channeled through appropriate Foci but are based on fundamentally different metaphysics under the hood: and because Path Magic only resembles Awakened Magick but isn't actually the same thing, there's no need to try to force Paths into being a lesser subset of Awakened Magick, which is arguably a “square peg in a round hole” exercise.

                  But if you want a Path-like structure to learning Rotes, you can insist on purchasing Rotes in a more structured manner: e.g., you can't have more two-dot Rotes than you have one-dot Rotes, etc. That said, I've considered the option, but ultimately decided against it.

                  Originally posted by Eldagusto View Post
                  What are thoughts about rotes allowing Mages to cast the rote with an appropriate attribute plus ability? Or for rotes you can add the highest sphere’s rating to your arete?
                  There are already rules for Abilities Enhancing Magick; they involve making an Ability roll land using the resulting successes to reduce the difficulty of the subsequent Arete roll. I wouldn't object to replacing that with an “Abilities Enhancing Magick” rule that instead adds Ability dice directly to the casting pool; but I'm not sure I'd want to tie that into Rotes. Just leave it as a general “if you have a suitable Ability and can work on into the casting of the Effect, you get the bonus dice”.

                  On the other hand, if you want Rotes to provide more of a benefit than a -1 Difficulty when you have sufficient Spheres for the Effect, I could see saying that the benefit is that you get to roll Arete+Sphere instead of just Arete, in place of getting a difficulty reduction. Just note that combining this with the above Ability Enhancing Magick variation can potentially give you upward of 20 dice in your pool: 10 from Arete, 5 from the highest Sphere, and 5 from the Ability.

                  My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                      I've developed a simple rote system for my games. Rotes, for my purpose, are codified magical applications, the sorts of spells you'd find passed down from master to apprentice or effects the mage PC has created and practiced.

                                      At the start, players get a number of Rote Points equal to...huh, I can't find it, but it was something like Arete + highest Sphere. Then at the end of every story, each PC would gain an additional rote point (Hermetics get 2--all Trads have their little bonuses). One rote point buys one rote. That's it. Xp can also be spent on additional rotes on a 1 for 1 basis. The benefit of a rote is also simple: 1s do not subtract successes (though the caster can still botch if no successes are rolled).

                                      As an additional house role, players often roll an Attribute + Ability to effectively use their focus or otherwise in conjunction with a casting. 3 successes adds an additional die to the Arete roll, while 5+ successes adds a success. Thus, an exceptional performance will forward the mage's purpose more than a mediocre one.

                                          Rotes are very much not just examples of what you can do with the Spheres; Focus absolutely should be a consideration as well. Rotes ought to have the following parts:

                                          1. What is the Focus?
                                          2. What are the Sphere requirements?
                                          3. What is the underlying Effect?
                                          4. What level of Feat is it?

                                          The Focus is mainly a matter of Practice, although Paradigm may factor in as well. (Obviously, I'm using the M20 terminology here.) Instruments and Abilities definitely factor in, with the Practice not only determining which Instruments and Abilities are appropriate, but also providing the framework needed to decide what the mage does with the Instruments involved and the choice of Ability determining how well the mage is at doing that. If you can't come up with an activity for the Rote that makes sense for your Focus, you can't perform the Rote. And if you're using a Rote, you cannot Work Without Focus and you must use the Instruments associated with that Rote (though if you've raised your Arete high enough to have discarded an Instrument that the Rote requires, you still get the -1 Difficulty for using an Instrument when you don't need to). This is the main price you must pay (other than whatever experience cost is involved) to gain access to whatever the benefits of a Rote happen to be.

                                          Spheres, I don't have much to say about: to my mind, they're mainly a bookkeeping abstraction, good for helping determine the experience cost of the Rote and for determining what it takes to improvise the Effect that the Rote embodies. That; and like I said, I wouldn't be averse to the notion that if you have enough Sphere dots to improvise the underlying Effect, you get to add your Sphere's dots to the casting pool.

                                          The underlying Effect is what it's all about, ultimately; but to my mind, the most important element to consider isn't “which Spheres are needed to do this?” but rather “how does the Focus apply to this?” Spheres are less about what you can do and more about what you can improvise; Rotes, as I envision them, provide another way to determine what you can do, independent of what you can improvise. Note that in earlier editions of the game, this emphasis on the importance of Focus would have translated to Rotes being linked to Traditions, Conventions, or Crafts, because before M20, your Practice was determined by your allegiance. As of M20, your allegiance tends to put restrictions on what your Focus can be (e.g., the Celestial Chorus does not include any atheists in its ranks, save for “crisis of faith” storylines which tend to be resolved by the mage in question either reaffirming her faith or leaving the Tradition); but no allegiance holds a monopoly on any Focus, and ultimately it's your personal Focus that determines which Rotes you can and cannot learn and use.

                                          Feats need to be considered (explicitly, IMHO) because not every Effect is going to be “achieve two successes to do this” (a Standard Feat). Note that the Feat determines more than just how many successes are required, although that's important; the more successes are required, the more likely you'll need to make an extended roll too achieve the necessary successes. But on top of that, a large enough Feat increases the Difficulty of the casting (M20 p.503, General Circumstances: Outlandish to Godlike Feat entry) and, if you need to do an extended casting, optionally increases how long it will take (pp.541–542, Optional Rule: Rite, Ceremony, and Great Work). Note that radical increases in the size of the casting pool significant increase how big of a Feat you can have a reasonable chance of achieving with a single roll, bypassing the need to perform it as a Ritual.

                                          Which brings me to your suggestion to make the pool for a Rote an Attribute+Ability roll. Attributes start at two dots, and are cheaper to raise than Arete (half the cost, in fact), and Abilities are even cheaper than that (a quarter of the cost); and both are much more widely useful than Arete is. This would mean that if Rotes go to Attribute+Ability pools instead of Arete pools, they need to be especially expensive to reflect their significant benefit. My proposed xp cost was based on the assumption that the pool for a Rote would be no bigger than Arete, and possibly smaller (though no smaller than the highest required Sphere rating). Being able to add the Sphere rating to the pool can potentially double the poop's size; but Spheres are right as difficult to increase as Arete is, which reins in how potent of a boost this is likely to be. I'd still want to increase the xp cost of a Rote; just not as much as I would for replacing the dice pool with an Attribute+Ability pool.

                                          My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                  Awakening's approach is two-fold: there are Rotes (which can only be created by Masters) that let you add an Ability to the pool and give it the Rote Quality; and there are Praxes (which anyone can create) which make it more likely to achieve critical successes. Awakening doesn't have variable difficulties, but it has a few “dice trick” options (such as a “10 again” rule, a “9 again” rule, a Rote Quality or “reroll the failed dice once”, and “critical success on 3 successes instead of 5”) which serve a similar purpose of enhancing the effectiveness of a pool without increasing its size. I think what Ascension has traditionally called Rotes is most analogous to Awakening's Praxes.
