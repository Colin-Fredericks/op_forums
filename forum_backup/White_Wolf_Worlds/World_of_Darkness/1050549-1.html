                        I always saw werewolves as being mentally equivalent to the main characters from The Walking Dead. Theirs is a harsh and brutal existence, and while they've all adapted to one degree or another, from the perspective of a relatively safe middle class first world existence, they are rather damaged. Howard's Conan stories, in which no matter how hard people may struggle, civilization is inevitably doomed to fall to the forces of chaos. But still you struggle on.

                        (And yes, from the human perspective, the Garou are monsters because almost everything that has improved the human quality of life in the past 100 years or more is usually something that damages the Garou Nation's interests, while at the same time, a huge amount of what the Garou do to combat the Wyrm and other enemies directly or indirectly harms human bystanders. If the PCs close down a factory that's impacting the environment, be it via monkey wrenching violence or by subtle financial maneuvers, you've won a victory against the Wyrm, but at the same time you've put a bunch of innocent people out of work, leaving their kids at risk for malnutrition and a lifetime of poverty and hardship. And odds are, most Garou wouldn't care even if you pointed this fact out to them.)

                                                    Before agriculture, human populations seemed to be stable at around 6 million (if you use older figures) to 60 million (if you use newer figures based on ecological capacity and genetic analysis). The question becomes how did humans stabilize their populations? Famine was rare when population densities were less than 1 person per square kilometer, disease was even rare because the low population density and lack of domesticated animals reduced disease to practically nothing (see the Americas and Australia for examples of practically no diseases before European colonization). War did not seem to exist (despite rare evidence of incidental interpersonal violence). In real life, human populations were probably kept under control with a combination of highly athletic lifestyles, extremely low carbohydrate diets, and (probably) near suicidal levels of adventurism (which can be seen today in modern extreme sports). In cWoD, the Garou and Ratkin probably did not have to do much to keep populations down until the advent of agriculture.
