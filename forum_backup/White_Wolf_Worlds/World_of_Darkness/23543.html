    What's on your game table?

    12-05-2013, 01:21 AM
    So... Let's say you're an ST or a DM/GM/All knowing dice god and you're gearing up for a Table Top session with yer gaming buddies. What's on your table? Books, dice and paper? Or do you have an entire toybox of maps, props, minis, stereo of atmospheric music and a buffet of soda and snacks? Are you the planning type setting up foe stats and NPC character notes way ahead of time or are you the fly by the cuff type with just a few notes to keep in the story on track and make the rest up as you go? Though this isn't a question so much about style as what tools do you use in your styles.

    Shall I go first? I fall somewhere in the middle of the extremes. I try and prepare a few things in advance like baddies and NPCs, but I know players can be very unpredictable and might wind up doing something that tosses all those characters out the window entirely. I have notes for storyline marking posts, but I leave how (and how quickly) the characters get to those points up to the players.

    I have not run a TT game in well over 10 years, but I hope to do so again soon so I have collected a box of items that I recall being useful. I may not need them, but I like having them around. So, for my players I have small folders for char sheets and enough note pads for about 5 people. A pencil box with pens, pencils and a ruler. I printed out an archive of every char sheet in every system I own books for. (That way if we need extra or someone's friend wants to join in I can just photocopy it cheap instead of having to hunt on a laptop and find a printer.)

    I have more D10's than GOD and need to get a bigger case to fit them in, but I can pull out a pouch or 5 if no one brought their own dice. I have soft toys since I know from experience regardless of groups and members something always gets tossed around or at someone in the heat of the game. So in my game box I have several Koosh balls and the ST gets a plush Holy Hand Grenade. It also gives figety players something to do besides pick at book bindings or start tearing up bits of paper.

    I have an ST gavel, cuz sometimes you just have to make rulings loud and final. And as a joke one group I was in allowed the use of a magic 8 ball if someone was especially argumentative or whiney they can leave a ruling up to chance. This was amusing as the 8 ball always seemed to favor the ST's decision. (Any more arguing got the Gavel)

    I have a heavier plastic binder and clipboard for the ST screen and all my notes and NPC sheets. From my D&D days I have a vinyl roll out map with squares on one side and hexes on the other with special markers. I doubt I'll need this, but it might come in handy.

    I still need to get a bin to keep everything in, preferably with wheels so I can toss books in and transport it if we're going to play someplace other than my house. That's on my wish list.

    Know what else I don't have that is essential to a Table Top RPG? A TABLE! Yup. We don't have a dinning table anymore since house mates moved out about a year ago and took theirs with them. That's something I need to replace before I can start getting a new group together.

    Something that was brought up in another thread was food at people's games and there were a few really yummy sounding ideas that involved crock pots. So now I'll have to get myself one of those some time. Anyone else got a cool gaming set up?

      I tend to plan sessions quite loosely, having just a few pages of notes and a few floeshed out NPCs made in advance. I rarely have any trouble making up things I need on the fly, and in my experience, the sessions get better that way - no railroading, no frustration over planned stuff which never materialises. Besides these notes, I usually have the core rulebooks close at hand, along with pencils, dice and something to drink. I never use music, and tend not to use miniatures or other props, though I sometimes create written handouts, and often prepare maps then and there.

                      I ST much like Loxosceles, evidently.

                      At my table, I have:
                      Tablet computer. It has music, internet (for quickly pulling up a random picture of whatever NPC my players are trying to screw over), notes, digital copies of the books, so much stuff. Seriously, they're so useful - I've almost completely eliminated paper on and around my table.
                      ST Screen. I use one that has space for homemade inserts, plus it's in a landscape style, rather than portrait, so I can watch my players better.
                      Dice. Including my super-special "Storyteller dice," which have no 1's, but two 10's. Before the flaming torches and pitchforks come out, I use them only for NPC rolls, and NPC's don't get to specialize and do not benefit from "exploding 10's," or rerolling, or whatever system you may use, but the narrative demands that they don't trip and fall down an elevator shaft or accidentally shoot themselves in the face, either.
                      Dice tray. Everyone needs one of these, they are so nice. I've built a couple after being asked to by players, and they love them.
                      I have several mechanical pencils around the house that players are welcome to use, but I ask them to bring their own. Players are also welcome to bring any books they like, but I've found - with my group, at least - eliminating the stack of books around the table discourages distraction. Instead, I encourage them to write down anything about their powers that they need to know - Mr. Gone's add-on sheets are excellent tools, for both me and the players. We use either my dining room or coffee table, depending on myriad factors, mostly whether or not my girlfriend is gong to bed early. We put down the vinyl hex-mat which doubles as a tablecloth and impromptu notepad, forever solving the coasters debate.

                      As for the Koosh ball thing, it all depends on situation and presentation. In a group of friends, I don't think it would be seen as condescending. We had a player that loved rolling dice, always, forever, for no reason other than fidgeting. Rolling them on the table, rolling them around in his hand, flicking them like marbles into the dice tray - even when it was his turn and he was engaged with the story, he'd still be playing with the dice. Then another player gave him one of those Stkfas toys, and now he only touches the dice when he needs to. Now that's completely different then if you're meeting at say, a college gaming club, and the first thing that happens is the ST handing out Koosh balls and saying "If you need to fidget during the game, play with this."

                                  Originally posted by Loxosceles
                                  A thread of "What's in your game room?" would produce a different answer than one of what's at the table.
                                  Very true. I have an office/nerd cave where I store all my physical books (and toys & the like), where players can't get their greasy hands on them.

                                  As to the dice tray, I think they're fanstatsic. I've built several myself, just from materials from the local arts & crafts shop. They cut the clatter of dice rolling on a tabletop, but if you like that sound, you can use a base to replicate it without worrying about your table. Personally, I like (mostly) silent rolling, so I use a 6x4 inch tray with a thin layer of foam over cork board, mounted on balsa wood, with a felt flooring. I've built some for players that prefer more bounce - felt, foam, balsa wood, no cork - and one for a player that wanted more "clatter" but not as much as bare wood (simple - thin felt over the wood). In the center of the table I have a 9x11 oval, which my players love. No corners for dice to get stuck in, the lip is an inch high, so no rogue dice rolling under a chair or table, a dark grey felt for great contrast - I'm really proud of it. My only regret is I didn't paint the damn thing before I put it together, so now it's that neutral grain instead of something cool. As for cheating, well.. it's not the reason I use dice trays, but I did have a player that loved fudging his rolls, and this had put a stop to it. While no one ever said the dice tray was a check on him, he did seem resentful about it and treated it like a craps table.
