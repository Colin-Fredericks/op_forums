    Starting a Mage Chronicle with powerful players

    07-10-2017, 01:44 PM
    Hi,

    I just finished my first chronicle and my players are eager to start a new one. Since they played for 2 years with the same characters, they want to start with new ones to try other paradigms. In this chronicle, they started with nothing, Awakening alone in the middle of New York. They were reached by a group of Mages who taught them Magick and created all together a Chantry. At the end of my chronicle, they reached Arete 4.

    I would like to start things differently, giving them now the role of the mentors with a well established Chantry or at least a lot of experience, may be giving them enough points to start at Arete 5. How would you handle the character creation ? Would you just give them a lot of experience points to spend how they see fit ? Or, would you just fix different starting creation points (in each category) ?

    How would you use a "Apprentice" kind of background trait ? I have one of my players wanting to have an apprentice but I'm not sure if I just role-play the thing or make him buy it with experience points. If I do that, what advantage can I give him ?

    For you to know, this new chronicle will probably start just before the Avatar Storm or the destruction of Doissetep. The point will be to play a political chronicle in the Revised metaplot. I want to begin just before to make them feel what the Avatar Storm cost them. None of the players know the Mage timeline at this point (since I played before the Avatar Storm in the first chronicle and they're new to the game).

    Thank you for your answer !

                  • "The Illuminati": Has a lot more Backgrounds but a Sphere point less. Additionaly, the Illuminati can start with certain Backgrounds, like Resources, above 5 to represent mastery of the mortal world.

                      Kudos to you. Master level play can be insane. Power levels in Mage aren't linear at all. Morel like the inverse log kind of exponential and 5 dots in a Sphere is much higher on that line reaching up to infinity (Arete 6?. 6+?) than 4. And 4 is a LOT higher than 3, 2, 1 clustered down near the vertex.

                      I've always run Adversarial Backgrounds EXACTLY like Flaws (there are even 100% 1-to-1 correspondences between several of them). I'd run an Apprentice in which time must be invested as per the Apprentice Background, but the amount of care and feeding would be directly related to the Freebies gained. It would be a high dot Apprentice who would be actively messing up your Sanctum through their incompetence. An Apprentice who has graduated might be a mid-range Ally. One that should be graduated or is really more of a Journeyperson still hanging around might be a high dot Retainer.

                      Since you're worried about your players min-maxing if the get a glut of XP to spend, then don't give them that. The best way to do that is to allocate where the XP would go by using an advanced version of the normal character creation system. That's what that does.

                      I dunno, but some raw numbers might be something like:
                      Attributes: 8/6/4
                      Abilities: 19/13/7
                      Arete: 5 (cannot be raised by use of Freebies, because.... then it's a whole other game and and you have to face some questions outright, like Arete 6 = infiinte power or not....)
                      Spheres: 12 dots, (I dunno, but you might want to break this down further. I probably would. If you are looking for the "5 dot Sphere" experience, maybe require one Sphere to be 5 dots and one of their Sect Specialty Spheres be 3 dots, if they don't choose to have a Specialty Sphere at 5. In any case, I would probably say that a Specialty Sphere needs to be at 3).
                      Willpower: 5 (STRONGLY urge them to buy it up if you are using the "can't use a Sphere unless you've got that many temp WP" and even more strongly if you are using HDYDT-style Mind effects, unless they want their adversaries and possibly fellows having an open door policy with their brains)
                      Backgrounds: 10 (and figure out how high you want to let them go with Backgrounds 6+)(If you are using the Chantry idea, you might want to throw them as many additional points as you are requiring them to spend).
                      Freebies: 28
                      Flaws: 10 (And maybe up to 5 extra for Echoes? To represent the kind of permanent Resonance a Master would have picked up)

                      YMMV on the above, but I was slanting it to the higher end of power in regard to how mage characters normally start out, based on your premise that this is the game where they get to bring out the big guns.

                      One idea might be to REQUIRE them to sink points into a mutual Chantry as a Shared Background. Each player would be able to say what part of the Chantry's constituent Backgrounds their expense indicates (which tells you what role they play in it). This might be particularly great for min-max players, you toss in the codicil that the real political power/leadership role (whether it's overtly acknowledged or not) is dependent on who puts the most points in. You might get a bidding war Amber DRPG-style to determine who the top dog the Chantry is. And such a bidding war is a great way to simulate the history of the characters in the Chantry and create some internal tensions within the group. Masters are like Elder vampires. Even if they are ostensibly still a cabal, with that kind of power comes individual interests that don't always line up...

                      As a general note: Remember that the players will probably be fairly inept at using their Spheres. There is a HUGE difference in the level of ability and knowledge a player will have if they have to slog their way up than if they are given super-powers outright. They'll probably end up having some tricks they learned getting to Adept in the other game and will be having fun with the new mega-power of 5 dots Spheres, but, since they didn't really earn the power as players, they will be somewhat inept compared to what such characters. Keep that in mind when creating whatever adversarial situations they are facing. TBH, this is actually a problem with mage characters as is. They pretty much start midway along the path, as opposed to how Vamps and Werewolf starting players do and the players don't know what their character's Spheres can do compared to the intimate knowledge gained if you have to start out at Initiate levels or in an "Awakenings" game.

                                  I've been tinkering with a system for well rounded, powerful characters. My initial suggestion would be something like this.
                                  You divide the 9 spheres into 3 tiers of interest, choose 3 spheres to be your tier 1 spheres, 3 as tier 2 and 3 as tier 3.

                                  You cannot progress to the 4th dot in any of your tier 1 spheres until you have 3 in all your tier 1 spheres and at least 2 in all your tier 2 spheres. Tier 1s doesn't have to have any rank in them. If you want the 5th dot, you have to have the other tier 1 spheres at 4, tier 2s at 3 and tier 1s at 1.

                                  For example you have a member of the Order of Hermes. His tier 1 spheres are Forces, Mind and Prime, his tier 2 Spheres are Life, Matter and Spirit and lastly his tier 3 spheres are Correspondence, Entropy and Time.

                                  If he wants to have the 4th dot in Forces, he needs to raised Prime and Mind to 3, Life, Matter and Spirit at 2.
                                  When he wants to buy the 5th dot of Forces, Mind or Prime, he has to have sufficient mastery (4th dot) in the other two, his tier 2s must have advanced to the 3rd rank and the rest to at least 1.

                                  This leaves a well rounded yet, extremely powerful character and it also makes sure that the players doesn't rush to the 5th dot in one sphere, while leaving the others at 1 or even 0. It also makes sure that it takes quite a while before the characters can wreck the game with spheres at 4 or 5.

                                  Now sure, this system makes it impossible for a character to be a one trick pony, but in my opinion thats a good thing.
