    Base Time


    Purview: Time, Timing, Triggers, Duration. For some reason the writeup uses ‘Tricking the Flow.’ Special Rules and Limitations

    1 - Like Correspondence, Time has its own range chart to deal with attempts to look through, reach through, or move through time.

    2 – Time can extend duration at rank… the base writeup doesn’t say.

    Ambiguity Resolution – Duration Enhancement

    While Time’s writeup says it can extend duration, the actual rules just point to spending successes via the Optional Dividing Successes rule, or adding Entropy 3. Possibly this means that if Time is included to extend duration the Optional rules are no longer Optional, and adding Entropy 3 as well just extends the duration indefinitely until some trigger is met. If the Optional rules are used already, it seems Time would only be involved for the Entropy condition.

    3 - Travelling back in time has difficulty and paradox penalties. If this applies even on a botch it explodes very rapidly. The 50 year time jump in the book would garner 8 points of paradox if successful and 56 points on a botch. It is possible to read the paradox penalty as only applying when the mage actually time travels, but it isn’t the most obvious or direct read.
    I personally think these rules are unjustified and quite badly thought out, but they are the rules.

        Time 1

        • Time Sense
        Temporal understanding begins with the mage’s own perceptions of time. At this stage, she develops a precise internal clock and can spot the temporal ripples left behind by (or, in many cases, developing ahead of) Time Effects. Other phenomena, too, leave disturbances in the time-stream, and the mage can notice them as well. Combined with additional Spheres, this Rank allows the mage to detect the influence of the Time Sphere on other spells or patterns too.

        Internal Clock (Time Sense)

        A mage can know basic facts about Time like the current time. There is nothing indicating that this is limited to a specific convention, it should work with just about any way of measuring time. A mage should be able to get time of day, time of year, year, years in AUC (from the founding of Rome), daylight savings time, and possibly even the current time zone.


        Sense Time Effects (Time Sense)

        A mage can sense nearby manipulations of times. From the wording this includes time effects in nearby times, for instance time effects that are about to happen. This does not put any restriction on what Time effects can be detected.


        Sense Stable Temporal Loop

        Whether stable time loops are possible in your game is up to you. If they do exist, though, a mage with this power should be able to sense if he is in one and sense what he has to do to break free or stay in the loop.

        I bring that up because IF stable loops are a thing in a game then it is arguably that they don’t suffer from the increased difficulty and paradox of time travel. The spell isn’t violating causality but reinforcing it. That’s at least a plausible game element and this power would be important to using it.


        Conjunctional Effects


        Detect Time Effects on Patterns

        Ambiguity Resolution

        There are no coherent examples. From the text it sounds like time effects on some things require conjunctional spheres, but either this means any time effect on any pattern or spell is invisible to Time senses without other spheres, or there are specific things that ordinarily don’t show up without help. The former would be ridiculously constrained, the latter needs more definition.

        The best I have is that this lets a mage detect when objects or things have been or will be manipulated by time effects but are not currently, recently, or about to be. A mage can detect if an object was moved through time or aged, for instance. Now I would have thought that this would need Time 2 to look into the target’s past or future, but maybe there is temporal residue.

                    Time 2

                    •• Past & Future Sight/Thicken the Walls of Time

                    Now the mage can look forward or backward through time. Although those impressions are fleeting, hazy, not entirely accurate, and bound by the limitations of that time and place (that is, what a bystander in that specific time and location could sense under the circumstances), they allow the Time-seer to catch glimpses of the past or future.

                    By itself, this Effect allows the mage to see in her present location only. By combining Past/ Future Sight with other Spheres, however, she could read the probable past or future impressions of objects or places (Matter), living things (Life), and alternate locations (Correspondence). Entropy 2 even allows her to glimpse multiple futures and pick out the one
                    most likely to occur.

                    Reversing her powers of perception, that same mage can thicken the walls of time, which makes other Time Effects more difficult. Each success she rolls deducts one success from the attempts of other Time-savvy characters.


                    Look Forward to the Future, or Past to the Back (Past & Future Sight)

                    Bonus points if you recognized the reference without looking it up. A mage can look into the past or future. This power is limited to the current location, though Correspondence allows the spell to follow other locations and Pattern Spheres let the spell follow a pattern rather than a location. No guidance is given on how to determine the appropriate rank of pattern sphere but the spell is essentially locking to the pattern so that’s how I would determine it.

                    While this does look through time, it doesn’t involve moving in time so as written it shouldn’t invoke the +3 difficulty and Paradox multiplication. That said, mages can sense this, which means a mage in the past could be affected by this spell, so a mean ST could treat this as a risky endeavor.

                    Ambiguity Resolution – Entropy

                    Note that the Entropy addition likely only lets the mage know which timeline is the most likely, Time is probably the sphere that is looking into multiple futures.


                    Alternate Past

                    One potential use of Entropy here is, when scrying into the past, taking a brief peek into offshoot timelines. This is a bit strange so I’ll give an example. Say a mage needed to see the face of someone who usually wears a mask. He looks into the past several times but doesn’t find a time where the target removes their mask. He does find one where the target almost does, but is distracted by a phone call. So the mage uses entropy to try to peek into a timeline where the phone call doesn’t happen. That timeline isn’t what happened but it is close (maybe it even could lead to the present anyways) so Entropy allows him to briefly see it.


                    Temporal Semaphore

                    It may seem cheap to say a mage risks Temporal Paradox if someone in the past detects his scrying, but a clever mage could use scrying itself as a form of communication. At certain times that only he knows he turns on his senses to check for temporal scrying. These moments would be keyed to certain plans he has for the future. In the future if he decides that plan was a bad idea he can scry on his past self in that moment, his past self will detect the scrying and change plans accordingly.


                    Time Thickening (Thicken the Walls of Time)

                    A mage can also make it more difficult to cast Time effects into a specific time and place. Though this is written mostly in the context of blocking scrying and travel, it should work on other effects like speeding and slowing time.
                    This doesn’t involve moving in time so it doesn’t invoke the +3 difficulty and Paradox multiplication.


                    Time Ward

                    Correspondence 2’s Thickening effect appears to be the basis for minor wards, so Time 2’s may also be the basis for Time based wards. Presumably these would be wards that make it more difficult to act in the range of the spell during a specific time period. Since spells, by default, are cast on places, this wouldn’t be much different from the Correspondence based ward.

                    Setting conditions on the ward would likely involve the same kind of supplemental spheres that Correspondence does.

                        Time 3

                        ••• Time Contraction or Dilation/“Bullet Time”/ Rewind Time

                        Speeding or slowing her relationship with time, the mage can now gain multiple actions, slow other characters or phenomena, or rewind small snatches of time. In game terms, every two successes allow the character to take one additional action that does not involve casting magick (only one Arete roll may be made per turn); or else slow another character, object, or even herself down by one increment per success. (Four successes, for example, would slow a person down to a quarter of his normal speed.)

                        By rewinding time, the mage can also move her immediate surroundings back one turn for every two successes – an Effect that pulls her out of the normal flow of time and allows her to retcon an action or two. (See above.)

                        Combining this Rank with other Spheres, the mage can affect other Patterns (Forces, Life, or Matter 2), cast Effects across distance and time (Correspondence 3), move back in time while recalling events from the future she just left (Life 3/Mind 1), or even invoke multiple probabilities (Entropy 3). Again, such attempts are Paradox magnets with awful long term consequences.

                        Speed or Slow Time (Time Contraction and Dilation)

                        Exactly what it says on the tin. I want to note that as written it doesn’t take supplemental spheres to slow time for a person or thing. Locking spheres would still be necessary if the target moves out of range, though.

                        This doesn’t involve moving backwards in time so it doesn’t invoke the +3 difficulty and Paradox multiplication.


                        Temporal Singularity

                        When I picture this effect, I normally think of two different flows of time, the normal one and the sped up or slowed down one. A mage could reasonably have a gradient of time, say designate a location where time is running its slowest and the closer someone or something is to it the slower time passes for that thing. It’s a bit like pop culture depictions of how time functions in the near proximity to a black hole.

                        Reload Quicksave (Rewind Time)

                        The mage can also briefly rewind time a matter of turns. He is not included in the rewind process. Despite the name I gave this, the mage is not restored to the state he was in at the point of return unless he adds extra spheres.

                        This is directly stated in the Base Prime section to use the enhanced difficulty and paradox rules for time travel and is automatically vulgar (though quite possibly without witnesses), so barring any helpful modifiers this has a difficulty of 10 and multiplies paradox by the number of turns.


                        Fast Forward

                        A mage should also be able to fast forward several turns, which is functionally equivalent to disappearing for several turns. Since this is forward movement, there is no difficulty or paradox penalty.

                        Conjunctional Effects


                        This is one of the bad entries for conjunctional effects, the given examples are quite vague.

                        Affect Other Patterns (Time 3 + Pattern Sphere 2)

                        Ambiguity Resolution

                        Affect other patterns… with Time 3? In general? I can only assume this is meant to refer to locking effects. The lack of any specifics is jarring.

                        Cast Across Distance and Time

                        Contradiction Resolution – Cast Across Time


                        I would have thought, based on the way Correspondence and Spirit scale, that Time 2 was the rank to cast across time. Time 2 doesn’t claim the ability, though, and we can read “cast Effects across distance and time” as giving the power of casting across time to Time 3. Correspondence 3 is still wrong as casting across space is Corr 2. That said, a mage would have to have Corr 3 to use Time 3 across space, so this line is not wrong about the requirements, it just doesn’t match how M20 states sphere costs

                        Casting back in time is not one of the events directly stated to cause Temporal Ultra-Megadox, but it does involve manipulating the past in a significant way so STs may easily and reasonably say that it counts.

                        Move Back in Time While Remembering

                        Contradiction Resolution

                        In other threads I considered this a contradiction because just a paragraph before a mage could rewind time without having to add spheres to remember. However, if a mage is rewinding himself as well as everything else (which he might undo recent wounds), he would naturally lose memories unless he does something to stop that. I don’t see why the mage needs both Mind and Life, though. Either should suffice.

                        Another possibility is this effect lets the mage duplicate himself. He appears in a past where his previous self still exists. That self continues the way he did before (within reason) until such time as he casts the spell to rewind time. The later self gets to act however he wants. It’s a time based co-location effect where one of the selves knows what is going to happen, which might explain the particular use of Life 3/Mind 1.

                        ST’s who use this the second way should decide whether the future self can cast on the same turn as the past self. That’s normally not possible even with self duplication, but the future self also isn’t on the same turn in his timeline as the past self.

                        Both versions count as rewinding time for the purposes of difficulty and paradox.

                        Invoke Multiple Probabilities

                        Ambiguity Resolution – There is no Resolution

                        Invoke multiple probabilities to do what? I can’t resolve this because there’s too little substance to even be wrong. If the book had said ‘At Time 3, a mage can cast Time 3/Entropy 3 spells’ it would have said just as much.

                        If anyone has any ideas, let me know.

                        Added: Aleph suggests that this could be invoking alternate pasts. Pasts that did not happen but were likely to happen. Two applications of this would be pulling an item from an alternate timeline that came from that alternate past, and a microrewind that causes that alternate past to happen.

                                    Time 4

                                    •••• Time Determinism/ Trigger Effect/Time Bubble/ Anchor Point

                                    Now the mage learns to withdraw herself from the normal flow of time, hold Effects until they get triggered by events, or – by adding in Correspondence, Forces, Life, Matter, and/or Spirit – capture other beings or phenomena in bubbles of time. Thus, a mighty (vulgar) Time/ Forces/ Entropy Effect could capture a tornado and shunt it off into no-time space until some trigger sets it free. At this level of expertise, the mage can also set a temporal anchor point for herself for when she dares to travel through time at Rank 5.

                                    Stop Time (Time Bubble?)

                                    This is one of two things that I can count as withdrawing from the normal flow of time and the one that makes sense in Time 4. The mage can essentially freeze time while still moving around.
                                    Despite dramatically playing with the flow of time, the mage is not changing his location in the time stream so this doesn’t invoke the +3 difficulty and Paradox multiplication.


                                    Nap Time

                                    A tired mage can stop time and take a nap. This is nothing special, but it is definitely what I would use this power for.


                                    Trigger Effect (Trigger Effect)

                                    The signature ability of Time 4. The mage can essentially pause a spell until a given time or condition is met. This doesn’t specifically say that the spell can determine or detect those events with Time alone and I suspect that it needs supplemental spheres similar to how wards and geases do. Strictly speaking, though, that isn’t stated here.

                                    This does not involve travelling back in time so there is no increased difficulty or paradox… or is there.


                                    Propagation Through Time Takes… Time?

                                    In the movie Looper changes in the past seem to take time to reach the present. Someone loses a body part in the past but their future self continues to have it until a specific point then gets to see it disappear. If this is how time travel works in your game, there’s another purpose of Time Trigger. Setting a countdown on a destructive spell sent to the past gives a mage time to leave the area before the aftereffects of the spell reach the present. If he sends something radioactive to the past, he can leave the scene before the radiation ‘appears’ in the present.

                                    While the trigger itself wouldn’t cause an increase in difficulty or paradox, casting into the past probably would.


                                    Temporal Anchor Point (Anchor Point)

                                    Ambiguity Resolution

                                    This would be such a powerful and interesting effect… if M20 gave the slightest indication of what it was talking about. Yes, this is setting a point that is allegedly useful when time travelling, but with no system I don’t even know how or why a mage would need to do that. Perhaps it is meant to be a point that doesn’t take extra successes to travel to, regardless of how much time is covered.

                                    It might also be a point that bypasses the normal difficulty and Paradox penalties for travelling to.

                                    I set a temporal anchor point before looking into HDYDT to figure out what was going on. As often happens when I look into that book, I found myself back here with no memory, a bad headache, and a nasty note from myself.

                                    I’m kidding. The bad headache is because I do remember what HDYDT says. It nerfs Time 5 time travel to make this anchor point necessary and then says Temporal Anchor points let a mage return to that Anchor point with Time 4 instead of Time 5. Apparently Time 5 time travel is supposed to be substantively worst than Time 4.

                                    This may be the setup for time travel, but by itself the anchor doesn’t involve moving in time so it doesn’t invoke the +3 difficulty and Paradox multiplication.


                                    Conjunctional Effects

                                    Trap Things Outside Time

                                    A Mage can isolate things in a pocket of no-time removed from the rest of reality. Whether this pulls the targets out of reality or just makes a location inaccessible because no time is passing within is unclear and this effect could probably work either way.

                                    Contradiction Resolution - Pattern Spheres

                                    It is unclear why a mage would need to include supplemental spheres when targeting spheres aren’t really a thing and the subjects aren’t going to move outside the spell. That said, this spell could easily involve separating buildings from foundations and other pattern splits and if that happened pattern spheres would be needed for the separation.

                                            Time 5

                                            ••••• Temporal Travel/ Time Immunity

                                            The “Dr. Who Effect” allows the mage to exist outside of time; immunize people, places or things from time’s passage; or travel forward or backward through time. Such godlike feats… feats that often cause a mage to become forever lost to history… are often best left to the Storyteller’s discretion. Even for Masters, Time travel is a mysterious and maddeningly imprecise art. For extensive details about such things, see the sourcebook How Do You DO That?

                                            The Dr. Who Effect (Time Immunity)

                                            Originally posted by Terry Pratchett
                                            “Time was something that largely happened to other people; he viewed it in the same way that people on the shore viewed the sea. It was big and it was out there, and sometimes it was an invigorating thing to dip a toe into, but you couldn't live in it all the time. Besides, it always made his skin wrinkle.”
                                            This is arguably two effects, exist outside of time and immunize people against time. There’s a lot of overlap between the two, and they both fall under “The Doctor Who Effect.” For that matter so does Time Travel, but that still needs its own section.

                                            The mage can make himself or other people exist outside the flow of time and/or immunize them against its passage. This has several possible meanings, but every one I’ve come up with makes sense for Time 5.

                                            This does not involve movement in time (almost the opposite) so it doesn’t invoke the +3 difficulty and Paradox multiplication.


                                            A Fixed Point in Time

                                            The mage can make himself or someone else immune to changes in the timeline. Even if written out of existence the target will continue to exist.

                                            This would also make a lot of sense as a metamagic ability for Time Masters, though by default it is an effect.


                                            Temporal Robustness

                                            The mage can make a target is immune to damaging or complicating time effects such as being split across multiple times or time speeds. The target also can’t be hindered by slowed or stopped time.


                                            Functional Immortality

                                            The target is immune to the passing of time and does not age.


                                            Time Travel

                                            Travel in Time. It’s time travel, it functions like time travel however that happens to work in your stories.

                                            The management would like you to know how very, very much they hate this effect, they think it is a bad effect and that you should feel bad if you want to use it in your campaign. How dare you want to use the Time sphere to actually travel in time.

                                            If a mage actually uses this to go backwards, it is specifically stated to be a godlike effect, so 20+ successes at difficulty 10 plus two or three extra successes for going over the difficulty cap. In fact, the section on how to handle spell difficulties above 10 takes the time to specifically call out time travel as dangerous and rare, because the management really, really wants to make this point clear.


                                            Alternate Pasts

                                            One idea I’ve had for a time travel centric game is that each Tradition and Convention has its own past. All the pasts lead to the current moment but they are otherwise mutually exclusive. If such a scenario existed, a mage could go into multiple different pasts or jump between them.


                                            Everything Else (???)


                                            As with all Rank 5s, Time 5 should be able to do just about everything possible within the Sphere’s purview. Unless the ST specifically wants something locked behind archspheres, it should be possible here.
