                    2. Anarchs unbound.

                    Now, I know, that it will sound shocking for someone, but, still, when judging any RPG product I personally consider its usefulness for my games. I really don’t care for about kilobytes of purple prose that some sourcebook’s authors have included just to show how they (or their Mary/Marty Sues) are awesome. I want a product that I can use in my chronicles. The biggest trouble of all time was to find a product that would allow me to play and stage an adequate, normal and canonically accurate games set in Russia. So far, sWoD had been extremely unhelpful. No, it was downright awful. There were 3 books of VtM line that contained what could be called a “Vampire\s Russia Lore”. It was “Clanbook Brujah 1st ed”, “Clanbook Brujah Revised” and “Guide to the Anarchs”. About 1-2 pages each worth of “canonical Russian Lore”.

                    Each and every one of them is despicably horrible. Each one.

                    You know, just yesterday I’ve conducted a small experiment of sorts. On the message board where I post often there are a lot of Russians, Bulgarians, Serbs, Bosniaks, some Poles, Greeks one Brazillian and even one Canado-Italian patriot. So, I’ve posted an excerpt from the “Guide to the Anarchs” describing Bulgaria. The first response was “Oh moi Boh, WTF was that?!”. The second would be censored everywhere. The fifth response got into a deep historical, political and socioeconomically analysis that can be summed up in “Every single sentence is wrong. What kind of drugs the author was high on while writing that piece of bovine feces?”.

                    That’s why, as I’ve said previously in my OP, I won’t discuss the original WW books here. Otherwise, I’d have to write a comment about every sentence in their “canonical” descriptions of Russia, sometimes devoting several paragraphs just to describe how one of their sentences is plainly wrong.

                    What I've wanted was a book, that would give a Russia a justice in the VtM setting, a book, that would allow me to draw upon it’s wealth of material to set my chronicles in “Russia by Night”, that would be both interesting and canonically correct. While a lot of other European countries were graced with the adequate write-ups WoD-style, describing the history of vampires here, the strongholds of the Sects, the standing of various clans and etc. setting wise, Russia didn’t have the same treatment. And I really, really hoped, that this time, under the “Onyx Path” aegis, the situation could be improved.

                    Enter “Anarchs Unbound”.

                    Just reading the list of authors already sank my expectations. So, Justin Achilli is not only the developer of the whole line – he is one the authors here. A man, who wrote “Clanbook Brujah revised” – and I’ve already gave my opinion about its “Russian section”. Justin “Everything I write is already a canon, bitches!” Achilli – “White Wolves” personal version of Dan “I don’t give a damn about the fluff – lets add more comic books level of cheesiness here!” Abnett. An author who is both prolific and lacking in writing talent, with, apparently, nobody to tell him about his drawbacks.

                    But, surely, after all these years, Mr. Achilli actually devoted some time to self improvement and his writing won’t cause an instant gag reflex from the readers, right? Right!?

                    The title like “The Rise of Soviet Vampirism” already don’t promse anything worthy, objective and useful. Because “In Soviet Russia YOU drink vampire!”, or something like that.

                    The Russian Revolution of 1917, like most revolutions, almost immediately turned into a bloody civil war. Casualties on both sides of the conflict between the Red Bolsheviks and every Russian who opposed them numbered in the millions, with Tsar Nicholas and his family merely the most famous victims. By the time the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics was officially named, the Romanov dynasty and the hereditary nobility that had supported it since the 17th century had been swept away. The few Russian nobles to survive fled westward, to France, Britain, and beyond, for the most part to die in obscurity. Tonight, the non-claimant heir to the Tsar’s throne is over 90 and lists “insurance company executive” on his resume.
                    This is the first paragraph – and already it reaches the level of crappiness, stereotypical portrayal and historical inaccuracies of Ye Goode Olde WoD. Did the authors know that in the 21 centaury historical facts can be checked so easily that writing such things should be just shameful?

                    Oh, where should I start? First of all – about which Russia Revolution did the authors talk here? There were 2 in 1917 –one in February (that toppled the Czar), another in October (the Bolshevik one). Is it too hard to remember?

                    Next, the second sentence implies that the Russian Civil war was “Ebul Red Bolsheviks against the rest of Russia”, which is just plainly wrong. What about the Entente's Intervention, that drew to Russia soldiers from France, Britain, USA, Japan and even Romania ? What about German and Austro-Hungary occupied territories? What about the Czechoslovak Legion? What about Finland? What about the extensive use of Latvians and Chinese (yes, Chinese) by the Bolsheviks? What about all the wars that suddenly free Caucasian republics got themselves into – including a very unsuccessful one versus Turkey? Finally, what about Poland?

                    But, no the whole Russian Civil War is covered in just a couple of sentences. One of the most dramatic periods in countries history (not to mention – real mother lode for WoD scenarios and settings) got just a couple of sentences choke full of inaccuracies.

                    Oh, and finally – the text presumes some kind of genocide of literally all nobles in now Soviet Russia that every one noble either emigrated or were executed. Well, there were a lot of executed nobles, as well as the one who chose the exile. But not everyone. What about the general Aleksey Brusilov? What about the writer, the "Red count" Aleksey Tolstoy? What about diplomat count Aleksey Ignatyev? What about Lenin himself? And what about the mere fact that about 70% of Russian officer corps fought on the side of Bolsheviks during the Civil War, the majority of them – noble-born?

                    See? Just one paragraph worth of several sentences, but already a commentary larger that the original! Is this the new “V20” that the authors strive to – as bad as the original?

                    For the Russian Kindred, the Revolution actually started in 1900. Long after the rest of Europe had embraced modernization and made at least a pretense of trying to create a functional middle class, Russian gentry still held their peasants in a state of deliberate ignorance and deprivation through official state policies. By happy coincidence, these policies also fulfilled the desire of comfort-dulled vampires for massive herds that required neither maintenance nor discretion. The Tsarist Kindred, a loose confederation of Ventrue, Toreador, and Old Clan Tzimisce, were insular, arrogant, and imperious, and they generally showed little but disdain for the other clans that weren’t part of their courtly society.
                    Well, on the one hand – there is something positive in this passage. We learn that the ruling positions in Russia by night were held by Ventrue, Toreador, and Old Clan Tzimisce (the last one was deduced by the Russian Storytellers long time ago and included as a semi-canon long, long time ago). But – what about Camarilla? How did all this arrangement work? Did the Camarilla just accepted that somewhere in its domain there are towns claimed by Tzimisce (“non-Sabbat, yeah, sure, like we gonna believe it!”), but that other Camarilla aligned vampires recognize their authority and integrate them fully into the existing power structure (what an affront for the posh Victorian vampires, I say!). This tidbit is interesting but leaves more questions, than answers, i.e. how Russian vampires were structured pre-Revolutions?

                    No, this paragraph is more devoted to the portraying much of doom and glum in Russia. Here, we (surprisingly, me included) find out, that the main cause for all Russian revolutions was the absence of a middle class (preferably in its idealized American iteration, with car, house and 2.5 kids), not something other like, you know, everything Grandpa Lenin already mentioned in his works? Also, I’d like to see the said “embrace of modernization” by the “rest of Europe” in Spain, Portugal, Italy, Greece, Bulgaria, Serbia, Austro-Hungarian Empire, Denmark and Sweden. If authors would grace us with it, I’m sure it would be a fun read.

                    Finally, an extremely hilarious part (for me at least) about how the "ebul Russian pomeshiks", somehow, “held their peasants in a state of deliberate ignorance and deprivation through official state policies”. I guess, our esteemed authors have never heard about the Emancipation reform of 1861, that have freed the Russian peasants from their serfdom. So, no, Russian gentry had no power whatsoever to “held in ignorance” people over whom they had virtually no power. And if our well-read and clever authors of “Anarchs Unbound” would actually research the said time period, they would discover (to their undoubtedly great astonishment), that life in Russian village after the Reform of 1861 was indeed “Getting Better” (tm), with the spread of Zemstvo sponsored education and medical care.

                    Don’t get me wrong – there were a lot of enormous problems during Nicholas II reign, that finally resulted in the Revolution of 1905-1907 (btw, why don’t mention it and the Role Kindred played in it?). But what is listed in this paragraph by the authors – this is just plain wrong. Russia of t he said period could be a really scary place even without obligatory WoDish hamfisted grim darkness, with the famines in 1891-92, the famine of 1901-03, the unsuccessful Russian-Japanese war of 1904-1905, Revolution of 1905-1907, terroristic actions by the "Combat Organization" of the party of Socialist-Revolutionaries (EsErs), that offed 2 ministers, 33 governors, 16 mayors; 7 admirals and generals and 26 police agents; the peasant uprisings, battleship “Potemkin’s” uprising, the Moscow uprising of 1905-6, the beginning of the Russian parliament, the vide variety of political parties that were present both as official and “underground” Opposition. In short – a lot of excellent Anarch (and Vampire) material was just ignored. Why? What for?

                    The First World War greatly weakened the Tsar’s hold over his subjects, and the power of the Tsarist Kindred alliance diminished with it.
                    Okay, authors of “AU”, tell me this – how exactly the war that whips up patriotic fervor and gives a police even bigger authority to arrest, try and quickly dispose of the body afterwards (in the name of national security, of course!), the fact that the Czar personally took the responsibility as the commander in chief, the public shunning of all “unpatriotic” publications and speeches, somehow diminish the power not only of the Imperial Russian administration, but also of the “Kindred alliance”? The only thing that perhaps weakened “Tsar’s hold over his subjects” was the Dry Law.

                    A sizeable coterie of Brujah ancillae enamored of the theories of Karl Marx had infiltrated Russian society just after the turn of the century, and as the Great War began, these insuregnts paved the way for later mortal revolutionaries such as Lenin, Trotsky, Kerinsky, Kropotkin and many others, first by helping to smuggle many of them back into the country after long exiles and then by covertly providing financial, logistical, and supernatural aid to their various revolutionary movements.
                    Oh, there are so much wrong in just one (1) sentence, that it’s simply unbelievable!

                    First of all – what about the res of Russian political spectrum of the early XX centaury? Just don’t tell me there wasn’t any Brujah liberal-democratic “Cadets”, center-right “Octobrists”, chauvinistic anti-Semitic monarchists of the "Union of the Russian People", Social Democrats, where both wings of it, both Bolsheviks and Mensheviks, claimed to represent the true Marxists teaching. Finally, wjat about the most known, badass, terroristic and popular among the peasantry party like SRs? It won’t surprise me if the vast majority of Brujah (especially, the Russia-born ones) would support them from the get go. But Lenin’s Bolsheviks? A marginal party that was in the March of 1917 pretty much obscure one?

                    Next, how did a Brujah clan arranged something like

                    1) Safe passage in the closed car train for the Lenin, for his journey from the Switzerland to Petrograd through the Germany – with the German authorities agreement.
                    2) The passage forvTrotsky and 300+ of like minded “revolutionaries” from the USA to Russia?

                    No, I’m really interested how the clan that was incapable to act together from the nearly an Ancient times, would, suddenly, organize such costly and dangerous operation, that would require a level of international organization that is more characteristic for Termers, Nosferatu and Ventrue, but defiantly, not for “Brujah-as-written”! Or, how would Brujah provide any kind of successful help (be it financial, logistical or supernatural) to anyone during the Russian Revolutions of 1917 if they are so disorganized as a clan, with too many factions (even in one particular country) thinking that the “common resources” should be spent their way?


                    And finally – did the personal beliefs and prejudices forbid our esteemed authors from checking the Internet (even Wikipedia!) from finding out that
                    1. Alexander Kerensky was in Russia all his life prior the 1917 Revolutions and that he wasn’t a Bolshevik – he was SR.
                    2. Pyotr Kropotkin, besides that he was one of the founding fathers of Anarchism, was also a high-born noble who, also, didn’t require “Brujah’s help” in returning back to Russia (and if they DID arrange his return from the Switzerland, then see my complaints in the Lenin’s example earlier)


                    The Brujah’s plans for revolution were well-conceived and almost flawlessly executed, while the response of the Tsarist Kindred was weak, vacillating, and crippled by internal dissension. The revolutionaries spent years identifying the feeding restrictions of several of the most important Ventrue nobles, and when the revolt began in earnest, they were able to deprive these elders of their vitae sources. They were also able to locate the resting places of many Russian Tzimisce and steal away the soil upon which they depended for sleep.

                    Wait-wait-wait-wait here! What?! Did authors just in the uncertain terms said, that in 1917 in Russia there was only one Revolution, i.e. “Czarists vs Bolsheviks”? What about the February revolution, the one tha'vet toppled Nicholas II? Or, what, you decided that too much historically accurate information without crappy clichés (“Bolsheviks toppled the Tsar!”) would somehow result in the head’s explosion of your readers, who have to face the horror of too much new historically correct information?

                    I'd also like to see how these plucky revolutionaries managed all these amazing stuff. Say, how would they eliminate a heard of Petrograd’s Ventrue who feeds on officers (good luck!), or, more importantly, who would they “steal away the soil” of Tzimisce. Especially the last one. If my players would come to me with such suggestion – I will undoubtedly allow them! Only to see how the “Heartless Bitch Reality” tears their plans in shreds – and them as well. But, I guess, here the Storyteller was either too generous, or such an attempt even wasn’t played in real life.

                    Within a few years, almost the entire ruling establishment of pre-revolutionary Russian Kindred had either met Final Death or else been forced to flee.
                    Wow-wow-wow! Slow down here! So we have here something on par with the original Anarch Revolt, the total destruction of Camarilla/Elders power over 1/6 of the Earth, and, what, nothing? No reaction from the Camarilla? Not even “Blimey! Those dastardly Brujah Bolsheviks just have toppled the Camarilla established authorities from Warsaw to Vladivostok! We, as Camarilla, must do something decisive about the local Kindred, what not?”. Why this event wasn’t called previously as the “Second Anarch Revolt”? Why wasn’t it called like that now, in that very book? Because the change that it describe is monumental (if true)! Just Google-search all of the main cities of Russian Empire. Then imagine that in 90% of them Camarilla’s ancien regime suddenly (I have no other word) fallen and gave way for… something Brujah-Revolutionary. It’s a huge territory with a lot of big towns, some of them quite ancient. And then – poof! – Camarilla just loost control over them. Is it so outrageous for me to expect some sort of reaction from Camarilla officials, hidden somewhere in the original WW cWoD books… or in this “AU” volume? ‘Cause I can’t find it!

                    Imagine that! The greatest political catastrophe in the Camarilla's history since the 15th centaury – and not a word?! Either that or (or!) the change wasn’t so widespread, the Brujah Council remained as nominal part of Camarilla and promised to respect it’s Traditions and Protocols. But then where is confirmation of that? I can’t find it in this new book! Or maybe (which is more likely) our esteemed authors just didn’t give a damn about all this stuff and expect us all to accept their so-called “work” as the “new well-though canon”. Such plot-holey piece of donkey’s dung? Thank you, but no!

                    And our esteemed volume speaks nothing (nothing at all!) about the SABBAT’s involvement in both pre- and post-Revolutionary Russia. I mean, how would they skip such a prize?! It was right here, during the Civil War the opportunities were enormous! But nooooooo! Authors preferred just ignore all that! Why?!

                    Soon, Russia was only one state within the larger USSR, an emerging global power under the dictatorship of Joseph Stalin, the first of several autocrats whose brutality served the Brujah Council’s aims. The Brujah never directly controlled Stalin or his successors (or at least not to the very end). However, their devotion to Leninist-Marxist theory left them convinced that Soviet autocracy would eventually evolve on its own into a perfect socialist state, and they were loath to interfere in this “natural evolution,” even as Stalin’s policies led to the deaths of millions and Kruschev’s brought the world to the brink of nuclear Armageddon.
                    Where is the “facepalm” icon here? Ok, this will do.

                    You know, every author has his favorite subject. Like Mr. Achilli here. He wrote in “Clanbook Brujah Revised” a lot of stuff, including, how, actually, Brujah had to share power with other clans, that Brujah Council (because in Soviet Russia it must be called a “Soviet” – a Council!) was a snake pit of political bickering. These facts were further corrected/retconned by other Clanbooks and supplements, that, finally, there were quite little from the original Achilli’s write up of Russia. But here he got his chance to present his ultimate (and crappy as well) version of “Russia by night”! This time, he concentrated on Brujahs only. Sharing power with other clans? Oh no, what a ridiculous though! You can burn all the books that say otherwise! Hmm, and impressive pyre you got here…

                    And also, what about Kruschev who, supposedly “brought the world to the brink of nuclear Armageddon”. For anyone even remotely familiar with the Cuban crisis it’s quite obvious that the whole situation wasn’t as clear cut as presented here, i.e. “Ebul Rushins are threatening the World! OMG!”. And that’s not even discussing the NATO’s nukes in Turkey, deployed here in the spitting distance of the USSR (in the name of democracy, freedom and all ideals that the authors of “AU” express, of course!).

                    The Council never formally identified itself as an Anarch enterprise and both its brutal authoritarianism and its quiet bigotry against non-Brujah were certainly inconsistent with the mainstream Movement’s ideals. But so far as the Camarilla was concerned, the Soviet Union and its satellite states were Anarch Free States in everything but name.

                    And done nothing about it?! So, some upstart Anarchs from the “Glorious USA’s” California demand a constant Justicar Prsence, but not entire Russia? A territory much (much!) bigger, populous and with longer history of Kindred settlement gets the official “Meh, whatever…”?! I’ve always knew that Camarilla Elders were senile – but this is a new low mark (either for Elders or authors)!

                    The defining characteristic of Soviet-style Kindred politics was a total rejection of the traditional, neo-feudalist “Prince of the City” model in favor of a national “rule by coterie” government that had never been seen before and that was, as far as the Camarilla Princes were concerned, utterly terrifying. For its own part, the Brujah Council certainly considered itself ideologically allied with the Anarch Movement and would spend the next seven decades seeking to export Marxist revolution around the globe, as much to distract the Camarilla from attacking Mother Russia itself as to spread its own revolutionary dreams. For the Kindred, the Cold War that marred the 20th century was simply one more front in the War of Ages.

                    Good! At long last we have another tidbit of useful information! That “Soviet Russian Brujah” ruled over their cities via “councils”, not the Prince (as if the term “Brujah Council” wasn’t an enormous hint…). That’s nice! There is no more Princes in the Brujah’s domains – but what about the rest of titles? Is there a Sheriff, or is his function delegated to “Troikas”? What about the Primogen – did the rest of clans still have a representative? What about the Keeper of the Elysium – did the Revolutionary minded Brujah even allow such title to exist? How did the “City Soviets” resolved basic Kindred law dilemmas, i.e. domain disputes, rights of creating ghouls/progeny? There are so many questions – and no answers! ANSWER ME, BOOK!

                    For most of the USSR’s existence, the Camarilla was not privy to the inner deliberations of the Brujah Council. However, Kremlinologists among the Camarilla know something happened to the Council during the 1980's. While the Council had permitted Leonid Brezhnev to rule for nearly twenty years, his successors, Andropov and Chernenko, were each quickly removed (possibly, some speculate, by direct Brujah intervention).

                    Where is a facepalm icon when you need one? Oh, there it is!

                    What a pile of utter nonsense! Seriously? Vampires now are offing terminally ill soviet gerontocrats? What kind of crazy world it is!? Andropov was in “working condition” for just 6 months! He spent the rest of his life dying! Chernenko was already dying when he became the head of the USSR. Killing them off for real would be a gesture of mercy on behalf of Brujah.

                    Also, this passage (written, undoubtedly, by Justin “I’m a Vampire-God, baby!” Acilli) totally ignores the information from other source books. Like, oh, “Clanbook Ventrue Revised” (written, thankfully, not by Justin Achilli), for example, where there is said that caln Ventrue always maintained contact with it’s members in the Soviet Union, even had a cordial ralations with them despite all this “Cold War” nonsense. Beside the fact that this contradicts the assumption, that Camarilla totally abandoned Russia after the Revolution, this piece of information also points out that you don’t need any bloody Kremlinologists (i.e., as demonstrated time and again, “some charlatans”) when you have “an insider’s information” via one of the Camarilla’s well respected clans.

                    But, nah! Lets ignore that!

                    In 1985, Mikhail Gorbachev took charge and immediately initiated a number of economic and democratic reforms.
                    Not immediately, not initiated, not personally, not economic and defiantly not democratic reforms. Otherwise the sentence is perfect (in the West’s “Gorby’s Worship partyline” at least). Oh, and this sentence full of love and adoration also misses Gorby’s atrocious national politic, that resulted in Nagorno-Karabakh conflict. As a final note – I, as a Russian, personally desire to every Westerner Gorbachov’s lover’s country to experience everything the USSR had experienced under his rule. With a personal Nagorno-Karabakh, of course.

                    Simultaneously, the Camarilla unexpectedly received word from the Council that it wished to discuss a possible “normalization” of their relations. While the Council expressed no interest in formally rejoining the Camarilla, it was very interested in establishing some type of alliance against unspecified mutual threats.
                    Then, in 1990, all communication with the Brujah Council abruptly and completely stopped. Every effort to communicate with the Brujah leadership failed. Within weeks, any attempt to communicate with any Kindred anywhere in Russia was equally unsuccessful. By the end of the year, Clan Tremere reported to the Inner Council that all of its supernatural mechanisms for spying on the USSR had been rendered inoperative.
                    [Sigh]

                    So you’ve decided to be “oh so faithful to the original” and use Baba Yaga? How pathetic!

                    Look, this all “Brujah Council as presented” is a weak written, crappy purple prose – nothing new here for me and for other’s, who want to use Russia as a VtM setting. But reviving of Baba Yaga and of evverithing lame-o stuff indentified with her? Seriously?

                    Lets talk honestly here, okay? All this “Brujah Council of Russia” was early “White Wolf” invention, because their game saw the light in 1991 when the USSR was still a country. And then it (suddenly!) collapsed. Our authors had very approximate knowledge about their own country, and atrociously low knowledge about Europe, and the non-existent knowledge about USSR and Russia. So, without straining their beautiful brains with some “facts” (bah! who needs them, anyway?) they started to seek a plot device that would allow them both
                    1. Describe Russia without tiring them with that dreadful “research”.
                    2. Discourage players and Storytellers from further explore a territory that they, proud White Wolf, don’t know a jack about – and have no desire to learn.


                    Enter “Baba Yaga” and her “Plot Deviced” Shadow Curtain.

                    “Because”, thought WWs, “the Western Public is already thoroughly indoctrinated upon the «Iron Curtain» myth – it’s stupid enough to accept as written this pulled from our collective posterior plot devise, that would make Russia (in the most ambiguous way, ‘cause we suck at geography!) effectively unplayable.
                    Oh, and we don’t give a damn about them primitive Russkis in Russia that would have some troubles with running WoD games set in their countries. They are not human being’s anyway!”

                    Either that or the inebriation upon a lot of restricted substances (my personal theory is that it was crack, that helped WWs with the metaplot for the most of the 90s, but I can’t prove it) after which any shitty idea suddenly seams awesome.

                    My personal opinion? Baba Yaga (as written) and “Shadow Curtain” are just lame plot devices used in attempt to solve the obvious plot hole. It was true for the 90s, it is true now. The fact that WWs realized how lame their 2nd plot hole was (i.e. Baba Yaga as the big bad and the source of all evil in Russia), that they had to resort to the second plot device (and 3rd plot hole in the row!) just to get rid of it speaks volumes. So, I must ask – why revive something already crappy, plot-holey and lame?!

                    Don’t get me wrong – I think that Баба Яга could be a terrific character for any WoD chronicle set in Russia – and not even VtM one. In Russian folklore Baba Yaga has so many roles, connotations and positions, that limiting her just tot “Da Ebul Witch of Da Ebul Russia that has always been Ebul anyway” is just wasting so much potential! If Joseph Campbell had known about Russian Myths and Legends while he wrote his “A Hero with a Thousand Faces” (a book that a lot of WW authors listed among theirs sources of inspiration) then Baba Yaga would’ve find her rightful place in the “Adventure Cycle”. She would become a psychopomp, a First Guardian between the world of Humans and Others. She testes the bravery of all newcomers with her apparent command of magic, skulls with the gloving eyes upon her fence and straightforward claim of being a cannibal. But just with the right choice of words, some respect that is due to the elderly, with the performance of some quest or domestic chore she easily become Hero’s ally and helper. Really, a more interesting concept, than what was presented by the original WW “Baba Yaga’s” write up, do you agree?

                    Simultaneously, and with a speed that stunned Western observers, the entire Soviet Union and the network of satellite states that surrounded it simply collapsed. Within the space of a few years they simply turned their backs on communism and adopted Western-style free market principles and nominally democratic political systems.
                    Uhmm… How should we consider this? That Baba Yaga was totes pro-pemocratic/nationalistic/russophobic Revolutions that engulfed co-called “Eastern Block”? Or that Brujahs, despite the evidence of supporting Gorbachov and allowing various dissidents to live, were the only ones who’ve kept USSR/Warsaw Pact together? Also, what’ve happened to the theory of “Vampires, really not influence real life politics” that was adopted since the Revised?

                    In the summer of 1991, the Camarilla sent in the first investigative team of archons to find out just what the hell had happened. Within hours of their arrival, all contact had ceased and no team member was ever heard from again. The same fate befell the second, third and fourth investigative teams, the last of which included a former Justicar. Since then, the Camarilla has given up on finding out what is happening in Russia and is reduced to hoping that whatever is responsible doesn’t spread. Thus far, it has not, nor has it initiated any sort of hostile action or indeed any communications at all with the West. Tonight, the Camarilla ignores Russia and hopes for the best.
                    Well, thank you very much, “Onyx Path” folks, plus all those authors you employ, that, perhaps in the past, had the experience with writing WoD books! Now it is official – thanks to your stereotypical vision about Russia, I can not stage any playable and canonically correct VtM chronicles set in Russia! Screw everything that Russian WoD fans (blissfully unaware of your “developments”) have written about “Russia by night”, its history, current situation, clans powerbases and sects’ plans. “Ye Olde Godes of Dev Team” ruled that Russia is just another “no-go territory”, that they are either too lazy (or too ignorant) to describe in full. This putrid shit-piled “Shadow Curtain” have worked 20 years ago – what should prevent it from working now, in our Globalised Age? No, what really, besides logic and facts, butwe have already ruled that out (as well as the opinion of Russia resident gamers)!

                    While the destroyer of the Brujah Council does not appear to be a threat to the Camarilla, it has been devastating to the Anarchs. The Soviet Union had been quietly supporting Anarch-based revolutionary activities around the world for over 70 years. Throughout the 1990's, the Anarchs in South and Central America and in Africa suffered serious losses due to lack of financial and materiel support. Luckily, in the 21st century, help of a very different sort would arrive.
                    No, really? “Soviet Russian” Brujah’s sending “military consultants” to Cuba or Nicaragua upon a ship? What kind of madness is that? And even if such operation was successful – where are the confirmation of that via older fluff of cWoD or something from that book?

                    Verdict: This splat book just've killed any hope for a normal VtM game set in Russia. Just imagine that! You have a core book of VtM, no matter what edition – i.e. you have just enough information to portray clans and sects as they are, in their "pure state", in the theoretical “vacuum”, without any local flavor or peculiarities. And then came the book, that, in theory, would widely expand your experience in playing one of the main Vampire factions that is so universally widespread.

                    Instead you get a piece of “go-se”, that basically tells, that you were running your chronicles wrong all the time (with the additionall information how your country sucks as the cherry on the top), so you just are not interested in whatever else is written here. Net_Kindred? Oh, swell – remind me about them when they (and about 90% of other character groups presented in the book) would become available for my FUBAR’ed by the official developers country.

                    In short – “Anarchs Unbound” is bad. What is doubly bad is that it became a “strike from where you least expect”. A Russophobic rant as the new official cannon for “V20” published in “Anarchs” supplement? Honestly, I didn’t expect this.

                    But still, there are still more atrocious “WoD20” books published by “Onyx Path” – and I will discuss them all in my next posts! Stay tuned!

                        Your rant is ~5 times longer than the section you're ranting about.
