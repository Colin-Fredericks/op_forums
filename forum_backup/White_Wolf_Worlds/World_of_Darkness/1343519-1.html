            Since torture has been mentioned, here's my two cents.

            Regular torture, the kind that exists IRL, works very well if you need information that is falsifiable. For example, if you need to torture someone for the code to a safe, and they lie, then you get instant feedback on their lie. The longer it takes to falsify the information given to you the less effective torture is.

            So torture is not a good means of gathering information, in most cases. The tortured person believes they will be killed since torture radicalizes most victims - it tells them that every worst expectation they had of you is true. Therefore they are more likely to lash out with a "fuck you" response that is as inconvenient for you as possible, giving believable false intelligence.

            Here's a quick summary based on what I've read:

            - torture is effective when it comes to acquiring rapidly falsifiable information like a code or nearby location

            - torture is an effective means of intimidating a person and a populace in some cases - historically it has been used more often as a punishment in reality

            - torture can be effective in eliciting the exact coerced response you want from a person - if you want someone to admit they killed someone, torture can make them do it... and torture IS an effective means if you don't actually care whether they are guilty. A fake confession can be a great excuse for cracking down on a group within society that you're not fond of - after all one of them just admitted to conspiring to kill the President!

            - torture is mostly ineffective if the information you're seeking is time-sensitive - a captive of even average intelligence will realize that they only need to hold out a certain length of time, which will strengthen their resolve

            - torture ensures that your captive has their worst suspicions of their captor confirmed - when the tables are turned (by the captive's organization, allies or friends, if not themselves) they will use similar methods

            - torture is primarily ordered and supported by those who don't perform or witness it - because it has so little intelligence value and does so much damage to both the tortured and the torturer, and actually loses you potential informants and allies, it only continues since those who order it are detached from it

            Note that bargaining isn't the same as torture; "if you give us this time-sensitive non-falsifiable information, and we find out it's true in due course, then you and your family will be protected etc". Torturers can utilize bargaining, and often it is the bargaining which is successful and not the torture.

            MAGE STUFF

            Now since Mage is, well, Mage, using torture to perform a Mind 2 to 4 effect where you trawl through a person's mind is completely valid. But it's a method which has loads of downsides, and there are many other options. So Mages would only perform torture if they BELIEVED it to be a highly effective means of extracting intelligence (and this myth is prevalent throughout society, and within some law enforcement and intelligence communities), or if they LIKED it as a means of gaining information (this would apply to Nephandi, sociopathic mages, mages who practice darker or sadistic magick).

                            The immediately falsifiable aspect doesn't really change the counter-arguments against the effectiveness of torture. Just because you can show immediately that an answer is false doesn't tell you why you got a false answer. Did they lie about knowing because you were torturing them? Did they try to give you an honest answer but remembered things incorrectly (the code was 12345, but they said 12354)? Or are they actually just lying to you despite knowing the truth? All you know is that the answer was false, not why, and thus don't know how best to proceed. Torturing someone that doesn't actually know the answer is futile in the first place even if there was a high likelihood they'd know based on other intel. Increasing torture on someone that though they were honestly giving in but made a mistake runs a risk of shutting them down. If they're purposefully lying after torture, there's no reason to believe more pain will change whatever gave them the mentality to lie directly in the first place.

                            And lets be realistic here, the whole premise of an immediately falsifiable piece of information, that's no under a time demand (or related to time sensitivity, something like a passcode might have an autolock after too many failed incorrect attempts), and has no non-torture solutions that are at least as effective and definitely more moral, quickly become rather fanciful to meet all these criteria.

                            There's no such thing as an unbreakable safe. Why spend days torturing someone when you could just call a professional safe cracker (assuming you don't have one as a group with the resources to hold people captive and torture them without intervention or oversight)? Enemy troop deployments/etc. are going to become time sensitive the moment the enemy realizes you've captured someone that has the information to spill even if it wasn't immediately time sensitive before. Oh, and as many intelligence operatives have testified to, the adage of catching more flies with honey than vinegar applies to this as well when it comes to effectiveness. Etc., etc., etc.

                            Taking everything into account, even situations where torture might yield useful information, there's no defense of it. It could work, but there's always a better option in anything but the most contrived hypothetical examples, and that's before factoring in the moral/ethical side of it.
