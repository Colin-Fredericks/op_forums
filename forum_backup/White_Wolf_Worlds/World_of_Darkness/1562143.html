    [V5] Project Rules - how do you use them?

    07-25-2026, 11:20 AM
    I've read both the core book Project Rules and the Player's Guide version, and I really want to know how others engage (or not) with those systems.

    Projects rules at first seem like an interesting concept: how to execute long-term initiatives by the players whose benefits are tangible and can be objectively defined.

    But I found them badly written and excessively punishing, at least until you manage to reliably get 12+ dice pools, when it's maybe not hard enough. I don't think they're too convoluted, but they certainly could use better wording.

    And the Player's Guide version basically throws everything away with what boils down to a way of describing regular Extended Actions, but easier to perform and with wonky wording. By the way, overall I get from that book that the writer had way less understanding of the basic rules than they should, even if the ideas within are sound.

    Making better rules shouldn't be hard, but first I'd like to know from the community: do you use Projects at all? How? What changes if any you make and why? What's your opinion on the RAW for them?

      In all the times I've played V5, Project rules never came up. I'll be honest, I just finished reading them; they are so well hidden I wasn't even aware they existed, lol.
      I don't have access to the Player's Guide, so I'll comment on the Core Book rules.

      Outside of the initial roll, I don't find them particularly punishing, as long as you don't try to expedite the project. It seems to me like the project advances on its own at each increment after the initial launch test.

      Goal Rolls seem broken to me. I don't like that they can finish the project in one attempt. You can technically launch a millennia-long project, get a lucky roll, and have it end the week after. I also don't like that a non-thinking entity rolls a pool. I'd rather have a fixed difficulty, unless it's a very chaotic and unpredictable project.

      I also don't really like rolling Background + Skill, but I know it's something V5 uses elsewhere. In my opinion, Backgrounds are tools, not skill sets.

      I also dislike the gambling aspect of it. You can gamble Backgrounds, which are worth experience points, and lose them. It's been a while since I've played V5, but as far as i remember there's no sanctity of merits, so those are just lost experience points. Since you can't spend Willpower, you're only one unlucky roll away from losing all your stake at the project launch, this feels bads and is completely outside the player's control.

      I don't know what I would change personally. I find the rules simple and yet weirdly unwieldy. Nothing that an extended test or a social maneuver system like CoD couldn't handle on its own.

      Generally, in my current CoD campaign, I ask player to accumulate a number of successes to reach their goal and if they have background dot they can spend, such as resources, contact, allies, etc, I let them add them as automatic success toward their goal.

      In my non-vampire homebrew system, the way I handle project is that it has a base difficulty and a completion threshold that’s a fixed number of successes. If you beat the base difficulty when you make a test, you add 1 + the margin of success toward the completion. There are other technicalities for handle complications and failures, but it essentially boils down to this.

      Another way to project would probably be to assign it "Doors" and, at each given increment, ask how the player wants to proceed, then set a difficulty based on what they are trying to achieve and how it relates to their project. On a win, they'd open a Door, or two on a critical win. If they wanted to expedite things, maybe I'd add a penalty based on the number of extra Doors they wanted to open.

      I wouldn't let a single die roll complete a project; otherwise, it could have been solved in a scene.

                          Alrighty, then let’s start with RAW.
                          I’ll restrict myself to the core book, since it’s all I have access and try to run each example with these three stated goal in mind:
                          • I want to use the rule as written
                          • I want the character to be able to at least start their project.
                          • I’ll engage in good faith, to strongman the rules.
                          Example 1 - Netsuke

                          You already gave all the necessary details for the project, so first we need to settle on a dice pool for the launch test, which will be determined by your approach. Since the Regent is a Kindred, discipline uses are prohibited in the launch roll. We have to use background + Skill.
                          Any of the following would be appropriate:
                          • Haven + Craft to prepare to set your workshop.
                          • Resource + Occult to find interesting materials with obscure spiritual meaning.
                          • Contact + Academic to find interesting lore about netsuke, and materials native to the state of Virginia in an attempt to craft something that both represents your origin and destination.
                          • Insight + Status to find information about what the regent would prefer for material, by talking to people from their entourage.
                          Normally you would have to stake at least 1 dot for the benefit, but it’s not required. Let’s say the Benefit is one dot Mawla. The Regent is certainly powerful, but they won’t devolve their whole attention to you after one gift.

                          Since the scope is 1, you decide to bet 2 dots of haven a bit of a stretch given the project, but we end up justifying that you are making a place for your workshop, you are spending increased time in your haven, running the risk of attracting more hunter around your safe place or other court member starts to investigate your increased absence. Two dots of havens are lost as a result of installing your workshop. (I know a bit intense for a Netsuke, but let’s say you have a very messy workshop).

                          Let’s assume you go with Contact + Academic and the launch roll succeeds, we roleplay a scene where you go around antique Japanese shop to find inspiration, look online for inspiration, etc. You eventually set up for Cornus Florida(Flowering dogwood) as it’s Virginia state tree.

                          Once that’s done, we set up the Project die to 10. Since you only have 2 dots of haven and 2 dots of craft, we are looking at a pool of 4 dice. Rolling before the Project die reaches down to 3 is a very risky endeavor so you opt not to roll.

                          Once the project die reaches 3, you realize that you won’t make it to Elysium in time if you don’t start working faster, so you decide to roll Haven + Craft.
                          You roll a pool of 4, I roll a pool of 3. You win by a margin of 2. Now only 1 die remains. Since the Elysium is in 2 days.

                          Conclusion: You Successfully complete your gift in time. The Regent will be touched with your gift. We roleplay a scene at the Elysium where your character gift the Netsuke to the Regent, who’s touched by your thoughtfulness. You gain a dot of Mawla, which I award for free for choosing to risk your background.
                          Example 2 – Arcade Game

                          I’m not sure how your character reaches 12 in the dice pool(I also can’t find legal bonuses, but I’ll assume you have it, no worries). However, without more detail it’s difficult a bit to determine how I would run it, as I don’t know what the pool constitutes.

                          Since you have a pool of 12 however, we can assume you’ll ace the launch test. Since you’re looking for fame, the launch should probably involve an actual online announcement to drive hype for your upcoming game.

                          With 12 dice pool, your game can probably be safely completed within 4 nights. As once the project dice rolls 6, you can start rolling at anytime to reduce the project dice. (It doesn’t seem like there’s a time limit between each attempted rolls).

                          Since each roll is supposed to represent a dramatic shift maybe your character use Haven + Survival, stays awake during the day to time crunch, or they hire some extra help.

                          Benefits: Influence in the gaming industry might also be appropriate for benefits. Contacts or retainers also seems appropriate.
                          Conclusion on Raw
                          Not going to lie, I think I have hit a wall.

                          I was about to engage with the other examples, but I find the system frustrating in some aspect.

                          Your projects are interesting, but running raw with the limitations of skill + background is incredibly limiting, I really dislike that rule. Not all characters have backgrounds and forcing interpretation on how to use certain background feels weird.

                          The goal roll is also incredibly weird: you also somehow have to wait for the project die to decrease on its own before you can attempt to roll, then you can try to rush in the end to catch up on missed time. This feels weird.

                          Also, since the goal test is so hard, it pushes the player to always seek the most optimal dice pool, while I feel like a project often involves multiple skills.

                          Your listed examples seem fine, but since it all involves a single dice pool, it feels like there’s not much to work there(Not your fault, the fault of the Project rules). I would want at least a little bit more interaction in a project, or creative choice to better reflect the decisions that come with running a complex project.

                          How I would run the examples

                          First, I should state that I would not award free dots for projects nor would I allow a player to risk their dot without the sanctity of merits. I would simply award experience as I would with any other successfully completed plot kit or story beat. If the player took part in a project that’s excuse to gain the background dot they were interested in gaining. That’s not really too relevant, however.

                          With your given example, I think a social maneuver system, re-fluffed for the project would work best. This is what i would recommend.

                          Or if you wanted to be a bit more involved in the project, you could outline each step, working with the player to find how they want to engage with their project and bring it to life. Maybe I’d leave some space opened for extra complications, or, etc. As you'll see bellow, this is a bit more involved however.

                          If we wanted a basic number, maybe I’d go with 1 + twice the amount of background dots gained. I wouldn’t necessarily set a fixed time; I would instead base it on what would feel right for what’s been asked and the method used.

                          I would set a maximum number of failures per steps, probably 2, or award an increased penalty based on the number of failed tests, the test would become impossible if the penalty reached 3.

                          Taking some of your examples, here's how it could work

                          Example 1 – Netsuke.
                          You want to craft a netsuke for the local Regent to gain 1 dot of Mawla. This would be a 3-step project. Let’s outline the steps:
                          1. Material: You need to find the right material that will impress the Regent and where you can find it. I determine that it’s easy enough, you need a successful Academic + Intelligence test at difficulty 2. Let’s say this takes a night.
                            1. Critical win: You’re very inspired or find some very good material, gain +1 on the crafting test.
                            2. On a win : You acquire the material. Remove your project penalty.
                            1. On a failure: You need to spend 2 dot resources, or you can’t find what you are looking for. If you don’t spend resources, your project penalty increases by 1.
                            2. Total Failure: Your project penalty increases by 2.
                          2. Crafting. You need to craft the items. A netsuke is a small item, so I’ll ask for Dexterity + Craft. At your chosen difficulty, maximum 6. This takes 3 nights.
                            1. Critical win: Incredible, you’ll have +1 to impress the regent.
                            2. Win: You successfully crafted an item. This takes 3 nights. Remove your project penalty.
                            1. On a failure: You are wasting precious materials. You wasted 1 night instead of 3. Your project penalty increases by 1.
                            1. Total Failure: You wasted 3 nights and you are nearly out of material. Your project penalty increases by 2.
                          2. Gifting . The most stressful part. You want to impress the regent, what if they simply refuse your gift? Roll Charisma + Etiquette to impress the regent while you over them your gift at difficulty 9 - original your crafting difficulty. This is a simple meeting and takes nothing more than a few minutes at Elysium or at her office.
                            1. Critical Win: The regent is very impressed; she makes a comment about how the Night society would stand to have more kindred like you. You gain +1 on your next interactions at Elysium and you can buy the Mawla background at 1 dot.
                            2. Win: The Regent is touched. They tell you that if you need anything come and ask, she’ll see what she can do. You gain a Mawla at 1 dot.
                            3. Failure: The Regent doesn’t understand the significance of your gift. You may try to explain yourself using another skill or use Ettiquette again. Your project penalty increases by 1.
                            4. Total Failure: The Regent misinterpret your intentions; you only have one more chance to make it right. Your project penalty increases by 2.

                          Example 3 – Ujigami.

                          You want to build a cult centered around an Ujigami. For the sake of this project, I will assume that no actual spirit is actually involved, but that it centered around building a cult, simply because I do not know what it could represent in masquerade term.
                          This will grant you 1 dot of contacts, 1 dot of influence and 2 dots retainer as you gain a devoted cult follower. Since this is 4 dots project, this is a 9-step project. This is a lot of steps, so I will group some of them.
                          1. Finding the Ujigami – Step 1 to 2: During this step, you investigate the city; looking at how a spirit from Virginia could manifest. I’ll ask for resolve + Occult at difficulty 3 to keep your mind open despite the different land. Each test represents a week of investigation.
                            1. Critical Win: You complete steps 1 and 2.
                            2. Win: Progress by one step. If your complete step 2, remove your project penalty.
                            3. Failure: You suffer a setback. You can roll again but increase your penalty by 1. If your penalty reaches 3 the project fails.
                            4. Total Failure: You suffer a serious setback, increase your penalty by 2. If your penalty reaches 3 the project fails.
                          2. Recruiting for the cult – Step 3 to 5: Building the cult represents recruiting people that open to your beliefs. I’ll ask for a manipulation + Leadership or Wits + Insight. Each test takes 1 week of networking.
                            1. Critical Win: Count as one extra step, up to step 5.
                            2. Win: Progress one step. If you complete step 5 removes all project penalty.
                            3. Failure: You suffer a setback, increase your project penalty by 1.
                            4. Failure: You suffer a serious setback, some people are starting to be weary of your insistence to recruit them into your cult, increase your project penalty by 2.
                          3. Building the Shrine – Step 6 to 8: Starting the Shrine involves finding the right place for worship or simply building it. Roll either Intelligence + Craft or Resolve + Investigation at difficulty 4.
                            1. Critical Win: Count as one extra step, up to step 8.
                            2. Win: Progress one step. If you complete step 5 removes all project penalty.
                            3. Failure: You suffer a setback, increase your project penalty by 1.
                            4. Failure: You suffer a serious setback, some people are starting to be weary of your insistence to recruit them into your cult, increase your project penalty by 2.
                          4. Gaining your cult follower– Step 9: You need to find the right person. One that either believes in your cult or will be devoted enough to do what you ask of them. Roll Wits + Insight against difficulty 5 to find the right person, or Charisma + Leadership to motivate someone to step up. Each test takes a week.
                            1. Critical Win: You find your devotee they are perfect. Heal a point of willpower damage as you find newfound confidence in your endeavor.
                            2. Win: You complete your project, your cult is built, you can now worship far away from your home.
                            3. Failure: No one is the right fit, hopefully you can find someone during the next ceremony. Increase your project penalty by 1.
                            4. Total failure: You are reminded that you are no longer home and you struggle to connect. Increase your project penalty by 2
                          Conclusion

                          I realized after having typed it that the second project is quite long. I don’t think the storyteller needs to plan everything in advance, but maybe dividing the project in multiple simpler project might work better if steps are going to be more free from.

                          It would also benefit from serious rules for win, failure, total failure and critical win. That would require a lot more effort in designing it.

                          Maybe limiting scope to a single dot per extended test might be better, I’m not sure.

                          Hope this was useful in any way, but i think your original assessment of the Project Rules are correct.

                              I like your version, and agree with your observations.

                              To be honest, the rules for Extended Actions in V5 are by themselves better Project rules than the Project rules. Regular Extended add to a final result like normal. Serial Extended works as a fixed succession of tests you must beat in order, very similar to your take. Hard Extended fuse both, you make independent rolls and at the same time the extra successes add to a final goal. And Cascading Extended is like Hard but instead of adding to a final goal, the extra successes work as bonus dice on the following roll.

                              And then we get to the end of the book and the Project Rules are what they are...

                              Player's Guide version is just a Regular Extended Action with 3-6 successes as goal, which is actually quite low.

                              Oh, on my bonuses: I have High-Function Addict (stimulants) and Laboratory in my Haven, each gives a +1 dice bonus and are not in the exclusion clauses of Projects. I have a 10 dice Pool for coding thanks to Specialization and usually this is Intelligence. With Int 4 + Technology 5 + Specialization it's 10 dice.

I find that extended tests work best when time is a factor, otherwise, I prefer a simple roll or series of tests to achieve a goal.

                                  As for your character's pool, i see, your character is incredibly skilled!                                   When I run an improvised investigation in my current Tokyo by Night VTR chronicle, since successes are a bit rarer (8+), I’ll usually announce to the group that they need to hit a number of successes to clear the investigation. The only difference with true extended test is that I don’t limit the number of tests to attribute + skill pool, since only time is a factor. So I’ll generally ask for a bit more success usually 10 to 20, but I also convert merit dots into straight success if they make sense. We’ll do a little montage where I ask everyone how they contribute, maybe run some micro scenes or two, and I’ll reveal basic information for each 1/3 of the necessary, as much as it makes sense, until they complete the investigation and find out what they want.

                                  For example, last game player was trying to find a financial fraud investigator’s house so they could blackmail him into leaving the poor millionaire kindred of the coterie alone. I asked players to accumulate 15 successes and how they wanted to proceed:
                                  • The millionaire being a hacker tried to find information about his online trace to track him back to his home. Rolled intelligence + Computer, rolled something like 4 successes.
                                  • One player had contacts & allies within the Yakuza, used something like 4 dots of merits + rolled a socialize + manipulation to asks where her ‘’uncle’’ office was near the tax agency headquarters. Rolled something like 2 successes, for a total of +6. She learned the area in which the guy lived, they were only missing his house.
                                  • Once they had an area, the Mekhet investigated using Auspex 1 to find the most likely house for their target. I allowed her to use her auspex pool at the cost of a rouse check. She managed to clear the investigation.
                                  Unfortunately for them, since they moved a lot of influence, quickly without being super discreet about it, I diced to roll to see if the agent could notice their investigation, I rolled something like 7 successes for the bad ass agent who was used to this kind of shenanigans having investigated of lot Takahashi fraud in the past, so he managed to leave his house and take a flight outside the country before they could find him.
