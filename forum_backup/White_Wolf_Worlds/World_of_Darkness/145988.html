    Enquiries

    05-22-2014, 04:41 PM
    I am really having trouble with the WoD. When the words, "Our World in a Fractured Mirror" come up, it isn't just about the game description - it is about the game and the game publication. There once was this wonderful new company: White Wolf. I was introduced to it while in college, when the mokole were first learning how to shift. Then came Black Dog, and that was cool. And now there is Onyx Path, Mind's Eye Theatre, and all these others I don't know and don't want to know.

    I want to be a fan of the game. I would like to set up some online RPG and support the game. I don't want to have to ask a hundred different people for permission to use some art or icons or something to be a fan in support of the game. I don't want to have to risk a lawsuit for myself, my site, or any players each time we share a story using terms from the materials in order to be in character. So who would one ask? Who gives permission? Where does one get fan kits? Because I can tell you from experience so far I have gotten no responses from emailing CCP, White Wolf, or Onyx Path.

    Further, I was excited when I learned they were going to create a better core rule system. I liked the idea of a centralized gaming system that ties the world together and makes the entire World of Darkness one big game. Then came the down side - the entire world was destroyed. The clans, traditions, conventions, tribes, kith, and guilds were all obliterated. Had the system got too complicated? Yes! Why? Because there were too many systems. Was the story line bad? Not until ...

    I want to play in the World of Darkness again. I even want to see the functionality of a unified system. Here is what I will not do. I won't spend another thousand on game materials. Like Dungeons and Dragons, I am through with that. Wizards of the Coast has lost me as a customer due to the repeated expectation that I purchase hundreds upon hundreds of dollars of core rule books and supplements which are incompatible with everything I have acquired over 40 years of gaming. So, now I am looking at a problem with World of Darkness. The game appears to be destroyed. Just as interactive game mechanics are created, the best game White Wolf ever made (Mage the Ascension) is utterly wiped and made completely incompatible without having to completely rewrite the entire book again from scratch. Soooo,

    Is the game I want to play available?
    Is it affordable?
    Is anyone ever going to support the WoD with fankits?
    Is there a way to get permission to be fans and share the creativity without feeling like the Ventrue are in charge of everything involved?
    Is there anything left of WW more than the husk where cubicles used to be?

    Sorry, but I wouldn't bitch if I didn't care,

    Whithers

      Onyx Path Publishing is, for all intents and purposes except those related to copyright ownership, White Wolf Publishing as it existed into the 2000s. White Wolf Publishing as a corporate entity no longer exists; it is a trademark label owned by CCP, the gaming company which produces EVE Online, and has been since White Wolf Publishing and CCP underwent a merger in 2006. For awhile CCP maintained an internal publishing staff composed of the people who previously worked for White Wolf Publishing; those people have since either moved on to other jobs (many of which, sadly, were put on the World of Darkness MMO project, which has been discontinued; we wish them the best) or went on to form and work for Onyx Path Publishing with the intent of licensing the rights to continue production of tabletop books for the White Wolf properties. By Night Studios has a similar licensing deal for LARP rulebooks. To my knowledge, Onyx Path and By Night maintain friendly relations, but aren't necessarily coordinating their mechanical works.

      I'm not sure what you're asking permission to do. Could you clarify what it is you fear being sued over? As a general rule, most works that don't involve making any money off the White Wolf intellectual property, if not technically legal, exist in a safe grey area where CCP and Onyx Path Publishing aren't interested in pursuing legally.

      White Wolf maintains a back-library of past World of Darkness products — for both Worlds of Darkness — in PDF form for sale on DrivethruRPG, and many are available in hard copy as part of Drivethru's print on demand program, with plans to eventually offer the entire back-catalogue in print through the program. Onyx Path has also been publishing new books — again, for both Worlds of Darkness. For example, Mage: the Ascension has had four books produced in the past couple of years, completing the unfinished set of Revised Convention Books for the Technocracy. The project also involves producing a series of 20th Anniversary Editions, which are large compilations of the classic core material for each of the Classic World of Darkness lines. Masquerade and Werewolf 20th are available on DrivethruRPG, with Mage 20th in development and Wraith 20th coming in the future. These are large nostalgia products collected with a goal of providing a somewhat revised ruleset (it's very similar to Revised Edition) and cramming as much material and character options in the cores as possible. They are, consequentially, pricy (they were originally envisioned as luxury products for a limited run); they're largely compatible with the revised rulesets, though, as I understand it.

          If one attempts to find art to support a WoD fansite, for backgrounds, headers, footers, notifications, etc. in use on a fan site, all materials are privately made and or corporately posted. There are not now, nor has there ever appeared a single fan site kit that I can find. I suppose I could use art from some of the PDFs, if that is allowed... I could also tweak some photos off the internet for that dark or macabre twist - but for some things - ie. fonts, symbols, etc. a kit with permissions to use the materials inside as a fan would be helpful. No one seems to care to make that available - with the closest thing being a few icons here at Onyx Path - maybe. But as far as avatar images, backgrounds, character shots, theme art, etc. nothing has been set up for fans for on line fan sites to use as a kit.

          Second, there was at one time a serious almost threatening posturing from White Wolf about the use of argot in anything that one did - whether for personal fun and sharing with friends or with the intent of trying to go professional. It certainly made any and all materials I wrote about my characters and backgrounds something I was unwilling to involve game specific terminology in very heavily if I intended to share with friends on the net, or more than in a very close setting. At one point I remember reading, and even asking about, the limitation of the number of allowed words one was allowed to have in a written document before it was considered to have crossed the line. That kind of approach is not fan friendly.

          Ok, if WW no longer exists then they shouldn't be maintaining anything.

          I am aware that there is a new game set in a WoD that is so utterly different from everything I have as to be utterly unrecognizable and make everything I have obtained useless. Further, what I read about Mage the Awakening destroyed any interest I would have had in the new game. Replacing Qlipothic Entropy with a Death Sphere? Fine. Changing some of the Attributes? Fine. Adjusting the avatar system to more of a morality/path system like the other games? Fine. Obliterating the paradigm wars for this who romps on Mount Olympus whoey? Balderdash! The entire point to the original philosophical debate of Mage was "How do we manage to be true to our vision and support the vision of others with the limited resources we have?" The new game destroyed magic by defining it in one manner. Destroyed the variety of the traditions. Set up evil as good and good as evil, while eliminating the existence of either.

          In the previous game it was clear that each group of the supernaturals played a part in or related to the metaphysics of the world in some way. Now the synthesis is gone. The mages are no longer the reflection of the mad weaver's mind. The changelings are no longer the vanguards of the Wyld. The vampires are no longer the sundered children of the Bringer of Harmony. The garou are no longer angst as those who alone can see the truth of corruption. The wraith no longer show us what it means to transcend the tragedy of our shadow selves and ascend to heaven's light. The story was written into a hole with Orpheus, but it could have been opened to a thousand worlds with Hveðrungr the Trickster, with Maya the bringer of Delusion, with Angra Mainyu the Lie: the existence of evil based in true malice (against the Wyld, Weaver, and Wyrm) versus tragedy which is when two opposing ethics are in conflict.

                              Ehhh...I don't think that's a fair characterization of the nWoD. I'd say that largely, the settings have a lot of similar elements, but with differing emphases. The cWoD emphasizes about vast conspiracies engaged in bitter, ideological, secret wars. nWoD does have those things on occasion, but they're not as frequently emphasized. My favorite things about the nWoD are the toolbox emphasis of the setting - there's less of an overt singular direction, but more suggested possible goals and activities - and the emphasis on the utter weirdness of the world. For instance, demons don't come from the biblical god and are instead rebellious functions of a vast impersonal machine. There's a lot of good in both overarching settings, but I recommend judging each individual gameline on its merits.

                              If you're coming from the classic world of darkness and want to know if there are any games you might enjoy in the new world of darkness, I personally recommend the most recent ones: Demon: the Descent (it's a game about biomechanical spies), or Mummy: the Curse (it's a game about trying to discover who you are, while you serve a dread purpose [you get your own minions!]
