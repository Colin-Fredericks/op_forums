      Vampires (at least the elders) really like a strong difference between rich and poor. There's not much a vampire can do if everyone earns $60k and lives in a nice house. The obscenely rich offer a great deal of entertainment, and the really poor offer a great feeding ground where nobody asks questions if someone goes missing.


      Camarilla elders, quite happily, are great fans of racism. Apparently, sabbat are more ethnically diverse than camarilla, so instilling a good bit of racism into the populace can cause a good plan to go awry when a sabbat gets called out for his race, frenzies, and attention is drawn. Though this information isn't really something those elders are happy to share with the idealistic neonates.

      Werewolves?
      Bernie sanders would have saved america. The total opposite of vampires. Maybe some werewolves will be happy with a socialist system,since the above vampire strategy really encourages the wyrm (even if socialism would arguably strengthen the weaver) but some tribes (shadow lords, silver fangs, Glass walkers, bone gnawers) have varied interest in how economics work and favour.

      Mages?
      Syndicate- Free market capitalists, probably.
      NWO- Probably socialist.
      Technocracy in general- Anything that favours science.

          The official word is generally that mortal politics is decided by mortals, because saying that a particular politician or movement is influenced by supernatural beings is likely to cause offence. It's less of an issue with time, which is why various books offer suggestions on how to use the real-life strange rumours about Rasputin for example, but for more modern individuals it's left to individual Storytellers to make decisions about what's really going on in their setting.

          Nixon is probably distant enough now to feature in asides about various supernatural groups accusing each other of influencing him because none of them would admit to it themselves.

                      This is an interesting question, if one that ultimately can be answered only in play, by you and your friends. If it works for you.

                      Supposedly, most of the characters on WoD are quite into mortal politics. It isn't further discussed in the books to avoid obvious hazards, and for similar reasons, or simple disinterest, most groups don't pay it any attention either. But at least on the background of the setting, we can see the relation is actually intense.

                      A vampire have an invested interest on how the city is. First because it affects her feelings and beliefs. Second because it directly affects her unlife. It affects her food and her shelter, her possessions, her context, and her (hidden) society. Vampires are specially used to become big, if hidden, players at a local level, so city politics is an intimate part of their unlives. They will be connected, they will be influential, in as many circles as they can. They will try to make a difference, be it for personal or altruistic interest. And since state and country politics can affect their cities, with far less intimacy they can still have invested interests on most of it.

                      Werewolves have an interesting relation with politics. On the most part, they try as much as they can to stay at large of mortal society. But their interests are too frequently linked to politics. Some try to ignore it, but others become very involved on said politics. Environmental groups, ethnic questions, protected sites. The purview of interests of the Garou Nation too frequently crosses with political issues.

                      Mage goes a step further. Belief is their bread and butter, in a too literal sense. The Technocratic Union is a huge Conspiracy, the kind that have some of the greatest legends of world-spanning conspiracies as just factions of them, like the New World Order and the $yndicate (the Gnomes of Zurich). The Traditions fight yet, if loosing it, an Ascension War with the ultimate prize as the Consensus, the collective belief of humanity. Politics everywhere, on their intent, as their weapon, as their shield, religion, science and politics are their very medium for the true fight. And if you get this out of the way, a single Mage is still a being of Paradigm. It means that belief shapes her magic and her life. Most will go to any length for their beliefs, because such disposition is what made them Mages to begin with.

                                              In general I think that most vampires and Pentex would tend to focus more on the state and local governments than the national government. Due to difficulties with operating directly in person (sunlight, travel, etc) most vampires who dabble in politics probably won't invest heavily in national politics. At most a vampire might try to help back a member of Congress in exchange for the Congressman giving that vampire's community various pork or red meat type initiatives. Vampires might donate money to national candidates, but in general I can't imagine most of them being terribly interested in doing so.

                                              Pentex would also tend to focus on the local and state level. Buying off state and local politicians is a lot cheaper than federal ones, and those are the guys you need to bribe to get around zoning restrictions, avoid local investigations, etc.

                                              The Technocracy would meddle in national affairs - their goal is mastery of humanity as a whole, and being able to steer a country like America lets them (to a degree) steer the world.

                                              Still, for the most part I suspect that there is little direct supernatural meddling in most major, national elections - it's easy to imagine Pentex or a local Prince engaging in various tactics (fraud, bullying, voter intimidation, etc) to get a specific politician elected, but those kind of tactics don't really work on the national level.

                                                          Mages.

                                                          Mages are mortals.
                                                          Mages don't have anything on them that changes how they interact with normal people (say perhaps a little fluff about how they get a little more isolated from people subconciously aware of the difference, but that's nothing to rage or being undead) They don't have rage, they can go about in the day. They don't feed from people, they don't shatter minds through revealing true forms, and they're "vincible", they don't have the physical confidence that gives the social difference a vampire or werewolf might have.

                                                          Mages are the one group in world of darkness that we can simply define as mortal+ (well, sorcerers, psychics and to a lesser extent offshots like kinfolk can be similarly defined) So... well... Their interest in mortal politics is the same as any other humans would be. The NWO and Syndicate? About the same level as very influential corporations and lobbyists in terms of politics. Itteration X and progenitors are to a large extent just research groups. Traditions can be likened to hobbyists, organisations or whatever.

                                                          Mages don't have political opinions by virtue of being a mage. People are mages, and they just happen to have this view and fit in with this group. The difference between a person with an ideology and a mage with an ideology is magick, their political opinions aren't significantly affected by it.

                                                          A Werewolf, by contrast, Will have the political state of things affect the environment and can potentially spill into the umbra. This affects their relations with spirits. Furthermore, Garou society is highly segregated from normal humans because of rage, delerium and the veil. This allows them to huddle up together and foster more extreme opinions that are unchecked by wider society. It is in their best interest to not only protect the environment from polution, but to keep the populace happy and free of wyrmish influences.

                                                          A vampire needs to feed and keep herself entertained. She needs to control the herd and shape it to suit her. Policies dicatate who's on the streets and on the menu, and how much they can get away with. The more corrupt the system, the easier it is for vampires. Poorer neighborhoods are more forgiving, homeless are tasty, the rich offer entertainment.

                                                          But, back to mages. They don't have any such needs. Most of them don't visit the umbra to see what problems werewolves are facing, and none of them need blood, fear sunlight, or are emotionally dead. Mages can do incredible things, but they're largely things that people can do with a bit more spruce in them. A progenitor is going to have cooler things in his lab than just any old biological science guy, but they're not going to be very different otherwise.

                                                          Signatures are for terrible people who want to be caught.
