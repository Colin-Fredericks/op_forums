                                        Since we're kinda done here and all, clarification on my own point from the other thread:

                                        I also posited that as game design is expanded and refined, that new editions are more appealing to the customer base for RPGs. But that's because new editions represent refinements that incorporate expanded - either through innovation or experimentation - design concepts. Not every new edition succeeds, but in aggregate they trend towards overall game play experience improvement by virtue of better designs learned from all the decades of RPGs being made.

                                        The whole point is to push back against the idea of a mechanically static RPG that would last for generations of players is tenable (not even getting into the matter of preference that is if getting to that product is desirable). That a game that tries to avoid mechanical overhauls is inevitably (at least with the current state of RPG design having not yet fully explored all the boundaries of what RPGs are capable of, since games are still coming out with unique iterations of old ideas let alone new ones) bound towards abandonment.

                                        Not because of any fickleness on the behalf of the customers, but simply because better games born from expansion and refinement would supplant the old ones. Like so many other things, there will always been people that like the classic icons for that status, and some even to the point of rejecting anything but a classic. Classic cars can be marvelous pieces of artistic expression, and incredibly visceral fun to drive, but we didn't stop making cars after a collection of classic designs were made. We keep making newer and better cars, even if a lot of them are boring samey cars. Even with all the old classics, we also manage to make new classics every few years too.

                                        Likewise, RPGs have their classic iconic games that people still play after decades, but as RPG design evolves and improves game play experience, newer systems are the ones that sell the best.
