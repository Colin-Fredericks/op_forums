                        Cosmology doesn't matter that much to a lot of games in practice. Werewolf may be an exception because you interact with spirits so much. So it's entirely possible to have a game where the truth behind the various myths is unknown and the focus is on the character's actions and the things they interact with. The games are out of balance and there are a few crossover rule concerns, but honestly those can be overcome with a few rule tweaks.

                        The bigger issues for me are themes of the respective games and supernatural glut, especially politically.

                        Let's say we have a corrupt mayer in some city your party is in. Maybe he's controlled by the Ventrue or maybe the Giovanni, maybe he is in league with Pentex, maybe he has a literal deal with the Devil or demons, maybe he is controlled by the Syndicate or Nephandi, maybe he is controlled by one of the numerous other factions across numerous game lines that have influence in politics, wealth, or mind control. There are so many groups out there secretly controlling the world that it gets silly, plus what ever normal humans are doing too. What about the deep dark woods where people tell scary stories about - do the Garou live there, are their nature spirits, is it haunted by wraiths, is there a Gangrel in there, are the Unseelie faeries, is it corrupted by the Wyrm, is it just normal? Of course you can choose one of these options for the above questions, but when you have so many options and so many creatures that magic looses a bit of its magic.

                        When it comes to actually playing games with all these creatures thrown in, then I feel like the themes of one or more games will often suffer. This may be the most important hurdle for me personally. If you have a game with a mage, a vampire, and a werewolf maybe you can ignore the conflicting cosmologies and be alright and maybe you can tweak the rules a bit and balance out the creatures, those won't be problems. What will be a problem is what this group actually does. Are they going to do battle with banes to protect the forest, will they fight against becoming less human, will they get involved in political schemes adjacent the Prince, will the try to escape from the oppressive Technocracy agents in the area? They could do any of those things, but basically we have to go with the themes of one game line to the detriment of the others. About the only sort of crossover that is consistent with all game lines is that there is some super evil enemy and various creatures need to work together to fight it, which still fails to utilize much of the valuable options in the various games.

                            I think it all fits together about as well as the real world all fits together, which is not to say that the myths of every human culture all fit neatly together. The real problem arises when you try to take all the mythologies and cosmologies as literal truth that must be reconciled into a unified field theory of Darkness. That can’t be done. They’re all very deliberately designed to work for their particular adherents and not really for any one else. All those things are just narrative models for understanding the world with a limited perspective and a particular set of experiences. It’s the fans that tend to turn them into objective truths and complain that the setting doesn’t make any sense.

                                            I thought Mage was originally based on chaos magick. But yeah, the problem with totalizing theories of the universe like Hermeticism is that while you can cram everything into them, the things you’ve crammed in probably don’t resemble their original selves anymore. The Garou believe in the Triat because it makes sense to their experience and explains the spiritual phenomena they encounter. Mages believe in a number of other things because it fits their experiences. Vampires have various sets of beliefs that aren’t even compatible with each other, which are attempts to rationalize their experiences.

                                            It’s models within models. Models all the way down. Even if you try to posit a reality underlying all the models, you’re just creating another model (hear that, mages?). WoD has always been thoroughly postmodern like that.
