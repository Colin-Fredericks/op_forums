            A bit of brainstorming on the Mage side:

            Attacking its Essence may be necessary if battling in the Umbra, but generally a bad Idea. Because they have a ton of Essence (up to 100). In order to blow it up in one shot for sure, a mage would need somewhat like 52 Successes devoted to Damage (assuming maximum Essence). That's not very doable.
            An Archmaster of Forces can get that done in 26 successes, more realist, but not easy.
            With extra powerful powers like Antimater (that it's successes x3 to damage instead of x2), or those combinations of Prime + Forces that somehow add a multiplier it could be slightly easier, but still hard.
            All in all, an Archmaster could have something under his sleeve that does that kind of damage, but it's not as cheap as just eleporting away. Of course, a starship armed to the teeth can dish that kind of damage by deploying misiles and rays, but again, starships ain't exactly cheap.

            NC on the lower ends of the Essence pool are more easy to kill. A normal mage would need 15-17 successes, wich it's reasonable to have under the sleeve. It's still more expensive than runing away.

            Of course, if the thing Materializes with 12 hp or so, it's much more vulnerable to damage attacks, but depending on rules this may not be the case.

            Another approach it's to have something that can reduce it's capability to cause mayheim.

            Countermagic could work because a Crawler's magic depends on having many successes with not that many dice but, since the crawler can do anything, you would need ALL the Spheres to be sure.
            Depending on the ST, you may need to use Sphere vs Sphere when countering, meaning having all Spheres at the correct levels to create a similar effect-in that case, the mages would be hard pressed to compete even assuming that they have the dice.
            And then, even if you manage it, you still are facing a slavering monster that can overpower werewolves even without using magic.

            While Traditionalist Mages usually flee from scene when faced with such odds, if they stay to fight it's likely to be because they're defending an area. And that means Wards. Now, a NC can probably dispel wards, but not all wards are equal or obvious:

            A Spirit 4 Ward can impede a spirit to Materialize or use any power from the other side of the Ward, but let's say the reality breaking powers of the Crawler allow it to breach the Ward and Materializes or whatever.
            You could have built your wards in such a way that when the NC approaches (or breaches your Spirit 4 Ward to Materialize) a big ward activates in the area. But this time it's a Minor Ward (Spirit 2/Prime 2/Corr 2). Minor Wards don't prevent passage, but instead they limmit the hability to act of the baned beings. A Minor Ward substracts dice from ALL the actions of the baned creatures inside the area of effect. With "just" 4-6 dice less at everything, the NC would be unable to use it's powers to breach the Ward nor to break any reality. Bonus points because while you're still facing a slavering monster, the monster now has less dice at everything.

            Much better than Countermagic, if you ask me...and the better part it's that you don't need to win Initiative against the Crawler, nor to expect it. It could work against any Bane that decided to enter (albeit you could make one especific for NC).

            Similar effects could be achieved with Spirit 4 by creating interference in the spiritual power, similarly as when you affect a Werewolf to give her a +3 to all Gifts difficulties. Since NC has to roll to Break Reality, this could be effective.
            Yet another approach would be to sidestep the Essence of the beast entirely.

            It's said that Hermetics have a special Time 4 + Correspondence 4 Rote for beings that are too powerful to be defeated. The Rote (probably a ritual chaneled trough an Artifact or a Time trigger) puts the thing outside time and space, efectively removing it from the game until the spell expires. Correspondence vanishes the thing, but Time it's what makes it unable to use it's powers to get free (by freezing the enemy in one second)

            With Time 5, a mage can always stop time and search for enough dinamite to stop it. As has been said, if enough dinamite doesn't work it's because you didn't used enough dinamite.

            Of course, there's the Lord of all Cheese. The "I teleport it somewhere dangerous, like to the Sun". These things are why people give their creratures Countermagic when Mages enter the table. Still, I would think that such a spell it's unlikely to work without a ritual.
            Of these powers that need to be prepared beforehand. The Ward and the Hermetic Seal are the ones that I can see mages may have prepared. The first because no one wants Banes in their yards, and the second because it's a clasic.
            Powerful damage rituals ported in charms/gadgets wouldn't be rare either, probably not so powerful as to one shot a potent NC, but still useful.
