    "Big Five" crossover games

    02-09-2015, 02:44 PM
    Has anyone done a game with the "big five," as in have a Vampire, Werewolf, Mage, Changeling and Wraith in one group? If so, how did it work out? What was the overall plot? Were Mages overpowered? Were Werewolves overpowered? Was different game mechanics a major problem? What about the conflicting cosmology of the games?

    And more importantly what was the explanation for how they all came together? Been thinking about perhaps trying to bring the idea to my table and was hoping for some perspectives.

      I have sort of it was more going of a big 7 so add HTR and demon in there. We originally had two mages a hunter and a risen wraith but the last two left then we got two demons. It ran pretty good. The overall plot was a small town where nothing really happens has something BIG happen and it's up to the players to work with the various supernatural powers to deal with it. The different mechanics were not an issue save for some of my unfamiliarity some mage stuff.

      The mages were overpowered unless they were dealing with a fellow mage or this one vampire which had an ability that gave him extra dodge dice which they managed to beat with luck, but the game was more focused on the story and they weren't OP enough that it broke the game or the story line and everything that I wanted to happen, happened in some manner.

      How the group got together was that each of the characters had a reason to go after the big bad, The Hunter's girlfriend was killed by the big bad and the big bad was also responsible for his imbuing, the Mage's have a truce with the local Cam's who got something important stolen from them by the big bad and so to keep the peace they wanted to get the stolen item back, also their chantry didn't really have much for them to do a lot so this gave them something to do and test their magic out on. The wraith was supposed to infiltrate the local camps and work for them so that's how he would get involved, and the demons got information that some world changing event was going to happen that was linked to the big bad so they were hoping to try and stop it or find out what it was.

      It also helped helped the at all the players and myself were good friends and have been in many games together so we have a strong trust and know that were not going to be trying to screwing each other over.

      I was also in a crossover game that allowed for any splat so long as it fit the story and it worked well. We had 4 vamps 1 werewolf and 2 Hunter side characters.
      Save for myself and the sheriff everyone was playing a character new to the supernatural world or out of the loop so no one really had any prejudices and the local vampires and werewolf's had a
      truce were "you stay out of our area, and we'll stay out of yours" deal unless shit hit the fan and they had to temporally pull resources.

              My opinion:
              The Big Three (Werewolf, Mage, and Vampire) work well enough together. Even then, I do some slight (or major) system tweaks to make things jive a smidge smoother. Wraith is really awkward or really easy, depending on the player and character. So I don't have to deal with the headaches of including Risen (which, in my mind, is another half-splat) I strongly encourage Embody 4. Changeling is a huge pain. I incorporated CtD in a crossover game for only one Chronicle. It worked, but I stole mercilessly from Land of Eight Million Dreams and added some odd alterations. If I had to do it over again, I'd use DtF with a "goblin mod."

              The following are some of the tweaks that I use:
              Use the magic system for DA: Mage. It just meshes better with crossover’d systems.

              Werewolves work well enough with minimal finagling, except for one tiny thing: Crinos. Werewolves in these games can only be in Crinos for [Perm. Rage] turns. I think WtF does something similar.

              Vampires and Sunlight. I mess with this a great deal in Crossover games. It kills the flow of the story when one the characters can’t function AT ALL for half the day.
              In Crossover Games (only): Vampires can spend a Blood Point at sunrise to take soakable Bashing dice (not levels) from Sunlight. They have to cover up (hats, ponchos, long sleeves, etc.) but they can still do stuff. They just have to do it carefully. To balance this out, vampires (regardless of the time) soak other damage like a human (bullets do lethal, no halving Bashing, etc.). Also, no sun screen. For no reason other than it seems dangerously close to that threshold of absurdity.

              While some of these are a little odd, they work well enough for me. It helps curb random RAW-lawyers that may get incorporated into the troupe and helps me set the proper tone for game expectations.

              And now, the obligatory “YMMV.”

                  I tend to dislike mixed PC groups, but a few here or there can work well. A single mage with a pack of werewolves can work. Vampires and Mages of certain types can work. Wraiths and Mages of certain types can work very well. Changelings are a pain in the ass.



                  When mixing Vampires...

                  Instead of neutering the sunlight thing for Vampires, allow them to create a single ghoul with character creation. It will be a sort of second-skin for them. This will be their daylight character. They might have more ghouls, but this one is the one they PC.

                  In the past this solution has allowed me to keep some dark and cursed themes on the Vampire while giving them access to Mage time frames too. It does give them an advantage, but less than (I think) being day walkers.

                                          Regarding why the group exists, i had them all working for Samael, or Lucifer as he is also known, living in LA guarding the vampire parents of the Last Daughter of Eve. The child will inherit the power and be the avatar of Lilith on Earth during the Apocalypse. So the story started set in 1991, with the group working for their patrons, senior exemplars of their kind who had allied with Samael at some point in the past. When their patrons were killed they took over the duties of their seniors, and when the parents were killed they took over raising little Alia.

                                          The game is a mix of the characters pursuing their own interests, and protecting the child and LA from incursions into by other interested parties, including Lilith's agents on Earth, who are understandably concerned about getting their Mother's avatar back.

                                          The timeline progresses 5 years between every season, and each season a new problem rears its head that the group as a whole need to deal with.

                                          Samael is a good-ish guy, the biggest guns have concrete reasons not to throwdown till the night of the Apocalypse, and the players each have reasons for seeing Samael's will be done, from compassion for the parents and the child, to pure self interest(Raising a God, how is that not beneficial?).

                                          The setting has a lot of changes, minor and major to make it all fit, including an actual Hell for the dead, ala Yomi in the East, and Hell-lords who rule it, and so on.

                                          Regarding the number of Mages, while 1 in a million leads to several thousand mages, most of them leave the Earth behind and go beyond the gauntlet for a variety of reasons. The single largest block of Magi, the Technocracy are involved in protecting the borders of the Universe from the Outsiders, creatures from the previous Universe desperate to come into the new Universe.(Hello Dresden-verse Meets Exalted. The Outsiders are the remnants of the Exalted-verse Primordial Demon Hordes) With that war ongoing the Technos throw as many Mages as they can spare at that war, and the Traditions, despite their enmity often find themselves performing similar duties. That leaves a minority of mages on Earth, from either the Traditions, the Technocracy, or from Orphan groups to play around on Earth. Mages also just find the world beyond the Gauntlet more congenial as there is no Paradox, and Mages can achieve godlike acts in the Umbra or the Underworld.

                                          It's not that the Outsider War is the single most important game, but it keeps the Magi occupied, while other factions run around in the shadows of the WoD, awaiting the Apocalypse and advancing their plans. The Outsider War also blinds the Mages to just how damn dangerous the War between the Wyrm, the Weaver and the Wyld actually is. By ignoring that conflict the Mages may save the Universe just to see the Wyrm destroy everything, the Weaver freeze everything to Stasis, or the Wyld unmake meaning altogether.

                                          Anyhow, thats part of the setting adjustments i had to make. Its been fun so far.

                                              It is a pain in the ass when running a Crossover game period. All the variants in the game? Different creatures are way more powerful than others like the Garou who are the strongest starting in the game. The Crossover rules are a nightmare.

                                              I remember Jess Hening and Justin Anchilli saying that if they made just a Crossover book for Mage and Vampire with all the rules it would be as big or bigger than the Core book of either of the games they ran.

                                              I happen to like running something like Vancouver: Dark Alliance which is the bastard child setting White Wolf in the day wish it never made, where Garou and Kindred work together. I like it. It is fun to play for Vampire and Werewolf players. I have found over the years that to help the Kindred I have had to make some House Rules and changes to get the Kindred just able to even come close to being playable with their Garou Pack mates. The whole Vampire not out in the Sun thing I had to change. I had to come up with alternate rules on it (using Julian Luna and other Kindred from Kindred the Embrace as inspiration for the rule). It works but even then the Kindred are seriously lacking. The fact they can't go to the Umbra. Handle BSDs etc like a Garou can makes the game difficult.

                                              A complete cWoD crossover game... with everything. Good luck with that.

                                                      Changeline and Wraith are probably the hardest to bring together, as the Deadlands and the Dreaming are the hardest barriers to cross. Pretty much anything else can work, as long as the ST is conscious of what the players can do. (And Wraith can come in via Risen, KotE, or Mediums/Necromancers.)

                                                      An 'End of the World' setting can make it easier as those barriers start to degrade, and also wide and varied enough threats, and powerful enough to require working together. (The physically tougher holding a line while the mystics perform a ritual of some sort as one example.)

                                                      One of the issues with later 2nd Edition and 'Revised' was that a few developers were under the assumption that gamers running a crossover were 'doing it wrong'. But in the end, if you and your players are all having fun... you aren't doing it wrong.
