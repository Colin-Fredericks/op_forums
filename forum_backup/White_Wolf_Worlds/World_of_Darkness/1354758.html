    D&D spells conversion

    12-13-2019, 09:49 AM
    I know that there are lots of D&D fans here. So, just for the fun of it, let's try to convert the most famous spells from D&D 3.5 to M20. I'll start with some basic ones. Also, if you find more than one way to do it, post all you can.

    >Magic missile: missiles of energy? Forces 3, maybe Prime 3. Better with Forces 3/Prime 2. That's thr same for Fireball/Lightning/Scorching ray etc...

    > Hold monster: Forces 2 or Mind 4

    >Fly: Forces 2 or Correspondence 3+Life or Matter 2

    >Haste/Slow: Time 3 or Forces 2 + Life 2

    > Alter self: Life 2

    > Shapechange: Life 5

    > Time Stop: Time 5

    >Stoneskin: Life 3

    >Suggestion: Mind 3

    >Shield/Mage armor: Forces 2

    >Identify: Matter 1 or Forces 1 or Prime 1. Better with all of them.

    >Summon Monster: Correspondence 4, probably with Spirit 3 depending on the creature.

    >Vampiric touch: Life 3

    >Dimension door: Correspondence 3

    >all Bigby's hands: Forces 2

    >Bull's strength/Cat's grace/Owl's Wisdom etc: Life3

    >Mordenkainen's sword: Forces 2+Spirit 3?

    >Finger of death: Spirit 3+Life 3?

    Some spells are harder to convert, like Wish or Leomund's secure shelter.

    I noticed that Forces is the most used sphere. Life and Mind seems to go in 2nd.

              The real key to converting the spells from DnD (we'll assume 5E, though most spells have remained contextually consistent over the editions relative to their respective functions) is to make absolutely sure you're understanding the metaphysics involved in DnD and how they translate into Mage mechanics/metaphysics.

              Examples of some conceptual errors with your list:

              Magic Missile: is a Force (DnD terminology, which in Mage/WoD terms equals Prime) attack that doesn't harm inanimate objects, only (semi)-living targets (which include undead via the mockery of life-force they possess). The missiles also automatically hit their targets, barring any intervening magic that prevents such (they can't be Dodged/evaded). That means that they aren't a direct manifestation of a "typical" Prime:2/Forces:3 "I'm gonna blast'em!" effect. It has qualities of Prime:2/5 (rubbing the bones/empowering an object to inflict aggravated damage, in terms of relative power/effect, with qualities of targeting/affecting living targets -- note how Prime:5 is the way the Disintegration spell works relative to translating DnD to Mage, vs using a Life/Matter effect depending on the target, because the salient aspect involved in again a Force (Prime) dissolution), with a wholly cosmetic visual manifestation (technically involving Forces, relative to RAW, but essentially likely a "freebie" special effect for most ST's POVs); it also incorporates Correspondence:2/3 for unerringly hitting a given target (mechanically, the damaging effect is effectively being "teleported" to hit the target; the visual effect of a "missile" streaking to hit said target is, again, a matter of fluff/window dressing). As the result is an attack and not a "hanging" effect, it isn't necessary to establish a (P)attern lock on the target, just incorporate Correspondence (2 for a single target, 3 for multi-vector) for the auto-hit aspect.

              Bigby's Hand (assorted, though in 5E they are all under the providence of one spell vs. most previous editions) : Forces:2 is insufficient because the effective (S)trength/kinetic power that the Hand can create is greater than (usually) can be directed consistently from ambient forces-in-motion. It's Prime:2, Forces:3, as the Hand actually has a coherent (P)rime pattern and can be targeted (unlike most ambient Forces effects in-motion) by mundane forms/interaction. On the flip, though, it gives it more "oomph" and substantial existence, as well as its "solid hologram"-effect that it has (translated, of course) in DnD.

              Stoneskin: while Life:3 can simulate the mechanical effect of boosting physical resistance/resilience against physical harm, it's important to note that the DnD spell, proper, actually *transforms* the superficial skin of the target (the caster, in this case) into stone (albeit flexible vs rigid). That's means you have to throw in Matter:2/3 depending on edition. (This is a key example of how, in Mage terms, the (P)aradigm involved relative to effect can't be overlooked; that is, the Sphere effects used must cover the spell's indicated effects, which is why Matter is also required.)

              Dimension Door: Correspondence:3 is insufficient for reasons similar to Bigby's Hand above -- even in 5E, when/where the spell is at its most relatively limited, it still allows for the simultaneous translocation of 2 beings (and their equipment, plus what they're carrying as long as it isn't alive), and more so in some previous editions where it could transport several persons. So Correspondence:4, with the (P)aradigm restriction of relative distance (or limited to being a Simple spell limited to a single roll and successes spent to cover distance plus incorporate targets, if wanting to blend it more so with Mage's metaphysics).

              Identify: you start with the correct base (Prime:1), but hit along the wrong tracks (Matter/Forces). Identify doesn't analyze the material composition or even energy expression of a given item, but rather susses out the mystical (P)atterns that the object has/contains. That's a critical distinction in this regard. Properly, relative to DnD metaphysics, Identify would use Prime:1, Mind:1 and Spirit:1 to note the metaphysical powers, uses and -- when/where applicable -- intentions of the "magic item" in question. Similarly, when used to discern what spells are currently affecting a living or inanimate target, it does so by again discerning the surface "intent" of the magic in-progress (Prime:1, Mind:1) or the applied metaphysics of The Weave (DnD metaphysical term-usage) as it applies to an object or creature (Prime:1, Spirit:1, as the mystical forces sussed out are not directly related to the physical forms in-and-of-themselves but the metaphysical aspects interwoven into The Weave, which in Mage/WoD terms involves Spirit).


              As you are likely guessing by now, it *isn't* quite as simple as it first appears to just port over the spells of DnD while retaining the (P)aradigm of that setting-as-translated-into-Mage. Some spell effects that are "high level"/complex for DnD are quite simple for Mage (teleportation, for example) while others are "simple" in DnD but metaphysically more involved when brought over (Magic Missile, for example). Using the above examples should get you on the right track to translating them more accurately -- particularly if you (re?)-read more about the underlying metaphysics of DnD (a lot of the fluff that isn't directly addressed in the game mechanics, as such, but across the various editions/decades which in turn are the foundation for why the mechanics are handled the way they are).

              Oh, and Leomund's Secure Shelter: Prime:2, Matter:3/4, Forces:2, Spirit:2 (though other variants are possible, because Mage(tm) ). Prime and Matter conjure the material aspects into existence and shape/form the shelter (which reflects the materials of the locality it's conjured into, though not utilizing any local material in question; the form, however, has an applied resiliency equal to stone even if of inferior composition, hence the higher Matter aspect when/where appropriate). Forces and Spirit allow for the ephemeral "ghost" to perform tasks as-directed by the caster, as well as mystically secure/prevent casual entry from others (simulating the Unseen Servant and Arcane Lock spells which are a part of the spell as-cast). Those also allow for a variation of forewarning and security that also comes with the spell (via the Alarm spell which is also incorporated). (Naturally, Correspondence with Mind can simulate the warning system, as well, but the Forces/Spirit version keeps the number of gears turning to a more reasonable level.)

              Hope that helps!

                  While I agree that 99% of D&D Force damage should be tranaslated to pure Prime on the grounds that it's usually described as raw magical energy (this is also reflected on the kind of beings that resist Force damage, which are very rare sorcerous beings), I think a case could be done for Disintegration to have a Life/Matter component.

                  Disintegration, if I remember correctly, states that if the target it's killed by the damage, it's transformed into "fine ash". Prime 5 dissolution doesn't ususally leave fine ash (everything it's transformed into Quintessence), albeit I guess it could do so.

                  A case could be made that for the damage caused by disolving something with Pattern Spheres to be interpreted as Force damage if it's treated as "pattern rending". because in those cases, nothing "of" a pattern it's causing the damage. Life 3 raw damage it's not really "cutting" or "percing" or "acid", even when it may manifest as internal cuts and blisters, because there's no actual force or element involved...it's the magick itself that causes the damage by unraveling the life pattern. Which also would explain neatly on D&D terms why soaking effects like that of a Life/Matter based Stoneskin can't soack it unless it's explicitly built to stop Pattern/Aggravated damage, whereas cutting or piercing damage conjured with Forces would be soacked.

                  I think Disintegration could be regarded as something of this order

                          Spells like Tenser's Floating Disk, Mage Hand, and Mordenkainen's Sword also would use Forces 3 + Prime 2, because the user is creating a construct of (telekinetic) force.

                          The same might be said about the spells Unseen Servant and Mordenkainen's Faithful Hound...assuming we go on DnD's metaphysics for Paradigm.

                          If we're going with Mage and its folkloric inspirations, an Unseen Servant or similar might be either a construct of force given (invisible) shape and purpose, or they could be spiritual beings summoned to perform a task. If the latter, it would be various levels of Spirit. Spirit 2 to call them to service (probably pre-arranged through a pact negotiated between the Mage and the Umbrood), possibly with additional Spirit 2 Effect to lower the Gauntlet so the spirits can more easily affect the material world. Spirit 4 to potentially bring the Umbrood over to act "in the flesh", though an Umbrood with the right Charms may not need this.

                          At least according to DnD, though, Forces is all that's needed.

                          If the Unseen Servant et al. ARE Forces, they may or may not need Mind to give them intelligence. In most cases, an Unseen Servant only performs menial labors, and can thus be assumed to need no mind. The Mage does all the cognitive heavy lifting, giving the directions in much the same way that a broom might be animated to sweep a room or a sword be animated to fight on the Mage's behalf.

                          If these constructs of force are meant to act independently for whatever reason, they may need intelligences baked into them. A while back, I argued that "Create Mind" - normally exclusive to Mind 5 - should be broken up between different levels of Mind. Mind 3 to create an "Expert System", Mind 4 for non-sapient "animal" intelligence, and Mind 5 for full sapience and self-awareness. If manual labor is all that's required, Mind 3 would be the only necessary component under this model. If we don't use this model, though, then I'd argue that Forces alone should be needed for simple tasks when the Mage isn't around; the Effect being left with a long Duration and set to mindlessly perform its task.
