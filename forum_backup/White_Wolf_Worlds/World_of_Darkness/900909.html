                                  Committing to an actual answer: Yes. I have used it obliquely, sparingly and with a very light touch. It also depended entirely on the character of the Mage chronicle in question. I remember having a character see a Pattern Spider using its webbing to bind and compress together natural spirits into a highway. That was a LONG time ago. Sometime in the mid-90s. That's probably the strongest direct connection, though cabals have fought a Black Spiral Dancer Theurge and his Banes at one point in the midst of very badly maintained oil refinery. Was the Wyrm or Weaver ever mentioned? No. Were the trappings used? Sure. However, I have run entire games where a LOT occurred in the Umbrae and the Triat were conspicuous by their complete absence.

                                  However, though the High Umbra gets mentioned incessantly as being the "most mage-ly" of the Umbrae, it's never been used much in any of my games. Most of the characters who have heavily invested in Spirit are the kind of casters who don't give a fig for talking to the English language or discussing philosophies of the color Blue. They tend to be shamanic types and the Vidare has been the Middle Umbra. Followed by those who cared a great deal about talking to the dead and messing with the Low/Dark Umbra. With a heavy dose of whatever Umbra Conventional Space and Etherspace belong to. Though the High Umbra gets a lot of press, the mages who are the most directly involved with Spirit (e.g. Dreamspeakers) are those that are seen as being intrinsically tied to the Middle, not High Umbra. (Sure there are SOME Dreamspeakers that tend towards the High or Low Umbrae, but a lot of flavor text bends against that.) EVERY Dreamspeaker in EVERY Mage game I've run has had a lot of Spirit. A few of the Order of Hermes mages and Choristers have had it to summon angels and gnostic archetypes.

                                  So, I guess, the Triat is actually something of a thing to deal with, as a lot of the detailing of the Middle Umbra started in Werewolf and is still characterized by that basis.

                                              I've never run a Mage game, but I've always run my chronicles with the assumption that Mages know better than the others how the universe really works, and that the whole World of Darkness really follows its precepts. (Wraith deal with very existential/metaphysical issues as well, and some Gaunts may know more about the nature of Oblivion and the Neverborn better than Mages). So anything that happens in the other games I run, is done with that mindset. So even though I generally only run Vampire and Werewolf, I do it with Mage in mind. This has never been apparent to the players though.

                                              As such, I hold that the Wyld, Weaver, and the Wyrm are mere anthropomorphized understandings/caricature of the Mage paradigm of Dynamism, Stasis, and Entropy. The Garou may understand many things and details about the spirit world more than Mages, but they are actually quite weak on major metaphysical philosophies. Attributing all their problems to the Wyrm is simply their way of dealing with the Problem of Evil.

                                              The game was originally written with the intent that all of them crossovered to one another. That quickly fell apart as 1) there were not enough common mechanics to make it work, 2) as each line developed, little bits of inconsistent canon were introduced especially as the first games had many more supplements published before some of the game lines had even begun, and 3) the various line developers became very protective of their own line and couldn't work well with others to tell a single setting. There were too many ideas (and egos) that worked against each other. So before too long, the line changed from crossover is great and allowed, to none of the games crossover and canon in one game shouldn't be taken into the next line. That way each developer could do whatever they liked and ignore what another developer was doing. Well, it is at least one way of dealing with office politics.

                                              But before that happened, to me it was very clear that Dynamism, Stasis, and Entropy was the basis for Wyld, Weaver, and the Wyrm.

                                                  I do not use it directly. I tend to think the Werewolf trinity is a narrowed concept of the metaphysical trinity. The last one, even if not named that way in all paradigms (and therefore in all Traditions) is present in all beliefs. Some Choristers will speak about the different faces of the One, other Dreamspeakers will speak about the greater spirits entities and most Hermetic will directly recognize the metaphysical trinity.

                                                  I'm not sure Mages do know better than others, but they surely think they do. In my opinion, the Wyld, the Weaver and the Wyrm are part of Dynamism, Stasis and Entropy. May be they even exist as spiritual beings. Since the metaphysical trinity is built on concepts, spiritual representations of these concepts should exist. In my WoD, the Werewolf trinity is formed of these representations. However, this is a personal interpretation.

                                                          (I shouldn't be jumping in on this, as I have a shit-ton of stuff to do... but a friend linked this topic to me with a because of how long this type of discussion has gone on across the various forums... and because of the strange -- almost bizarre -- cognitive dissonance that seems to affect a number of people concerning this "cross-over"...)

                                                          The Weaver, Wyld and Wyrm are simply the Garou/Fera perceptions and manifestations of Stasis, Dynamism and Entropic aspects in the (broader-ranging) Mage (C)osmology. The 3-Ws are simply the (mostly) Middle-Umbra manifestation of the more broad/permeating aspects of SDE (in whatever order) in Mage (not only in the High Umbra, as some misattribute, but also in the Deep/Far and Low Umbrae, as well).

                                                          It creates absolutely *no conflict* whatsoever, save for in the minds of those who don't quite "get" how different things appear differently to different beings/entities as well as manifest in somewhat different ways along the different levels (and reaches) of the various Umbrae of WoD cosmology.

                                                          (Now, *in-narratively*, there can be centuries-to-millennia-long debates/arguments between various mage and garou *characters* as to how "x,y and z" are this, that and the other... but *meta-narratively*, there should be absolutely no issue whatsoever in crossing over the 3-W aspects into a Mage setting. Hell, for a number of Dreamspeakers as well as more than a few Verbena, the 3-W are something they'd likely have learned about and/or interacted with during various excursions into the Middle Umbra; they might refer to these "entities" by somewhat different names and/or conceptualizations -- particularly in the case of especially erudite/aware Mages, who may have a broader perspective/insight into the universe as a whole than most Garou/Fera would or do -- but by-and-large they'd be just as aware of the Middle Umbra "identities" of Stasis(Weaver), Dynamism(Wyld) and Entropy(Wyrm) as most were-creatures, particularly in the case of the aforementioned Dreamspeakers, save again for having different names and/or metaphysical perspectives...)

                                                          It's really no big deal at all. Like, at all...
