    Originally posted by Verlan View Post

    An examination of the dangers of powers and hubris is certainly something that is possible in Mage. The game, however, does not adhere entirely to that framework. If anything, each reader is intended to come away from Mage with their own interpretation as mages. To say that mages are unequivocally monsters is like saying that miracle-workers throughout history were monster. Even apart from ethical considerations, it is just a myopic way of viewing the world.
    Book of Secrets is but one of many books where the author directly states it: make no mistake, Mages are monsters.

    What happens is that the game, as all other games in WoD, has many themes to explore, and angst about your own monstrosity isn't the main one by far.

    Angst for being a monster isn't particularly important (but still possible) in any line but Vampire. Yet Werewolves take lives almost as an afterthought, fairies are emotional vampires at best and serial kidnappers with some frequency, your best Demon is a cult leader with all that entails. They're monster, inevitably, all of them.

    Mages aren't simply enacting miracles. They're raping reality with the stated goal of making it what they think it should be, without any concern for what other people want or need it to be.

    They bend and break rules, abuse minds and destinies, they trash around as they see fit. And the common people can't do shit about it, they're stripped of any right to interject or adequately resist, they're robbed of any sense of safety on even the most basic expectations on how the very reality around them should work.

    It is true to the game even if it isn't a focus or theme, which is another thing entirely. You shouldn't play Mage ruminating how despicable your nature is. But while your character may never see it that way, they're monsters.
