    M20 v MRev

    10-10-2019, 04:56 PM
    So, I have always played the revised edition of WoD. When V20 came out I didn't pick it up because I had all the revised books and was told that it was the same game. so I didn't really find it was a value to me. Recently I have found myself in a Mage game and was told be various players that M20 was the best so I picked it up. As I read it though I find myself growing more frustrated with the book.

    My question is is it really worth switching over from Revised and why?

    Just to let people know, I do not find having all the rules in 1 book appealing becuase it suffers from bloat and is more difficult for me to navigate because of it, so that is not a reason to switch over in my mind. I have already had ot switch out much of the book marking to make it more manageable to search and don't even get me started on the terrors of Ctl + F for this edition.

      I very very much prefer M20, myself.

      First off, while the corebook is a tome, not all the rules and things got squeezed into it - essentially the original content planned for it instead became four books.

      * M20 Corebook
      * M20 How Do You DO That? - A book that tries to give a lot of examples and explanations on how to do certain Magickal effects. Contains neat things, but also contains controversial ideas that cause some Sphere bloat. I still recommend it, if you simply treat it as "The Book of Phil's Houserules" and pick what you like, drop what you don't. The book itself suggests that very way of handling it.
      * M20 Book Of Secrets - More general stuff that didn't fit into core. You get almost all Merits/Flaws of all the old mage books combined, some adjusted, some neat new ones. Also more paradigms, practices, archetypes, instruments, combat maneuvers, detailed Wonder creation, rules for Child Mages, details about the justice systems among the different factions, and a big M20 FAQ
      * M20 Gods & Monsters - Big book of NPC (and weird player) templates and rules (From mundane humans to gods) including a character creation system for all of that, akin to the old Bygone Bestiary book.

      Personally, I'd get all of those; Each brings good stuff to the table, and make for a rather complete package. All you really need some of the old books for is fluff, like the Trad/Conventionbooks.

      Now some explanations regarding M20: The whole edition is geared towards players of pretty much all previous Editions, especially Metaplot options.

      * The Traditions, Technocracy and Disparate Crafts are all contained in core, and each is an equally valid group for player characters.

      * Metaplot events (Avatar Storm, fall of Doissetep, corruption of the Technocracy, withering of Magick, Fall of Horizon, betrayal by the Hollow Ones etc.) are all pick-your-poison-if-they-happened, in a sandbox fashion. Each "Future Fates" entry has explanations and points as to what choices will affect your game in what ways, and what mood they set.

      * Magick rules have gone back to 2nd / Sorceror's Crusade by default; The total amount of Arete successes counts for all the effect's properties (Only when using Damage + Duration at once does one of them have to be split out.). This has the advantage that you don't need a lot of extended rolls or big rituals on average to pull off things due to the small Arete dice pool, like Revised tended to demand. But the book of course also offers the optional rule of splitting up the dice for every aspect of an effect, like in Revised.

      * Instruments (called Foci before) are no longer bound strictly to whole spheres. Instead Mages simply use instruments in a fashion where it makes sense for their practice and effect in question - heck, they can use different instruments for the same effect, if it makes sense. Dropping instruments (i.e. foci in Rev) then allows you to do those *effects* you used to do with them without the use of an instrument. This system to me makes a lot more sense, because the Focus assignment per sphere always seemed nonsensical, given how different the effects per sphere can be, and thus called for different ways of creating them.

      * The Corebook has a supergood listing of how a Mage's Belief/Paradigm -> Instruments (prev. called Foci) -> Practice work (Now called a Mage's Focus as the total package), and Book of Secrets just expands on that. It is really really good for introducing new players to how Mages think and work, and also gives you a lot of freedom in coming up with stuff for a character.

      * Each Tradition/Sect/faction now has multiple affinity spheres, with the character picking one of them at chargen. Finally.

      * Paradox is lessened back to 2nd ed levels (Example: 1 paradox for successful vulgar effects), which means your Mages won't be constrantly buried in it for small things. Again the book offers an optional rule for "Reckoning" Metaplot use a la Revised and lists the old rules

      * As written above, the Avatar Storm is optional

      * Sphere Specialties!

      * Really good Sphere charts for common Magical effects

      * The Arch Sphere system(s) of the old books are *very* optional - M20 stresses that you can pretty much pull off anything with the 1-5 Sphere levels, if you are creative enough and get enough successes to do it.

      * Two indexes in the back! A general and a rules index. Amazing thing, given they seriously let you find the anything in those tomes.

      I can't list everything, really, There are a lot of little neat rules, adjustments, ideas, fluff and explanations, but those are the big mechanics that pop up in my head immediately. There is a lot more stuff around it all.
      Hope that helps!

      EDIT:
      It also contains a section involving the whole Hypothetical-Average-Bystander / Hypothetical-Omniscient-Observer / Rresult-based / Process-based debates among mage fans when it comes to how Magick works, what the advantages and disadvantages of each approach are, and what Mage 20 assumes by default. ( Hint, it's Hypothetical-Average-Bystander and Process-Based )

                      They really are some great books. I suggest you start with the M20 core and Book of Secrets. They are very well written and much more consistent than the old books. There still is a lot of fluff, but it is far less distracting and doesn't fog up the rules. What I love is that it is written by a gamer for gamers. Satyros doesn't pull any punches either. Another thing I love is how all the extended bits & pieces are now consistent with one another. A big deal is to have all the Merits and Flaws assembled in one book, knowing that a singe author has read them all and made revisions as needed to preserve consistency.

                      Now that I have typed that word, I now realize it is the beauty M20's. Consistency. Sure, you can find everything you want in all the old books, but there are innumerable minor contradictions you have to resolve. Plus there is a general sense of "refinement". We have had decades of "play-testing" and years of feedback that guided Brutaco as he wrote the thing. And he gets personal with the reader. No hidden mysterious powers that be. He discusses various approaches, mentions the controversial rule some people might still like and puts it in there as an option.

                              I'm with Ambrosia on this M20 vs MRev thing.

                              A few more stuff M20 has that I think it's worth mentioning:

                              Reality Zones:
                              This is something I always wanted Mage to have, the Idea that the Consensus holds different power in different parts of the world (depending on the local beliefs) was always kinda floating there in the material, but this was never properly ruled. Now it is. What is Vulgar and what is Coincidental can change to some extent depending on the local consensus. This provides a more sensible background for the Ascencion War: Now to try to change the local beliefs (or protect them) makes a difference, you can trully "win territory" for your Paradigm (or lose it).

                                                  M20 has a system for Resonance that I personally prefer over what MRev provided. It also revised the Quiet rules, though that's mainly a change in terminology (Jhor is now called Morbidity so that the term Jhor can go back to its original meaning of a type of Resonance; and Clarity is now called Denial, which fits it better). That said, Book of Secrets introduces the Negative Men, a variant on the Marauders who are in the thrall of Denial rather than Madness and tend to instinctively generate countermagick instead of shoving Paradox onto others.

                                                  The single biggest change in the game systems is the way paradigm is handled. In particular, magical styles are no longer hardwired to your faction: that is, you don't need to be a Verbena to be a witch, or an Etherite to be a mad scientist, etc. (The opposite still holds: while not all witches are Verbena, all Verbena are witches.) It does this by splitting the Theory and Practice sections that were in the MRev Tradition write-ups into their own section of the book, independent of any faction, and having the M20 faction write-ups merely reference which ones mages in that faction subscribe to. In fact, even the list of suitable magical tools has been shifted out of the Tradition write-ups and into the Practice write-ups.

                                                  (There's also a massive shift in terminology in this regard: what earlier editions called foci, M20 calls instruments; what earlier editions called Paradigm, M20 calls Focus; and what earlier editions called Theory, M20 calls Paradigm. Practice is still Practice.)

                                                  One effect of these shifts is that Orphans are no longer anything-goes mages with patchwork paradigms; instead, they're “merely” mages who have refused to join any of the factions. Taken as a whole, their magic is still all over the place; but considered individually, there's often little difference between an Orphan and a Traditionist other than political alignment. In MRev, if you wanted to play a witch, you'd play a Verbena; in M20, if you want to play a witch, you play a mage who Practices Witchcraft, who may or may not be a Verbena.
