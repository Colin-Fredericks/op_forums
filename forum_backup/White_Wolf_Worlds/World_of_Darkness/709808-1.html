                    There was a bit I was reading in the Revised Book of Madness about how, from the Werewolves' perspective, all the forces of corruption come from the Wyrm - not just figuratively, but literally. it also laid out a canonical metaplot explanation for the forces behind the Nephandi, no matter their breed, and how those forces were the dreams and emanations of the Neverborn, which meant that, if you used that explanation in your Mage game, the so-called Wyrm and Maeljin were incarnations of the Neverborn's dreams (since the Malfean Nephandi worshipped the Maeljin Incarnae, and all Nephandi's objects of worship were placed under that clause). I 'm hoping (and think this is the case) that this is no longer canon, but both of those interpretations allow for the forces of evil to all be working together because they all have the same bosses with different email addresses.

                    Also, it's a useful storytelling device, especially for storytellers who have players that want a bit of everything. Rather than have to come up with individual plots for each villain, they can work together to achieve the same aim, cutting down on potentially overwhelming amounts of plot for the players to deal with/chase. If the outcome is reasonable to all involved parties, even if the reasons come from different places for each, it makes sense that they would agree to work together, at least for a little while, to achieve their mutual interest. It just so happens that the interests of the bad guys line up more often than those of the good guys (especially when the good guys have a reason to hate all the bad guys, like the Garou do with vampires, mages, hunters... pretty much everyone actually).
