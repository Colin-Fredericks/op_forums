                  Oh, so many thoughts on this... I'll start at the top and work my way down.

                  1. 'Need' a new Bygone Bestiary is too strong of language as the NEED is not there, but it's a book that could use another look in the current classic World of Darkness (a bit of punning that I leave stand!). I'd think, though, we won't see such a thing until after the release of a 20th Anniversary Edition of Changeling: the DREAMING* NOT Changeling: the Lost. Things like bygones and chimera and what-have-you seem to only apply to that game when discussed around books, even though Bygone Bestiary was ostensibly a supplement for Mage: the Sorcerers Crusade, but not exclusively so. Go figure that, hey?

                  2. The idea of a monster manual is NOT bad in-and-of itself, but really by the time of a Monster Manual IX offering the giant vampire dire shrew of Vecna for my Dungeons & Dragons campaign it gets, let's face it, bloody ridiculous.

                  3. I'd like to see a new version of Bygone Bestiary with a proper 'toolbox' ethic all the way throughout. I mean, there's some wonderful 'creature building' work in the original, but the chapter of monsters (the one that feels too close to a Monster Manual for my taste) doesn't offer quite enough in terms of possibilities. Further, the creation process in Bygone Bestiary feels very mechanical (again, a little too D&D 'roll D100 on table of Monster Tail Features' for me), but I dunno if there's much of a way around that. Finally, I didn't like the conceit of these things as playable player characters; this is MUCH more of a 'mileage varies' sort of thing that any of the above, though, as some of us actually DO want that in the book. I'm just not one of them.

                  ...All of the above notwithstanding, I think that Bygone Bestiary is a wonderful addition to the World of Darkness, and I'm glad to have it. That's something I cannot say about every book in my collection.

                  * Edit: I know better!

                                      The problem here is that different game-lines view "bygones" differently. In WtA they are mostly Wyrm monsters that should be exterminated and are already covered in the "Book of Wyrm" in its various incarnations. In CtD they are "chymere" - critters made out of "dreamstuff" that, usually do not influence the real world.

                                      But in MtA bygones are real. The trouble here - they go "poof" (or "splash") due to Unbelief and Paradox. Plus, they are "thaumavores" - i.e. they need to consume Quinessence regillarly just not to die in the real world. So, naturally, they tend to "nest" near Nodes. You know, a sought after sources of power by nearly every faction in MtA. Now try to imagine how much of them are still alive in the "real world" after the centuries of the Ascension War.

                                      There were a reason why early Tradition mages "evacuated" bygones to Umbral realms - to preserve them from the Unbelief. I see no reson for them to be any large presence in the modern WoD, barring the instances when a bunch of Marauders decides to bring them back "to play".

                                      For me the original "Bygone bestiary" was totally useless. And I see absolutely no way how it's v. 2.0. would be any better. Ultimately, MtA is not a game of killing monsters and looting their corpses for Tass - at least, in my understanding. Surely, a new "Book of Madness" in its "Marauders" or "Umbral Denizens" chapter could cover some iconic creatures like griffins, dragons or unicorns. But nothing warrants an entire book about them. Maybe I do not see something here, then blame it on the fact that I'm totally unfamiliar with the tabletop DnD.

                                      As for the dragons - Aleph, have you seen "Sin-Dragons" from DA: Mage? Not a big loss, IMO. Actually, a chornicle set in M:TSC centered around slaying these monsters (ideally - by the Order of Reason group) is something I've been considering for some time.
