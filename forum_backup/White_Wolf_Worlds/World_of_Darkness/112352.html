                              Ice and snow effects would be very thematic for Tzimisce Koldunism (published in V20 core, and the Players' Guide to the High Clans). The Way of Wind 2 offer a slow freeze effect that could freeze a limb over several rounds. For a 2nd dot power, that's pretty impressive.

                              If you want a purer "ice mage" schtick in Vampire, I'd talk to your ST about making a custom Path/Way to do it. Template it on an existing direct-damage Path, using most of the system and changing the cosmetic effects.

                              Hermetic Path of Winter's Bite (Tremere style):
                              Start with the Lure of Flames rules. Instead of making fire, you make ice crystals. Rather than the Palm of Flame effect, you may summon a small chunk of ice within your hands. More successes on the activation roll allow for more complex shape. 1 success yields a rough, irregular ice crystal. 5 success will allow you to summon a miniature ice sculpture in the shape you desire.

                              When you use the "place flame where you want it" side of things, you are inflicting a deep freeze in that area instead. Frostbite inflicts damage to living and unliving tissue just as fire consumes it. Inanimate objects are similarily damaged by freezing or moisture, though this is often less destructive than fire. Surfaces become slick, dangerously so at high levels of the Path.

                              Koldunic Way of Ice (Tzimisce style):
                              Based on Way of Fire. Use its system for rolls and damage/magnitude.
                              • Frozen Resolve
                              Gain soak dice against ice and cold effects equal to your rating in Way of Ice.
                              -or-
                              Borrow the first dot power from Way of Wind or Way of Earth. Either one can be given an icy theme and make sense.
                              Grasping Soil becomes Winter's Shackles and has ice crystals growing and snaking up the victim's legs instead of dirt.

                              •• Freeze
                              Direct damage freezing effect. Just change it costmetically. The target partially freezes.

                              ••• Blizzard Pallisade
                              Functionally like Wall of Magma, but the barrier consists of a highly localize storm of roiling snow and ice.

                              •••• Cold Snap
                              Functionally like Heat Wave. All moisture is drawn out from the victim, swirling into a cloud of tiny crystals that dance around them until both cloud and victim collapse.

                              ••••• Freezing Geyser
                              Functionally like Volcanic Blast, except that solid rock isn't left behind (it just doesn't make sense). Geysers of fast moving, freezing cold water erupt from the earth and any other pipe, chasm or body of water in the vicinity. Like freezing rain, this gush freezes upon contact with anything, encasing the affected area in an icy prison and inflicting severe frostbite upon any living or unliving things touched by it.

                              The cold is severe enough that the ice remains at least for the rest of the night, trapping everything within it, no matter how warm the weather might otherwise be. In cold winter climates, the ice can remain until the natural spring thaw.


                              Your ST can set a threshold for how much aggravated damage from either of these powers would count as limb-shattering. I'd go with two for an arm, three for a leg, but he/she may feel differently. I just made these up on the spot, so they probably could benefit from refinement before play.

                                  Per others responses, as well: yeah -- a mage could create cold effects very easily, so long as their Sphere-emphasis provided for that. Forces:2 could freeze simple objects/things pre-disposed to being frozen (by controlling the heat in, say, a given body of water to be directed elsewhere); Forces:3 could more readily freeze most things in general by transforming any active heat into its metaphysical opposite (freezing people, etc.,); Matter:2 coupled with Forces:3 would allow you to manifest ice from practically thin air by transforming ambient air/moisture into water and then freezing it (if you wanted to create massive amounts -- relative to the environment you're in -- of ice, then you'd have to transform local rock/whatnot into ice with the above, or add Prime:2 to it to create such from literally nothing).

                                  I'm surprised you (the OP) have said you were wanting to make a vampire or a mage but haven't "found anything" about creating ice-effects: that sort of thing is clearly indicated in Mage via the listed spheres/effects. (Of course, in Dark Ages: Mage, things are even conceptually simpler, as the general concept of a given thing can be included in a given Pillar, as is the case with ice/freezing things, which can be easily accomplished with, for example, Vires:3 for a Hermetic mage, Winter:3 for an Old Faith/pagan witch, or Hjalder:3 for a Valdaerman/Norseman; the other Fellowships can similarly create such effects by adding a lesser level to a comparative base Pillar to effect such, like with The Messianics using Rapha-El:2, Uri-El:2/3 to manifest simple or slightly more complex cold/ice, etc...). Just saying...

                                                  In regards to Mage, there are a million ways to do this. It depends on your paradigm.

                                                  Forces could control the heat of an object, removing it would cause things to get very cold and freeze.
                                                  Matter could actually control ice itself, but would have difficulty affecting the environment without a major working.
                                                  Entropy and/or Time could make already existing ice stronger or weaker, take it further or closer to the melting point. (sort of an obtuse solution)

                                                  So if I was creating a full-fledged Ice-Only type mage, the best way to get him going would be Forces 3, Matter 3, Prime 2. I can't imagine much ice-related you couldn't do with that alone.
