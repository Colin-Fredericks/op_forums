    Share your house canon for us about Mage: The Ascension

    08-20-2017, 08:54 PM
    Pretty much what the title says. Share what you have as canon at your table even if it's not canon in the main games about factions, players, spheres, or how magic works. Talk about anything and anyone as long as it fits into the M:TA universe.

    Metaphysics

    * Every Awakened mage actually "knows" how in-universe magic works. They understand it's a matter of using WillTM to influence RealityTM. This includes Technomancers, Celestial Choirsters, Independents, and Traditionalists. However, Paradigm is still vitally important to keep you from going CRAZY trying to figure out how to live in a reality which is inherently subjective. In fact, thinking too hard about this is a major cause of going Marauder.

    This also means the Ascension War is essentially less about what is "true" than what method you think is best to get humanity to accept magic and as a result, is sort of arrogant and disgusting as it attempts to force what route humanity will rise above itself.

    * This is not the case with Hedge Mages who genuinely have to believe their magic works exactly along the lines which they're taught. It is notable, though, that Traditions actually can "only" teach Hedge Magic to Sleepers and hope they awaken (converting their Paths to True Magick) as Awakening is not something you can induce.

    * True Mages are outnumbered by about 10 to 1 in their Traditions by Hedge Mages and practitioners versus True Mages. Within Traditions, you can actually rise very high with Hedge Magic but True Mages are considered the prodigies of their field. Also, in some Traditions, if not all, there's not actually that much difference viewed between them except how "good" you are at it.

    * Almost all mages hate casting "cold" and while some Traditions have tests to show you CAN do magic without foci, in practice no one likes it.

    * There's a faction of mages called the Realists which include many Technocrats but also include SoE, VR, and OoH who believe while MAGES affect consensual reality, SLEEPERS do not and that reality can be affected by magic but exists interdependently of it. This staggeringly practical view of reality answers a lot of questions about the universe but annoys a lot of mages on principle and is an oddly unpopular theory.

    * Correspondence can be used to travel into Outer Space but there's no real point as most of it is just incredibly hostile dead space.

    Ascension War

    * It's been almost 17 years since the Ascension War ended in a truce. As such, the youngest Traditions and Technocrats are genuinely confused why anyone cares since the universe, religion, and science hasn't ended because of said treaty. This annoys the Masters of both sides as they "grew up" in the 90s where both sides were indiscriminately murdering the others.

    * The NWO's use of Tradition brains for Awakened HIT marks could restart the war, which is what they're planning. Unknown to the Traditions, this might actually cause the NWO's MIB division to be purged by their fellow Unionists if it was discovered.

    Technocracy

    * Generally, the Technocracy leans to the reactionary Right in terms of its politics. It's the Military Industrial Complex, Capitalism, and status quo. It's also prone to massive infighting as the Cold War was essentially a conflict among Technocrats as well as Sleepers. It's just Traditionalists failed to notice this as they were still hunted by both sides. The Union's immortality also meant many of its leadership were actually atavists versus progressives.

    The Avatar Storm has resulted in many more progressives emerging in the Union, even as they're kind of shocked and appalled there were eugenicists and phrenologists in high positions before it.

    * The Union's seeming omniscience was due to its infiltration of law enforcement and intelligence agencies globally. Much of the ground work was actually due to the fact they could say, "be on the look out for X" versus their own people.

    * The Union was never quite as influential as it believed itself to be in science, politics, or capitalism. It was outstandingly rich and powerful but often times followed the whims of the Consensus and updated their views rather than try to cling to them. The Time Table of 1890 has nothing similar to the time table of 1990 or the one which will be in 2090.

    Sleepers can and do make their own contributions to science all the time.

    * The Syndicate's magic basically functions on having unlimited expense accounts and making sure that purchases and deliveries are ridiculously efficient.

    * Threat Null is believed to be alien invaders by the rest of the Union and Nephandi Technocrats. It's really not WRONG if not RIGHT.

    Traditionalists

    * The Pre-Avatar Storm Masters of the Traditions wanted to roll back the clock and destroy modern civilization. They were just smart enough to keep this from their apprentices. However, the modern Traditionalists see nothing wrong with science and flushing toilets next to their magic. Their ecumenism worked far better than they ever imagined.

    * The Celestial Choir has the largest number of Marauders to deal with. This is due to the fact religious mages either adapt to universal cosmic truths or retreat into dogma. This faction, called The Zealots, is a common problem for them.

    * The Sons of Ether have a severe image problem because 80% of them are serious scientists who have a sense of whimsy about their work. The remaining 20% basically have degenerated into justifying anything they believe as true, which gives them a bad name.

    Most SOE really want to make magic "sufficiently analyzed science" but it's a case of Turtles all the Way DownTM so far. The Technocracy has supported their quackery, even going so far as to release brainwashed SOE back into the Traditions.

    * The Order of Hermes' method of doing magic is substantially longer and more time consuming than any other method. However, it is a trade off for how comparatively easy it is to TEACH. A Cult of Ectasy mage might never learn Life even if they master Time in six months. The Order of Hermes, however, can always guarantee they will have the basis for the effect you want.

    * The Euthanotos include a substantial number of doctors, healers, and pacifists among them. Their reversal of the Good Death ethos confuses many Traditionalists as both sides treat it as a valid philosophical disagreement versus an actual conflict. There's also a Christian and Voodoo sect of the organization which many find even more confusing.

    * The Virtual Adepts are outstandingly rich and powerful in the New Teens, especially since so many of them have "grown up." They see no reason to return to the Technocracy, though, but have grown distant from the other Traditions. Some believe they can "rule alone."

    * The Hollow Ones are actually a natural consequence of mass produced information about magic, theory, and science resulting in hidden "secrets" becoming available. The fact they are spontaneous mages should be a hopeful sign but their interfaith smoothie elements annoy the Traditions--which is hypocrisy on a massive scale.

    Nephandi

    * The desire to wipe out everyone and everything seems like an insane reaction to the world but Nephandi come in multiple varities: 1. The ones who are tricked into the Cauls because of personal pain, demonic pact, or a belief it will provide a sort of nihilistic enlightenment. 2. The fact the pressure of knowing reality is flexible and subject to human will but humans STILL make it a hellish World of Darkness full of monsters causes many to believe the universe should start over without mankind.

      My house canon changes from campaign to campaign, but I have a couple of key tenets:

      1. Reality is made by everything with a spirit, but some spirits get more votes. Essentially, there is a Consensus, but the Consensus can be moved if enough believe differently.

      2. Since there are many realities, they can overlap at various levels. What is Above Is Below, but not all the time. Things just don't go up some Great Chain of being or from the molecule to the protein to the gene to the organism to the species (biology).

      3. The overlap of Realities at various levels is why the Science of the Technocracy is Control. Technocracy Science is reductionism, which works nicely in labs under controlled conditions but not in the real world. The Technocracy is working to define theory in a certain way so that people don't dispute gravity and other theories.

      4. Mages are special humans, but they are special because of their understanding, not anything innate. Any Sleeper can, with sufficient will and an experience with opens them to the possibility that reality is subjective and that the world is what they make it, Awaken.

      5. Hedge magic I barely use as it's a whole subsystem to create those who can do magic but aren't mages. If I differentiate from hedge mages and True mages, it's that hedge mages are partially awakened--they just have to go by their rituals instead of seeing the ability to change things with focused will.

      6. I tend to avoid things like the Avatar Storm that seem like metaplot-based efforts to weaken particular spheres. There are some cool ideas with the Avatar Storm, but I downplay it as you can't really do Umbral travel without substantial workarounds in the Avatar Storm framework.

      7. Since reality has many levels, there is no global victory in the Ascension War. Victory in the Ascension War thus becomes being awesome enough to get one's paradigm popular enough that it has persistence in the High Umbra, the realm of ideas. That way, a Mage may eventually succumb to mortality, but they will live forever as a name linked to certain ideas.

      8. Mages are the most powerful supernatural. The reason they don't really control everything is mages aren't monolithic and there are enough of the other supernaturals in various groups that no mage group has been able to exert its power completely. The Technocracy, were it willing to annihilate its various Reality Deviant associates in the Syndicate and Void Engineers, could come the closest but humans always have this odd believe in things outside of Nature because they don't see everything at all scales and time periods.

      I have never run a total 'Traditions' win campaign, but the Traditions always have turned back the tide of the Technocracy and made a place for their ideas.

      I like a lot of the history and lore in Mage, but always ignore that which is annoying. Most players don't seem to read all the lore, so I go more with what players bring into the game than the official lore.

          Concerning Nephandi, I contend that the process of Avatar Inversion completely alters what the Fallen find emotionally fulfilling. Unlike normal people, who can learn to obtain pleasure from evil but can be assumed to have "positive" passions outside what they've learned (an evil person can still enjoy sex, food, and the prospect of having children), a Nephandus can only be satisfied when evil is done. Such is their new Normal, and it colors their emotional contexts and priorities. They want to kill, despoil, corrupt, torture, and ruin, because it's what gives them joy.

          This is an inherently non-logical force, but is the undercurrent and driver for all that the Fallen do. All other explanations that Nephandi give for why they do what they do are, in fact, after-the-fact rationalizations for what they would be doing anyway. In the way that a normal person would feed a hungry stranger out of pity, then try to make logical excuses for the act that was principally motivated by raw emotion, a Nephandus would inflict pain on a stranger out of sadism or hate, then come up with a reason for the act after. They are already resolved to be bastards in their hearts; philosophizing about why they should do it is them trying to justify the acts to themselves, and to others (especially those they want to convert to their cause).

          This is also why so many different Nephandi have different end goals. They each have their own Paradigms, meant to contextualize the negative desires they share, and how the world around them works in relation to those feelings. They are, indeed, regular mages reflected in a mirror darkly.

                  Metaphysics

                  * Every mage knows that magic works, but not why it works or how it works. Even the 'purple paradigm' of the Traditions is not absolute, and Mages perform feats of blatant impossibility on a regular basis requiring a redrawing of the paradigm every time... or to just ignore it because the old paradigm still works perfectly fine and Reproducing a feat of impossibility isn't a guaranteed thing.

                  * Hedge MagicTM Is actually just sleepers making use of highly advanced talismans. At the heart of every tradition of static magic is an artifact that without which, they would lose their powers.

                  * Hedge Mages are more or less the minion squad of whichever True Mage crafted the path of Hedge Magic they make use of, or one of the few free paths who've kept their source of magic from the technocrats seeking to eliminate them. As such, they're a relatively numerous, but niche group.

                  * Awakening can be induced with relative ease in any sleeper who can muster the effort to read and practice the techniques in a Primer. True Mages are about as plentiful as military special forces, or expert scientists. Or other such 'rarities but not completely rare' things.

                  * Most smart mages are akin to batman. While any mage can hypothetically do anything on the fly, it's generally acknowledge that quick casting is virtual suicide, and Rotes are for parlour tricks, not combat. Charms, whether in the form of scrolls, potions, bullets, or gadgets are generally the most common form of 'combat magic' and both sides of the Ascension War make frequent use of artifacts and talismans.

                  The Ascension War

                  * The Ascension War is more or less over, reducing from a hot war to a cold war between the Technocracy and those Traditional Groups with the strength and conviction left to oppose them. While those Traditionalists without those traits are more or less trying to find a place for their beliefs in the Technocracies new world to limited success. The Technocracy kills the former as they always have, and scoffs at the latter. Though Traditionalists are more or less permitted to do their thing so long as they don't 'break the mask' so to speak. As long as magic is kept hidden away and out of the public eye, everything is ok.

                  * Several factions within both the Traditions and the Technocracy are attempting to restore the conflict to Hot status, within the Technocracy, the Progenitors and Iteration X known for their bloody minded insistence that all vestiges of the Traditions must be wiped out. Whether the former due to increasing frustration with the latest waves of pseudo-scientific alternative medicine, or the latter due to being built for war and knowing nothing else. Among the Traditions, the Order of Hermes, the Virtual Adepts, and the Verbena are considered the 'hotheads' of the alliance. The Order of Hermes due to their pride and arrogance, the Virtual Adepts due to being relatively successful in their war for cyberspace, and the Verbena because they're desperate to save the planet before untamed organic life is replaced with some tamed thing that the Technocrats cook up in a vat.


                  The Technocracy

                  * The Technocracy is EXCEEDINGLY progressive, and has been for decades. Having been in the position to define social progress for decades, the very concept of Progressivism was constructed as a means to weaken the social hold of certain traditions who had previously drawn from ethnic groups for new members. With the destruction of those ethnic groups as concepts, the Technocrats cripple their enemies ability to recruit.

                  * The Union is more or less 'in command' of the governments of the 'west' with it's influence being limited outside of that sphere. China and Russia, while possessing their own branches of the Technocracy are less connected to the greater whole of the Union and beset with their own problems they refuse to talk about. Southern China, near India is largely Void engineer territory.

                  * While individual inventions are not always designed by the Union, the laws of physics and other such 'underlying structural models' and as such the proper meat of science are entirely fabricated by the union. You might be able to invent a toaster without doing magic, but that's because the magic was done for you to lay the ground work.

                  * There is an established 'thing' where people raised within Iteration X somehow end up in romantic entanglements with Verbena, every, single, time. Converts and late joiners do not suffer from this. Some say this was caused by entropic manipulation.

                  The Traditions

                  * A large portion of The Traditions really do want to destroy modern civilization. A few pay lip service to wanting to keep science alongside magic, but the truth of the matter is that if they brought back the mythic age, the Consensus would die, and along with it all of modern civilization. Mages would still be able to use their toys, with some work done to make it so, of course, but the sleepers would lose all these advances.

                  * Those who do NOT want the above to happen are more or less open to working with the Technocrats to create bubble realms. Pockets of registered 'weirdness' that are quite simply off the radar. The bizarre temple in a junkyard, the flooded court in a slag heap. Places where magic can still survive and thrive, but is also contained.

                      Metaphysics:
                      - I like to think most Tradition and Craft mages will acknowledge that magick seems to transcend Paradigm, but tend to each have their own ideas about why that is, and what it means to overcome the need for Instruments. They also have extensive explanations for why mages from other groups are able to do what they do, despite seeming to defy Reality as they know it.

                      For instance, the Technocracy - when it bothers to dwell on the subject - probably sees Traditionalists as employing a form of psychic power, their minds drawing upon scientific principles they don't understand. The ritual and pageantry are just psycho-dramas, meant to direct their powers in lieu of true understanding. This is also part of the reason the Technocracy eschews psychic powers as a rule; they may think that superstitionists have put too much of their Enlightenment on their brains, creating psychic nodes that are unstable and prone to mishap. How else to explain the bizarre events that happen around and to the superstitionists, if not that their overactive psionics are reacting to unconscious feelings and desires? Better to focus the Enlightenment through tools and technologies, which can more safely take the strain of scientific work. (It's additionally why the superstitionists tend to seem bonkers; screwing around with their psyche is unhealthy).


                      - Linear Sorcery is the result of methods of Magick being so accepted in the Consensus, that they can be employed by those who understand how. They are now part of the rules of Reality. But I go a step further, maintaining that mundane skills are the result of Linear Sorcery and more eclectic forms of magic that have become so accepted by the Consensus, as to become ubiquitous. What is the Drive skill, but a refined form of the Path of Conveyance? What are guns, but specialized Instruments for the Path of Hellfire? Simple machines like wedges, ramps, and pulleys were primal Instruments of Magick, capable of negating the weight of great loads (Forces 2) or splitting into solid materials (Matter 3). Even the act of Speech is a method of Telepathy, facilitated by a series of grunts, hisses, clicks, and inflections, along with a number of subtle gestures, facial expressions, and body movements. And Speech is SO ubiquitous, it doesn't even need a whole Ability; a character is assumed to have this magical power by default, and need only take the Language merit to have more of these Speech methods available to them.

                                      Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                      * The Order of Hermes' method of doing magic is substantially longer and more time consuming than any other method. However, it is a trade off for how comparatively easy it is to TEACH. A Cult of Ectasy mage might never learn Life even if they master Time in six months. The Order of Hermes, however, can always guarantee they will have the basis for the effect you want.
                                      I wouldn't agree that Hermetic magick is easier to Teach. It's more consistent, certainly. The Ars Hermetica are formulaic, like recipes. If all elements are in their proper place at the proper time, the spell will take the proper shape, every time. If the spell fails to turn out the right way, either the theory or the execution of the ritual was flawed. None of this "Magick, do what you will" nonsense, where you have, at best, a vague idea of what will happen. With Hermeticism, it's like programming: garbage in, garbage out. When done right, it is controlled and exact as the movement of the planets themselves.

                                      But, again, this doesn't make it easy to learn. Far from it, actually: the Order of Hermes makes it quite difficult to learn. This, incidentally, is by design.

                                      First, those formulas take an obscene amount of occult factors into account. The positions of planets and constellations, alchemical properties of metals, the names and domains of angels, elementals, and gods, magic words in several (often dead) languages, the numeralogical correspondences of words, complex geometric designs, ritual implements like cups and daggers, seals of solomon, and the madness that is the Enochian system (not the language, the system as put down by John Dee and Edward Kelly; a system even the Golden Dawn finds complex and high level). Not every magus will use every element - not unless she studies broadly - but any one of these can be dizzying to keep straight.

                                      Moreover, the Order of Hermes makes the curriculum more difficult than it probably needs to be. There's a reason initiates tend to need to learn multiple languages over the course of their study, just to read their text books. That reason is the same reason why they need to jump through all the hoops put before them: to weed out the failures. The lazy, the sloppy, the weak-willed, the impatient. Those looking for quick power or shortcuts, or who aren't committed to the lifestyle of the magician. All of these people are a danger to themselves or others, and should not be provided the keys to creation lightly. An initiate is either proven to lack these poor qualities, overcomes them through force of will and hard work, or washes out of the program.

                                      The Order of Hermes are elitists, but they are so because they take Magick seriously. While everyone should have the chance to reach for the sublime reaches of mysticism, not everyone can or should make it. There is a hierarchy of creation, and a person can only raise their station by lifting themselves up by their bootstraps. One who would be called Magus Rex is expected of nothing less.

                                                  Iteration X

                                                  * There's actually a reason Iteration X was Indoctrinated up to level 10 to the point everyone assumed they were the Borg and it wasn't the fact the Computer was doing it. It's generally out of a fear of another Sons of Ether where the power to command matter basically brings out most nerds inner supervillain.

Also, bluntly, a lot of Iteration X members seethed with serious grudges against society for various reasons. Grudges which manifested as wanting to be the boot rather than the ant (or they'd be with the Traditions).

                                                  It's also made their claims of being "Where Virual Adepts go when they grow up" a very hollow statement.

                                                  Progenitors

Progenitor labs tend to work on designer drugs, medicines, superpowers (concealable ones), psychic powers, and enhancements for the body. They actually have made X-men style mutants and while this is vulgar as hell and NEVER going to be on the Time Table, they still work to their paradigm.

                                                  * Progenitors, like Iteration X USED to be, cannot learn Dimensional Science. It breaks their brain and causes a lot of them to have severe confused reactions.

                                                  NWO

                                                  * If you pass a series of complicated tests designed to prove you're a lying duplicitous bastard (essentially) who has a moral flexibility even to the Technocracy-- essentially, by withholding information from your superiors when they leave a trail of clues for you to find which upends the party line (in a sort of Reverse 1984) they will lead you to Room 202.

                                                  What is in Room 202?

                                                  A man with a file.

                                                  A man with a file who will explain, yes, vampires, magic, ghosts, fairies, and more are real. The Technocracy's entire paradigm is bunk and the other Conventions are quite possibly either stupid, in denial, or varying degrees of insane (possibly all at once). It is your job to keep them like this because the alternative is global annihilation once people figure they can create nuclear explosions with their mind.

                                                  You pass the test by never ever bringing up Room 202 again unless you become the man with the file.

                                                  Syndicate

                                                  * The Syndicate gives the same speech as Room 202 but does it over lunch and a power-point presentation. It ends by the provider telling students that sharing these facts to other Technocrats will probably get you killed.

                                                  * The Syndicate is made up of roughly two different factions. 1. The Syndicate which believes Capitalism is GoodTM. 2. The Syndicate which believes Capitalism is a necessary evil. Generally, a large number of socialists and even some outright communists have belonged to the Syndicate as it is the faction of Wealth. It's just a violent and not particularly friendly discussion of how to make it so the masses produce enough to gain the benefits of it.

                                                  * Quite a few Syndicate members are mathematicians and economists who are not rich. The stereotype they are annoys the hell out of them.

                                                  * The Syndicate is actively at war with Pentex now and doing a shocking amount of damage now that it's Nephandi corrupted branch is gone.

                                                  Void Engineers

                                                  * The whole, "The Void Engineers were a hated and disliked part of the Union" was never actually true nor were they ever actually thinking about defecting to the Traditions. The lack of focus on Outer Space stung quite a few VE but honestly, it was THEY who had the biggest objections to further outer space research.

                                                  This is due to the discovery of the "alien invasion" of Changeling: The Dreaming and many other events.

                                                  * Void Engineers generally accept alien races exist on other planets but are much more concerned about other-dimensional aliens RIGHT HERE, RIGHT NOW invading. They generally have more knowledge about other supernaturals than anyone but the NWO (who have it in their files but don't comprehend it) but have generally misfiled it. Vampires? Other-dimensional parasite possessing body. Werewolves? Other-dimensional parasite? Fairies? Other dimensional parasites and actual aliens from another dimension. Ghosts? Ghosts are real but psychic-phenemenon and not "really" people.

                                                  Generally, the Void Engineers are huge proponents of the Pogrom and always have been. Ditto, the militant ruthless technocracy. The Traditions just misunderstood because the VE don't think the Traditions are the reason why the Union should exist. The VE think they should be killing every OTHER supernatural rather than worrying about a bunch of psychics.

                                                  Ironically, VE actually think Hunters are monsters possessed by aliens as well and hunt them when detected while thinking Mages are to be left alone.
