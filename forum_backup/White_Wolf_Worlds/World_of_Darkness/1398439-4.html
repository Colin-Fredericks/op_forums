    I like the Thrall of the Wyrm as a potential risk that will somehow taint the Garou. That discourages having too much Rage, or at least not balancing it out with sufficient Willpower.

    However, I share some of the other posters' queasiness at how it is written in the game. Every player will have their own comfort level with the subject matter. For some, it is just best to fade to black and state something generic like "you've done something abominable" and move on. Others like to delight in excess. Not every horror fan likes movies like Texas Chainsaw Massacre or the various torture porn movies that came out. Tastes and preferences differ.

    For my chronicles, I use the Thrall of Wyrm mechanic as something that "permanently" leaves the character as being marked by the Wyrm. It is something I leave up the player to determine for their character, although the other players and I can help them brainstorm. So if they want to use the Thrall examples per breed as written, that's fine. Or they may select something else - like they are compelled to eat fresh bloody meat on the night of the full moon (or their auspice). So now they raid nearby farms and kill and devour livestock. It may not seem any different than normal wolf feeding behavior, but it's an actual compulsion. Or maybe they feel murderous thoughts towards a kinfolk of their bloodline, or the person they love. Or they develop one of the signs of the Wolf - like their eyebrows now connect, or their index finger is as long as their middle finger, or they have tufts of hair at the bottom of the palms. Anything that might reflect actual werewolf as monster lore. It might be left for the PC to roleplay when they feel like it. Or we may set up some kind of mechanical Flaw or Willpower role.

            A Lunatic is a Garou who represses the Change. They resist their primal urging and don't transform, but at the cost of becoming mentally ill from repressing the beast inside them. Some times this can result in them snapping and killing people. Much of the first edition supplement Valkenburg Foundation is about them and takes place in an institute where they can be treated. It is possible to help Lunatics realize what they are and help them transform and accept that they are Garou. But others never get better and remain trapped in one form, though it may be possible for them to adjust in staying in that one form.

            It's distinct from a Lost Cub because a Lost Cub eventually changes on their own. It's just delayed. A Lunatic though, had the Change come upon him, and resisted it, suppressing the wolf, and this unnatural repression causes psychological harm. Although I write this from a human perspective suppressing the wolf inside him, it also happens from lupus who presumably are terrified of the sudden onslaught of rational thought and suppress the human inside them.

            As written, a Lunatic Garou never changes. But it is very clear the inspiration is the old Lon Chaney, Jr. Wolf Man movies, so I usually have them transform into either Glabro or Lupus according to their auspice moon, but with the Beast in control. It's mentioned that Garou are usually forced to kill Lunatics in order to protect the Veil or prevent them from being used by the Wyrm.

            I think as a concept it is only found in Valkenburg, but it may also be mentioned in the first edition corebook. But it wasn't something mentioned later on in future corebooks or the various Player or ST Guides, so it was an element that quickly became lost.

            As an ST, I often try to run at least one story involving Lunatics anytime I run a chronicle. Usually early on after the PCs have settled into their characters and are comfortable with the setting. I then make them responsible for discovering someone is a Lunatic, and deciding how they will deal with that NPC. It's always been well received by my players. I once played a PC whose background was that he was a former Lunatic who was eventually found and coached out of it by his NPC Mentor. His back story had a lot of the traditional pop culture tropes.

                                            Originally posted by Sergeant Brother View Post
                                            Back to the topic of Werewolf. I think it was fine for W:tA to diverge from both Hollywood and traditional werewolf myth, it basically had to in order to make a functional RPG and werewolf society.
                                            This is true. When designing an RPG, you must have a compelling basis for PCs to get together and do things together. For the most part, I think Werewolf succeeded in it. However, I do think it could have done a better job of matching their version to popular expectations. I don't think it requires that much change in presentation. There were some elements in first edition material, but instead of building upon them in second edition, they actually discarded them.

                                            Originally posted by Sergeant Brother View Post
                                            I problem instead was that they made it so that werewolves kill vampires on sight and that a starting werewolf can kill like 10 elder vampires at once. Both the intense hatred and extreme power differential made vampire and werewolf crossovers almost impossible and in fact made crossovers with most other WoD creatures really hard. Both of those things could have been moderated and not reduced W:tA playability at all.
                                            I won't speak to the mechanics except in general while I like the World of Darkness setting, I've always thought the mechanics of the Storyteller System to be horrible. Quite fun in creating a character, but much less so playing it. So I can't defend it in terms of game balance or anything else.

                                            However, I don't think it's a problem that the Garou don't like vampires. First, anyone who is alive should have a problem hanging out with vampires. It's like you are hanging out with a smallpox virus. It's the veritable Scorpion and the Frog fable. The Scorpion at some point is going to try to kill you because that's its nature. Any mixed gaming group of PCs that has a vampire is going to have this problem. The powers and nature of a vampire will make them inherently untrustworthy. It's not a problem specific to Werewolf.

                                            Second, from the very beginning of the game, it showed lots of examples of Garou not killing vampires on site. In both the Central Park setting of the first two corebooks, and Rage Across New York, we were given many cases of Garou interacting with vampires. These were seen as deviations from the Garou norm, but they were fairly common deviations. Especially in an urban based environment where you'd expect most vampires to be in, the default was some level of acquiescence and cooperation provided certain guidelines were kept by both sides.

                                            I think what developed into a zero-tolerance policy was something that came from the fan community, not the game. I think this was done for several reasons.

                                            First, there was a reaction against the presumption that Garou and Kindred would always have "treaties" instead of the situation in Dark Alliance: Vancouver being an exception. This happens in any game when an exceptional element becomes popular and becomes the new default. See the hate many people had for a certain drow ranger.

                                            Second, werewolf players really seemed to take the Litany much more seriously than vampire PCs ever did the Traditions. This wasn't so in early NPC portrayals of the game. As the game turned more into some kind of heroic fantasy game rather than a game of Savage Horror, even groups like the Bone Gnawers - which were shown to have extensive dealings with vampires - were thoroughly cleansed of anything that might smack of them not being true frontline fronters for Gaia. But if you ignore that and go back to first edition and early second edition takes, the game opens up. Your rural sept of Fianna and Get aren't going to do it. But urban septs will need some sort of tolerance just to get things done, and specific pack or character concepts even elsewhere can also be fine with some interaction.

                                            Third, if you run any Werewolf game in the wilderness or with any tribe being available to the PCs, of course you are going to run into a scenario where at least one PC is going to "veto" any involvement with a vampire. But if want to run a game where that is an option - even if only for certain NPC interaction as opposed to bringing in a vampire PC - I think an ST can easily create a plausible scenario to do so. She then only needs to pitch it to players and make this something their characters can accept. Maybe not like, but accept in the way that people in a corrupt environment have to operate in in order to survive.

                                            I know I am in the minority of this as this issue always come up, but I don't think it's an issue for the game.
