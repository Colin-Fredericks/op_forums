    Lifeweb Review/Critique

    12-29-2014, 11:59 PM
    Okay, so Lifeweb is out. Yay! Nice to see Monitor love. Nice to see anything Wraithly get love after all this time, really. Before I start kvetching, I do want to thank everyone for their hard work on the game; I do appreciate it. So, some complaints/observations/rants:

    Tsuchigumo are Lifeweb empowed petty criminals,


    Should probably be "empowered".

    If the Fetter somehow wound up in the Underworld, the difficulty is 7 if it's in the Shadowlands, 8 if it's in the Tempest, and 9
    if it's in the Labyrinth, closest to Oblivion.
    Sentence needs cleaning, but more to the point: Unless the Shroud also has very high difficulties, then those are some very high difficulties for a Fetter in the Underworld. I can see Oblivion jacking up the use of Arcanoi, but reading this it seems like it would be easier to access a Fetter that was across the Shroud in some cases than if it were right next to you in the Shadowlands. That doesn't seem right.

    Even the subtlest Lifeweb uses can have an observer effect, potentially warning the target that
    the Monitor is examining him. When a wraith is subject to any Lifeweb art that isn't
    immediately obvious through other means, he may make a Perception + Awareness roll. One
    success alerts him to the fact that someone's toying with part of his being; with three or more
    successes, he identifies the Monitor as the source.
    This needs clarity. Can a wraith perceive a Lifeweb art if it is being used on one of her Fetters at a remote location, rather than on herself directly? Assuming she gets three successes, how much information is revealed about the Monitor? If the Monitor is concealed somehow, such as being part of a crowd, behind a curtain, or hiding with a different Arcanoi, will multiple successes reveal his location?

    When the Monitor extends her senses to one of her Fetters, the player rolls Perception + Lifeweb.
    Each success on this roll allows her to use one die of her normal Perception + Alertness pool to
    survey the Fetter's location
    Mathematically, this is gonna be a small dicepool by the end of things. It's also rolling dice to see how many dice you get to roll. I might suggest just making it a pass/fail roll, or making Perception+Lifeweb the roll to look around the location.

    If the player spends 1 Pathos when activating this art, she also may assess the Fetter's location
    with a Perception + Awareness roll, subject to the same dice pool limit
    I cannot readily understand the difference between "surveying" and "assessing" a location. You might want to clarify.

    Vibrations in the Web (Ancient)
    While Sense Strands focuses on the Monitor's own Fetters, this complementary art extends her
    perceptions outward, enabling her to perceive the connections between other wraiths and their
    Fetters.
    System: When the Monitor examines another wraith, the player player spends 1 Pathos and rolls
    Perception + Lifeweb. The target may resist with Willpower if he desires.
    Does the target need to be aware to resist? It would be weird otherwise. Or is this one of the uses of Lifeweb that is immediately obvious?

    The player spends 2 Pathos and rolls Intelligence + Lifeweb. For one turn per success,
    the wraith may use any Arcanos art she knows as if she were at the Fetter's location (though she
    may still use them normally at her own location if she so chooses). She also may speak through
    the Fetter, with her voice seeming to originate from it
    Seems kind of expensive for the overall effect.

    If the wraith isn't already using Sense Strands to perceive her Fetter's location, every two
    successes on the roll to activate Strand Channel count as one success for Sense Strands. This
    applies even if the Monitor doesn't know Sense Strands
    A weird and needless complexity.

    The character first touches the living being, place, or material object that she wants to
    make into a temporary Fetter. The player spends 3 Pathos and rolls Intelligence + Lifeweb.
    With any successes, the target becomes a new one-dot Fetter for the Monitor.
    During the same scene, the Monitor may transfer the temporary Fetter from herself to another
    wraith, whom she must touch. The player spends 1 additional Pathos and rolls Intelligence +
    Lifeweb (difficulty 7). If the subject wraith is unwilling, this becomes a resisted roll against his
    Willpower, and the Monitor's Shadow gains 1 temporary Angst. If the Monitor succeeds, the
    temporary Fetter transfers from her to the target wraith
    Once again we are rolling dice to roll dice. Couldn't we just make it one roll, and just say transferring to another wraith ups the difficulty?

    The Monitor may use Splice Strand to repair the effect of Sever Strand. All difficulties increase
    by 1, but success restores the Fetter at its full strength and permanence (and ongoing Pathos
    maintenance is unnecessary)
    Why would it be harder to reattach something that already had all the prerequisites of being Fetter? I would remove the difficulty increase here.

    The Monitor must touch the target Fetter. The player spends 2 Pathos and 1 Willpower
    and makes a resisted Intelligence + Lifeweb roll against the Fetter owner's Willpower. Each net
    success reduces the Fetter by 1 dot and grants the Monitor's Shadow 1 temporary Angst
    I rolled so well that I wound up in Catharsis? That is potentially a lot of Angst for the effect. A straight Angst cost would be more fair, and more tempting. Something is wrong when the Puppeteer can destroy your wife's soul and get less Angst than I can for just reversibly severing your emotional connection.

    Drawing on the spiritual tug of a Fetter, the Monitor can travel to it with breathtaking speed.
    This allows not only convenient travel for ordinary business but a swift rescue of a threatened
    Fetter. However, a wise Monitor looks before leaping – threatening a Fetter to lure a wraith into
    ambush is a time-honored tactic.
    System: The player spends 3 Pathos and 2 Corpus and rolls Perception + Lifeweb. Successes
    determine travel time, regardless of distance:
    1 success One hour.
    2 successes 15 minutes.
    3 successes One minute.
    4 successes 15 seconds (5 turns).
    5 successes One turn.


    Okay, first of all is the cost. Are you kidding? Three Pathos and two Corpus is not "convenient travel", it's a punch in the face. So if this cost is kept it's going to be only for dire emergencies. Secondly, and more importantly, this power punishes mild success more than failure. Say some enemy is smashing up your Fetters, and you use this power to stop them. If you fail, one Fetter is smashed, too bad, but you might have time to protect another one. Just get one success, and now not only is your Fetter still broken, but also you're stuck doing nothing for an hour. I would push this time chart down a bit. If your Fetter is genuinely in danger, you likely have turns, not minutes, to do something about it.

    It might even be better to have two versions for travel and for emergencies, with one costing more but being faster, and the other costing less and being slower. Just a thought.

    The Monitor must touch the being she wishes to bond as a Fetter. The player spends 5
    Pathos and 3 Willpower and rolls Intelligence + Lifeweb. The number of successes determine
    the new Fetter's strength:
    1-2 successes Fetter •
    3-5 successes Fetter ••
    6+ successes Fetter •••
    This makes for a pretty weak Fetter for the cost, and has kind of a wonky structure.

    The Monitor can use this art on a living (or undead) supernatural being, but doing so requires an
    additional Pathos expenditure equal to the subject's Willpower. The subject also must spend 5
    points of whatever supernatural energy it uses in lieu of Pathos (e.g., Blood, Rage, Quintessence,
    and if all else fails, Willpower). Using Souls Interwoven on a vampire or other undead creature
    also gives the Monitor's Shadow an amount of temporary Angst equal to half the deathless thing's
    Willpower    .
    Why add all of this? It's just extra complication, and doesn't add anything except a middle finger to cross-over games. I'd keep the bit about vampires and similar granting angst because it's thematic - might even expand on it - but the rest should really be dropped.

    When the Monitor uses any Arcanos on a wraith, the player may spend 2 additional
    Pathos to apply that Arcanos' effects to one of the target's Fetters. Alternately, she may apply the
    Arcanos to a location or object that is the focus of the target's Legacy or Memoriam background.
    Alternately, when the Monitor uses any Arcanos on a Fetter or a focus of Legacy or Memoriam,
    the player may spend 2 additional Pathos to instead apply that Arcanos' effects directly to the
    wraith in question
    Are the first two uses applying instead of or in addition to applying to the target Wraith?


    Also, on a final note, I think it might be best to consider structuring powers a little differently. I might put the roll and costs separately from the main body of the text when possible, so they are easily found when quick-referencing. Just a thought.

                              The Angst cost for vampires (and potentially other creatures) make sense because anything that is associated with death, destruction or decay is typically tied to Oblivion, and invoking the forces of Oblivion leads to a rise in Angst. Vampires cause angst because of their representation of death - even the nicest vampire is as dead as an iron nail.
                              Vampires on the other hand are eternal by nature not decaying or representative of normal death so it's arguable. They are in a larger metaphysical sense Wyrm tainted with a considerable touch of Weaver thrown into the mix but they are not directly Oblivion related unless speaking of certain twisted and fallen creatures. But if the Angst cost is added there for such a justification, and metagamely as a balancing factor for them being more enduring Fetters than most, it should be a set cost instead of dependent upon the Vampire Willpower. Willpower represents resolve, and while a strong willed beings tend to have stronger Shadows as well it isn't by itself something that implies the touch of Oblivion, and as such is one of those things a Psyche uses to oppose the call of Oblivion and the whispers of his Shadow. Better explanation would be that the Vampiric Beast is touched by Oblivion, though again that is somewhat stretching it as the Beast seeks survival above all else where as Shadows apart from a few exception tend to be self destructive actively seeking Oblivion and even those few exceptions tend to wish to survive only to spread their pain to others, but if that is the case then it shouldn't be said to apply to all Undead beings only those infected with a Beast and still the cost being tied to their WP is odd to say the least.

                              Also there seems to be no explanation or reason for why it cost so ridiculous amount of Pathos and in addition to that a ridiculously large contribution of the other beings energy as well. At best the cost can be 15 Pathos, 3 Willpower, (5 Angst, but only if Undead, being Nephandi even in service of Oblivion has no such extra cost) + 5 Blood/Rage/Quintessence/Willpower which is ridiculously much considering the effect. Besides why it is Rage for the Garou and Fera? Considering were talking about a merger of souls such spiritual matters would likely be governed by Gnosis rather than Rage.

                              To me it just seems needlessly punitive to Crossover games and the Angst cost still seems a bit random as it isn't tied to actual Oblivion taint, which for example the Nephandi have a lot more than most Vampires ever will, but simply the Undead state. So in my personal opinion a more reasonable cost along with either more universal Angst cost, I'd see it being more relevant to living subjects in general rather than supernatural only since tying a living person to a death thing -you- is much more Angst filled moment than tying an Undead being to you, that if not a set number at least would be tied to how Oblivion tainted someone is.

                                      I can sense other wraiths normally already. Having to pay Pathos to tell if other wraiths were present is just weird, and no, it shouldn't be worth a Pathos. If a supernatural presences is threatening a character's Fetters, then I placed it there because I want the characters to go immediately check out that threat. That becomes a potential plot hook, as they go to investigate further. If it's something they have to pay for, then either as a GM never use it, which means they never feel the need to pay for it, or if I do use it, it builds player paranoia and it turns into Pathos tax - an obligation. Obligations aren't fun.
