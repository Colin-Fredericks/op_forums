      No more than any other fanatic, I imagine. Human politics don't always carry over into Underworld politics. But there are a few options.

      I would say most 'Al-Qaeda' leaders (there's at least some doubt about them being a single organisation as opposed to a loose network of related groups) would die in the Middle East, where things would be much more up for interpretation than they would in the West. They'd be full of horrid thoughts and would probably be Shadow-Eaten straight out the Caul.

      Any extremists who die in the West would be mere tools for more powerful conspiracists (usually themselves based in the Middle East), but in my opinion would probably die thinking they had fulfilled a higher purpose because of the nature of their indoctrination. As such, they're likely to Transcend (it's not always about being a good person) or fall straight to Oblivion (they have no loose ends to tie up, because they literally think they're serving God and are off to a better place).

      If they didn't skip the Underworld altogether, I'm sure the weight of their crimes would drag them down into the Labyrinth as spectres too. There could be some quite nasty spectre cults that emerge from this.

      As Wraith ended in 1999, it doesn't go into the issue at all, so this is conjecture. However, I think Orpheus might mention the Shadowlands of New York?

                      The thing you have to remember when you consider how to handle wars, terrorism, or really anything in Wraith, is that being dead changes everything.

                      You are not the same person you were when you died. And that's not a euphemism or a metaphor either; in Wraith, when you die all the complexity, identity, and self deception is scoured and subsumed until the core emotions keeping you here are laid bare to the world. Your Passions become quite literally your only reason for being and what you were in life becomes a lot less important than why you were, in life.

                      It would not be unheard of for the ghost of the man who erected a monument, the ghost of a foreign soldier who died protecting it (and the people around it), and the ghost of a terrorist who blew himself up trying to destroy it, to unite in protecting a common fetter, or if the motivations were right, in advancing a common agenda. Likewise it would not be unheard of for the ghosts of a soldier with feelings of betrayal and the terrorist who killed him to join forces as renegades against the Hierarchy.

                      Culture, belief, and ideology played (and still play) a huge role in making Wraiths who they are, but are far from the only influence. Nothing unites like a common struggle and Oblivion, as they say, pulls on everyone.
