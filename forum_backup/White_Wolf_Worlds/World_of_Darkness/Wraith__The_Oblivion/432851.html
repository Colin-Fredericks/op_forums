    Reasons for Great Maelstoms 1..2..3..

    03-08-2015, 02:44 PM
    I imagine if you know about Wraith the you will know about the Five Great Maelstroms which caused devastation to Stygia in particular.
    Looking at them not all would seem to cause a cataclysm on a world (or underworld) changing level.
    For instance I can see why the fall of Rome would cause the First Great Maelstrom in Europe, but not in the Kingdoms of Jade or Ivory to name a few.
    I also realise that written from a western-centric point of view in the beginning of WtO, the Great Maelstroms as they are also make perfect sense.

    What has gained my curiosity is, as GM's how would you portray these events from a non-stygian viewpoint?

    Do other levels of the underworld experience other disasters which could be heralded as Great Maelstroms, or are the first ones recorded the ones shown as one of the Five?

    I imagine that the fall of The Flayed Lands might seem all the more traumatic for the inhabitants of that Kingdom if the Heretics were shown to have the power to raise an evil such as the Maelstrom, a previously unknown phenomenon. Or do they simply view it as something which heralds the next cycle, a broom to sweep away the old to prepare for the cycle to come.

    Was the arrival of the Jade Emperor preceded or announced with a Great Maelstrom?

    I'd like to know what people think, the source material is so much fun I can't help my let my imagination run riot

      In general, the arrival of a Great Maelstrom is that it reflects the arrival of a huge increase in entropy into the world - civilizational collapse, mass death, societal despair, etc. Whether the Maelstrom is the final result of an ongoing build up of such things, or whether it is also a harbinger of things to come is debateable. In any event, it is a signal to all Wraiths that "Oblivion has increased its power greatly."

      It doesn't matter where that occurred. So not all Wraiths or Dark Kingdoms may know of the proximate cause; nevertheless they know something bad happened. It may not even be what people think it is.

      Going back to the First Great Maelstrom, there is actually a lot of problems with the official story (this is most likely due to the ignorance of real history by the writers). The official story is that Rome fell so the First Great Maelstrom was caused as a result. This actually does not make any sense.

      1) The Roman Empire did not actually fall because the Eastern Roman Empire still survived and would continue to exist for another thousand years. In fact, it was the more populated and rich half, and in the next hundred years would ever reclaim significant portions of the western empire.

      2) No great traumatic event occurred in the Skinlands in AD 476. All that happened is that an officer of the Roman army (Odoacer) deposed his commander (Orestes) and sent the titular emperor of the western empire (Romulus Augustulus) into a monastery and sent the imperial regalia back to Constantinople. Rather than appoint a new puppet empire himself, Odoacer simply asked the emperor in Constantinople to recognize his Roman military title and as Germanic king, which the emperor did.

      3) Rome was not even the capital of the western empire at the time; Ravenna was. Furthermore, it was not sacked in AD 476. When Rome was actually sacked in AD 455, there was no maelstrom.

      4) The actual damage to the empire had already been done by that point, and the west was actually in a stable situation again - albeit with non-Romans ruling most of the empire. The real devastation was to come later with Justinian's attempt to reconquer the west during the Gothic Wars, and with the collapse of the Mediterranean economy when the Muslims invaded a century later.

      So from the actual perspective of wraiths in AD 476, the First Great Maelstrom likely did not make any sense. There either needed to be some kind of secret history of why it happened, or somehow Fate knew that this was actually a turning point in human history that would not be apparent to most people at the time.

          While not a single event, something that relates to the down fall of the Roman Empire and a major changes in the metaphysical balance of power in the cWOD is the fall of the White Howlers (wiped out ~200 BCE) and the emergence of the Black Spiral Dancers are a full Tribe following the Wyrm (contradictory information in the books on how long it took for them to truly become fully dedicated to the Wyrm).

          Wraiths wouldn't know the specifics of that, but probably assigned a period of increasing metaphysical entropy that finally built into the First Great Maelstrom the sacking of Rome more as a symbolic marker more than belief that the sacking itself set it off fully on its own.

          There are also significant Shadowlands events that are linked to many of the Great Maelstroms. Such events could be linked to what pushes something into a Great Maelstrom or not (The Third was caused by the conquest of the Americas but also the destruction of the Flayed Lands on top of it).

          Most of the Great Maelstroms are also associated with periods of time, not single events. Even if a single event is seen as the final straw. It also makes sense that the periods seem to shrink. The decline of the Roman Empire took centuries, the plague took decades if you include the Asia parts of the pandemic, the conquest of Mesoamerica also took a bit of time, The two World Wars were only a few years, and the Week of Nightmares was... a week. It makes sense that the First Maelstrom took some time to build up as each seems to grow in power more and more quickly.

The later ones occur on a more global stage so they are actually Panglobal Maelstroms, but it seems odd to comb through world history to find things that happened at exactly the same time as the fall of Rome to explain a Maelstrom happening in China, North America, etc., particularly when the events just don't seem to add up to the kind of catastrophic change, either cumulative or instantaneous .

And those don't track with the Black Death, etc.

                          China definitely has flashpoints of it's own, but, as far as seminal moments of change in history, the Qin Empire seems like it would have generated a Maelstrom from the shear amount of Pathos and how MUCH was coming across the Shroud. Oh, and ended up producing the eternal (until suborned and replaced) Emperor of the Dark Kingdom of Jade. But it doesn't match up to any of the "Great Maelstroms" of Stygia.
