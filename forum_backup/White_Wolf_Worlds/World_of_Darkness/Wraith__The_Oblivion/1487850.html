    Need some ideas for nautical themed Tempest adventure

    07-25-2022, 04:56 AM
    Ahoy! I'm planning a nautical scenario that's going to be a Wraith reskin of Moby Dick + Rime of the Ancient Mariner and then segues into 20,000 Leagues Under the Tempest + Das Boot. Ahab/Nemo is going to be a Ferryman NPC who hunts part of his Psyche that got separated and lost in the Tempest. Players are going to be crew of the relic vessel- a WW2 era submarine probably. I have Sea of Shadows and there's a lot of useful material in there for the first part, but I'm struggling with a few specifics, especially for the second part. It's been decades since I last ran a Wraith game, and I think I'm a bit rusty.

    First of, Argos. I'm very sure at least one player is going to pick some dots in Argos, and I'm a bit concerned that it might trivialize some of the story elements. I need a good explanation to limit the reach of some abilities without shutting it down unfairly. Navigating the Tempest in proximity around the vessel is fine, I just don't want them to peace out to the next Necropolis.

    Next, recovery. I have no experience how well regaining Pathos/Corpus works while in a bottled environment like a submarine for a long time. Running out of 'food' is a classic for nautical adventures, but I don't want the players to starve either. Any advice on this is very welcome.

    For the second part, I had the idea that truly ancient lost cities sunk into the depths of the Tempest, and have the players explore them. If possible I would like to avoid places that players might know from other splats because they have relevance there, like Carthage or Enoch. I have some ideas for Göbekli Tepe and Harappa, but more ideas and what encounters they hold are welcome.

      Going by the powers listed in Wr20, I don't think you have much to worry about until the Argos 5 Initiate power. Most of the powers are effectively personal. Stormreader (Argos 4 Common) can reduce maelstrom strength but it only does that for travel. That's why the roll is a perception roll; the Harbinger is finding the safest path, not forcibly quieting the storm. Maitre apres Dieu is the only one that can really mess your story and that's a level 5 initiate power, so you have lots of reason to disallow it on a starting character. Conversely, that power would make a lot of sense for your NPC.

      As far as Pathos is concerned, make sure all the characters have Passions that they can engage in as a part of this crew. Unless you plan on having shore leave time, they aren't going to have the chance to gather it from the Quick. Play up the intercharacter conflict that comes from enclosed spaces and tense environments, it's not just story fodder, it is their chance to feed. Also make sure they have Dark Passions that are applicable. While they might not want their Shadows to feed, you do.

      I'd add multiple incarnations of Troy to your list. Troy II, Troy VI, and Troy VII would be good candidates as those appear to have potentially violent ends. I'd also suggest having some locations that are just buildings rather than the entire city.

          I did some of my own rules in an adaptation I did for Gurps (half complete). You can find those here:

          http://forums.sjgames.com/showthread...=180897&page=7

          For Argos, it just depends on the level they have on it actually. Just limit it and it's fine; adjust where necessary. If not enough, just place some special rules for it to where they are, but be sure to give something in it's place instead. So, if simply teleporting to the nearest necropolis is too much, let them do something like blinking in combat - adding their argos rating to Dexterity etc, something like that.

          For recovery, being away and isolated is quite enough to make it harder already, while still not impossible.

          Interesting cities:

          - Tenochtitlán - the former capital of the Aztecs (nowadays, Mexico City). In my opinion, this one would be even cooler than Atlantida - and possibly a massive spectral nightmarish town
          - Machu Pichu (Incan Empire)
          - Tikal (largest mayan city)

          So, those 3 were the main pre columbian american cities, respectfully for the aztecs, incans and mayans. Those are great places for said purpose, with pagan gods, high culture and a prevalence of human sacrifices. Possibly specter infested places of nightmares.

          Other than those:

          - Atlantis (classic)
          - Pompey - great place for the underworld, destroyed by a Vulcano
          - Troy - another mythic (and real!) place. Maybe you'll even find Achilles in it!
          - El Dourado - this one, like Atlantis, is fully mythical - but in the Tempest, all goes. You may find a bunch of spaniards conquistadors cutthroats and mercenaries in an obscessive quest to try to enter it.
          - Sparta - barely nothing is left of it irl. If you want realism thou, be warned that the spartans were a bunch of cowards and traitors, and their society were entirely based on slavery, and that spartans were only ever trained to SUPRESS THEIR SLAVES who far outnumbered them, not to fight in wars - which is why they were such cowards. But you can go along with the false narrative of the movies if you'd like.
          - Memphis - the most important capital of ancient Egypt. Again, awesome place. Maybe you'll find some mummies, or perhaps even Anubis!
          - Babylon - love ziggurats? Well, look no further than the old capital of the Babylonian Empire!
          - Tyre, Sidon, Byblos - the main Phoenician cities
          - Pasargadae, the first capital of the ancient Persian Empire.
          - Dvaraka, modern Dwarka: one of the 7 sacred cities of hinduims, and home of Krishna himself. The ancient city was destroyed, modern Dwarka was built in it's place (much like Troy was destroyed and rebuilt several times). Maybe you can find Krishna himself!
          - Camelot (another mythical)
          - Alexandria - this one would be in my top 3 choices, and the reason is almost self evident.
          - Shangri-la - another mythical one

          I would add the Terracota Army and the emperor's tomb, but those are already part of the Dark Kingdom of Jade so, that's out.

          Anyway, that's an starting pick from the top of my head, but do a small search and you'll find countless more

                                                  For examples of cities sunk into the depths of the Tempest, I would only select cities that are truly and utterly destroyed. Not just cities that have ruins. Tenochtitlan still exists, it is just called Mexico City. Alexandria still exists. Rome still exists.

                                                  Individual buildings that have been destroyed can still show up, but they usually appear in the Shadowlands first. And if important are sometimes disassembled and shipped off to Stygia to be reconstructed.

                                                  So I'd be looking for cities that were destroyed - suddenly and totally would be best - and are now completely uninhabited. You want something truly catastrophic for it to slip into the Tempest instead of appearing in the Shadowlands that could be pillaged by wraiths. Cities that gradually went into decline and slowly abandoned probably aren't the best choices. Their sudden destruction would provide drama that creates a hook or story for the location. For our purposes, I think even possibly or likely fictional cities are OK provided there are real legends attesting to their existence. Here are some examples that come to my mind.
                                                  • Sodom & Gomorrah (destroyed by God in the Old Testament)
                                                  • Irem/Ubar (mentioned in the Koran, supposedly destroyed by a sandstorm)
                                                  • Akrotiri (destroyed by the Thera volcanic explosion in the Bronze Age)
                                                  • Troy (as already mentioned by Ramnensis, but I'd go full Iliad/Trojan War here inspired more by legend than historical reality simply because that is cooler)
                                                  • Vilcabamba (the lost capital city of the Incas sacked by the Spaniards)
                                                  • Vineta (the probably fictitious "Atlantis of the Baltic" lost in the 12th century)
                                                  • Pompeii (already mentioned)
                                                  • Tanis (the Egyptian city where Indian Jones finds the lost Ark of the Covenant in Raiders of the Lost Ark. Its history there is fictional, not historical, but I'd use that because it is cooler.)
                                                  • Karakorum (the Mongol capital razed by Ming troops in 1388)
                                                  • Camelot (the castle/court/town of King Arthur)
                                                  • Port Royal (Jamaican pirate town destroyed by earthquake in 1692)
                                                  • Roanoke Colony (British colony lost to mystery, but in the World of Darkness the site of the destruction of the Croatan tribe of Garou against the Eater-of-Souls)
                                                  • Lyonesse (legendary island/city sunk overnight, from Arthurian legend)

                                                          Port Royal is actually mentioned in Blood-Dimmed Tides (thanks again Asmodai ) as a Necropolis of note and gets a description. The book has a long section for WtO focused on Ghost Ships and piracy.

                                                          Other useful tidbits I got out of it:
                                                          - Water is solid for Wraiths by default. Who knew? Not me.
                                                          - Some dedicated Merits/Flaws and alternate Arts
                                                          - Interestingly, Monitors are used as navigators (triangulating with fetters). I still prefer Oracles for the role, fate-powered navigation is just too cool to dismiss
                                                          - The shroud/tempest wall is paper-thin in deep Skinland oceans. So if I need a trip to the Skinlands, that's actually less of a problem than I thought.

                                                          As for cities, I agree that (violently) destroyed ones are better for use. I don't want to exhaust the experience, so I will probably narrow the selection to three or four and pepper them with single buildings or small sites in between. It gets a bit philosophical which city counts as destroyed rather than abandoned and decayed, since many where rebuilt and lived on under different names. For example, would Byzantium count, or did it just become Istanbul? Sites like Pompeii and Herculaneum are definitively easier to categorize in this regard.

                                                          I also remembered Doggerland, a previously inhabited land-bridge between the British Isles and the continent that sunk beneath the waves in just 300 years or so. I may keep that one for a WtA game though.

                                                          I'm currently building the Dolomedes, the Type VII-C U-Boat that will be the relic vessel. It's a really fun part of the scenario prep.
