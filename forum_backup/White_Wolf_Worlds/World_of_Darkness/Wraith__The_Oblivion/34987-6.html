                                01-21-2014, 09:18 PM
                                Random answer time, as the snow comes down in Carolina...

                                [Bear in mind - all answers given relate to as things are /now/. There may be some changes coming. You have been warned.]

                                As things stand now, it's one Corpus, one item. Industrialized wraith-farming is a little more Robin Cook than I was willing to go. This, incidentally, is why

                                The official symbol of Stygia - The Key and the Mask are the two symbols that commonly stood in for Stygia. Groups on the outside looking in might have had...less flattering suggestions.

                                A wraith cannot turn off LIfesight - at least, nobody's ever admitted to being able to do so. *cough cough*

                                Ghostfacers are awesome. And some of the best ghost stories of all time have had humorous elements - I never want Wraith to be exclusively grim (or Grim). If you go back and look through the books, you'll see all sorts of sneaky jokes; horror and humor go hand in hand. So, to make a long answer even longer, yes, there will be some light-hearted elements. And I love the picture from Book of Legions of the guy underneath the soda machine.

                                Just as there is no crying in baseball, there is no sledding in the Shadowlands, unless the snow is native to the core identity of the place. You can get snow from Maelstroms, but I wouldn't recommend sledding in it.

                                Dark Kingdom of Jade was actually the second book I developed for WW, and I have great fondness for the Dark Kingdoms material. Kowloon was never specifically addressed, IIRC, but as far as I am concerned it's a lone, defiant outpost and a constant thorn in the Empire's side. Or it was, at least, before Xerxes made his left turn at Albuquerque.

                                No umbral reflections. When you go to the Shadowlands, you go all the way. I've got no patience for souls that won't commit :-)

                                Favorite ghost story - that's tough one, but I think the list starts with "O Whistle And I'll Come To You, My Lad" by M.R. James. My bookshelves are full of James, Oliver Onions, Robert Aickman, T.M. Wright and more. And yes, my first non-WW novel was in fact a ghost story, called Firefly Rain :-)

                                Avoiding Zombiism - With anything, you need to draw a line about what fits and what doesn't, and then you have to be ruthless in excising the material that's across that line. Enforcing it can be hard, but the alternative is what we in the video game biz call "feature creep", and that's a Cthulhoid horror that you really don't want to deal with.

                                Broad-spectrum answer: I intend to get as much into the book as possible, and I have no idea as to any of the logistics vis-a-vis KS or whatever. Right now, my main antagonist is the book outline, and until I get that done I have blinders on to anything else.

                                I don't know what was intended for Monitors before I got there, but I always regretted not having the chance to make people realize exactly how scary they could be. Not necessarily /bad/, but scary. That being said, imagine a few of those folks going into business with the late Mr. Artemus Vanderwal....

                                Speak not the Gaga's name, lest ye summon it.

                                And as for Wraith's ending...well, I meant what I wrote in the afterword to Ends of Empire, and I think those words still say it best. I wish we'd had more time to tell the story, but I was very lucky that I got as much time as I did, and I appreciate you all coming along for the ride. And, I got the chance to close the door and turn out the lights in a way that very few creatives in any medium ever get to do. If Wraith had stayed dead, I would have been creatively satisfied with the sendoff that we - Bruce, Geoff, Ed and the rest of the crew - gave it. It is and always will be one of the creative achievements I am proudest of.

                                That being said, right now it just means I've got a helluva starting point.

                                ​Deadguy out.

                                                            You have questions. I have about five minutes at the keyboard before I need to go deal with the terrible thing happening on the stove in my kitchen.

                                                            Erik - That was all me, and thank you for the kind words. The original Erik story in WPG was inspired by an executive outplacement firm I was working for at the time, which had a lovely office just off Newbury Street in Boston. One of the key office features was a giant map with huge stickpins marking where our various offices were, and that's what inspired the original story.

                                                            Maps - One of my main stated goals for W20 is to make it more accessible. So, yes, I fully intend for there to be a map. Hopefully, someone with better small motor skills than mine will draw it.

                                                            Wraith Sexytime - Think Passions, not....err, never mind. Going to leave that figure of speech right where I found it and back away slowly.

                                                            General note - Not every dead person becomes a wraith. Not every item that's destroyed becomes a relic. Extrapolate as you will to all applicable instances :-)

                                                            And that's the smell of terrible things happening to some unfortunate root vegetables and pork belly what never did no harm to nobody, so Deadguy Out.
