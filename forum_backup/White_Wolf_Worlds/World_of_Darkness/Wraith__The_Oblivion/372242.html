    Dead and Deader - Vampire and Wraith Interaction

    01-16-2015, 09:21 AM
    OK, so first a confession:

    I've never played Wraith. I've never read a Wraith book.

    The sum total of what I know about Wraith comes from the Kickstarter and from the White Wolf Wiki. That's it.

    However, I was won over enough by the above sources and some awesome people on the forums to the point where I backed Wr20 enough to be getting a physical copy of it in the future.

    So with that out of the way lets move on to the next point:

    The one game from the CWoD I know well is Vampire. Specifically Revised Edition and Dark Ages Vampire. Played in a fair few games over the years. Including the longest running campaign I've ever been in that lasted 3 years.

    I've been brain-storming the beginnings of a worldbuilding process that should end up with a WoD that integrates Vampire, Mage and Wraith within it bounds (all the rest are not being used, save KotE if it gets an Anniversary edition and I'll use the Masquerade version of Werewolves AKA Lupines and Demons AKA Infernal Patrons). To be clear in regards to this statement. This is not a setting where I will ever run a mixed group (well not for anything but oneshots maybe), if people are playing Vampires and dealing with Vampire issues any Wraiths run across or involved will be NPC's. Each group of supernaturals is going to be pretty much doing their own thing. However, there will be points of convergence where the actions of one group may effect the actions of another group.

    Ever since I backed M20 in March last year I've been thinking on and off about how Vampire and Mage might relate to one another. So things like how many shady deals the Technocracy and Camarilla might have going on, how the Order of Hermes and the Tremere might view each other and if the Chakravanti might still be hunting the Nagaraja etc.

    Obviously Wraith is less well known to me and I've had less time to think about points of convergence/interaction between the two groups. However, I've come up with a few ideas and I was wondering if I could get some feedback and additional ideas/hooks/possibilities from the Wraith fanbase here on the forums.

    I've decided to divide the thoughts I've had on this topic into two categories: General and Specific.

    As I don't want this post to resemble an essay I've decided to get feedback on the general points first.

    General:

    1) So a quote in the Revised Masquerade book (and possibly V20) in regards to Wraiths is something like:

    "You'll make a few of these over the year, trust me."

    That got me thinking.

    If a Vampire lives long enough they are going to fuck-up and kill someone at some point. That's just the lore of averages coming into effect on a being that is predatory by nature. This means there is a chance that they will at some point create a Wraith. Obviously Vampires kill humans, sometimes out of necessity, sometimes in keeping the Masquerade, sometimes because the human is caught up in the Jyhad.

    The one death I want to focus on here is when a Vampire kills a human by draining them dry during a feeding session. I think this warrents a special look for a couple of reasons. Firstly, the act of feeding and the kiss is a bit of a supernatural link between the Vampire and his/her victim to start with. Which I think thematically gives a descent starting point for the Vampire being a fetter. Secondly, the act of feeding is kinda a violation and I feel is the sort of thing that's more likely to produce a Wraith then just a plain out necessity killing of some Vampires ghoul lackey.

    One question I have here is this:

    I can people killed by vampire in connection to necessity, Masquerade or Jyhad as falling under Violence for the purpose of Legion assignment (Smiling Lord right?). However, not every feeding is violent. So I am wondering what Legion those who died from Vampiric exsanguination would fall under? (Fate maybe?)

    2) Moving on from that focused point. Vampire has a merit and a flaw that lets any Vampire have a bit more interaction with a Wraith.

    The Merit is "Spirit Mentor" (3pts), which is basically a Wraith that is well disposed towards the Vampire in question and helps them out. Mainly by offering advice and guidance, but also just being a person the Vampire can talk to who's not involved with the Jyhad.

    The Flaw is Haunted (3pts), which is having an angry Wraith follow you around and makes life difficult for you in some way. By default its a former victim of the Vampire who screws up feeding attempts most of the time. However, it's customized by the Storyteller, so it could undermine your plots, position in the city or disrupt your household. Its implied it can't directly harm you, but it can work towards your destruction.

    Personally I really like these particular merits and flaws. One of my previous characters picked up a Spirit Mentor in play due to some of his actions and the actions of his Sire. It added an interesting dynamic to the game.

    So here is the question:

    Do you guys think its viable to have a Vampire character take both the Merit and the Flaw and have one represent the Wraith and another the Wraiths Shadow? With the idea that the Wraith in question has trouble keeping his Shadow under control now and then.

    I kinda like the idea of maybe a oneshot or few session game where one PC is the Vampire and one PC is the Wraith and they kinda have a misadventure/plot together. Nice little personal story to be had in that I think. Could be played up for either fun (48 Hours), dark comedy (Withnail and I), slice of life (Being Human) or horror/thriller (Six Sense) depending on the ST and Players mood.

    Unfortunately, I can't really talk on the matter of Wraith merits and flaws. I don't know if there is anything similar that might help plant story seeds for interaction between Wraiths and Vampires.

    3) I was thinking about the idea of a Vampire as a Wraiths fetter in the Skinlands. If that's possible at all (I'm not sure if non-objects can be fetters).

    So in some ways I guess it sucks. You rely on the Vampire that killed you to stay close to the Skinlands and not get sucked down into the Tempest/Sunless Sea. That's just one more injustice that the universe seems to have thrown at you. Its very likely your not going to like the Vampire in question. Yet it is kinda in your interest to keep him alive and around. This does not mean you can't cause trouble for him of course, so vengeance can be had in small amounts.

    However, the fact your tied to an undead being might be pretty good for you in the long run right? You've got a Fetter that's pretty much immune to mundane aging. So you don't have to worry about him "breaking" like a normal object or dying like a mortal anchor. Vampires are also a fair bit tougher then Humans, so the likelihood of death by accident or mundane violence is a little bit slimmer too. So you could have a way back to being close to the skinlands for centuries or millenia if your lucky.

    I've got no real question here, however, I am very interested in peoples thoughts in regards to this point. Do you think its possible a Wraith starts of resenting their Vampire killer but over time basically grows to kinda accept the fact that their killer is the only thing keeping them close to the world they knew? Maybe even accept that after all the loved ones and friends are dead, their killer is going to be around and forgiveness could actually occur?

    I'll be back later with the more specific points of Vampire that cross-over I feel. For now I must rest and think.

    "There is no God in the Badlands." - Sunny

              Ok before launching into my specific points I'd like to thank Dam and Ajex for replying to the more general points that I posted up a few hours ago. Due to the size of this post I’ve divided it in half (making it three posts total now rather than two).

              Specific:

              Necromancy:-

              I’ve divided them up by Path’s that actually interact with Wraiths as I’ve not got the energy to list every single ritual. However, ask a pre-emptive question:

              Do Wraiths have a particular stereotype about Necromancers within Stygian culture? So are they culturally loaded to be wary of them, scared of them or more open to deal making with them?

              Most of the below is just my observations and thoughts about how different Necromancy Paths might effect a relationship between a Wraith and Vampire. At some points I post questions, but really I am more looking for other people’s take/ideas/thoughts on how these powers might change a Vampire/Wraith dynamic.

              The Path of Bone –

              So most of this path is not really directly going to relate to Wraiths except the Level 4 and 5 powers.

              Level 4 is a pretty horrific way of getting to interact with your loved ones again as a Wraith, since they temporarily pop over to your side of the Shroud while under the effect of this power. It’s pretty drastic, but its an option for desperate or amoral Wraiths who have a Necromancer ally.

              Level 5 is of course a massive bargaining chip for a Necromancer. As its basically (as written) gives the Wraith a second shot at life in a mortal body. Sure it’s not the Wraiths original body, but he get to live a life again. Though who knows what his Shadow might get up to in the world of flesh?

              The Path of the Cenotaph –

              Lots of good stuff here, mainly for the Necromancer to get one up on a Wraith, but more altruistic uses can be seen.

              Level 2 lets you see fetters which is pretty useful for any Necromancer who is trying to bargain with a Wraith.

              Level 3 helps find weakpoints in the Shroud. Handy for working out the best places for a Wraith to attempt to influence the Skinlands.

              Level 4 lets you find new Wraiths as people die. Surely a boon to anyone doing an Reaping?

              Level 5 allows the Necromancer to make “fake” fetters for the Wraith. While they are only temporary, the ability to keep having fetters in the Skinlands and therefore stay near the living is a pretty hefty favour to grant.

              The Path of Haunting –

              This whole Path is great for leveraging Wraiths at two levels.

              Firstly, it’s very useful as a way of tormenting people the Wraith might want vengeance on in general. This is particularly true of the Level 5 power, which could be fluffed as the Wraith ally gaining easier access to the victim rather than a random group of Specters.

              Secondly, it’s a great tool to threaten the Wraiths loved ones with. As Level 4 is basically putting a mortal through a harrowing (something now Wraith would wish on person they cared about). Level 5 draws Specter attention to said loved ones. Another thing most Wraiths could not conscience having done to someone they cared about.

              The Path of Sepulchre –

              Level 2’s uses are obvious. It lets you summon a Wraith, even one who is sucked down into the Sunless Sea. That’s pretty useful for making sure a Wraith never escapes you and as a “rescue operation” to drag an allied Wraith back if they get banished there.

              Level 4 is a bit like the Level 5 Cenotaph Path. Except it grants less freedom to the Wraith, goes give it a Fetter to stay out of the deeper Underworld though. So it might actually be done more benign reasons then it might first appear.

              Level’s 3 and 5 are both gross interaction powers. Basically Dominate on Wraiths and an ability perfect anti-Wraith physical attack. I’d put both in the “purely antagonistic” category. That being said, its handy to have a Necromancer ally who can pull this off the Wraith’s ghostly enemies. Gives a bit more of an immediate tool for the Necromancers use in a more Wraith focused game. Though the question is if a Wraith would ever actually trust a Necromancer that has these powers?

              The Path of Ash –

              Probably the best set of Necromantic powers for a straight Vampire/Wraith crossover game. As it lets you interact with not only Wraiths but the Underworld more directly.

              Level 2 is talk with Wraiths, which I presume is something any materialized (as in on our side of the shroud) Wraith can do? So the power is useful for allowing cross-shroud communication.

              Level 3 allows you to hold onto ghostly objects, meaning you can keep hold of relics and such for your Wraith ally. Alternatively, it might allow you to steal said relics and then use them as a bargaining chip with more adversarial Wraiths.

              Level 4 is the power you want to have adventures with your Wraith ally in the Underworld. Though you going to need to bring a load of blood with you. The number of ways this helps about a Vampire/Wraith game are huge, so I’m not going to try and list them here.

              Level 5 makes it much easier or harder for a Wraith to interact with the living world. It’s easy to see the bargaining value of this ability. Not only for those Wraiths that want to interact with former loved ones (and will do what the Necromancer wants if they ever want to be able to do so again, in a more cut-throat relationship between the two parties), but for Stygian official’s who want to prevent interaction between Wraiths and the living.

              The Vitreous Path –

              Level 3 is where this one gets interesting. Being able to absorb the energy of entropy itself (Oblivion taint anyone) or eat ghosts like they were mortals. Obviously, the latter ability allows for the Necromancer to survive in the Underworld without having to carry around a load of blood, which is useful for crossover games.

              Still considering soulmarks and all that, I imagine someone who is using this ability likely looks pretty dodgy to Wraiths.

              Level 4 makes mortals, Vampires and Wraiths look like they are tainted by Oblivion, which is pretty bad for their social standing. Plus a fantastic tool for setting up rivals for a fall. Do you think it’s possible that this power might even temporarily strengthen a Wraith’s Shadow?

              The other ability here is to summon a small group of Specters to cause havoc for possibly miles around. However, it’s not all bad as the Necromancer does not control them directly (unless he/she has Compel Soul) and they can’t sense the Necromancer to trouble him/her (save for a botch).

              I imagine this is a risky prospect with a Wraith around (and for the Wraith who is around the Necromancer) but I could see it being used as a distraction or weapon of last resort.

                  Ok so for the third part of this brainstorming process. As before, I’ve got a few questions below. Most of its ideas/thoughts/hooks I could think of with my limited knowledge of Wraith. Once again, anyone with a better grasp of that game should feel free to chip in with ideas here.

                  I’m focusing on the various Clans and Bloodlines that deal with Wraiths. I have no idea if any of them receive any coverage in the Wraith books. If there is a cannon Wraith perspective on these groups of Vampires, I would be interested in them.

                  Specific:

                  1) The Giovanni:

                  This is the big one as they say. I'd like to state for the record I'm not really a fan of the Giovanni. They are a little dry in their fluff for me. Plus I have never really loved the taboo necromantic mafia thing. However, I do know their fluff reasonable well.

                  Seems to me they are pretty much jerks to Wraiths. They treat them like a resource rather then as individual people. They want to catch 1 Billion Wraiths and use them to enact some massive Necromantic ritual to become Death-Gods. Which may or may not involve sundering the Shroud as well.

                  So...with what little knowledge I have of Wraith, am I the only one that thinks their being taken for a ride by some powerful force in the Underworld?

                  As a follow up question: Does Wraith detail how Oblivion corrupts other supernaturals? I know Mages get a build up of death resonance called Jhor, which sounds like Oblivion taint to me (does Wraith talk about that at all?). I've never seen anything in any Vampire book's that deal with Necromancy talk about Vampires getting corrupted by Oblivion though. So I was wondering if Wraith addresses this issue.

                  2) The Cappadocians:

                  So the Cappadocians were a lot less involved with Wraiths according to the fluff. Since they focused on the more material aspects of death and entropy. Though the new V20DA makes that a little less clear and implies the Cappadocians did have some dealings with Wraiths before the Giovanni showed up.

                  However, the Harbingers of Skulls (the survivors of the Giovanni purge) did hang out in the Underworld for around 600 years. So I imagine they must have kicked at least one Dark Kingdom during that time (likely Stygia). During that time they likely had to cut some deals with at least one faction of Wraiths to survive.

                  I was wondering if anyone had any idea what group within Stygian society would be most likely to ally with or "look after" a group of powerful Necromancers who were physically in the Underworld (rather then being the Wraith of a Mage for example) and were all low generation elder Kindred?

                  3) The Samedi:

                  These guys are based in the Caribbean. They practice Voudoun Necromancy and seem to be the favoured Kindred (unlike the Serpents of Light who he kinda trolls and who also practice Voudoun Necromancy) of the Loa Baron Samedi (which is also the name of the Bloodline founder). Necromancy is not as wide spread among them as it was for the Cappadocians nor the Giovanni in the modern day. However, they are probably the most notable "new world" necromancers in Masquerade.

                  So even a relative Wraith layman like myself can see a link between the Samedi and Les Invisibles. Especially considering the latter's close ties with mortals. This might cause conflict, as Wraiths of Les Invisibles might not appreciate Samedi feeding on their still living relatives. However, the Les Mysteres beings that Les Invisibles serve could easily be the same Loa entities the Samedi pay homage too. So the two might groups of supernatural might be working towards the same ends.

                  I can see a lot of potential for cross-over with these two groups just on face value. However, I am curious to know more about Les Invisibles and their masters before I am completely sold on the idea. That being said, I have no real question on this one, save that I am interested in hearing other peoples take on possible relationships between the two groups.

                  4) The True Black Hand/The Nagaraja:

                  So Enoch is a city in the Sunless Sea right? A sort of mini-Dark Kingdom all of its own. Lorded over by the True Black Hand, which calls Vampires, Mages and odder things its members. I vaguely remember it being stated some old and powerful Wraiths also worked for/with the True Black Hand as well.

                  As such I have the following question:

                  How well known about is Enoch in the various Dark Kingdoms?

                  Are the True Black Hand as stealthy and removed from events as they are in the material world? Always guiding but never showing their presence? What sort of thing in Stygian/Underworld politics might they want to influence? Considering they try to bring order to the world, but also drive it towards its end.

                  Also, considering the metaphysics of the Underworld and Wraith, it seems sorta likely that the Aralu the Black Hand worship/serve are Neverborn (or powerful Onceborn maybe). Anyone thought on that one?

                  The Nagaraja have a great ability to control Wraiths, but also alone among Kindred Necromancers can use Oblivion as a power-source. Controlling Specters, causing entropic breakdown of substances and even being able to feed off Wraiths as if they had blood points.

                  Considering all this, are they sorta seen as "boogeymen" among the Wraiths that know about them (which I imagine is a small number)? I mean if any group of Kindred is corrupted by Oblivion its the Nagaraja.

                  Do you thinks its plausible, given the Nagaraja's attention towards the study of Oblivion and the True Black Hand being a Gehenna cult that they might work with as well as against Specter forces in the Tempest? Perhaps pitting different Onceborn against one another but at the same time helping them take down other factions that indirectly oppose the True Black Hand in the Underworld? Maybe the True Black Hand even has Specter Cults in it, would that be viable?

                  5) The Rest:

                  So off the top of my head there are three others groups of Kindred that involve themselves with Wraiths:

                  - The Serpents of Light
                  Pretty much fall into the same sorta relationship as the Samedi, except much less likely to be on friendly terms I feel as the Loa don't seem to like them much. So likely a more tense/antagonistic relationship at least.

                  - The Setites
                  Setite Sorcerers have the option of learning a few powers that interact with Ghosts. Most of these are focused on the Egyptian dead though. So I imagine that's involved with the Dark Kingdom of Sand in the Far Shores and likely beyond the purview of most Wraiths. Still, could bring about some on/off again conflict with the Ferrymen due to their relationship with Anubis.

                  - The Impundulu
                  The new Laibon bloodline from V20DA. These guys like to hunt down comparative magics of all types and in particular other Necromantic magics. So I don't think its too much of a stretch that they are likely interested in the study of Wraithly abilities as well. They are already pretty well primed for Crossover with their bloodline weakness and fluff tying them to a Life Sorcerer/Mage. I can imagine a relationship between them and the Dark Kingdom of Ivory as similar in its variability to the one between the Samedi and Les Invisibles. Some Impundulu might be on good terms thanks to proper ancestor worship and respect for the Old Gods. Other's might be on bad terms due to misuses of their power.

                  In regard to these three factions I've not really got any questions. Once again, I've just stated by suppositions in regards to them. I am interested in the ideas and thoughts of people who know Wraith better in regards to these groups however. Obviously I know this requires a bit of knowledge about Masquerade as well, but I'm interested in the Wraith point of view primarily.

                  Anyway, thanks for trudging through this essay to provide me with some answers.

                              Right, this is the surprise extra round stuff I was thinking of while writing up all the other points from the other posts. Hence its late addition, honestly though, it’s the last bit of my brainstorming process.

                              The Metaplot Stuff –

                              So I’ve been looking through the Noddist/Clan Lore for a lot of the Clans. It seems there is a fair bit of potential for different Antideluvians to have had at least some interaction with the Underworld and Wraiths. I’ve listed the ones I feel might have some relevance to both Wraith and Vampire.

                              Obviously this is not the sort of thing your average PC is going to find out or interact with, but I think it’s important for setting background and world building.

                              Antediluvians:

                              Cappadocius -

                              According to the Gehenna book Cappadocius pulled a fast one on Giovanni and ended up in the Underworld looking for a way to God (to eat him…Ashur was a little crazy). He’s either a Wraith or a sorta Astral/Soul Projection with access to all his powers (because he’s an AntiD and they kinda break the rules for most Disciplines anyway). He’s at least 9000 years old when he entered the Underworld not to mention the most powerful Necromancer on the planet.

                              If I remember correctly he sources mention he was hanging around in the Labyrinth of all places for most of his nearly 600 year stay in the Underworld. So it’s not likely he had that much interaction with most of Wraith society (because only a few of them are crazy enough to go down there). Frighteningly I guess it’s more likely he’s been “picking the brains” of the Specters down there and maybe trying to glean some method of eating God from the hive-mind of Oblivion.

                              Still a being of this magnitude of power is likely to have some impact right? I mean someone had to notice. Sure likely a lot of them went “Let’s stay the hell away from that thing.” but it’s still an interesting point. You can’t really just ignore something that powerful, because it has the potential to screw up all our carefully laid plans.

                              Augustus Giovanni –

                              Cappadocius is opposite number and would-be replacement among the Third Generation. He’s the youngest and weakest of the Antediluvians. However, he’s a frighteningly powerful Necromancer and still a massively powerful Vampire.

                              Some of the fluff indicates that he knows Cappadocius’s soul got away from him and that despite his best efforts he can’t locate an item suitable enough to try and Summon his old Sire’s soul to him. As such he has made some efforts (usually through Wraith proxies/slaves to Clan Giovanni and his own childer) to find Cappadocius in the Underworld. He’s not been successful as of yet.

                              The tie in between Augustus looking for Cappadocius and Cappadocius kinda-sorta-not really hiding from him is an obvious plot link.

                              Other stuff that can be said for Augustus is that he is quite active for an AntiD and does enjoy a good bit of politic when he can. However, the fact that he’s the mastermind behind the “capture a billion ghosts” plan likely means most of his interactions are pretty hostile towards the Dark Kingdoms.

                              Do you people think it’s possible for Augustus and his Clan to have a less antagonistic relationship with a Dark Kingdom? Otherwise he comes off as a bit of a Necromancer boogyman wanting to kidnap every Wraith that comes near his Clan.

                              Set –

                              So the Gehenna book states he’s physically kicking around the Underworld (though it never explains exactly how he got there, or how he survived the Sixth Great Maelstrom when Cappadocius does not) and waging a conflict with “The Western Lands” there. One could also ask the question of what he’s feeding off down there, but once again he’s an AntiD, so let’s not dwell on that too much (it’s implied his Lector Priests keep him fed on Setite faith anyway).

                              This sounds to me like he’s trying to either take over a Far Shore realm, or he’s at war with the Dark Kingdom of Sand. Considering they are allies with the Ferrymen, this likely means he’s in conflict with the Boatmen too.

                              Now, the Ferrymen are all mysterious obviously and the Dark Kingdom of Sand is one of the best hidden places in the Underworld, so it’s unlikely this particular conflict has drawn much attention. Surely though this will affect how Wraiths might actually get to the Dark Kingdom of Sand though? Or at least be used to explain why the Ferrymen are so busy most of the time.

                              You could also bounce off the question of where Set is getting the forces to attack said Far Shore or Dark Kingdom? I mean he’s a frightening powerful Vampire for sure, but he’s up against an entire Pantheon of Gods, their Wraithly followers and the Ferrymen. Seems he’d likely need some help right? Considering how some people link him to the Wyrm, its possible he’s able to draw help from Specters and other creatures of Oblivion.

                              Arikel –

                              One story (though not the cannon one according to the Gehenna book) holds that Set killed Arikel during the time of the Second City. Considering he ate her heart in this tale. It’s possible such a gruesome death might have left her as a Wraith.

                              Let’s add to this that another name she goes by is Ishtar, famous for travelling to the Underworld and trying to get a loved one back to the land of the living.

                              If she did become a Wraith she’s likely one of the oldest ones in the Underworld (presuming that she’s not been destroyed by Oblivion. So she could be a mover and shaker in any one of the Dark Kingdoms (though Stygia is most likely…not including Enoch of course, if we consider her Wraith/Specter as a possible member of the Aralu) under another name. Alternatively, if her Shadow won out, she would be a good candidate for one of the Onceborn maybe?

                              Ventru –

                              Unlike Arikel, the cannon version of Ventru’s story has him die (by Troiles hand) outside the gates of the Second City shortly before the city fell. Some evidence exists that he planned his own death and might have even be suicidal. So like the non-cannon Arikel, there is a descent candidate for him to become a Wraith.

                              Like non-cannon Arikel, this would make him one of the oldest and most powerful Wraiths in the Underworld (presuming he survived once again). It’s hard to imagine him not getting involved with Wraith society on some level. The jury is out on if he ever really enjoyed leadership roles and politics, but he was certainly very good at those things.

                              From what I roughly know he makes a pretty good candidate possibly for the Quiet Lord (who is pretty mysterious in cannon even before we try and crossover fluff gymnastics) of Stygia I think. Not sure how else he could work. Possibly as one of the First among the Ferrymen, that would be kinda awesome as well.

                              Other Entities:

                              Abel –

                              He’s the first human to ever die and the first murder victim. It seems pretty thematically appropriate that he becomes a Wraith to me. Hell, he’d technically be the first Wraith.
                              One Gehenna scenario even has the PC’s call up Abel as a Ghost to confront Caine. So it seems to fit that he’s still around and not Shadow-Eaten by the modern nights.

                              One thought that springs to mind and keeps him in the same line as Ventru above is that as the first person to die from violence he’d be the default choice for the first (not the later backstabbing one) Smiling Lord position (if he got involved with the Stygian experiment under Charon).

                              Alternatively, a nice little twist could be that he did become Shadow-Eaten and his Shadow started off the whole manifestation of Oblivion in the Underworld. Maybe he’s now a stupidly powerful Onceborn or even the entity sitting at the heart of the Void.

                              There is always the Ferryman option as well. Alternatively he might be a Shining One on one of the Far Shores. I feel he’s unlikely to transcend until his business/issues with Caine are resolved.

                              Eve –

                              So she’s the Lady of Fate right?

                              Seems she has enough metaplot ties as it is. Don’t really need to come up with more for her really.

                              Though I imagine if Abel is a presence in the Underworld, she has some sort of relationship with him. This relationship could take any number of forms depending on the state/status of Abel obviously. I feel it’s an interesting idea she’d have him to keep an eye on Charon/The Deathlords if we go with first theory.

                              Also am I the only one that wonders what would happen if the Lady of Fate and Lilith ran into each other again?                               The Neverborn –

                              So the Neverborn in Wraith are entities that predate our realities existence. Existing in this reality is torment to them and they hate every aspect of our reality. They want everything to cease so that they can go back to non-existence (as we understand it) which seems to be their natural habitat. If they ever woke up they would pretty much start demolishing our reality in short order. Luckily they spend most of their time asleep because the Underworld, while less painful to them then the living one, kinda dopes them up a fair bit.

                              So the Children from Clanbook Baali are entities that predate our realities existence. Existing in this reality is torment to them and they hate every aspect of our reality. They want everything to cease so that they can go back to non-existence (as we understand it) which seems to be their natural habitat. If they ever woke up they would pretty much start demolishing our reality in short order. Luckily the Baali use atrocities and the “breaking” of their True names to keep them asleep in the hidden and deep “places” of the living material world.

                              Hang on a second…

                              If we tie this in with the story at the start of the Baali Clanbook that some of the Children burned up and died when God created the light at the start of time, well, it seems the Neverborn might be “dead” (I use this term loosely, I don’t thinking something that pre-dates our reality can really die as we understand the concept at least) Children.

                              Anybody else had this thought? Anyone come up with other possible links between Vampire stuff and the Neverborn?
