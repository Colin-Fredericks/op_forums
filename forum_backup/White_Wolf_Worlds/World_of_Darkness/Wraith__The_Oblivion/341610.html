    Seven Original Far Shores

    12-16-2014, 02:34 AM
    When Charon founded Stygia, he did so upon returning from the Far Shores bearing "seven signs", which granted him the authority to act as supreme judge over all the dead and determine their fates. I was thinking about the era of the Stygian Golden Age, when the Shining Ones and their Ferrymen controlled the Stygian ports from their Temples and souls regularly traveled to the Far Shores beyond, and I was pondering what Far Shores Charon did have such commerce with, and appeal to the spiritual authority of in his "seven signs". Unfortunately the Far Shores have never really been explored in canon, so here's my take...

    - Elysium: A paradise garden when the Shining Ones discovered it, colonized to grant succor and peace to those worthy heroes who needed peace and healing from life's conflicts to find Transcendence. It's debatable when things started going wrong. One school of thought is that peaceful leisure gave way to debauchery, which fed various shadows' demands for more and more extreme pleasures, and the corpus of the residents became the medium through which to craft such marvelous experiences and beauties. More economically minded Hierarchy scholars argue the more mundane answer that the arrivals extended past the ability for the existent plasmic paradise to support them, and as with soulforging in Stygia they became the building materials to extend the paradise - but unlike Stygia, as a Paradise, the demands for low population density and plentiful resources were so extreme that the VAST majority of souls were harvested for a few elites to maintain their pleasure garden.

    - Purgatorium: A realm of cleansing punishment and purifying flames of the river Phlegethon it feeds. Long predating the Medieval Church's belief in the realm of "Purgatory", many Mediterranean belief systems spoke of a realm of punishment that souls passed through. The Ancient Greek stories of carefully designed punishments for figures like Tantalus and Sisyphus, speak of this realm (as opposed to the "Tartarus" where Titans and chthonic horrors are bound - that's the Labyrinth). Since the Hierarchy's condemnation of the Shining Ones and the "Heretics" it's been easy to paint the agonies of this realm as proof of the malice of their foes - but in truth, this remains one of the uncorrupted Far Shores, still frequented by loyal Ferrymen, and dedicated to the cause of aiding Wraiths on the path to Transcendence... through atonement for their sins and regrets.

    - Asphodel: Minos himself made visit with Charon to this misty empty realm early on before the rise of Stygia. It had only one feature of note - the river Lethe, which washed away memories of souls. Minos made close study of the phenomenon and how to recollect memories from the stream, laying the foundation of Mnemosyne. As a Far Shore its guiding guiding philosophy has been that as attachment to the fetters and passions of ones life prevent Transcendence, than to wash away knowledge of that life would allow for it. How successful that has been is unknown, as is whether the realm ever became corrupt, as so many who investigate the question end up forgetting the answer... or who they even are. There is though a community of Wraiths upon the plains of the Realm who have built a strange pseudo-egalitarian state of nature community in the Veil of Ignorance they have found from their past identities.

    - Irkalla: An earthen realm of natural decay. The dead here face the reality of their state as their corpus rots as they pass through its doors into deeper and deeper layers, and feed the strange plasmic vegetation that the realm bares, all of which have bizarre properties, or even animal-like behavior. The Shining One who rules this realm, called Adonis or Tammuz is a being of unspeakable beauty and vitality as he has, like others of his brethren, grown corrupt and draws on the corpus and pathos other wraiths shed in their descent in a never ending quest to rebuild himself to become a TRULY living god, rising from darkness over the skinlands.

    - Amenti: See Mummy Second Edition. Yes the Stygian treaty with Anubis and the "Dark Kingdom of Sand" with respect to the Ancient Egyptian Dead was one of the protected passages of souls to the Far Shores in the Stygian Golden Age.

    - Valhalla: A realm of battle known by many Germanic names over the milennia, but the later Norse one is what it is best remembered by. Valhalla collected those souls (from the cultures that sought it at least) whose deaths in battle prepared them for the war against Oblivion. And at the same time, in making war on each other in training, and on Oblivion, the Shining Ones of this realm sought to let the resident souls seek Transcendence through the conquest of their own Shadows. Unfortunately, sometimes the Shadows win. Over the centuries the Realm has become less geared toward the conquest of Oblivion and more toward senseless violence and destruction, becoming more and more infected by Oblivion in the process. The line between Eiherjar and Spectre have blurred to non-existence on the battlefield.

    - Kur: This towering peak, far beyond the height of any mountain in the Skinlands, presents a physically manifestation of the quest for transcendence. Its trials and guards forced those who sought to climb to struggle to overcome their own weaknesses. The original Shining Ones of this Realm are gone - believed destroyed in the First Great Maelstrom... or possibly overthrown by climb-hardened Wraiths furious over succeeding the ascent and not finding Transcendence waiting as a prize at the summit. However the Ferrymen have retaken the peak more recently.


    Other Far Shores besides those original seven who Charon trucked with exist of course, and many regularly received Stygian souls before the banning of the "Heretics". There was the watery paradise of Mag Mell, which fell to spectres in the Second Great Maelstrom, though might still exist to be rescued; the airy expanse of Irij where souls become birds and stars (for better or worse); and of course the infamous Paradise of the Fishers where the imprisoned "saved" souls sing unending hosannas in their wailing agony.

    Other Dark Kingdoms are associated with other "afterlives" in the Tempest as well, or have seats of power that can be considered Far Shores in their own right - such as Swar and Karta.

                  I like the list of Far Shores as a potential list. I'm not sure I'm as on board with all of them being the ones Charon visited at the foundation of Stygia. It's supposed to be roughly contemporaneous with the founding of the Roman Republic, roughly 8th Century BC (753 BC, according to the Romans). Some of those locations described are geographically far beyond the reach of knowledge of the area at the time (Romans/Greeks/other Mediterranean civilizations + Mesopotamian, possibly extending to India). Some of them are from later eras.

                  In the building of Stygia, it specifically mentions certain cities representative of civilizations contemporaneous with this period - The Near East (Sodom & Gomorah, Jericho), Meospotomia (Babylon), Greece (Athens and Sparta) and identifies Egypt as a specific region. It seems like those civilizations (or previous ones) would have been the origin points of the Ferrymen who became the Shining Ones and would represent the Far Shores that Charon was able to visit. I'd probably pin the period in question to be from the time of Charon proper to sometime well before the founding of Rome and the regions as per the ones listed in the first paragraph. In which case, I'd be leery of Nifleheim & Valhalla (beyond Charon's world and later in time, earliest attestation about 1500 years later), Mag Mell (Irish, same), Irij (Slavic,same), Paradise of the Fishers (start of Christianity, about 700-800 years later). I think all those SHOULD be Far Shores, either later discovered (Paradise of the Fishers) of or associated with realms later conquered by Stygia, but independant and unknown to Charon and his Ferrymen at the time of his journey across the Sunless Sea to gather the Seven Signs.

                  So I'm on board with Amenti (Egypt). 125% 1000%

                  For Mesopotamia - Irkalla and Kur, I would probably conflate , since Kur seems just as much, if not more, a god/dragon than a place. Possibly he was killed and Irkalla was built out of his body, similar to the way the world was built out of Tiamat? (Ereshkigal the "Shining One" et al, kills Kur - some dragon-monster-plasmic/specter thing and build the Far Shore of Irkalla on it's remains?).

                  For Greece - I can some reason to conflate Elysium/The Fields of Asphodel/Tartarus, but I see reasons to separate them out as well... Perhaps Elsyium & Asphodel together and Tartarus/Erebus together, though Erebus (dark waiting room of the dead) really seems like it's the Shadowlands and Tartarus (place of eternal torment for those overcome by their wickedness in life) might be the Labyrinth instead. I would probably go for that. The Fields of Asphodel surround/stand in front of the Elysian Fields. Most end up in Asphodel, with the precious few escorted through to the divine peace of Elysium. Also Asphodel might be the remains of the Minoan underworld, so there some possibility for it being VERY old and perhaps discovered by Minos (+ his brothers, Rhadmanthus & Sarpedon), as per your storyline? Then the brothers, with or without Minos, build Elysium? Rhad. and Sarp. are the Shining Ones of Elysium?

                  For some other options for the other "original 7 sign giving Far Shores":

                  How about 1-2 from the Cannaanites? Sheol for the Hebrews and Nepesh (Mot's Realm of Snot) for the Cannaanites and later Phonenicians (They REALLY don't seem to have enough in common to be conflated).

                  Considering Charon's Mycenaen origins, I would also throw out the Hittite underworld, as it contemporary with Charon's own time (so some the wraiths wandering around early on would be from that time... oh, and Ilium/Troy was a Hittite city back during Mycenaean times). For an underworld which translates to "the Dark Place", it would seem awfully bright if it has it's own sun goddess, aka as a "Shining One". Plus it is the regions origin of IRON. Which... ya know....

                  But all those cultures (Egypt, Elysium, seen likely to provide seven signs that can be placed on "great iron table.

                  That's about all I can come up with preliminary poking and not really diving deep into research materials. Other ideas:
                  - Perhaps the Assyrians and/or Persians?
                  - Something going on in Eastern Europe, a la Illyria or even the Scythians (ooooooh.... I'd love that)?
                  - Etruscans? (which effectively includes the Latins since they are operating with a native view heavily eclipsed by Etruscans then, later, by the Greeks)
                  - Something deeply ancient and Celtic -like the trilithons and pillars with the skull niches - for the Gauls of southern France/northern Italy/peri-Phoenician Iberia
