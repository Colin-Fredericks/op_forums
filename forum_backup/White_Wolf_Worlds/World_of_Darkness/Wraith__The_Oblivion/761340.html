    Guidebook to Stygia, Chapter Three: The Agora (Marketplace)

    11-09-2015, 06:14 AM
    "if you want it, we've got it. If you don't want anything, you'll change your mind after you see my wares." Ashadel Abdul-Razzaq, Agora Merchant.

    "If you thought the Americans where bad, with their mindless consumerism and their worship of greed, you haven't seen how hungry for profit the average Stygian Merchant is when its all that's keeping him out of Oblivion" Comrade Ivan Andropov, Member of the Communist Party in life.



    The Grand Agora

    Like most things in Stygia, the Agora started small, and much like a shelf accrues dust or a tree grows moss, began to expand as the weight of ages pressed down on it. Starting as a ramshackle collection of shops, founded by Merchants who found that while they couldn't buy their way out of death, they could still profit off the dead and continue their economic expansion in the depths of the Underworld. In those days, the Tempest was relatively calm, and as the Romans, Greeks, Etruscan's, and Carthaginians traded across the Mediterranean, so too did Stygia.

    And with every ship load of spices, rare metals, gems, books, or nearly anything else, came full shops and stores eager to liquidate their stocks and make as many Obols as possible. In the Agora, the Obulus is as much your Emperor as Charon is, and as much a king as your Deathlord. Nearly anything can be found here if you look long enough, and most Wraiths have a very long time indeed. But despite this veritable eternity, money changes hands rapidly and the tides of commerce and capitalism are just as unmerciful and unyielding as they are in the Skinlands. The Agora is frequently the most chaotic place in Stygia, with financial empires rising overnight, then plunging as their funds dry up with a string of failed ventures. There's quite possibly more dead businesses that have passed on from the Agora then there are wraiths in the entire city!


    Small Businesses and Startups

    The fundamental building block of any economy is the small business. And over here, to compete with larger groups of merchants and guilds (Not the capital G-Guilds that where banished centuries ago), most small shops specialize. While you might be able to walk into a large shop here and find just about anything in large quantities, most small shops here in Stygia specialize on doing one thing VERY well. One shop might have the reputation of being THE place to go for swords, another might specialize in finding the latest novels, even going so far as to hire Sandmen to urge suicidal teenagers to keep copies of certain books near their person in their dreams.

    But while this does give them a position in the market, and does give them the potential to grow, the dead tend to be competitive. Oh sure, there's not the immediate need of 'if I don't make enough I starve to death', especially when you might well of starved to death a few weeks back which is the reason you're in Stygia to begin with, but the dead thrive on Passion, and many Merchants have their Passions tied into competition. Edging out their peers, suppressing competition, hiring a Masquer to cripple a swordsmith and then taking advantage of his somnolence to edge him out of the market, all of this has strong emotions tied to it...And can be milked for Pathos.

    Of course if you take it too far, you'll start finding your Shadow becoming significantly stronger. Its just has easy to feed your Shadow as it is your Psyche in the cutthroat game of Agora economics. The Deathlords and City Guard are aware of this, and more then one sales extravaganza has been interrupted and delayed by an 'anonymous tip' that a clerk might be a Doppleganger, causing them to get a visit from a Pardoner...And giving a rival the time to set up his OWN sale and undercutting strategies.



    Food Stalls and Restaurants

    Surprised? The dead don't need to eat, true, you've probably not really felt hunger since you arrived here. Oh sure, you might feel a small phantom twinge of it every so often when you realize you've not eaten or drank anything in a while, but that's purely psychological, the part of your mind that remembers being alive and still attributes living needs to your corpus. That's not to say, of course, that Wraiths CANNOT eat or drink...We just can't do it traditionally.

    Food and drink in Stygia is exclusively composed of Pathos. Devouring some Pathos adds that Pathos to your reserves, and fuels you right on up (The Doomsayers have a deal for provisions with some of the larger restaurants and eateries in Stygia, run out of Pathos in the Labyrinth? Enough energy to power Argos is a bag of crisps away!). But again, we don't NEED to eat, you get just as much Pathos from your Passions, and its far more reliable. So why do Wraiths bother at all?

    Simple.

    It makes us feel alive again for a moment. Pathos, when shaped into food, gives us the taste and texture of the food, in addition to filling our senses with the memories and feelings woven into that Pathos. A wraith who gets the lions share of his Pathos from listening to his living lover play the Piano donates Pathos to a cook, the pathos is then distilled into a glass of wine, and then another Wraith drinks it. The imbibing Wraith would then feel the same emotions the original Wraith did, along with getting some Pathos out of it! Is it any wonder people like to eat in Stygia?

    The most famous restaurant in Stygia is the Banquet Table, located near the heart of the Agora District. Founded by the wraith of the Royal Chef of Henry the VIII (Who also founded the Chef's Guild) after his death, the Table trains any Wraith willing to pay their dues and swear an oath of secrecy in the art of creating Phantom Foodstuffs. They also frequently purchase different 'flavors' of pathos, and I myself even made a few sales during my Enfant days! Rumor has it that daring diners can even consume dinners made of Angst, carrying the emotions tied to dark and horrific events ranging from gleeful murder to rape. If this is true, its highly illegal and if you should find any evidence, I encourage you to take it straight to your Legion's authorities.

    Sidebar: Alternate Ursury Art, "Dinner Time"

    This Art works essentially the same as "Transfer" but Pathos can be converted to a physical form and shaped by the user. Instead of feeding Pathos directly into someone, it simply takes on a physical shape in the Shadowlands, and can then be eaten at any time by any Wraith. The technique is simple enough to learn, but the Chefs Guild of Stygia considers it a trade secret, and more then one Wraith has been tossed into a Harrowing after trying to figure out how they make such delicious and meaningful treats. As mentioned above, you can do the same thing with Angst, but if the foodstuff is not consumed within a day, the food dissolves into nothingness and the Angst returns to the Wraith who it was created from. You'll need more then simple Ursury to cheat your Shadow out of its due, I'm afraid!



    The Slave Market

    Now, I'm aware a good number of you are probably already considering setting this book on fire just upon reading that word. But, its worth noting, that Stygia is a VERY old city, and many of the Wraiths populating it are from an era when Slavery was just another thing, a part of life as natural as hiring immigrants to do cheap labor is in today's Skinlands. The buying and selling of Thralls, as we call them, are perfectly legal throughout the domains controlled by the Dark Kingdom of Iron.

    That is the way things are. Regardless of your views or politics.

    I'm not here to tell you what you should think, or believe, or to feel. I'm here to educate you on the way Stygia operates and how to get by in the city. I'll spare you my feelings on the subject has best I can. But slavery is legal, and you are probably not going to change that, the sooner you accept it, the easier you will Slumber.

    Now, to the matter at hand.

    In recent years, the institution of owning Thralls in Stygian territories has undergone something of a reform. Thralldom is still used as a punishment or restraining method for lawbreaking Wraiths, reaping Thralls is still legal, and indentured servitude is seeing quite the heyday, in the chaos of the Shadowlands, many would beggar themselves to make it to the safety of Charons city...And those who can't beggar themselves simply sell themselves.

    But as I mentioned, there have been reforms, as more and more Civil Rights leaders around the world died, pressure was increasingly put on the Hierarchy to, if not ABOLISH slavery, at least lessen its cruelty. The Deathlords convened, and decided on an acceptable compromise, proclaiming from the Onyx Tower a series of reforms. The most important of these was the strict implementation and tracking of sentence times. Indentured servants can no longer have their durance's 'forgotten' by their masters and simply never released, and the Hierarchy keeps strict tabs on those servants legally Indentured and enslaved through lawbreaking. A sentence of 20 years now, theoretically anyway, actually DOES end after 20 years. In addition, the ownership of Eunuchs, the disgustingly lobotomized Thralls of the Yellow Springs where outlawed by this proclamation. Any Eunuchs in the city had a year to be turned over for forging and some compensation, after that, any Wraith caught with one would be punished by a fifty year durance as a Thrall.

    Of course, with the Tempest the way it is these days, this has less and less effect the further you get away from Stygia. Yet another reason more and more Wraiths are finding their way to the Isle of Sorrows. Now, if you wish to BUY a Thrall, the Chattel Houses around the Agora are at your service. There's any number of institutions selling Thralls, but all of them report to the Servants Guild, run by Plantation Owners and colonial overseers who took their skills in managing...human resources as it where in life, and put themselves to work for Stygia.

    You might find yourself wondering what Charon thought of Thralldom...I have printed below a quote from the Emperor of the Western Dead on the subject

    "Though I do regret the essential need for labor and controlling those too weak to help themselves, and to punish criminals for whom death is not an option and consigning them to Oblivion is unthinkable. I do not, for a moment, regret taking the actions that MUST be taken for the security of Stygia and the Dark Kingdom of Iron."



    The Doomslayers Grand Chapterhouse

    Yes, its true, the Doomslayers do dwell just on the edge of the Agora in a massive hall where the greatest of their number plan their grand crusades against the forces of Oblivion. You might find yourself asking, why there? WHY would a group of warriors dwell so close to the markets? The answer is simple, like any army, the Doomslayers have material needs. Swords, spears, bows, blades, tools, all of those are required in the march against Oblivion.

    Ever pragmatic, the Doomslayers simply decided that if they needed to buy goods, why not make the trip out of the way as short as possible? A Doomslayer can get their next mission from their superiors, and buy a new Stygian Steel blade within the span of a few hours. In addition, the Doomslayers bring things back from the Labyrinth and all corners of the Shadowlands in their hunts and grand marches, and these goods often pay for a new suit of armor, a new set of Moliated improvements, or a new blade. As in everything else in the Agora, death turns the wheels of commerce.

    More details on the Doomslayers can be found in later chapters, but as the Doomslayers are on the edge of the district, their Chapterhouse was worth mentioning here, however briefly.


    Guilds and Malls: The big players of the Agora

    Now that we've discussed small business, now its time to discuss the big. The Shadowlands reflection of Trajan's Market was the first major marketplace in the Agora, and still exists today, albeit with dozens of extensions added on over the 1900 some years its existed down here. Roughly a dozen other major Malls and Guildhalls exist within the Agora, packed full of shops owned by anywhere from one to dozens of Wraiths.

    To go over all the details of every marketplace in the Agora is beyond my skill and would take dozens of Volumes. And considering that people tend to either go for the biggest, oldest, or most famous landmark in any given area, we'll stick to Trajan's Market. Once the closest thing the ancient world had to a Shopping Mall, Trajan's Market has since grown exponentially into a miniature Mecca of capitalistic outgrowth. If you need to find a relic, you can find it here. If you want to hire a mercenary, you can find him here, if you want to possess a weapon of great and terrible power you can find it here, and should probably bring several wheelbarrows of Obols.

    Recent rumors have stated that in the lowest parts of Trajan's Market, something...unusual can be found. If you know the right password, they say, you can draw the attention of something that is of the Skinlands, but is very much dead. A walking corpse that buys and sells goods that even in Trajan's Market cannot be found anywhere else, this "Harbinger of Skulls", as its supposedly called, also trades in information, and will trade a kings ransom in Obols for any information about a specific Italian family...At least if you believe such rumors, I'm simply putting it to print as its become a well known tidbit in the Agora district.



    Sidebar: Technological goods in the Shadowlands

    There's no Kickstarter in the Tempest, there's no Gofundme, no crowdfunding beyond crowds that throw money at you when you hit on the next must-have item for the citizens of Stygia. While the Quick value their iPhones and cars, the dead value anything that can bring excitement and passion to their unlife. Electronics are...still somewhat scarce in the Shadowlands, but more and more Wraiths come out of their Cauls clutching smartphones and tablet computers every day.

    As a result, while still nascent, there is something of a Tech Boom in Stygia these days. The Artificers are still trying to get their minds wrapped around just HOW an iPhone works in the Shadowlands, much less how it can get a signal in the first place over here. When before the average person didn't have as much of a tie to his technology, a computer was just a thing he worked on or a thing he did his school work on, today people live out a sizable portion of their lives on the Internet. And that passion makes for powerful relics.

    Rather then electricity, Shadowlands computing technology runs on Pathos. The same emotions that brought the device over here in the first place run through its spectral wires, and provide power to it. More curiously, wireless signals seem to have a limited potential to cross the shroud, meaning that a shadowlands reflection of an internet cafe might just permit a Wraith to access the internet!

    What does this mean for the Dictuum Mortum as Wraiths start to figure out that they don't need to end a flame war or a blog just because they died? Probably nothing good.
