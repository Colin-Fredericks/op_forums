    Masks of the Hierarchy

    12-23-2023, 10:30 PM
    I am interested in hearing ideas and descriptions of the various masks worn by members of the Hierarchy. I think it is an interesting detail of the society, but I've been disappointed at how few details we've been provided. They are supposed to be a "fundamental element of wraith society", "symbols of high station", "extremely fashionable among many wraiths" and "depersonalize the office". Possessing a mask for an office that one does not have is supposed to be a crime punishable by discorporation, so it is very serious.

    I think to some degree this is supposed to allow us to use our imaginations and give us free reign. And I like that. However, 1) have examples would give me something to work with, and 2) if masks are used to represent offices, then it stands that even if they are individually different, the broad categories of ranks (Legionnaire, Centurion, Marshal, etc.) should have a style of mask that obviously denotes their status. If they are too individualistic, it's not obvious what rank in the Hierarchy one is.

    My idea is that there should be some continuity between any masks worn by the same rank, and some common motify for masks worn within the same Legion. Lower ranks would have more standardized masks, but they would become more individualistic as one goes up the ranks. Many of the Legions also have various orders and other sub-groups within them, and I imagine these have special masks designating their membership though these two might be distinguishable by rank.

    I imagine there are also "private" masks worn by ordinary citizens that are simply fashion accessories which may mark some kind of status, but which would not be confused with masks for actual office holders. Certain professions might also have common kinds of masks that indicate the type of service they provide (a merchant who deals in soul pathos crystals, someone skilled in castigate, someone who can safely escort you through the Tempest). These masks are probably regulated to a degree by the Hierarchy, and the wraith may have paid some kind of registration fee to obtain such a mask that is a symbol of some kind of professional competence. The origins of these kind of masks probably date to the Guilds.

      Some sourcebooks have given us some details. The Hierarchy sourcebook gave us some of the broad powers of the masks of the Deathlords, though not what they looked like. Still, I imagine it is a rare chronicle for one or more of the Deathlords actually make an appearance. I am more interested in the lower ranks as those wraiths are the ones PCs will likely encounter.

      The 1e corebook gives us some possible examples, though they don't explicitly say they are "masks". One is the "blindfold of justice" a rank 2 artifact that covers the eyes of an "Enforcer". We see this in several illustrations of the corebook. To me, this seems to be a common mask for Legionnaires. And we might see other Legionnaires who wear some kind of cloth mask that is fashioned around their head - kerchiefs, bandanas, face masks, scarves, keffiyehs, etc. - that can conceal their identity at least partially. One thing I like about the blindfold of justice is that it does have powers, which Hierarchy masks are supposed to have,

      Unfortunately, the one example we have of a possible Centurion (Jameson in Little Five Points, though he's not specifically called that) doesn't see to have a mask at all. Lost opportunity there.

      The only other thing that seems to correspond are the muzzles worn by barghests, but there are enslaved, moliated thralls so they aren't really masks. But we do have an evocative illustration from Josh Timbrook that shows the muzzle as clearly looking like the jaw of an animal, so I think they count as a mask. But for a lobotomized thrall, all muzzles are likely the same. We know braces of barghests are controlled by one or more "keepers". Again frustratingly we're not told what actual rank a "keeper" is, but given their responsibility I assume they are specialized Centurions. I'd probably make them wear more elaborate masks covering their head that looks much like an animal head (say a wolf) that denotes their mastery of the barghests.

      After a (very) quick reread of the Hierarchy sourcebook, I can't find much written about masks. But the artwork certainly shows a lot. There are some who have very simple carnival / Venetian masks. Some masks that are overly large that extend way beyond the head, and full face covering masks that look monstrous. But I don't think there was any thought at providing some kind of system.

      I have not reread my other Wraith sourcebooks, but I don't remember any real details on masks by them. If anyone does know of references, I'd appreciate to know which books and their pages (if possible).

      Otherwise, I think this is something that just needs player ideas. So if you have any, or have seen any good examples during play, I'd love to hear them. For example, I think some group would probably have some plague doctor style masks. They are just so distinctive and rather cool looking. Don't know what group I'd assign them to yet though.

      Thanks!

              The masks were likely direct representations of the deceased, symbolizing a continuation of the dead's identity in death, similar to funerary statues and incisions, immortalizing an idealized depiction of the deceased - but strangely funerary masks like the ones found at Mycenae have not been found anywhere else in Mycenaean culture

              The Mycenaeans used three main types of tombs: shaft tombs, chamber tombs, and tholos tombs. Shaft tombs are the earliest type of tomb which are rectangular graves cut into the bedrock. Chamber tombs are designed to look like houses, with an entrance passage (dromos), an entrance (stomion), and a chamber. Tholos tombs were similar to chamber tombs, but they were larger and their walls were built using corbelling. This technique gave tholos tombs a beehive shape.
              The Mycenaeans buried their dead with grave goods that show the deceased individual's social status. Elite tombs included gilded weapons, masks, jewelry, tools, and vessels made of rich materials like gold, amber, and silver. The grave goods in lower-class burials were not made with these luxury materials.

                  I found some canonical mask descriptions in the Book of Legions. There's clearly no sense of theme or reason, just individual writers wanting to write a "cool" description. But I think they can work for the purpose of inspiration.

                  The Warriors of Lethe (basically a special order of suicide bombers of the Silent Legion) wear featureless masks of midnight blue.

                  The Legion of Onyx Tears are the bodyguards of the Beggar Lord, Legion of Paupers. They wear tall, black masks with stylized weeping eyes and laughing mouths.

                  The beastmasters (keepers of barghests) of the Beast Corps of the Penitent Legion wear helmets with stylized hound's heads with bared teeth.

                  The soldiers of the Grim Legion wear heavy helmets of a stylized human skull.

                  The Laughing Lady wears a huge mask with an enormous toothy grin that looks like a maw going to bite.

                  The Smiling Lord wears a skeletal mask.

                  Not much to go on, but we have descriptions of two of the masks of the eight Deathlords. We know two special orders have their own special masks, which makes sense. Other special orders likely have their own unique masks as well. The mask of the keepers of barghests match what I expected earlier. The only thing that seems off is that ordinary Legionnaires of the Grim Legion would have outright helmets. I see that more as being a thing for Centurions.

                  There does seem to be a theme that the Grim Legion masks involve a skull. While that makes some sense, it would seem more appropriate for the Skeletal Legion.

                      Originally posted by Shadeprowler View Post
                      Charon originally is from the ancient civilization of Mycenae
                      If the use of masks predates the foundation of the Hierarchy (12th century), their origins as death masks is pretty plausible. From there, it's possible that the mask evolved in style to those of classic Greek theater, and reflected the 'role' of the Wraith in society. The Legions are older than the Hierarchy, so the latter could simply have picked up that fashion.

                      Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                      There are some who have very simple carnival / Venetian masks.
                      I personally think Venetian masks were the core inspiration here. Two points of interest: the use of masks predates the carnival tradition, and their use in Venice almost coincides with the foundation of the Hierarchy. Their main function was to disguise the identity of a wearer of upper social status and allow them to indulge in all kinds of 'uncouth' activities. The side-effect of dissolving social boundaries only appeared later with the inclusion in the carnival.

                      If we go with that speculative timeline, we end up with something that provides a degree of anonymity while still codifying the social status of the wearer. That code has probably evolved into something very abstract that would be hard for outsiders to pick up on, but easy to recognize for a Wraith integrated in the society like with all fashion - and as a consequence, may not seem directly related to the actual station of the wearer from an outside (Player) perspective. That could at least be used as a rationalization for the 'cool' designs that favor style over overt symbolism.

                      When I described them to players, I went with a 'Dave McKean take on Venetian masks' just for the aesthetic. Fashion-coding is so abstract that any little detail could mean anything, so I think the ST can be free with the degree of symbolism they want to include.

                              After doing some research on Venetian masks, I have some ideas on a general scheme of how I want to portray the Hierarchy. For those who want to follow some of the terms I'm about to use, I think this URL can provide the basics. Another good URL is here. The below is subject to change, and it is not intended as a straightjacket, just some general guidelines for my chronicles.

                              As I've already mentioned, inspired by the Blindfold of Justice artifact, I am thinking of limiting the rank of Legionnaire/Clerk to cloth or lace masks. That might be blindfolds, scarves, hoods, veils, shawl, or anything else that could be used to cover the face. Very limited powers as artifacts, mostly limited to perception to help out the patrols.

                              Centurions will have helms to provide protection to them in combat. They lead patrols and are the ones who need to get their hands dirty. Various types of helms are possible, but in order to effectively function as masks they either need to cover the face in full, or obscure most of it like the Corinthian helmet. No helms that are open faced or just cover the top of the head. For the civil equivalent of Adjustors, I'm not sure yet. Helmets wouldn't be appropriate for them. Maybe a mask of a helmet similar to oldstyle goaltender masks (think Jason in Friday the 13th, not modern hockey masks) or ballistic masks - something very utilitarian looking, but could still be decorated.

                              Marshals/Inspectors are where we start seeing use of the Venetian style masks. They'll have some variant of the classic white volto/larva mask. Easy to elaborate on to make many different varieties.

                              Regents/Ministers use some form of the bauta mask usually combined with a hat, hood, or cape of sometime. It's beak like chin easily distinguishes it from the volto style mask. It looks a bit more imposing.

                              Overlords/Chancellors use more full face masks, often of animals or legendary beings. Such masks really don't show up in Venice, but are popular in Carnivals in other cities. Alternatively, the dama mask is also used, distinguished from the bauta and volto by being gilded and enameled instead of white.

                              Anacreons have very elaborate full head masks/helms. They often incorporate chains, another motif of the Hierarchy that often does not get utilized. This is entirely due to the picture for Governor James Johnson in Necropolis: Atlanta which is the only character portrait that has a mask.

                              The various half-masks worn at Carnival, based on the commedia dell'arte, are typically used by freewraiths. Unlike full masks, the half masks aren't regulated by the Hierarchy's sumptuary laws because they won't be confused by the masks the Hierarchy actually uses.

                              The plague doctor masks with their long, beaked noses are only used by the Magisterium Veritas, the secret police of Stygia that owe loyalty directly to Charon. The grim riders of the Order of the Unlidded Eye, of course, wear knightly helms with visors.

                              The Silent Legion is one exception. They use the moretta style mask which has no hole in the mask to speak through. Many masks of this style did not have ties around the head, but was held in place by the wearer biting on a button or bit in her mouth. The reason, of course, is that it's the Silent Legion. At low ranks, this might simply be decoration. But at higher ranks, the masks might actually require this preventing the wraith from talking, but of course provide the ability to communicate via thought or perhaps in a disembodied voice. Of course, the voice itself is unique to the mask and does not reflect the wraith's actual voice. I am thinking whether this should extend to the legionnaires where they would wear a cloth gag as well.

                              Likewise the Penitent Legion, which serves the Laughing Lady, incorporates some kind of jester or jolly design in their masks. Laughing mouths and bells are very common.

                              I'm still thinking of good motifs for each of the legions. Though I am considering that the Legion of Fate would have more Greek style theatre masks though still the size of the Venetian style masks.

                                              I found another canon description of Hierarchy masks so as a public service I am updating this thread. The Buried Secrets sourcebook lists Masks as a level 1 soulsteel artifact. We get some more details.

                                              Official Hierarchy masks are made from polished soulsteel; each forged from a single traitor's soul.

                                              Their only artifact "power" is that they adjust to fit any wearer not extensively marred by Moliation. Otherwise most masks are strictly functional and decorative.

                                              Anacreon masks look like heraldic skulls.

                                              It also confirms that barghest trainers have masks with a distinctly houndlike feature, which we've previously discussed.

                                              I'd probably have this mask available at Centurion rank and above, but not used for the typical legionnaire.

                                              There is also a level 2 artifact weapon called Soulfire Mask. These are Stygian steel masks designed for high ranking Hierarchy officials for protection. Their "grimacing visages" have a soulfire gem. Upon activation the Soulfire Mask either produces spewing ribbons of flame that attacks someone, or surrounds the mask wearer with a nimbus of flames that absorbs physical damage. Since Anacreon masks are listed under the normal Masks artifact, these Soulfire Masks are probably given only to those high officials who are especially favored for past service or perhaps considered to be specially targeted and need more protection, not just given out. I imagine they are distinctive in and of themselves because of the presence of the soulfire gem. That might be to discourage an attempt in the first place.
