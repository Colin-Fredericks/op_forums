                    May I make a recommendation? Were I addressing these issues, I'd say that powers to sense and communicate with the dead work fine on all denizens of the Shadowlands, but that powers that bind ghosts to one's will only work as advertised on drones — the proto-wraiths who haven’t yet had their Cauls removed. Wraiths, spectres, and other of the more self-aware of the ghostly population should at the very least have an enhanced ability to maintain their free will in the face of supernatural coercion. As for addressing it in Wraith terms, I'd probably use whatever Wraith does to address mortal mediums and necromancers (e.g., so-called hedge magic) as the vehicle to present these design principles. If that means punting it to a hypothetical Wr20: the Quick and the Dead supplement due to space concerns, so be it.

                    Basically, I don’t like the idea of the protagonists of Wraith being easily reduced to chattel by protagonists of another gameline.

                    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                        @Deadguy: The newest update on the Kickstarter has me mulling over a topic that's more than a little bit intense, but it's worth mentioning. Do you mind talking Charnel Houses for a second?

                                                        I'm ethnically Jewish and lost direct blood relations to the Holocaust; my great-grandfather fled Poland, and his village was wiped off the map. Wraith is favorite game of all time, with a considerable amount of deep personal meaning to me, but Charnel Houses has always left a really sour taste in my mouth. I feel like the intent - teach something important through gaming - was well-meaning, but the end result just feels really distasteful.

                                                        The industrialized murder of my kin and millions more is not a game. It's not something I trust the average group of tabletop game players to handle well, and doubly so through a veil of fantasy that allows the whole thing to seem less real. I also get that since the book is specifically on the Shoah, it has to be Jew-centric, but in doing so I felt that painting a picture of the Holocaust with no attention paid to the others who lost their lives was more than a little disgusting.

                                                        My boyfriend is Romani, and the World of Darkness' spotty history with its handling of his people leaves a lot to be desired; it's a real shame to this day that the genocide of them and many others was swept under the rug by the book.

                                                        No disrespect meant, and I'm sorry for the wall of text, but this struck me as my best shot at ever getting an official reply. I'd love to hear from the Deadguy himself on this.

                                                        Remi. she/her. game designer.
