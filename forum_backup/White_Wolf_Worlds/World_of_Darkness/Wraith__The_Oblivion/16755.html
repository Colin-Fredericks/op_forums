    Was Wraith's setting too punishing?

    11-23-2013, 04:22 PM
    While doing some googling on Wraith, I came across this rather... depressing review of the game. The overall point of the review is that Wraith's setting is much, much more punishing than the premise (being a ghost) implies. While the premise implies something not unlike Patrick Swayze's Ghost, the actual setting turns up the bleakness to Warhammer 40K levels, except without the over-the-top awesomeness. I've cross-referenced this with the wiki and what books I could get and the assessment seems to be accurate, but the books really don't pull together the sheer bleakness implied as well as this review does. Is Wraith really much more bleaker than it has to be?

    EDIT: I've removed the attachment and just will post the review here:
    Originally posted by Chris De Lasombra
    For reference I would like to let you know right off how much I am devoted to WW and both the New and Classic WoD. I own all but 6 books from the CWoD and they are just reprints of stuff I already own and almost all of the NwoD, but it has been a bit more difficult to keep up with what is available since the distribution model has changed and I am horrible about keeping on top of it without my game store calling me to tell me my new book is in. What I am attempting to make relevant here is that I have supported WW WoD since I preordered the Vampire the Masquerade First Edition in 1991 and have no plans on not doing so even if I am not completely ecstatic about a game line they put out. No matter my complaints I am still addicted.

    Now on to my comments. Personally I thought the powers kind of sucked and I have to disagree with you about combat being better than any of the other games (at least that is how I read the intent of your statement). Sure, over all there were a couple nifty Arcanoi powers, but as a whole, they just did not add up to the cool factor like Mage or Vampire in my opinion. I liked them about as much as the Changeling powers.

    There are a couple of things that I disliked about Wraith, though I was absolutely enthralled with the concept of the game as a whole and obviously liked it enough to buy all the books. I still like reading the material, there are some really great things about the game and world that I find more than interesting, but as things stand I would rather read about it than try to play or ST it to any extent. For me it is similar to the likes of many of the Monte Python or Mel Brooks movies. They are much more funny to talk about and remissness than try to watch any more. The material is great, but I ran far too long on nostalgia and became disappointed because of the expectations I had and was no longer provided

    After reading Wraith Second Ed, absorbing the material and finally discerning how it all fit together, what I saw was a mess, not that First Ed was any better. More on that later. Definitely not the type of tragic mess and laughable disappointment I encountered by glancing through the D20 supplement, .The Book of Erotic Fantasy.. Oh, the tragedy that was .The Book of Erotic Fantasy.. Marlin Brando put it best I think. .The Horror..... but there was a melancholy disappointment that to this day I can not shake.

    Anyway, after I pieced together the .Big Picture. it looked to me that Danskey took a lot of really great ideas, both on his own and transfers from Fist Edition and threw them in a blender full of syrup, extracted the jumble, tied some of the pieces together with old frayed and molding bailing string, stuck some bubble gum for some color and stapled the rest with rusty staples pulled from his ninth grade homework. There were aspects of both Editions that I preferred over the other, but over all Second Edition was better.

    Allegory to this, I experienced the same thing when I read Hunter the Reckoning once I reached the rules section. What the hell is wrong with these people and why the hell did they find it necessary to change the formula that they had used successfully with every other game in the line? Hell, as different as they where, even Aeon, Exalted and Scion only made tweaks to an already solid and wildly popular system. Sure they all have/had their problems, but I challenge anyone to name a system that does not in one form or another. I had given up on the system they mistakenly (again, my own opinion) wrote for whatever reason for the Hunter Game and decided to just rewrite it to what I was familiar with and it ended up being pretty damned easy. It has been working just fine and my players are happier for it, especially those that were familiar with the Hunter system. After reading the Edges section a second (possibly a third) time and trying to figure out what the hell the host of authors were trying to accomplish since the paths made absolutely no sense in the order they were printed. What I noticed was if the individual powers were broken down and rearranged in a particular order, they actually would easily form paths of progressions that had a cohesive and understandable theme that I, and surely everyone else that plays or runs in the WoD would be much more familiar with. A few of the Edges did need to slide up or down on the scale from where they originated to fit where they were needed, but let's be honest, Hunter Edges were grossly overpowered for the level they were, but it is true that they only progressed up to level five so once I found that out I was not as against it. Ultimately moving a few few of them up or down had little effect on game play from what I could tell.

    Back to Wraith. The first problem I still have with Wraith is the structure of the society and by extension the Dead Lands as a whole. The second is that over half of the Arcanoi are not only illegal to use, but you can be taken away for simply being able to use them and there is literally no way to hide the telltale proof that everyone knows about.

    So lets start with the .citizens. of whatever Necropolis you may have happened upon, miraculously surviving till you actually got there or you were ridiculously lucky and were Cauled in a .civilized. Necropolis and the reaper that freed you did not immediately sell you into slavery or as scrap metal for some amazingly unknown and extremely unlikely reason. The citizenry Wraith that have .lived. in this Necropolis long before your freshly squeezed ass showed up likely see you as nothing more than a tool, future slave, their new footstool or someone to throw to the wolves so they can escape. Long story short, by some grand miracle you were able to last long enough to avoid any number of horrible fates and acquire an adequate leverage of some sort to keep the Slavers and the local Constabulary from just breaking down your door (if you even have one since materials are scarce as all hell) and executing any number of the exciting, unspeakable things that could easily happen to your character that you have likely not even spent a single XP on. In all reality, this entire time your character, likely with absolutely no helpful information to work with, has to try to figure out what the fuck is going on, how to survive, how to feed and where you can find a safe place to hide where all the sheltered space is already claimed. All this while trying to fend off the Wraiths so you are not hauled off by any number of people or even sold in the middle of the .night. by the same wraiths neighbors that you have to coexist with or face the unknown wild, the loneliness that will inevitably feed your Shadow and access to all the Harrowing's you could ever wish for.

    So here are all these wraiths around you that have been around an unknowable amount of time, know more than you do and would likely sell their own mother just to avoid their inevitable fate, living under the same cobbled together ghetto with an overabundance of other lying, back stabbing yet understandably paranoid pieces of shit that will gleefully throw you under the proverbial bus in the blink of an eye if they thought it would get them favor with whoever the local Wraith in charge is for even one more day of freedom. It turns out that fear is a great motivator to lead you to do things you would not not normally do

    Even though the Hierarchy claims to have law and order they are obviously nothing more than a bunch of bullies that prey on anyone weaker than they are and utilize those willing to do anything to do their dirty work and gleefully hand suffering out to everyone else around them with absolutely no consequence for their actions. Actually they are rewarded for their extremely evil behavior since in reality the Dead Lands are nothing more than a mashed together cacophony of barely controlled chaos stemmed from the stumbling attempts at fascist control through fear and intimidation and the resulting anarchy that it produces as a byproduct from the Renegades. No, the Renegades are not your saving grace either. They are not your friends, and like everything and everyone I have talked about before, it is unlikely any of them would come to your rescue even if you were being raped by a thorn dicked, venomous velociraptor that spits acid and three of his friends even if they could without any threat to themselves. The lot of them are not noble freedom fighter, they are brigands, thieves and murderers, not your friend.

    Which brings us to Charon, the totalitarian, fascist, dictator that supposedly rules over all of the Dead Lands by mere threat of being the monster under your bed that wants to consume your soul and there is no proof whether it is actually a monster or Charon since he has not been seen for so long nobody really remembers what he looks like. Not that a Wraith of his age and likely power would not possess the simple ability to change what he looks like on a whim anyway, so Charon could really be anyone, anywhere, at any time. There are of course the stories that cannot be proven false and actually because of Wraiths disappearing on a regular basis it is more likely that he is out there and actually does want to eat your soul because it is the only way he can continue existing. By now you may have realized you are slowly edging into doing things you never thought you would be capable of doing in a million years. It may even frighten you that you have to admit to yourself, you can't blame him for doing what he needs to stay alive even though it means your death and probably scares the shit out of you for more than one reason. The realization that there would be little to nothing you could do to escape him or fight him off if he did come for you anyway so really.....still just as fucked as you were to begin with. Eventually you will be caught in the wrong place at the wrong time and your bad day will happen. The possible horror filled ends you your first trip through the deadlands will likely be more violent than you imagined all the times you were forced to think about it. Likely it will be something that was human at one time much like yourself and it will rip your eyes out, replace them with coals of Bale Fire while making you watch with your own ripped out eyes then shove them up your ass so you are forced to watch yourself burn from the inside out. All because you finally gave in and trusted someone that turns out to have been taken over by the same darkness that you have struggled against and managed to keep suppressed.

    Oh and the ghetto you live in is still not safe by the way so you are still nagged by not only your thoughts, but your Shadow's as well. with escaping somehow before someone bigger and badder than you (still just Cauled remember) beats you into submission and sells you into slavery or worse, to an Artificer that will turn you into a broom, money a brick wall or a sex toy for his pet Loveland Frog. This of course should lead one to escaping into the ridiculously dangerous badlands with constant Wraith mauling Maelstroms that will shred you to pieces in less time than you could find unoccupied shelter. Which is unlikely because if you did find a .safe. place to get out of the storm, something else that is uniformly bigger and more bad ass than your still newly Cauled ass would never let a prime real estate location like that go used let alone unattended. Just for the sake of argument and whimsical fancy, say you did make it that far and actually avoided getting too near a Nihil, or one that just happens to open up directly beneath you, just because they do that sort of thing. All you have to look forward to is a lonely existence with your own wretched hate filled and manipulative Shadow slowly eating away at your sanity until it completely turns you into an evil life hating POS. Your list of options disappearing quicker than a dollar bill between a strippers ass cheeks, you somehow, by the grace of the god (the same one that probably made this place, or in the very least let it happen by the way), find a Pardoner. Not that you would know what that is or what they do, but what do you think the odds are she would actually help someone she does not know that can't offer her anything worth a rabid goats afterbirth in trade except going back to the previous problem of slavery and/or being turned into a Shadowland monsters favorite sex toy, when her own Guild and the service she can provide would get her ass in serious trouble with the Constabulary just by admitting she can do it. You think she would chance that? I am damn sure she would not in the cutthroat environment the world of Wraith provides.

    For entertainment sake lets just say there is the strikingly unlikelihood that you could find a Renegade that will not add you to his of soulsteal forged My Little Pony collection, and considers to let you join them. Again, you still have absolutely nothing to bargain with, or offer, not even a stitch of fucking clothing on your back in some cases. You are more likely to get thrown into a Nihil by Gunnery Sergeant Hartman while informing you not only are you a queer, but a steer to boot. One way or another you end up Harrowing and it is more than fair to say that a character surviving this long under those circumstances would be a considered a miracle. That is when you discover that you just to come back and have the pleasure of doing it all over again only a little quicker this time because your Shadow just gained itself a little more power.

    That all said, I do like me some horror, personal or otherwise, but even I have my limits. This would be more like being subjected to a 168 hour nonstop marathon of looped back to back screenings of .Requiem for a Dream., .The Road. and .The Human Centipede. sprinkled with Justin Bieber videos and interviews. But mostly it is the split in Arcanoi you have to deal with as a player that I really don't like. And not many of them are all that special or cool enough that I would want to play because the super powers are fun to use and experiment with. Essentially the vast separation of Arcanoi between what is useful in the Dead Lands and what is useful in the Prim Material have a very obvious line that makes a campaign set solely in the Dead Lands a lot less exciting when any concept I can think of for said campaign could be applied to another game that has better options for useful super powers. Quiet honestly, I play games to personalize something that I am not capable of doing and using my imagination and puzzle solving abilities to solve presented problems utilizing those powers to their fullest advantage. With Wraith it just feels too striped down and too often not accessible.

          EDIT: I've appended the review to my OP since it is easy to miss.

          I’m not talking about the being dead part or even the Shadow (both of those things are manageable despite the bad rap), but the fact that the people and institutions look like they came from Warhammer 40K. Pretty much everyone will try to enslave, sell and soulforge you at the first opportunity, you can be executed for simply knowing half the arcanoi in the game (and you carry obvious tell-tales), and pretty much “your afterlifespan is measured in minutes.” Assuming the troupe ignores or severely downplays that, there’s a sharp divide in the game’s already indistinct focus: is the game about a ghost dealing with the living, or about a ghost dealing with the bizarre afterlife? There’s no real connection between the two and you will be executed for trying to associate with the living.

          The game has a very, very good idea at its core, but the execution is far too extreme and loses sight of the original point. You’re dead, so obviously trying to deal with the living with be fraught will problems, but it’s absurdly cruel to create a horrible, malevolent afterlife society that actively discourages you from pursuing your unfinished business.
