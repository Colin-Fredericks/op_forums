        Deep and personal, revolving around resolving Passions and Fetters.

        Survival in the Shadowlands from slavers, spectres and other threats.

        High epics, looking for Charon and following the meta-plot.

        Religious and philosophical game with the Heretics as protagonists.
        Crossover with one or more other cWoD lines. Mage and Vampire are especially suited for this.
        Day to day Shadowlands stuff, as low-ranking members of the Hierarchy.
        War! Either the Great War or the Stygia/Jade conflict.

        There posibilities are literally endless.

                        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                        So as another couple of questions that spring up from my casual reading of on-line sources about Wraith.

                        1) If you are a Wraith that finds itself in the Skinlands in an immaterial state, how easy is it for you to interact with the material world?

                        To expand on this a bit, is it made easier by the intervention/involvement of other supernaturals? Can Mages with Spirit and Vampires with Necromancy maybe help you out? Just thinking of possible points of "cross-over" that might facilitate both PC and NPC actions and plots.
                        I'm not exactly sure what you are asking here, in the first half of the question. The Shadowlands and the Skinlands are like reflections of one another, so in a sense wraiths are always in the Skinlands in an immaterial state. A wraith can see and hear anything that occurs in the Skinlands, but remains invisible and intangible to mortals and other SKinlands creatures (unless some magic is involved).

                        Any wraith can pass through solid Skinlands objects by spending a point of Corpus, passing through doors, walls and even people with minimal effort and discomfort.
                        Actually manifesting in the SKinlands is tougher, though, and requires Arkanoi. The Outrage, Inhabit, Puppetry and Embody Arkanoi enable wraiths to affect the Skinlands in different ways. Pandemonium and Flux also allows a wraith to affect the Skinlands.
                        With Embody, a wraith can push themselves through the Shroud and manifest fully in the Skinlands. Inhabit and Puppetry allow a wraith to posess objects or people respectively, while Outrage allows a wraith to manipulate objects in the Skinlands crudely.

                        As for other supernaturals, it varies. There is little a mage cannot accomplish with enough preparation and knowledge, and allowing wraiths to manifest in the Skinlands is certainly within their power. There is also a Necromancy Ritual that lowers the Shroud, which can help wraiths with the correct Arkanoi to manifest.

                        2) How jarring is the transition of game themes and type if you start off playing Wraith very much involved with the Skinland stuff and then get sucked down into the Underworld? Does it really, really alter the game? Is the tonal/thematic change kinda jarring?

                        Going form an urban modern horror game to a sorta dark fantasy setting and all. A lot of people I know like to roleplay in the WoD precisely because its urban and modern and just basically our world. So their is no forced parlance or anything to dilute a player/characters interaction and reaction to the world. I' kinda wondering how Wraith might get around that while still involving Underworld stuff.
                        The transition doesn't need to be jarring at all. It can be as simple as a shift in focus, like an evolving Tv show. Look at the show SUpernatural, for example, and how it slowly grows in scope; from a show about two brothers hunting ghosts, it slowly grows into an apocalyptic showdown between heaven and hell. A Wraith chronicle can grow in the same way, from close and personal to broad and sweeping, with higher and higher stakes as time moves on.

                        Urban horror elements don't have to go away entirely. Since the Shadowlands mirror the Skinlands almost exactly, you will always be wandering the streets of New York (as an example), even though the city will look decaying and rotten, and there may be some details which remind you that you are in the Shadowlands (such as destroyed buildings that still stand, and wraithly alterations to the skyline built with soulsteel).

                        3) What's up with the Neverborn and Grandma?

                        The latter seems to have been added in Orpheus for the purposes of providing an even bigger "big bad" above the Neverborn. Is she suppose to be their mum or something? Does anyone know how she might be setting worked in Wr20?

                        The former seem interesting, however, if their all "dead but dreaming" (I get a serious Lovecraft vibe of these guys, in a good way) in the Wraith setting how much impact do they have on said setting? How can they impact the world short of waking up and causing obvious doomsday plot?
                        I haven't read the entire Orpheus line, so I don't know much about Grandma, but I can tell you about the Neverborn. The Neverborn are Lovecraftian entities who arose from the darkness before the world was created, and who dwell sleeping at the edge of Oblivion deep within the Labyrinth.
                        Though they slumber, they still make their will known to their spectre priests and attendants, who obey their dreaming commands. As antagonists they serve the purpose of dreadful presences behind the scenes, who combat existence through their spectral servants. All the advice in the books says to keep them behind the scnes, and I think thats good advice. You don't kill Cthulhu in a Call of Cthulhu campaign, and the same holds true for the Neverborn.

                        4) So Wraiths from other Dark Kingdoms have different souls based on cultural beliefs basically?

                        Does this also tie into the fact they get different ghostly powers? I noticed the 185K stretch goal talks about the Jade kingdom getting different powers. Are they alternate levels of the same powers or are they brand new power types?
                        Wraiths are wraiths no matter what Dark Kingdom they come from. The Jade Empire wraiths aren't different from Stygian wraiths the way Kuei-Jin are from Kindred. They do have access to unique Arkanoi, though, which have arisen due to special circumstances in their culture and history, which are rare or unheard of in Stygia. The Jade Empire has three or four (can't remember right now) unique Arkanoi that reflect the cultural differences between the Jade Empire and Stygia in terms of how thei view and treat their Shadows, Spectres and soulforging and soulsteel.

                                                        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                        2) Is a "multi-Kingdom" type player troupe viable for a Wraith game? If so how unusually/special-snowflake like is it? What sort of Wraith stories can be told by a multi-Kingdom game that might not be possible in a straight Stygia game?
                                                        It's an option in Wraith: the Great War, in the context of wraiths who'd normally fall under other Dark Kingdoms ending up in Stygian territory thanks to the First World War. You have the Guilds hunting down wraiths from other Kingdoms for the secrets of their Arcanoi (because they're unaware that learning such Arcanoi is based on soul structure), and many of the wraiths in question trying to find a way back home through the Fourth Great Maelstrom.

                                                        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                        3) Can someone give a players point of view on what a Dark Arcanoi is? How does possessing such a power alter the Wraith? What is the cost? What's the social sigma attached to knowing such powers?

                                                        If anyone needs me to provide more context for these questions I'll be happy to provide such.
                                                        All of the following comes from Doomslayers:

                                                        Normally, Dark Arcanoi are available only to Spectres, but Doomslayers can learn them thanks to their exposure to Spectres and Oblivion, which means any of: a Pact of Doom with the Shadow, learning from a Spectre who knows them, learning from a wraith who knows them, or implants. Learning them from a wraith or Spectre takes on the order of months.

                                                        They cost basically twice as much as do standard Arcanoi, and, if bought during character creation, can only be bought with freebie points.

                                                        Wraiths switch out the Angst and Being costs of Dark Arcanoi for Pathos and Willpower. All dice rolled are considered Shadow Dice (that is, each 1 rolled strengthens the Shadow).

                                                        The Doomslayers want to be very, very sure you know what you're doing with Dark Arcanoi; it requires approval from the higher-up Doomslayers in order to start teaching or learning them. Teaching without authorisation means the Hierarchy and Doomslayers are going to come down on you like a ton of soulsteel bricks.
