        Page 420 of the PDF file:Maleficence 5 Conversion: The entire write up is confusing in regards to Spectres. Under the 20th edition rules, Spectres have a Willpower score and a permanent Pathos score that represents the strength of the Psyche. Conversion as describes allows for the lowering of Willpower or the raising of Permanent Angst. It calls out Spectres in the description. Spectres already have a permanent Angst of 10. As written this power can only lower Spectres Willpower. Reading the description, it seems as if it should be able to permanently lower Specte Pathos rating.

        This is more of a clarification suggestion. If the intent of Conversion is to affect Wraiths, or lower the Willpower of Spectres, then slight wording would simplify this. If the intent of Conversion is to also be able to lower the permanent Pathos rating of a spectre's Psyche, this to needs to be clarified.

        The intent of the art on Wraiths is obvious, create Drones or Spectres. WIth Spectre's; can this ability weaken a Spectre's better half, or is it just meant to make them have less Willpower?

                p56-57 "The Seat of Shadows, home of the Iron Legion and its Ashen Lady, rests on the northernmost of the “seven hills” along the Road of Lords, and it sets the tone for the whole district. Each morning it appears in the dawn light as a beautiful, darkly gleaming grove of soulsteel and Veinous Stair stone spires, rising almost as high as Charon’s own Onyx Tower. As the day passes, it seems to age and crumble, and at night it’s a tangle of ruins punctuated by only a few usable halls and chambers. Then dawn comes and it renews again. Many buildings in Ironville do the same, out of a shared emotional resonance."

                There is dawn light and evening darkness in Stygia? In what sense is the Sunless Sea sunless, then?

                p154 "Hung outside a building, the lantern advertises a Pardoner’s services; carried by her it illuminates his way in the darkest of places. Each lantern’s unique to the Pardoner who owns it, for she either creates it herself as part of his “apprenticeship” (or, more rarely, gets it from a master who knows him well), but most Pardoners favor traditional designs with baroque, macabre adornments."

                The pronouns are switching within the same phrase, yet refer to the same individual. Highly confusing. In addition, the use of a parenthetical phrase clashes with the "either". You can't use "either" to contrast a pair of items in a sentence, and then put the second item inside parentheses, or else the reader will look for the second item after the end of the parenthetical. If it's important enough to be connected to the "either", it should stand on its own and not be slipped into a parenthetical aside. Finally, the "but most Pardoners..." clause should probably be in its own sentence. This whole passage could use a rewrite to be comprehended on a first reading.

                    This was addressed in another thread, but so far no one has posted here as an errata, and I suppose this needs at least a second look at: p. 56-57, Ironville section, speaks of "dawn light" illuminating the Seat of Shadows each morning.

There is no sunlight (or even natural occurring light) in Stygia, the Tempest, or bellow/beyond. Stygia itself counts hours by sea tides and water clocks. The closest there is to natural illumination is light from the Shadowlands coming from Nihils appearing as stars on the sky.

                    It would be better to remove the reference to light and state that the Seat of Shadows simply seems to grow older with each cycle os Stygian time.

                                                        Gonna add my list so far, so I have at least something up when this thread closes:

                                                        Pg. xli: "The spirit clings to its past, never quite trusting and strong enough to let go." Really, really weird phrasing here, to the point that I'm not entirely sure what it's trying to say. It's not even poetic or archaic like much of the intro fiction. Just... weird. I guess it could work as needing both of those traits, but it still sounds decidedly "off."

                                                        Pg. 17: Chapter 10 is listed as being titled "Adversaries," and containing both Spectres and other creatures of the Underworld. In the book as released, it's simply titled "Spectres" and is solely about Spectres, with other denizens being in Chapter 12.

                                                        Pg. 30: "As their first act, the Senate granted lands to Shining Ones near the docks of the Shining Sea." There is no mention of a "Shining Sea" elsewhere in the book. It's most likely a mistype of "Sunless Sea."

                                                        Pg. 49: "They are her, the reasons she feels compelled to continue to defy death." Now, something definitely went wrong here, but there's a few possibilities. It could be missing a word after "her" that would make this sentence in full read properly. It could be missing emphasis on "are" that makes it clear that it is, in fact, stating that a Wraith is their Passions. It could just be a bit of weird phrasing that sounds off.

                                                        "Fetters are the things she feels most strongly about. They may be things she loved in life: living relatives and loved ones, places where treasured events happened, such as the tools she used in satisfying work." In this passage, "Such as" should be "or." Tools are not places, and can't be examples of them, which is what "such as" would mean in this context. Even if that were correct, there'd be a missing "or" before "living."

                                                        Pg 54, though for context I'm including some text from Pg 51, the error is on 54: "In some places, the less formal and direct roads keep to older, rougher ways; in others, they tunnel over or even pass along the top of the official route." This should almost definitely be "tunnel under." There's no mention of anything over the Road of Lords to tunnel through, and even if there were, this would effectively be saying that some paths go above it twice.

                                                        Pg. 54, "An Isle of Legacies" sidebar: "Stygia is a city where the continued memorial interest in the living matters intensely..." This should be of the living, as the full sentence makes it clear it's not about people memorializing the living (which is weird), but about the living's memorial interest in certain buildings.

                                                        Page 69: "It was they who brought Stygia to the table to establish the Covenant of the Millions..." The Covenant of the Millions is mentioned as though it's something readers either should already recognize or will be informed about later -- something of significance that will itself reveal how significant the Ferrymen's role actually is... but it's never mentioned anywhere else in the book.
