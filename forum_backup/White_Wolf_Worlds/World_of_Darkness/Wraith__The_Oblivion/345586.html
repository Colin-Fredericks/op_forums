    Shadowguiding and Harrowing in W20: Questions from a potential player

    12-20-2014, 05:12 AM
    Hello,

    This is my first post here. I used to play lots of RPG when I was younger and Vampire was my favorite. However, I have kept hearing good things about Wraith but never read or tried the game. I now see there is a kickstarter for a new edition of Wraith, which has caught my interest.

    Reading reviews of the old 2e Wraith it seems like the overwhelming majority of them stresses how depressing the game is (and bleak, and dark, etc). I did want to take a look for myself though, and got hold of the pdf for the 2e and have read that from cover to cover. While I can see were the bleaknessand horror comments might come from (the art with the secretary with fleshhooks comes to mind...I like the more suggestive art), I also see a very deep and engaging world full of interesting opertunities for adventure and fun games.

    The two sections, while interesting, were I see my group might run into problems is the shadowguiding and harrowing sections as I get a somehwat antagonistic vibe (I don't want my players to start throwing the cutlery). Will the new rule sinclude options for lessening the more antagonistic/evil aspects of shadow and harrowing while still keeping them as mechanics? I see some allusions to this in the feature list of the kickstarter but more information before pledging would be nice. Will there be a sample chapter released later in the kickstarter for those of us who are on the fence?
    Tags: None

      There's the Shadow Player's Guide and The Sea of Shadows books. Both add onto the material on Shadowguiding and Harrowings respectively. Undoubtedly some of that knowledge will find its way into Wr20 and Dansky has mentioned that they'll make it a little more accessible. But until its released or they give us some more previews on the material I can't say.

      I will say this though, WtO may seem like the bleakest, darkest, most depressing game of the OWoD but beneath that outer layer there are themes of spirituality and hope. In fact of all the lines its the only one that has a definable end goal in the Rulebook. No matter how powerful a vampire becomes there's always a bigger vampire out there. No matter how many Wyrm minions and Weaver slaves your werewolf destroys there's always more out there coming from you. But in WtO you've died and become stuck in a bad situation yet despite all that you have a means to escape, by overcoming your characters baggage, their demons, etc.

      Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. They're indomitable. Indomitable.

                      You could pinch the Kindred of the East style of 'Shadowguiding', if it's too dark for your group. Basically, represent the Shadowguiding with contested rolls between Shadow and Psyche. Likewise, Harrowings could be a series of contested rolls, or just one (but that puts too much emphasis on luck, given the results of a failure or a botch).
