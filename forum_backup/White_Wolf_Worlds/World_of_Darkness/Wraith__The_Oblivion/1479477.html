    Suggestions for a game of Wraith that is also a murder mystery

    04-25-2022, 05:31 AM
    I was thinkering with the idea of introducing Wraith to my friends with a scenario with elements of murder mystery.
    The victims of the murder are the characters, or their death is somewhat involved with the murder even just by accident

    My main issue currently is why the characters should want to solve the mystery at all. I mean, the murder isn't what keeps them to transcend. It's not their unresolved issue from their life
    But I'm very open to suggestions on other matters too, because I'm very inexperienced with this

    I should add that I am not too fond of realism, so I'm ok with stuff that "would work only in a movie but not in real life"

    P.S. I have a lot of experience in running tabletop rpgs, but I have zero experience with Wraith even as player character. Currently watching a game on youtube (Echoes of New York) and also reading a lot from the books. I'm having a blast btw, this game is way more amazing than I ever thought

      That sounds like a fun premise. I'm curious, though: You say, "The murder isn't what keeps them to transcend. It's not their unresolved issue from their life." But why *couldn't* it be the issue--or one of them, the one they all share--that they must resolve as part of their path toward fulfillment and meaning as a ghost (and perhaps Transcendence)? I'll go further: how could it NOT be the issue?

      A common piece if advice to Wraith STs is to have the PCs all share something that ties their goals together: a shared Fetter, shared Passion, shared manner of death (and by extension, shared Legion), or shared Reaper/Mentor. Creating the party around some shared metaphysical or NPC feature is a great way of giving the characters a reason to stick together and drive toward the same goal (solving their own murder, for instance) while they are still getting their ghost feet under themselves and learning the ropes of wraith existence. Beyond that practical reason, it's worth remembering that only souls who are extraordinarily restless and driven become ghosts; the others never end up in the Underworld or stay only briefly. I imagine that anyone who was murdered, or whose death was incidentally caused by a murder, and who yet clings to existence, probably has a deep-seated desire to understand why they died, who dunnit, and to either influence events from beyond the mortal veil, or come to some new understanding about what their lives or deaths mean so they can move on.

      I say, embrace the murder mystery. They are the victims, or the collateral damage of a murder attempt, and until they figure out what happened, how their loved ones have responded to their passing, and decide how to handle it all, they will not be satisfied, they will be Restless.

          Thank you for the answer.
          The idea was that the murder could indeed be tied to one of the PCs, but maybe it turns out that way later.
          As how could NOT be the issue, my idea was that as you said yourself the other might have been collateral damage
          My idea was something like a plane crashing or a bus falling in the river or some other very unfortunate happenings that kill a lot of people. A fire in the building maybe.
          But then some hint will be given that the fire was started intentionally to kill someone specific and made it look like an accident. Some kind of conspiracy akin to a spy movie or such thing maybe, or a mobster killing another mobster. Or some other melodramatic murder mystery.
          I'd prefer not to ground the story too much to current reality

          In searching inspiration in the last days I found an actual play called Echoes of New York.
          It was quite great, and with a few familiar faces from L.A. by Night too.
          What surprised me was the big fun they all had while being actually so mean to each other
          And what I thought about it is they could do so because their characters didn't hit near home.
          Two characters were 12 years old Titanic victims, one was a murdered woman from the 50s, one a pilgrim lady from 300 years ago and one was a "not definitely Tony Stark". So they really were characters distant from their real selves.

          My players are familiar with D&D and Call of Cthulhu.
          I don't want to traumatize them, but I think they will like Wraith if the game will be handled in a certain way.
          And my conclusion is to encourage them to play characters that are very much detached from themselves: a mobster, a world-famous surgeon, a spy, the city's greatest detective. Such things.
          That way their first Wraith game will hit less near home.
          It's still a game about death in a personal way, and we're old enough to already had our share of death.

          I'm happy for your suggestions, and also kinda proud of myself for having already thought some of the things you listed.

          My basic idea right now is that the players are a much diverse cast of melodramatic individuals with very strong personal goals (unresolved matters) that are all on a train and then the wagon explodes and they are all dead. In the shadowlands they meet a slaver first things first, and they must try to save themselves. Wether they are able to save themselves or they fail to do so, a possible mentor figure (and saviour if needs be) arrives and explains some of the whats and what nots. Then they get separated by the mentor from further dangers and then let's say what we could call the prologue ends

              I've actually pulled this off once... as a one-shot where the mystery was central to the different character's achieving Transcendence (it was also the early 00s when we knew the WoD was ending, so I was running a small-scale End Times game with it).

              The group actually really enjoyed the mystery aspects, and the "who hit their Transcendence goals" aspects... especially because the original murderer's Wraith (his Shadow was manipulating a copy cat killer and part of the mystery was how the new killer knew things that nobody alive could have known) was one of the ones that managed to Transcend.

              I'm honestly not sure how to suggest expanding this out to a longer game. Like a short 5-6 session game could have given me more time to flush out details and make the investigation deeper, but the time pressure in-character with the Sixth Maelstrom brewing, and out-of-character of only having one night to run it helped ensure we had a strong mutual drive to keep the pacing tight.

              Starting with a murder-mystery as the first part of a longer game can work, but then I think you really need to figure out something else for the idea of "why these Wraiths came together in the first place," that's going to persist past the mystery itself.

              Also, if you've got a decent number of CoC players... they can probably handle some darker stuff. CoC might focus on cosmic horror and all that, but CoC games aren't afraid of hitting close to home too get the PCs involved.

                      So, I set the original murder in a small town sort of setting rather than a big city. The PCs were almost all residents of that town, so there was a lot of built in interconnection between the wraiths. The PCs ranged significantly in terms of how involved they were in the murders, vs.it being part of their lives because small towns with a murder spree touches everyone. As a one-shot, they were broad pre-gens that I let players fill in details about as a group to some extent. So one of them was "Best friend of one of the victims," who was otherwise just a normal high-school student who died in a car accident. I had a selection of things to inspire them, but the player picked that her character was secretly cheating on her best friend with her boyfriend, which lead her to become an alcoholic which lead to the accident. She also worked with the "Small town cop in charge of the case but in over his head," player to establish that one of his secrets was that he falsified her cause of death to leave out she was drunk because he felt he failed the community by not bringing the killer to justice.

                      The "Big city reporter that came to the small town for a big scoop," had accidentally leaked information that lead to the, "Overly protective parent worried about their children being next," to the killer and executed him because she didn't trust the police.

                      So there was this huge almost Christie-esque level of interconnected layers between the PCs. Everyone had secrets, and a lot of the Passions and Fetters were related to those. They might all go back to a series of murders, but many weren't abut the murders directly. There was also some good drama that came from one wraith wanting the truth of what they did to finally come to light, and others that felt that the world was better with the story they'd all played a part in shaping into what the living world believed happened.

                      And mostly I just had a small pile of index cards for each pre-gen, that added a bit of a "lifepath" style way of expanding them. "Your character did X, pick card 1, 2, or 3 for how they felt about it, and follow the directions," was how they all got their Passions and Fetters finalized.
