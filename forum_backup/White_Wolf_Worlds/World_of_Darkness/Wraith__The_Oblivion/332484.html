    To the guys who have played Wraith: Lets teach others how to play Wraith!

    12-06-2014, 05:04 PM
    One of the first things most cWoD gamers will say when the topic of Wraith comes up is "Yeah, it's a cool idea but it's unplayable."

    Now, I know that's not true. I have played and enjoyed Wraith on many occasions and had buckets of fun. But still, it's the impression many people have of this beautiful game.

    So, now that Wr20 is a reality and Wraith will have it's rennaissance, I feel the time has come for those of us in the community to show everyone who is even slightly interested in Wraith that the game is not only playable, it's brilliant. I know many people will back and/or buy the book for completions sake, having invested in all the other books as well, and leave it on their shelf to gather dust and moan softly in the night, when they could have actually played the game and had some of the best roleplaying experiences of their lives!

    So let's do this!

    To the people who have gotten the impression that the game is too difficult to play, to demanding or even downright unplayable, come here and ask your questions. I (and hopefully others who have played and loved Wraith) will answer to the best of my ability.

    To the guys out there who have played and enjoyed Wraith, join me! Let's tell people just how great an experience it can be!

    First of all, I feel I need to adress the Shadowguide. I have seen a lot of people call the system broken, unusable and unplayable. Two players per character? How could that possibly work?

    In my experience, it creates awesome roleplaying opportunities. I think some players get stuck in a competitive mindset when it comes to Shadowguiding. They read the rules and think "ok, so I'm supposed to work against this guy and be an asshole to him. How is that fun?" It doesn't have to be a competition though, and ideally it never should. Shadowguiding isn't about messing up a player character, it's about helping the player to tell awesome stories about his character! You get to be a part of a two man team whose job it is to put focus on the issues that really matter to that character. The mechanics will help you do this. Passions and Dark Passions, Fetters and Thorns, all these things create a vortex of opportunity to tell amazing, deep and passionate stories about a ghost with strong and emotional ties to the world of the living.
    The key part is to go into it with these expectations in mind. Being a Shadowguide is not about being an asshole, it's about helping the player bring out the best and worst in his character.

                      But enough about me! I think I owe this thread some on-topic stuff.

                      To me, Wraith works best as a psychodrama, and that means you need tragic protagonists. I like to build from the flaws on up for my characters. I had a player come up with a gal who lived in an abusive relationship, and never had the strength to get out of it before she died. Her disgust with who she was meant quickly found a love for Moliate (in a desperate attempt to change herself externally, in the hopes of fixing the person inside) and Castigate (so as to help others make sure they never end up as weak as she was). Most of her Dark Passions were fixated on her still-living abuser (a confusing jumble of wanting to hurt him back and to try and redeem him) while her Passions revolved around the concept of moving on from him and finding new love. That's a damn good character right there, built from "I wasn't strong enough."

                      There's a funny expression I've heard a few times that I really like: "Find someone whose inner demons get along with your own." I take that as gospel for Wraith troupes, and encourage folks to make characters together. Don't go with anything so blatant as "we were friends/co-workers/whatever" in real-life (the Underworld is far too lonely for such happy accidents), but make your flaws work together. Say you have a father who helps other people with a self-destructive lack of regard for his own well-being and a cheerleader who starved herself in a desperate attempt to earn validation from someone, anyone; those two have the makings of an unhealthy bond that will keep them together (and cause lots of drama!) even if the plot loses steam. You don't just need unhealthy codependency; people with similar experiences and traumas are likely to band together just because having someone else who understands is a rare blessing.

                      As for running the game itself, I always advocate for running fast and loose with the rules, and tailoring your experience to the players' wishes. My idea of Wraith doesn't appeal to many, but my group was more than happy to have some fun with it as "horror game with the added novelty of already being dead." We've also tossed around "the Underworld as a means to explore all the lost civilizations of history," which is awesome and has little angst at all! Find something that works for everyone, and always remember that your end goal is fun.

                      Remi. she/her. game designer.

                              .
                              Similarly, in one of the Wraith games I ran, one of the characters was transgendered. She (identified as fully female) also immediately (and obviously) gravitated towards Moliate. In some ways, her death allowed her to fulfill her lifetime desires, in a sort of post hoc way. Her Shadows was the "Be a Man" identification she had struggled against her entire life. The dance between the two was very compelling (especially since, as stated, Moliate was front and center on the table). There was also an Artifact in play that allowed interesting juxtapositions of self-identity. (I put that in before the characters were made. It was synchronicity that the story of the Artifact and the story of the character were dramatically nearly perfect for each other). The character could have been an offensive disaster of stereotyping and misunderstanding, but it was handled very well by both the player and the Shadowguide. Probably the best two at those interactions at the table.

                              TBH, (and to get to the topic), I don't know of many other games that could have even begun to handle that storyline as well as Wraith. The setting and rules really enabled the kind of exploration of those ideas that just wouldn't have been as fully realized elsewhere. I guess we could tackle the same issues in a Mage game, but it would have come across very differently. Probably something more along a mystic union of opposites, a creation of a rebis entity.... Very cool but not really intrinsically as personal or visceral.

                                          Why is this character going into catharsis all the time? What is she/he doing to wrack up so much Angst? Catharsis doesn't happen all that often unless the character is somehow wracking up Angst. Considering how far apart the Shadow and the Psyche are with this character, what is this brutal nasty hateful Shadow offering that is so enticing to this nurturing, loving, beacon of hope Psyche? Catharsis and Harrowing are important to the game but they aren't something that happens every session by any means. And that's not "every session for this one character" that's "not every session for the entire circle of wraiths."

                                          Also, the Shadows doesn't have to be such a complete polar opposite. In fact, it doesn't have to have anything to do with how the Psyche's take on the afterlife. They just both have to have something to do with the person who they both were before the death and the Shadow has to have a more nihilistic bent than that Psyche. (Probably. There are always exceptions. A Psyche could be a right bastard while the Shadow might be a great drinking buddy, but that's more a special snowflake situation.) This character could just as easily have a smarmy salesman Shadow (Soooo... We healed him, now, what's he gonna do for us?). The Shadow could be terrified child (No! No! No! Don't risk us by going into the Tempest just to HELP him! The Tempest is scaaaarey! And I'm sure someone else can help him, it doesn't HAVE to be you...). The Shadow could be psychoanalyzing the Psyche all the time. (Now we both know why we're going to help him and it doesn't really have anything to do with you being altruistic, does it? It's all about the fact that you couldn't save you little brother from his heroin addiction... When are you going to stop wasting time trying to help people who don't realy need your help and deal with the REAL issues....). It could be an "I told you so" type that waits until after the good deed to pounce on some negative outcome and grind a sense of failure into the Psyche. Etc. Etc.

                                          This also seems to assume that the Shadowguide is going to be acting in a very hostile manner against the Psyche's player. Not in a "we're all here to play a game to have fun" way, but in a "ha! ha! ha! I now have the power to make your character suck and make sure you have a miserable time during the session! Because that's how I get my jollies!" I know players of the second ilk are out there, but if that's your group.... you might want to think of playing something other than Wraith. Honestly, if that's even ONE person in your group, you might want to wait on it.

                                                      One thing I cannot stress enough: the Shadow is not your evil twin. It is not your exact opposite. The Shadow is the part of yourself you hate, the things you don't admit to having. Your Shadow is a self-destructive pile of repression and shame, every impulse and cruel thought you work so hard to keep down. Catharsis doesn't have to be an exercise in mustache-twirling, puppy-kicking villainy.

                                                      But these things work better as examples. Say a Wraith has Protect My Wife (Love) as one of his primary Passions, looking out for his darling across the veil. A case could easily be made for Keep My Wife Loyal (Greed) as a Dark Passion; though he'd never admit it, our hypothetical fellow would hate for his wife to move on from him and ever love again. Let's say he spots his darling going on a date with a nice young man; his Shadow whispers to him about how this guy looks dangerous, he uses some fun Arcanoi to write a bloody message in the mirror when her date goes to the bathroom, and he scares away the suitor while doing what he thinks fulfills one of his Passions. The Shadow is insidious like that; he makes you do what you don't like to admit you want, with self-destruction as the eventual goal.

                                                          Originally posted by Laughing Lord View Post

                                                          My understanding how the shadow would be something like this. Say I have a character who is a complete saint (caregiver nature) and works with his castigate and argos to provide glimmer of light for other spirits. The shadow would then be a brute out to do the exact opposite, a bully out to hurt others and cause pain.
                                                          Honestly, that kind of psyche/shadow pairing is somewhat unlikely. What you should be looking for is not the opposite of a given emotion, but the dark side of that same emotion. For instance, the Psyche may believe that he helps people because of Altruism, Compassion, or Penitence...and he is right, partially. However for the Shadow, this may be a matter of pride (Seek Praise from others for Being Such a Swell Guy), manipulation (Help Others So They Feel Obligated to Me), or denial (Redirect Blame By Faking a Nice Guy Persona). The psyche would never admit he does good things purely to satisfy his own ego, and thus would shove these things into the province of the Shadow.

                                                          A good Psyche for a brutish Shadow is likely a character who does use violence, but normally would use it towaards a "higher cause": a soldier in service to patriotism, a policeman in service to justice, or a gang member in service to loyalty. They won't want to admit to themselves that they also engage in violence because of rage, racism, despair, or violence for it's own sake, and thus repress it to their Shadows.
