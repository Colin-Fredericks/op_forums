                                        Well the way I see it Deathmarks, at least those involving how they died, reflecting the Legion character images in Wraith: the Great War.

                                        Which means that four of them can be more easily spotted.

                                        Skeletal Legion: gaunt and sickly, signs of the sickness that killed them

                                        Grim Legion: marks that represent their fatal wounds

                                        Iron Legion: physical apprentice aged to an exaggerated extent

                                        Emerald Legion: same as the Grim

                                        If you look at those four Legion characters from WtGW they all represent that to some form or another. The face of the Skeletal Legionnaire looks gaunt, with sunken cheeks. The Iron Legionnaire has cobwebs clinging to his body like some antique left in an attic to collect dust and cobwebs. The Grim man has visible bullet holes while the Emerald Legionnaire has been very clearly run over by a car.

                                        Added to this is the Legion of Fate who bare the marks of Fate in addition to whatever other signs of death they may have. Unfortunately WtGW doesn't really do good job in illustrating this as the image of the LoF is a pilot who's been virtually cut in two. However there is an earlier fluff bit in the book with a LoF member remembering his death and reaping by another LoF member, describing a symbol on his arm that sounds like the LoF symbol.

                                        The remaining three, Paupers, Penitent and Silent are a little harder to spot as they involve mystery, madness and despair. The Penitent of WtGW is a nurse with a number of stab wounds in her chest, implying that she worked at an asylum and was stabbed to death by a patient. The Silent is a particularly sad and pathetic looking soldier with no visible signs of how he killed himself. Finally the Pauper is a female factory worker, again no visible signs of what killed her though that might just be the point. Plus I know a bit about those times and what conditions in those factories were like back then and there are a number of things that could have killed her.

                                                I interpret Arcanoi as the development and refinement of different aspects of wraithly nature, and as such all of them are the expression of innate abilities of wraiths that can be mastered with enough trial and effort. They are not the proprietary knowledge of any Guild or Dark Kingdom, although to have link with the factions that have specialized in them can certainly make their development easier.

                                                I have strong objections to limiting Jade Arcanoi to Asian wraiths, for various reasons. There are various cases certain Stygian and Jade Arcanoi (e.g. the Castigate, Way of the Soul, and Way of the Farmer combo; the Keening, Intimate, and Way of the Scholar combo) synergize so well for their purposes that I expect the wraiths in Stygia and the Yellow Springs alike that really care for mastering fields like mental manipulation or Shadow/P'o control would strive to learn all of them. The Dark Kingdoms of Iron and Jade (and Western and East Asian civilizations) have been sharing contacts for a long time and with things like Pact of Doom and the Hive-Mind cultural barriers to exchange of knowledge do not matter that much for the dead anyway.

                                                Moreover and more importantly, since East Asian wraiths are told to know almost all Stygian Arcanoi, I assume it is only absolutely fair and necessary to give Stygian wraiths similar access to Jade Arcanoi and to suppose something similar to the global spread of Eastern martial arts took place. I care very little about political correctness issues and I'm proud of it but the notion of East Asian, African, or Indian wraiths having free access to Western or "common" mystical insights and being able to develop and hoard their own special arts rubs me seriously wrong in several ways.

                                                Last but not least, the Stygian and Yellow Springs notions of the soul seem so alike as to make the conclusion necessary the Psyche and Shadow, Hun and P'o, systems are petty much the same thing with different names and work the same way, and hence powers that target them are essentially compatible. Even Kuei-Jin basically seem a different type of Risen more than anything else. Therefore, I do not discern any serious metaphysical obstacles to a Stygian or Yellow Springs wraith mixing Stygian and Jade Arcanoi, not any more so than studying different Arcanoi in the first place. Chains of the Emperor, of course, may be a special exception and rather more difficult to learn for obvious reasons (lack of teachers). Things may or may not be different for other Dark Kingdoms arts, I have paid much less attention to Great War stuff, but probably they are not, since IIRC the two-part organization of the soul seems more or less universal for all modern Dark Kingdoms.

                                                Dark Arcanoi would not make much of a difference in this regard, apart from the fact I've more difficulty to understand why wraiths would be so interested in learning most Dark Arcanoi at all, although Doomslayers are told to be often so in their book. Most of them seem of exclusive use to Spectres or normal Arcanoi may do the same thing better for wraiths. The relevant exceptions seem Hive-Mind (it seems exceedingly useful for Doomslayers pulling espionage missions in the Labyrinth) and Larceny (any wraith may be potentially interested in a way to stave off Oblivion damage to Corpus). To a lesser degree, I could see a few Harbingers and Spooks of a martial bent being potentially interested in learning Tempestos.
