    My Primary Problem With Wraith; or, "Why do Asians have different souls than us?"

    12-18-2014, 09:19 PM
    I believe the Deadguy has explained the reason behind the various Dark Kingdoms having different soul structures for the wraiths who die within them is based in cultural identifiers in the world of the living, which is fairly shaky logic but makes some degree of sense, especially in the context of consensual reality that the oWoD partially operates off of. Where that falls apart is that the Psyche/Shadow structure is a creation of the Wraith writing team with no particular significance in Western Europe and the United States, while everywhere else in the world has unique set-ups apparently based in their living counterparts (nevermind the fact that Africa and Asia are somehow monolithic enough to have one soul type cover their massively diverse populations).

    My question is then this; why is the Psyche/Shadow structure not universal? And failing that, why isn't the West characterized by something with clearer parallels in the thinking of the living?

    Remi. she/her. game designer.

          It's not a Wraith-centric issue at all.
          The CWoD as a whole was a victim of the 90's fascination with all things Asian.
          Through many game lines we were presented with characters from the East that from a Western perspective were "similar to you but BETTER than you."
          In retrospect it's not only annoying for both sides but sometimes a little bit racist.
          But it was the 90's and stuff happened.
          It had very little to do with the actual writers and their beliefs. it was simply a sign of the times.
          I have a feeling a lot of this will be addressed as Onyx Path venture further into the 21st century.

          I had a conscience once. Alas, I appear to have misplaced it.

              I think that some the of "The Asian counterparts of X are different" trend in the cWoD also just came out of an enthusiasm to write more material. It's fun and interesting to say "how might this manifest differently?". Grabbing a geographic region and saying "it's different here" gives a writers lots of freedom to create more cool stuff; stuff that might not have fit well in the existing setting, but that still feels related to the core game. Some of the folks at WW might have initially siezed upon Asia for this because it was the 90s. After it had been done once in the WoD, there was a precedent set, so for later books it might have been seen as an established way to write exciting new setting variants.

                                              Something else to remember, especially when discussing the Yellow Springs and Stygia, is that powerful beings mandated how things must be for the restless dead of wide swaths of the mortal world. Qin Shi Huangdi and Charon, respectively, had the power in the crazy days of yore to lay down laws for the dead, and these laws wound up with far more reach than only one nation or one specific group of 'former-mortals' (to coin a term). I mean, that's a very in-game view, but still...

                                              On another note, I choose to believe that all sorts of heretical pockets may exist in the extant Dark Kingdoms, possibly even extending as far as to allow for differing soul structures. These beliefs are likely localised and shared across the Veil, making it possible for something like, say, the Dark Kingdom of Obsidian to still exist in a very small capacity. Know, however, that I base this on some wonky, sometimes outside proper Wraith 'canon' sources (e.g. Mummy: the Resurrection, Exalted: the Abyssals). That said, the World of Darkness is built on the premise that more options remain possible than what we see in any printed books.
