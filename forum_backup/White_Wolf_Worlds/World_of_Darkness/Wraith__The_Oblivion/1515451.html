    Magic in the Underworld

    06-18-2023, 06:44 AM
    I have been thinking about the apparent lack of magic in the underworld lately. It is a realm with strong symbolic functions in which metaphysics are more important than physics, and while trying to employ the scientific method is mostly a futile endeavor, it should make a fine playground for magic instead.

    But we do not see that, at least, overtly. We know all splats lose their Numina after death; it is the big reset button. Mages lose their Avatar, Vampires their Disciplines (including any Blood Sorcery), even mortal Sorcerer Paths and Psychic Phenomena are gone. The only exception I know of are Mummies, who retain command of their Hekau - but they are not Wraiths, and can not learn Arcanoi. Magic itself is not banned from the realm: Workings of the rare Quick visitor or even rarer enchanted Skinlands object work just fine.

    This seems to indicate one of two things: Either Wraiths can not work magic, or Arcanoi are magic under a different name.

    Let's have a look at some classic magic disciplines (lower case d):

    Astrology is impossible. The sky in the Underworlds is a blank canvas with Nihils taking the place of stars. Mortal practitioners of the Starlight Path report seeing a different sky and are even able to use it for navigation. Unless a Wraith is somehow able to gain that sight, astrology is out.

    Divination on the other hand is very present. It is the bread and butter of Oracles and covered by the Fatalism Arcanos.

    Alchemy exists, but not under that name. Usurers are able to transmute Pathos, Plasm, and Corpus. Artificers and Masquers transmute Corpus via Soulforging and Moliate respectively, and the various 'elements' of the realm can exist in different phases (powdered Angst, liquid Pathos and so on), and even alloyed to achieve new properties. Alchemists (the Guild) do something similar with hardened Memories, aka Relics. Some processes, like a Usurer charging a Soulfire crystal with Pathos, might count as Enchanting.

    Theurgy - as in: the invocation or channeling of external powers - does not seem to be a thing. The Underworld appears to be cut off well enough from whatever realms the invoked entity dwells in, and the only exception is arguably dealings with Spectres and Oblivion, which may count as a form of Infernalism. I will include Shamanism here, because Spirits of the Middle Umbra usually do not answer in the Underworld.

    Summoning, Binding, and Warding only appear scarcely, and mostly in the context of Spectres. Harrowings might count as an example. But as with Theurgy, summoning entities from other realms seems to be impossible, or at least I'm unaware of any examples. I'm also unaware of any wards against Spectres or Whistimmu.

    Necromancy is a funny one. If there is magic in the Underworld, arguably all of it would count as Necromancy. However, it may exist as an inversion - Arcanoi dealing with the Quick, such as Embody or Puppetry, would take its place. Vivimancy?

    Thaumaturgy - as in: spell sorcery, not the specific Blood Sorcery - is the tricky one. If Arcanoi and spell sorcery are equivalent, then everything falls into this category. There is, however, one big thing missing from Arcanoi that is a staple of Thaumaturgy: Arcanoi do not have Rituals. As far as I know, no Arcanos has Ritual usage that results in effect beyond those of the arts.

    I've seen discussions on how Arcanoi may be an expression of Sphere Magick, but I'm not convinced. Functionally they more resemble Path Sorcery, but on the other hand appear to be more flexible with their alternative arts.

    The most interesting thing about asking whether Arcanoi and magic are equivalent - in my opinion - are the gaps that Arcanoi currently do not cover: Rituals are the big one, and I could see potential in expanding Arcanoi with a Ritual system. Formalizing Underworld alchemy is also attractive, as is tapping into Oblivion as a form of black magic for Wraiths.

          The only official book I know of that explicitly addressed this question was the original Sorcerer, with its optional “Change of Life” systems (pp.54–56):
          A magician who dies but becomes a ghost begins as an Enfant without any of her previous Advantages. Like any other ghost, she starts out at square one with the usual traits, although her mortal abilities remain. At the Storyteller's option, The magicians mystical savvy might influence the Arcanoi she eventually masters (see sidebar); the points, however, are lost. Death is a new beginning.
          According to this, sorcery is lost to wraiths. At most, it suggests a direction that the new wraith might choose to push her development of Arcanoi; but the connection isn't strong enough to warrant any kind of leg up on developing them. This is in contrast to vampires and mages, where sorcerous expertise can be converted into Spheres or Disciplines, albeit with a readjustment period: the book suggests six months or more, after which existing dots in Paths or Rituals can be converted into dots in appropriate Disciplines, or translated into freebie points that are used to acquire Arete, Spheres, or related traits. (There's also a flat prohibition against sorcerers becoming shapeshifters or changelings, which is why Gifts and Arts don't appear on the “Starting Points” table in that section.)

          So, if we take this seriously: sorcery, sphere magick, and even vampiric Disciplines can be thought of as related to each other; but Arcanoi are something entirely different, isolated from the rest. This would argue for “wraiths can't learn magic” and against “Arcanoi are magic”.

          That said, the book was written at a time when the writing staff was furiously trying to put a lid on “crossover powers”, due to bad experiences with the undead shapeshifting Abominations and with Samuel Haight. So take it with a grain of salt.

              Fundamentally, the Underworld is composed of memories.
              Wraiths are effectively memories of themselves, and they carry memories of items (in the form of relics) with them.
              The buildings and locations of the Shadowlands are memories of their Skinlands counterparts, and their appearance in the Shadowlands is a direct function of how they are remembered in the Skinlands - at least until enhanced using the materials of the underworld.
              I think the answer lies there - Wraith are... echoes of a person, and not the "whole" soul

              Parts of Being according the Egyptians (and many other cultures, but lets use the egyptians)
              Khat, the Physical Body, was often preserved to allow a smooth resurrection - obviously a Wraith don't really have this. Unless they become a Risen/Kuei-jin
              Ba, the part of the soul that ventures into the Underworld - basically the Ba is a Wraith
              Ka, the part of the soul that remains close to the body and guards it from desecration - so we already have a "missing part"
              Ab, the Physical Heart, the seat of consciousness - see the Khat/body above
              Khu, the physical and mystical representation of a being - basically the Aura, that ties the spiritual and material parts together. Since Wraith don't really have a body (or had one untill it rotted away, turned to dust) this one is really weak to the point of almost nonexistant. Auspex 2 descirbes the Aura colour of a Wraith "Weak, intermittent aura"
              Ren, the True Name, a vital part of existence and the soul, a "brief" summary of what the person is
              Khaibit, the Shadow, which represent the darker urges and emotions of the soul - The Shadow of a Wraith (or the Beast of a Vampire, P'o of a Kuei-jin)
              Sahu, the indestructible part of the soul that will dwell in the fields of A'aru - The Eidolon of a Wraith technically (or the Hun of a Kuei-jin. tho' the Hun more like a bit of the Ka, since it lingers around the corpse, waiting to the P'o - the Hun could be the two combined)
              Between these parts flows Sekhem, the force of life itself that binds the other soul-parts together.

              To preform magic, you have to be "whole"
              A Wraith of a Vampire don't have disciplines, because she is missing parts of her Being - but as soon as it became a Risen, Disciplines i na limited form, allowed, may learn Celerity, Fortitude, Obfuscate, and Potence. The Kuei-jin are a special branch of the Risen in a sense, since their creation requires every part of the being to reunite, while a "common" risen needs first of all a Conduit, to store and lock away its Shadow, so incomplete still - more complate than before, but still missing parts, so this limits the potential abilites

              Entities like the Innocent of the Mokolé are wraiths, never been able to dream their Archid shape, but the connection to the mnesis allows them to steal, devour, corrupt or alter Memories, but themselves are while spirits in a sense, have Arcanoi

              I would say, as long as you are "whole", you will be able to prefrom magic... any kind
              But after you became a Wraith, a memory, an echo what you were? Then you lost something... and either transcende, move on, or back to your body to be a Risen or Kuei-jin

                                      Looking at the Arcanoi, they tend to fall in to a few of categories. There are the ones like Moliate, Argos, and Castigate that manipulate things specific to the Underworld or wraithly existence. Then there are the ones like Embody or Outrage that work to reach across the Shroud. I wouldn't consider either one to be magic per se. The former is linked to the metaphysics of the world they are in and are just as likely to stem from the nature of plasm, pathos, etc. as it is to magically manipulate them. The second group maps (imperfectly) to spirit charms that accomplish similar goals.

                                      That said, there are a handful of outlier Arcanoi that point to something different. Keening, Mnemosynis, Intimation, Fatalism, etc. These all work with things that are certainly relevant to Wraiths, but are not exclusive to them or to the Underworld. We can easily make the argument that these are a kind of magic that Wraiths have tapped into.
