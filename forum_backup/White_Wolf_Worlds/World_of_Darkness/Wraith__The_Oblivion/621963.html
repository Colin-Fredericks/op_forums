    Evil Wraiths?

    08-10-2015, 04:59 AM
    I've read a fair few opinions that state that a wraith can easily be "less than saintly" and that shadows don't have to be super negative, "mwahaha" evil types to qualify as a shadow. They can in some bizarre ways desire something logical, even perhaps good, but ultimately that end would likely be self-destructive to the wraith and result in Oblivion claiming them. So I'm asking everyone here for their opinion on the matter. Is it possible to create a wraith that would be "evil" by society's standards, perhaps even murderous, taking sadistic glee in causing harm to others, while the shadow is self-loathing, self-hating, and self-destructive, seeking to bring the evil wraith in question to the ultimate demise — Oblivion.

    Obviously this would be more suited to an "Evil Chronicle" or as an NPC that PCs would have to track down and fight or something. Opinions?

      Yes, it is entirely possible. If, say, a supremacist is using his sick ideology to resist the pull of Oblivion, or a Serial Killer is continuing the murder spree that gave him purpose to do the same*, they are Wraiths. And I'd say a large percentage of Shadows are already of the opinion that Oblivion is all that they (the Psyche and the Shadow) deserve.** The fact that in this case the Wraith is a truly horrible person doesn't really change anything.

      Keep in mind, though, that just because the awful Wraith is getting Pathos from his actions, that doesn't mean he isn't also spreading Angst far and wide. And just because the Shadow might have a legitimate case that the Psyche should be stopped, that doesn't mean it won't work with Spectres and other Shadows to advance the cause of Oblivion.


      *Admittedly, I'd expect Serial Killers to become Spectres, but it is possible for it to go the other way.

      **As opposed to 'tired of the pain of existence,' or 'want to see the world burn,' motivations other shadows might have.

          I don't see why not.

          I did something similar for a one-shot at a convention once. The group of PCs were all connected in various ways to a serial killer (including victims, family of victims, police involved in the investigation, etc.) and thus had a lot of overlapping fetters and passions which started coming up when a copy-cat killer started up. One of the PCs (and yes, I know this is pretty horribly cliched, but it was a long time ago, and it was a convention one-shot; nuance is hard) was a "blank slate" ghost that couldn't remember who they were in life, and was rather a complete dick because of having these connections to people, places, and things, they didn't want to care about. Meanwhile, their Shadow was trying its hardest to out this character as the original killer.

          Not necessarily cackling evil vs. moral Shadow, but it worked pretty well. And it didn't even need to be an "evil chronicle," though that sort of setup doesn't work for longer running games.

                                  (Just jumping in after posting in another Wraith thread, which in turn reminded me that I hadn't posted in this neck of the forum in a number of years...)

                                  It's very easy for players/STs to over-simplify the psyche/shadow dynamic, as well as make into an equally oversimplified (and in some cases very incorrect) "good guy/bad buy" scenario. While the psyche generally is more "positive" and the shadow is much more often "negative", this need not always be the case, as the more underlying truth is that the psyche works towards holding the wraith together (maintaining spiritual integrity... proverbially and literally) while the shadow works towards eventual self-destruction (or, when manifested as a spectre, spread such destruction to as many others as possible). In the case of "evil" wraiths, the shadow may in fact have "positive" aspects that work towards undermining the "evilness" of the wraith in question, disrupting his spiritual integrity. (For a simple example, think of the "evil" of Dexter Morgan, from "Dexter", vs. the "evil" of Hannibal Lecter -- the former would be a wraith, in this example, while the latter would be a spectre.) It gets really pretty deep in terms of redefining -- and re-perceiving -- what "good" and "evil" are... literally taking it to a whole new dimension of insight/conceptualization....
