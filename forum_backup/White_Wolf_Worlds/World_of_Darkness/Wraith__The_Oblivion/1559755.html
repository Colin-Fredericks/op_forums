    Dreams in the Underworld

    05-04-2026, 09:04 AM
    Originally posted by Hamlet
    To die, to sleep;
    To sleep: perchance to dream: ay, there's the rub;
    For in that sleep of death what dreams may come
    I've been prepping a dream journey for a crossover Chronicle for a while, and I would very much like to have one leg of it in the Underworld. While reading up, many questions came up the material (especially WTO) left unanswered. I'm pulling material from Werewolf, Mage, Wraith, and Changeling for this to fill some holes, but the main issue is that the larger picture of dreams in the Underworld are, well, underexplored.

    Guild Book Sandmen of course offers plenty of examples for the Phantasm Arcanos and the modus of the Guild, but there's the rub: Sandmen are largely presented as showmen, with only a few tangents on what else to do with dreams other than to provide entertaining or terifying illusions. Some of it is surely a legacy of the setting being the first, and I can't expect crossover aspects like umbral mechanics to be fully explored. There are however some fundamental things I feel could have been addressed, or I may be unaware of. Most of them concern the dreams of Wraiths themselves.

    First of, some basics: Wraiths do dream. Phantasm allows to watch and manipulate the dreams of the Quick as well as a Wraith in Slumber. The Underworld before the Sundering is repeatedly described as a realm of "memory and dreams", as the two concepts tend to overlap. Beneath the Labyrinth, the Neverborn slumber and dream of destruction. The Sandmen know of the Oneira, mysterious dream-lords at the far end of the Dreaming.

    The first question goes back to the Shakespeare quote: How do the dreams of the dead differ from those of the living? Do Psyche and Shadow share the same dream, or separate ones? If so, do they overlap and distort each other? It's probable that most dreams will center around the (Dark) Passions, or at least those will have an influence.

    Mechanically, do Wraiths have a Chimare - or maybe two? Does the Fetter they Slumber in provide the Chimare, perhaps influencing the dream? It seems a similar concept to some Fera who maintain Den Realms, so maybe the work similarly - more so for Phantasm users, who could consciously shape them.

    The dream-space of the Underworld seems to be connected to the larger Dream realm, but at the same time the Sundering seems to have separated the Underworld in some capacity. Mortal dreams can not reach the Underworld as they used to, which to me at least indicates that the Shroud extends as a barrier between the dream realms of the dead and those of mortals.

    If you would use full crossover mechanics, dreams provide an escape route from the Underworld that many Sandmen could use instead of the more convoluted process of becoming a Risen.
    The Keremet are an Adhene described in C20, but these are mortals brought into the Dreaming at the moment of their death - so, more or less re-routed. I don't think there is an example of a Wraith exploring the Dreaming directly, or vice-versa, a mortal or supernatural exploring the dream realms of the dead.

    So how separate are those realms? Is it akin to Skinlands and Shadowlands, or a more metaphysical barrier like crossing between umbral realms? Can Oblivion reach into the Dreaming?

    It seems to be further complicated that Wraiths outside of the Dark Kingdom of Iron play by slightly different rules, with the Bush of Ghosts and Karta as two examples where the Wraiths are much closer to their dream realms (although the Dreamtime seems to be a special case anyways in every system).

    It's possible some of the questions are answered in books I don't have, if so, please let me know. In general I think that - despite having a full Guild Book - there is still much potential to explore, especially in the overlap of memory and dreams.

      Unfortunately, the Dreaming is one of the areas of the WoD I have the least familiarity with. What I can give you is that I've been trying to figure out why the Mage Entropy Sphere should have special properties over the Underworld and Wraiths, because it doesn't actually make sense that it should do what it does. The best I've come up with is that there are certain natural laws, tendencies, or fates that are properties of the Underworld and Entropy can affect those. So for instance, we could say Oblivion is the natural fate of all things in the Underworld and as a fate Entropy has a certain degree of influence over how quickly it approaches.

      If Oblivion has the character of a geas or fate, then it would have aspects that would reach into the Dreaming, though likely very badly. It does destroy dreams and imagination either by erosion or because of its natural dramatic weight, so I would imagine when it does reach into the Dreaming the results are not good.

      I'll also add that in Orpheus, Phantasms can actually travel by entering one person's dream and leaving another's, so there's some evidence that Wraiths can temporarily step through the Dreaming. Staying there might be a trickier proposition.

          I always assumed that the resonance the Entropy Sphere has with Wraiths is because the Underworld and its inhabitants are entropic in nature - the Underworld is the literal last stop before either Transcendence or Oblivion, both which remove the essence of a being from this reality. But I can see the case for Oblivion being a Fate too; it is as much endpoint as it gets.
          The difference I see with dreams in contrast to physical energy is that dream stuff is not finite, and so entropy can not really apply. Of course, without dreamers who are themselves bound to entropy, dreams are still dependent on it as an emergent property. In a way, Oblivion might be a more 'natural' endpoint for dreams than for the dreamers.

          At the moment I'm leaning towards having the dream realm of the Underworld overlapping with but separated by the Shroud from the larger dream realm. It fits the narrative with the Sundering, and travel through the 'mortal' part could be restricted by the Fetters pulling the Wraith back. There are some other consequences that could be interesting (Dream Haunts, Dream Maelstroms, Tempest connection) to explore, but I'm holding back on that for now.

          I haven't considered Orpheus as a source, good call. I may also need to take another look at Mummy.

          Unfortunately, the Dreaming is one of the areas of the WoD I have the least familiarity with.
          You know Wraith though, and I also welcome speculation on the other questions I had since I doubt there is a definite canon answer to them anyways.

The real question is whether you want to allow other creatures to enter or leave the dreams of the dead in a crossover game. It seems to me if you want to explore this element in your games, the answer should be yes.

              Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
              So how separate are those realms? Is it akin to Skinlands and Shadowlands, or a more metaphysical barrier like crossing between umbral realms? Can Oblivion reach into the Dreaming?
              Oblivion manifests everywhere because every place knows some kind of entropy. The lower regions of the Astral map to literal hells and eventually the Labyrinth and Oblivion itself. The Wyrm and its taint appear almost everywhere in the Umbra, and I would argue that the Abyss itself eventually links directly to Oblivion.

              Of course, those places are all at various "distances" from the Oblivion and the Underworld. Certain places are obviously "closer" to the Underworld in some way. I'd have any travelling wraiths first arrive in those kind of places upon "leaving" the Underworld. I'd probably use similar description and mechanics as when Garou try to cross the Membrane. You may want to have some roll to see if the wraith can cross it. Once "gone", they'd be subjected to various penalties and other effects (some of which might be a danger to others as they are bringing Oblivion with them in a way that it is not supposed to do so). The farther they try to get away from the Underworld, the worst those penalties are. I'd use as inspiration how Werewolf applies different effects to the normal rules as Garou are in the different Near Realms.

Though in truth, Transcendence is also a destination, and it isn't supposed to be an unnatural one. Indeed it used to be the normal one.

                      No the problem I have with Mage's take is that it acts like Entropy is not just tied to Oblivion as an end but to Oblivion as a substance. Or at least Angst as a substance.                       I have similar problems when Mage ties Entropy in to the Wyrm.

                      Wraith Dreams

                      Yes, the Psyche and Shadow likely share dreams. They may view them through different lenses, though, much like how they view most things through different lenses. The Eidolon likely shares the dream too, though as with most things, it doesn't play an active role. I don't think any of the three of them twist the dream in any kind of intentional sense during the actual dream, unless the dream is lucid or some specialized Phantasm power is in play. Instead I'd say that any twisting is done in hindsight, in much the same way that the Psyche and Shadow disagree about memories.

                      Passions, dark or otherwise, likely show up frequently in dreams. I imagine that when a wraith is slumbering in a fetter, memories and feelings associated with that fetter are more likely to show up in the dream as well. Dreams are one place, though, where a wraith's monomaniacal tendencies are likely muted.



                      Connection Between the Dreaming and the Underworld

                      The big question here is, what is your purpose in making travel between the Underworld and other realms difficult? That is, is it storyline based? Systems based? Metaphysics based? The reason should determine the method.

                      By default, yes, Wraiths are largely trapped. I'd say the idea of a Shroud that separates Underworld dreams from the rest of the Dreaming is quite workable. Given ghostly connections to dreams, though, I'm inclined to say limited Dreaming access is better. Two other options here are:

                      1) A sort of quasi zone that is within both the Underworld and the Dreaming, which is broadly the same as the idea you suggested, but the Shroud manifests in different places for different beings. Wraiths find the Shroud between this zone and the rest of the Dreaming, changelings find the Shroud between this zone and the rest of the Underworld.

                      2) That wraiths can enter the Dreaming, even travel from dream to dream, but they can't exit it to anywhere but the Underworld. Or in Orpheus' case, possibly to the immaterial part of the Physical world.

                          The big question here is, what is your purpose in making travel between the Underworld and other realms difficult? That is, is it storyline based? Systems based? Metaphysics based? The reason should determine the method.
                          Right now, metaphysics. I prefer the sandbox approach and have stories be an emergent result of player interaction with the world. I can file off narrative edges ad hoc if they occur.

                          Here's a few more points I'm pondering from having dreams as a Place within the Underworld:

                          Dream Relics
                          Dreams can be a place of Memory, which is the basic building material of the Underworld. Is it conceivable that such memories could be hardened enough for Sandmen to take them into the Underworld or Shadowlands?

                          Dream Fetters
                          Mortals can invest a surprising amount of passion into dreams (though usually more of the waking kind). They want to be an astronaut, but can not make it. They want to build their dream house, but never get around to it. These are the kind of unresolved passions that make Fetters. I like the idea that dreams themselves could act as Fetters which anchor a Wraith in the Shadowlands, but not from the Skinlands. On the other hand, dreams may be 'too soft' to qualify, or resolving such Fetters may be impossible.

                          Maelstroms and Tempest
                          If we assume (as Black Fox mentioned) there is a stronger connection to the collective unconscious and the Specter Hivemind, I think a Maelstrom would be able to extend into the dreams of Wraiths. Establishing a connection to the Tempest that way, the latter might sit on the 'borders' or 'between' of Chimares of individual Wraiths.

                          Meaning, travel between individual dreams might be possible through the Tempest (and perhaps Argos or Nihils) instead of a larger dreamspace. That could be done if exiting a Chimare works like stepping sideways, with a direction of 'up' or 'down'. I think I prefer a connection rather than creating a 'Dream Tempest', as the realm is already a turmoil of fading dreams and memories. Does that make sense?
