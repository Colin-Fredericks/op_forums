    [Wraith: The Oblivion] Marriage in Wraith (my two oboli)

    03-01-2014, 01:34 AM
    A few folks asked Deadguy a question about this. Rather than take up more space in that thread, I figured I’d take a stab in a new thread.
    Marriage in Wraith

    I think the institution of marriage has narrow applications in Stygian society. The only occurrence of marriage would be pre-existing relationships. For example, IIRC, John Soulis and his wife are still married (Oh, and what a happy, fairy tale marriage they had). Since Wraiths are souls that have things holding them back, I could see people who identify themselves as couples continue that on the other side of the Shroud. As in life, so on in death. Kind of like the Maitlands in Beetlejuice.

    Being one of the Restless Dead means there’s something keeping you around. Finding that special ghostly someone, settling down, and having 2.5 striplings and a barghest in a haunt with a white picket-fence isn’t really the act of someone who can’t/won’t “move on.” Not that love and romance don’t have any place in the Underworld; quite the contrary. Those are powerful emotions (and Passions). Just ask Erik. Thematically, it just feels out-of-place with the Wraith setting. Unless you have a terribly bleak view on marriage, I would avoid incorporating it into your games.

    [On the other hand, I think marriage would be fascinating to incorporate into a Changeling game. Imagine: Redcap wedding!]

    My two oboli. Agree? Disagree?

          Actually I would argue the opposite. First off WtO itself does not have to be a bleak, depressing game, it gets stereotyped as a bleak, depressing game. Yes it wears a grimdark cloak but if you dig beyond that you can see the aspects of hope and spirituality. And it's the only CWoD with an achievable end, where you can actually win. With VtM you're damned and no matter how powerful you become there will always be someone older and more powerful than you. WtA, you're fighting a losing war because of the mistakes of your ancestors and if you're lucky you might die saving the world for another night. MtAs, Big Brother has won and you're in many ways a terrorist whose ideas for the world probably aren't much better than Big Brother's. With WtO, yes you are dead but if you chose you can address what's holding you back, the mistakes and shortcomings of your life, gaining closure before ultimately passing on as you were meant to.

          Marriage and love can still be very important to a wraith as Erik's story has shown. And yes, it can be a positive thing and work in WtO, more so than in some of the other games. In fact they have many potential avenues to explore said concepts. What happens when a couple die together in an accident and become wraiths? Are they still together after death? How do their Shadows react to this? Do they work to claim them both or drive them apart? What if they're reaped by two different groups, say by the Hierarchy and the Renegades respectively, and don't meet each again until months later, finding themselves on opposing sides? Do they strive together to achieve Transcendence or to simply survive in the Underworld? For that matter what would Transcendence mean to them? Would they both even agree to it?

          And those are just things I pulled out without even thinking too hard on it.

          Of course like with anything, love and marriage can be twisted into something dark and terrible. A great example of this can be seen in the Vampire: the Masquerade Bloodlines game. One of the early quests sees you enter a hotel haunted by a pair of ghosts, a husband and wife who went on vacation there with their kids years ago. While there the husband became convinced that his wife was having an affair which she wasn't and snapped. He killed his wife and children, set fire to the hotel and killed himself. However it didn't end there and the two of them became wraiths. The husband, obsessed with keeping his wife has trapped her in the hotel while trying to keep everyone else out.

          Ultimately it's all about how the players and the ST handle it. Even the best ideas can fall apart horribly if handled poorly.

          Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. They're indomitable. Indomitable.

              With respect, I don’t think you actually disagreed with anything I wrote. In fact, I think you confirmed it:
              The only occurrence of marriage would be pre-existing relationships. … I could see people who identify themselves as couples continue that on the other side of the Shroud.

              Not that love and romance don’t have any place in the Underworld; quite the contrary. Those are powerful emotions (and Passions). Just ask Erik.
              I think symbolic marriages might be utilized in some circles. I could easily imagine the Legion of Fate using for its iconic symbolism. I could also see the Laughing Legion using it with a cross between symbolism and parody. As Nothing mentioned, Heretics and the Jade Empire might use for those reasons. In a weird way, I could also see Nephwracks (maybe even some Onceborn) using perversions of wedding ceremonies to cement alliances.

              The difference is a thematic one. As a symbolic ritual, yes, I could see Wraiths utilizing it. In these cases, it wouldn’t be just a matter of “getting married.” It’s the symbolism and intent behind the ceremony, whether ritualized alliance-forming (which many cultures do, to my understanding), parody, or Shadow-eaten perversion. A drive-thru Las Vegas Elvis minister saying, “You may kiss the bride,” invokes much different imagery than a Barker-esque Nephwrack saying the same thing.

              Would Anne and Andy run off to their Legion’s Chaplin and get hitched? It just seems inappropriate to the setting.
              Would an Anacreon marry a Dybbuk to reinforce a working relationship between the Kingdom of Iron and the Kingdom of Wire? Maybe…

              I think that a juxtaposition of such incongruous elements – “getting married” and being dead – might invoke an inappropriate comedic tone. Again, this is my take. If a troupe wants more Lydia and less Kirsty, there’s nothing wrong with that.

                  Tragically, it does seem that there is an unfortunate focus on Wraith being such an unrelentingly “bleak and depressing” game, as AkatsukiLeader13, so adroitly put it and it definitely doesn’t have to be. It can be a world of hope for many.

                  I also agree with Akatsuki's statement: “marriage and love can be very important to a wraith.”

                  First marriage doesn’t anthropologically speaking originate in a top down order, so I would think it hardly a matter to an individual if the “government” or other leadership have necessarily instituted a formal version of it. For a wraith, I would suggest, it would stem from their own personal beliefs while they had been alive and the importance to them in their society, culture, spiritual background and personal belief from which they originated.

                  While there can be “twisted” views in the realm of the undead as there are “twisted” views of many things in the world of the living, there would also be pure and true views as well.

                  If there were no hope or “life” to live in the underworld pretty much everyone would be doing nothing but mope around and quickly succumb to Oblivion.

                  Charon, as any good emperor, set up a system of merit and reward and gave people in society something to strive for, a purpose and a place while being in the underworld. He also promoted beautifying the Underworld with art and culture.

                  Why, for example, would Stygia have the Library of Alexandria, if one could not have other interests than resolving things holding you back from moving on? Then again, some might not want to resolve them, anymore than they did while alive. Yet again, a wraith might have not been able to do certain things in life, whether from age, fear, opportunity, illness, and so forth, and may feel more freedom to explore things here, even beautiful things here.

                  As it is, I think that “marriage” in Wraith would fit very much into the world, since it could be that people find love on the other side of the Shroud and some would want that sense of commitment and belonging reflected from their personal beliefs and culture while the had been in the world of the Quick.

                  In the Jade Kingdom too you have “families,” so this would also make sense. I believe I mentioned before a wonderful ghost book, called “The Ghost Bride” based off an ancient Chinese practice:

                  “In Chinese tradition, a ghost marriage (also known as a Minghun or spirit marriage) is a marriage in which one or both parties are deceased.[25] A ghost marriage was usually set up by the family of the deceased and performed for a number of reasons, including the marriage of a couple previously engaged before one member’s death, to integrate an unmarried daughter into a patrilineage, to ensure the family line is continued, or to maintain that no younger brother is married before an elder brother.[26]” -http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghosts_in_Chinese_culture

                  I would think in the Iron Kingdom “getting married”, wraith to wraith, would also very much make sense for more Western and for simple “human” reasons.
