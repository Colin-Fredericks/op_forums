    101 Wraith: the Oblivion chronicle concepts

    02-09-2022, 12:57 AM
    I love Wraith: the Oblivion's setting. Even though it's on the top of my wishlist and can probably find the players for it, I've yet to run it. The problem is I've yet to think of a storyline, a chronicle concept I can get really excited about as a Storyteller. I need something more than just the slice of afterlife stories of player characters pursuing their Passions and navigating Necropolis politics. Perhaps something special about who or what the PCs are and where or when the chronicle is set? I don't know yet.

    Please give me some inspiration. What do you think are interesting concepts for a Wraith chronicle?

      1) The characters are Renegades and Heretics plotting an actual overthrow of a Necropolis whilst maintaining their cover.

      2) The characters have attracted the attention of a Ferryman or Pasiphae who seems to be pushing them into dangerous positions.

      3) War Footing - The local Necropolis is hammered by unusually frequent maelstroms (lower case 'm'). Can anyone figure out why while they prepare for the next storm?

      4) Someone or something in the Skinlands is able to summon and bind Wraiths. One of the Deathlords has authorized limited suspension of the Dictum Mortuum to figure out what is going on.

      New to Wraith 20:

      5) The characters are cloud chasers who seek out important elements of making Soulsteel from the Tempest.

      The Doomslayer Trio:

      6) The characters are part of a hunters who hunt Spectres (and possibly Renegades) and drag them back to the forges.

      7) The characters are a team of inquisitors who try to identify Dopplegangers that have taken over Hierarchy positions.

      8) The characters are active Helldivers who take the fight to the Labyrinth

          9) Emergency Response Team - When disasters occur in the Skinlands there is the potential for a windfall of drones and mortwights to send to the forges, relics to sell, and, I suppose, fresh wraiths if you feel like separating those out.               I think Wraith benefits from having a much tighter chronicle concept than the other game lines. The other games lend itself to power fantasies, but few people fantasize about becoming a ghost. The most obvious goal of a wraith PC is to Transcend, not stay behind and grow more powerful through XP. So many players already have a concept for one of the other games, and simply plug it into whatever chronicle an ST is running, and that usually does not present any problems. But Wraith is so personalized with its fetters, passions, regrets, and shadows, I think it's much harder to simply plug in a random character. PCs need to fit in to the chronicle concept to a much higher degree than the other games. Also, the other games have a more limited expanse of their supernatural creature. So you can get away with a random group simply by saying they needed to band together to survive. But Wraith defaults to hundreds, if not thousands, of other ghosts in the local area, so conceivably each PC had other choices available to them. You need something more so that the Circle makes sense.

              The one idea I had for a Wraith chronicle centered on the idea that all the PCs were somehow tangentially related to one another. But it didn't require the PCs knowing each other before hand (it was OK if they did). They'd find out about their mutual connection after they died.

              The core concept was there was a business that unknowingly possessed an Oblivion artifact. That business was in the process of being merged or bought out by another company. That second company was being manipulated by a spectre cult who wanted to find and recover that Oblivion artifact. All the PCs were somehow involved in that. How they were involved all depended on their main character concept. If they were oriented towards a profession, maybe they were involved in doing due diligence on the merger. If they were oriented towards combat, maybe they were a security guard or head of security. Maybe they were a newspaper reported covering the story. Or a major owner of the business. Or a PI hired by a rival firm to investigate their competitors. How I'd fit them in all depended on what they wanted to do, and work with them to fill in the details.

              But now all of them are dead, in a relatively short time from each other. They all had the same reaper, and it would up to them to discover the connections they had to one another. And once they realized that, I assumed, they would think their deaths were somehow connected, and they would begin investigating that. To what extent they may have been purposefully killed, inadvertently killed, or just random chance they happened to die when all the others did, would all depend on their character backstory. But my intention was to at least imply that their recent deaths were part of some nefarious plan. And I'd give each PC at least one clue that would put them on the path of learning about the Oblivion artifact, the Spectre cult, and why the merger needed to be prevented.

              My hope was to have a plot that would require them to spend a lot of time dealing with the Skinlands and the Shadowlands. I'd have the main plot running which mostly dealt with the business in the Skinlands and discovering ties to a Spectre Cult (which would lead to them trying to stop activities in both the Skinlands and Shadowlands), and then the "ordinary" stuff of dealing with the Shadowlands that would happen with any new Wraith. As the chronicle progressed, I would them throw in a monkeywrench that caused the local Necropolis to go haywire. Local unrest, Renegade attack, Hierarchy politics, whatever. That was to eliminate the PCs going to the local powers to stop the Spectre cult, as they'd be dealing with this other issue. At the end, they'd need to deal with the Spectre cult themselves. I wanted a major part of the game to be the PCs involving themselves in the Skinlands and the living somehow, and not just doing stuff in the Shadowlands and other wraiths.

              Like you, I never managed to run it or any other Wraith chronicle. But it was my general idea of how to run a chronicle that involved the things I wanted the game to be about.

                  It's also worth looking at the various metaphysical places a game can be. The world of Wraith has dramatically different realms and they lend themselves to different kinds of games. Some rough thoughts:

                  The Shadowlands are fresh memories, just out of reach. Most of the wraiths here have a strong and detailed idea of who they were and are. The shadow of the living world infiltrates almost everything that happens here, even for those Wraiths who stick to the Dictum Mortuum. The dichotomy between the living and the dead is at its strongest here, often because the Shadowlands often blurs that line. Indeed the Dictum Mortuum itself, dramatically, is a tool to draw that line back into sharp relief.

                  Shadowlands games lend themselves to stories about fresh ghosts, attachments to the living world, dichotomy, and feeling. Wraiths here tend to feed on the passions of the living world and cling to their old identities.

                  Stygia is a place of old memories, remembered in broad strokes but with many of the details worn away like weathered stone. Unique appearances are replaced with masks. The unique passions of life are replaced with the obsessions of the dead. The strange customs and behaviors add up to the grand idea of a city that never really existed and yet nonetheless is a resolute and unshaking monument against the storm. The Wraiths here are detached from life and from each other but held together by the grand fiction that is Stygia.

                  Stygian games lend themselves to finding sanctuary in a safe but alien haven, strangeness, adaptation, and deciding what to keep and what to abandon. The Wraiths here depend on their own passions for sustenance and those passions have changed. Things here are a mix of relatable and alien in equal measures, the better to test what is important to the characters.

                  Games that mix Stygian and Shadowlands life straddle that line. The Shadowlands ground the game in life and the living world. Stygia grounds the game in the alien truth of the Underwold. Both convey immutable truths that are nonetheless mutually exclusive. Those who walk this path must translate life to those who don't remember death and translate death to those who haven't accepted that they've died.

                  These games lend themselves to problems in translation, otherness, divided loyalties, and resolution. The Wraiths who adapt the best to this existence are the ones who can resolve issues in their own unlife, both because resolving Fetters is a necessary part of being in two worlds and because they wouldn't last long in the role if they couldn't.

                  The Tempest is a place of disconnection, primal memory, and fever dream. The living world has little sway here, though its treasures and scars do. This is a place where only the strongest parts of a thing remain, but those things are stronger than ever. You can either blow in the nightmare winds, or erect your own nightmares against them. One thing is true, nothing stays here unchanged.

                  Tempest games lend themselves to unpredictability, desperate trials, adventure, and change. They also test what is important to the characters, but usually in fast unpredictable ways. Tempest games also lend themselves to a central, critical theme that drives wraiths to even brave the winds.

                  The Labyrinth hides away things so they never see the light of day, but a place that reveals the truth. The vast majority of residents don't want themselves or their stories to exist, but they can't escape them either. You go to the labyrinth to hide or to find, but which one you get is not always your choice.

                  Labyrinth games lend themselves to questing, becoming, preserving, and abandoning. Only those who build themselves to live here or build themselves to fight here can sustain themselves for long.

                      11) The Quest for the Far Shores - The Characters are trying to reach a specific island in the Tempest; an allegedly uncorrupted Far Shore. Of course no one is sure where it is and finding a trustworthy captain is quite the challenge.

                      12) I am Spartacus - The story of a Thrall rebellion.

                      13) Nobody Knows Your Name - The characters are regular wraiths, complete with passions and fetters. For some reason no one besides them remembers their corporeal lives. Even close loved ones, both living and dead, have no memory of them at all.

                      14) These Are Their Stories. A courtroom drama surrounding petitions to the local Magistrate. The characters are advocates and bailiffs trying to navigate the tensions between local wraiths, the edicts of a distant Stygia, and a detached Magistrate who's not in complete control of his Shadow.

                          These Are Their Stories is an idea that's stuck with me and I kind of want to run it now. This is my basic idea:

                          The Necropolis of [Insert City Here] is a mess. Due to some scandal it's been mismanaged for years. The Hierarchy is trying to clean it up but at the moment they are projecting more power than they actually have. The council of Anacreons is officially full, but there are only five real members. These five take turns hearing cases.

                          The judges are supposed to decide cases based on law, but most of them let their motivations, goals, and prejudices strongly influence their decisions. Among the five are:
                          • A firebrand who is trying to shake things up
                          • A strict and draconian judge who might have a spiteful streak
                          • A corrupted politician (unproven)
                          • An enigmatic and sometimes capricious judge
                          • A pragmatist who puts the interests of Stygia first and expects the characters to do the same.

                          Two of them are struggling with their shadows (I don't know which two) and one of them will eventually fall.

                          The characters are a mix of advocates, guards, and therapists (pardoners) assigned to cases. Resources are thin so they can be called to be either prosecution or defense. They have a limited time to investigate before presenting their case to the Anacreon. There will be an opposing side.

                          Much like the judges the players have the option of letting their prejudices affect their arguments and their actions will have an effect on Necropolis society.

                              15) Ferrymen by Fate
                              The player characters are all relatively old and experienced wraiths that basically have almost no hope to trascend anymore due to their unresolved issues being impossible to resolve (or at least with time they came to believe so).
                              By chance they meet a very fresh wraith whose unresolved matters in life remind each member of the group of their own, at least partially (it can only be explained with very odd and peculiar twists of fate).
                              They set up to help this new wraith to trascend.
                              Maybe they even feel compelled to do so by "something", if the players are the kind that don't like to set up goals before a chronicle started

                                      17) All the characters died the same day, because they all lived in the same large condo (or where there by chance the fateful day) and the building was caught in a fire and they were among the victims. Aside from their personal unresolved matters, the plot revolves around the discovery that the fire wasn't an accident and the mistery behind it to be investigated in the skinlands.
