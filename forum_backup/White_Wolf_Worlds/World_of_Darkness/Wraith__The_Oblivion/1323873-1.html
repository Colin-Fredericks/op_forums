        I think in a Wraith game that things should be Wraith centric, just like anytime you run a game in any system it should be centered on that. At the very least, in-character things should be explainable in terms of how those characters understand things in their milieu.

        At one point in time, I was very exclusive in how each layer of the Umbra was divided, but now I am much more open to figuring out ways how creatures and people can voyage throughout the spirit worlds. So some amount of inter-umbral travel is appropriate in my view.

        Here's the next.

        Electronic Voice Phenomenon (EVP)

        Phenomenon: Sound recording equipment left alone in haunted places record bizarre, strange noises when there should be no sound recorded at all given the time and location. After being manipulated, these recordings sometimes reveal actual words, or at least sounds that exhibit speech patterns, or other signs of intelligence.

        WoD Explanation: Much EVP phenomenon is either false or faked, but sometimes it is real. EVP picks up the background noise of the Tempest or local Shadowlands, a random phenomenon that happens after a significant time has passed at the last presence of human life. All cases require a low Shroud rating. In the case of noise from the Tempest, the ST may determine a temporary Nihil has formed in order to allow any noise to pass through. ST may decide to allow something of importance (like a spectral plot through the Hive Mind, or an overheard conversation of Renegades temporarily staying over at the Haunt) to reach the Skinlands for plot purposes.

                            This is something that doesn't get much observation now, but it is a very old type of haunting. I use its German name here, but other cultures have their own terms. In Ireland, it is called a fetch.

                            Dopplegangers (bilocation)

                            Phenomenon: While White Wolf has used the term Doppleganger to refer to a wraith permanently consumed by their Shadow, it also has an older meaning - the “double walker.” A doppelganger is a shadow-self of a human, a phantasm of the living. Typically it only appears before the owner of a doppelganger, but there are exceptions. It sometimes manifests in a mirror or other reflective surface, although it casts no reflection on its own. Dogs and cats have been known to sense doppelgangers.

                            These types of apparition are often mistaken for the ghost of a dead person. Some think that the doppelganger is an etheric counterpart of the physical body that is reserved for the purpose of carrying the soul after death. Occasionally, in times of illness or great distress, it would seem that this ethereal replica is somehow projected or detached from its physical counterpart through some mysterious process.

                            Many famous people have reported this baffling phenomenon - Queen Elizabeth and Johann Wolfgang Goethe are two examples. The doppelganger has even been projected over considerable distances to materialize in full view of witnesses. It is said to be bad luck if it is seen, and rarely a doppelganger becomes visible to friends or family, often causing great confusion. It is often an omen of impending death.

                            The fear of the double is observed, if unknowingly, in the custom of covering all the mirrors in a house where a death has just occurred. The double of anyone passing the glass, it was once thought, could be projected into the mirror, and carried off by the deceased to the afterworld.

                            WoD Explanation: Dopplegangers are the awakened Shadow in possession of the soul’s plasmic body manifested or embodied in the Skinlands while the person is still alive. The Shadow is not typically “awake” until the passage of death activates it by the closer proximity to Oblivion. However, there may be times when still alive that one’s Shadow, the unconscious, reaches out. The Doppleganger sometime materializes only to its owner, and might not be seen by anyone else.

                            The Shadow projects usually for two reasons. One is that it senses the approach of Oblivion because the person will soon die, and its projection is a premonition of doom. The second is truly rare, occurring only when the subject has intense emotion, desire, or passion when thinking about another person or place. The living person fuels the Shadow with such Pathos/Angst that it is able to project in the location of the place or person.

                            Because of the non-euclidean geometry of the Tempest, this projection could conceivably be anywhere. The double instinctively attempts to connect with the source of such Passion. However, the amount of Pathos/Angst needed to fuel this is so high that the double is not maintained for long.

                            Some practitioners of black magic are able to consciously project their double. After falling asleep or entering a trance, the double goes forth to wreak havoc in the world of the living. This could be mirrored by a Merit, a Demonic Investment, or perhaps a rite with one of the Paths of Sorcery like Ephemera.

                                        Poltergeist is really over the top in terms of ghost stories. Real world ghost stories (actual ghostly encounters people have reported) don't have anything like that. Even legends and fictional ghost stories are typically more down to earth - not leaving much evidence that it happened. So it needs some sort of plot device that explains how a house could have blown up.

                                        If I had to actually narrate the first movie in Wraith terms, I'd likely have the Beast be some kind of Nephwrack or other horror, and that it was an intervention by one of the Legions off camera that destroyed it, and they used some kind of special device to shock the Shroud back into existence which is the implosion the family saw - the house was so Oblivion tainted at that point that it belonged "more" into the Shadowlands than the Skinlands. Likely, I'd have Wraith PCs be skinriding the family during the movie in order to learn what is happening in the Skinlands so they can report back to the local Necropolis what is happening and the level of the threat so that the Legion can adequately prepare to fight the Beast.

                                        I'd introduce the PCs by having them keep tabs on parapsychologists and mediums as part of the Legions recon on possible violations of the Dictum Mortem. So when they go to investigate the house, that's when the scenario begins for the PCs who then begin to learn about the nature of the activity.

                                        Here's another version of hauntings that Wraith does not properly address.

                                        Hauntings, residual

                                        Phenomenon: In a residual or non-intelligent haunting, a scene is played out like a tape recorder. It repeats itself again and again whenever "played back". It is theorized that the phenomenon is from a “psychic impression” because very strong emotions can theoretically leave an imprint in time.

                                        For example a house may have had a man fall down the stairs. Sounds of somebody falling down the stairs are now heard often in the house. This is most likely a residual haunting. Residual hauntings are distinct from "intelligent hauntings" which indicate some amount of free-will of the ghost, or interaction between the ghost and the living. While intelligent hauntings may have common themes, they do not have the repetition of residual hauntings.

                                        WoD Explantion: Many residual hauntings are drones: mindless wraiths without enough willpower for conscious action. However, some residual hauntings have other causes. Since many drones are reaped and sent to the soul forges, such residual hauntings would cease if this was the sole explanation. While not all drones are found and reaped, any activity prolonged enough to attract attention of ghost hunters would certainly be noticed by Reapers as well.

                                        In such cases, one solution is that the physical location itself is fueled with Pathos caused by the Passions of the living about a specific incident or set of events. Manifestations occur by spontaneous Wylding as if through the Pandemonium Arcanos. Such “haunted locations” might even bedevil wraiths and be beyond their power to control. Such places might be actively policed by the Hierarchy.

                                        Another solution is that such Haunting occur when a place serves both as a Haunt and Fetter for wraiths. The combination spontaneously provides such effects.

                                        Another explanation is that intelligent wraiths relive the acts that fuel their Passions. Maybe their Passions are very specific, or such acts are crucial to maintain one’s Memoriam or Legacy.

                                                    This is a phenomenon that some games already have explanations for. So this is already easily simulated, but this is putting it in perspective of use within a Wraith game.

                                                    Old Hag

                                                    Phenomenon: Psychologists have recognized 'Old Hag Syndrome', or sleep paralysis, for many years. Scientists believe it is simply the product of a half-awake mind, but there are those who believe the cause is supernatural. Victims wake up in bed to find that they cannot move, even though they can see, hear, feel and smell. They often experience the feeling of a great weight on their chest and sense that there is a sinister or evil presence in the room. This presence sometimes manifests itself as an old hag with evil-looking eyes, hence the name of this syndrome.

                                                    WoD Explanation: No doubt some occurrences have mundane causes, but this does not make for exciting roleplay. In fact, Hags do exist and prey on humanity while it sleeps.

                                                    Hags are a type of Tempest creature like Legendaries. The Malfean Lachesis also has Oblivion-ravaged spectres called Hags. Both collect souls, and sometimes attempt to claim those of the living. They attack through use of the Arcanoi Phantasm and Usury. Either or both could be responsible for the phenomenon.

                                                    Hags desire the vitality of young adults, but rarely have the power. Instead, they prey most effectively against the elderly and children, especially newborn babies. The Hierarchy knows of such creatures and views them as servants of Oblivion.

                                                            I agree that Plasmics are a great resource for any Wraith ST. Sea of Shadows does a great job conceptualizing them and organizing them into categories, but really needed to provide more specific creatures that fit the setting. Perhaps once I exhaust my list of normal hauntings, I'll post types of Plasmics I think should be in the game. It's not really a "Haunting", but many are based in lore or fiction about the afterlife or the world beyond.

                                                            Another thing I think travel in the Tempest requires is that most hoary of old school RPG concepts - the wandering monster/encounter table that includes different wraiths, plasmics, and zones. It wouldn't be used in typical D&D fashion in terms of X minutes go by, roll on the table. But to help organize different areas of the Tempest thematically so we know what kinds of encounters predominate in those areas, and it would help differentiate them. Such a table would actually be a very efficient way to distinguish between different Tempest areas. And for an ST running an extended Tempest voyage, it would help him determine what kind of events happen along the way regardless if he rolls randomly or picks his own.
