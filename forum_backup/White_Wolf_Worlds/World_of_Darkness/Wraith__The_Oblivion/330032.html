    Sell me on Wr20/Wraith the Oblivion

    12-04-2014, 08:20 AM
    Hi all,

    So back during the M20 Kickstarter Campaign I started a thread about being “sold” on the concept of the game (link below):
    Sell me on M20/Mage the Ascension - Onyx Path Forums
    http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/main-category/main-forum/the-classic-world-of-darkness/mage-the-ascension/90822-sell-me-on-m20-mage-the-ascension
    Hi all. So this is pretty much what it says on the tin for the most part. Some context... I'm a long time WoD player (around a decade) but its been about four years since I played anything in the Classic World of Darkness. Since then I've been firmly entrenched in the NWoD. Recently after a few years of a


    I intended it as a sorta casual thread, but it ended up being a really interesting thread with a lot of conversations I found really engaging. So I thought I would do the same thing for Wraith now that he Kickstarter has started.

    My CWoD experience is limited to Vampire the Masquerade/Dark Ages. So I’m a complete layman when it comes to Wraith. I basically know it mostly takes place in the Underworld and that its pretty grim-dark that’s about it. All my “ghost” knowledge is pretty much filtered through Vampiric interaction with ghosts and the underworld. So the Necromancy discipline and some True Black Hand fluff.

    I have some experiences with Wraiths NWoD equivalent Geist, but I am well aware that the two games are about as thematically different as it’s possible to get. Or at least that’s the impression I’ve absorbed from the general conversations about the two on the forums. However, I am interested in the contrast between the two from the experience of people who have played both games.

    To be a bit clearer about what I’m kinda curious about and want to see explored in this thread: I'm interested in people’s thoughts about Oblivion as well as just a basic explanation of the game. After all, I can look up the detail on Wiki and TvTropes anytime, not to mention the Kickstarter has done a good job of detailing the basic details via the preview docs. The point is I really want people’s thoughts about Oblivion.

    Issues like:
    What makes it a fun game for people?
    What good experiences (and not so good experiences/problems) have people had with the game?
    What do people think are the overarching themes of Oblivion?
    What sort of stories the game supports?
    How does it differ from other CWoD games (beyond the obvious splat change) and how is it similar? (Both in general themes and in specific setting ideas)

    These are just examples, but they are the sort of thing that I think demonstrate the "core" of a game and what it means to different players.

    I also have four sorta more specific questions:

    1) Just how depressing is this game? Considering I’ve heard it referred to as “the Grey Book of Depression” in previous conversations on this forum.

    2) How has the whole Shadow play thing worked out for people in practice? In theory I get that it’s a kinda need idea. From the anecdotes of the one group I know who have played Wraith before; it was a nightmare to actually implement.

    3) How does Wraith fit into the “Urban Horror/Fantasy” of the WoD in regards to the fact that the majority of the game takes place in another realm removed from mortals? Something I have always liked about the WoD is the “through a mirror darkly” aspect of the setting, so I’m wondering how something that steers more towards outright fantasy might work and still be part of the WoD.

    4) What is the “morality” of Wraith? Is it very “shaded of moral ambiguity” or more “black and white” in its take on things? I usually build stories around antagonists that drive them, so I’m a big believer in needing a good villain. Hence I’m kinda interested in who the “bad guys” are in the Wraith setting (both on a minor/personal and major/existential scale.

    Well, that’s all for now.

    I would like to thank everyone that takes the time to reply in this thread. Let awesome conversation being!

    "There is no God in the Badlands." - Sunny

      1) Just how depressing is this game? Considering I’ve heard it referred to as “the Grey Book of Depression” in previous conversations on this forum.
      Wraith society is basically hell : A tyranny where every crime can result to an eternity of having your consciousness imprisoned in a sword or other objects. Slavery is endemic and quasi-legal and you can leave the world in horrible circumstances and wake up in chains or on the auction block.

      Setting-wise horrible events in history are explored, I mean there are books on First World War and the Holocaust out there

      Homewer I have never thought of the game as more depressing as Vampire "I am a monster and will become worse as the centuries pass", Werewolf "The devil is winning the War in Heavens", Mage "Cosmic power brought low by human pettiness" or Changeling "You're going to weaken and die"

      The main difference is Wraith has a clear victory path. Your character can solve the issues which tie him to the living world and Transcend to whatever place human souls go. You can make peace with your darker urges, understand them and shackle them to your own good.

      Society-wise Wraith is the most horrible of the World of Darkness, Indiviually-wise this is the most hopeful

          First off I should point out I've never had a chance to play WtO, I've read plenty of WtO and Orpheus but I've never had a chance to play it.

          That said WtO can be a very interesting, if different, game. What makes it fun? Do you like ghost stories? Wanna try it from the other side? Want to have tense adventures into bizarre, impossible places, encountering dangerous monsters? Want to explore themes of spirituality? The ideas of life and death? Play games of politics without involving mortal governments?

          The overarching themes? Generally WtO has many of the same themes of other OWoD games but its biggest themes are those of spirituality, life and death, what do you when bad things happen to you and what you will do address the baggage you've acquired over your life.

          There are two really good WtO review videos from the Gentlemen Gamer and the Grumpy Celt and I recommend giving them a watch.

          1) It can be very, very bleak and depressing. Stygia can very from relatively benign albeit corrupt and bloated government to a horribly corrupt and monstrous empire. And your own worst enemy is in your head. That said there is an undercurrent of hope within the line. Even in the grimest book of the line which talks about wraiths created by the Holocaust there is an undercurrent of hope. For instance, one group of Holocaust wraiths are trying to redeem Spectres, wraiths gone mad by their pain and Shadows, while another, at the time of the publication, was trying to ensure the construction of a monument on the site of their mass graves so that their still living families and others can come and remember them which creates Pathos to sustain the wraiths.

          And of course there is Transcendence, only achievable end of the OWoD lines. With vampires, no matter how much power you achieve there's always a bigger fish out there. Werewolf, your enemy is more numerous and powerful than you and pretty much have already won. But with WtO you can resolve your unfinished business and escape the nightmare of the Underworld, moving on as you should have.

          2) Haven't played, can't comment much on it. But I've heard it can be difficult if you don't have the right players. Dansky's said they're working on tuning up the Shadowguiding so we'll have to see how it comes out.

          3) Yes, while wraiths exist largely in the Underworld they have several means in interacting with the living. There are several Arcanoi, ghostly powers, that allow them to interact with the living, they can become Risen by possessing their dead bodies, there are groups and individuals that go out and seek out ghosts such as Orpheus.

          There's also plenty that could be done within the Underworld itself.

          4) The morality is much the same as other lines. The primary enemy of WtO is Oblivion, its soldiers, the Spectres, and the wraith's Shadow. Oblivion is just that, its the force of unmaking, sitting at the very bottom of the Underworld that's slowly devouring everything. In Orpheus, the spiritual successor of WtO, something rises from Oblivion, or perhaps Oblivion itself awakened for the first time since the beginning of the universe. The Spectres are wraiths who have succumbed to their Shadows and Oblivion, with the Shadow becoming the wraith and the wraith's become a powerless voice in the back of their heads. They come in various forms and power levels, ranging from corrupt wraiths to creepy ghost children to ancient, inhuman king-priests of Oblivion. The Shadow is the worst parts of the human they were manifested as an independent presence within the wraith's mind, connected to Oblivion itself.

          Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. They're indomitable. Indomitable.

              I was in a fairly long Wraith game over the past two years, and I never felt depressed. But, that's me. Wraith is very open about its subjects, i.e., death and dying and regret, and that can be triggering to a lot of people. On the other hand, Wraith is also about setting your affairs in order, fixing mistakes, and maybe even moving on.

              Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
              2) How has the whole Shadow play thing worked out for people in practice? In theory I get that it’s a kinda need idea. From the anecdotes of the one group I know who have played Wraith before; it was a nightmare to actually implement.
              Shadow Guiding is the most controversial part of Wraith. It worked very well in my group: we all respected each other, and the ST was clear that the point of our Shadows wasn't to ruin other players; it was to provide drama and enforce theme. That worked for us, but we were all pretty upfront about what the expectations were (and we have fun when Bad Things happen to our characters). There are some groups that would be incapable of this. There are other groups that would dislike the concept in principle. Shadow Guiding ultimately comes down to taste and group cohesiveness. That said, if someone plays a Shadow, goes out of their way to make the game miserable for another player, and then declares the system unworkable, perhaps the problem is not with the game. Anything can be awful if it's not done in good faith. Shadow Guiding is about trust.

              Shadow Guiding does need some more storytelling support from the books, but that's going to be a big part of Wr20.

              Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
              3) How does Wraith fit into the “Urban Horror/Fantasy” of the WoD in regards to the fact that the majority of the game takes place in another realm removed from mortals? Something I have always liked about the WoD is the “through a mirror darkly” aspect of the setting, so I’m wondering how something that steers more towards outright fantasy might work and still be part of the WoD.
              Wraith CAN be more straight fantasy than the other games, because it mostly takes place in the Underworld. But there's a LOT of hooks in having players interact with the rest of the World of Darkness. Your most important traits are important people. places, and things from your living days. Neglect them, and Oblivion sets in. Wraith was once described to me this way: All the politics are a distraction from the truly important things, and most of those are in the mortal realm.

              Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
              4) What is the “morality” of Wraith? Is it very “shaded of moral ambiguity” or more “black and white” in its take on things? I usually build stories around antagonists that drive them, so I’m a big believer in needing a good villain. Hence I’m kinda interested in who the “bad guys” are in the Wraith setting (both on a minor/personal and major/existential scale.
              Morality is pretty baked into Wraith. You have a trait called Angst that slowly fills depending on your actions. The higher it gets, the more power your Shadow gains. It's not necessarily about being a good person; it's about finding balance with yourself. Neglect your dark side, and it'll grow resentful, and be less willing to work with you. Feed it, though...

              The main antagonists of the game (aside from...entropy) are Spectres. Spectres are Wraiths who've been overtaken by their Shadows. They offer a cracked mirror view of what every character could become if they're not careful.

              Anyway, I hope that helps. I love Wraith, and I hope you get a chance to look into it more!

                                  I have played a whole campaign of wraith and it has been one of the best rpg experiences that I ever had. Wraith is a very sad and bleak game if you only see the surface of it, however deep down wraith is game about hope, redemption and growth. No matter how bleak the situation or how strong your suffering is, there is always a light at the end of the tunnel. Giving yourself to oblivion is the easy path. Fighting to keep going on, to care, to try and find meaning in your existence is difficult and painful but so much more rewarding. Wraith taught me a valuable lesson when I was at my lowest: to never give up. What we do, what we feel, what we care about, everything matters and that is what makes living worth it. Because at the end of the day you can look at yourself in the mirror and say I'm still here and I'm still fighting and that is the greatest victory of them all.
                                  However Wraith has also been one of the most infuriating and frustrating experiences I ever had. Wraith is a rpg that demands a lot from the players both in terms of implication and maturity. Perhaps I just had bad luck but man, do I have some horror stories about how people behave playing this game. It seems that emotional maturity is severally lacking with some people. The shadow system although a great idea on paper is almost completely unplayable in practice. People just take it too personally or they don't take it seriously at all. At the beginning I started playing wraith only with my best friend and the gm. The gm carried our shadows the first sessions and then he let us give it a try, we had a great time. Then more people joined and i didn't end well, at all.
                                  My advice is that wraith is totally worth playing it but make it sure that you are with people you trust and can get intimate with, playing it with strangers or people you don't really trust is a recipe for disaster. I hope my advice helps you.

                                      A few thoughts I have on Wraith:

                                      If you're familiar with the NWOD, I've always thought that Promethean was the more accurate successor to Wraith than Geist. The themes and moods of both games focus more on humanity and what it means to be a person. They're coming at it from opposite ends, though. Promethean builds up to having a human life, Wraith looks back on what that life was and the regrets and losses.

                                      Wraith requires an examined life. The Passions and Fetters and Shadow are all based around what the character was like in life. What they miss, what they treasured, what they regretted, what would be their inner-most asshole, how they died, who they were, who they wanted to be, who was important to them, and what are they going to do now that they are in this place.

                                      Wraith is the most alien environment because there is no basis in anything familiar. The Umbra can be strange, but much of it is based on the natural world, or a Mage has created a world based around their ideas, or there's a theme within this random Realm that has occurred. Stygia is based on what people left behind, molded and mutilated to fit their needs at the time, stretched out over the choices of millions of Wraiths over thousands of years. It's a modern high rise built on a public courthouse built on a Gothic church built on a Greek temple built on an Egyptian pyramid. There is no cohesion beyond what was needed at the time to survive. There are no themes. The physics that would break these buildings in the Skinlands does not apply here. And that's just Stygia, where there has been an ATTEMPT at cohesion and a single guiding vision. The Far Shores can be even worse.

                                      And then there's The Tempest. A literal Sea of Madness. A place that erodes the soul, tears away identity and twists all of it towards entropy. Before at least the minds of humanity, many and shifting though they may be, could be attributed to the environment. Within the Tempest, things that were never human helped build that place. Things that have been so twisted as to no longer BE human are roaming the place. Things that bear no relation to reality, humanity, logic, physics or anything else wander the Tempest for purposes only they know.....maybe.

                                      All of that together makes it a very evocative environment.

                                                  I love Wraith. I used to call it "the best game I've never played." But since then, I have both played and run it. Including playing in a Dark Kingdom of Wire game at Gen Con back in the day. It doesn't get more bleak and depressing than that. Though there were several people there with the usual Wraith issues, the few of us who were passionate about it, made it an awesome experience. And the Shadowguiding rules worked well enough, even in a game of 12 strangers.

                                                  I've run one chronicle and one "mini-chronicle" where the players took a break from their mages and played wraiths in the same setting who were playing a story tangential but related to the main Mage chronicle plot (and one that illuminated some underpinnings to that game's plot arcs). Neither one of those dealt with a lot of the "coolness potential" of Wraith settings. Both were set in necropoli. No excursions to the Labyrinth or Stygia or the Far Shores or other Dark Kingdoms. Still, both games were amazing. Once again, the Shadowguiding and Harrowing rules seemed to work fine for us. That may have had something to do with the players being used to taking on other roles from time to time during the other characters' Seekings in Mage. (Which, when I think about it, I tend to run as sort of 'reverse Harrowings). (Sytgian politics was off stage to a degree in both games, but not front and center... I think they met one Wraith messenger who was one Fetter away from not being able to come that close to the Skinlands anymore). I did use the Ferrymen to a degree. But they really showed up more in the Mage game than the Wraith game. (One was set in St. Louis and the rivers were very important thematically and plot-wise. A Ferryman in a ghostly steamship who would take people where they needed to go - not wanted - for the price they needed to pay, was just too good to pass up).

                                                  One thing I did notice about a necropolis based game, all the historical research I poured into it paid off in spades. Finding appropriate leaders and notables, knowing what Memorium was present around the city, figuring out what wraiths and sorts of dead folks would gravitate to the Hierarchy, Renegades or Heretics (or just lay low and try to stay out of the way... which was usually a sort of default Hierrachy position), etc etc.
