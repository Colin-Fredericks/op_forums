    Originally posted by tasti man LH View Post

    That was one of the questions I submitted to a Q&A interview with Dawkins. Specifically at 20:30 - 26:35.

    Basically...it was a deliberate a design choice to bring down the Arcanoi, not a mistake.

    That the old 1e-2e versions of some of the Arcanoi were "all over the place" and nonsensical to what ghosts are typically described as doing in ghost stories. That to him, tweaking the powers was to bring things down to what ghosts are more commonly known for doing.

    And when it comes to compared to the other WoD gamelines...he sees no issue with wraith being at the bottom of the totem pole compared to the other gamelines. That traditionally, ghosts are not known for causing all-powerful, god-like feats at the same level that vampires, mages, fae, etc are. And that ultimately, Wraith is really difficult to have crossovers anyways due to a.) wraiths spending most of their time in an Otherworld and b.) it always being really hard for the other splats to get to the Underworld/Lower Umbra due to needing very specific powers to get there...which is going to lead to a smaller pool of player and build options.
    Oh wow - well thanks for this clarity. At least it clarifies the complete lack of logic in that line of thinking. All throughout history you have trivial counters to powerful beings that are not wraiths... from Garlic, to running water, to having to invite someone in, to cats, to only being if you've committed incest or some other craziness. To somehow decide that wraiths should be weaker is as arbitrary as you get - especially given the clear lore in 1e and 2e (countless examples, even explicitly mention mechanically in books like buried secrets, book of mirrors, ghost towns, etc etc etc).

    Their assessment of level 5 Arcanoi in 1e/2e as somehow godlike...and then he contradicts himself, twice, and just... Christ. Whatever, glad we ended up buying 1 pdf and not the massive investment we were hyped about. Wraith - In Memoriam indeed.

        Not sure which ones you're looking at - many are traps. Some are flat out worse than picking up any makeshift weapon or using a normal skill.

        Originally posted by TIKank View Post
        Let's take Obliviate. It ignores all armor and can only be soaked by the "appropriate" arts. Later in the book, it states that the other supernaturals have not found a way to deal with it. I take this to mean it ignores things like Garou soak. Add that with the fact that only certain supernaturals can even see a Wraith, makes the ability insane. You can just stroll up and throw AGG.
        Obliviate was spoken about with shock and awe in Hierarchy books, core books, guild books... you name it, it was feared.
        It is now functionally inferior to a stygian steel weapon. Literally, dont use an action to obliviate.

        It is soakable by appropriate arts which would mean, appropriate abilities by others. It is not soakable by wraiths because they suddenly lost their ability to soak aggravated (magically - despite explicit reference in 2e). In 2e/revised, it was explicitly stated it was aggravated and unsoakable, in wraith, mage and werewolf! Talk about consistency!


        This is not about making an uber character to beat all other supers - this is about logical consistency. One of my pet peeves in game development and something I have always tried to avoid - is being inspired by cool flavour text, stories, fiction... then you pick up the mechanics and there is zero alignment or completely irrational for a certain lauded power to exist. Lets continue with the Spooks:

        You're finally learning to focus your anger so that you can take it out on a target. Great! I can now throw a stonehand punch... Yes. I feel I can turn that anger to use it to fight oblivion (Read the spooks guildbook on The Cause. Absolutely fantastic).
        Now a bit more and i'm learning to master this art...Now I can focus my anger to do even greater harm! The stuff of oblivion itself!! Yes... Yes! YE- wait what? Wtf? I'm an Armadar and you're telling me I gotta feed my shadow, shed an ass-ton of corpus, pump pathos, and get up reaaaaalll close and hope to actually touch them/it/whatnot?? faaaaaaaaaaawk that.

        Glad to see the 4000+ years of Outrage tuning and development by some of the angriest and most lethal wraiths has resulted in such an effective power!

        If the author/contributors wanted to hobble wraiths, they could have at least had the class to give "resistance" options much like the Mage20 night-folk counterspell rules. Even those on their own, caused significant backlash- but would have been a respectful approach to offering power level differences. Instead, the interviewed individual literally says the decision was made to put them at the bottom of the totem pole. Im glad I have now been informed on how to play wraith, despite the previous clear stories, guidance, example and text that attracted me to the game in the first place!
