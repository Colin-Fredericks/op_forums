                                                    Originally posted by Lunar View Post
                                                    There are totally too many Guilds (one each for each Arcanoi? ), so maybe they should be placed together in one big book along with lacking ones?



                                                    As a foreigner I am nearly sure it is Dick-Tum More-Toom cause my language (polish) is pronounciated in very similar way to latin and derives many words from it.
                                                    Double "u" can be even said: Dick-Tum More-Too-Um. But no single "y" out there..
                                                    Just going off what was taught in higher level Latin classes designed for classicists and several classes in historical linguistics.

                                                    In any case, it's just guesswork. Albeit good guesswork. The fact Romans wrote poems where the meter and certain rules(like elision) affected pronunciation helps a lot. Particularly of vowels. But vowel pronunciation is very slippery over a very short period of time. Particularly when you don't have TV or radio or recording media to slow such things down. (In any case, go listen to recordings of people talking from a recently as the start of the 20th Century and there are definite differences that are not due to persistent accents, idiom, grammar or vocabulary. If you want to see how fast sounds can diverge, spend some time places like Rhode Island or eastern Mass. Even today, literally every separate town has it's own pronunciation of words, particularly vowels.)

                                                    Using modern Polish pronunciation as a guide to Ancient Roman pronunciation is probably not the most accurate way to go. Much like using modern English spelling for word pronunciation. It's been a long time since anyone said "knife" as "kehnee FAY", knight as "kehnigh it" (with that "gh" actually sounding more like "ch" in the Scottish "loch") or "through" as
                                                    "throwgh" (same "gh" sound) instead of "thru". Hells bells, we can't even tell a thorn from a "y", hence all the "Ye old inn" etc. Which is really just "The old inn" using a letter that's no longer in the alphabet we learn in school that looks a lot like a "y".

                                                    Long and short of it, say Dictum Mortuum the way you want to. One pronunciation is going to be pretty common across a Necropolis, whether it's from the populace at large just reading the words and coming up with it, having it come down from Hierarchs who had to learn Latin in school the "Weni, Widi, Wici" way or some folks that learned the "Vei, Vidi, Vichi" of Church Latin, or a temporary visitor from Stygia who actually spoke Roman Latin....
