    Why I Love Wraith The Oblivion

    12-09-2013, 04:26 PM
    I’ve decided to start this post because Wraith is my favorite RPG. While I could have responded to a recent thread regarding the misconception of the “sheer bleakness”, “un-playability” and “too punishing” aura, Heavy Arms, nothing, AstukiLeader13, PapaBear and others have already adroitly responded to it. I would rather focus on what I love about Wraith.

    Part of why I adore the game stems from my love of story for story’s sake. Not that one can’t do so in other WOD worlds, I just feel that Wraith has a richer arena and mechanism for this built in.

    One of the things I find enchanting about Wraith is the very fact that unlike the other WOD venues, it has its own fantastical world and things may, but do not have to, take place in the “real world.” The underworld has a dark Alice in Wonderland feel to me and it is a wonderful, mysterious place to explore and adventure in all on its own. In a sense it’s a world within a world within a world. Perhaps for others this is why it is a bit discordant, because the “real world” is much more easily accessible and familiar, but for me I actually love the fantastical world.

    The inhabitants of Wraith are the most diverse in that they can come from different time periods, cultures and walks of life. Weapons can be a mixture of ancient and new and there it makes sense. Not to say that you can’t have vampires from different time periods but they interact in the current world where they majority of the inhabitants are from whatever time the game is taking place in.

    I also enjoy the idea of the Shadow, regret, fetter and psyche as I believe that you can create very rich and nuanced characters and it gives you the ability to examine “who” they were in life, the good and the bad and where they go from there. Do they give in to their darker side which can allow a short term advantage but at what cost? The fact that the Shadow gets stronger as the character does is something I like as well. The Shadow also allows the entire group to stay active and involved in what is happening. Admittedly, it does take a certain maturity of character to pull this off well and with respect to the sensitivity of your fellow compatriots. You are after all entrusting your character with someone else. For people who really don’t wish to do this, then they really don’t have to use the Shadow. Like in anything, take what you like and leave the rest, it is your adventure.

    To me I feel that Wraith is the “most” hopeful of all the WOD. It’s not like Vampire’s don’t have Gehenna to look forward to, Werewolf to Ragnarok, etc. In Wraith, I feel that you can pursue something better, you can try to find Transcendence if you choose, you can try to find a new path. Where do you go from here?

    I also feel you can have a great variety in adventures, besides the obviously serious ones, such as trying to come to terms with your regrets or resolve your passions or dark ones such as having Hunters trying to destroy you and your fellow Wraiths, you can have high adventure in the underworld and even humorous, light hearted ones. You can try to help some of the living. Think of movies like “Topper” or “Bettle Juice”, “The Ghost and Mrs. Muir” and so on. It is actually up to you. You can even have a mix of the serious and light hearted.

    Recently I read a really wonderful book that reminded me of Wraith and the Kingdom of Jade, called “The Ghost Bride” by Yangzse Choo which I a bit of a Robinson Crusoe, coming of age story that just happens to adventure in the Chinese underworld. I would recommend it for anyone who likes Wraith.

    These are just a few of my personal feelings about Wraith.

    Cheers!

      Wraith has always been my favourite, as well, so I'm going to take this opportunity to show it some love.

      I've always been a fan of horror, since I was 6 years old and the only thing I would read were "Goosebumps." When I was 9 I watched the original Night of the Living Dead and loved it. For me, being scared has always been exhilarating.

      As I recall, it was '97 when I discovered Wraith. I was 11 at the time and had a passing familiarity with Vampire, when I came across a copy of WtO 1st edition in a used book store near my house. I bought it, or got my mother to, and pretty quickly realized that it was in my best interest to keep her from seeing a good deal of the artwork in it - not the sort of thing most parents would want their preteen children exposed to. And, honestly, some of the art terrified me - specifically the guy with the phone of page 69, the "sailor" on 236 and the fat woman on 248. Worst of all, though, was the skeleton in the keyhole on the cover - I'd see it glowing faintly from across the room but it took me a while to figure out what it was. Once I did, I'd turn it over or cover it before I went to bed.

      Now, 16 years later, I still have that 1st edition copy of Wraith. The binding's completely destroyed, the skeleton doesn't glow anymore and electrical tape has been holding the whole thing together for close to a decade now. My collection's grown in the intervening years - even after I sold every other RPG book I had, hundreds of them, I not only kept Wraith but bought more any time I found a store with something.

      Here's the sad part: I know the setting like the back of my hand, but I've never gotten to play Wraith.

      People have always been put off by the setting around here, thinking it's too dismal. I might be responsible for that, as I heavily emphasize the darker aspects of the game when I try to introduce them to it. But, to me, to do otherwise would largely take away from what's always been its main appeal to me - the sheer emotional terror. This isn't the terror of some mythical creature that's going to steal you into the night or some beast that's going to tear you apart. To me, Wraith is the most frightening cWoD line because of the realism inherent - and I don't mean "real ghosts," I mean the themes involved. Losing your family, living a life unfulfilled, dealing with your own self-destructive impulses, confronting the apparent meaningless of existence and struggling to maintain some sense of self in the face of that. Perhaps it's just me, perhaps it speaks ill of me to admit, but these are things I can relate to all too well. Wraith gave me some sense, in the worst of my years, that I wasn't alone in what I was feeling - that someone, in some way, understood that sense of despair and hopelessness well enough that they made Wraith.

      This ended up much longer, and much more personal, than I'd intended. But in summary, in my own weird, overly verbose and nihilistic way, I wanted to express my love of this game and gratitude to its creators.

              Despite making House Rules for (what felt like) half the game, I LOVED the setting. It felt like a necromantic cross between Beetlejuice and Mad Max (with much more, to be sure). The layout of the Underworld, the travails of the dead, and - for two or three background points - you could have a sword made out of someone’ soul. I thought the entire mythos was fascinating. Of all the 20th editions, I’m most looking forward to Wraith20.

              I remember what made me pick up Wraith. I was at a friend’s house reading through World of Darkness: Combat. I happened to lay the book down on its front cover and noticed a funny looking woman going Bruce Lee on two funny looking guys. In the hands of her assailants were swords that looked amazing.

              I asked my friend, “What game are these from?” pointing to the swords on the back cover.

              “I’m pretty sure those are supposed to be Soulsteel. From Wraith.”

              “Really? Do you have a copy?” He did, and it was less than a week before I acquired a copy of my own.

                          Wraith is my favorite RPG too! I love the setting and my particular interest is in the World of Wraith versus playing a wraith in a crossover or in the land of the living. I do love Vampire, and like Werewolf, but really enjoy the elements of story in Wraith. There are so many different possible permutations of how it can be played, whether high fantasy, adventure or more serious with the resolution of Passions, Fetters and seeking Transcendence. I think today's audience is much more than when it was first published and much of the gaming audience has also matured from being a teenager just wanting to go out, get treasure and fight things. (Though that can be fun too!) I really wish it had gone for the whole run and got the full treatment it deserved and hope much of that will be filled in in the anniversary edition which I am looking forward to!

                          I also like that Wraith is ambiguous when it comes to things like Transcendence or spiritual beliefs so that the storyteller and players can weave that in if and how they want. There are some games, even in the World of Darkness line, my friends, myself and others I have spoken with just won't play because some of the content is not compatible with our beliefs. I absolutely respect people's different views, but its nice when something can be open to everyone, and it increases your potential audience!

                          The idea of the Shadows adds for some great storytelling. We have never had any problems with it in the games I have played in, but a lot depends on the group, their synergy and how you game. The worlds to me are sandboxes and those things where there is not enough description or other elements are fine because it lets the storyteller get creative and the group too. The rules shouldn't box you in, but allow you to use them and add things as you need to and what helps make the story and play better.


                          My thanks for creating such a special and terrific game!

                                      To put it shortly I love just about everything in it.

                                              Wraith was and still is my favourite CWoD game. Given I'm prone to essay length posts too often, I won't go into heavy detail.

                                              A lot of it is a redux of what others have said: sheer variety, the wonderful style, how evocative and truly -personal- it was (doubly so if like me, you lost a significant other or other very close family member) and the sheer number of directions you could go with it; to say it was diverse and truly supported all sorts of settings, antagonists, time periods, play styles etc. is no understatement. Of the more mechanical aspects, for the longest time (outside of DA: Fae) I really liked their approach to Arcanoi, I -really- liked the Angst system (much more than the morality systems or lack-thereof a lot of CWoD had) as a good mechanism for presenting your character's gradual twisting of identity. The writing, at least in my opinion, was some of the most consistently well done and tight of the lines. There's a reason why some of its books got such high praise like Chanrel Houses of Europe: the Shoah and it was well warranted among the CWoD lines.

                                              It did have some rough spots, but I felt overall they were fairly minor, and often they were addressed regardless such as if you didn't want to have PCs portraying The Shadow for each other, the book certainly didn't disuade you or present it as "Hey, it's your game (but you're not doing it right)." like others did. I -never- felt I was being talked down to by the writers, something I can't say was true of some other game lines. Of course it sucked that 2nd Ed forgot to put in the system for gaining Pathos from Passions (big whoops), but at least we got it in Buried Secrets and as an errata! If there was one glaring error, it was that.

                                              The art direction alone was spectacular: it was one of those games where with multiple artists, it all tended to fit in very well rather than look out of place or clashing. It didn't hurt by Wraith's nature you could really go hog wild with artistic design! It may seem odd (and I don't wish to leave any artists out), but I can normally just point to one particular artist that was -the- major highlight of the earlier games. With Wraith there wasn't just one that really seemed to set the tone better than others for me. That was always nice as an artist myself.

                                              It's also the one game I didn't find a substitute for in NWoD that that dealt with similar critters and themes; there has been no substitution for it, it just hits all the right buttons for me. A note here, or a brushstroke there that resonated with Wraith for me (and to be clear, by substitution I don't mean redux or the like, but something that had a similar impact, made me like it as much or prefer it over a CWoD game dealing with that critter or tackled things in a way I preferred for the critter type and themes), but at the end of the day... I want Wraith. Perhaps something in the future of the nWoD will give me what I crave on things ghostly, but right now it's back to CWoD for that without complaint. It's the only one that's still managing to drag me back, that inexorable pull of Oblivion just cannot be resisted indefinitely.

                                              For me it's the only CWoD kickstarter that I not only want to see, but think is -necessary-. The material has been very difficult and expensive to find at times compared to a lot of the other lines. Having a 20th Ed would been a boon not only for cleaned up and collected material, but sheer accessability, and by dint of that, exposure to new players who aren't keen on tracking down some old or hard-to-get books en masse.

                                                      This might sound a little bit melodramatic, but Wraith played a part in saving my life. I suffered from crippling depression for a number of years, and Wraith really spoke to me at the time. Oblivion sounded tempting, to say the least, but what was really important to me was the Shadow. There's a bit in the Shadow Player's Guide that explains precisely what the Shadow is; that it isn't evil - selfish, shameful, and self-destructive, sure, but not evil - and is fundamentally a part of you. Hating your Shadow doesn't do much good, and trying to destroy it is tantamount to a lobotomy. Reconciling my self-loathing and destructive urges with who I am as a person was really important. I talked to my therapist about "one of my nerd games, where you play as ghosts" fairly often. And then I slowly crawled through all that shit.

                                                      I'm on the other side of that now, mostly. Wraith is forever going to be one of my favorite works in any medium. (On a cheerier note, I fully intend to get a Silent Legion tattoo sometime soon, as a giant flaming middle finger to Oblivion. I'm not enlisting in the Silent any time soon).
