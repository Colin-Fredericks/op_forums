                  Originally posted by Katrina View Post
                  Plasmics are not spirits. They are non-sentient or very alien "lifeforms" that are created out of Plasm and dwell almost exclusively in the Tempest. When the Garou refer to spirits used for making fetishes and found in the Umbra, they refer to the Middle Umbra, which is not the Underworld for wraiths which is also known as the Dark Umbra or Lower Umbra.
                  It's better to phrase that as "Plasmics are not [S]pirits." -- they *are*, in fact, "spirits" -- just spirits of the *Low* Umbra, rather than the Middle or High Umbrae (unless, of course, you want to go up to a given High Umbral Lord and try to persuade it into believing that *it* isn't a spirit... good luck with that...). It's very, very important to understand that both in-narratively and extra-narratively there are many terms used with different meanings/applications in the CWoD... and this is intentional. The writers *intended* for there to be a degree of misunderstanding between splats/settings, even on a gaming level (this was addressed in at least two books and on several Ask a Question confabs back in the day), wherein the same words/terms have different meanings/usages depending on who is using/saying them. (For example, the High Umbra for Mage is what the Garou refer to as the Deep Umbra, while the Deep Umbra for Mage is what Garou -- and most others who would use the term -- would refer to as "outer space"; this cross-use was directly addressed in one of the WW cosmology books.) Same thing with [S]pirits. The better clarification is to cite how most splats are more directly linked (if linked at all) with a given "level" of Reality(tm), and unless directly specified otherwise, their powers/whatnots interact with those levels and seldom with others.

                  Examples: Vampires are quite removed spiritually from much -- but not all -- of the broader universe: they have no spiritual presence in the Middle Umbra (hence, there are only one or two rare powers -- Thaumaturgy's Spirit Path, and the original work-up for Spiritus -- that have anything to do with it), a very weak presence in the High Umbra (and usually only accessible by accident or intense will via Auspex; otherwise, they merely project into the very near Penumbra), and a moderate presence in the Low Umbra (which they are, of course, more [un]naturally gravitating towards, and therefore have more rituals/powers connected to that level). Garou have super-strong affinities for the Middle Umbra, but a very weak one to the High Umbra (hence why usually only the more esoteric Stargazers, or rare Silent Strider or Uktena, have any connection there), and only the very bravest/wise (or foolhardy/stupid) venture far into The Other Worlds (the Deep Umbra of Mage's cosmology); their connection to the Low Umbra is all-but-nonexistent (save, of course, for the Silent Striders or more whackjob Uktena). Mages have a natural bend (usually) to gravitate to the High Umbra, but can individually be more linked to any of the Umbrae depending on their personal beliefs and paradigm/mystical praxis (this is actually covered better in Dark Ages:Mage, in terms of magic, than in Ascension or Sorcerers Crusade, but anywho...) -- the Low Umbra, for them, however, is the most dangerous, conceptually, to tread, as the spiritual forces there were more readily prone to ending the mage's life in such a way as to leave him as a wraith -- therefore still cognizant and aware -- but sans his Avatar/god-self, which of course sucks mightily for the character in question.... etc....

                  To take things a step even further, it is perfectly within their (perceptual) "rights" for, say, High Umbral spirits to not view Middle Umbral spirits as "spirits", proper, or vice-versa, simply because their spiritual makeup/identities are so different from each other. Same thing with Low Umbral essences relative to the Other Worlds: it is metaphysically perhaps more accurate to refer to the [C]orpus of Low Umbral beings as being [P]lasm, but that doesn't not make them "spirits" -- they are. They just aren't "spirits" of the metaphysical composition as, say, elementals found in the Middle Umbra, or "spirits" of abstraction found in the High Umbra... or super-weird shit that doesn't properly match *any* of those "types" and is found in Far Umbral realities out in the Deep Umbrae... (and we come full circle, where the Ethernaut mage and the Stargazer get to baffle each other somewhat by using the same terms to refer to very different [C]osmological realities...)

                              Plasmics.
                              As far as the Wraith mythos is concerned, plasmics are the underworld’s equivalent to “critters.” Sometimes they’re tiny critters. Sometimes not. The “distant cousin of epherma” is ectoplasm; a stuff similar (if not identical) to the substance that comprises a wraith’s corpus.

                              Summoning?
                              No idea…
                              Honestly, I’m not sure what the impetus behind such a summoning would be. Or who would do it.

                              With the understanding that plasmics are Underworld critters, I can’t think of a mentality or desire (however fictional and magical it might be) that would be willing to put the time and energy into summing a plasmic.

                              Not to say there are groups who don’t summon animals – I just think that’s more a “shamanic” sort of thing and those tend to summon more natural spirits. [Gafflings?] However, there are also people (and groups) who help (and harass) the dead. A few different groups all with different practices, but I’ll just generically call them “Necromancers.” Necromancers summon the Restless (sometimes the Shadow Eaten) and don’t seem to have much interest in plasmics.

                              On the other hand, that might be an interesting addition to a story; maybe errant (or dopy) necromancers summon a random Labyrinth plasmic instead Ghostly McGuffin. Think: the scene in Prometheus where they encounter vag-snake.

                              Fetishes
                              I’m not going to tell you that it’s *not* possible, but consider the philosophy behind how Fetishes (I’m assuming we’re talking WtA Background and not just random tiki masks) are made. It involves a “shaman” creating an object-vessel-dwelling for the spirit, one that resonates with the nature of said spirit. Then, the shaman coaxes (which has a variety of implications) the spirit to enter the vessel and divest itself of some power to make something more (the Fetish).

                              I suppose you *could* place a plasmic in an object. Personally, I think that would create a very weak haunted object, but that’s just my initial thought. They’d probably be more useful as raw materials for the forges.

                              Summary
                              In general, my thoughts are,”You could, but why would you?” And this is with my understanding and interpretations of Wraith. If you have an entirely different set of ideas about plasmics, run with it. If you want plasmics to emanate from wraith farts and have sapience, go for it. I will fully support it and be here to make your vision of intelligent-wraith-fart-plasmics come to fruition.

                                  I am not aware of anybody that makes regular use of summoned plasmics... they are kind of obscure as far as spirits go... even in Wraith they are a rarely-mentioned class of spirit.

                                  To summon one, I think at the very least that means drawing it from the shadowlands into the tempest. Assuming that they follow the same rules as any other underworld spirit, that means they need fetters if they are going to be there very long. Even wraiths versed in Lifeweb have difficulty in making fetters that are more than temporary. So this adds another step to the process that most spirits don't require. If you summon them, quickly put them in the fetish (If that can ever be a quick thing) it's not clear to me that being in the fetish will protect them from being once again banished to the tempest.

                                  Using Lifeweb as a model, it takes Lifeweb 4 to make a temporary fetter that requires a daily investment of willpower to maintain. Ouch. Lifeweb 5 is kind of a strange means of creating a more permanent fetter, which involves a wraith making a bargain with a living person. The living person gets a boon of the wraith, and becomes a permanent fetter (so long as the person lives) and then after death that person almost always becomes a wraith. The two wraiths get a free passion to protect one another, and the new wraith usually ends up the thrall of the former.

                                  Lifeweb 5 sounds a lot like a fetish agreement to me, so maybe it could work... But you may end up as your Plasmic's lunch after you die... 0_o

                                  Bringing it into the Skinlands from the Shadowlands may be even harder. But there is a lot that an underworld spirit can do to affect the skinlands anyway, without fully crossing over. It can be done (The Embody arcanos as well as the Risen are examples of this) but I get the general feeling that the Underworld doesn't like to let go of its own. So whatever you do it's gotta be strong enough to resist that pull of Oblivion.

                                  But this is all conjecture. Maybe plasmids aren't subject to needing fetters, they just think the tempest is nice and cozy.

                                      I think you're missing the point. It is a bias of applied terminology that says "x" vs."y" is this, that or the other. Note my referencing the "Deep Umbra" term for Mage and Werewolf: the same term referring to two distinctly separate things. It's a similar thing to using the term "spirit". As for Low Umbral beings, they *are* spirits, just differently stat'd/powered; being undead, they don't have *living stats* the way Middle and High Umbral spirits do, but metaphysically different stats (Corpus, Pathos and Willpower for Wraiths, which are much more "fluxed" than with the other realities because they don't recover their strength/power the same way, due to how their level of reality -- and their metaphysical existences -- works). It's very "though a looking glass, darkly", much as how vampires recover power only by feeding, but werewolves are (quite literally) living batteries of [Dynamism], which is why the latter regenerate so much more easily than vampires do, and they have considerably greater "juice" flowing through them, for the most part, than even many of the most powerful of the vampires do. The difference is, one is an active agent of [D]ynamic, living power, while the other is an [E]ntropic consumer of said power. Same thing with the spirits of the various Umbrae. They're all still spirits, even if their metaphysical make up is quite different.
