      Any wraith in the Shadowlands - even a "newbie" - can see into the Skinlands (a.k.a the living world), just by thinking about it. However, it looks "wrong" to them. They see the marks of Oblivion everywhere - the first traces of sickness, that small bit of rust . . . any sign of decay is immediately (and glaringly) obvious to them. But yeah, they can see everything - wraiths make great spies, in that sense.

      They can't actually do anything to Skinlands people/items, though. Barring specialized Arcanoi (wraithly powers), all they can do is watch. Their words are lost on the wind, their touch no more substantial than fog . . . wraiths are trapped on the wrong side of the Shroud, no matter how much they wish otherwise. That's part of what drives them to study forbidden Arcanoi like Embody or Possession, or to try to Rise . . . its their only hope, their one last chance to touch their loved ones (or crush their living enemies).

                      *nods* I play it that the Shadowlands is a part of reality, just on the other side of the mirror so to speak. That it shares the space of the living world, though the lands of the dead are filled with the memories of those things not around anymore as the only things the wraiths can physically touch and interact with. The Skinlands are still around them and they walk the same streets and such as the Quick, though the wraiths pass through the stuff of the Quick and get made incorporeal by it.

                      For example there is a house of someone in the Quick, it also is placed in the same location as the Shadowlands, though only the things broken and forgotten about are able to be touched and are still around for a wraith, who was possibly the previous occupant and the house is their Haunt. The wraith and the current living occupant both live in the same house, though the Shroud means they cannot interact properly, though of course a weakening come nighttime and certain Arcanos can make things happen, if the wraith doesn't mind breaking Charon's Law.

                      Only difference to me between the Skinlands and the Shadowlands is roads, railways or bodies of water in the latter can sometimes eventually become Byways that lead down into the Tempest. I tend to invision this as a road out of town eventually leading in a certain place to a Nihil like portal, usually this road is downhill or leading into a dark tunnel or some other shadowed or downwards bit of a journey.

                                      To answer more fully also, the Wraith stays in the Shadowlands, think of like two overlapping dimensions, where people populate the same space, though cannot interact with those of the other space. If a person could see the Shadowlands side, they'd pass right through a wraith they saw there and for the most part, everything is pretty much the same on each side, aside for the odd relic or Haunt over in the Shadowlands. A wraith can see Skinlands stuff just fine AFAIK, though it's sort of different. Maybe someone else can add more about that.

                                      To answer the last question, Ned doesn't leave the Shadowlands, they walk through the Skinlands building and end up on the other side, though in the Shadowlands equivalent still. As said above, you still share space with your loved ones as a wraith, though neither side can touch the other and the Shroud keeps the Quick from seeing the wraiths and Shadowland, though there are exceptions of course, such as Arcanoi usage. Phantasm, or dream walking is a great way for a wraith to interact and talk with a loved one, through their dreams perpetuating the idea of a loved one having recurring dreams of someone they lost.

                                              (Just jumping in...)

                                              It's not a game mechanic paradox, but rather a matter of thematics and "levels" of dimensions: when a wraith is favoring the "skinland"-side of things, it's reflective in the Shadowland of the *physical* wall in question -- he can pass through it via the rules as presented/indicated. *However*, when he "descends" deeper into the Shadowlands of the Low Umbra, things may reflect what exists in the physical world, but can vary greatly depending on how "removed" the wraith/location is, dimensionally speaking, from the Dark Penumbra nearest the Skinlands. (That is, there may not be any direction/spatial change in locality, but rather a change in "nearness" and "far-ness" relative to the interacting with the physical world.) Remember: even the Shadowlands (Dark Penumbra) have gradients and degrees of relative dimensionality -- the "closer"/"higher" to the Skinlands a wraith is, the more "alive"/"real" things will seem; the "further"/"deeper" into the Low Umbra he is, the more conceptual and boarder-line abstract things get (the Robin William's movie "What Dreams May Come" is an excellent example of this concept, and highly recommended by many for understanding/conceptualizing the Low Umbra). The "walls" that a wraith wouldn't readily be able to pass through are the ones in Stygia, deeper into the Low Umbra (though still within the broad reach of the Shadowlands/Dark Penumbra, as Stygia isn't as deep as, say, Duat or other "deeper/farther" realms, The Far Shores, et al). In the relatively rare instance wherein a physical wall(or what-have-you) and a near-Skinland "stygian" wall existed in the same spot and nearly the same "level" of dimensionality, it would be a cue to the wraith in question that someone has been mucking about in the place he's at (solidifying "reality" in a given place, 'porting in Stygian "materials" to the near-Skinland Dark Penumbra, or some sort of "witchy-pooh" is at work). Regardless of the reason, he'd have a pretty good idea that something's up because even though he can normally pass through a physical wall, he *wouldn't* be able to pass through the co-existing stygian wall (there is not paradox in this, at all -- it'd be the same as me being normally able to pass through air, but unable to pass through *that* section of air because of a static forcefield... same conceptual idea).

                                              Hope that helps!

                                                      Walls that exist in the Skinlands also exist in the Shadowlands, but as echoes of themselves. So a wraith can walk through them as per the rule of ouch.

                                                      However, a wall that only exists in the Shadowlands is solid to a wraith, and they must walk around it or find a door. Such walls were either destroyed, and so crossed over (think of this wall as the ghost of the original wall and thus as solid as a wraith, because they're made of similar stuff), or were fashioned from things in the Shadowlands to begin with (usually the corpora of wraiths, so again made of similar stuff to a wraith and so solid).

                                                      In Stygia and the Tempest, everything is either made there or crossed over there, so is solid to a wraith.

                                                      As per Ghost, a wraith could jump onto a moving train in the Skinlands, but would go incorporeal. She couldn't hold onto that Skinlands train, however, without one or more Arcanoi. The train isn't made of the same stuff as she is. She can walk through it, but can't touch or interact with it without Arcanoi.
