    Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
    A bit more on this:

    The books still refer to it as Tempest for history sections pre-Third Great Maelstrom (perhaps for convenience), but it indicates that it still functioned as a separate dimension. I take the descriptions to mean that the 'Gauntlet' between Tempest and Shadowlands used to be a lot weaker, and you could just step into it in some liminal spaces without the use of a Nihil, like a Shallowing in Mage terms. That would satisfy the conditions that Stygia used to be connected to and later severed from the Shadowlands.

    Functionally, I see it working not too differently from a Harrowing, but much milder. The realm would be taking the shape of a transitory landscape or narrative that symbolically reflects a traveling soul's (Dark) Passions, Memories, and so on. It would present a trial to overcome that resembles a stage in their Transcendence they struggle with, or unresolved conflict, etc.
    It could be that the Tempest and Shadowlands overlap to such a degree that denizens and structures of either realm are used as the stage for this, allowing for encounters that are tailor-made for a character's narrative. This keeps with the 'speed of story' travel time the Tempest has, too.

    Traveling groups are interesting, because it would be a chance for a character getting an outside perspective on their troubles while experiencing them. I never played a Group Harrowing, but I imagine the dynamic would be similar. And on a side note, Argos would probably still focus on travel, but with a slant towards 'questing' to complement the mechanic.

    I would love to see everybody else's take on a tame Tempest, because I think it's a pretty important element of the setting.
    I think that’s a good start. The gentle transition would help maintain the Sea as a different layer while still making it seem as if it was connected to the Shadowlands. I’d probably make the change from the shadowland to byways seamless, and the changeover from byway to [Tempest] similarly smooth. So you could sail to the River of Death from pretty much any Shadowlands body of water, and enter the [Tempest] proper by leaving the River, but going straight from the Shadowlands to the [Tempest] would still take a nihil.

    My watchwords for the modern Tempest are fury and storms. I don’t fully stick to the ocean themes but I do stick to those. For the early Tempest I would instead choose journeys and trials. I’m thinking landmasses and overland journeys are more common.

    That said, I think the Tempest should still be largely nautical in nature. A calmer ocean, but still more ocean than not. It means the harbingers don’t need too much rework. Most of their powers should still work with that setup.

    While the Dark Ages Tempest would not be a constant storm, there should still be the threat of Maelstrom level storms from time to time. In counterpoint there should also be the risk of Doldrums that future eras would never see.

                                                        One thing regarding the territorial conquests is that there is a distinct possibility that they were enacted by Fisher crusader Wraiths rather than Charon's Legions. There are several indications for this:

                                                        Some time - actually around the crusades - the Dark Kingdom of Iron's outline started to follow the spread of Christianity instead of the extend of the Roman Republic / Empire as it did before. The Fishers are mentioned to become the strongest faction in Stygia, and that requires a military power at least on par with the Legions. The conquests in return would have seriously bolstered the political influence of the Fishers, even within the Hierarchy - who are more likely to follow power rather than philosophical convictions.
                                                        That actually gives a good idea of just how powerful the influence of the Fishers would become until the Revolt, and it did not stop with it. Most notably they were still able to conquer the Dark Kingdoms of the Americas much later, after they had been expelled from Stygia.

                                                        For the setting, I think it is likely that the Shadowlands also mirrored this aspect of medieval Europe in that swathes of the Dark Kingdom of Iron were owned and under jurisdiction by the church, either by concession or conquest. Wr20 even mentions 'Death-Bishops', the ecclesiastic counterpart of the Deathlords, who could have been in control of these dioceses. That provides some interesting set-up for the later years after the Revolt, because while externally the Dark Kingdom of Iron appeared as a bloc, there would be a strong internal divide between 'church and crown'. And at least for some time, Fisher activity would not be solely restricted to the Far Shores.
