        Sad confused thoughts of the morning.
        Well, there can be some (apparent) overlap between a Passion & a Dark Passion . . . but the emotional core will be different. After all, there's a difference between wanting your wife to be happy, and wanting to keep her in a box.

        A Dark Passion is the dark side of the wraith's psyche, parts of himself that's he's not proud of, and tries to keep in check. As such, it is part of his personality . . . but its a drive that conflicts with his core goals.

        Take your example. In life, that wraith was probably the kind of guy who didn't want his wife to have any outside hobbies, or hang out with friends if he wasn't around, too. And if he found out she was talking to an ex-boyfriend, or attractive single guy, it'd eat him up inside. He wants her to be happy . . .but he can't help how unhappy he feels when he has to share her attention with others.

        And as a wraith, you see that dichotomy in the Passion/Dark Passion pairing. He truly loved her, he wants her to be happy. He doesn't want her to spend all her time grieving for him, because he can see how much its tearing her up. Seeing her smile and be happy again, that's his goal. Even if that means she finds new love with another guy.

        At the same time . . . she's his. When she spends her days mourning, and suffering, then she's thinking only of him. It shows how much she loved him. If she's moving on, then he's losing her. His one and only, his soul mate . . .his precious.

        So the wraith's Psyche, pursuing his Passion, wants to help her move on - to find her smile again. But his Shadow, nursing its Dark Passion, will keep trying to convince him that her efforts aren't real, aren't good enough. And they never will be:

        - That new hobby? Its just a distraction. She doesn't really like it. He should destroy it, so she can go find something she "really" likes.

        - That new club meeting? The other members aren't "really" her friends. He should wreck the relationships, before they hurt her - and so that she can go find some "real" friends to move on with.

        - That new guy who's flirting with her? He's just trying to get into her pants. And he's not good enough for her. So what if he's wealthy, handsome, and has a Nobel Peace Prize? She can do better. Run him off now, before he breaks her heart.

        The wraith's Shadow will never, ever admit that she's legitimately moving on. Because if she's moving on with her life, then she's going somewhere he can't follow. He's losing her. And in the vile, twisted parts of his soul that fuel a Dark Passion, he'd rather see her miserable and alone, mourning him for the rest of her life. Because then she's devoting her life to him. He gets to keep her. And she's his.

        But. . . he truly loves her. Just knowing that she was happy, that was enough to get him through the worst days. He'd have gone to Hell and back, just to see her smile. And the wraith's Psyche wants her to be happy again. Even if that means losing her . . . It will break his heart, but if that will make her happy, he'll do it.

        And there you have it. The desire to protect a loved one, to see her happy again, versus the urge to keep her as his own private treasure. Those dueling impulses are going to bring Psyche and Shadow into direct conflict . . . which is what being a wraith is all about. Will you listen to the angel on your shoulder? Or the devil?

        Can you even tell the difference?

                                                    Katrina - It's largely contextual, both in the setting and the mechanics. Using W:tO 2nd as a basis, Aggravated Damage is detailed on pages 232/3, and says "Aggravated injuries may be healed only if the wraith Slumbers and spends three Pathos points." Syntax breaks this down to "A wraith can heal their own Aggravated injuries by Slumber and spending 3 Pathos," because things like the Legacy Background (pages 130/1), and the Moliate 2 power Sculpt (151) offer different ways of healing Aggravated Damage. It also makes sense in the context of the setting - Usurers are a very powerful Guild, and their having access to one of the few powers that instantly heals Aggravated Damage only strengthens their stranglehold (and one doesn't want Masquers to be solely responsible for healing Aggravated. They can't be trusted). Finally, the book makes no specific exception to the type of damage healed, and in fact, only explicitly says "heal wounds[,]" implying that the damage - of whatever type - is less important than the Corpus/Health levels (i.e., Charitable Trust "targets" the Health Levels, "bypassing" or "ignoring" the damage type).

                                                    Cheers!
