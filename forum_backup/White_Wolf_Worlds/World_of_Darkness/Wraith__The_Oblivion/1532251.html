      Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
      Might I please ask if there is any list of each Necropolis mentioned in official sources?
      Not that I know of. I think the general assumption is that if a place has enough Wraiths within the Dark Kingdom of Iron it qualifies for a Citadel, so an exhaustive list would be a bit pointless. It's a bit like asking whether a city qualifies as a metropolis or not. The relative size and strategic importance within the region seems to be a factor, too. A comparatively smaller city might become a Necropolis if it is the largest settlement within a region.

      Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
      I know that not every city has it’s own Necropolis and I’m interested in finding out whether there’s a specific type of city that is required to support such an outpost of Stygia.
      As mentioned, for large cities a Citadel seems to be the default assumption. I can think of a few special cases that influence whether or not a city becomes a Necropolis:

      - Large Renegade or Heretic enclaves
      Probably rare and not all that large. According to the books they dot the landscape between greater Necropoles or exist inside them in hiding. There are some spots in which a city is contested (Dublin, Paris) but as far as I know, there aren't any large cities within the Dark Kingdom of Iron that are under Renegade or Heretic control proper. Perhaps with the exception of Vatican City.

      - Places of large calamities or violence
      The Dark Kingdom of Wire being the prime example here, but similar places could exist on a smaller scale elsewhere. Something caused the death of a large number of people and the Wraiths stuck to the place, resulting in a large Shadowland settlement without a Citadel. Although many of these would have been reclaimed by Stygia in time, some may have kept their independence. I see those mostly situated in border regions with other Dark Kingdoms.

      - Fallen Citadels
      Again, I don't recall any city mentioned that has fallen to Spectres and is controlled by them. The aforementioned places of calamity and violence are good candidates, in which a city was left open for invasion. Hiroshima and Nagasaki come to mind if they ever were part of the Dark Kingdom of Iron in the first place.

      - Skinland Necropoles
      Conjecture, but I would assume places with unusually large burial sites attract a large number of Wraiths that could result in independent communities. Paris is a good example.

      - Large cities outside of the Dark Kingdom of Iron
      This one should be obvious, but it depends on where you draw the borders.

      Overall it seems to be largely up to the ST how they want their cities in their chronicles.

      Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
      Incidentally, it has also occurred to me to wonder how long it took for the living city to which a Necropolis was attached to equal the population of it’s sepulchral counterpart (Given that for much of human history people lived fast and died young).
      Hard to say, but I would assume at least a four or five generation delay until a somewhat sizable Wraith population accumulates. I generally don't think a Necropolis has anywhere near an equal population to its Skinland counterpart. Becoming a Wraith is an exception, and Wraiths don't stick around forever either.

          If you want to investigate wraith demographics this passage from 'Ends of Empire' detailing London might be of interest:

          "Seven million human beings live in Skinlands London. The vast majority don’t become wraiths when they die — less than one soul in 20 emerges across the Shroud in an environment as relatively calm and peaceful as the Great Britain of the 1990s. Far more people become wraiths upon death in times of great calamity and suffering, and wraiths can last (with care and luck) a very long time. In the modern day, about a million active wraiths inhabit the London Necropolis"

          Do keep in mind that London isn't a typical example. It's ancient, American cities of similar size will not have built up a wraithly population quite this large. Other European metropoli might not quite measure up either, cause London is known as the "First Necropolis". In the Third Great Maelstrom's wake it was Stygia's first major outpost in the Shadowlands. Then again; mainland cities have seen more war, and other atrocities, in recent centuries.

                              For the purposes of the game, most ghosts in the Shadowlands are young. There are some old wraiths in the Shadowlands, but they need their Fetters to be there. The need for Fetters limits how many old ghosts can really be in the Necropoli to manage things. Really old ghosts that lost their Fetters have to reside in Stygia. I imagine most STs do not make Stygia an important part of their chronicles, and that most PCs either never visit or visit very little. If you want the PCs to encounter older, powerful Gaunts, an ST needs to utilize Stygia more.

                              Stygia probably would be utilized more except there's never been a sourcebook for it. While some descriptions have been provided in several sourcebooks, it still means every ST would have to put in a lot of work to make a "living" city that would inspire STs and PCs and provide lots of hooks. Much like we never got a book on the Heretics, a full blown sourcebook on Stygia was one of the great failures of developing the game line.

                              Long term survival as a wraith is really about how strong their Shadow is and whether it can overwhelm the Psyche and turn the wraith into a spectre. That contest is probably determined fairly early - probably within a few decades for most wraiths. Any wraith that survives to a century can probably expect a long time of existence, though probably in Stygia.

                              There's always going to a certain amount of decline in all age cohorts over the years through violence, Maelstroms, spectres, finally falling to one's Shadow, and so on, but it is probably small most of the time for those wraiths not actively involved in military operations of the Legions. The big exceptions would be the times of the Great Maelstroms when all hell breaks loose and many wraiths of all ages and anywhere in the netherworld are destroyed.
