    Wraith Dark Ages History

    06-30-2023, 02:42 PM
    Dataweaver mentioned the idea of Dark Ages Wraith in the Magic in the Underworld thread and it got me thinking about what that would entail, historically. I threw together a few thoughts of what I know about that era and I welcome everyone to add more.


    Basic Setting Points

    A Dark Ages Wraith would necessarily take place between the First and Second Great Maelstroms, which happen because of the sacking of Rome and the Black Plague respectively. If we use the 13th century like other Dark Ages it is close to the end of that era.

    Stygia was sacked during the First Maelstrom and Charon rebuilt it as an Empire. By the 13th century it should be well established, but the cracks should be starting to show.

    The Guilds were still legal and they were rising in power in Stygian society. For most of this era they are not a unified force. They would come together to achieve that full political power until right before the 2nd Maelstrom. That process would already be in the works, though.

    Christianity was becoming more and more prominent in society (hence the Fishers). How prominent depends on where in this stretch of time, but by the end of it they’d established their own Far Shore. The Fisher Rebellion itself happens in the wake of the 2nd Maelstrom, but the foundations would happen well before then.

    Transcendence was still accepted and encouraged and the Far Shores were still promoted as places of healing and Transcendence. It is not clear when the Shining Ones started falling, but Stygia didn’t even look into the matter until the Fisher Rebellion.

    That said, the Ferrymen largely left Stygia after Charon declared himself Emperor. Most people who depart for the Far Shores via Stygia are doing so by other means.




    The Elephant Missing From the Room: The Tempest

    The Third Great Maelstrom in the 1600s is said to have turned the Sunless Sea into the Tempest and permanently separated the Shadowlands from the rest of the Underworld. So what does an Underworld without a Tempest even look like? I don’t know, but we have a few clues:

    Argos existed before the Third Great Maelstrom and it is specifically named after a mythical Greek ship. Presumably some sort of navigation was needed in the Underworld even back in the Dark Ages and it was more sea related than anything else.

    The River of Death has existed since Antiquity, so byways in some form or fashion were still a thing.

    Stygia and the Far Shores were always located on the Sunless Sea, which does not correspond to any kind of real world sea. Or at least much of it doesn’t.

    The Maelstrom was said to have separated the Shadowlands from Stygia, so the two used to be more connected.




    Possible Elements to Explore

    Charon’s descent - Some of Charon’s edicts are said to have come from his Shadow, though it is unclear which ones. Most of the potentials are yet to come but there should be some subtle indications all is not right for his Psyche.

    The New Far Shores – The Fishers and other established their own Far Shores, presumably without Shining Ones. There’s a lot to explore in what that means, how they found the space, how quickly they fell, etc.

    Doctrine and Dogma - For a variety of reasons, the Fishers as a group would have a major disconnect with the beliefs they espoused in life. It could be worth exploring the potential crises of faith and how that led to the Fall of so many in the faction.

    Other Dark Kingdoms Swallowed – It’s not talked about much but there were probably quite a few Dark Kingdoms that spanned Europe before the First Maelstrom. This is likely mostly resolved by the 13th century, but a few holdouts may remain until the 2nd Maelstrom.
    ​

          IIRC, the biggest obstacle to a Dark Ages: Wraith (besides the fact that they'd quit doing Wraith books at that point) was that before ... I want to say around the 1600s ... there weren't really much in the way of Necropoli in the Shadowlands the way there were in the modern game.

          My personal work around for this was that Dark Ages: Wraith would essentially be two different games.

          The first (Dark Ages: Wraith) would've focused on relatively younger restless dead still tied to the Skinlands from between the 6th century and the 13th century "present", who were largely unaware of Stygia and any of its problems or politics. Instead they are completely focused on their own small forts, castles, towns and cities and surviving day to day existence as restless dead. To them, this is Purgatory, where they have to purge themselves of their sins before being worthy of Heaven, and if they happen to come across Stygian wraiths, those are clearly denizens of Limbo who may wish to drag them down into Hell. This would be a medieval gothic low-fantasy setting, with knights and priests and bandits and what have you, where the people have to occasionally deal with freakish demonic monsters (specters and such) as well as their own moral weaknesses (Shadows). Sort of like Army of Darkness with more psychodrama and less slapstick. And it's a society that would've been entirely swept away when the Great Maelstrom of the Black Plague hit.

          The second (Dark Ages: Stygia) would've been the wraiths of Stygia, primarily Roman era survivors of the First Great Maelstrom who would be a bit more experienced and powerful than your average starting character. This would heavily revolve around Stygian politics and plots, the Guilds and Legions, and all the other baggage that Stygia comes with. Basically, the political aspects of Game of Thrones, HBO's Rome series, and film noir tropes set against an ancient backdrop.

              Some key events between the first and second Great Maelstroms for reference


              476 – ~1000
              Rebuilding of Stygia with Charon as emperor, senators become Deathlords 1,2,3
              Departure of the Ferrymen 1,2,3
              Foundation of the Legions and the Imperial Order of the Sickle 1,2,3
              Restoration of the Onyx Tower and creation of the Seawall in Stygia 3
              Decline of the Shining Ones and rise of the Fishers 2

              ~1000 – ~1200
              First Crusade (1096 – 1099)
              Treaty of Paradise between Fishers and Charon (tithe in exchange for souls) 2
              Adaption of feudal systems, formal creation of the Hierarchy 1,2,3
              Introduction of Thralldom 2,4
              Proliferation of Renegade enclaves 5
              Second Crusade (1145 – 1149)
              Third Crusade (1189 - 1192)
              Institution, rise and War of the Guilds 1,2,3
              Dictum Mortuum† 1,2,3
              Foundation of the Piercers of the Veil in Germany (monastic ghost investigator society) 6

              ~1200 – 1347†
              War of the Guilds (ctd.) 1,2
              Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, Seventh Crusades (1202 – 1270)
              Sack of Constantinople (1204)
              Albigensian Crusade (1209 – 1229)
              Foundation of the Shadow Inquisition (1223) 7
              Great Famine of 1315 – 1317
              Hundred Years War (1337 – 1453)
              Black Death (1347 – 1351)


              Unless definitive dates are given, all years are approximations
              †Different books place the marked events at slightly different points in time
              1 Wr20, 2 Wr2e, 3 Hierarchy, 4 Player’s Guide, 5 Renegades, 6 Ghost Hunters, 7 DA: Inquisitor

                          Some thought on the Guilds:

                          The Guilds started as collegia, a lose umbrella term for occult orders, mystery cults, social societies, or simply interest groups. During the feudal period, new Wraiths brought the concept of guilds with them and found an already compatible framework in the Hierarchy. Charon, ever eager to copy what was happening in the Skinlands, supported the consolidation of collegia into guilds. This is practically identical to the Skinlands history of guilds, where the consolidation and organization happened during the late 11th and early 12th centuries. By the mid 13th century, one could find as many as 100 different guilds in a single large city.

                          The ensuing competition and rather ruthless methods of dealing with it are equally mirrored in the War of the Guilds. While not an open war as such, the conflicts between Guilds themselves and non-guild artisans and crafters surely had their share of aggressive absorption, breaking, and political maneuvering. The books already cover some examples in their history sections. As No One of Consequence said, this is great soil for political intrigue games.

                          What that also means however is that during the 13th century, the Guild landscape would be very different from the 16 Guilds of the modern era. Instead of being defined by their Arcanoi, it is more likely that Guilds were defined by their trade, and there were much more of them. Some of the Guilds would already have started strong and defined (Artificers), while others were just one among many. It is likely that the different factions within the modern Guilds are the result of mergers or takeovers, such as the fusion of healers, bankers, and moneylenders within the Usurers. A Guild-Wraith would be more specialized in their Arcanoi spread towards a specific task, and it is even possible that some Arcanoi or arts have vanished together with their trade and Guild.


                          Some thoughts on Necropoles:

                          With the conception of the Hierarchy and the implementation of feudal structures, it is easy to map the Skinlands roles of medieval society onto the Shadowlands. A large city would have a ruling noble and the presence of guilds and military, which then collects tithe from the land granted to the noble. There are of course going to be obvious differences, as Hierarchy points out, because the only commodity of the Underworld are souls. By and large, the overall infrastructure is very similar though. The value of the land would depend on how many new Wraiths it produces that could be used as recruits, Thralls, or raw material.

                          While there would be no Citadels as such, castles would be the logical substitute, especially if they had been subject to siege before and parts of them have crossed the shroud. Monasteries may provide an alternative, but are probably less favored. Some sort of communal shelter is likely, because ‘regular’ Maelstroms were still as dangerous as they are now, and it factors into the protection a noble can provide to peasants in return for their tithe.

                          In the IMO very likely scenario presented by No One of Consequence that the larger part of the ‘rural’ Shadowlands populace falls into the category of Wraiths who think themselves in purgatory, I fully expect Hierarchs to play into that and guilt-pressure them into servitude. That would be in direct violation of the Treaty of Paradise, but let’s be honest, the Hierarchs would take the treaty about as seriously as the Dictum Mortuum.

                          As for the tithe, it is of course a lot more situational than a regular harvest, but a wider spread of smaller communities would be helpful in finding Drones or alert Legion knights towards a Spectre. Dark Kingdom of Jade runs with that idea and the Way of the Farmer as a dedicated Arcanos, but I think convincing a Wraith reporting and collecting lost souls in need of ‘purification’ is their duty is easier.

                              Some thoughts on geopolitics:

                              Gauging the extent of the Dark Kingdom of Iron is difficult, since there are not many sources. We know it began in the Shadowlands of the Mediterranean and expanded outwards. Up to the first Great Maelstrom it followed the outlines of the Roman Empire, towards Northern Africa, Central and Eastern Europe, and Britain. Post second Great Maelstrom it tended to follow the spread of Christianity, which is an interesting point in regards to the aftermath of the Fisher Revolt. There is also the important question of the involvement of Stygia and the Fishers in the Crusades, but that warrants an examination on its own.
                              Geographically, this leaves a few holes on the map; there is room for a possible Dark Kingdom of Ice in Scandinavia, Northern Germany, Denmark, Scotland, Iceland, the Shetlands, Faroe Islands, and the modern Baltic Nations.

                              Eastwards are the Kievan Rus and the most important force of upheaval at the time, the Mongol Empire. The Golden Horde is invading the whole damn world and with it also parts claimed by Stygia, in the Balkans, Hungary, Byzantine Thrace, Poland, parts of Germany and Austria. They stop and turn at Vienna in 1241 when Ogodei dies and they need to decide whose turn is next.
                              I recall reading that Wraiths moved with Horde (there was a story about a moving Haunt), but the source eludes me at the moment, and my first impulse would be that in the Shadowlands the Mongol Invasions were used as an expansionist opportunity by the Dark Kingdom of Jade – someone with more knowledge of eastern Wraith history please correct me on this if I’m wrong. It may also be that the Mongol Empire represented a Dark Kingdom† of its own, given that China got the same treatment as the rest of the world. Either way, the invasions would also be a major point of conflict in the Shadowlands.

                              Mantaqat Khayal, the middle eastern Shadowlands, have already been subject to the Keening for 600 years in the 13th century, so there is little information. One possible option is that Wraiths of the area followed the Muslim conquest to escape the situation, and now exist as an isolated Dark Kingdom in Northwest Africa and the Iberian peninsula. If so, it is one of the old Kingdoms in decline, as the ongoing Reconquista likely parallels expansion of the Dark Kingdom of Iron.

                              According to Mummy 2e, Neter-khertet, the Shadowlands of the Dark Kingdom of Sand had already fallen to Stygia, implied to coincide with Roman rule in Egypt at the turn of the millennium. It does not seem like the native Wraiths that had retreated to Amenti attempted to re-seize control after the first Great Maelstrom. But maybe they did and the lands are now contested territory.


                              †The wiki mentions Erlik Khan in the Amenti entry, but I’m unable to find further sources on him.

                                      Some thoughts on the Crusades:

                                      Before moving on to the Fishers, I want to first look at the Crusades from the perspective of the Underworld, and mainly from the perspective of Stygia.
                                      While Shadowland conflicts often mirror the conflicts of the Skinlands, I could not find any hints of Charon or the Hierarchy being invested in the various fronts of the Crusades. This could be either an omission because focus was on the larger historical picture, or Stygia managed to hold conflicted territories from before the first Great Maelstrom and did not care much.
                                      However, assuming the existence of additional Dark Kingdoms and Stygia’s starvation for resources after the restoration period, it would be likely that the Legions fought alongside the living armies in Scandinavia, Andalusia, Anatolia and the Levant. It’s also the more interesting variant, imo.

                                      The Legions did follow the Crusades in either case (undoubtedly along with contingents of Fisher missionaries) to pick up the valuable resources the wars left behind. This resulted in a large influx of Relics and souls, which upon crossing the Shroud eventually distributed themselves into different camps. Some of the Christian and Muslim warriors simply continued their conflict in the Shadowlands, while those realizing themselves not in the respective afterlives they were promised either joined Stygia or the Fishers. Since the latter only presented a viable option for Christians (the Fishers are very much Cross, not Crescent), there is the question whether an analogous faction existed for Muslims. Wr1e at least mentions them “waiting to be judged at their respective temples”, so there is that. I can imagine quite a few making the pilgrimage to Mecca in search for answers, only to find it impenetrable and ravaged by an eternal Maelstrom and the Keening.
                                      Anyways, this swelled Stygia’s Legions and the Fisher’s ranks, both military and civilian. By the time after the second Great Maelstrom, the Fishers had become the largest faction in Stygia, so they would be well underway to that point in the interim.

                                      Ashen Knight explores many of the themes a Wraith-Knight might find themselves confronted with, even if they don’t have to drink blood or fear the sunlight. I think much of it could be transported to Wraith, or at least provide an option for tie-ins. It’s certainly an attractive archetype for a player character, with a large enough variety of factions to declare for. Even the Guilds might hire some mercenaries for their own war efforts, and others would pick up the sword again as Renegade robber-knights.

                                      One thing the Wraith books do not touch on is Jerusalem. There is plenty of information for Vampire and some for Mage, but what was the city like for Wraiths? How many of the Crusaders that fell on the way would have continued their trek in search of salvation? What about the Fishers?

                                                          The creeds that pretty much every Christian of the time (and many, many today) used say that Christ descended to the dead and rose again. If some of the oldest Fishers claimed to have been there for that event and been commissioned by Him to spread the Church in the Underworld, that would be plausible to most incoming Christian wraiths. Even if they claimed to be empowered by those who were there, that would still work. In fact, it would be quite notable if they didn't claim that.

                                                          A Dark Age Wratih wouldn't have to say if that was true or not, in fact its probably better if doesn't. But consider the differences in the character of the Fishers actions depending on what they believed. Consider the Fisher Far Shore. If they were just a motley crew of lost Christians, then setting that up (or believing it if someone else set it up) was an act of desperation and a usurpation of forces they did not understand. On the other hand, if they thought they were commissioned by God to advance the Church in the Underworld then it was built with authority and a sense of duty. So much about the time period changes based on this one question.
