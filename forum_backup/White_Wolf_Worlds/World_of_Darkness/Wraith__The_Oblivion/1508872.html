    Need help to flesh out a small necropolis in a WW game

    04-19-2023, 06:26 AM
    Hello !

    I run a play-by-forum game of Werewolf located in Scotland and the story will probably see more and more Wraith crossover (main game would still be WW) as the players dig deeper into the subject. I have a very low knowledge on wraith atm, just the basic so I'm not completely butchering it.

    I'm trying to adapt it in a simple way while trying to keep the essence of the game. To simplify the crossover wraith, and other dark umbra denizen, are a specific family of spirits that is not well known to garou, except a few like silent striders, and impervious to most gifts. That's the actual adaptation rules if I'm not wrong.

    For those of view that knows both games, I see some familiar names from the WW forums answering questions, how would you adapt wraith specifics lore or systems to be integrated seamlessly ?

    Now in a little while my players will be going to the Dark Umbra to investigate on a spectre, they don't know yet what it is. From what I got wraith aren't really powerful and they arent feeling too good when a spectre is around so the local wraiths will be happy to oblige since the newly arrived spectre is scaring them, but they may ask for something in return in hope to obtain favours from creature that can run errands for them in the skinlands.

    Now the issue is that I have a bit of trouble imagining the life of a wraith : who they are, what's their fetters and passions and why, what's his day to day. Probably because I don't know the game much but I have read the rules and I'm still having a bit of trouble imagining their "life".
    For the purpose of the game I would like to flesh out the settlements as it's very likely it will be the necropolis my players will mostly interact with and I wanted it to know if some of you may have some basic ideas for residents, plots that could be happening around, thins like this. It doesn't have to be fancy or anything.

    The main idea here is to have something ideally not too big, with no "counterpart" in the skinlands anymore, ruins at most. If anyone is interested here are some points regarding the settlement :
    - It's in the highlands, in the cairngorms, most likely more around the north.
    - Long long ago, would have been a pictish religious or sacred site.
    - In way later ages, it had changed to a small village with peculiar/pagan funeral rituals that kept fuelling the small necropolis there.
    - Around the 18/19th century bigger cities had grown around and the already struggling small village died from starvation
    - The necropolis is old and big enough to collect newly born wraith around and have a bit of population, but not big enough so the Hierarchy keep a vigilant eye on it. Like maybe 2 or 3 wraith from the Hierarchy that would be here to maintain order, but with enough leeway to accept bribes or similar thing?

    I hope this wasn't too much, I can get carried away about the game I run ! I'll be ofc glad to answer any questions, or even tell me if something don't add up.

      Just 2 or 3 Hierarchy wraiths seems like an incredibly small number for even a small Necropolis. All Hierarchy Necropoli have an extensive bureaucracy and military structure in place. This would have deteriorated a lot if there's hardly any souls to be Reaped anymore, but there'd still be some Hierarchy hierarchy in place. But maybe you don't have a community of hundreds of ghosts in mind? If you just want dozens I suggest having this village's Shadowlands counterpart just be a few Haunts.

      Something in between could be having the village be an outpost of a larger nearby Necropolis. For instance Edinburgh, cause it's quite fleshed out: https://whitewolf.fandom.com/wiki/Edinburgh_(WOD) With a couple of Marshals, one from the Skeletal Legion and one from the Iron Legion, and their underlings keeping the peace.

              A Necropolis only really exists as a processing point for Stygia to organize new souls for the Legions. Full blown Necropoli are probably only going to exist in Glasgow and Edinburgh in Scotland. Though there may be smaller versions in Inverness and Aberdeen to handle the geographic spread. These lesser Necropoli might be ruled by a single Overlord of one of the Legion, or a council of Overlords, rather than Anacreons. And they probably report to the Anacreons of one of the larger Necropoli closest to them.

              Funeral rituals don't "fuel" Necropoli. A Necropoli is just an administrative center with lots of wraiths. At best, they'd be considered some form of Memoriam which can provide Pathos to individual wraiths. And unless those rites are still being done, there's no benefit to current wraiths.

              So given your description of the area the Garou are exploring, there probably are not any Necropoli at all. But that does not mean there are not any wraiths. All sorts of places have ghost stories. A ruined castle, a small tavern in the country, an abandoned graveyard, and any number of desolate locations can have ghosts. And some small towns are known for having lots of them. So don't worry about creating a "Necropolis". That is irrelevant to your needs.

              Instead, you will want to create probably one (or more) Haunt (an area which wraiths use as a refuge). A Haunt is just a game term for what we would normally call a haunted house or other location known for ghost sightings. It is basically a place where the Shroud is weak, allowing wraiths to use their powers to affect the living/Skinlands better. Then you want to populate the area with some wraiths. It is not necessary for all wraiths to dwell in the same Haunt or even dwell in any Haunt.

              You really just want to think of your classic ghost stories (real or fictional) in creating your wraiths. You just need to worry about who are they, and what is keeping them here instead of moving on. You don't even need to try to tie that into concepts like Fetters or Passions. That is probably not needed. If you want to identify what Legion the wraith is supposed to be in, that is simply based on how the wraith died so it's generally simple to assign. While enrolled in a Legion, probably all these wraiths are "free wraiths" - ordinary ghosts doing their own thing.

              Far from a Necropolis, but closer to this area, there might be a bureaucrat from the Hierarchy who does process new wraiths, register them into the Legions, and so. That wraith probably has the rank of Regent and may have a few flunkies. He would be the highest ranking member of the Hierarchy, and sends regular reports to whatever Necropolis has responsibility for that area. He probably travels every now and again to check up on things, but probably leaves the other wraiths alone. He has a lot to do. It is entirely possible your Garou PCs will never meet a ranking officer of the Hierarchy during the time they are there. To simplify things, you probably don't want them to do so. If the topic comes up, the wraiths can simply mention that the closest representative of the Hiearchy is some distance away and has lots of things to do.

              In terms of what these wraiths do daily, they need to collect Pathos to survive. So they need to be around people engaged in whatever Passion that provides Pathos to the wraith. Otherwise, they probably just keep to themselves and look for ways to keep themselves entertained. Observing people and talking to some of the other local wraiths probably qualifies.

              Spectres are not necessarily more powerful than wraiths (and wraiths can be extremely powerful on their side of the Gauntlet - just because wraiths often find it hard to project power in the Skinlands does not mean they are not powerful in the Shadowlands). It is just that they are malevolent. They also don't do well in the Skinlands. Most spectres, in fact, cannot access the Shadowlands at all. But the less powerful ones can like Dopplegangers (wraiths who have fallen to their Shadow), or Striplines (spectres created from children who died terribly under the age of 10 - these tend to not last long in the Shadowlands; they are not actually a separate "caste" of Spectres but a category that can apply to different castes). Spectres tend to have too much Oblivion in them to be able to manifest in the Shadowlands for long.

              But certainly any wraiths in the area would want to get rid of a spectre. And if the military forces of the Hierarchy are far away, they would probably welcome interlopers in the Dark Umbra (like your Garou PCs) to get rid of it for them.

              So for your needs, I would keep things restricted to a Haunt for the wraiths to congregate in for shelter (maybe an abandoned church cemetery). Have a few places in he Skinlands that different wraiths "haunt" because that is where they do to harvest Pathos, watch over their Fetters, or serve some other need for them. Not all your wraiths need to be at the Haunt at the same time. If you want more variety, create a few more Haunts. But you probably want to keep the number of wraiths manageable.

              In terms of the wraiths themselves, while more recent wraiths are more likely to exist than older wraiths, you probably want a wide variety of ages and concepts. There is no reason why you can't have a Roman centurion who died at the Battle of Mons Graupius, one of the early Gaels who conquered Scotland from the Picts, a wife to a warrior who died fighting for Robert the Bruce, a Highland warrior of the Jacobite era, and then some modern people. Create whatever NPCs who need to provide an interesting experience for your Garou PCs. You just need a reason why each one remains tied to the Shadowlands and have not moved on.

                          You don't need more Wraiths to make this work, you need them more active. There are a lot of Wraiths, potentially millions in the western Shadowlands alone. However there impediments to recognizable hauntings. There are social impediments like underworld laws against interacting with the living, and metaphysical impediments like the Shroud and the Fog (which function very much like the Gauntlet* and the Delirium respectively) which make it very difficult for Wraiths to do anything memorable. What you need for more ghostly activity then is Wraiths willing to risk breaking the law and locations where they can easily act and there are enough humans around for some of them to remember.

                          *Mostly. Wraiths can see across the Shroud easily, but they don't really cross it much. Instead they have powers that let them reach across it.

                                              Just a few ideas or suggestions from our WOD Imperium campaign (set in the Victorian Age/Savage West), so the era may not work for you but maybe some of the ideas might be of use.

                                              Our head canon is that the Dark Umbra both the dark penumbra/shadowlands and dead kingdoms were impacted earlier by the conquest of the new world, maelstrom’s become common and a Great Maelstrom causing the tempest to become volatile since the 1600s.

                                              This resulted in the Hierarchy controlling areas of the western world but with much difficulty wilderness areas such as Scotland effectively isolated for long periods of time.

                                              The British Isles being an old land of the dead largely cut off from an already decentralised Stygia and until the 17th Century ruled by its Overlord Anacreon William The Conqueror.

                                              The conqueror is disposed by Henry Tudor despite the efforts of The Wife’s to stop him, The Tudor is aided by a group of Corax based in The Tower.

                                              The Tudor enforces a strict policy of capturing or soul forging future English and Scottish Royals, including Elizabeth I, but notes that successive rulers fail to become wraiths seeming to lack the will possessed by rulers from more murderous times.

                                              The werewolf aspect is we use the lore from Ghost Towns, in that most of not all of the tribes have rituals to enter the Dark Umbra, that the Storm Eater and reality battle between the Technocracy and Traditions has made the Gauntlet & Shroud very temperamental and volatile.

                                              Our WOD is much more integrated with the Dark Umbra home to dark spirits inhabiting the blasted hearths and tempest as well as Wraiths and the Artificers Guild use Momento Rites (wraith sorcery) to reinforce Haunts into Necropoli (maybe something for your settlement village).

                                              The Scottish Lochs are great roiling paths deeper into the tempest and haunted by phantasm great beasts.

                                              Appreciate you don’t want to incorporate too much Wraith lore but hopefully some ideas there.​

                                                  Ok, I'm not an expert in Wraith lore but I am a ST Werewolf since late 90's and, as far as I know (and that knowledge I used during my games everytime my PC meet any wraith):

                                                  1. Every wraith needs Fetters to exist. It can be a place, living creature (human or animal) or object. Most wraiths have at least few of them. Destroying any of them sends wraith away for some time (so they basically cannot interact with PCs for that time). Destroying all of them annihilate the wraith. Getting hold onto one of the Fetters give control over the wraith (it's some kind of blackmail, you bend the wraiths will by threatening the fetter).
                                                  2. Every wraith has a few goals. These are called Passions. They can be as simple as "giving back the book that I borrowed" to as complicated like "protect all soldiers". If those passions are fulfilled the wraith will transcendent and is no more.
                                                  3. Every Wraith has a dark side called the Shadow. This side of his personality tries to destroy the wraith. It manifests as voice in wraith's head.
                                                  4. There are some wraiths that let the shadow take control. These wraiths are called Spectres and basically are some kind of evil spirits
                                                  5. Almost everyone can become a wraith as long as it is a mortal. The way that he or she died matters not. Many supernaturals can interact with wraiths and Shadowlands (some mages, Giovanni/Hecata vampires) but almost none of them can become one. Of course it is your game so you can decide otherwise.

                                                  Now I know from my fellow WoD fans that it's a big simplification of wraith but is accurate enough to be used during my games. It's the story that metters, not the mechanics. If you drop down the entire Hierarchy vs Renegades vs Heretics conflict down (you can find details on White Wolf wiki) you will basically get a small haunt in which wraiths stick together to survive.

                                                  As for Werewolf remember 2 things: two tribes that interact with wraith the most are Silent Striders and Silver Fangs (especially Ivory Priesthood). Also White Howlers, but they are no more. And that no Garou will ever become wraith (Kinfolks can) except those tainted by the Wyrm.

                                                  As for inspiration I would search it in "Ghost Towns" (a Werewolf/Wraith: the Wild West supplement; there you will find a few small settlements for Garou/Wraith population) and "Haunts" (a Wraith: the Oblivion supplement about Haunts). If you don't want to flip through the entire W: tO sourcebook just read the description of Arcanoi and Guilds on ww wiki and use your imagination for the rest. And my girlfriend showed me "Ghost", a comedy series about a haunted house. Those different characters also can be a great inpiration for your haunt.
