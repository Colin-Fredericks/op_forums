        Originally posted by Crowley View Post
        Any chances to elaborate on What happend on that game, how did it played out?. Also, did you based Carpenter off any existing character? as for his mannerism, way to talk or such? how do you picture him. who could play him on a movie or which character from comics, books, tv or movies could be a good carpenter a like ?
        Oh boy. I'll keep it brief, as I don't want to hijack the thread — or go down a whole "tell me about your character" rabbit hole.

        It was a long time ago so I forget exact events, but I do recall that neither the other players nor characters knew that Carpenter was a Spectre. Anyway, the session involved him leading them someplace that was ostensibly important to their mission. Really, he was calling in a mess o' Spectres while he kept them distracted. Much of this was regular ol' roleplaying, supported by judicious use of Intimation and Mnemosynis. It was going surprisingly well (for Carpenter) — even the other players weren't quite aware for quite a while what he was doing, till someone finally used some power (Lifesight?) to scan Carpenter and realize what he was. After a very ugly 4-on-1 fight, Carpenter was finally forced to flee since the other Spectres hadn't arrived yet.

        Carpenter was based off one of my old D&D characters from years earlier. His genesis came from a whole thing with how the game was set up and how all the characters were introduced in-game. The gist is that I was burned out on the genre and had literally no idea what to play, so his personality emerged full-blown directly from my subconscious.*

        Some other Carpenter-like characters you can use as a guide? Let's see… guys like John Constantine, Keyser Söze, and Petyr Baelish come from a similar mold.


        *Bit of gamer trivia: That D&D character? "Carpenter" was simply an alias. Likewise with "Dennis Maxwell" in the World of Darkness. His real name? No idea, to this day. Yes, the character that I made up, I do not know his real name. I expect a psychologist would have fun digging into that…

            Right. First, the news: I've started assigning sections to specific authors. I think you'll be pleased with some of the familiar faces who are coming back to the fold for this, and there are some very talented new writers climbing on board the Midnight Express as well. So, all kinds of happy about that.

            Second: Charlie is perhaps understating how perfectly flummoxed he had the rest of that Circle. And he maintained a perfect poker face while doing it. Never play cards with Charlie Bates.

            Third: Officially, CHoE superseded Berlin by Night in the greater WoD continuity. My stance in doing CHoE was, and is, that it was disingenuous of us to talk about how we were making games for mature minds without tackling the consequences of that head on, as there was a big hole in certain parts of WoD continuity that pretty much coincided with WW2. So I proposed filling that gap, and I made it clear that we weren't going to be blaming the Holocaust on vampires or werewolves or anyone else - that there was plenty of evil perpetrated by human beings to go around, even in the WoD. So vampire Himmler is, IMHO, hors d'continuity, and should stay that way.

            Fourth: What's an obolus worth? There will be a slight overhaul in the Stygian economy coming down the pike. In the intervening years between Wraith 2nd and now, I had the good fortune to marry someone who's professionally trained in economics and statistics. I will be asking her a lot of questions.

            Fifth: Nobody's riding a wraith dinosaur but me, you hear?

            Deadguy out.

                                Hey, folks - just dropping in quickly. Got a double fistful of editing to do tonight on Lillian Cohen-Moore's excellent novel Guidebook to the Village By the Sea, which IMHO is pretty darn Wraithy.

                                Anyway, on to the questions:

                                1-Artists - I don't have any say on the artists in the book, though I can bat my eyes at Rich Thomas winsomely and make requests. Honestly, we haven't even started discussions on that yet.
                                However, if you want to see who a couple of my favorite Wraith artists are, there's a profile that Polygon ran of me a few months back that featured a few pictures of my home office, and you might recognize some of the pieces hanging on the walls...

                                2-The icon is the Great Key of Stygia. I happen to have a physical copy of it hanging from my office ceiling. True story - the last time I took a picture of it, I got orbs like crazy, and weird streaks of light in the shot. Now, I don't believe orbs are ghosts and I don't think my office is haunted, but....Wraith curse, anyone?

                                3-Entertainment is Stygia is, in a word, awe-inspiring. Imagine Shakespeare performed by the ghosts of the actors who were in Shakespeare's original theatrical company. Imagine a concert where The Lizard King, Mama Cass, Elvis and Freddie Mercury performed together. (Not Cobain, though. He had an unfortunate Castigation incident a while back.) Of course, that's if you're in the sort of social circles where that kind of stuff is available...

                                4-Writing staff - we are a long way from final on this. Right now I'm matching up certain specific writers from Ye Olde Days with sections I had them in mind for when this process started, but we ain't close to done yet.

                                5-Recommended reading for Wraithy folks: Steve Rasnic Tem's Deadfall Hotel. 'Cause it's creepy and very well written, and Steve is a mensch who owns the coolest author signing pen I've ever seen. If folks are interested, I'll be posting more of my favorite ghost stories on occasion. Some of these are Wraith inspiration, some are just things that I found worthwhile to read. Feel free to ignore or not as desired.

                                Deadguy Out
