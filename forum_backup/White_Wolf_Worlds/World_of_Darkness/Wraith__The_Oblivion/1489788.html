    Wraiths underworld seems a bit confusing to me. I know there's a 'shadowlands' which is basically twilight but for CWoD. But what exactly is the tempest? Is it the shadowland equivalent of our oceans? And why are the 'dark kingdoms' always mentioned separately from the shadowlands? Are they in a separate world? If so, how do they rule over all those wraiths in the necropoli?

      The Shadowland's oceans are little different from their Skinlands equivalent. The Tempest is another plane of existence. Nihils are portals that connect the Shadowlands with the Tempest. Stygia and most other Dark Kingdoms are on that level of the Underworld, Necropoli are only outposts and a relatively recent development. The way Stygia rules all those Necropoli is no different than Imperial Rome ruling all those far off provinces. Or half the world once being ruled from England. There's an expansive bureaucratic and military hierarchy in place that keeps everything connected.

          Imagine the Underworld like a filled bathtub. Take a world map, cut it into confetti around cities and landmarks, and sprinkle it on the water surface- roughly in the same relative position as they were originally.
          The confetti is the Shadowlands.
          A big city on a piece of confetti is a Necropolis.
          The bathtub water is the Tempest.
          Several confetti flakes in roughly the same region are a Dark Kingdom, although this depends on what the Kingdom actually claims.
          Sprinkle in some extra confetti. This is Stygia, the Far Shores, and any Underworld landscape that has no representation in the Skinlands.

          Note that there are some oddities:
          - Some roads from the world map are still connected, even though they are on separate confetti flakes. These are Byways.
          - You have some flakes of the world map with nothing but real world ocean floating on the bathtub water. That's Shadowland ocean, which is distinct from the Tempest. The two are closely connected, though.
          - The River of Death connects all confetti pieces with each other. It's a metaphysical thing.
          - Usually, you have to use a portal (a Nihil) to cross from the confetti into the bathtub water and back out, but sometimes you can just walk in. It depends on the particular confetti flake.

          Now, our bathtub has a drain smack in the middle. Once you pull the plug, it tries to suck everything in, although the bathtub water never runs out.
          The drain is Oblivion.
          The hair and refuse that gathers on the grate of the drain is the Labyrinth.

          The analogy fails at this point, because some of the extra confetti is actually closer to the drain than others. There's a shortcut from Stygia to the Labyrinth, for example.

          This is all metaphorical of course, and the actual layout of the landscape is at the command of the Storyteller. They can shuffle things around pretty much as they need it for the story.

              Basically, there are three 'functional' layers to the Underworld, one inaccessible, and a possible lost extra layer. The Shadowlands are an analog to the physical world, almost exactly. Some physical features are closer to how they were in the past (relative to the present, possibly different points in the past). Necropoli are "cities" of the dead, created from Relic buildings and the like. Such cities usually overlap the physical living cities, and many buildings, usually the oldest, most important ones, have a presence there as well. If possible, and the being in question can leave the general area, one could theoretically travel the entire world. Shadowlands water is more 'solid' to Wraiths, so one could 'walk' on it. Byways are spiritual paths connecting various points in the Shadowlands to each other and to points in the Tempest. One layer 'below' the Shadowlands is the Tempest, a near constant storm of water and clouds, that surround safe points within it. At one time, the Tempest was much calmer, but some unknown cause changed this sometime before the founding of Stygia. The largest of those spots are called 'Dark Kingdoms' by Stygia, a realm where the dead of the Western world can exist when they no longer can survive in the Shadowlands. These other "Kingdoms" use different terms, and many derisively call Stygia the 'Dark Kingdom of Iron' in retaliation. Other smaller safe points are either older, less populated 'Kingdoms' or "Far Shores,' more recently created 'Kingdoms' based on religion or philosophy instead of a cultural bias. The true Far Shores may or may not have been discovered, but they were places where a Wraith could work out their issues and truly move on. These Far Shores may have once been their own level, but at least part of their original function got stolen and corrupted by the Malfeans into what is now known as Harrowings in the Labyrinth. The original function of the Labyrinth is unknown, but it is now the home of Specters and the Neverborn. It lies beneath the Tempest. At the bottom of the Labyrinth lies the Maw of Oblivion, where everything goes to cease to exist. Somehow, beneath that, is the emptiness of the Abyss, never fully mentioned until Demon: The Fallen came out. This is where true demons are imprisoned. This is also the possible source of the Obtenebration discipline in Vampire, as well as other unknown things.

                                      No. There are Byways through the Tempests, and Nihils can act like wormholes to other places in the Tempest. Normal wraiths do not want to go through Nihils though, that's very dangerous. Byways are a relatively safe option - they are stable paths in the Tempest. But use of Argos here can make travel on them safer.

                                      The Hierarchy also builds Tempest Ports in many Necropoli that provide openings between the Shadowlands and the Tempest. They appear as large, floating oval mirrors above byways and channels. They lead to established Byways. Tempest ports are located in a heavily guarded area of the Necropolis to make sure enemies cannot invade through them. Tempest ports give one automatic success on a Wraith’s Argos roll to go there. Ports can be closed at will, unlike Nihils.

                                      The best sourcebooks to answer your questions on the geography of the Underworld is the Wraith Players Guide and Sea of Shadows.

                                          It's worth going into what the various realms of the Underworld are thematically, because there's very little limit to what can be in the Underworld provided it is related to memory and emotion. The question is where would it be?

                                          The shadowlands are the place of fresh, coherent memories. Things that are and things that recently were. That's why they map well onto the Skinlands, because they mostly are memories of the Skinlands (with some minor alterations of emotion and perception). If the living strongly remember something or it has a strong connection to something that still exists, it belongs here. That's why Wraiths need Fetters to stay here.

                                          The Tempest is the place for memories and emotions that are untethered from the living. Anything that has become unmoored but still has presence ends up here. The majority of memories like this are small sensations or images without context, and that's what the storm is. It is a fever dream turned into a monstrous hurricane; is a roiling mass of flotsam and effluvia. I like to flavor the winds with strange memories like a waterfall of dolls heads or the spray of acid and broken glass, for that reason. Strong, unmoored memories, such as the collective perceptions of a generation, also end up here and these become the byways, islands, and other stable points.

                                          The Tempest is also the place for anything that was never part of the living world. That's why the Dark Kingdom capitals and the Far Shores are there, and were there even before the rulers lost their fetters. The Dark Kingdoms are things that never existed in the living world and the Far Shores were supposed to help Wraiths Transcend from it. Neither belongs in the Shadowlands, so they are here.

                                          The Labyrinth is the place for buried and forgotten memories. Unwanted things go here. Mostly these are the horrific, angst-laden things that people would rather forget, which is why all the Spectres and their Amphiskopoli are down here. It's not all Angst, though. Some things are buried to be preserved and these things belong here too. There's at least one area of the Labyrinth which houses wraiths (not spectres) dedicated to writing the true history of the world. That too belongs down in the depths.

                                                      It's pretty close. The Tempest is to the Shadowlands much like the Shadowlands are to the Skinlands - an extra dimension that overlaps them. The books use terms like 'below' to signify the distance from the Skinlands, which is more confusing than helpful imo, because they aren't really directional.

                                                      The Dark Kingdoms are Shadowlands. The Shadowlands are 'broken up' by the Tempest and 'Dark Kingdom' is just a label for a 'region' of the Shadowlands. That means if you want to move from one Necropolis to another, you would either have to go through the Tempest or use a Byway.

                                                      Example: You are in the Shadowland Necropolis of Rome in the Dark Kingdom of Iron. You open a Nihil (there is an Arcanos for that) and move into the Tempest. You travel inside the Tempest until you reach your destination. You open another Nihil and emerge in the Shadowland of the Cairo Necropolis in the Dark Kingdom of Sand.
