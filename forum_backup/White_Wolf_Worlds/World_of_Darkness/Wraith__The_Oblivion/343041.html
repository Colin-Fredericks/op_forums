    tips for running demo/convention games

    12-17-2014, 12:27 PM
    What wisdom does everyone have regarding exhibition one-shots of Wraith?

    I've run maybe three over the years, and have now been inspired to do another one soon. I'd appreciate any tips people have. With any RPG, there are differences in how you'd set up a campaign vs. a one-shot with pregenerated characters. I feel that with Wraith that's even more true.

    Advice I'd give:

    Make sure every pregen PC has at least one arcanos that lets them reach coherently across the Shroud, preferably Embody or Inhabit. (Which is also how the Orpheus designers seem to have felt, as I recall.)

    Don't give any PCs an arcanos that deals exclusively with Wraith-specific setting elements, such as Argos, Castigate, or Usury. (You don't need to show them all off. You will already be spending a long time explaining the setting.)

    Should you choose a dedicated shadowguide? It's a little easier than explaining all the mechanics to all the players. It can be great or not depending on how readily the chosen shadowguide takes to it. It's great in the hands of someone who's a fast study, who instantly clicks with the intent of it. But other people can get this pile of shadow sheets and character backgrounds, and merely be overwhelmed and confused, never doing much if you don't take extra time to coach them. I'm not sure how to screen for that, or whether you should try if you're running this for strangers. I know some people bring their own "co-GM" to be the designated shadowguide, which seems to work well.

    As with any RPG session, logistics are simpler if you trap the PCs in a confined space. You can use a handy maelstrom, siege, or whatnot. (Again, there will be plenty to explain about the metaphysics of even a simple space, hence I suggest de-emphasizing exploration.)

    Speaking of character backgrounds, I like making the PCs all old friends or family, so it's a no-brainer why they're allies.

    ...although I guess if you're going with the confined space approach, maybe you'd rather stir up trouble among them? I don't know, I usually avoid that kind of thing, especially among mutual strangers. Not everyone is good at playing at antagonism without leaving at least a slight bad taste in the mouth of their target OOC -- which brings us back to the Shadowguide challenge again.

    Thoughts?

                      Halve the plot to split the difference. Part 1) The assembled Wraiths have a really good reason to trash the Dictuum Mortem.... Perhaps they all share a threatened Fetter or have a linked set of Fetters and Passions that all are threatened by opponent or opponents they can affect through use of their obviously illegal Aracnoi to haunt the hell out of the opponent (and they'll therefore have to have to have a good justification for having high levels of naughty Arcanoi in the character write-ups). Transition - there's some reason that going after him/her/it/they draws the attention of the Ghost Cops... leading to Part 2) On the run from the Hierarchy...

                      That way they get two great tastes in one package - the Quick vs. the Dead and The Downs and Outs of Living in a Post-Life Police State.
