    What are Arcanoi?

    12-13-2014, 08:07 PM
    So, I'm not terribly familiar with Wraith. I've taken a look at the core book, and I dig basically everything about it. I decided to pledge the Kickstarter and get in on this action.

    Anyway, the recent Stretch Goal update about including the Dark Kingdom of Jade Arcanoi (as well as the Arcanos previews on the KS page) raised a question in my mind.

    Again, I'm not terribly familiar with Wraith, so I'm sorry if this is either entry-level information or an ultimately pointless question, but:

    Are Arcanoi socially-constructed phenomena? The previewed Arcanoi have both "modern" and "ancient" arts, implying that their usage has changed over time, even if they fall under the same themes. So, Argos is all about travel, regardless of what aspects of it you're mastering. And apparently the Arcanoi practiced in Stygia are different from those practiced in other Dark Kingdoms.

    So, what lies at the base of the Arcanoi? Are there any powers that are "fundamental" to Wraith-hood? Some Arcanoi, like Usury and Argos, seem like the sorts of things any ghost would find helpful in their day-to-day undeaths, so I'm having a hard time imagining how different other cultures' spooky magics might be.

          Well technically each Guild is connected to one of the sixteen main Arcanoi. You couldn't exactly be a member of the Harbinger Guild without Argos or a Pardoner without Castigate. Originally, those Guilds effectively controlled their particular Arcanoi within Stygia as they were the ones to really begin explore the nature of the Arcanoi and develop their power.

          There are several kinds of Arcanoi. There's the main Arcanoi which embody the common ghostly powers. In the western lands of the dead, within Stygia anyway, those sixteen powers were tied to the Guilds until they were broken several centuries ago, though the Guild do still exist albeit without much of the political power they possessed and still quite to their particular Arcanoi. The Arcanoi have become divided into four categories based on how useful they are to Stygian society and don't interfere with the world of the living. The Great Guilds and their Arcanoi are the most vital to Stygia and largely still operate openly. Beneath them are the Working Guilds whose powers are technically illegal but tolerated as they don't interfere with the Skinlands. Then there's the Criminal Guilds, their powers are illegal as they all affect the Skinlands and the living. Rounding them is the Forbidden Guilds which are pretty hated in addition to illegal.

          Of course that only applies to Stygia's official stance, not everyone within Stygia tows the party line and that doesn't extend to the Renegades and the Heretics.

          Then there are the other Dark Kingdoms. Many possess most or all of the common Arcanoi and some have their own unique Arcanoi as well, born out of the cultures and beliefs of those regions. To my knowledge those Arcanoi can only be learned by the wraiths of those Dark Kingdoms.

          Finally there are the Dark Acranoi of Spectres, though some are learned by Doomslayers, wraiths that explore the Labyrinth and fight the Spectres there.

              I don't know that there is any real basis for the arcanoi beyond simply being abilities wraiths can learn because they are wraiths. Most arts are fueled by pathos at least, though many require expenditure of willpower and can increase angst.

              Most arts replicate things that we generally think ghosts are capable of doing, much like disciplines replicate things we think of vampires of being able to do.

              It's suggested in the books occasionally that the underworld is supposed to be a place that guides the dead come to terms with their loss, and on to transcendence. However that function has become lost and corrupted, so instead of transcendence awaiting every wraith at the end, there instead is oblivion. (Transcendence is still possible, just more difficult, and the Hierarchy considers it a heresy.)

              Anyway, it could be that the Arcanoi were supposed to be tools for the wraith to use on their way to transcendence, a means to give their final farewells, etc. Perhaps the arcanoi are themselves corrupted as well, and expanded upon in ways they weren't meant to be used.

                              Okay, so I think the guild metaphor is helpful for understanding them, insofar that Arcanoi are akin to the crafting skills of medieval European guilds, which also helps explain why there are both ancient and modern variations. Techniques and philosophies change over time. The arts of Castigate stick together both because of their logical grouping (powers that affect the Shadow) and because of the word of the Pardoners' Guild. It makes sense that other cultures would have developed arts related to affecting the Shadow (in the same way that other cultures would have developed carpentry), but their naming conventions and conceptual understanding of these arts would vary. In this model I guess the overarching categories exist more as a necessary gaming abstraction than as some kind of mystical glue within the world of Wraith, in the same way that any other World of Darkness monster isn't going to measure their strengths on a one-to-five scale just because that's how the information is presented to the players.

                              Cool.
