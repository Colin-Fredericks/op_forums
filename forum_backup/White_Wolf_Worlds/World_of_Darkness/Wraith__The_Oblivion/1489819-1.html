                How about this: the Dark Kingdom of Iron did fall, and Orpheus did gain a foothold in the Underworld. But that was almost 25 years ago; and unlike the Orpheus gameline, the 6th Great Maelstrom didn't scour the Underworld clean. Likewise, unlike the first edition of HtR, ghosts weren't forced across the Shroud and into the Skinlands en mass. Since then, the Great Maelstrom has subsided and a new Hierarchy has begun to rise. It isn't yet to the same level of power as the old Hierarchy had achieved before its demise; and with Orpheus in the mix now, it has more challenges to overcome than its precessor had.

                Where the Hierarchy holds sway, you can run the same sorts of games as you can with Wraith 20, with one difference: the Guilds are back, as a foundational part of the new Hierarchy. But there are also many more Necropoli than there used to be that aren't part of the new Dark Kingdom of Iron. There are now whole Necropoli that are run by what earlier editions would have called Renegades. They're now independent city-states; if the Storyteller chooses to use one of them as the focus of his chronicle, the result will be somewhat similar to the Wraith Project mentioned earlier in this thread: the Necropolis will basically be on its own against the threats faced in the Shadowlands.
                ​​​
                The new Hierarchy has to contend with more threats than its precessor did: it used to be that the only thing you had to worry about was Spectres from the Tempest; but now there's a renewed danger from the Skinlands as well: with Orpheus and its rivals in play, the exploitation of the dead by the living is in full force. The new version of the Dictum Mortuum isn't so much a command as it is good advice: wraiths who interact with the living are far more likely these days to run afoul of ghost hunters. If you want to run a chronicle that deals more heavily with the Skinlands, one option is to partake in the Hierarchy's war against Orpheus.

                My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                    Before enumerating them, my elucubrations are based on the supposition that the authors will want to bring back the focus to "ghost stories", instead of the dark fantasy of the Underworld. So I think they are going to:

                    1 Follow the Orpheus plot of the Underworld being wiped out. Stygia fallen, the Dark Kingdom of Jade fallen, everything else lost contact. The hierarchy, guilds and structures of the Underworld completely destroyed. Some Necropolis could have survived, and there the Hierarchy could have evolved to something else more local and isolated, feudal kingdoms of the anacreons, but not empires.

                    2 Put the focus on pathos, shadows and fetters. I think the focus of the next edition is going to shift towards a more personal horror, without the political machinations of the hierarchy, the wars between empires or the labyrinth as a place you could visit. Spectres would keep existing, but as something more vague and alien, completely unknown and unplayable (the castes blurred).

                    3 Strengthen the tempest, which means the game would have place between the skinlands and the shadowlands. Beyond, the 6th maelstrom still rages on. That contributes to the former points.

                    4 Orpheus could an antagonistic or a player option, they are a corporation exploring the underworld after all. And Terrel & Squib would be definitively antagonistic.

                        I basically drafted a really long post covering a lot of the same ground, then my phone's "digital wellbeing tools" kicked me out of Brave for being online too long, and the website didn't save my text. Damn it!

                        But as you've ninja'd me now, I'll just say I agree with these ideas. I will say why I think WTO focused on all the dark urban fantasy stuff, though.

                        As you suggest, there were always (at least) two broad playstyles for Wraith: one was the ghost stories thing, and the other was the dark urban fantasy sandbox, which is also what VTM and CTD often defaulted to.

                        I think even the 1e core book writers knew that while WTO had a really appealing world and core concept, it didn't create a lot of active drama for players if played the way it was intended.

                        You could sort of narrate how you were moping around, looking at your mortal life as it fades away, protecting your Fetters and trying to resolve your Passions, but that was all very emotionally intensive, required a lot of interiority that doesn't work in a TTRPG (as opposed to in a novel, say) and gets repetitive quickly. It's hard work and not necessarily always fun. It creates a huge barrier for entry, too.

                        The writers knew that on some level, so they created this byzantine bureaucracy to interact with and a really detailed Underworld for you to explore. And this part of the game rapidly expanded as the gameline did, because it was far more playable.

                        The first supplement, for example, was Necropolis: Atlanta, and that immediately set the stage for what this dark urban fantasy sandbox would look like. It's the basic pattern established by VTM and Chicago by Night.

                        We had a few books like Love Beyond Death, but that actually came out after we'd already had books like The Midnight Express, which was pulling players towards Stygia and the Tempest. And honestly, I don't think the WoD would've ever been as popular as it was without those kitchen sink settings that could draw in a diverse range of players.

                        Within a year of WTO landing, you could adventure in the Dark Kingdom of Jade and explore the Sea of Shadows. With books like Doomslayers: Into the Labyrinth, you could even play Aliens via Beetlejuice.

                        Wr5 will probably jettison most of that, as you say. They probably will keep some of the dark urban fantasy sandboxy stuff, because they know that's a big draw for Vampire, but it will be lessened so that it doesn't become the main game.

                        ​The Hierarchy will probably be fractured, and functioning like independent colonies rather than a huge, Underworld-spanning empire. More like the Camarilla of V5 than the Camarilla of 1e to V20.

                        Maybe the Hierarchs will have all retreated to their Citadels, leaving the rest of the Necropoli to the others. Between cities will just be an endless storm, so there won't be much communication between Necropoli.

                        Renegades will be more powerful, Heretics will be on the prowl for new recruits, and travel into the Tempest and to Stygia will be very difficult indeed. There may be no central ruler of the Hierarchy, unless there's a group of new Deathlords, but they might make it fuzzy enough that the DLs have just retreated rather than definitely been destroyed. Charon will be gone.

                        I think it is do-able, but the risk will be that it's more H5 than V5, and so there might not be a lot to do besides the one "focus" that the devs decide on. And for Wraith, that may be enough to guarantee its death (again). Because it was always a hard sell, and that "personal horror" version of the game was the hardest part of the setting.

                        Orpheus strikes me as more likely to get the 5th Edition treatment than WTO, as it has a clearer focus and nukes the metaplot. It's much more newbie friendly, as well, which is a big focus for WW/Paradox now.

                        But Orpheus is not entirely consistent with the way wraiths work in WTO (e.g., Shadows are more like Pasiphae in Orpheus, which could fit into WTO, but may require some in-character justification for why Shadows are independent beings now). And there may not be much scope to play wraiths in that game at all.

                                    Originally posted by Rhywbeth View Post
                                    Edit: It just occured to me that possibly the Dark Kingdoms could get the Sabbat/Get treatment, the maelstrom consumes everything within the tempest and everything within it falls to Oblivion becoming warped, Labyrinth-like citadels populated with armies of spectres.                                     That was basically what Orpheus did: the Shroud became an impassible Stormwall, and everything in the other side got consumed by the Tempest. In my more jaded moments, I tend to think that ParaWolf will end up making Wr5 as a reskinned Orpheus, jettisoning the plot that Orpheus featured but keeping the idea (from nearly every book after the first one in that series) that you're not working for Orpheus or anyone else.

                                    That is, they're likely to take the Nature Categories, Shades, Horrors, and Vitality/Spite/Stains and Crucible systems from Orpheus, and possibly even the Laments* while jettisoning the assumption that you are or were connected in any way to Orpheus, downplaying its existence to just another element in the setting. I could especially see the Orpheus-style Shades being given a prominent role in this vision of Wr5, complete with the idea that all playable wraiths have an innate ability to Manifest in the Skinlands.

                                    Orpheus was, after all, an attempt to reframe the “play a ghost” gameline in a way that “learned from the mistakes made by Wraith”. And given what they did with Hunter, I wouldn't put it past them to try to do something similarly radical to Wraith.

                                    And given ParaWolf's propensity to borrow elements from Chronicles of Darkness, I could also see them doing things like incorporating ideas from Geist into this. If they don't go with Orpheus' “Spirits have evil twins” idea, they might try to adapt Geist's notion of Synergy instead, saying that part of the personal horror of the game is that you need to face your Shadow and come to terms with it. If they do, then the Shades would take on elements of Geist's Burdens — though on the positives side, this might allow us to retain something akin to Wraith's Passions in the form of a reskinning of a Burden's Aspiration. They might also incorporate concepts from Geist's krewes into Orpheus' Crucibles, making Crucibles the central social organizing principle of the game instead of empires of the dead or corporate masters.

                                    * though I could also see them saying that the Spirit Lament is ubiquitous; this would, after all, technically be Wraith, not Geist. And even if they keep the Laments, they might do something like renaming the Spirit Lament as the Wraith Lament, making it the default option, and relegating the others to an Appendix focused on Wraith-adjacent gameplay. Maybe even turn the Risen into a “fifth Lament”.

                                    I could see them keeping the full range of Laments around, and possibly even expanding on it, because ParaWolf seems to like borrowing ideas from the Chronicles of Darkness when updating their World of Darkness gamelines to X5; and having Projectors and Risen and possibly other “back from the dead and once again among the living” character options would make it easier to incorporate ideas from Geist into Wr5.

                                    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
