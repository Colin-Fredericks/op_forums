    Any ideas for a potential 5th Edition?

    08-11-2022, 08:33 PM
    Been thinking about this for a while now. With Vampire and Hunter 5th out, and Werewolf and Mage 5th both being worked on, I've gotten curious about what the other splats would look like in a modern 5th Edition.

    Does anyone have any potential speculation on what a 5th Edition of Wraith might end up looking like? I admit, I'm not too familiar with the splat myself; closest thing I came to really playing it was that Afterlife VR game that got released a while back, and even then, it didn't really explain too much of the lore. As a result, I have no idea what a modern version of Wraith would really look like; the only idea I have is a possible rebellion against the Hierarchy, most likely via the Renegades. Anyone have their own opinions?

                  I don't know why Wraith would need a major setting change in order to have a 5th edition, other than that's what Paradox seems to like.

                  If we are going that route though:

                  Ends of Empire leaves a completely different set of wraiths in charge of Stygia to remake it as they see fit. It would be quite reasonable to run with that into just about anything. I would guess a less restrictive Dictum Mortuum and a more active Stygia, and a battle against the 6th Great Maelstrom and Co, but the nihil-ridden sky is the limit.

                  The Wraith Project explored a world where Stygia was gone, the Necropoli were isolated, and Wraiths had to defend against the stormfronts of the 6th Great Maelstrom. It was a fun exploration, as I recall, though I'd prefer a setting that doesn't sideline the Tempest and the Labyrinth.

                  Then there is Orpheus, which tackled a world where everything was scoured by the 6th Great Maelstrom and the underworld was metaphysically altered by it. Strip out the stuff about living projectors and Grandmother and you have a reasonable setting for NuWraith. I'd really prefer things don't go that way, though. I love Orpheus, but it isn't Wraith and it would not be a good replacement for it.

                              It was a fan project, and it looks like it is mostly gone. Some of the files are still available at https://www.cattail.nu/wraithproject/.

                              Orpheus was very much its own thing. It is a WoD game and it does follow the Ends of Empire, but it has its own themes and they color much of the game design.

                              In Orpheus the 6th Great Maelstrom changed everything, to the point that the game is barely recognizable metaphysically. It actually takes several books before it becomes clear that this is the same world as Wraith. The 6GM wiped out everything. Stygia, the Necropoli, everything. The majority of the game takes place in the skinlands/shadowlands and it is nigh impossible to get further into the Underworld because of the impenatrable Stormwall. When we do finally see it, it is not a stormtossed sea but stormblasted wastelands. Everything was stripped clean. Meanwhile ghosts themselves are manifesting differently, it is (comparatively) easier to Transcend, Drones can be uplifted, Spectres behave oddly, and there's a new threat rising from depths of the void...

                              Of course all of these changes were possible because Orpheus was playing by its own themes. None of it really works in Wraith, which is why the Orpheus section in Wr20 was so pared down.

                              That would be my issue with mixing the Middle and Low Umbra. I don't think it's controversial so much as it doesn't do much for the themes of Wraith. Most spirits are just general threats and they don't underscore or put pressure on the Wraithly condition, and some even take the pressure off by providing easier access to resources.

                              What holes are you trying to fix?

                                      I can see where you are coming from. For my part, I don't think the goal is to impede Wraiths from interacting with the living, I think it is to make it risky. If the Dictum Mortuum disappeared I would see the need for something else to drive that risk, and predatory emotion spirits attracted to human fear could work. Outside of that, I wouldn't do much to increase the difficulty of interacting with the living.

                                      As for Spectres and power plants, they have a significantly harder time interacting with the living world or even seeing the living. Very few Spectres will have the right combination of mindset, powers, and capabilities to plan and successfully carry out an attack on the living of that magnitude. Few enough that it's not cheating to just leave them out of the game. Going after targets important to the PCs is another matter.

                                      I do like the idea of the ambiance of a deeper emotional ecology to the Shadowlands, though I'd have them originate in the Low Umbra. Memories and emotion are the stuff of the Underworld after all. My main consideration is that they can't be an easy replacement for soulsteel or relics. That's easy to handwave, though.

                                              Honestly, I'd be happy for Wraith to stay as it is -- in the forever-maybe-never-land of Wr20. It seems kinda poetic not to rouse the Beast for something that might ruin what it was.

                                              That said, I see Wraith: The Great War as a good model for how wraith society might survive the Sixth Great Maelstrom, but amp it up even more. Make Stygia a great unknown your characters can seek out or ignore as they wish. Everything would be more isolated, but not necessarily completely destroyed.

                                              Using V5 as an example, which uses parts of the Gehenna plot but not all of it, it might just be that the Sixth Great Maelstrom is neverending but doesn't completely obliterate Stygia. It would do for the setting what the Avatar Storm did for Mage, without completing nuking the whole thing.
