        Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
        I thought that might be where you were coming from. I agree that you can definitely see some fingerprints of Jungian psychology in Wraith. However, there are notable differences too.

        Most notably, a Wraith doesn't need the Shadow as any kind of driving force to pursue his Passions. It might occasionally provide a boost in the form of Shadow Dice, but it is not a dominant driver. Nor does it play any mechanical role in resolving Passions, except by accident. Nor does it play any role at all in developing new ones. You could view this as a flaw in the system mechanics, but I don't.

        The way I view it is that in wraiths the Passions (regular and dark) are terribly inflamed. That's why Wraiths even persist. The Psyche and Shadow are not healthy and functioning parts of a complete soul, they are the parts that desperately resist moving on.

        The Psyche is the part that cling desperately to the world. The Passions are not in any way balanced, they are drives the Psyche clings to with monomaniacal fervor even in the face of death. It doesn't need the Shadow to help push them. Indeed a healthy Shadow of the kind you describe would almost exclusively push towards resolving them.

        The Shadow, though, is not healthy, as it is the part that would rather cease than continue on in any form. Any self critical part of it bends towards self destruction. Any mediating role it has is subsumed by the Dark Passions, which fuel that desire for self destruction and also drive it to action.

        Perhaps we can say that if the excesses of the Shadow are cured it would function much like a Jungian shadow, but it is not functioning in that role for most Wraiths. The Psyche must drive and mediate itself, and it does.
        Yes, Wraiths are unhealthy psychological manifestations. I dont know if the writters designed it on purpose by using ideas on psychology, or if this has been a subconscious sublimation in the form of art, which is not uncommon - many Greek tales possess such attribute, like the myths of Narcise and Psyche.

        Wraiths have the two side of the same coin that drives us all, which Freud represented under the Greek Gods "Eros" and "Thanatos", or the "Pull towards life" and the "Pull towards death". Those two are our most basic psychic instincts - and in the game, they are clearly represented in both the Psyche (Eros) and the Shadow (Thanatos).

        An excess on either end can create an unhealthy mind - and that's where ALL Wraiths find themselves in.

        The "Thanatos pulse" (the Greek God of death) obviously leads to self destructive behavior, but an excess of "Eros pulse" (the Greek God of Love) leads to hedonism and nihilism.

        Ironically, suicidal tendencies are caused by EROS, not Thanatos. Suicidal people take their own life because, paradoxically, their pull of life is too big to bear. In their desperation to fulfill thei unquenchable thirsty to live, which apparently nothing can satiate, they take their own lives.

        For instance, the person that takes his own life after the end of a relationship or after discovering a cheating. "If I can't live with that person, there's no purpose in living at all".

        I think that's well represented in the game in the form of the passions and fetters. Why do you get closer to Oblivion when you lose your passions and fetters? Because you are literally losing your "will to live". It's the same mindset of suicidal though. "What's the purpose of fighting against Oblivion if I can no longer protect my daughter?"

        Every time a Wraith's Psyche can no longer indulge in his "Eros pull" (his passions), he gets closer to Oblivion - just like suicidals irl that see no way to achieve their life impulses (if I cant have my children/my dream job/the love of my life/my life's purpose - life is not worth it)

        Thanatos pulse on the other hand is self destructive in nature, but it is what keeps us in check. It's what allows us to deal with loss - otherwise our Life impulses would turn us all suicidal.

        But we all know what unbalanced self destructive actions cause - self sabotage. It is the case of people that are so afraid to be hurt by a loving partner that they actively sabotage the relantionship, so that you never become too dependant on that person to the point of being unable to live without him/her

            Wraiths are therefore, by their very natures, VERY unhealthy people. Only a few of the dead actually turn into Wraiths, right?

            And the ones that do... Are those consumed by their Passions (Eros pulse).

            It's no wonder that many suicidals turn into the Silent Legion - aka become Wraiths, instead of immediatly plunge into Oblivion. If what a suicidal truly wanted was to end his existence, why would his passions bind him into the world, instead of his soul immediatly plunge into annihilation?

            Because suicidals dont want to embrace destruction, they want to LIVE. They want to live TOO MUCH, to the point that it becomes unbearable not being able to.

            All Wraiths refuse death, they refuse the end and refuse to let go or to move on, because their "Eros pulse", their pulse of Life. Wraiths want TO LIVE.

            Even Spectres do. Because Spectres still have a Psyche and Passions. Thus, even thou Spectres represent individuals that surrendered to their worst self destructive impulses (Thanatos pulse), they still have the almost unbearable Eros pulses, the desire to live.

            Spectres that lose all of their Eros pulses... Disappear. Are consumed by Oblivion. Their self destructive impulses finally destroying every part of their personality (with the game's beautiful metaphor of Oblivion as a physical manifestation of that).

            Like I said however, the "Thanatos pulse" is necessary to accept the pain of loss. The death of a loved one, the end of a relationship, the changes in life.

            A Wraith's Shadow however is an unhealthy self destructive pulse.

            But in my opinion, this is were Transcendence gets in.

            Transcendence (mental harmony) is impossible without the Shadow, because in order to achieve it, you need to be able to integrate your self and your Shadow, in order to finally come to terms with your desires and your will to let go.

            When a Wraith suffers a loss and is throw into the Labyrinth to experience a delicious nightmare, this is an artistic representation of an individual's trauma.

            When you are in the middle of a traumatic experience, it feels like living in a nightmare for the time - exactly as being dragged by a nihil to the Labyrinth to be tormented by the Shadow.

            After that nightmare, we come out from the process of grief either broken or in acceptance of the loss. Healthy minds eventually come to terms to the loss, but if the individual cannot manage to overcome the trauma, he/she gets stuck into it. This is the entire mechanics of Harrowings.

            Without the Shadow to drag you to the Labyrinth however, how do you deal with trauma? When a Wraith who has no Shadow have one of their passions destroyed, how is the Wraith able to experience the trauma and heal from it?

            That's why Im saying, the very concept of removing one's Shadow dont make much sense without destroying the Wraith

                There are two different issues here, Transcendence and Motivation.

                On a Transcendence level, Ferrymen are making it much more difficult for themselves to Transcend, though I think for a different reason than you mention. Harrowings are certainly the primary mechanism for bringing Wraiths to breaking points with their Passions and Fetters, and in the past may have even been a major way of resolving them. However, Wraiths are quite capable of resolving their Passions and Fetters without Harrowings. Indeed that was part of the purpose of the Far Shores, back before they fell.

                Where Severance really impacts Transcendence is in the resolution of the Dark Passions. The Ferryman is essentially giving up most of his ability to deal with those. He's deeply repressing them (externalizing them and burying them in the labyrinth) rather than processing them, and he will never have the same ability to address them again.

                But that's about whether the Ferryman ever Transcends, not about if he continues existing. On a motivational level Severance frees the Psyche to pull against Oblivion without resistance. Yes, in the inevitable spiral around the drain that is the Underworld, everyone who doesn't Transcend will eventually fall to Oblivion, but in this case eventually can take millennia and that's quite a bit longer than most Wraith's manage.
