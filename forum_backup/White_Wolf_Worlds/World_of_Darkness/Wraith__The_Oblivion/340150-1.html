                    I'm personally still going to go with "no blank space". The area of modern Iraq and Iran (and Pakistan and Afghanistan) is considered to be Stygia and probably is as much as anywhere else that is part of the Empire, but not part of the "core territory" in Europe. The deeds of Alexander and the rule of the Selucids seems as much of a decisive event as to have brought it under the influence of the future Dark Kingdom of Iron. There might be a high incident of Renegades and Heretics there and some of them might be claiming authority based on past and subsequent non-Roman/Stygian influences, but there seems to be enough influence to put it under the heavy hand of Iron.

                    However, I don't see any reason NOT to have a Dark Kingdom of <insert something relevant> for the areas of under long-term Persian sway that don't seem to have been gobbled up by adjacent Swar and Stygia. It would certainly make the Near East and adjacent Middle East a more "interesting" place in a way very similar to how it was during Roman and Byzantine times. With a heavy influence of Heretic from the more recent dead to include but not give dominance to the current religions of the region. However, it's going to be a tough row to hoe to figure out what whatever is there is like. Is it more pre-Zoorastrian? (More like a classic Mesopotamian) Or very Zoorastian? Or the later overlaying the former? In any of those cases the sources are going to be hard to get to. Is it going to be more like the Persian Empire? (Which would be my bet, since Stygia is a lot more like the Roman and British Empires than it is like Dis, Hades or Hell) If so, which one? That of Darayava and Xsayarsha? The Parthians? The Sasanian?

                    I've always thought that the ancient Dark Kingdoms were sort of "lost". As has been said, the Dark Kingdom of Sand isn't poaching modern Egyptian wraiths. The don't have the right kind of souls, all two-party (or three, maybe, w/Eidolon) instead of the proper nine. Plus the Dark Kingdom of Sand has a sweet set up for Memoriam that is still generating a steady Pathos flow. But I never really saw it as a place you could just catch the Midnight Express and go to. Or even get to the closest station and have a bit of a hike. Though more the latter possibly than the former. I always got the impression that the old, non-harvesting older Dark Kingdoms were dislocated in a typically wraithly 4-D strange way from the current Shadowlands. Maybe you could catch the Midnight Express to Cairo, but crossing the Nile westward might be problematic as all the necropoli of Kemet of old might be present.... (Or maybe they all "detach" and float off and become Far Shores as they become less relevant to the Skinlands?)

                    If that's the case, then the Dark Kingdoms of Obsidian, Clay, Gold and Flint might be pale remnants of their former glory. (Though those are pretty oversimplified visions of the New World and Australia in any case.)There isn't a lot of Pathos flowing to them. They aren't harvesting new wraiths for the most part. And the underworlds/Dark Kingdoms of other cultures would be in similar straits. There probably isn't much of the Mesopotamian Dark Kingdom (of Dust?, considering their view of the afterlife) to get to anymore. Likewise the Dark Kingdom of the Levant (of Mud/Ooze/Phlegm? That's how it's pictured in Canaainte texts, though you get something pretty much like the Mesopotamian one as well when you look at ideas like Sheol). Then you have the Shadowlands of the Hittites, etc. etc. The Dark Kingdom of Sand might be doing as well as it is because of the proclivities and power of the Ancient Egyptians. Those of other cultures might not be doing so hot...

                    And, as for Central and Northern Asia... wow... Probably under Stygia? Maybe? I don't think so... But what to do?

                            Picking up on the ideas in one of the early editions of Mummy, I think it has to do with the Quick (i.e. the living) and "memorium" (the power of memory to feed the dead) writ large. Stygia, the Dark Kingdom of Iron, was founded by Greco-Roman wraiths . . . societies that still fascinate and influence Western civilization. Their empires may have fallen, but their beliefs, culture, and society have endured and inspired. The Romans aspired to be Greeks, the Byzantine and Holy Roman Empire (Germany) tried to be the Romans, then came the Renaissance, when everyone was trying to be the Greeks and the Romans . . .on until today. We live in societies governed under (modified) Greek democractic or Roman republican style governments, debate classic Greco-Roman thinkers, perform their plays . .. And just how many buildings have Greek columns in front?

                            Heck, I even had one history professor who argued that the sole purpose of Western civilization was to hold onto Greek culture and Roman law. That may be hyperbole, but it still reflects a massive amount of memorium flowing to the Underworld, and its never truly ended. If anything, its probably increased, as Western societies spread to the Americas . . . So it makes sense that Stygia would also grow and prosper. Why else would North American wraiths end up there?

                            I'm not as familiar with Chinese history, but I believe similar circumstances prevail there. So it makes sense the Dark Kingdom of Jade would prosper, too.

                            But consider Amenti, Dark Kingdom of Sand. According to Mummy, it was once a major player . . . but has fallen into ruin and been eclipsed by Stygia over the years. Few go there anymore, except the Shemsu-heru and Ishmaelites (the Egyptian mummies). And that makes sense. Who wants to be a part of the ancient Egyptian afterlife? Spending all day, every day of your life toiling for your god-king, just so you can die . . . and do the exact same thing again in the afterlife, for all eternity? Amenti is sustained by a trickle of memorium from tourists who want to see the Pyramids, and the odd individual who decides to have his body mummified.

                            By that logic, there's probably a Dark Kingdom for every culture that ever existed . . . assuming they haven't dissolved back into the Tempest. But as the Quick abandon the beliefs and practices that once fueled it, and wraiths stop going there, the Dark Kingdom slowly disappears from the scene. There's probably a fallen Dark Kingdom for the Norse, the Celts, and so on. Meet a Ferryman's price, he can probably still take you there. But they're not going to be competing for Necropoli in the Shadowlands, nor marching to war anytime soon (or ever)

                                            Frankly, it seems daft to assume that Greek culture and Roman democracy, in the form of Stygia, would come to the Underworld and usurp native cultures that had existing for millennia, despite the fact that even now Greek culture and Roman democracy aren't particularly popular concepts in the region, just because. The Middle East had culture while we were still banging the rocks together (I exaggerate, but you get the point). I find it very hard to believe that early Mesopotamian or even later Persian rulers wouldn't have built their own empires and cemented their rule, as did Charon in the West and Yu Huang in the East *millennia later*. The mythical figure of Gilgamesh, for instance, would be a prime candidate. He was king of Uruk and is remembered today, so would receive at least a trickle of memoriam from scholars and poetry readers.

                                            Let's not forget the pride Middle Easterners have for their own cultures. Iranians have huge pride in their Persian heritage. People still read Rumi and the Sufi poets. Islam is a *huge* cultural force. Therefore the wraiths of that region will still be reaping pathos and memoriam from sources that are specifically tied to Middle Eastern culture--not Western, long-dead cultures that existed in the Middle East for what is essentially a drop in the ocean. Add to this the fact that a predominantly non-religious culture (Stygia) is to be assumed to rule a population for whom religion is often intrinsic to their own culture, and it simply doesn't make sense.

                                            I like the idea of a Dark Kingdom of Dust. YMMV, of course, but that's the wonder of the internet :P
