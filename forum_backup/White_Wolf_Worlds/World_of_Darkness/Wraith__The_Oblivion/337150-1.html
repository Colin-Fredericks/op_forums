                    My interest in Kansas City is partially in how well it fits into all the campaigns. It is a limited population metropolis with a current metro-population of around 2.4 million in over a dozen counties (7,952 sq. mi./20,596 sq. km). It has a history that involves multicultural aspects from several indigenous peoples (both native to the area and relocated from further east). There are strong immigrant traditions from the English, French, Germans, Hungarians, Irish, Italians, Jews, Mexicans, Scots, etc. The Asian Chamber of Commerce in KC includes members who are Chinese, East Indian, Filipino, Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese. There is an international airport in the sparsely populated north, and another airport downtown. The one would be great for Werewolf movers and shakers, while the other is perfect for Kindred to arrive further inside the city - away from the dangers of the wild. The setting contains some interesting religions - remnants of the Spiritualists movements of the late 1800s, a few Mormon splinter groups, Amish and Church of the Brethren, Judaism, Islam, Sikhs, Bahai, and Unity Village which has a strong ancient Egyptian symbolism in its structures. Famous African American heritage tie ins include Negro Leagues Baseball and the development of Jazz as a cultural influences. It's sister city is Seville Spain, and there could easily be a Lasombra Anti-tribu connection in not only that choice - but the architecture of KC's most prominent and exclusive business district is designed from that of its sister city. The area is full of potential as not merely a book for wraith, but for cross game and cross cultural influences.

                    Now, as to why ST supplements may be more American centered has much to do with the knowledge base of those who created the game. It is difficult, especially in a nation where political correctness marches to the drums it confiscated from the Gestapo, to create said materials. If those materials are in any way 'perceivable' as stereotypical then the company will be trounced for carrying to the stereotype. If the product does not have the role models (stereotypes) that players want to role play then they will be equally disappointed and feel the culture they like is slighted. Thus far, the World of Darkness has done a relatively fantastic job of navigating those waters - but not without complaints by those who choose to be offended by the minor imperfections in their efforts. (Instead of choosing to be exalted with the brave efforts to do the best they can with the resources available to them.)

                    If they were to do a region of the world, for a game, about which they can only know what is printed for them to read - then the result would be limited to what American English readers can conceive in the translated reports of such materials. Should they even employ experts in that region, then the results will either be from Academics whose materials will defensible from a legal standpoint or from native non-academics whose results will carry local limitations (read bias) as well as insights to which academics cannot ascribe (unverifiable oral traditions about events - myth). (For instance here in the KC area there still exist oral stories of the barbarity of Union oppression during the Civil War. Accusations of murder to steal property rights for the Union affiliated banks, elite, and train companies, theft of property by Jay Hawkers, slaughtering of livestock and burning of crops, placement of women and children in concentration camp like conditions after the brutal murders of all males 11 years of age and up, etc. The academic history and the oral myth differ but are both based on real events. The best way for supplements from so far afield to be developed in my mind, would be from the direct offerings of those native to those regions, and familiar with the game mechanics and existing lore, to develop and donate them - accepting whatever editing Onyx Path may provide, and then allow them to be published. A precedent for this exists in video games like Neverwinter Nights where the player community was able to create longstanding additional materials (mods and haks) for the game which were then published through the Bioware website supporting the game.

                    And to re-emphasize what I said before, I am not down-playing any other city or region. I was just asking for Kansas City - a city whose corrupt political machine put the man in the White House who won WW II and then had that same political machine dismembered by morally justified reformers whose politics have left it gutted in prominence, tarnished in glory, and bereft of interest in the national spotlight. What better place for secret and monstrous societies to run rampant than the hidden heart of the country?
