    What says Wraith to you?

    12-23-2013, 07:33 AM
    Here's a question I've been pondering. it may have to do with mood, but I'd like to ask Primarily Story Tellers about their influences when they run Wraith games for their players. I've only ever participated briefly and never run my own. I guess my question is what influences you in modern media. Not necessarily what gets you in the mood, but what paints a wraithly picture for you visually or audibly. One example is say Silent Hill games or movie where you go "OH! that's totally how I picture the Near Shadowlands now." Or "That movie has totally given me my next hook for my Wraith game."

    I guess why I'm asking is because even though I'm a big horror buff I just don't find many movies or books that give me that jive to imagine it playing out in the Wraith setting. The exception being cultural real world histories and documentaries about funeral rites and whatnot, but those do not equate to the Wraith game. A lot of my favorite movies could be seen as the work of Haunters and be translated into Wraith terms, but it doesn't go any father into the shadowlands that the creepy face on the other side of the mirror. All kinds of movies with ghosts in them, but they're not Wraith.

    Rich, if you're looking, it'd be cool to know what helped influence you when you created Wraith? (besides "our Town" and The Theogony of Hesiod and anything else in the suggested: part of the Wraith corebook)

    So I'm hoping some of you out there have a better knack for picking out stuff from modern media that matches how you picture the Wraith setting, or stories that you find translating easily into Wraith 'life'.

    For me, knowing what I know about ancient history and perception of the dead I think muddies how to imagine the Wraith setting. I'm having trouble suspending my disbelief. I'm biased and I need help from the rest of you out there to trump what I know and help me find the fodder to imagine the Wraith setting as you see it.

    PS: Sometimes I'm really crappy at explaining things, so maybe this request makes no sense. Feel free to say so and I'll try and explain myself better.

      Here’s my list of Wraithly-inspiration, in no particular order of importance (or other organization).

      City of Lost Children (Caro and Jeunet, 1995)
      The “lab” would be fitting as a Hierarch’s office or the backdrop of a Harrowing.

      Constantine (Lawrence, 2005)
      Opinion: Horrible movie. Now that’s out of the way…
      There is a scene in which Neo delves into Hell so he can find the dead something or other of someone (I didn’t really pay attention).
      The important part!
      When he gets to Hell, it looks like a hellfire-blasted reflection of our world. Minus some flames, I’ve used this scene as a cornerstone for referencing the Shadowlands.

      The Frighteners (Jackson, 1996)
      If you’ve seen it, you know what I mean.

      Sleepy Hollow (Burton, 1999)
      The Horseman is a great example of dramatic use of the Embody Arcanos.

      The Mad Max Movies (Miller)
      Post-apocalyptic movies in general are a great source of inspiration. In a sense, everyone in Wraith has gone through the end of the world. Their ends, at least.
      The first film, Mad Max (Miller, 1979), has a good tone for population – you get a sense of things starting to break down – a good Wraithly sense of desoloation/isolation.

      Beetlejuice (Burton, 1980)
      Like The Frighteners, if you’ve seen it, you know what I mean. The cartoon series is also good.

      Resident Evil Movies
      I hate all these movies. Mostly because (IMO) they don’t make any sense. But that’s alright, because we’re taking all their weird crap and making our own story out of it. The stuff that doesn’t make sense is still okay to use – Underworld physics doesn’t have to make sense. The best Harrowings are the ones I ran without a sense of logic (although I still had purpose and structure, mind you).

      What Dreams May Come (Ward, 1998)
      Both frightening and hopeful. Good dead-folk stuff.

          A few random thoughts:

          I have a bookcase full of "true" ghost stories and local supernatural folklore. Read enough and you see patterns emerge on what folks think is waiting on the other side - and what might keep you hanging around post-mortem
          Read Thomas Ligotti - especially stories like "The Red Tower" and "The Frolic", then follow it up with Gormenghast and a few books on architectural strangeness of the ancient world. Derinkyu, Petra, Mt. Nemrut, the Paris catacombs and the ruins of Herculaneum. Read Graham Hancock, even - just don't take his conclusions seriously. What matters is the sense of epic, crumbling grandeur.
          Try a Swedish band called Gosta Berlings Saga, especially the Detta Har Hant album. Mix it with some of Mark Snow's soundtrack work, especially the music he wrote for Millennium.
          Check out a PSN game called Rain. Chase it, if you can, with Eternal Darkness and installments in the Fatal Frame series. Alan Wake and Amnesia might also be good inspiration.

          Deadguy out.

                                  I find all of these examples really interesting and I hope I can find the time to explore them all. Thank you very much and anyone else reading Please throw your suggestions out there.

                                  I personally have a lot of ghost movies and while I can't say I've seen any ghosts in my conscious life I find all the stories of experiences that other people have had with shit they can't explain very very intriguing. I have to say that from the amount of ghost stories people have and movies about ghosts, Wraith just doesn't seem to have very much of that material. It's a Part of Wraith, sure... but not a huge part. One thing I've found in reading and watching shows with ghost stories or why Ghost hunters feel the need to go running around in the dark trying to talk to things they can't hear or explain things they feel but can't see; it all seems to boil down to wanting to know what happens when we die. What waits for us on the other side. There is a lot of hope in their stories because they really want to know if the people they've loved might be waiting for them or that someone they've lost is ok and in a better place. There's the hope that the goodness they've believed in all their lives can be reached when they cross over.

                                  And well According to Wraith, it's all BS. Granted not every person who dies will become a wraith. There's certain conditions. Certain bonds that hold a soul back. The Wraith then has to resolve these Fetters in order to Transcend and their journey then stems from the despair of being dead and confused as all get out to finding the way to let go and move on. So yeah... it does have hope, but that is mainly through the Shadowlands and not a great deal with the Skinlands unless the ST chooses to focus the game that way. For all the interaction with the living that makes for such great Ghost stories, Wraith to me doesn't seem to be about explaining the other side of those stories. So I can't relate Wraith as a setting to some of my favorite movies like Poltergeist or House on Haunted Hill, or The Eye (the original Japanese version). Maybe some of you out there can totally explain how to relate Wraith terms to those movies, but I just couldn't make those connections. The ghost stories just do not seem to have any connection to the Wraith system. Not in the way I can watch a Vampire movie and assign clan labels, or watch a movie with werewolves and relate the folklore to a First change storyline.

                                  Another thing I find confusing and frustrating is that the state of the Shadowlands has so much range of location and meaning and depth, with all their horror and danger and strife with all the souls within them, that it really parallels incredibly closely with the many cultural ideas of Hell, And not even in a Fiery demon pitchfork sort of way that it might as well drop the pretense and be Hell because that's what the corruption of the system has made it despite Charon's good intentions. In that sense if it was a hell I can totally picture that. Definitely the type of place from those scenes in the movie Constantine, Tempest winds and all. Maybe not as hot, but definitely the dark and decrepit reflection of the world of the living. It's unforgiving, it's cruel, it's filled with the souls of people used as furniture and weapons and coin. It's a place of bleakness and misery where salvation is a distant glimmer many strive to hold on to and few find true redemption. So call it what it is. Hell.

                                  So this is why I'm having trouble wrapping my brain around Wraith's setting interaction and even the movies and subject matter I thought would make things more inspired and paint out that picture for me have not worked. Again, thank you for all your help and keep the suggestions coming. I really appreciate you guys.

                                              To elaborate on an earlier post; The World Ends With You is a game about dead people given a second shot; if they can survive the week-long Reaper's Game, they can come back to life. Before they can participate, whatever they find most valuable is taken from them as an 'Entry Fee' - one characters loses their memory entirely, another loses his sister's relationship with him, things like that - to be returned to them upon victory. They occupy a plane of existence known as the Underground, which is much like the Shadowlands, and cannot be seen by the living.

                                              It's a great game, and the stories it tells all focus on interpersonal relationships. It makes good inspiration for looking at how the dead interact with their Passions, and also happens to just be all-around great. Fantastic soundtrack.

                                              Remi. she/her. game designer.
