    Multiverses in Wraith the oblivion

    01-31-2023, 07:16 AM
    I am reading The book of Oblivion of Wr20, a book that a priori did not interested me so much, as it contains uber-optional information about the more high fantasy aspects of the setting (which I am less interested in) as for example, the Dark Kingdom of Swar, the Spectres or the Tempest as a plot device. It even depicts Jimmy Hendrix as a helldiver of an elite squad. But never, never I was expecting the references in the Labyrinth chapter to time travel and trans-universe-travel. It says that one particular character and, some time later, his relic airplane came from another "continuum", which means another temporal continuum or universe, as it references things that havent happened.

    That perplexed me. I was expecting the Labyrinth as a place of horror, but instead I found there the highest fantasy peaks in all the setting. That could respond to the "wyld" being trapped there, as the Haunter guildbook says? I do not know. What do you think about it? This was for me constatation of Wraith being a game of high fantasy, and not so "personal horror".

                                                      Yes well, I dont use Ferrymen either (indeeď, they are mysteries and work better that way), but I dislike the concept of it, which is why I would've prefered they had never been given too much details about.

                                                      The very idea of the Shadow is 100% based on Jungian psychology. And I really mean it. Jung analysis psychology in the form of the "Archetypes" (sounds familiar?), and one of if not the most important archetype is that of the... Surprise surprise... The Shadow.

                                                      In Jungian psychology, the Shadow is... Guess what... The part of our personality that is responsible for all self criticism. It is that little voice in our consciences that say "are you not gonna clean your room AGAIN? The entire week you have been postponing, and today one more day of it?".

                                                      An imbalanced Shadow can create a neurotic and depressive individual. It's the person with a very low self image, that constantly belittle themselves. "No wonder I dont have any friends, how would anyone want to be close to someone that is such a mess as myself".

                                                      But the thing is, for Jung, the idea of the "Self" was not a monolithic and self contained thing, but rather a complex web that arises from the many Personas we have. For example, he identified many "masks" that we use for social life - you are one person when with your family, another at work and yet another with your girlfriend. Which one of those however is truly you, your true self?

                                                      And you are yet a different person when alone in the confines of your mind. And besides those masks, there are the many archetypes, like Anima, the feminine side of males, and Animus, the masculine side of women, and several others, with special place for the Shadow.

                                                      An unbalanced Shadow is problematic as I showed above, sure, but the Shadow is an integral and indispensable part of the Self. In order to have a balanced and health Self, we need to have ALL those aspects of our personality balanced and in harmony, and that includes the Shadow.

                                                      A healthy Shadow helps an individual to correct mistakes and stay on proper course towards a healthy development. A healthy Shadow is "constructive criticism". Instead of being the voice that crushes you, it's your inner voice that says "are you just gonna quit like that, before giving 100% of what you have? And how will you be able to live with yourself if you give up right now?", and with that you push yourself to keep going and triumph, even when you're tired and willing to give up.

                                                      That's why I said that a Wraith losing his Shadow would be nothing short than lobotomy. You'd either turn into an automaton, because you would lose that which is your driven force to act, or you would turn into a psychopath, because the Shadow is also that which gives you brakes on your impulses (God, I wish I could punch him in the face... But I am not an animal to do that, am I?).

                                                      I love the aspect of the game that Transcendence is NOT the destruction of the Shadow, but rather INTEGRATION with it. It is the holistic view that Jung had about it too, that your Shadow archetype is NOT to be suppressed, but rather to be "absorbed" in a harmonic way within the Self.

                                                      And this is why all the thing about cutting your Shadow off for the Ferrymen has always been an absurd and nonsensical idea, which I could only imagine happening on lobotomized people. I mean, try imagine yourself without your inner voice and inner criticism, without the ability to see what's wrong within yourself and absolutely no drive to change NOTHING. Imagine that your house is an absolutely a mess, with roaches and rats eating pieces of pizza in your carpet and you are absolutely fine with it and doesnt feel the slightest need to fix anything. That's more or less what lacking a Shadow would mean

                                                          I thought that might be where you were coming from. I agree that you can definitely see some fingerprints of Jungian psychology in Wraith. However, there are notable differences too.

                                                          Most notably, a Wraith doesn't need the Shadow as any kind of driving force to pursue his Passions. It might occasionally provide a boost in the form of Shadow Dice, but it is not a dominant driver. Nor does it play any mechanical role in resolving Passions, except by accident. Nor does it play any role at all in developing new ones. You could view this as a flaw in the system mechanics, but I don't.

                                                          The way I view it is that in wraiths the Passions (regular and dark) are terribly inflamed. That's why Wraiths even persist. The Psyche and Shadow are not healthy and functioning parts of a complete soul, they are the parts that desperately resist moving on.

                                                          The Psyche is the part that cling desperately to the world. The Passions are not in any way balanced, they are drives the Psyche clings to with monomaniacal fervor even in the face of death. It doesn't need the Shadow to help push them. Indeed a healthy Shadow of the kind you describe would almost exclusively push towards resolving them.

                                                          The Shadow, though, is not healthy, as it is the part that would rather cease than continue on in any form. Any self critical part of it bends towards self destruction. Any mediating role it has is subsumed by the Dark Passions, which fuel that desire for self destruction and also drive it to action.

                                                          Perhaps we can say that if the excesses of the Shadow are cured it would function much like a Jungian shadow, but it is not functioning in that role for most Wraiths. The Psyche must drive and mediate itself, and it does.
