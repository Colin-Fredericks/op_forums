                Thing is, everything in the Underworld is based on emotion AND memory. I think this is kinda what nothing is getting at. That is, the old orchard where you used to play and whatnot WOULD be in the Shadowlands, but proper wilderness – as the OP specifically asked about – would not likely appear at all in the Shadowlands. This may pose issues with those of us who believe that the Shadowlands and the Skinlands are a one-to-one image of each other. The fact of the matter is that they are not such.

                Now, to be fair, *I* do take a pretty narrow definition of what constitutes 'wilderness' being places not used much by anyone, and that includes such 'obvious' things as widely visible mountain ranges, long stretches of otherwise empty highway, and the 'stuff' that's all inbetween here and there are wilderness. Those things just don't exist in the Shadowlands 'coz no one cares enough about their memories of them to conjure a reflection. The Shadowlands are close to the Skinlands in many ways, and the most notable is how the thoughts and feels of the living sculpt the Shadowlands.

                On some level it's possible that the dead could sculpt their reflection of our world, true. However, this becomes difficult when one factors in how brief most wraiths' stay in the Shadowlands is. As fetters are lost, sheer Passion cannot keep one of the restless dead from falling into the Tempest. There ARE some few who do so, but they do not have the power to keep things in the Shadowlands like living humanity does.

                ...This is borne out by 'the RULEZ' in passages that say travel between necropoli mus be made via the Tempest or other means. A ghost cannot 'walk' from Denver to Miami and then 'swim' to Bristol. Yeah, there's SOME contradictory evidence to this (in books like Blood-dimmed Tides and in one of the Werewolf: the Wyld West titles, I think), but the rules as written don't allow for proper wilderness in the Shadowlands.

                As ever, mileage varies, and I'm all for weirdness that contradicts whatever *I* think is 'canon' in a game. The above is more my own take on it than anything else, and know that I DO appreciate this conversation!

                        Sorry, guys, it seems like the intrinsic rules of Wraith don't back this up. The Skinlands ARE. That's what the Rule of Ouch is all about. You can't possibly be saying that every tree a wraith avoids walking through is a tree that was DEEPLY, TRULY cared for, every parking structure was utterly meaningful, etc.. They are THERE and the wraiths much deal with them. A city isn't some weird mashed up psychotropic salad where the downtown is larger and more full of structures cheek-by-jowl and the McMansion suburbs are squished up tiny little places because of their lack of meaning. Psychic imprint doesn't fold or stretch or compact or negate space, it just effects how present said material will be in the Shadowlands when its Skinlands tenure is done. The land doesn't have to be a sous-Relic to be there. It's THERE. Sure, that downtown is FULL of stuff. It's full of all the things that are there in the Skinlands jostling with and overlapping with the memories of the past buildings people did care about and the structures wraiths have built from the detritus of fading memories and jury-rigged together. That's another reason Haunts rock. Skinland architecture/geography and that of the Shadowlands are definitely sympathetic if not actually congruent.

                        Because the Shadowlands are reflection of the Skinlands until you start going "that-a-way" off the map to where-there-be-Tempest and cities built of memories. The "land shaped by passion" is out there. Sure, Stygia might be squishy and shaped by passion more than geography, but, like Swar, the Dark Kingdom of Sand, the Far Shores, et al. it's because in essence it's more of an idea of a place than a place. Sorta a shaped part of the Tempest. Effectively, Stygia, accessible via Byways and Tempest, is the nearest of the Far Shores.

                        Besides, even if I did buy into this "passion" = "space" idea, doesn't a farmer care about his fields? In a fundamental way that is more visceral than, say, people care about a shed behind their house? Or the entire area a trapper uses for his lines? The area over which a hunter ranges to catch his prey? Doesn't the sea matter DEEPLY to the fisherman? The road to the traveler? How many people have to care before something becomes "real enough"? After all, it just takes ONE wraith to make multiple things into Fetters.

                        I would by that the Tempest may be a little "closer" in places where there are less people. But it seems just as likely that it's the opposite. Nihils happen where bad things happen to people. Where there are few people, fewer bad things can happen to them and there would be less Nihils. Also, it seems to take increased population to generate Maelstroms more frequently (particularly since a lot of Maelstroms, Great and otherwise are just massive examples of Nihil production. Ruptures due to bad things happening to people as opposed to pin-pricks). Maybe the Wilderness is MORE solid and dependable. But there are less Haunts when things do get bad. (Nasty emotional weather downwind from those memory and deed choked cities). And there is very little Pathos out there to keep a wraith going. Fetters are far away and Slumber isn't going to be an option. And, if anything bad does happen, it's off to the Tempest and back to your Fetters which are probably back where there are people, etc. Wilderness isn't absent, it's just so painfully, desperately empty....
