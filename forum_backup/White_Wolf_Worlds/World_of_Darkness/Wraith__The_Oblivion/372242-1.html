        Okay, this is a long post...

        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post

        Set could be using powers similar to the Nagaraja. That is, if he even retains a physical body. He might be something akin to a mummy (first and second edition, not revised), as their stories are intertwined, or he may at least have used some of the stolen knowledge his side used to create Bane Mummies to give him some kind of possibility of bodily resurrection.

        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post

        Arikel –

        ...another name she goes by is Ishtar, famous for travelling to the Underworld and trying to get a loved one back to the land of the living.

        ...So she could be a mover and shaker in any one of the Dark Kingdoms (though Stygia is most likely…not including Enoch of course, if we consider her Wraith/Specter as a possible member of the Aralu) under another name.
        She could be one of the Aralu, yes. One did have that name in Dirty Secrets (there were four or five Aralu, and Inauhaten said one was Arikel; others think Loz could be Toreador). This ties into the possibility I outline below for Ventru.

        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post

        Ventru –

        Unlike Arikel, the cannon version of Ventru’s story has him die (by Troiles hand) outside the gates of the Second City shortly before the city fell. Some evidence exists that he planned his own death and might have even be suicidal. So like the non-cannon Arikel, there is a descent candidate for him to become a Wraith.
        Likewise, Ventru could also be one of the Aralu. It's implied one of those beings might be Ventru (Nergal). If you prefer to ignore other cosomologies, then the Aralu may be the wraithly embodiments (or whatever lingers after the 'death') of Antediluvians.

        However, Ambrogino Giovanni saw angelic beings evacuating the city upon its destruction. It's hinted in the Hunter ST's Companion (and this fits with what Ambrogino saw) that the three Aralu/angelic figures seen leaving Enoch might be the Messengers who give the Imbued (Hunters) their power. If there were indeed five Aralu (legends vary from three to five), then the other two Aralu could be the Ministers themselves, who are the masters of the Messengers (the Scarlet Empress and Ebon Dragon who also empowered the Kuei-jin in past ages).

        All this is particularly interesting, though, because Gehenna specifically states that the Scarlet Empress is a facet of Lilith (who has become elevated to the level of a god/Celestial Incarna, equal to Lucifer and Yahweh), who is also a ruler of the Dragon-Blooded (precursors of the Kuei-jin in early plans for the game) in Exalted. Some consider the Ebon Dragon to be Caine (KotE lore), however that's less certain, as Lilith and he part ways in most sources. Giovanni Chronicles I does suggest that Lilith and Yahweh are the dark and light halves of a Godhead, however, which might imply Yahweh was the Ebon Dragon instead (or 'dragon' could refer to Lucifer or even another aspect of Lilith). In this version of reality, it implies Celestial Incarna/gods take different forms for different cosmologies and depending on whoever's viewing them (e.g., Yahweh is also the August Personage of Jade). This fits with the subjective reality concept in mage, and the mention of Creation actually existing on various levels of literal and metaphorical existence in Demon, making many of the conflicting myths all true at the same time to a degree. It could also be true that with each new age of man, the consensus reshuffles a little bit, so that reality reshapes itself retrospectively and the truth evolves/changes, creating inconsistencies in mapping out any objective cosmology.

        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post

        Abel –

        He’s the first human to ever die and the first murder victim. It seems pretty thematically appropriate that he becomes a Wraith to me. Hell, he’d technically be the first Wraith.
        One Gehenna scenario even has the PC’s call up Abel as a Ghost to confront Caine. So it seems to fit that he’s still around and not Shadow-Eaten by the modern nights.
        Abel, or at least the memory of him, is kept in one of Oblivion's Memory Towers. So he's already accounted for, and is separate to Charon.

        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post

        Alternatively, a nice little twist could be that he did become Shadow-Eaten and his Shadow started off the whole manifestation of Oblivion in the Underworld. Maybe he’s now a stupidly powerful Onceborn or even the entity sitting at the heart of the Void.
        If Abel is imprisoned within one of Oblivion's memory towers, it could imply that he is the buried Psyche of Oblivion, and Oblivion is his Shadow. However, the Grand Maw/Grandma (Oblivion's 'consciousness') is basically a giant symbol of the Monstrous Feminine (in critical theory, the devouring, Freudian vagina symbolising the threat of the mother who threatens to swallow the feeble-minded man-child who is terrified of such things). She sounds more like a Primordial than anything else (like Malfeas, or Autochthon, or Gaia), and could be either Cytherea or a similar generative being who died and therefore took on a new, corrupted purpose. Alternatively, she could simply be an aspect of the Wyrm (the aspect trapped by the Weaver and driven mad, known as the Wyrm of Balance).

        Orpheus implies she could be God's dark twin, but the dark twin of Gaia would be more likely. It's implied in Orpheus that Oblivion is a fundamental paradox, and hides its own paradoxical nature in its memory towers (Abel, who conversely connects Oblivion to all of life itself). It might be that Oblivion is the dark twin of the universe itself, and the embodiment of the paradox of Creation emerging from Nothingness. Or, going right back to the Wraith core book and forgetting everything else, Oblivion could simply be the pain of the universe when the Neverborn were killed. It wishes to create (as revealed in Orpheus: End Game) in a strange way, but not because it is a generative being in itself. Rather, it seeks out creation (with a small 'c') because it is inextricably tied to Creation (with a big 'C'). Its duty is to destroy, so that Creation might create anew and thus give it more things to destroy again, with each half of that reality feeding the other (Creation to Oblivion, Oblivion to Creation). Creation and Oblivion could, in effect, be the ouroboros, with the destructive head (Oblivion) gorging on the generative tail (Creation), but the generative tail also emerging from the destructive head.

        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post

        So the Neverborn in Wraith are entities that predate our realities existence. Existing in this reality is torment to them and they hate every aspect of our reality. They want everything to cease so that they can go back to non-existence (as we understand it) which seems to be their natural habitat. If they ever woke up they would pretty much start demolishing our reality in short order. Luckily they spend most of their time asleep because the Underworld, while less painful to them then the living one, kinda dopes them up a fair bit.

        So the Children from Clanbook Baali are entities that predate our realities existence. Existing in this reality is torment to them and they hate every aspect of our reality. They want everything to cease so that they can go back to non-existence (as we understand it) which seems to be their natural habitat. If they ever woke up they would pretty much start demolishing our reality in short order. Luckily the Baali use atrocities and the “breaking” of their True names to keep them asleep in the hidden and deep “places” of the living material world.

        Hang on a second…

        If we tie this in with the story at the start of the Baali Clanbook that some of the Children burned up and died when God created the light at the start of time, well, it seems the Neverborn might be “dead” (I use this term loosely, I don’t thinking something that pre-dates our reality can really die as we understand the concept at least) Children.

        Anybody else had this thought? Anyone come up with other possible links between Vampire stuff and the Neverborn?
        In Exalted, the Neverborn are dead Primordials, whose undying nature meant the Underworld was created when they were 'slain' by the gods/Celestial Incarna (an act which ruptured Creation because of the paradox of killing beings who can't die). Their living siblings were imprisoned in the body of Malfeas (another Primordial) and became the Yozi (probably the masters (Urge Wyrms?) of the Maeljin Incarna/Infernal Exalted of the oWoD/Exalted). As Malfeas exists in both Mage and Werewolf, we might assume the two realms are the same in both the oWoD and Exalted. In which case, the Children might be any Primordial (Yozi or Neverborn) which the Baali can contact.

        However, Demon: The Fallen and later VtM supplements later confused matters, by implying the Children were simply Earthbound (and tying the Earthbound into a Judeo-Christian context). This perspective might be considered biased, of course, as the angels and demons were all creations of Yahweh, which means they would recognise his worldview, rather than the more polytheistic one implied by other games (in which Yahweh is just one of the Celestial Incarna, albeit their presumed ruler in the West, who collectively seized power from the Primordials before them). The Earthbound could merely have opted to become the servants of the Primordials, which would allow both options to be true.

                                Well the OP is specially talking about crossover games, so bringing in other games lines is kind of the point. You can keep the mythologies separate if you wish, of course, but this thread was started with a question exploring the tangles that occur when the gamelines are brought together. This post continues in that vein (seeking interconnections, even if they're, well, in vain).

                                Having the Black Spiral directly connected to the Labyrinth (via a spiritual pathway) doesn't mean the Wyrm and Oblivion have to be one and the same. Yomi Wan has pathways leading to and from all sorts of other worlds. I think you thought I meant 'connected' as in 'the same' or 'spiritually related'. I'm referring to routes like trods or dragon lines or byways. That doesn't necessary imply any of these interconnected things have an objective existential/causal relationship.

                                However, back to the idea of crossovers, while the Wyrm is active and Oblivion is passive, you could go the Yang/Yin route of KotE and interpret them as both being the Yang and Yin aspects of the part of the Wheel that destroys/recycles. Not the same thing, but mirroring each other in an active/passive binary. The oWoD is full of such dualities, so it's really not a huge leap of faith. Again, this isn't explicit in the material, but we're supposing again for the sake of crossovers.

                                The parallels between Exalted and the oWoD, on the other hand, are just useful for helping to conceptualise some of the ideas. Neverborn claim they either came into existence at the moment the Underworld was created, or predate Yahweh's Creation altogether. Exalted has both as true (if you see Yahweh as semi-analagous to the Unconquered Sun). I know it's an act in futility, but so long as the correspondences between the two settings exist, there will always be some relationship inferred by players. I personally find it fun, but I know others hate linking the two.

                                Back to 'outside of Creation'--I meant beyond even the Umbra (which has a form). Literally the darkness before God said 'Let there be light.' I'll explain why I mention this in a minute. There's constant reference to this nothingness or darkness in various gamelines (it's the place the Children apparently fell from and one possible origin of the Malfeans, for example). This darkness is the antithesis of Creation and the absence of God. In a Gnostic sense, it is everything outside the world of the Artificer God, or the shadow simultaneously created by the light of what the Artificer built, and perhaps even the Divine Reality (less likely). In a Greek paradigm, it could be the world of the Titans (Primordials) who existed before the Olympians (gods). Thus those who are banished from God's presence, or receive 'The Anathema of the One' as it's been described (in the Guarded Rubrics), are creatures of darkness that often can't bear the light (Caine, Lilith, the Children). Demons can bear the light, but are literally denied it while in the Abyss.

                                The Abyss could, of course, be in the Labyrinth in the DtF cosmology. It's probably the gameline I know least, and as you say, it seemed to contradict so much of the earlier source material (although each game does, to a degree). I have heard that theory before (it might even have been the post of yours you're talking about). The implications of it being in or adjacent to the Abyss, however, are certainly interesting from a wraith perspective--particularly now there are cracks. Can you imagine battling escaped demons as well as spectres? And some of these greater demons must surely be able to command the spectres? (On a side note, re: demons having little grasp of the Underworld, I'd always seen them like deluded golems of Yahweh, believing his strict interpretation of reality, whether it was true or not.)

                                An idea that occurred to me (again, not necessarily a likely one, but one a Storyteller with a crossover campaign might be interested in) is that perhaps Grandma wasn't Oblivion, after all, but a demon who, for whatever reason, had occupied Oblivion. We know the Earthbound inhabit locations because they don't have bodies (in this sense they're very similar to Primordials like Gaia, who are a location or realm in themselves), so if we see the Grand Maw equally as a location (the lip or event horizon of a black hole, in effect) then that could be a possibility.

                                Getting deeper into abstract philosophical points, Grandma could even be the native nothingness from which Yahweh and Creation actually came in the first place (she's motherly, sort of), as things definitely spew from the Well of the Void (such as maelstroms). Gnosticism has Sophia as the mother of Yahweh, and she represents a kind of primordial knowledge. Again, I find these possibilities intriguing. Anyway, sorry, I'm getting waaaay too much into abstractions here... We got off-topic about vampires and wraiths, didn't we? :P
