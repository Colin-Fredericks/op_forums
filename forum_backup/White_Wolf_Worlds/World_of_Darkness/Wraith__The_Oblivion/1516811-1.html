            Well the Catholic and Orthodox will be the most prominent, and for the purposes of the Underworld, there's not much difference between them. The schism was driven more by politics and language barriers than anything else. That's not to say there aren't theological differences, but those differences aren't likely to matter to the average Christian wraith. The single biggest disagreement that would be widely known involves the primacy of the Pope over the other bishops and patriarchs. Since those are living leaders who decide matters of faith as they pertain to the living, its not likely to matter as much in the Underworld.

            For the rest, it depends on the group and the differences they had with the rest of the Church. The Albigensians, I suspect, are not going to have an easy time. Their beliefs stand in direct contradiction of the Creeds (Apostle's, Nicene, etc.). They directly dispute major elements of the faith that the average Christian is likely to know (the incarnation of Christ, the resurrection of the body, the virgin birth, etc.) Not only that, the Albigensians are going to be rocked much, much harder by the nature of the Underworld than most wraiths. As I recall, their faith claimed that the body was the source of sin and that the soul freed from the body was freed from sin. Given that Albigensian ghosts were spirits who were not freed from sin, they'd have some soul searching to do (no pun intended).




            ​

            I agree. That's a separate question from whether they thought they had been empowered to set up, though. Either origin for the Fishers could set up their Far Shore with the best of intentions. If the Fishers thought they were The Church Suffering* directly set up by Christ during his descent would do it thinking that they were divinely commissioned to do so. If they didn't have that grounding they could either claim to still have the mandates they did in life, or just set it up to follow the teachings they knew as best as possible secure in the faith that would be enough. Any of those three options would be good faith efforts to help wraiths Transcend.

            *The Church Suffering is a Catholic term for the part of the Church currently in Purgatory. The Underworld doesn't map perfectly to Purgatory, but if it isn't a great term for an organization in the Underworld I don't know what is.

                                        Vessels of significance in the New Testament:

                                        St Peter and St Andrew’s fishing boat.

                                        Zebedee’s fishing boat. That’s the one St John and St James would have used

                                        The vessel the Apostles were aboard when they saw Christ walk on water. If it’s not one of the above.

                                        The vessel Jesus calmed the storm in. If it’s not one of the above.

                                        The vessel Jesus preached in. If it’s not one of the above.

                                        The best, though, is the prison ship that was wrecked while carrying St Paul to Rome. It is a good size for transport, it has a relic creating event, and it has good thematic resonance with both the ideal and the fallen elements of the Fishers.

                                                Originally posted by voidshaper View Post

                                                The boat was a very early Christian symbol. It has been used to represent the Church itself reaching a safe haven with its Christian souls onboard, thus a symbol of safety.
                                                The central seating area of the church is called the Nave which derives from the Latin for Ship.
                                                he mast of the boat itself often formed a cross and thus represented the cross especially in times of persecution when the cross needed to be disguised.
                                                A fishing boat might represent the Kingdom of Christ and his followers. Jesus told his disciples to “follow me and I will make you fishers of men”.

                                                The boat embodies the voyage of life, of coming full circle and also the last journey, taking us back to the waters of our beginning. Eric Neumann describes the ship of death “leading us back to the swaying, gliding somnolent rhythms of earliest childhood, of the primordial ocean and the night”.

                                                            Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
                                                            Regarding the Tempest, it would be strange to me if the Sunless Sea and Sea of Shadows did not function similarly to how the Tempest functions now: as an overlapping dimension which lies between the Shadowlands and the deeper parts of the Underworld. It's metaphysical role for me is the spacial expression of the soul's journey towards the different ends it could seek - a disconnect from life (and the Skinlands through the lens of the Shadowlands) towards the beyond. As such, I would expect it to be a more visceral realm that is sensitive to the expectations of the traveler before the Maelstroms turned it into the chaos it is - Nihils become the 'Gates to the Underworld' and Argos the skill to navigate the 'tests' of the journey.
                                                            A bit more on this:

                                                            The books still refer to it as Tempest for history sections pre-Third Great Maelstrom (perhaps for convenience), but it indicates that it still functioned as a separate dimension. I take the descriptions to mean that the 'Gauntlet' between Tempest and Shadowlands used to be a lot weaker, and you could just step into it in some liminal spaces without the use of a Nihil, like a Shallowing in Mage terms. That would satisfy the conditions that Stygia used to be connected to and later severed from the Shadowlands.

                                                            Functionally, I see it working not too differently from a Harrowing, but much milder. The realm would be taking the shape of a transitory landscape or narrative that symbolically reflects a traveling soul's (Dark) Passions, Memories, and so on. It would present a trial to overcome that resembles a stage in their Transcendence they struggle with, or unresolved conflict, etc.
                                                            It could be that the Tempest and Shadowlands overlap to such a degree that denizens and structures of either realm are used as the stage for this, allowing for encounters that are tailor-made for a character's narrative. This keeps with the 'speed of story' travel time the Tempest has, too.

                                                            Traveling groups are interesting, because it would be a chance for a character getting an outside perspective on their troubles while experiencing them. I never played a Group Harrowing, but I imagine the dynamic would be similar. And on a side note, Argos would probably still focus on travel, but with a slant towards 'questing' to complement the mechanic.

                                                            I would love to see everybody else's take on a tame Tempest, because I think it's a pretty important element of the setting.
