        Okay, so over the last 24 hours I've read 38 pages of comments, but I don't think this has been mentioned:

        Rich, will there be enough info on Legions to make them playable from the core book?

        What I mean is, Guilds got their DPS in WtO, and Legions got theirs in WtGW. Because the detailed info on Legions and Guilds never appeared together, in one book, I always felt I had to use both books to create a fully rounded character.

        I know that Legions are kinda like Auspices, but also like Tribes, and that Guilds are kinda like Clans, but also like Disciplines. And I like that. It allows for the most flexibility in creating characters in any oWoD game, excluding Dark Ages: Fae. But as both Arcanoi and the manner of one's death are important in play, I feel both 'splats' should get sizeable write-ups.

        Prior to reading The Book of Legions, I had absolutely no idea how to play everyday death in a Legion, for example. The details on Legion politics and seats were invaluable in WtGW too.

        I'm glad Heretics and Renegades are also getting more space in the core book, as well as spectres. It really opens up the options for play right from the beginning. Half the players in threads here want personal games about fetters and the world of the living, while the other half wants games of dark discovery and intrigue (well, maybe a third each, with a third wanting a bit of both). Presenting all this info together allows for the widest range of styles right out of the box (or out of The Behemoth).

        I'd like to reiterate, too, that the Bush of Ghosts seems a bit of an Orientalist stereotype (more so than the DKoJ), and I'd always suspected its treatment in the sourcebooks was pretty much indicative of the small part Western wraiths are allowed to see of it. Will we get hints of the more nuanced and diverse cultures existing behind this 'magical kingdom' smokescreen? Even a few words to the effect that Western wraiths see what their Imperialist hearts expect to see, but that Africa itself isn't one nation (as Sarah Palin seems to think), would go a long way.

        Finally, as I'm just starting a new game on Skype on Sunday and various players seem to keep arguing about one particular thing, can you give us the tiniest of spoilers from the new book: can wraiths soak aggravated damage or not? The publishing of 2e soak difficulties for fire suggest yes, but the rulebook never stated explicitly the difference between normal and aggravated damage (except in how they're healed). Half the camp thinks wraiths should be soft, and only soak bashing, while the other half thinks they should be hard and soak everything. Or is it the middle way of vampires, with an art or at least armour providing the only way to soak aggravated damage? It has quite a bearing on tone, ultimately.

        Thanks

                Legions - They should be playable from the core book. One of the big goals I have is smoothing out the occasionally incongruent ways Legions, Guilds, and factions fit into Wraith society, which is to say they didn't always play so well together.                                                     Hey Crowley,

                                                    I always describe the Shadowlands as being like an alternate dimension. It occupies roughly the same space and time, but sits on top of the dimensions we see. So time would run exactly the same as in the Skinlands. In the Labyrinth, or deep in the Tempest, however, things become a bit more abstract...

                                                    In terms of Stygia, it exists in yet another dimension. While the Shadowlands can best be imagined as a transparent grey shroud draped over the mortal world, the Tempest is like hyperspace. It exists under the Shadowlands, connecting every bit to every other bit. Stygia exists as an oasis within that hyperspace. Think of it as a pocket reality or, if we instead imagine the Tempest as a nebula, as a star or planet within the cloud.

                                                    Thus, Stygia doesn't correlate at all with anything on a human map, because it exists in a place where space is fundamentally different.

                                                    In terms of the sun in the Shadowlands, I'll return to the idea above of that plane being like a shroud over the Skinlands. The sun would shine through such a shroud, but its light would be dimmed and slowed down. It's going to be dull and red, or at least dark orange.

                                                    If you look at the cover of The Hierarchy, that has an orange sky that might give you an indication of the sky of the Shadowlands.
