I'm not talking about Mediums who can talk to the dead, but what they were doing in the movie or the ending of the Frighteners. What sort of effects would happen to their health or sanity? Would they be subjected to a Harrowing?

      This is an interesting subject. I'd suggest looking to Orpheus and Guildbook: Sandmen for inspiration and thoughts about this.

      Crossing the Shroud and travelling to the Shadowlands can be a harrowing (not Harrowing) ordeal for a mortal. The Dark Umbra is not like the material world. Emotions have a wheight and substance there, and the strong energies of death can infect a soul and take root. Mortals who go there and return should not be quite the same as before.
      Crossing into the Shadowlands physically requires powerful magic, and it's usually only done by Awakened mages. The Shroud is so thick these days that the deeds of Orpheus are all but impossible. Still, there are always exceptions. During certain days of the year at certain locations, the Shroud can grow dangerously thin. Giovanni vampires have access to NEcromancy rituals that may thin the shroud even further, and other works of dark, necromantic magic could have similiar effects. The poor, unfortunate soul who stumbles through one of these "holes" in the Shroud would find himself in a very dangerous situation. Living flesh was never ment to withstand the dark influence of Oblivion, and as a GM I would make the effects of Oblivion harsh and punishing if a body was exposed to it for any prolonged periods of time.

                          From Orpheus’ write-up of Sleepers (a type of Projector):
                          Sleepers receive the same sort of training that skimmers receive but lack the key physical, psychological or spiritual trait enabling skimmers to project more-or-less at will. Instead, sleepers enter special chambers reminiscent of the earliest cryogenic tanks that Orpheus used to preserve its clients in a state of near-death. Orpheus technicians replace his blood with a serum that maintains the organs' health even as they ought naturally to decay and atrophy and reduce the sleeper's core temperature to around minus 80ºF. Orpheus consultants refer to this process as “flatlining.” Once flatlined, the sleeper can project just as a skimmer does, and acts in most ways as does a ghost. His soul is lightly tethered to his body, though unlike a skimmer, he is in no danger of ghostly damage pushing its way out to his body.
                          Orpheus’ Sleepers are Flatliners. And if I understand it correctly, the “multiple near-death experiences” thing is more a pragmatic recruiting filter of “if you’ve done it before, chances are you can do it again” rather than “there’s something supernatural about you”. Other companies (or “freelancers” operating on a shoestring budget in a university’s labs) may not hold to the same standards, with the likely result that prospective “Sleepers” often end up as vegetables instead of Projecting. This, I think, would be the main difference between a professional Sleeper and a first-time Flatliner: in the latter case, there’s a very real chance that your spirit will immediately Transcend or be sucked into Oblivion, scuttling any plans you might have had about Projecting. Not everyone who dies becomes a ghost. By contrast, Sleepers have already demonstrated that they have what it takes to stick around.

                          Orpheus also made a few assumptions about some metaphysical changes brought about by the Sixth Great Maelstrom, such as the transformation of the Shroud into an impenetrable Stormwall (and the notion that spirits of the dead reside on this side of the Stormwall rather than on the other side of a Shroud) and the fact that Spirits get Spectre nemeses instead of Shadows; but that’s inherent to the timeframe the game was set in.

                          I’m curious how Wr20 is going to handle Orpheus and its Projectors. I suspect that Sleepers and Skimmers will be handled just like Wraiths while they’re Projecting, with all of Orpheus’ business about Shades and Horrors being replaced by an ability to learn and use appropriate Arcanoi while Projecting. That’s certainly the way I’d handle it: most of the Shades and Horrors found in Orpheus were inspired by Wraith’s Arcanoi, after all.

                          My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

The point is that Sleepers are suitable candidates because of the multiple near-death experiences, rather than having had multiple near-death experiences because they’re suitable candidates. (Skimmers are potentially a different story in that regard, as they have a key physical, psychological or spiritual trait that lets them project more or less at will; whether they always had that trait and it was activated by their near-death experiences, or they developed it in response to their near-death experience, is an open question.)


                                              Originally posted by Neall View Post
                                              I kind of considered Shades to be an integral part of the Orpheus experience, though things may change between now and final draft time.
                                              OK. Really, my issue was less with that side of it than the business of Horrors vs. Arcanoi. That said, the bit about Shades was essentially giving everyone — Projectors and ghosts alike — an x-splat, where the nature of your manifestations, the powers that you are naturally inclined toward, and your general temperament are all bundled together. On the one hand, I don’t want Projectors to be radically different from Wraiths while they’re projected (that was another big part of the Orpheus experience: if a skimmer or sleeper dies while projecting, he remains more or less the same — just without a body to return to), so my thought was for Wraith’s take on Orpheus’ projectors to be more wraith-like.

                                              OTOH, I could see presenting the Shades as an optional system for Storytellers who like the notion that human souls naturally fall into broad types, leading to wraiths getting classified as poltergeists, banshees, wisps, etc., with built-in affinities for suitable Arcanoi — which in turn would lead to the Shades being a significant factor in Guild recruiting.

                                              What I don’t want to see is “you’re a projector, so you have a Shade. Oh, look; your body died, and now you’re a wraith. Bye bye, Shade…” Almost as bad would be the notion that Orpheus’ Spirits exist alongside wraiths as similar but distinct denizens of the lands of the dead.

                                                          Here is the preamble of the Dictum Mortuum...
                                                          I, Charon, Emperor of Stygia, by the power of the words of the Lady of Fate, do hereby set these laws down that they may serve to bring order between this world and the former;(1) that no wraith under any circumstances for any reason is to penetrate the barrier that divideth the Land of the Dead from the Land of the Quick;(2) to engage in congress with those that have not yet passed over, or speak to the Quick of events yet to come;(3) or in any way, shape, or form harass, threaten injure or otherwise compel those in the Land of the Quick to do the bidding of those in the Land of the Dead;(4) or import upon those in the Land of the Quick any desire or compunction to take a life, be it their own or that of another, in order to effect the passing over of one or more to the Land of the Dead, be they friend or foe, parent, child or lover...
                                                          (ennumeration mine)

                                                          If you take a strict reading of this...

                                                          1)A member of Orpheus Group who has crossed over into the Land of the Dead is already there, and so interacting with that person does not cross the shroud.
                                                          2)If you follow this by spirit, then a member of Orpheus Group who is in the Shadowlands should not be spoken with at all. One could argue, however, that a member of Orpheus Group who is in the shadowlands has "passed over" and thus can be interacted with freely.
                                                          3)Note the word "in" the Land of the Quick. not "of".
                                                          4)Again, the use of the word "in". Also can be taken as a forbiddance against helping Orpheus Group to cross over into the Shadowlands...

                                                          It's also said (p. 52) that the Code is continually updated, but most Shadowland agents don't have the time or energy to read it, let alone enforce it, and simply concentrate on keeping the peace. It's possible that Stygia could declare Orpheus Group to be an enemy of the state and a threat to the Dictum Mortuum, or it could be simply treated as a local issue and left to the individual Necropoli to sort out. I guess it depends on how big a threat Orpheus is to Stygia.
