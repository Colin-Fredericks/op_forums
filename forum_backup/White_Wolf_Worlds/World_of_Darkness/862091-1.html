        Regarding civilian hunters interactions with the government agency tasked with investigating and dealing with the supernatural (hereafter referred to as the agency) generally would not want to work with civilians, or even have them aware of what they are dealing with. Generally civilian hunters would be viewed as dangerous variables at best or domestic terrorists (probably even supernaturals) at worst. If they did run into a civilian hunter I see a few things happening assuming the civilian hunter wasnt killed during the initial meeting.
        • The civilian hunter is going to be brought in for questioning (especially focusing on where they got their supernatural information and any weaponry they had).
        • Any weapons the civilian hunter had will be checked for legality.
        • A background check will be run on the civilian hunter not only to get personal information but to check for any warrants, arrests, or other legal issues.
        • The civilian hunter will probably be held for at least the day if not longer (assuming the previous background check came clean and the weapons are legal) while the agency determines the best course of action.
        • Edit [If the above come out clean A will probably happen. If there are problems or suspicions B will occur] A) If funding allows the and the civilian hunter acquitted themselves with the proper propriety they might offered a job working for the government, otherwise the agency will most likely tell the hunter to stop what they are doing with a warning that if they continue the next time they cross paths the agency will not be so lenient in a civilian interfering with a governmental investigation (something along the lines of "While we appreciate the desire to protect yourself and others we cannot have civilians endangering themselves or others with reckless actions. If this happens again we will prosecute you to the fullest extent of the law."). The agency will then probably continue to do some monitoring of the civilian to insure compliance with their request and to check for supernatural connection. B) Something came up illegal or suspicious, and the agency will establish cause to continue to hold the hunter and either begin normal legal prosecution (using everything applicable with no legal infraction being too small to use after all they got Al Capone on tax evasion) or use various anti terror measures to hold the civilian hunter as long as possible while they being investigating them more in depth.

        No matter what I do not see the agency condoning civilian hunters attempting to deal with the supernatural, the chance for death or corruption are simply too high, the stakes too great.

Tasks they might not agree to do (but aren't flat out) have the player roll one of the player's social attributes (based on how they were asking for the mentor's help) + mentor rating; with a difficulty based on the task itself (generally how dangerous) on a success the mentor agrees to the task, failure the mentor denies the request (perhaps saying something along the lines of, "I thought you were further along than this, you shouldn't need me to do this.", on a botch the request has angered the mentor (possibly even consider lowering the mentor rating or losing it entirely if the character has angered the mentor before). If the mentor does agree to a task 'beneath' them, the mentor doesn't necessarily have to do it themselves, if its beneath them they might have other underlings/students who can do it and then the mentor simply reports the results.

                        Mentors of things like Unseelie fae, Sabbat vampires, and other 'antagonist' groups are not necessarily grudging about helping you. Rather they want to shape you into a useful apprentice or protege or may just like your character for some capricious reason. The mentor clearly sees potential in you and doesnt want to waste it and their help may less come with orders and more ask for favors and aid dealing with minor issues they dont have the time to deal with themselves, but need a trusted aide to handle. They might treat the character paternally or push them to better themselves with challenges (a mentor like the latter is more likely to phrase requests to the character as orders). Remember the mentor should be on the same side as the character, just because they are part of an "evil" group they are united in their cause. Where such a mentor might come off as more evil is in response to failure as they will be less forgiving especially of multiple failures, such a student could lose rating in mentor or lose the mentor altogether as the mentor no longer thinks the student is 'worth their time'. Also student who too often seeks the mentor's aid or seeks it for things they can do themselves are much more likely to irritate the mentor and run into the same problem as a constant failure, the mentor may lose interest in their student and no longer help them (something along the lines of, "I have no time for dullards who cant even handle the most simple tasks on their own; I was supposed to enlighten you, not hold your hand".

                        Each dot of resources covers a range of incomes and ownings so there I always saw some leeway as to whats available as temporary accommodations based partly on the characters other holding and assets, basically the super wealthy can live like the middle class easily for a long time even when away from 'home'. Here's what I saw as possible options for that 'temporary/indefinite' level
                        • Resources 1: A motel room, a hotel room, a cheap apartment, a trailer, a RV
                        • Resources 2: A nice hotel room, a decent apartment, a cheaper condominium, renting a small house
                        • Resources 3: An extravagant hotel room, a nice apartment, a condominium, buying a smaller house, renting a larger house

                                While an RV might not always be most appropriate, because of the range of prices and qualities a RV is always a possible option. Famous music stars and some of their tour buses or hotels they stay in are good examples of 4 or 5 resources characters and what the 'indefinite' level is while traveling. Without knowing the resource ratings of the examples you are asking about I can only guess but here are my thoughts (with a guessed permanent/temporary resource rating) on what they might do while traveling
                                • Circus member (1/None) Normal accommodations are either provided by the circus or they own an RV/Mobile Home. When traveling away from the circus, they arent making any money and would either be using their RV/Mobile Home or spending their small amount of extra cash on a cheap motel and wont be able to stay long maybe a week, up to a month at best and the character would then not have any 'extra' cash on hand for awhile after.
                                • Shadowlord Judge of Doom (3/1) Considering the political nature of Shadowlords I generally assume they have a higher average resource rating than most other tribes with the exception of maybe Silver Fangs and Glass Walkers. When the Judge is traveling (which is often) they would use whatever would be most appropriate for the area where their next target is; so if in the city the Judge would get a hotel room, if the target was in the wilds the Judge might rent a RV and 'camp' in the woods, if the Judge was invited to an area to hunt someone the group that invited them might provide lodging.
                                • Silent Strider (1/None) Depending on how they travel the Silent Strider might very well just shift into lupus form and just find a spot to sleep like any other canine would (areas protected by trees or rocks while in the woods, public garages or subways in the city).
                                • Eshu Messenger (2/1[six months]) Eshu are reknowned for their wanderlust you could make an argument they would be more likely to own an RV/Mobile Home as their normal accommodations and thus only use that six month period when they choose to go somewhere they can't drive to probably staying at a hotel when they do. Depending on their status as a messenger it's possible the Eshu could include payment for lodging as part of their fee for being a messenger (so if hired by a Sidhe they may end up staying in a 5 star hotel, while if they are hired by a redcap they might get enough to barely cover a cheap motel).
