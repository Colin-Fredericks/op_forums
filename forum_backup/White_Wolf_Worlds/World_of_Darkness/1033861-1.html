    Now, forgive me, but I think you're exaggerating things.

    The consensus shifting wasn't only the working of the OoR. They played a part in it, a notably big part yes, but they weren't the only reason.

    The events in Vampire history weren't the result of the existence of the Tremere, nor the born of the sects.

    The Tremere could go against the Tzimisce, because that was before the existence of big organizations and the Tzimisce were extremely scattered, they didn't wage war as one. On the other hand, The Tremere were highly organized, they were basically a militant group even before their vampirism.

    About the Technos dealing with Vampires: I'm pretty sure they put out the occasional silly one who's causing a lot of trouble, but Vampires, as I said are very good at regulate themselves. Their whole culture is based on hiding. Moreover the Union have other fish to fry. Oh, yes, they'd love to eradicate those bloodsuckers, on principle. They might even succeed. But they not without losing too much in the process and exposing their backs to the Traditions, Werewolves and other groups.

    And most importantly: the Vampire are just don't the focus of Mage. I'm a fan of crossover and sure, if you're going to do a crossover game and want to use both frequently, there'd be more skirmishes between the two and manipulation, espionage, etc. I'm on the firm opinion that it's doable perfectly. Maybe, with the WWP's plans of uniting the WoD into "One World" wo'd see more stories, modules, setting books and whatnot like that.


    If nothing worked, then let's think!

            Originally posted by PMárk View Post
            It depends on which version do you use and how much do you want to ST them as that. In recent books, like the revised era, M20 and the new convention books they aren1t so much like that. They like to picture themselves as such and sure, the Traditions are also buying into the omnipresent enemy thing, because that's easier to rally against. But according the books I read (and I admit I didn't read a lot of 1e-2e books yet) they really aren't that way. They have very limited resources, most importantly in the manpower side of things. They're sretching very thin, because there's a very limited number of people awakening in a given time and they have only a portion of them. Yes, they have fingers in a lot of pies and they have access to a lot of things, like government institutions and law enforcement but they can't survey everything constantly and you don't want to share inner things with the sleeper subordinates, or send said subordinates against supernatural threats. Yes, if you are making noise and bring attention on yourself and becoming a major problem, they could strike with a lot of concentrated force, but normally they can't monitor everything an be everywhere.

            Or look at it that way: if they were really omnipresent and omnipowerfull, they'd have eradicated the traditions and every other supernatural critters a long time ago. But they didn't, because they can't.

            Honestly, I don't agree with you conclusion that they make sense only as an omnipresent thing. Yes they're powerful, but they aren't the only ones. They don't clash with the Camarilla, or the Pentex in all-out war, because in reality, you go to war, if you have a reasonably good chance to win without too much loss knowing if you lose too much, a laughing third party would saunter in and swipe the floor with you. That, and Vampires tend to be minding their own business and stay out of the way. Until they don't and then, you'd need to bring out the bombs and sun-lasers.

            Being the most powerful group in any given game doesn't necessiate in my book that they must control everything. Yes, they likely the biggest dog in WoD, but don't think in absolutes, they're other not much smaller dogs. They could be menacing, extremely powerful and dangerous without invalidating every other group, IMO.
            The Union doesn't need to be Deus Ex Machinae on the WoD. Their propaganda is enough for their role as "The Big Brother" of the world. And I know they have their hands full, at any given moment. I haven't disregard that. But like Goebbels said "a lie told 1.000 times becomes a truth", the propaganda should suffice to convince the vampires to keep their masqerade, or any other group to follow similar protocol. The Traditions don't fight the Union because they are bold and think they have a chance, they do it because no one else will. The traditions believe that the final victory of the Union would be just slightly better than a victory of the Nafandi. If not for the Ascension War, the Traditions would do the same as the vampires: hide and survive. Which is what they do after the Avatar Storm and the realization that the war was over, and that they lost.

            Should all the denizens of the WoD unite, the Union would vanish. True enough. But that will never happen. So, why would the Camarilla start a war that it cannot win alone against them? To have a glorious death and give freedom back to the Sabbath, Garou and Traditions? No group would do that, so the smartest thing to do is to hide from their anger. And let others weaken them while being crushed.

            The war of the Traditions is the same of the Garou... Its a lost battle, but they must keep fighting, cuz no one else will.
