    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    Why the Syndicate?
    Two answers:

    1. It was a supremely marketable movie about the revolution being sold to the masses by the Machine as they do so love to do. Buying the merchandise of anti-capitalism is one of the Syndicate's best tricks.

    2. This is more my opinion but the Syndicate are actually the least threatened by mysticism among the Technocracy. The hard materialism of the Progenitors and Iteration X isn't really necessary in the soft science of economics, sociology, and credit management. Indeed, the idea of waking up to control your environment is something they're not afraid of the way, say, the New Order is because they fully believe Cipher's argument would persuade 90% of the Masses. Which is something that genuinely frightens the VA because many of them privately agree. Much like the sisters themselves, the VA fear that a large portion of the public might actually agree with the Union's argument and don't so much misunderstand the Matrix's message but disagree with the conclusion.

    They prefer the imaginary steak even if it costs them their freedom.

            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
            Two answers:
            I wouldn't say the first one is the same as saying they loved it, as that would be expected of any blockbuster, really. It is like releasing oxygen in the air and getting pissed that the Syndicate also breathes.

            For the second one, Matrix actually hurt Economic theory more than any "hard science". Sure, it makes no sense from the perspective of energy producing, but otherwise it seems pretty good from the view of other Conventions. Yet it shows a world where money and trade aren't just unnecessary, they're unnecessary twice, reduced to merely an insignificant fluff in the Matrix. Neither the Machines or Zion ever need an economic system. I think the Syndicate would find it pretty offensive.

            No Convention gives a lot of damns to scientific adherence in movies. They're super-scientists, not actual researchers, they believe in breaking the paradigms of the known universe all the time. They believe in giving the masses as stable reality, not in living themselves in one, they want as much wonder and exploring the impossible as any other Mage. And they know what's in a frigging movie. But their core ideas would be the actual think to worry about.

            The Iteration X can see in Matrix the power of the Tool. Even Zion survives through machines, and machines can become an ecosystem of their own. Sure, they may not like the machines revolting, but at the same time it is a tale of using tools to transcend the usual limits of humanity, about a man becoming a messiah through its artificial connection to the machine. Progenitors have a nice tale about bio-engineering, the power of the biological brain and the biological body as an efficient power plant. NWO can watch their own tropes turned into guardians of reality as people know it and devising it in a way that will convince people, create stability for society and make everything fine as long as those meddling Deviants stop interfering (or, after the Architect's explanation, even giving RDs a place for the betterment of the Masses).

            And the Void Engineers don't care as long as there are more things to explore. I disagree that the Syndicate would worries less about hard sciences than the Void Engineers. Like, no way.

                I'm currently working on my next campaign in which I want to adhere to a 'cassette futurism' theme.


                The background of the setting is inspired by one of the 'Ages of Darkness' posts on this forum, one where the Scanners movies were a real thing that the Technocracy brought about and covered up - because I want an emphasis on Psychic Powers instead of the usual computers and death rays

                However the game itself takes place in an alternative 90s based on the TV series UFO and Space 1999 because I want to examine the hubris of the technocracy in creating those extravagant horizon realms and what could go wrong (this is possibly an alternate cause of the Avatar Storm)

                To add a further twist the protagonists are agents from the future sent back to the nineties to try and prevent the various accidents (multiple sources - Terminator, Quantum Leap, Sapphire & Steel, Twelve Monkeys, Source Code, etc.)


                My only trouble, and this is where I could do with some help, is that I'm finding it difficult to link the 70s/80s psychics to the UFO/Space 1999 setting in a harmonious way. How does having psychic powers connect to the space race and aliens?

                - any suggestions would be welcome


                References

                Cassette Futurism: https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.p...ssetteFuturism

                Ages of Darkness 'Rust Never Sleeps': http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...92#post1382192

                    I might have some ideas. Before getting into those, I just want to make sure I have something clear in mind about the shows you are referencing. The storyline entry for UFO was a bit ambiguous as to whether it was set on Earth or the Moon. The opening sequence of the show has ships or rockets launched from underwater (presumably ocean), vehicles deploying into forest and some lunar stuff going on. Six and a half minutes intoEpisode 13:Survival, a ranking officer of SHADO instructs others to collect the remains of a "port" from "outside" and the next scene is on the Moon. At least by this point in the series, the action seems to be centered on a Lunar Base. The storyline entry for Space 1999 says that the Moon is propelled by an explosion through a black hole and that inhabitants of Moonbase Alpha have a space adventure, encountering alien civilizations as the Moon hurtles through space.

                    In your campaign, players travel backward in time to the nineties from a future timeline in which setting up extravagant (lunar?) horizon realms went badly. If you are questioning how to connect Psychic Powers, the Space Race and Aliens, I would first ask another question. What is the relationship between Psychic Powers and Time Travel? IIRC, the Scanners in the movies were all trying to win nosebleed contests, attacking each other with telepathic powers that weren't precognitive.

                    The people from the future timeline obviously Did Not Benefit from Precognitive Psychic Powers, since they sent the players back to correct an unanticipated set of problems. This would imply that the Scanner-type people did not develop precognitive abilities or did not share any information gleaned that way. This leads me to another idea. If there are both Telepaths and Time Travelers, can telepathic contact be established between the past and the future, even if the Scanners don't ordinarily possess any powers related to Time?

                    Here is how I would set things up. Establish the Scanners from the past and the Technocrats from the future (who sent the players on their mission) as having highly competing agendas. Each has something the other can benefit from, but there is an overarching conflict that must be resolved or won in a zero sum sense. Give the players limited information and perhaps a biased perspective they take from the quest-givers. Then, slowly parcel out information that would allow them to pick a side or make peace, the latter being the more difficult to achieve. Between the important NPCs on either side, I would create a vendetta. Someone is responsible for the death of the other's daughter or something like that.

                    A logical answer to the previous question could be: Some Humans developed Telepathy. Telepaths detected Aliens and alerted governments to their presence. Governments allied to get the Space Race going, in response to Alien Incursion.

                    Other details might be: Before being detected, the Aliens established alliances with humans, conscious of their presence who are willing to serve Alien Interests, either because the Aliens are good liars and convincingly claimed good intentions toward the human race or the human allies are people willing to put personal short term interests ahead of the good of humanity. Some Telepaths who are misunderstood and hunted are so pissed about the way they are treated that they willingly ally with Aliens against suspicious government agencies.

                    Add to that Technocratic Conventions and the different Traditions and you get a lot of options. I wouldn't try to cover every Convention or Tradition on first draft. Progenitors, New World Order, Sons of Ether and Akashic Brotherhood would be good ones to start with.                     Gnoma could be fun to have as major players, since they are both Spirit Summoners and Geomancers. Understanding metaphysical forces emanating from within the Earth could be important to defending the planet from Aliens.

                    Happy Writing.

                    Thank you for passing time with me in conversation. My Hacks.

                        Yeah UFO is a bit of an oddity if you've never seen it, it's also very very English despite being kind of intended for an American audience.

                        So the setting is that there's this secret organisation called SHADO (an acronym for Supreme Headquarters Alien Defence Organisation) which has it's headquarters hidden under a TV studio. They also have a secret submarine with a detachable fighter front end, various tanks and cars, as well as a small moonbase that houses space fighters. If, like me, you were a kid at the time you could get Dinky toys of some of these vehicles and they were the absolute best toys in the entire universe!

                        Space 1999 is a sort of follow on to UFO, set a few years later, where the aliens have been dealt with and there is a huge no longer secret base on the moon where they store all the radioactive waste from the earth, which, of course, blows up, propelling the moon off on it's adventures. Space 1999 is pretty much a horror series that terrified the pants off of me as a teenager (see this fascinating article:                         John Kenneth Muir's Reflections on Cult Movies and Classic TV: The Horror Mythology of Space:1999 (reflectionsonfilmandtelevision.blogspot.com))

                                        Originally posted by Dogstar View Post
                                        My only trouble, and this is where I could do with some help, is that I'm finding it difficult to link the 70s/80s psychics to the UFO/Space 1999 setting in a harmonious way. How does having psychic powers connect to the space race and aliens?
                                        One suggestion is to look for inspiration in the BBC series, Quatermass and the Pit. In it, aliens had abducted and modified ancient pre-humans in order to provide us with a mind similar to their own. This also implanted various psychic powers in us. The idea was that the Martians were dying, and were hoping that by modifying humanity's minds to be more like them, it would in essence be a continuation of their own race. Also see the book the Midwich Cuckoos (and its movie, Village of the Damned) which likewise has "human" children with developed psychic powers, most likely because of planted impregnation of women by an alien race.

                                        Or perhaps the psychic abilities are similar to the Remote Viewing and MKUltra experiments by the government in order to detect and protect Earth from aliens who have more developed esper abilities. Think of how various alien races (including humanity) had different levels of telepathic powers on Babylon 5. Perhaps psychic powers are an inadvertent result of contact from an alien mind to a human mind.
