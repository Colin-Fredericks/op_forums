                            Alice and the rest of the characters from Alice in Wonderland.
                            Alice is a newly awake changeling, probably on the border between childling (kid) and wilder (teenager). "Wonderland" is the fief of the Queen of Hearts, a sidhe monarch deep in Bedlam . . . and one whom no one has tried to depose yet. Her madness is ripping the fiefdom apart, to the point that no one realizes that Alice is new, untrained and without her Saining. So Alice just wanders through the local Dreaming, stopping at various freeholds, in search of a talking chimera in the shape of a white rabbit.


                            Pinhead and the Cenobites, as well the Lament Configuration
                            Pinhead and the Cenobites are kuei-jin akuma, in service to the Yama King known as Leviathan. Hellraiser III recounts how the man who became Pinhead sought damnation after World War I, and found it in east Asia. Tortured by Leviathan (and its minions), the now undead Pinhead buys moments of solace by helping damn others . . . although on some level, he still craves the pain himself. Oh, and the monstrous appearance that he (and the other cenobites) have is just a form of demon shintai

                            As for the magic puzzle box known as the Lamentation Configuration, its a mystic artifact, the result of an accidental collaboration between a technomage and a Nephandus (see Bloodlines), which permits direct access to Leviathan's Yomi (hell) realm.

                            Peter Pan and Captain Hook
                            Neverland is a realm in the Dreaming, reached by taking the trod found by following the second star to the right, and going straight on till morning. Two changelings contest control of the realm - a wilder sidhe from House Fiona known as Peter Pan, and an Unseelie sidhe grump (house unknown) known as Captain Hook. Both sidhe have enchanted mortal followers, while Peter Pan also has the support of a Never (chimerical pixie) known as Tinkerbelle.

                                    Originally posted by Crowley View Post
                                    ok this one is easy

                                    Explain the Five nights at freddys mythos in WoD
                                    I've been toying with this one for a while, and even have a potential scenario for it.

                                    The animatronics are just that - animatronics, albeit perhaps advanced ones. They may, in fact, have been designed by some technosorcerer with the Path of Enchantment.

                                    Fazbear Entertainment is of course a subsidiary of Pentex, but it's not a major branch by any means. In fact, its only "in the know" employee is a young exec who is just high enough in the Pentex hierarchy to know they serve the Wyrm, and some little bits about the supernatural. He noticed that, after those kids went missing, the animatronics have been acting up. You know, wandering where they aren't supposed to, staring at people, committing homicide on the night guards (and at least once taking a bite out of someone, leading to brain damage). Of course the suits are possessed by Wraiths of the children who hold a grudge against the Purple Man, but our Pentex minor exec doesn't know what a Wraith is. He just assumes the suits are possessed by some of those "Bane" things he sometimes hears about. And since the Wyrm apparently loves Banes and uses them to spread misery, he just goes along with the situation, quietly disposing of dead bodies, insisting the animatronics be allowed to roam the halls at night, and even actively making it harder for the guards to avoid getting killed. Sure, it means high turnover, but that's a given in the food service industry, and if it makes the Wyrm happy then it's all good.

                                    The game scenario, then, would be that the players investigating the rash of disappearances. Perhaps one of the party members' friends disappears, and that player character applies for the job to get to the bottom of it. A Mage game might make it too easy - if anyone has Spirit 2 it's almost insultingly so. But it might make for a good mystery, with the Child Wraiths asking the Mages to find who killed them and bring him to justice...preferably their justice.

                                    Magestuck: The Ascension
