        Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
        Indians in reality are far too devided, linguistically or culturally.
        Not too divided to agree on Sanskrit as their classical language.

        But really, this is just another red herring. The Tradition isn’t the republic of India. It’s a group whose number measure in the hundreds, not the millions. Nor are its members going to be practitioners of normal Indian religions. The Tradition basically is a cult in itself.

        Also, Indian/Sanskrit terms and ideas have been hugely popular in occult circles for well over a century. I’d go so far a to say they’re more attractive to many people than Greek ones. You keep positing impediments that don’t actually exist.

        Also, sometimes people just shift their terminology over time. It didn’t take long for most people to start saying Mumbai instead of Bombay, or Myanmar instead of Burma, or Beijing instead of Peking. That didn’t require an act of Congress. No vote was needed. Once they start to perceive that a different name is catching on, most people will go with it, if only not to be left behind.

        There’s also the fact that people who are too rigid in their thinking aren’t likely to Awaken in the first place. That requires a certain modicum of conceptual plasticity. And this actually becomes more true the more advanced they become, not less.

                                                    I don't think we're in disagreement per se. I think every single one of the Traditions operates as an umbrella in some capacity. The individual tradition books talk about different factions under each umbrella that use different approaches.

                                                    But if we're working with the standard mage setting, then our characters use magic according to their beliefs. The advantage to the Traditions in that respect is that we have centuries of practices that are reinforced by the individual group in question. Obviously an individual would provide his own spin on his practice, but there's a lot of psychological reinforcement involved with using old grimoires or artifacts.

                                                    Also, the Verbena aren't NEO-pagans. They're meant to be the real deal. The original witch archetype. The Old Faith folks who bring the practices of their ancestors in the present day. And at least as the Mage setting establishes it, those old rites were a lot more bloody and earthy than modern Neo-Pagan rituals. Et cetera. Neo-Pagans are included under the Verbena umbrella, but a lot of the older practitioners (some of whom may well have been alive when these original practices were new) look at them a bit askance.

                                                    Obviously you can play a neo-pagan orphan or Hollow One, and truth be told, that probably would be a more comfortable fit for many neo-pagans than the Verbena are. But I don't really see the appeal to that. If I'm going to play an old school witch, I'd rather go all out and embrace the Tradition, warts and all. Similarly, I don't see why I would bother making an orphan martial artist (though they're definitely possible too), when I have the entire history and complexity of the Akashayana to play with.

                                                        Originally posted by kalinara View Post
                                                        But Mage isn't like Vampire. Vampire can focus on the Anarchs, because clan is different from sect. You can be a Ventrue or a Toreador but not be in the Camarilla.
                                                        This is an analogy that I can't really comment on, as my familiarity with Vampire is almost null. I mention this only in case you have the mistaken idea that I'm trying to make Mage more like Vampire.

                                                        Originally posted by kalinara View Post
                                                        Mage doesn't really work that way. The Traditions aren't just political bodies, they're also the foundation of a Mage's entire practice. Not everyone is interested in playing a self-taught Orphan with a jury-rigged practice.
                                                        Actually, the whole point of M20's new Focus system is that the Traditions aren't the foundation of a mage's entire practice. That is, unlike earlier editions where “mad scientist” and “Son of Ether” were pretty much considered synonyms, where if you wanted to play the former you had to look up the Theory and Practice sections in the write-up of the latter, M20's revision to the whole Focus and Paradigm thing made it possible to design someone with the Practice of Weird Science without ever once going anywhere near the Sons of Ether write-up.

                                                        And the whole point of my post is that M20's Orphans aren't about “jury-rigged practice” anymore. That's handled by the Practice of Gutter Magic now; and while it's more common among the Orphans than it is among other groups, it's no longer presented as the default for them.

                                                        In fact, in an M5 as I envision it, the term “Orphan” wouldn't appear in the core book. The first time you'd see it would be in the supplement that fleshes out the Traditions, and it would be made clear that it's a derogatory term used by the Traditions to stereotype the independent mages described in the core book as a bunch of know-nothings playing around with powers they couldn't possibly understand because they don't have the benefit of the Traditions' patronage.

                                                        Put another way, “independent mages are Orphans, who use jury-rigged practices” would be presented as Traditionist arrogance, not fact. The fact would be that most independent mages would not be what Traditionists call “Orphans”, and that the Traditions aren't nearly as important as they think they are; and in terms of the practice of magic, the Traditions don't bring nearly as much to the table as they think they do.

                                                        And lest you misunderstand, there is one thing of great value that the Traditions absolutely do bring to the table: they're the “ecumenicals” of the setting, the mages who have raised the commingling of different practices to an art form through the pioneering of the Esperanto of the Practices. The Verbena aren't better at being witches than the independent witches are, nor are the Choristers better at being devotees than independent devotees are; but Choristers and Verbena are better at working together than independent devotees and witches are. And that would be touched on in the core book in the section that sketches out the Traditions as a group.
