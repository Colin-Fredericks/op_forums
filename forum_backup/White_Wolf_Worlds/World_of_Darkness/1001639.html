    Why is there no Cainite kung fu?

    11-11-2016, 02:03 AM


    So the stargazer tribe of werewolves (and presumably any other changer who goes through the trouble to bother) get Kalindo, an art of using transformations between larger and smaller forms to assist in movement and fighting.
    Mages of the Akashic brotherhood, the Wu Lung, and other traditions have their own fighting techniques that employ magickal effects (and, since we've all seen one too many kung fu films, aren't that vulgar). With mages, the five point palm exploding heart technique is real.
    Kuei Jin use martial arts. They use 'chi' for their powers, so why not?

    And, well, mortals and less powerful splats like changelings could probably learn a few things too. But the point i want to make is:

    Cainites have significantly more strength, speed,stamina and solidness than they should do. Many can generate more strength in an ankle movement than I can with my full leg. If I were to mount one in a submission hold, they could get up on the sheer principle that they can exert themselves without needing to worry about exhaustion.

    Now, Other than a move in a book where with 2 potence or feral claws you could tear a vital chunk out of another cainite to deny blood flow, there's nothing. Which is crazy, because when you pack the power of Hafþór Júlíus Björnsson into Steve Buscemi, you should expect more interesting results. Your body doesn't change past embrace.

    Now, I know, I know. White wolf have had a problem with racists. Just look at the stats of a Katana in comparison to a good, wholesome European sword. But in seriousness: "Martial Arts" are two words in the english language and Non-Asians have been playing around with fighting arts since at least alcohol and marriage. Be it Greek, Roman, Viking,Turkish wrestling or queensberry rules boxing. From lost American arts to practices hidden during the slave trade, schooled unarmed fighting has existed on every continent. Even Medieval and renaissance Knights had systems to get pesky armoured opponents down for a stab (It wasn't jujitsu) . I can't understate how the idea of advanced unarmed fighting techniques being an exclusively Asian thing is wrong (though I stress that there's nothing wrong with Asian martial arts). And while there's the occasional nice thing in the world of darkness, like a nod in the fianna book about traditional wrestling, the cainites having not developed several different martial arts using disciplines or even basic cainite physiology is just wrong. They're on every continent bar maybe the ones without food. Exposed to every major culture, and they have a variety of clan disciplines that ensure that most'll have their own trick or at least sensibilities.
    And now, whilst one could assume elders are strong enough to not need learning to fight proper (they could learn to get up instantly with enhanced strength and speed, or learn how to use a sword without breaking it) You've also got to consider a great factor of cainite learning: Cainites get really bored, early nights weren't filled with things to do, even politicking was limited. They could also practice far more techniques than mortals could without worry for permanent injury and less worry for death. (yep. Frenzy is a factor. But even Brujah could take steps to reduce it's chance to an acceptable risk) The possibilities are significant, and I could probably write a section of a book for them. But yet, we've had very little (point me to the books if I'm mistaken) Not all vampire games are combat focused, and the system sucks for slowness if you've got a few players, but Martial arts could make it more interesting (and potentially more lethal, meaning it's faster.) Vampires have the most potential of any splat for crazy fighting moves, even if they're a grim bunch. Their capabilities allow for more versatility than even Kalindo with comparable gifts (though likely avoiding the territory of a do practitioner, since that's largely for delivering effects)

    This isn't just about fighting. Cainites would just move differently in general if they were trying to exert themselves. They could easily use their mismatched power and powers in dancing or acrobatics.

          Myself and my Dark Ages group built up our players' fighting styles based on their country and time of origin, who taught them and how they could adapt that to vampiric powers. Once you get to 5+ brawl rating especially these forms start to disappear though, being replaced by complete flexibility and versatility, beyond what any rote method or single school (or even handful of schools) could ever impart. Thought, experience, skill take you to a level where minimum possible force and movement to succeed starts to become preternatural and alarming.

          It's also worth noting that, yes, despite conventional wisdom sophisticated martial arts weren't historically exclusive to Sino-Japan and the katana was far, far from the be all and end all of blades. I actually researched a wealth of ancient martial traditions, forging methods and materials etc. for another RPG I'm building and the depth and range of schools, weapon loadouts and styles is immense. With that said I don't put much of that in to V:TM simply because I don't want ST combat to get any slower than it already is. It makes for nice flavour in describing attacks etc. and helps guide choice of specialisations but I don't think the system can stand it going much deeper unless your players are very patient.

                  Because, with all the book space wasted on blood magic, wonky bloodlines and their powers, we really don't need more space wasted on wargaming mechanics.

                  Making Kuei-Jin into kung-fu "take a left through my sword and keep on going" was one of the tragic results of the botched attempt to make vampires more involved with the rest of the splats.

                  I like the idea, but 2nd ed Sabbat should have been enough for people to realize that wargaming with VtM was a terrible combination.
                  They went a long way to fix the past mistakes (except for Kuei-Jin, which are still a problem in crossovers) and made more interesting books (except for the blood magic ones), and DtF had none of that trench coat and katanas shenanigans.

                  VtM is a game of survival, intrigue and personal horror with no clear way to achieve any sort of victory, and it should stay that way. I'm not against martial art mechanics, but every effort put into it encourages players to make messes. And every pages wasted on combat mechanics comes at the cost of more interesting and worthwhile material.
