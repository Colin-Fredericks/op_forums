        Am I the only one that wished M20 had a metaplot update? The Metaplot asides gave options but didn't include much detail on "hey, this is what a world where the avatar storm died down would look like" or "hey, here are some masters that could return to the other side of the gauntlet". I tend to get plots from picking elements from the metaplot and using those to create large arcs.
        Contrary to how the forums tend to go, regarding metaplot, I agree with this OP. I liked M20's "New Millenium" metaplot direction and I'd like to see more of it.

        Generally, I get why a certain demographics of people tend to not like metaplot and why those tend to be the most active people on forums and such. However, I do like metaplot and tend to gravitate toward settings whichhave some measure of it. I like to follow the story from book-to-book, I like having characters to relate to. I like to see how the setting looks from the viewpoint of those characters "on the ground".

        Yes, sometimes, i hate when the metaplot goes in a direction I'm against (see V5), but all-in-all, I'd rather have metaplot, than not.

        So yeah, I do wish M20 having a metaplot update. The basis was good, I'd like to see it expanding.

        If nothing worked, then let's think!

                                        I'll have to disagree on both counts, sort of.

                                        First, the Technocracy definitely believes that the Masses influence Reality; but they also acknowledge that there's an underlying Truth that you can either work with our work against (the so-called Earthly Foundations). And they pride themselves with working with it: they see Enlightened Science as an extension of the natural order — what Reality would be like without mage interference. Their claim is that this is why they've been so successful in getting their ideas accepted by the Masses; and it's this notion that they're building on a foundation of absolute truths which they use to justify what they're doing as Science.

                                        And no, the Traditions are not the same hidebound jerks they were in the middle ages; they're post-modern progressive jerks. This is what distinguishes them from the Disparates, who by and large are still largely stuck in the past, attempting to hold onto their glory days. At the heart of the Traditions is a shared metaphysics that's flexible enough to be consistent with a broad range of magical practices — what earlier editions called the Metaphysics of Magick, and what M20 calls the “We Are All Gods In Disguise” Paradigm. It gives them a shared mindset that facilitates cooperation and an overall goal that unites them — thus, “post-modern and progressive”. But make no mistake: they're still arrogant jerks who see mages as being better than Sleepers; and their overall plan to help the Sleepers generally boils down to some form of “help all of them to Awaken”. Indeed, there are even some who are concerned that efforts to make life more bearable for Sleepers is a Bad Thing, because it discourages them from Awakening.

                                                For what it's worth, I have a rather low opinion of pre-M20 lore where the Technocracy is concerned; all too often, it boils down to “the higher-ups in the Union are liars, and the rest of them are dupes”. In the decade between the end of the classical World of Darkness and its rebirth with the Revised Convention Books and then M20, I worked up a head-canon to explain how the Union's perspective could be handled in a way that allows them to actually make valid points, and not just be liars and dupes. It just so happens that the bulk of the M20 lore pertaining to the Union is fully consistent with said head-canon. (I'm not fond of the Technephandi stuff, as I prefer the Union's flaws to be their own rather than the result of infiltration; but other than that, I prefer it to the earlier editions' fundamentally flawed approach.)

                                                As such, and since this is a thread about M5 Metaplot (that is, “what happens next?”), I'm inclined to take the M20 lore as a starting point and go from there.

                                                And for the record, the Union has always, in all editions, held that their Science is based on fundamental truths about the nature of Reality. In the First and Second Editions, this flew in the face of the argument that Reality is Subjective, which was the biggest cause of the aforementioned “they're either liars or dupes” issue. Revised introduced such notions as Cosmological Constants and Historical Inertia, which are what I originally based my head-canon for a less stupid Union on, in that they're examples of how Reality isn't exclusively a product of the Consensus. What M20 brings to the picture are the Reality Zones, and a mention when discussing the Technocratic Zones that the Union believes that their Zones are closer to the “base reality” than other Zones are. It's a shift from the earliest Edition's portrayal of Technocrats as “Magick isn't real, and I'll shoot you if you say or do otherwise” to a more nuanced view where the Union views Enlightened Science as the best option possible, to the point where they're willing to push it on everyone “for their own good”.

                                                    A bit torn on the question of metaplot. On one hand i like the extra flavor and feeling that there is areal living world going on around you that you get from metaplot. And i like much of the direction of the metaplot in M20. But i also find it can get very complicated and honestly rather pretentious at times. But after years of running Awakening precisely because I was tired of the metaplot I realize i kind of miss at least some of the deeper plot and background from Ascension. Oy, the dllemma! In particular i find that metaplot tends to engage one the things I like least in rpg players which is the tendency to take metaplot and background in the game literature as absolute and unchangeable. Truth is i have never played Mage (either version) as written straight out of the box. So the more metaplot, the more I fear players will be annoying about whether I include it or not. That ultimately is why I liked the M20 approach of providing metaplot in a modular way. If you want to take the metaplot,great. if not, also great!
