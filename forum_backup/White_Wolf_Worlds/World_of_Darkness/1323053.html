              Sorry to revive this even if it is on the first page, but I was waiting for approval and I still wanted to share my thoughts.

              Anyhow, the original prohibition against Revenant-Mages and Ghoul Mages from Blood Treachery was basically: "It's your game if you want, but this is a bad idea for REASONS." It was a warning against permitting an easy path to powergaming and other rule abuse as opposed to interesting character and story development. And there are good reasons to heed this caution. On the other hand, a powergamer will potentially disrupt things by definition, independent of any one particular 'path'. A powergamey human mage is not much more of a problem than a Revenant-Mage, since the problem is the powergaming and the person doing it.

              By contrast, someone who puts in the effort and imagination to construct an interesting, balanced character concept shouldn't be an issue (If you're goal is a balanced, interesting character, then you'll put as much effort into the downsides as well as the upsides.) Someone who isn't interested in balancing the obvious imbalance or in creating an interesting rationale for the character doesn't deserve it. If they're willing to work with the Storyteller and other characters to make it work, why should it be forbidden?

              Since the primary challenge is 'balancing' such a potentially unbalanced role the rest of this post are the ideas I've had for balance. First off, many of the limits for Ghoul Mages in 'Blood Treachery' are equally applicable to the Revenant including:

              - Blood addiction of the Avatar: In a sense this would be a weaker version, but it would be more persistent (You can't get the Revenant 'off' it, but it might take longer.) Rather than the Mage being able to survive their Avatar ratings in feedings, you might figure 1-5 years per dot in Avatar, depending on how generous you want to be. Alternately, you can raise Arete OR gain one new sphere for every point in the Avatar rating (but not both). Or the ST can simply decree the Revenant starts out addicted which would more effectively limit the potential of the mage character.

              The stronger Avatar still is the same risk for the Revenant as Ghouling, and may 'force' the Mage to seek out vampire blood.

              - The Revenant can never restore quintessence by any means except Vampire blood. They regain it the same way a Revanant might regain blood points, but that's it.

              Once the 'grace period' mentioned above ends (However its handled), the mage cannot raise their Arete at all (or automatically if your ST wants to go this route) or gain spheres.

              - The addicted Avatar and its Revenant are a beacon for 'evil' forces (Nephandi, demons and other corrupt entities, etc.) to try and address its cravings. If you consider (as below) that the Revenant-Mage is a target from both the Vampiric AND Magical societies, this isn't a trivial concern.

              - The Revanant's blood poisons the Avatar and it will eventually destroy itself. It can survive Avatar + Arete ratings in years, x4 for the Revenant's reduced aging. after this the Revenant inevitably loses 1 point from Avatar rating until its zero, then Arete until the revenant is gilguled. Given that even with the 'extended' span of time the Revenant would still live less than a century (and the last third of that in greatly declining power) - and probably much less given a lower Arete/Avatar score and limited ability to raise it (Small wonder Blood Treachery notes a Revenant-Mage might live to see 40 before gilgul!)

              - You still have to take the 7 point 'Revenant' Merit, plus whatever additional flaws the Storyteller may inflict on you to reflect the unholy union this represents. Dark Fate and nightmares were mentioned, but others like Echoes seem like they would also be appropriate (Flaws from VtM would also be appropriate)

              In addition to many of the limits a Ghoul mage operates under, you could add other. We know from certain merits (Shattered Avatar, Twin Soul) that Avatars could be broken up. We know from the Tremere that being Embraced will destroy your Avatar. Maybe 'half dead' states like a Revnant aren't enough to kill it, but fragment it (Avatar starts out weaker deliberately - say a max of Avatar 1 at the start.) And you'd still have to pay for the merit of course. Buying Twin Souls may be utilized in some way to 'stabilize' or slow down the Revenant's decline whilst at the same time bringing other complications with it (at the Storyteller's discretion.) The interesting thing about this approach to 'limits' is that it works more to create narrative reasons for that power which may or may not be achieved, as well as the built in drawbacks/dangers the hybrid faces (the progressive disintegration of their Avatar and magickal talents for one...)

              We also know from the Tremere example that you can apply 'True' magick principles to more static/linear type magic (which could include Vampiric Thaumaturgy) so an 'awakened' Revenant may have to do the same (reflecting some extremely time consuming, and costly, learning to develop any real power.) This could reflect as an inherent, across the board 'sphere-inept' for example, but it could also mean they can't easily be trained by any of the formal 'traditions' without extra time and effort put in to 'translate' those methods so they are more appropriate to the quasi-vampiric nature of a Revenant (Hermetics who have Tremere connections might be the easiest.) Of course, finding someone willing to do this (or not destroy the abomination) is a challenge in and of itself. Considering the time limit a Revenant-mage already operates under before Gilgul, this isn't a trivial concern.

              Lastly, Blood Treachery's cautionary tale that Awakend Revenants should be so utterly rare as to be impossible, which hints at them being extremely 'Vulgar' and thus more vulnerable to Paradox (and this may extend over into Vampiric disciplines as well, reflecting increased difficulties as the two 'sides' of the Revenant's nature war with each other.) They may operate under more obvious limitations like Conditional magic, crucial component, Sleepwalker, etc. This could also tie into the 'conflict' between Vampiric and Awakened natures. Or one could just decree a Revenant-Mage's workings automatically qualify as Vulgar and/or just institute a permanent, minimum Paradox rating that the Revenant must operate under.

              Those are just mechanical limits too. Narrative limits (which would compound WITH the mechanical limits translating in-character) can be even bigger. Such a character is likely to be seen as a threat, abomination, or both in both the Vampiric and Mage societies. We know from VTM that the actions of one Revenant could doom an entire family (making them a pariah not only from other Vampire clans, but their own family). Dracula's actions caused the purge of the Basarab Revenant family in VTM. Mages are likely to see them as an affront (like the Tremere would be), or something likely to be a Marauder or Nephandi (making it harder to get access to the training you need, finding allies, etc.) And let's not forget most Revenants are socially backwards, inbred, and largely inhuman creatures to begin with and their experiences in the outside world could be quite limited.

              Any of those limits could apply by themselves, or in combination, as necessary to 'balance' the obvious advantages of a hypothetical Revenant-Mage.

              If any of that is too complicated/potential for abuse, there are still yet other ways. Sorcerer (Revised) had an interesting merit called 'Struggling awake' where a sorcerer or hedge magician might - occasionally - pull of True Magick. That could apply to our hypothetical Revenant too without being a full-on mage and would incorporate much of the same rationale above without the complexity (have the 'struggling Awake' merit apply to a Revenant-Mage learning Vampiric Thaumaturgy rather than Hedge Magic Maybe they achieve one discipline/path instinctively, but they need to learn/research/adapt others to make it work.)

              One could further surmise that a Revenant-Mage could achieve more than one dot in Thaumaturgy (with a willing teacher and the required experience) due to their awakened nature. If you're really generous, you could assume that applies to any possible discipline, but that might be going too far (especially not without appropriate drawbacks to balance it out. 'Stronger Revenants or Dhampir' was already a challenge. One possible source for limits to counter would be the KOTE Dhampir, who can gain one extra dot per century of existence, but suffer lossts of health levels and attributes due to aging.) On the other hand, Struggling Awake + built in one-dot limits for a Revenant also work just fine as a way to curb powergaming at the Storyteller's discretion. The only requirement is HAVING some kind of Thaumaturgy to work off of (and in some ways this is MORE of a limit than a mortal sorcerer with the same merit would face!)

              I don't think these are 'perfect' solutions (there are probably things I've missed or am overlooking) and may not work for everyone (especially given how complicated some of the ideas can be!), but I present them more as a starting point people can work/adapt from to suit their purposes.

                                              Yeah the treatment of Ghoul/Revenant Mages has always been rather odd, considering the cost of Ghoul Merit itself and the fact that you cannuse low cost Merits to build a ”better” Ghoul Mage with all the same advantages and non of the downsides.

                                              The greatest advantages Ghoul Mages get?
                                              1) Slow (if Revenant) aging and fully stopped aging for salongin as you have a supply of Blood.

                                              Can be beaten by a 1 point Merit with no downsides.

                                              2) Access to Disciplines. Mostly 1st level only, physical only unless aside from Family Disciplines (Revenants) or Domitor Disciplines and higher only if Domitor is low generation. Aside from Blood bond as a disadvantage these Disciplines cost 20xp for the 1st level and 15xcurrent for the rest if allowed.

                                              Many 1st level Discipline effects can be either replicated by Coincidental Magick or low cost Merits. Non of the downsides.

                                              3) Finally one big advantage is that Magick that looks like traditional Vampire tricks is considered coincidental. This can be quite a big deal considering things associated with Vampires as it will allow some mind effects and even some transformation effects Etc.

                                              This is the major benefit that cannot really be replicated easily with other Merits but is it still worth the limitations to your Arete, Blood Bond and other disadvantages?

                                              So no Ghoul/Revenant Mages aren’t, atleast in my personal opinion, overpowered even without the limit to Arete. Blood Bond and the general Vampire connection is likely quite enough of a disadvantage to Balance it out. But if you wish to use the official Ghoul/Revenant rules the Black Hand book is the way to go.
