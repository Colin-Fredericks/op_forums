    I've been looking more into the book

    The Creeds are very crappy splats with no identity. There is Enterpreneurial, Faithful, Inquisitive, Martial and Underground. They are essentially methodologies you use to hunt and your backstory that use old HtR Creed names as sobriquets. Their only mechanical hook is what situation they get their desperation dice in - i.e. religious creed gets it against the supernatural, militant in combat, investigative in data gathering.

    Now what are the Desperation dice? The game has two public counters - Desperation and Danger. Danger represents how much you have pissed off the authorities and/or your quarry and results in higher difficulties and more opposition. Desperation represents how bad things are going for the hunters. The worse things are for the hunters the more Desperation there is. Now Desperation is ranked from 1-5 and any Hunters (again they insist on the capitalisation as opposed to the ordinary hunters) with a Drive and a Creed can choose to use the desperation dice in any field where their creed specialty applies.

    Desperation dice can never be rerolled, each 1 causes horrible consequences and/or raises Danger. 10's give a potential critical win, which seems to only be an extra margin of success. I got no idea what &quot;potential&quot; means here, I assume they mean you need double 0's? But why call it out specifically for desperation dice?

    Powers! (Edges)
    • Assets are Arsenal, Fleet, Library, Ordnance
    • Aptitudes are tricks your character has - Beast Whisperer (petmaker), Drone Jockey, Jury Rigging and Global Access (Hack the Planet)
    • Endowments are the actual powers - Sense the unnatural, Thwart the Unnatural (ignore powers) and Repel the Unnatural (use cross to drive of vampires) and Artifact (1 bonus die to certain skill)

    In general, the whole section is very sparse. For some reason explosives are separate from &quot;Arsenal&quot; as &quot;Ordnance&quot;. Edges can be customized with perks that improve their effects in certain contexts. All of them require activation rolls and most of them have Difficulty 4 to even activate properly. Some of them are downright going to cause issues in the future, I'd like to especially single out Global Access - &quot; ...the Hunter can gain access to any kind of data on any subject or person, in any conceivable system&quot; as something that's gonna be hilarious to interpret once Magic, Spirits and Hypertech exist in this setting. Drone Jockey seems very easy to abuse if you load up on perks and get multiple users (simply due to how action economy works in WoD).

    I still don't get why &quot;Drive&quot; is so special for the Hunters and why it makes them different from other humans beyond marking them as protagonists. For something they spend A LOT of wordcount on to make special, it doesn't really seem so in everything I've seen.
    That sounds so bland. I am still interested in Desperation and Danger, and how we might use those to fix my problems with Hunger, but the other stuff sounds so generic. And I wish they'd stop making everything Difficulty 4 (i.e., 8 dice needed!). Difficulty 3 is probably too high as it is.

            Just so that it doesn't sound it's all horrible forever, I'd like to share some thoughts on the design. It's Black, Grey and Orange on White, and it looks really striking, most of the art is actually drawn and not photography and its highly evocative, to the point that it feels like a better take on what CoD looks like. It's clear they had a decent layout person for this with their head straight on, and the book looks really nice, I think that on paper it might really be a stunner.

            Also it offers us glories as this absolute madlad who is someone I never knew I wanted to play before, illustration for "Physical Specimen" archetype:



            Now beyond the visual I have some new thoughts about the core conceit of the game. I'm not sure the authors were on the same page or were actually coordinating on what they were making here. The framework for playing a group of low powered monster hunters driven together by necessity and sticking together through brotherhood is there, and the game should be able to work.

            However, the way that Drive is written and presented is utterly inconsistent and painfully artificial. On one hand it's something that differentiates hunters and Hunters, but is defined as a need and drive of humans to fight the horrors in the night. It's clearly something special, but wherever they can they downplay it. This comes to the fore quite well with the Factions (I am not going to use the term "Orgs" as its dumb) where it insists that the hunters of the factions are neither Hunters, nor Driven. Which for some factions and characters makes sense, but a lot of the archetypes those factions represent are about people willing to do whatever needs to be done to deal with the dark. Quite a few of their front line people and probably even the leadership has to have true believers to survive and keep things going in the World of Darkness. Yet they are not Driven, they never become Driven and they are not true Hunters.

            Add to this the fact that the game itself cannot really decide what this Drive is, one one hand it's presented as getting things done that need doing while fighting monsters, on the other it fully supports characters that just want to study monsters or even become monsters! It's not helped by the fact that there is 2.5 pages of text about drives, and none of it actually explains what they are beyond a shorthand for "These are the protagonists". Worse yet, it actively tells us that Drive and Creed are NOT in game terms, they are distinctly out of game terms to help us out, but nowhere does it actually bother to tell us as the ST's and the players what they are and couches the terms in fluffy language and presents them as is.

            Worse yet, it implies that Drives are permanent, and that once your eyes are open there is no going back, yet it doesn't address this. Heck, it even rather inconsistently claims that you can finish your Hunt, and by finishing your Hunt you can go back to your life as it was once before. Alas, the game doesn't really care about the idea that you're playing a zealot willing to do anything to hunt down the monsters, you do get touchstones, but there is no impact of your Drive on your interpersonal relationships beyond what your ST devises for you.

            You can temporarily lose your Drive, when you roll despair on your dice. So what happens then? Nothing. You lose your special dice until you perform an act of atonement, and honestly practically all of these acts of atonement are things that your character would do anyway if they had that drive! They don't even insist you need to act irrationally towards that goal. No you just need to do something that's pretty much in character for your Drive. The book example itself for Curiosity is "Get New Information".

            It even goes so far to say that people who pick up arms to fight the supernatural for a moment (the average horror movie protagonist) are not Driven and never will be as long as they didn't turn into a full time monster hunter as a consequence of that encounter. If Drive is something everyone can have, would it not make more sense that it can come and go as narrative demands it?

            In essence, I love the idea of Desperation Dice for these character, but I hate the whole way the Drives are set up around it. I honestly cannot believe that 20 people are listed as authors, editors and consultants and not a single one of them noticed how disjointed this whole concept is!

                                OK, so, part of this is that he's not a rabbi, because he's saying that with his clothing.

                                Ultra-orthodox Jews have a very strict dress code, but like all people, there's a desire to dress in a fashion the help express things about themselves. As such, very small details have huge meanings; making them very hard to understand to outsides but bright as day to people that "speak" the coded messages.

                                So, to break down what the man in that picture is saying with how he dresses (to get this out ahead of time, he's coded as an Ashkenazic Jew, some of these things have entirely different meanings for ultra-orthodox Jews of other traditions) :

                                1) His hat is saying that "God loves Queers and Hates straights," and he wants to break into religious services just wearing that hat, carrying a pride flag, and scandalizing everyone with his nudity. The style of hat indicates he has strong reform minded sympathies (the shape of the hat, the length of the brim, and how this relates to the length of his hair are a complicated thing to decode precisely), and he has color on it. Color is a huge no-no here. Ultra-orthodox men are not allow to have any colors besides black or natural (leather/fur) on the external surfaces of their clothing. The lining of the hat could have color, but a band of color around the outside? That is super iconoclastic, rebellious, and so on. The only way that could be worse among that community is if it was red (which would be declaring himself a sex worker). To try to put this in perspective, a band of dark grey cloth that might be black if the lighting was different would result in months of gossip about a man.

                                2) He's wearing a theologian/academic's glasses. This is a statement that he's done at least the equivalent of a university level education in private Jewish schools with the religious training on top of any secular studies he's done (again, there's some implications based on his hair since it denotes a middle-aged man, not a current student), and has made a commitment to on going religious study for life even if that coincides with other things like a job and a family.

                                3) He has a well trimmed but long beard, high quality watch, and a nice vest. This indicates he is neither a practicing rabbi or a laborer. These are signs of someone that's a professional in a business or high-skilled low manual labor area. He is putting in the effort to balance signifying his success, wealth, and secular status, without bragging or boasting. That particular cut of beard is also favored in some groups because it can be tucked into a fully buttoned collar to stand out less when meeting with people of other groups; again indicating a businessman of some sort. A person that does manual labor (even things like cooking or delicate work) would trim to a shorter length so their beard doesn't get in things, and a rabbi would be pressured into a less "stylish" and more natural beard precisely to show he is not concerned with matters where adapting his presentation to be less "Jewish" would be important.

                                4) His talit katan (the fringed undershirt) is a very weird choice. That style is generally one that you'd only wear on the most important days of your life. It's like wearing a high end designer tuxedo to the gym, or even a priceless family heirloom. But it's also ripped, while his chest is exposed (huge no-no there as well), and we can see scars that would have ripped the garment (traditionally a talit katan goes all the way up to at least your collar bone). This is a sign of severe mental trauma and unresolved grief. This looks like he was attacked by a monster on his wedding day or something similar, and instead of seeking help and undergoing the traditional Jewish mourning rituals is still wearing the signs of his trauma out in the open for everyone to see. Despite the fact that the scars are well healed now, he's still wearing a ripped shirt (a sign of mourning). So he's basically begging people to have him committed because he's trying to commit suicide by monster hunting.

                                5) The pants and boots are completely unacceptable dress unless required by active military duty. From the waist down, he's a soldier in the middle of a war, while walking around with a sledgehammer. Given he's not wearing complimentary clothing above his waist, this is a very conflicted message. If he was in a literal war-zone, he'd be wearing other things elsewhere. In the context of Hunter, you can only really see this as a declaration that the world is a warzone and he's looking for a fight. A military background could explain his physicality though.

                                So... yeah. That's a lot of stuff that is a really mixed bag.

                                However, don't feel bad because I think, esp. if you put some character thought into #4 and #5 there, there could be a superb story here. You could do a lot with how the Drive has caused him to lose touch with his religious community, pushing his Touchstones away with a toxic mix of grief, combat training, and severe psychological damage, and only these little cues to express to people how much trouble he's really in failing because to most people that can read them he looks like a psycho ready to murder anyone that gets close to him.

                                It's just... disappointing to hear the book just tossed in "lets make a physical Jew" that checks off a bunch of "token" boxes while trying to be subversive by making him physically intimidating and powerful instead of nebbish; only to fail because they didn't give people the context needed to understand it.

                                                            Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

                                                            Asmodai called him a madlad, not a madman, which is a slang term for someone that tends to do physically dangerous things out of excitement or humor; though usually associated with men acting immature rather than "normal" youthful roughhousing.

                                                            Which, at least IME, is actually fairly common in the ultra-orthodox Jewish communities. For young men, they have grown up in a highly regimented culture, were physical activity is one of the few outlets of both personal independence and pent up emotional stress. One of my school friends probably had ADHD (wasn't diagnosed), and soccer was pretty much how he coped to get out daily emotional issues. Looking back on it as an adult, it's kinda of painful to know we didn't really recognize those pleas to get one more point in before lunch was over - even though we clearly didn't have time for more - was because he needed it to help with a day that was just going shitty.

                                                            There's a very "boys will be boys" attitude, and that they'll grow out of it, but plenty take way too long to (if they ever completely do), so nobody really thinks much about it.

                                                            He wants to say what he says, it is an intentional message.
                                                            And an interesting mix of conscious messaging and subconscious implications. A lot of the more overt statements tend to indicate a lot of unexamined deeper set issues that are still being communicated.

                                                            EDIT: I always find it curious that you can't formalize a request to have your religion's holidays in the US.                                                             The simple answer is... the whole "50 states" thing. Each state has it's own rules, and the federal government has rules where the state rules don't apply. Even within the same state, things depend where you live. I went to high school and university in the same state where I live now... but in two places that couldn't be more different in how these issues get treated.

                                                            And while I acknowledge there is a risk of getting trapped in this mode of thinking and never standing up for smaller reforms, there are so many bigger issues to solve than making sure I get 3-5 more vacation days a year than I do because I'm spending them on religious days where my Christian coworkers don't have to do that. I can't bring myself to fight for a few days of paid holiday leave when my country doesn't guarantee paid parental leave for everyone.
