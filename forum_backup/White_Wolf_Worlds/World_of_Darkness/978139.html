          I interpret it to mean that pooka can speak in riddles, metaphors or slang. Pig Latin is a good option, for instance. I actually find saying the opposite of what you mean or lying all the time is the hardest to maintain and the hardest to decipher. It's also the least interesting way to run a pooka.

          The old kithbook had some ideas that were decent. Personally, I find the kith flaw virtually unplayable as written. But making your character speak in poetry or rap lyrics, for instance, can be way more fun.

                      Answer questions with questions, exaggerate, throw in random details that never happened.

                      Pooka: "And then the Count walked in. He looked magnificent in his red over coat!"

                      Eshu: "But wasn't it a blue over coat?"

                      Pooka: "Nope, that blue tint was just a trick of the light off those shiny buttons!"

                      Eshu: "Are you sure?"

                      Pooka: "If perception is subjective, who can really be sure of anything?"

                      Pooka are so much fun to play! Hopefully folks will find them a bit more accessible in C20.

                                          Originally posted by Prince of the Night View Post
                                          ok still trying to develop my talent for it

                                          And in the thing in question the Seelie were, for the most part tolerated, even liked, though their was an undercurrent of resentment.

                                          is saying "I feel young and spry for my age" the best way to say "I feel old and world weary for my age"
                                          You can use metaphors, which are technically lies. "My bones are old as the hills. My soul worn down as the mountains."

                                          A Pooka's words are rarely going to be translated word for word. But you can convey the general meaning behind it.

                                          A Pooka may try to convey "I hate that Unseelie bastard because he killed my daughter and I want him to die." The listener may understand the Pooka wants the Unseelie bastard to die because he did something bad to him. An astute listener may realize the Unseelie bastard took someone close to the Pooka. Someone who knows the Pooka's story will realize exact what the Pooka is trying to say.

                                          Either way the listener is going to go kill the Pooka's enemy, which is what he wanted in the first place.
