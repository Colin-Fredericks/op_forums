    (Mass) Production of Magical-Items

    07-23-2025, 12:13 PM
    So, in the ancient Mayan economy, there were two types of trade; subsistence-trade and prestige-trade. The later were the production of trinkets and novelties for the nobility; some were said to have magical-powers so, why not Prestige-Trade be a trade in magical-items? Now could a hedge-magician create them? I know that Mummies can produce amulets, (and the undead) and Mages from M:tA can instill tass into items; I don;'t recall reading up on a power in the book, Sorcerer, or in Hunter's Hunted. In NWoD Second Sight, IDK if there's a power in there that lets them do that.

      There are many ways to create magical and magical-adjacent items in both WoD and CoD. Almost every splat has the means to make something. And among themselves, trade in such items not only exists, but is relatively common. "Minor" splats usually have grater limitations doing it, but they are still able to make something under the right circumstances.

      It's just that such items aren't usually sold for money, they're too precious for that. But barter and favors are a regular occurrence in exchange for magical items, sometimes even between splats.

      Garou and Fera make fetishes. While those items have a religious aspect to them, they still trade among themselves (never with non-Shapechangers);

      Mages make fetishes, all kinds of Wonders and magically enhanced items, and they frequently trade all of those, even with Sleepers and Nightfolk. Most of those items may be used by anyone;

      Demons also can permanently enhance or enchant items in a number of ways, and find stuff left behind in Bastions. Nothing at all stops them from trade with whomever they want, and sometimes their goals align pretty well with giving something to someone;

      Changelings make Treasures and Chimera galore. The greater problem with their items is that most people would have to be Enchanted to be able to use them, but that's not out of question, and Enchanting itself can be sold as a service. And they're by far the splat with less compunction against interacting with others. While some secrecy is always basic care, Changelings are extremely careless. To the point of hanging out with some Vampires that effectively hurt them with their mere presence, just for fun.

              Mass producing and selling minor sorcery trinkets isn't as uncommon as one might at first think. You can go on Amazon right now and get power bracelets, energy crystals, charms and whatnot. Sure, best case that stuff is all fraud and does nothing, worst case it irradiates you. But there isn't really anything that would stop a WOD sorcerer from creating a bunch of minor enchanted items that actually work and sell them. Even less so in societies where magic is just an accepted part of life.

              The Path is called Enchantment, and on low levels it produces tools that do things a little better, or charms with weak effects. Something like a lucky charm would be relatively easy - since it can only be cast as a ritual, the more powerful the effect the longer it takes to enchant. So for mass production only weak effects are really viable.

              That is, unless you use M20 Sorcerer rules as written and allow additional Aspects, which would allow you to enchant per area. Meaning you could stack up several boxes worth of ammo and enchant them all with a single spell as long as you crafted it yourself (which is a general requirement).

              I would actually allow this for lesser trinkets like good-luck-charms with a weak effect, or items with slightly enhanced utility. It's not like there is a lot of money in selling those anyways.

              The Path is a bit tricky to handle as ST, because it can emulate effects that have their own paths (Alchemy, Warding, Healing, Fate etc.) so sometimes it's difficult to decide what it can and cannot do.

                  Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

                  Honestly, I think this is a presentation that sometimes overlooks what money is useful for.
                  On a general sense I agree, and that's actually why I said "usually", which is more generous than the "almost never" the books imply.

                  I just think that in most (like, more than 50% of) cases they have two reasons not to:

                  A) They have other services that can be traded for money, especially with rich humans, on top of other means to get what they want, so the need to sell relatively hard to make items for money is lessened;

                  B) Usually they'll trade such items with people that can similarly provide services and items that can't be accessed in the larger market. Barter is usually the cheaper way to get access to such things.

                  But yeah, I agree that trading for money wouldn't be as uncommon as the books say (except for werewolves, because religion), even with other supernaturals. Money is always extremely useful, as it can buy anything due to its fungibility. The real reason the game tries to make this kind of trade frowned upon is simply to keep the Resources Background useful, which I think it is regardless.

                  Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
                  Mass producing and selling minor sorcery trinkets isn't as uncommon as one might at first think.
                  Such production isn't uncommon at all, and all modern societies accept magic as a part of life far more than most people usually admit publicly. Heck, some market places IRL had already to actively forbid fucking soul trading.

                  But that has less to do with what society accepts from magic and more with what the splats in the game are whiling to make and sell, and to whom, given their general interest in remaining hidden (both from society and from their enemies).

                  "Mass production", to begin with, has the problem of defining what you mean by that. Most splats have serious limitation on how much they can make, and usually can't employ heavy machinery in the process. Then you get to the problem of inconspicuous distribution, how to reach buyers without risking exposure to hostile parties in any step of the process. Finally, you get to the point where you need a reason to make an actual magical item to begin with.

                  Why sell an actual magical item to people who would just as readily accept any weird-looking trinket as "magical"? Splats that want to make money from the public at large don't need to produce magical items cheap, they can find ways to produce magic-looking things at scale. With their powers, they can lend extra credibility to almost any useless knicknack they really mass produce through mundane means, when they can't outright mass produce mundane items cheaply through their powers.

                  No, real magical items are more probably a very niche luxury market, something that fetches the prices that better reward their rarity and the risk of the trade.

                  Garou & Fera: they have to use carefully made items to make Fetishes and Talens, preferably manufactured things, although they can employ Kinfolk in this step. But the main limitation is the need to get and negotiate/subdue a spirit, this isn't something that can be done in large quantities without serious repercussions (and quick depletion of locally available "sources"). Not to say that making such things just to make money is usually seen as a bad use of a Shapechanger's time. Talens can be made in somewhat larger quantities, but that's offset by their single use nature.

                  For Mages: Fetishes still have the same limitations as Shapechangers, even without the religious factor. Wonders are hard and slow to make, on top of costly in money and Quintessence. Not to say that a Wonder creator is a reasonably specialized Mage to begin with, with relatively rare levels of Prime sphere. Items enhanced with Matter are a far more reasonable choice for something resembling mass production, although making them permanente and in large quantities would still require a powerful Mage, specially if the idea is to make them consistently.

                  Technocrats are a special case, as they can and do mass produce Wonders. But they have two problems: for one, their production barely meets their need (actually don't meet it, not really), so they have better uses for their equipment than to sell at large quantities (and they have better ways to finance their operations, anyway). Two, they keep careful control of this equipment because releasing it to the public or other supernaturals is actually against their imperatives. Quite the opposite, they usually have self-destruct measures, retrieve teams and whatnot to make sure those items won't leave the Union. Internally they trade in those items regularly, though.

                  Demons: those ones can make magical/enhanced items with ease with the right Lore, and they have few reasons not to employ this ability. If anything, most would like to keep tabs on the items they make to be sure they're furthering their plans, but doing shit for money isn't above them, and they can do so at large quantities, if not exactly industrial quantities. On the other hand they have a far better product in the form of Pacts, and are unlikely to focus too much on magic item production rather than on reaping Faith from prospective buyers. The items may be useful in this process, though, so they'll certainly be in the market in some quantity.

                  Changelings: what actually stop them from inundating the world with Treasures and Chimeras are the Mists and Banality wracking havoc on their products. The need for the buyer to be Enchanted to even be able to perceive the items is a hard limit factor, too. The Fae usually won't mass product per se because that would be a quite Banal process in most (but not all) cases, and also because they usually require rare materials. But they have a well established production chain, so those limitations are far less stringent than they are on others. In fact, the Fae distinguish themselves in the sheer quantity of magical trinkets they surround themselves with: while for all the others those items are precious and coveted, a Changeling is likely to have even magical toothbrushes, plates, anything and everything.

                  Enchantment Path Sorcerers: I don't know much about them, but they have to make the items themselves, isn't that true? That's quite a limitation on any form of "mass production", as they can't even count on others' work. Even with an area enchantment, if that's at all possible, they'd require a lot of work to amass the amount of items to begin with. Doable, but probably not enough fot it to be anything resembling cheap.

                          Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
                          The one exception I would make is cultures where customers see enchantment as an actual craft on par with pottery or basket weaving, and expect it to work beyond auto-suggestion (at least in within the WOD context). That's more of a meta-game exception though, because I like to think that an ancient Mesopotamian sorcerer peddling charms from a market stall in Ur is something that actually happened within the WOD.
                          Yes, but how do these customers evaluate that if they aren't supernatural creatures?

                          That's the thing. People believe in magic, and that's almost as true today as it was in ancient Mesopotamia. Artisans and sorcerers craft charms and peddle them from market stalls all over the world, including in any major US city. And many, maybe most of those people are dedicated to their craft and believe in the quality and power of their wares.

                          What happens is that, within the WoD, those charms are either very minor or powerless, because their makers are common people (even if maybe with high Occult scores). And people with actual supernatural powers can fetch high prices for their products, because they're rare. And no keen eye on the part of mundane customers will change that, not to say that most people simply don't have the means to find out if something works. All splats with magic-item creation abilities also have supernatural means to detect those items and their properties.

                          Real magic is elitist within the setting, with one exception: when mundane charms actually work. And that's maybe a better way to look at it.

                          Sometimes making things that actually work is simply possible without Magic. Priests bless water every day, it only takes one with True Faith for you to have a steady supply of Holy Water somewhere. In some Reality Zones, a lot of mundane trinkets may really work because that's how reality works there. It's perfectly possible that some crafting traditions and rituals awaken the nascent spirit of the final product, just because, and spirits like that are far more common than splats.

                          It's more likely, at least within WoD's metaphysics, that Ancient Mesopotamia (to stay with the example) had one or more of those situations applying, and so those common charms actually worked. And it may still happen in many parts of the world. In fact, it's possible that absolutely every place has some form of charm that works for real there (there are many such charms that we're so used to that we don't think about them as magical charms, it's just easier to spot it in other cultures).

                                              As far as utility goes, giving the Garou fully built Klaives of enough quality to be made into Fetishes is probably a better options. Less than that, and there's no big difference between any scrap of silver you give them, it'll have to be worked anyway and silver isn't particularly hard to work with (as they already have the expertise and tools), so just give a lot of scrap silver.

                                              But giving them high-quality Klaives has the problem Heavy Arms pointed out. It's too easy to give the wrong impression, especially if this is your first presentation. Even worse if you drop them at a Caern, as the last thing any Sept want from a Mage is for the human Willworkers to know where the Caern is. It's a better arrangement to first have a peaceful meeting at a neutral location, and only then moving to better gifts, but never at the Caern.

                                              If you're a Mage ally to a Garou, you'll never see the Caern. Get used to it. It's not otherwise a bad idea, though. A post-war setting where the last survivors seek protection from the Garou is a very interesting proposal for a game.
