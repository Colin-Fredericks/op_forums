    Hunter: the Reckoning ST tips

    11-23-2015, 10:56 PM
    So, I've begun work on an idea which may one day bloom into a full-blown H:tR game. I'm still very much in the preliminary stages, but can anyone offer me any tips on how to run a H:tR game, frequent pitfalls/things to watch out for, general advice? I've run a few games, and they seemed to be reasonably well-received, so while general-ST advice is always appreciated it's not really what I'm looking for here.

    Thanks in advance!

      Well, the big question is, are you playing with WOD experts, WOD novices, or a mix?

      HtR's generally not such a rough game to run. You get to custom build the monsters to suit your story. You can run it on the grittier side, or the more monster-hunt of the week side without any major tweaks. PCs aren't going to get a large list of powers (13 to 23 maxed out at end game). The biggest things are managing how much Conviction you hand out because that sets the tone for the game more than anything else, and remembering that the Imbued are pretty squishy and have shit healing powers so plan for some downtime esp. if you don't have a Redeemer with Mercy 6 around; unless you want them to regularly start scenes a few HL down.

      My initial question comes in, because HtR not focusing on having the other supernaturals being what they are in their books can really mess with expectations. People that have never really played WOD have no idea what to expect. They're not going to get all frustrated when vampires can walk in the sun without burning up, or humans turn into werewolves if they survive a bite. Experienced WOD players (often completely unintentionally and not maliciously) assume that if you're using a supernatural from one of the major lines, it'll follow those rules rather than use the HtR monster rules. I've seen PCs die because of players making these sorts of assumptions without realizing it. If it might be an issue, bring it up.

                  You shouldn't have to make any sweeping changes. In fact, the HtR lines does a lot of work for you in this regard in various supplements. There's a few supplements that are specific to different supernaturals/WOD groups, and they generally do a good job of giving you the tools to make NPCs that fall in line with how normal WOD characters from those groups operate, without having to reach out of HtR's toolbox.

                  Even if you never crack open a WtA book, there's enough material in HtR to run werewolf NPCs that should behave like the Garou well enough it isn't a problem.

                  My advice is more oriented towards talking to your group about it. Managing expectations works best via talking it out before the game starts, which avoids things like doing massive changes to prevent players from using OOC knowledge from the other games. Find out what they want. If they want you to make monsters without winks and nods to the rest of the WOD so it helps them treat a werewolf like a HtR werewolf not a Garou, do that. If they're cool with you mixing and matching things so you have Camarilla vampires that can go out in the day, but lose all their powers instead of burning up, you can do that too. If they really have trouble separating things, you can use the supplements or other gameline books to run them as-is, and account for things by the nature of the plots you put forward.

                          1) It's a thread for a new HtR ST, and Waywards are not newbie friendly.                                       It's kinda broken. It's one of the Wayward's "perks," though it also drives them insane (and aggressively violently so since they have to take a Derangement like Antisocial PD for it as well). It also messes with Conviction rates, which means it messes with character advancement.

                                      I definitely don't advise it for the first time with the game. There's a few things to consider beyond that:

                                      1) Make sure to read the Second Sight sections thoroughly. It doesn't automatically identify supernatural types. There are Edges for that. You're not going to get, "Demon, risen, vampire, vampire, werewolf, ghost," like this. You're just going to get, "monster, monster, monster, monster, monster I couldn't see before."

                                      2) Imbued can activate the Second Sight reflexively with an Awareness roll. If you want to put the PCs in a situation like this, that's how it should really come up. They get an inkling that a monster is nearby...and they start seeing how many are really around.

                                      3) Don't get hung up on this sort of specific situation. It's cool in our heads, but that doesn't make it fun in play. Really wanting something like this to happen all too often ends in railroading the players into it. What does this scene actually add to play? Are you going to have every PC go through this together, or is one getting all this and the rest are oblivious? Does it actually have anything to do with what the PCs are doing?

                                      If you really want something like this, the best option is to have it be part of a prelude or something similar that's generally not going to be using rolls and such.
