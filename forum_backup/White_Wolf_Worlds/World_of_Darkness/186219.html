    On Snowflakes and Golden Rules

    07-02-2014, 09:00 AM
    I'm a long-time player of the Classic WoD, and throughout my experience with the game the biggest questions I've had were regarding the viability of crossover characters. Abominations, yes, but also things like Cainite Magi (a la Red Sign chicanery), Mokolé with Hedge Magic, and Kinain of all varieties using Dark Ages Dominions. Things like, what a Weaver equivalent to a Ferectos would be, aside from just a non-Possessed Drone, or the bigger questions like "was that Mage in the old LARP book actually a Sidereal?" and "how did Geneviève Orseau keep her faerie side?" All throughout the books we find hints and tidbits (or huge craters like Sammy H) that point to greater compatibility.

    I know that the usual answer is "not only no, but hell no" (Time of Thin Blood), what I want to know is why? In the three global Cam chapters I ran with over the years, that was the secret naughty question on everyone's lips. If you indulged the notion, you had done something wrong according to the STs, who got rather angry about it for no good reason. Of course the golden rule would let you play any kind of combination you could think of, but the moment anyone finds out about it, welcome to hell. A lot of people think this would be something cool, but it seems like we don't usually get a voice or a fair discussion. I understand the point of view of the purists who think it should be done a certain way, and I respect that, but I still think about it. What I want to do is figure out what kinds of combinations are theoretically possible and figure out how they would work. If there is already a thread to this effect, please point me to it? Otherwise, I would welcome any suggestions.

    The topics on my mind:
    * Mages can be Ghouls, and (I think?) Revenants, but not Dhampir. I don't see any reason for this other than the writer's opinion on what is cool and what isn't. I think a half-vampire mage would be very cool. In fact, that is what I would love to play most of all. Not for the power, but for the concept! A sign of God's forgiveness indeed, if they could join the Celestial Chorus! Or better still, become Imbued. They're already portrayed as vampire hunters all over history and pop culture. D, Blade, etc. They have merits to see through vampiric trickery, even!
    * Off Topic: What if a Dhampir could pull a Saulot and become Dhampyr? (KoE: Half-Damned)
    * If the Conquistador's Sword was made once, it could be made again. Fera and True Magick. If a fetish can make it available, does that imply availability without said fetish? Primers, Avatar Storm shards? What about the Avatar-Diablerie mentioned in Ascension? If the Fera soul is only half Awake, why not consume one that is fully so? The Red Sign had some neat ideas about Cainites getting their Avatar back (which reminds me of Caine's Awakening and how he then mentioned learning Nine Disciplines, not to mention how close Golconda sounds to Arete), but could that same kind of Avatar-Diablerie serve the same purpose? Under the old system for Sorcerer, Alchemy could create potions to induce Awakening, the First Change, etc. A possible link?
    * Hsien Kinain. (Land of Eight Million Dreams.) Welcome to Avatar, the Last Airbender / Legend of Korra. Their reincarnation would seem to debunk this, but elemental infusions are a common enough trope. How could it work?
    * Psiads and Yes. One of the Sorcerer editions suggested using Trinity psionics if you wanted to bump up psychic phenomena. Chronologically, if you combined the two settings, Psiads (Aberrant) are around in this time period. What other types could theoretically be Psiads as well? (A Mokolé Energetic would be the second of my ideal troupe.) And what was the final ruling on who converts or keeps Numina? One edition said you convert it to new powers, one said keeping it could give you a handy backup power set. Which ruling won out?
    * Exalted. The Neverborn are messing around with the Rogue Council missives, that one Avatar spoke of the Usurpation as though it was once a Sidereal, and the Hsien and Wan Kuei falling out seemed to have been over the Usurpation. Oh, and the Scarlet Queen and Ebon Dragon concepts in KoE are killing me. And the Hunter Apocrypha about the Golden Heroes or Shining Ones, whichever it was. I notice the Maelstrom released them at the same time as the Demons, which certainly reminds me of the Jade Prison and how the Unconquered Sun wanted to call the Exaltations back after the Locust Crusade in 1st Edition. (Memory is rusty on all that... I hope I'm remembering that bit right.) So, what happens if an Exalt just didn't die somehow? Sitting out the Third through Fifth Ages in the Panopticon Cataclysmic to observe the world and monitor its stability for argument's sake.
    * Semi-Off-Topic: Which reminds me. Dragon Kings. The theory that Dragon-blooded were bred from half-dragon-kings always caught my attention. I don't recall from where I heard that, but I've clung to it for years. A Half-Dragon-King Solar would be amazing. (Or better still, a Sovereign Titanic Exalt who happened to be a Mosok Dragon King fused to the warped prototype Solar Exaltation through a process mirroring Investiture of Infernal Glory and involving an N/A Artifact that could bend the charm's rules a bit to avoid being bound, but still soul-fused in ways normally impossible. I really enjoyed that game...)
    * Lastly, why did they limit Hedge Magic to Bastet and Kitsune ONLY with the 20 update? Does it really unbalance a slathering Garou warmachine that much to have Kinfolk powers? And what if a Kinfolk with Numina gets juiced with that serum that sterilizes them but induces the First Change? (Or an Alchemist using a potion of First Change to become a Mokolé for example.) Would they then lose the Hedge Magic just because a writer wanted to clarify an old ST fiat? What is the real in-character reason for this limitation? If it's reasonable, I'll abide by it, but so far all of this has just seemed like irritated designers being irritable.

    I hope I haven't ruffled too many feathers by mentioning all of this. Not trying to start a fight, just wanting to brainstorm and gain some clarifications. Maybe I should just make a Shard or something like that, but I really want to be able to speak about this topic from a more informed point of view. Any input is welcome, but please be gentle.

    "I have spread my dreams under your feet; Tread softly because you tread on my dreams." -- Yeats

      (Just responding in no particular order, and only to a few things):

      Half-vampire Mages: It isn't a situation of the writers' thinking "x" is kewl while "y" is laym, but rather their taking the (very correctly so) metaphysical perception that the undead (vampires, and the like) cannot exercise True Magic(k) because they are literally missing the aspect of their soul/metaphysical existence that would allow them to do so (the God-Shard/Avatar/Eidelon/Fount/Sahu/any number of names). They are *dead*, in several mystical ways, and that includes working True Magic(k) (they can, of course, develop potentially any number of static/hedge-based powers and rituals, but they can't channel the raw power of the universe). With the "half-dead" mages (Dhampirs, or Revenants), it's a related case of their souls/essences being bogged/tied down far too securely for the Awakening to ever happen. (For Dhampirs, it's simply impossible; for Revenants, the books literally say it's a one-in-a-whatnot chance, it's so rare, and even if it did occur, said Revenant would eventually corrupt/erode his own soul before he even made it to 40 -- and that's being *VERY* generous, assuming he awakened as a teen and somehow developed Arete:10, had an Avatar of 5+). What some people misperceive about [A]wakening as a Mage (not a mage, but a *M*age) is that it *isn't* like being a wizard in DnD, or what-have-you, wherein the world/reality is so magic-abundant that hedge magic can reach nigh-godlike ability (wizards and such, btw, in most DnD settings are *not* Awakened beings, but "merely" practitioners of very powerful hedge magic; True Magic[k] -- the ability to sculpt/shape Reality(tm), in toto -- is the providence alone of the gods, via Alter Reality or, depending on Edition, similar abilities/delineations). There are far more hedge-wizards in the WoD than there are Truly Awakened Mages; vampires and the half-dead may be the former, but they can never be the latter. (Liches, of course, are the proxy-product of True Magic, and may continue to work such due to taking absolute possession of their god-shards; however, they suffer other side-effects accordingly, most particularly being Paradox prone in the 20th/21st centuries and onward...).

      Sorcerer and "x" : Certain aspects you allude to (such as Sorcerer's possibly being able to create potions/what-have-you's that Awaken others, do this, that and the other) are meant to be *plot devices*, *NOT* something that ever occurs with any regularity or predictability (and, appropriately, are scaled at the mythical 6th dot of Alchemy/whatever). Can there be a Mokole' who practices hedge-magic, or a Kinfolk who does so and then has the Change induced via [fill in the blank with the designated Plot Device power] ? Sure. (Hell, in that context, you *can* go ahead and give yourself the half-dead mage, from the above example -- he won't survive more than a few years or so, but if you don't care about that, then go for it...). But that doesn't mean that such things should in any way, shape or form be routine or even remotely predictable. To tack in another way: Garou, and similar, can *already* work rituals and employ spirit Gifts than simulate "x" effects for "y" reasons -- they don't *need* hedge magic, and in fact such practices may often run counter to the "normal" ways in which they invoke their powers/magic. In certain rare cases such as with the Bastet and Kitsune, those Fera are themselves "left-of-center", as it were, and represent the "exception that proves the rule". It's another reason, perhaps, for the Garou to be jealous (or at least envious) of the wisdom/power those two groups possess... with the Garou never realizing that said jealously is part of what blinds them, at least in part, to the possibility of *having* such magics in the first place... #RealFoodForThought

      Linking the two broad concepts above together: Yes -- of course achieving Golconda sounds quite a bit like [A]wakening/what-have-you: it many respects, that's exactly what it is! *However*... that doesn't change the underlying metaphysical elements in play. If said vampire, for example, remained a vampire (albeit a truly enlightened one), then his remaining soul-aspects "awaken", unify, and allow for him to connect to the broader universe in ways that even Caine/Antidiluvians can't: he doesn't use his blood as often to rise/sustain himself; he can develop his powers to near-godlike levels (up to 10 dots); he is free from induced frenzies; etc.... That *still* doesn't mean, however, that he can become a Mage -- he simply becomes *his* *own* *approximation* of such, given what is left of him, spiritually. On the flip, if a vampire is re-connected to his god-shard (via Osiris, True Enlightenment, "The ST said so..."), then he ceases to be a vampire, and may Awaken to become a Mage. (This, btw, is metaphysically perhaps *THE* most SINGULARLY POTENT thing that could possibly happen in the WoD -- that point CANNOT BE OVERSTATED.) Again, however, you would then have a *Mage* -- *NOT* a vampire-mage. (A few examples like that occurred when Osiris restored to life a number of his followers after All Hell Broke Loose -- a handful of them Awakened back to being mages, again; this, to restate, is however once again a case of PLOT DEVICE making things happen, and the juju involved was truly god-level shit, and even then contingent not only on a god-like being lending his power to the situation, but those affected having spent centuries -- in some cases, millennia -- keeping themselves spiritually/mystically pure enough for such to even have a possibility of occurring... again, PLOT DEVICE-level shit...).


      You covered a lot of other stuff, and I am on the road w/limited time for posting, so I'll just stick with the above for now. Hope that helps, for what it's worth...

          Well, in my opinion,crossover it's tricky. The different game lines have contradictory metaplots, incompatible rules, and, perhaps most important, they have different "moods" or "spirits". I don't know how to call it.
          And you may wat to have a solid crossover universe before doing hybrids, since they implicate crossover. And hybrids need to be balanced, they need rules of their own.

          There are many reasons of why the community of roleplayers it's usually hostile to hybrids. These are some of them:
          *They attract powergamers.
          *They are complex to ad to a chronicle. And for what?. It's really necessary to play an Awakened Dhampir to portray a sign of God forgiveness?. The mages have their own dramas after all, like a special perception of Reality. If you don't want to play with those, then probably you can do that better with an original house rule rather than adapting mages.
          *They are unorthodox .They always appear with labels like "Rare" or "Unique". So having lot's of them don't reflect the "canon" reality of oWOD. Even if they are "legal" within the parameters of canon rules.

          But then, all those reasons are very subjective. If the Storyteller and players either don't see them as problems or can cope with them, then I don't know why they shouldn't do it.
          The Golden Rule it's the right to play your game in any way you and your co-players want. And it should be used accordingly.

          Oh, and ,as far as I know,.Exalted isn't anymore the canonical past of oWOD

                              I agree, you can play an egotistical power hungry so-and-so who does things a different way. What I don't understand is why the other way isn't also valid, even if improbable. If a person who discovers some new kind of power would take interest in it, I want to know the reasons that they can or can't and how it would work. The usual solution of keeping the venues separate is fine, I understand that it's the default assumption. My objective is to find out what is actually impossible and why. What I really want is to explore the possibilities of how it would work if someone did these things. "If," not "should." I know this is an unpopular opinion, and I accept that. Again, I'm not trying to upset anyone, I'm only trying to figure out one of many options for playing an admittedly unique variety of character concepts, and for no better reason than "it's there." Like Kirk and the mountain.

                              Though I understand the trepidation of an ST who has a player wanting to play this concept, I must respectfully challenge the preconception that they are automatically up to no good. If the player is cooperative with the ST and other players, courteous of the kind of story that they are trying to tell, and puts in the effort to make the kind of character he wants to play, I believe that he should be rewarded for his effort and respected like any other player. It is a game, and the rules are only there to mediate disputes, not to keep people from having fun or being creative. There are so many rejoinders to this that I can already hear coming: "Play a different game" or "just use the golden rule", but those answers don't satisfy me. I want to use this system, I want to be able to validate a certain view instead of waving ST fiat around like a magic wand. I'm looking for the facts from the canon of the shared mythos we've all grown to love. Like Beckett, scouring the globe for truth. (Though admittedly without such a cool jacket!)

                              To answer Heavy Arms: Your last paragraph about the powermonger manipulating someone else into doing it instead: In that position, I would venture to say, "If you want something done right, you have to do it yourself." That's the usual trap villains fall into: Make the minion do it. But if you have the power yourself, you have the ability to enact change with it and you don't have to worry about losing it. And now you're more potent against the forces of your own venue that will come for you expecting normal powers typical to your type. It's misdirection, a trump card, an ace up your sleeve. Or it isn't. Or you do it the way you suggested. Whatever you choose to do. It's all about options. It's not a popular option, or the easiest, or even the best, but it is an option. I want to know how the option would work if someone did choose it.

                              In reply to JoeDuncan: While I respect your right to seriously question their motivations, I must insist upon "innocent until proven guilty" if only in regard to this theoretical situation. I realize there are powergamers, but to automatically brand someone is elitist and very unkind to someone who is just trying to play a game the way they want to. Your player who is good at running numbers sounds to me like he has a skill that he's proud of. Admittedly, I don't know him, and I'm sure you have reason to be annoyed with his antics, but my knee-jerk reaction is "good for him if he was clever and wants recognition for it." I feel there is nothing wrong with what he's doing until he uses it to unfair advantage and victimizes the other players or ST, at which point the mathematical skill isn't bad, the application of it is. So, if they're a known problem player, I understand they need to be handled accordingly for the enjoyment of everyone in the group, and I agree with you entirely. However, I'm talking about people who aren't just in it for a power trip or to boss other players around. I'm talking about people who would be responsible with these wildly unpopular options.

                              I once had this conversation with a fellow ST, and he asked me to describe how a character would go about it. I answered honestly with my usual ideas and then awaited a thoughtful reply. To my chagrin, he told me that no ST in their right minds would allow it to succeed. I commented that, in the absence of ST fiat, that there was no reason to say it couldn't happen. His reply was an irate "There's no reason it could either." And, yes, he was right, but so was I. Chance doesn't always favor the bold, but it could. If that's not the kind of game you want to run, okay. But it is the kind of game I want to run. I want to do it well and I want to do it right. Again, I want to know how it could, not why it shouldn't. Everything you've said is valid from one point of view. I want to see it from the other, too.

                                  Also I would like to note that htere is considerable difference playing a power monger character and being a powerplayer. Now years later the character has become a major frontline player for Teragen, he has survived through more battles than he would ever have wanted to be involved in and knows there will be major conflicts ahead, he is, partially because of his powers and partially because that is who he is, rather self-centered and as a Terat Marvel consciously seek to guide his own evolution into something greater and stronger. He is very much a power hungry character, yet I as a player have consciously avoided buying any straight out combat powers to him. He still brings into battle mundane firearms, even though he himself and many of the enemies he faces are all but immune to mere bullets, and so tends to win his battles through clever side-ways thinking and sometimes through perseverance and luck. It has been only now years later that I got my first power that could be used in an straight out attack power, because it is a power set that includes such in it's techniques I still avoided taking any such technique meaning that should I ever be tempted to use it in such a manner it would cost horribly in terms of Quantum points to use in such a manner. And even now the possibility exists only because such a Technique is part of the power set I bought, it was not the reason for the decision to get it I got the power because it was logical natural way for his powers to develop. So what I am saying that there is a way to play a power mongering character without being a min-maxing power player. By making IC logical decision in ways which your character grows even though you as a player OOCly know they are inneficient ways to develop your character compared to his goals and opposition.

                                              Originally posted by Cerushad View Post
                                              I would respectfully challenge everyone who speaks out against so-called powergaming and min-maxing to find a valid, non-paradoxical, logical reason why spending one's points in an effective way is immoral in a game that has a combat system.
                                              The existence of a combat system is irrelevant.

                                              The "immorality" of problematic game-play styles is ultimately that they violate the social contract that is part of any gaming group. While each group is going to be different, every group (either explicitly or implicitly) will have a set of standards on how much tolerance they have for putting systems over fiction. The contract exists because RPGs are a group activity and only truly function when everyone is having fun. Violating that contract is "immoral" because it inherently means valuing one person's fun over impact that might have on the rest of the group's fun.

                                              This applies to pretty much any term used in this sort of fashion; about players or the GM/ST/etc. or even the authors of the books.

                                              This does mean that there is never a universally applicable definition of terms like powergaming or min-maxing because such behaviors are always tolerable to an extent. The problem lies in violating social norms by taking any given behavior too far for the group's tastes.

                                              Spending your points effectively becomes problematic when your emphasis on that particular motivation exceeds the group norm and then becomes powergaming or min-maxing or whatever. Consider these things "crimes" of extremes, not absolutes.

                                              *However*: It isn't about need, it's about want.
                                              Personal want always needs to be balanced against the want of the group in a group social activity.

                                              Sure, you don't need to reinvent the wheel, but some times the new wheel you make gives you a good feeling, because it's your wheel the way you like it.
                                              In my experience such pleasure is short lived and largely less fun than anyone claims it is. The most enjoyment comes not out of re-inventing the wheel to feeling neat about it - since that feeling is fleeting as it only really applies to making the wheel - but of taking a stock wheel and modifying it for your personal tastes and style so that it will always be your personal take on things. What makes a character unique and special in a good way is not what mechanical widgets you can shoehorn into their sheet, but what you as a creative individual bring to the concept to make it stand out in a new and interesting way. A special widget might be part of making that happen, but the widgets are tools, not the expression of your creativity.

                                              I'm trying to figure out how, not asking permission to do so.
                                              As I said though, "how" is ultimately not something you're going to find answers for, for many of these questions. The books don't have them. They were never meant to have them. If we're talking about things the books present as interesting ideas, they are often not given a how explicitly because the exist as ideas for you to explore how over the course of a game if you're interested in it rather than letting you come out of the gates with the answer.

                                              If you want to run a game about trying to recreate a magical item from the books that was supposedly unique, having the books give you the answers makes that a pretty boring game. It also validates only one approach to doing it. Having it be a mystery lets you run the game in dozens of different ways that can all be equally valid and entertaining.

                                              We, as a community, can at best offer "how" we would handle such things. But you have accept that for many of us the answer is, "I wouldn't do that for X reasons."
