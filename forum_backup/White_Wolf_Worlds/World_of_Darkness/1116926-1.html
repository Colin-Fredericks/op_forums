    Wasn't BurritoMage meant the CofD 2eds?

    I still liked most of the 20th stuff, including M20 (and very much liked that one), but I'm not in favor how the writers of the various 20th books "abandoned" gothic-punk as an important part of the setting and every game. In older books, there was at least a few columns, designated to that and the 20ths don't even mention it, aside from the V20 core, and even that one did it only tangentially. It's just a big loss in my eyes, for a core theme and great descriptor of the whole setting to be ignored. I really hope Martin's remark of "gothic-punk is dead" doesn't mean it will be like that or even more so in V5 and in their iteration of WoD.

    But yeah, political correctness and inclusivity are very noticeable trends in the OPP books, and even more in the CofD ones. It's not surprising, regarding a lot of their writers and developers and honestly, most of the time isn't even a bad thing (although sometimes I feel it to be a bit forced). Ok, the WoD books were always leftist and attempted to be inclusive, but it's certainly a shift in focus and importance. Also, the old books were less PC in the sense that they were more "dark", more "edgy", but that was a good thing in my eyes. The new books are frequently a bit too... neat, IMO, albeit having tons of very good material and writing in them. I just liked the grittier version of WoD a bit better.

            I think you're misinterpreting me a bit there.

            I didn't say I have a problem with expansive games (Mage is only of my favorite WoD games and is by far the most thematically expansive). I said that I think VtM gets in its own way. Not just with the rules, but the nature of its setting elements.

            Like, with Mage, if I want to run a local politics Chantry game, the Ascension War part of the game easily compliments it even if there's never going to be a bunch of combat scenes with black helicopters and HITMarks, or space battles between blimps carrying magicwand fireball artillery vs. sci-fi space ships. The Ascension War can easily inform individual political stances on the local level, and picking a post-Revised point in the mtaplot, there's a lot of room for the Technocracy to impact the game directly without turning into a shooting war.

            Vampire has the Camarilla and Sabbat in active warfare the entire run, with the other Sects either taking a side or just hiding away. If I'm running a local politics oriented game, I need to set it far away from the Sect War. If the Sect War becomes a relevant part of the instance of the game, avoiding it turning into at least a side plot about violent conflict with other vampires is extremely convoluted. You can't just have some Sabbat take up residence in your Domain and make a deal for them to stay there.
