    Originally posted by AkatsukiLeader13 View Post

    Hard to say what the future of Mage is at this point. Though if it keeps with the general idea of what's in M20, the Technocracy will continue to the group that while having fallen from the noble ideals of their forebears, are striving to make the world better. Hell since the missing four Rev Ed CBs were released, we've seen the Technocracy moving further away from their original Western White Man outlook to a more diverse and global Technocratic Union with Technocracy Reloaded regarding the Technocracy of the 90s as rather antiquated relic that looks silly and dated today. It even straight up has the Union utterly frustrated by how the United States has turned out in recent years with some wanting to just label it a failed experiment and move on. Something that even the Rev Ed at it's most nihilistic would have taught was absurd.

    For that matter, even for as much as Victorian Mage slams the Order of Reason and labels them the villains, it still points out all the good the OoR is doing alongside the bad and that every Convention has good aspects to them, not just the future VE, SoE and VA.

    If Mage continues with where we're at right now, the likely state will be a situation not unlike what it was during MtSC, with the Traditions and Technocracy (and Disparate Alliance if they're kept around) in a murkier, less antagonistic state between each other. Some times they will fight each other, some times they will work together to deal with a problem and other times they will just keep a weary distance from each other. Meanwhile the Nephandi become a much more serious threat (which let's face it with what they are, they really should have been from the start), taking that mantle of the major antagonist faction. The Technocracy will, like I said above, continuing it's trend of reforming and struggling against problems internal and external as it becomes a more global Technocracy.

    Of course again, that assumes the general trajectory of Mage continues from where M20's at which we can't really say at this moment.
    My premise here is that the Real World™ trajectory isn't going to be what it is in ten years; that we're primed for a drastic change comparable to the Revolutionary War, the Civil War, or World War II, and at this point are just waiting for the spark that will trigger it. And Mage will surely change in accord to that.

    And while there's no way to predict what will trigger it or what the exact nature of that change will be, we can be confident that it will result in a resurgence in appreciation for the positive aspects of what the Technocracy represents. And in that environment, I wonder if a hypothetical M40 might be better off portraying something closer to an alliance between the Traditions and a reformed Technocracy for a better future, with the old model of “the world is going to Hell in a handbasket” being relegated to the dustbin of history once the Crisis is over and the subsequent High begins.

    As I see it, there will still be room for the Traditions in this new model, and they'll still be in opposition to the Technocracy; but it will be less a matter of an Ascension War and more a matter of trying to promote individuality and a healthy sense of rebellion in a world that no longer has any interest in such things. This also has historical precedent: most recently, the post-WWII era had writers who were lamenting the shallowness and conformity of the age.

    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                        The general concept of Mage was that the game (and the entire World of Darkness line) was set in a world where magic was dying and the supernatural was becoming more and more scarce. However, there was going to come a point where the End of the World happens, and just before that began, magic would likely flood back into our world, as that is what would be necessary for the powers of Evil to take control of the world before the Final Battle would begin. There are many other books and media that follow this general path, so it is by no means unique to WoD.

                        I don't see why that would need to change at all. It is a good set up and allows a lot of leeway for STs and excitement for PCs. Using that general concept, you can set WoD/Mage games in any era of history, in any culture, and you'll still have a fun game that satisfies any requirements for a WoD/Mage game.

                        The Fourth Turning is an interesting book, but like all "history is in cycles!" books, it is pseudoscience. Its merits as predictive future is extremely dubious, and I don't see its relevance to this game.

                        Likely, the Technocracy will be shown as increasing its power and the Traditions see further erosion as we head into a future that is becoming more dominated by science. But to what extent that substantially changes the premise of the original corebooks is probably marginal.

Once we get through the current Crisis (successfully or not; I'm not saying this as a mark of optimism), we're not likely to see the conditions suitable to another one arise until the early 22nd century, at a point where even today's Millennials will likely be gone (barring medical advances and extended lifespans).
