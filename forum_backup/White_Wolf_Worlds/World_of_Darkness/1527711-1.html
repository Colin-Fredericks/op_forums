                Originally posted by Cain Loup-Noir View Post
                The locked effects on the other hand does not cause genetic flaws, and if we understood correctly they do not cause paradox unless visible.
                Not exactly.

                So, once a spell has been cast, it can't generate Paradox later.

                However, if you cast a spell that's vulgar without witnesses, it's still vulgar, and thus still generates paradox.

                From a game design perspective, this is why Permanent Paradox is really a thing (esp. since it's not actually permanent). It exists to cover situations where you're in your Sanctum, the spell is coincidental, but when you leave, it would be considered vulgar (and again, remember that vulgar w/o witnesses is still vulgar), by having a form of lingering Paradox that hangs on your character. Permanent Paradox just doesn't actually "do" anything until you start causing more Paradox (I think this might be why you're thinking it doesn't "create" paradox unless "visible").

                So, if you have a bunch of Permanent Paradox incurring spells on you, and you just walk around not doing anything magical, nothing happens... if you provoke Paradox, it gets bad scaled to how much Permanent Paradox you built up by adding dice to your Backlash pool that don't go away like normal Paradox dice after a backlash.

                So, there's basically a risk-reward to this method. You can have these powers one you, and then try to act subtly and not incur Paradox, and get away with a lot of buffs to your character... but that comes with a high risk of blowing up in your face when things get tense - even a simple coincidental spell risks botching and causing Paradox - and all that power finally kicks back.

                    I think you are confusing spells that have indefinite duration, effects that are permanent, and wonders.

                    Spells with indefinite duration are active spells that will persist until they are ended. The spell is still there and can be unwoven if you are using those rules. Your locked protective shield is this kind of magic.

                    That's not the same as a permanent change, where the magic is finished and the change to reality is set. In that case the spell already did its work and the effects it left behind are a perpetual part of reality.

                    Finally, neither of those is a wonder, which is the ability to do sphere magic baked into an thing. A belt with a protective spell locked onto it is not a wonder. A belt that can cast a new protective spell on its wearer is.

                                            This is one of those areas I think slipped through editing.

                                            Life 5 says it is the rank for permanent changes.

                                            Life 3/4 can enhance attributes or make significant but not drastic changes and by the rules they should be permanent changes with enough successes (and it should be significantly less than 20).

                                            HDYDT backs up the second option, not the first.

                                            You could just choose one or the other option, but if you want to try to reconcile the two here is one potential way: Life 3/4 effects can be made permanent by making the magic permanent. Life 5 effects can be made permanent by making the change permanent.

                                            What I mean by this is that a permanent Life 3/4 spell is an ongoing spell. It will last forever but if it does ever end the target reverts to their normal pattern (although they keep anything they've paid experience for). Because it is a spell it is vulnerable to unweaving, which can be done at lower ranks. As a permanent spell it uses the basic rules for any permanent spell: successes for the effect plus 6+ more successes for permanent duration.

                                            The Life 5 spell, though, isn't a permanent spell, it is an instantaneous spell that gives the target a new permanent pattern that is theirs forever. There is no unweaving this one because there's nothing to unweave. The only way to return someone to their original form is to cast a new spell. The duration rules apply to the duration of the spell and this one is practically instantaneous, so the caster doesn't need to spend successes for increased duration. On the other hand indelible changes to reality are much greater feats than ones propped up by an existing spell, so this is a significantly harder spell.

                                            Both can change the target's pattern to something rejected by reality so both of them can cause Permanent Paradox.
