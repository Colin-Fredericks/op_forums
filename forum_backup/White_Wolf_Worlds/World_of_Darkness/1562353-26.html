                            I was honestly really excited about the idea of V6. I thought they would build on the legacy of V20 + V5. But what I have found, personally, doesn’t have the essence that all the other editions had, from the classic editions through V20, V5, and CoD.

                            My impressions:
                            • Drama Points: They feel unnecessary. If you use them exactly as described in the rules, players see them and start thinking about and fearing the consequences, and then they play accordingly based on what is on the table. This makes the adventure feel less natural. Also, I don’t like using them as a Storyteller. We stopped using them after the second session.
                            • Quickening: This takes us quite far away from what Vampire: The Masquerade is supposed to be. Giving out dice to be used on future rolls to build momentum might work well for a vampire in Frenzy or a werewolf giving in to their rage, but for an ordinary vampire, I don't like it. One thing is improving or rewarding the action you are currently taking; another is rewarding future actions. I'm sorry, but it takes me out of the World of Darkness, where we play so that bad things happen to us, where there are consequences, where we will often fail rolls and those failures will create more stories. This mechanic takes me away from everything I enjoy about telling Vampire stories.
                            • Vitae and Health being combined: I understand what they were trying to do, but our group really hasn't liked it. I will say that we do like Hunger Dice, and we've played with them in V5 and V20 with some changes. Our group had a great time adapting Hunger Dice to V20, and another group has played V5 directly.

                              Some of the problems we've encountered: we don't like the automatic healing. I've seen people at the table discussing and doing the math to figure out whether it's worth spending Vitae to activate Rouse abilities and Disciplines, only to fail the roll and end up worse off than they were at the beginning. As a result, in our games, many players preferred not to use certain powers in combat.

                              In the end, you get a situation where they want to go hunting just to make sure they always have enough Vitae available. On the other hand, having more mental powers use Willpower makes you lose the feeling that you are just blood. I don't like the fact that a vampire can use magic or Disciplines through Willpower. Mechanically, it feels like you're compensating for certain Vitae costs with Willpower, which is something that had never been done before. I don't like the idea of a vampire being able to rely on something other than Vitae to perform their powers.

                              Vitae (Health) and Mana: It might work well in other games, but I really don't like it for Vampire. It feels like a video game, something like Diablo.
                            • Beast/Humanity Tracker: We've always liked the downward spiral towards the jaws of the Beast. This system needs a very major rethink. I'm sorry, but I could spend hours explaining why I don't think it works and how the system is going to be abused in different situations. I don't understand why they are trying to introduce dynamism into something that shouldn't be dynamic.
                            • Social Conflict: We haven't really used it, no. Turning a diplomatic action into some kind of counterspell mechanic isn't our thing. In our storytelling-style games, everyone narrates. If you've given a really good performance based on your character's stats, great; if not, you roll the dice to determine the degree of your success against your opponent.
                            • The System: It's not badly designed. But Vampire has always been about the more dice I roll, the better. I roll a lot of successes and have better actions because I'm older or more powerful, and the opponent does the same, allowing them to act as a proper challenge.

                              Reducing dice pools through difficulty can be a very clever idea for other games, but for our group it doesn't feel like Vampire. Where is the possibility of rolling for the player without telling them the difficulty, keeping it secret, and creating fear and uncertainty? Where is the possibility of achieving truly glorious moments, like giving the best live performance of the last decade?
                            • Maturation: We liked this. The idea is good and interesting, and it's something that should have been integrated several editions ago. We were already using something similar ourselves in V5, in our own way. Now it's all built into the system, which is great.
                            • Clan Traits: The idea is there, but it could have been implemented in a much simpler way, with simpler powers based around the concepts and identities of the Clans. Instead, they've chosen to give them very powerful abilities to create Clan identity, and the result is that the characters become walking stereotypes. Not good.

                              The philosophical Brujah get lost because they are now the combat Clan or the super-strength Clan. I really like the idea behind this system, but not the way it has been implemented. They could have introduced Lore Sheets or something similar that could be expanded upon, tying together Clan, lore, and other elements.

                              Anyway, it's an Alpha, so maybe they'll tweak it.

                            I just want to say that this is my opinion, or rather our group's opinion, after playing several sessions. Maybe we've misunderstood or incorrectly applied some rules; there is a significant language barrier for us. It may also be partly our fault because we've been playing Vampire as our favorite game since the mid-90s.

                            We've also played many other games, from D&D and Pathfinder to Call of Cthulhu, OSR, PbtA, Trophy Dark, Trophy Gold, and many, many others. More than 30 years of roleplaying gives you a lot of experience.

                            I think V6 has become the amalgamation that many people criticize V5 for being. xD

                            It uses a lot of mechanics from games that are currently popular, and those mechanics work well for those games. But here, it feels like they're creating a Frankenstein, a Vozhd that doesn't fit together and doesn't feel like Vampire: The Masquerade.

                            “A beast I am, lest a beast I become.”

                            Again, this is just our impression and our opinion, and it is not intended to disrespect anyone.

                                        In V6, Vitae functions almost like automatic regeneration of normal damage. Normal damage that you suffer does not permanently reduce your ability to store Vitae. It simply represents injuries that a vampire’s body can quickly heal by using its own Vitae.

                                        For example, if a vampire is struck in the head with an axe, the blow may injure them, but their body rapidly regenerates and the wound disappears. Even a much more severe injury, such as having a hand amputated, could eventually regenerate. The idea is that normal damage heals automatically, like an extremely fast supernatural regeneration.

                                        Aggravated damage is different. It represents wounds that normal regeneration cannot repair. In addition, aggravated damage points occupy spaces in your maximum Vitae pool.

                                        For example, imagine you have a maximum capacity of 15 Vitae. If you have 3 points of aggravated damage, those three points take up space. Even if you feed and have enough blood to refill your reserves, you cannot exceed 12 effective Vitae while those 3 aggravated damage points remain.
                                        To recover those lost spaces, you must specifically heal the aggravated damage, and doing so costs considerably more Vitae than ordinary regeneration. As a result, aggravated damage does not just hurt you in the moment; it temporarily reduces your capacity to recover and store Vitae until you manage to heal it.

                                        In summary:

                                        Normal damage → rapid/automatic regeneration → does not leave a permanent injury.

                                        Aggravated damage → does not regenerate in the same way → requires spending Vitae specifically to heal it and occupies part of your Vitae capacity until it is healed.


                                        Originally posted by GreatHornedRatkin View Post

                                        In this regard, I disagree. I think V6's approach to Humanity is probably the best version yet. It veers away from the idea of Humanity being a codified set of ethics that you're expected to follow like religious tenets, and instead presents us with the idea that your Humanity is exactly that; YOUR Humanity.

                                        It's the conflict between your inner Beast and the person that you were prior to being embraced. What defines Humanity and how the Beast interacts with it becomes a highly personalized aspect that is different for each individual.

                                        Which I think is far more interesting to explore in regards to future character concepts.
                                        I obviously ignore V20 because, in my view, the old Humanity design has been surpassed by both Chronicles of Darkness and V5.

                                        To compare them, V5 bases Humanity on principles, beliefs, and convictions. These do not have to be Christian values. They can be a mafioso's code of honor, Buddhist principles, or even the principles of a Path of Enlightenment such as those used by the Sabbat (which worked very well in my games). This version of Humanity rewards and punishes characters according to the vampire's own internal moral scale, measuring the struggle between their beliefs and the monster dwelling within them that seeks to pull them away from those beliefs, their Humanity. Humanity is gradually lost, and recovering it becomes increasingly difficult. This is why older vampires tend to become more detached and inhuman compared to neonates.
                                        Now, Humanity vs. Beast is presented as a balanced scale, with Beast on one side, Neutral in the center, and Human on the opposite end. It feels more like an Order-Chaos-Neutrality alignment system, similar to Moorcock or D&D. The character moves back and forth across that scale.
                                        If you give in to the Beast and murder several hunters, as happens in the Alpha adventure, you move toward the Beast side one step for each killing. As a result, you gain Discipline bonuses because you reach Monstrous 3, but you also suffer penalties to social interactions and dealings with mortals.

                                        What happens next?

                                        Players generally do not want to remain trapped on the monstrous side. They know they need to perform more humane or socially positive actions, so they immediately begin trying to correct the imbalance before it becomes worse and move themselves back toward the human side.
                                        Traditionally, Humanity existed in the background. It deteriorated slowly over time. In this new system, because Humanity and Beast are managed through visible trackers and opposing meters, the mechanic has to be pushed into play in a much more direct and explicit way.
                                        • When a player's vampire has little Vitae, they feed.
                                        • When a player's vampire has little Willpower, they pursue Ambitions or whatever is necessary to recover it.
                                        • When a player is dangerously close to the Beast side, they rush to "fix" it in ways that often feel unnatural.
                                        • When a player is dangerously close to the Humanity side, they may rush to commit monstrous acts because they are uncomfortable with where their character currently sits.
                                        Lo explico de forma un tanto directa, pero esto es esencialmente lo que he experimentado en mis mesas.

                                        Nadie parece sentirse cómodo con este sistema en la práctica. Al principio, a todos les pareció interesante e incluso bastante ingenioso, pero después de un tiempo de juego empezó a crear comportamientos que se sentían artificiales. En lugar de que la Humanidad sea una lucha interna lenta y gradual, se convierte en un ejercicio de gestión de recursos donde los jugadores monitorean constantemente un par de barras y ajustan su comportamiento para mantenerlas donde quieren. El resultado, al menos en mi experiencia, es que la lucha entre la Humanidad y la Bestia se siente menos orgánica y más mecánica.

                                        Sé que esto todavía es una alfa y que las cosas cambiarán, pero les animo a que revisen las tablas que describen cómo es su personaje dependiendo de si está en Bestia 1, Bestia 2 o Bestia 3, y de igual manera en Humano 1, Humano 2 o Humano 3.

                                        Cuando leo estas descripciones, lo que veo no es realmente Humanidad, sino más bien una escala deslizante entre monstruo y humano . Mi preocupación es que esto tiende a animar a los jugadores a gestionar su posición en la balanza en lugar de experimentar la Humanidad como una lucha lenta y trágica que se erosiona gradualmente a lo largo de los siglos .


                                        Combat: Another thing I didn't like, which I forgot to mention in my previous post, is combat. It feels much more restrictive, with conditions being a major focus, and that was one of the things I liked least about CoD. It also feels very similar to games like D&D.

                                        All previous combat systems felt much more narrative than the current one. Because of that, there was much more room to describe scenes at whatever level of fiction or realism the group preferred. Now, like many other aspects of this edition, everything feels so heavily codified that it becomes abstract.
                                        You can place countless environmental elements around a scene: vases, fire hydrants, traffic signs, and so on. Yet it feels like the system is better played by focusing on its rules, distances, and mechanical structure. I don't like that approach. I've played a lot of OSR and D&D, and now it feels much easier and more natural to put Vampire: The Masquerade on a battle map and play with miniatures.

                                        That is not to my taste.

                                        I also don't like the idea of diablerie through mere physical contact instead of having to bite and drink from another vampire. The traditional act of diablerie is intimate, brutal, and transgressive. Reducing it to simple contact makes it feel less visceral and, in my opinion, less meaningful from both a thematic and narrative perspective.
