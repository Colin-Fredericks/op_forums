        Oh boy. I apologise for making a drive-by instead of substantiating my comments, I am genuinely surprised they would be that controversial.

        I will admit that the comparison to Gypsies was hyperbolic and unwarranted, but while it's not quite that bad I absolutely maintain that Charnel Houses of Europe is exploitative, ill-considered, in extremely poor taste and without merit.

        I did not comment to that effect to troll, and I especially resent the assumption from Malcolm that I don't know what I'm talking about. I own the book, I have read it in full (including the foreword) and while I wasn't aware that Jonathan Blacke was Jewish, the best that allows me to say about the book is that presumably it was created with no malicious intent. If you like it Malcolm, then defend it like an adult.

        Here are my criticisms in full:

        First and least of all, the language used throughout the book is overwrought and blatantly manipulative, in a way that the subject matter absolutely does not need and which only does it a disservice. At times it shades so far into the purple as to become outright disgusting, as when it's written that «The killings [at Auschwitz] started slowly, tentatively, like a new lover’s first bashful caresses.» What the Hell is that? Thus, while clearly meticulously researched the book still fails when considered as a textbook.

        How about as a game book?

        Unfortunately, in what I presume to be an effort to avoid trivializing the Holocaust, the writer has gone too far in the other direction, exceptionalizing its perpetrators, victims, context and fallout in a way that makes no sense from an in-game perspective. The bulk of the fictional material within the book is taken up with a description of the calamitous effects of the arrival of twelve million camp-dead Enfants in the Shadowlands -an entirely unprecedented number.

        Except it's not. More people died over a similar time frame in World War I, 25 years prior, fully half as many in just one year during the Holodomor. Of course, probably fewer of those became Wraiths... but even that ignores that while the Holocaust was ongoing, so was World War II, which killed at least 50 million people worldwide outside of the death camps. If the logistical challenge of the Holocaust revolutionized Stygian society, World War II should have drowned it in chaos.

        And hang on. Every single victim of the death camps became a Wraith? Well, yes, of course. It would have been impossible to write the book in any other way, an insult to every victim whose rage or regret or connection to their next of kin was deemed «not strong enough» by the writer... but this should have been the first of many blazing signposts to White Wolf that this book should never have been written, because it is as absurd from an in-universe perspective as it is necessary from a real-word perspective, and an insult to all other victims of human evil and their relatives. Wraith and real, murdered people do not mix.

        Besides, for all its protestations of preserving the memories of twelve million individuals, this book treats the ghostly victims of the Holocaust as a homogenous mass defined entirely by their victimhood and the circumstances of their deaths.

        This takes its most grotesque form in the descriptions of the free camps – which the text refers to as «ghettos» more often than not, in an intensely tone-deaf choice of words: The twelve million Holocaust dead are granted freedom and sovereignity over the Shadowlands sites of the camps and execution grounds where they were killed. Who would suggest that? Who would accept it? And who could possibly think it was a good idea to write, without seeing how dehumanizing it is? While some of the camp-dead would probably have enough Angst that the nightmarish reminders of their torturous deaths suited their temperaments and tastes, when you write something like this you have to consider that what kept these people clinging close to life -their Fetters, in other words- would absolutely not be limited to the indignities of the last days of their lives – they had relatives, memories and unfinished business from tens of millions of places all over Europe and the world; they will want to do almost as many different things as there are people.

        It's not only the victims of the Holocaust who are tarred with a broad brush by the book, but the perpetrators as well -when they are mentioned at all. It's by all means understandable that the ghosts of German soldiers and government officials do not receive a lot of attention in a book about their primary victims, but it is still unfortunate -all the more so because a book purporting to cover themes of individual heroism and culpability, guilt, forgiveness and redemption declines at any point to consider that a Nazi soldier or SS-man who became a Wraith probably didn't cling to life out of an unfulfilled desire to persecute Jews some more. At every turn they are presented as nothing but the most one-dimensionally evil of acceptable targets.

        The decision to paper over all this diversity with the ersatz post-mortem Israel of the free camps, complete with international criticism and prejudice, hunts for «escaped» Nazi ghosts and mystically significant «Never Forget»- sentiments (that are entirely incongruous with the presentation of relics, fetters and Harrowing elsewhere in Wraith) not only rings entirely false and leaves a foul taste in the mouth as written, but could not possibly manage to do otherwise. The sensibilities of writer and audience both quite understandably demand some kind of redemptive spin on the tragedy, a constructive reaction that suggests we can learn from it and in the future, rise above the hate and evil that caused it – but using Wraith as a vehicle for this message renders it off-putting and saccharine. It only highlights again how unsuitable the Holocaust is as subject material for a fantasy RPG, however dark in tone and maturely presented -the conflicting demands of the topic and the genre are irreconcilable. The write-ups and references given in the later sections of the book to those characters who were actual historical people further underline this – no, I do not want to make a game of hunting down the ghost of Mengele and welding it into a portal of screaming Auschwitz personel as the capstone to a latter-day Gate of Hell. The idea is obscene.

        It's not the only obscenity in the location write-ups either – the Auschwitz chapter in particular is rife with them.


        The [crematoriums], though hated and dreaded in equal measure, are
        vital components of the camp’s economy. Here, in these infernos, legions of monstrous dybbuks toil, soulforging Corpora and wire into needed goods for use or barter. Never is the vicinity free from the blast of the ovens, the roar of the flames and the screams of the molten.
        So the victims become the victimizers, in an eternal parody of the way they died. Lovely. Now in the context of Wraith this is indisputably an idea that makes sense, and arguably even makes for great horror- but again -that just highlights the impossibility of reconciling a respectful treatment of the Holocaust with the demands of good game material -I shouldn't have to belabor how far this excerpt is from being the former.

        I wrote this in a style that's primarily focused on in-game material, I realize, and I hope that it is not too off-putting or flippant of me to do so, as my criticism applies to both an in-game and out-of-game perspective on the way the book portrays Holocaust victims -it is a testament to the skill of the writers of Wraith that I feel I can confidently leave the real-world corollaries of the in-game criticisms to those of you who read this to follow.

        I hope those of you who asked me to clarify my position over the past few days find some value in this explanation.
