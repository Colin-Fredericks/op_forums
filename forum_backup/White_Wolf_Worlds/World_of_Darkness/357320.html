    Sorcerer 20th Anniversary Edition

    01-02-2015, 03:49 AM
    Those of you who have seen my posts on the Mage: the Ascension forum probably know that I’ve got some rather strong opinions about Sorcerer; if you don’t, check out the Acolyte Magic post linked to in the Ascension hacks found in my sig. The thing is, though, that that hack only addresses half of the issue of Sleeper magicians as I see it: it takes the concept of non-Awakened proto-mages who can work a limited form of Magick because their Avatars are struggling to Awaken and replaces the Hedge Magic rules basis with a minor variation of Mage’s Sphere Magick. What it doesn’t do is to take the Hedge Magic rules and refine them into a Sorcery system that stands on its own.

    To be fair, the original World of Darkness only had one book in it that addressed Hedge Magicians as their own thing: the 1997 book World of Darkness: Sorcerer, The Hedge Wizard’s Handbook. Prior to that, the most comprehensive take on Hedge Magic was in the Mage supplement Ascension’s Right Hand; and after that, Hedge Magic was put back under the Mage line with Sorcerer Revised.

    Let me also note that Mage 20th Anniversary Edition is technically two years late now, and Wraith 20th Anniversary Edition has also missed the year in which its title would technically be accurate. Not judging; just saying — I definitely agree with the “better late and done right than on time and done wrong” philosophy at work here. But it does mean that Wraith 20 will probably be coming out some time in 2015–2016, and Changeling 20 will probably be released 2016–2017. And hey, 2017 will be the 20th anniversary of Sorcerer…

    There are actually two things I’d like to discuss here: first, the viability of a 20th Anniversary Edition for Sorcerer; and second, what you’d want to see in such a book if it were actually to be published.

    On the first point, it’s worth noting that White Wolf didn’t stop publishing games in 2015: a hypothetical Sorcerer 20 would be competing with Kindred of the East 20 and Mummy 20 at the very least. Personally, I’d be OK with Kindred of the East never getting a commemorative edition at all; or if it gets a new edition, having it published closer to its 25th anniversary. I definitely would be more interested in Sorcerer than in Kindred of the East. As for Mummy, that’s a tougher call for me; but when push comes to shove, I’d be more interested in a Mummy: the Resurrection 20th Anniversary Edition.

    In terms of what I’d like to see in a Sorcerer 20: in a nutshell, an overhaul of the Hedge Magic system from the perspective of sorcerers being their own thing rather than a Mage spin-off, and with a consistent design philosophy rather than the ad hoc hodge-podge accumulation of Paths that were featured in the first two editions. What this means:

    • The notion that Hedge Magic has anything to do with Sleeper Avatars or that it’s empowered by the magician’s beliefs should be relegated to a small entry in a crossover section, where it would be presented as an unproven theory put forth by a mage about what sorcery is. This theory would get equal time with counterparts put forth by the likes of werewolves (“sorcery is a manifestation of the Wyld”, or some other theory about how sorcery traces back to the Triat, Gaia, or some other element of Werewolf’s metaphysics) or changelings (who would probably explain sorcery as something rooted in Glamour); and all such theories would be presented in the spirit of the parable of the Blind Men and the Elephant. Sorcerers would have their own explanation for what sorcery is, which would be based heavily on the one provided in the 1997 Sorcerer book: the world of darkness is itself inherently magical and full of arcane Mysteries, and sorcerers are individuals who have the curiosity and discipline neccessary to harness the world’s secrets for their own ends.

    • Mechanically, I’d start with the Paths covered in Sorcerer Revised plus the ones first published in the Dreamspeakers Tradition Book, Revised; but I’d take a back-to-basics approach to the write-ups, presenting them in the same way as the Paths featured in Hunters Hunted II. In fact, I’d do it so that the Paths featured in HH2 could be pasted into S20 almost verbatim. This might involve downgrading some Paths such as Hellfire or boosting others so that the Paths presented have a more even spread of utility. It might also involve recasting some existing Paths as minor variants of other existing Paths — or at least clarifying what their relative strengths and weaknesses are.

    • The book would not feature Psychic Phenomena; I’d save that for a companion book devoted exclusively to psychics, mediums, and their ilk. This would primarily be to free up space: I’d expect Sorcerer 20’s word count to increase substantially over what Sorcerer Revised got, even without Psychic Phenomena.

    • Things like Aspects or mana would be relegated to optional systems that the Storyteller could introduce if he wants the sorcerers in his chronicle to be more effective. Likewise, the “doesn’t work in front of witnesses” rule would be turned into an optional Flaw instead of a universal restriction on Hedge Magic.

    • Per the 1997 edition of Sorcerer, each magician would have a magical style that determines what magical instruments and practices he uses to work his magic. This would be cribbed directly from M20; but in keeping with the aforementioned HH2 compatibility issue, said magical tools would be presented as an optional enhancement to the basic Path write-up, providing some sort of benefit (such as an activation cost discount) if you use the tools, and/or letting you use a different Ability in the Path’s dice pool. Such rules would not be presented as a necessary part of the Sorcery rules, however.

    • In keeping with the aforementioned “blind men and the elephant” analogy, I’d want to introduce rules options that tweak the Sorcery rules in ways that bring them more in line with the underlying metaphysics of games like Werewolf, Wraith, or Changeling. Werewolf-based options, for instance, would tend toward a more animistic approach to magic — say, by having most or all Paths operate off of Social Attributes rather than Mental ones, or somehow tying into Gnosis. A Changeling-based option might present Sorcery as something that’s opposed by Banality and/or concealed by the Mists, or might introduce some sort of Realms-like enhancement similar to how the Mage-based options include an Aspects-like enhancement. And so on.

    Thoughts?

      I don't think Sorcerer could fill a slot as a 20th core book. So any issue with it competing with other books is lost on me. 20th supplements have been released both via KS and under the normal supplement design process that Onyx Path uses. The DAV20 and Wr20 didn't stop W20 Umbra from coming out. Sorcerer 20 is in the same boat as the Umbra, not a major core book; in terms of viability.

      And if we're getting Sorecer 20th edition book, I don't want WOD: Sorcerer with a newer shine. The 20th books are best when they include all of the source material and gather it together for us combined with some tweaking some of the more broken rules; and not just the rules.

      That means is should be a M20 supplement. That means Awakened mage organizations should get full coverage (and for M20 that means everyone, not just the Traditions) as well as Sorcerer specific organizations (something Sorcerer Revised included in as much if not more detail than they did when discussing the Traditions and Conventions; even if less detail than WOD: Sorcerer). 2/3 books that cover Sorcerers are Mage books, even if you don't like it. The majority of sorcerers are either affiliated with Mage based organizations, or their own, and M20 is already stated as having a stronger support for Crafts. This makes supporting sorcerer specific organizations the same as M20 supporting more Crafts.

      This also means Psychics should be in. Rites of the Blood for V20 got ~40 more pages than WOD: Sorcerer, or Sorcerer Revised. That's more then enough to include expanded details on Sorcerer organizations (both with existing Mage groups and their own unique ones) without needing to cut Psychics for a second book. The Faces of Sorcery chapter of WOD:S is only 30 pages. The TB: Dreamspealer Rev. Static Shaman entry is under 2 pages. Sorcerer Rev. had just over 10 pages already allotted to Sorcerer unique organizations. So you've talked about adding ~21 pages of material to Sorcerer Revised's length. Using up just half of the extra space it would get by being expanded to the same size as Rites of the Blood. There's no reason to be stingy here.

      There's also no need to have explanations for Numina for all the game lines. The idea that hedge mages might be using their powers as focused through their Avatars is not really presented as the absolute truth of how Numina exist (and there's a reason why there's a Merit for proto-mages rather than it being the base-line assumption). Werewolves and changelings can explain Numina however they want, just like mages can. We don't need lots of attention on crossover stuff because most characters in most of the game lines aren't mystic theory junkies like mages are. Werewolves don't know about true vs. hedge magic, they can't really tell the difference and explaining it to them is most academic towards how the Garou are presented as feeling about human magics. The same goes for the fae. The existing books have book far less word count into this topic than you have in these threads.

      Trying to have a whole set of Sorcery rules tweaks for different game line metaphysics is also just flat out unnecessary. There don't need to be Gnosis based Numina, or Glamour powered Psychics. How are these any better than, "Sorcerers are proto-mages" instead of them being their own thing?

          From a completely external perspective, I don't think it would fly as an independent product. There isn't a core rabid Sorcerer fan base like there is for Wraith or Changeling. There doesn't even appear to be one to the degree of HtR or Demon. From the tenor of comments I've seen, it's about as popular as, say, Mummy. I think we'll see people lining up to buy Kindred of the East 20+ before we see people putting money down for a Sorcerer20 core book kicking off a game line Kickstarter or the like.

          Second, What does Sorcerer add to the WoD overall? As a major line it falls into something along the lines of re-treading the same thematic ground that Mage does. Again, looking in from outside, Vampire is about bloodsucking nighwalkers, Werewolf is about people who get big, hairy and dangerous (and also are the bloody claw of the mutant lovechild of Greenpeace and animism), Wraith is what immaterial dead people get up to after the fact, Changeling is about the urban fantasy trope of what happens when you add fae to modern life,, etc. Mage is about people who can do magic. Sorcerer would also be about people who do magic. It's re-treading the same thematic ground. Since it covers the same themes, it naturally falls in with M20 as far as product placement goes. I don't see how it would be done any other way in non-in-game-context-$$$-sense.

          Here's where I will have my schizoid break:

          Having said that about Sorcerer20's viability as something independent, there is something a little unwieldly about having Mage mages being the exemplars of what the "people who do magic" in the WoD. The interface between themes is clunky, at best. The games are tied to a reality consistent with their theme. Those realities have internal rules and those rules are what shapes how whole situation works. Mage is tied to a mutable reality, in fact, inherently, to reality-BREAKING. That makes interactions dicey. In VtR, vampires can't stop being Vampires without some inherently mystical, earth-shattering secret thing with no known rules. It's a case by case thing with all of the cases lining up with "God says that's okay, but it's a one-time thing for this particular situation." With Mage it becomes more of a question of which of a 1000 way of creatively applying the rules would work or not work. Or setting up something pretty much arbitrary and saying "Your magick doesn't work... because it doesn't. Vampirism is beyond Mages. They cannot create nor destroy it. Just 'cause." Any explanation boils down pretty much to that, as there are many many other things that Mages patently CAN do (and have done) to the underlying rules of reality that really make turning Jill back into a human look easy-peasy, say, like making the Earth revolve around the Sun instead of vice-versa and getting the vast majority of people to buy into it.

          So, yes, I really do want some kind of Sorcerer rules. I see a real need for them for the games OTHER than Mage. I think it would be easier to have Sorcerer out there to allow for magic workers that might be more thematically appropriate to their settings. And it seems like it would be easier to take those kind of rules and port them over to each of the other scenes with minimal tweaking needed, much as you have described above.

          Another break:

          For Mage, I really have issues with the 1:3 ratio of Sorcerer:Mage that gets thrown out there. Are there a lot of games out there where a four person cabal is 3 Sorcerers to 1 Mage? If not, are all PC cabals a collection of special snow flakes? The rules are very fluid and somewhat opaque about how Mages of different paradigms work together, how easy (or more appropriately "how hard") is it for Sorcerers with much more rigid paradigms to do so? Why NOT have all magic workers in Mage be Mages? It doesn't seem to be a problem for MageA and it wasn't one for Ars Magica. And it seems like the default of the game Mage is that people are playing Mages, not Sorcerers and, , in most games as played and across the material published, there aren't a lot of Sorcerer's being used or presented. Certainly not 3 to 1. I'm not going to go through all the Mage cabals and chantries and adventures and setting books to count it all up, but I'm pretty sure that the numbers would bear me out on this. So, I wouldn't be too unhappy to see Sorcerers as a concept get downplayed down a lot in M20. Leave Mage to Mages. Sorcerers could be more structured to fulfill the same role that Enchanted do in Changeling, Ghouls do in Vampire, etc.

          And, yup, I do see the dichotomies there. I do want something Sorcerer out there for all the games to use where Sorcerer magic looks a helluva like what Thuamaturgy or Necromancy in Vampire, even though I think it won't fly unless it's a M20 book and I don't want those kind of Sorcerers in M20. (If they are there, I definitely don't want them to be the dark matter of the mage universe... out there, making all the difference in some hard to understand way.., but mostly invisible and not having any readily perceptible effect except in a very limited way.)

                      A little history here: as I alluded to in my first post, there has always been a dichotomy concerning the role of Hedge Magic in the original World of Darkness. When it first appeared (in its admittedly most primitive form) in Hunters Hunted, its role was to empower mortals to be dangerous from the standpoint of vampires. Ascension’s Right Hand presented them as Custos — non-mages who assist mages. Each of these views got further support later on: the Hunters Hunted perspective was revisited in several of the Year of the Ally supplements (Inquisition reprinted a few Paths with thematically appropriate name changes and introduced a few new Paths; Halls of the Arcanum introduced an Alchemy Path; The Quick and the Dead featured several ghosthunting groups that weilded Hedge Magic), then in the first edition of Sorcerer (which, among other things, collected all of the Paths thus far published into one place; and in the case of the new Paths introduced in Inquisition, renamed them), and most recently in Hunters Hunted 2. The “custos” perspective got revisited in Sorcerer Revised.

                      It’s worth noting that the 1:3 ratio thing is a Mage Revised thing — or, more specifically, a Sorcerer Revised thing — and Satyros has said that it will not be the default assumption in M20: the game will proceed on the assumption that most Mage organizations consist primarily of mages.

                      I would not object to a M20 Ascension’s Right Hand book that is to the original, first edition supplement as Hunters Hunted 2 is to the original Hunters Hunted; that would be the sort of book that Heavy Arms appears to be advocating. But that should not be what a 20th Anniversary edition of Sorcerer should be; Sorcerer 20 should take the broader perspective that the 1997 edition of Sorcerer had, of sorcerers as individuals who are not tied to or subservient to any one gameline. As presented in Sorcerer, they might occasionally be allies or enemies of mages; but no more so than they are allies or enemies of vampires, werewolves, wraiths, or changelings.
