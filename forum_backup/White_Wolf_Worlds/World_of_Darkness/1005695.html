    Examples of Time 1

    11-20-2016, 10:13 AM
    So, going through the sphere descriptions for M20 and comparing it to previous editions and it got me thinking, what exactly can you accomplish with just Time 1 and no other sphere ranks?

    I get that you gain a really accurate clock/timer/chronometer "in your head" and that you can detect and analyze temporal distortions, but it seems that is really limited compared to other rank 1 spheres, so I am asking the hive mind...

    What else could a mage, assuming _any and/or all_ aspects/situations/circumstances were favorable, do with just Time 1 and no other spheres?

    Here is an example I think could work. Assuming that a mage shares their temporal sense with other (and thus both mage and target would suffer) would intentionally mucking with one's natural sense of the passage of time work? If so, this could be used from making someone late to an important meeting.

      I had a small tirade over Time 1 on another post some time ago, so I'm just going to copy paste that here, I hope that's ok.

      Time 1 is the single most underrated sphere stat in the game. Time 1 doesn't just let you know what time it is, it gives you Perfect Time Sense, you can perfectly intuit how long things take and how much time you need to do things. You know exactly when you need to get things done before the bus leaves and never miss a bus. You know exactly how much time has passed since you arrived somewhere, so no disorientation for you. You know exactly what time it is no matter where or when you are. Accidental time travel? No reading newspapers to determine the date for you, that's already covered. Captured and taken hostage for hours on end? You know exactly how long they've kept you, how long you've been travelling and how long you've been stationary, information you might need when considering an escape strategy. How about doing complicated procedures? With perfect Time Sense, you can do about 10 or so at once staggered from one another and never get a single one out of sync.

      With enough practice, you know exactly how long it takes to get from one place to another on various methods, and can plan your day down to the millisecond with perfect precision. No moment is wasted, and nothing is left to chance. Combined with Entropy 1, this becomes insane! You basically become Batman levels of prepared.

      Mixed with other spheres, you can make on going effects stop at precisely the time you intend them to, no more "stop at the end of a scene, or the day" now it's "Stop precisely at 3pm tomorrow" or "Stop after exactly 15 hours". You can make Corr. gates which change location at specific times of day, Force bound temperatures that fluctuate between hot and cold depending on whether it's day or night, Spirit gates which only open for one hour at midnight. Guns that morph into gloves during the day... Any effect that you want to have multiple steps to it based on time delays needs Time 1 to pull of. Plus, with Time 1 you can sense all the temporal elements of other mage's effects. So if you come across these Time sensitive gates or items, you may not know what will happen, but you'll certainly know when it will.

      Also, what is the one sphere that when used, no matter how well you've planned, how much you've achieved, how many things you've accomplished, all of that can be rendered moot simply by making it all not happen? Time! What's the one point sphere that actually clues you in to when this kind of stuff is happening? Time 1! Think about it. You're in a battle and everything you throw at them, they seem to be ready for it. Any ambush, any surprise, any secret, they can react to it without a moment's hesitation. The truth is that they're using Time magic to change things to happen their way, and there is no way you could possibly know about this without Time 1. Knowing that mages are messing around with the time stream is impossible without this sphere. You don't know people are spying on you from across time, that they keep undoing your work and sabotaging your attempts before you even get a chance to pull them out of the bag. Time 1 is the only thing that might clue you in to what's going on. Without that, you're pretty much sunk.

                  With perfect sense of the passage of time, you become very good at music, dance, and other performances. It's like being a human metronome. Storytellers should give players difficulty reductions for such rolls when their Time 1 sense is up.

                  Having a good grasp of time makes certain physical feats - like stunt driving or leading gunshots - similarly easier. You know that if something is moving at a constant velocity, when the exact optimal moment to act would be.

                  Even just having Time 1 (and not using it) could aid with certain Perception rolls. In Mage: The Sorcerer's Crusade, in the Order of Reason supplement, they introduced the idea of "Alertness, Awareness, or Arete?". Basically, that just having the first rank of a Sphere gives a Mage uncanny insight into subjects without rolling Arete, so long as there's a way for the character to gather said information with their mundane senses (even if that mundane sense is Awareness of supernatural presences). A person with Matter 1 or Life 1, for instance, might be much better at detecting poison in a drink than someone without them, just from rolling Perception + Alertness.

                  Time 1 would afford the character a greater ability to determine how time has passed, relative to specific parts of the environment. It could conceivably work with Abilities other than Alertness or Awareness. With a little Medicine or Science, a Mage with Time 1 could roll them with Perception or Wits to determine how long a body has been decomposing. Science or Technology would permit them to see how long a bicycle was left outside, exposed to the elements, by the signs of visible oxidation and its mechanical features. A psychologist or therapist could use Science (Psychology) or Empathy to see how long a patient or other person has lived with certain traumas.

                              I don't recall arguing that Time 1 was unique in this regard. Though it suffers the worst of all of the level 1 Spheres.                                   Time 1 allows you more perception than you'd think. You can see ebbs and flows of the Timestream, this means you can see when time has been screwed around with, as well as areas where time is distorted. You'll see the trails of time travel, the residues of sped up or slowed down pockets of reality. You'll be able to see bubbles of Time affected realms within the Umbra. If Time magic has been utilised on anything at all, you'll know about it and be able to read when the trigger is meant to spring. This means being able to see things like Timed portals, Junctures, Temporary anchorheads and shallowings, as well as any kind of amulet or contingency spell, which would otherwise be invisible.

                                  You can say the same thing for every sphere.

                                  Corr 1: Without a map to explore or portals to feel out, it's basically just "Where am I now?"
                                  Entropy 1: Without a planned out story or fleshed out destinies, all you have is "was that random?" or "how weak is it?"
                                  Forces: 1: Without a proper understanding of physics or mystic energies, the scope of this sphere is whittled down to "Do I see light? How hot is it?"
                                  Life 1: Without thinking about the biology of the things the PCs may encounter, all you really have is "is it alive?"
                                  Matter 1: Without a comprehensive knowledge of materials, the scope is shrunk down to the level of "metal, plastic, wood, or brick?"
                                  Mind 1: Without an understanding of Resonance or Auras, you're pretty much limited to "sense emotion".
                                  Prime 1: Without nodes and junctures, you've only really got "is Magic happening?"

                                  At some level, all the spheres are as effective as the ST is at providing for them.

                                  Ok, so you don't know where you are, but with Time 1 you can get a sense of your captives schedule, as well as intuit the correct rhythm to step in order to match their footsteps, walking behind them without them hearing you sneak away. What would Corr 1 give you? An incomplete map of the room. Certainly useful, but without Matter you can't look past doors without extra difficulty, without Life or Mind you can't sense the presence of guards, and without Forces you can't tell how quickly anyone is moving or whether there are any moving parts, or sounds etc. The map is good, but not great, just as good as having perfect rhythm and schedule knowledge.

                                                          Heavy Arms The problem i have with your interpretation of Corr vs your interpretation of Time is that you claim Time 1 requires other spheres to do anything outside of its two given rotes, while Corr 1 can do anything without need of help. There's a contradiction in place, because if you state that Corr is able to sense all other patterns without needing their sphere, but Time isn't capable of sensing time based magics like timed portals, Amulets, or similarly programmed events without other spheres, then you are giving Corr an inherent bias.
                                                          If you want to rely on "that's what the book says, therefor it right" then you do the whole sphere system a disservice.

                                                          At its core, the sphere system is meant to be a level of guidelines to give you a rough idea on how to approach their use in magic. It is the limits of our ingenuity as well as our ability to see the patterns presented by both our mages paradigm and the spheres convention that should dictate what is and isn't possible, not just "well if you look at page 39, it clearly says it's only thing".

                                                          I had a game where an ST allowed Time 4/Entropy 1 to be used to "save your game", basically making a reference point in time which you'd jump back to in the event of the players death. The idea was that they imagined time as a stream, and Time 4 allowed you to bend that stream around things or places, so the logical conclusion is that you could bend it so far that it actually meets itself, like a piece of string making a knot.

                                                          No where is that in the book, not even under Time 5. Instead Time 5 would cut through that process of having to prepare a "checkpoint" before hand, and just allow you to jump without need of worry.

                                                          Similarly, I've had a game where someone was able to change their hair colour with Life 2. If you go RAW then only Life 3 should allow "alter self" type effects, but if we're going to lump "giving myself claws" with "changing my hair from red to blond" and say "sorry, you may be able to manipulate plants, but your hair colour is just too advanced for you" doesn't that get a little silly?

                                                          If your main argument against doing new and interesting thing with the spheres is "the book doesn't say anything about that" then you're basically treating the game like so many over List based magic systems in which you need a defined rote before an effect can take place.

                                                          We could, for example, say a good Time 1 effect should give you an extra success or a reduced difficulty on Dexterity based things, such as dodging or fighting because you can feel the perfect time to move and can sense the pattern in your enemy's fire, but if you say "book doesn't talk about that, so no" then of course it's not going to fly.

                                                          The rotes are meant to be examples and ideas from which to build from, not hammered down "this is how one does magic". The descriptions of the spheres gives you an idea, a feel for how the relative powers of the spheres would work. Yes there are many different ways to approach a given solution, but that does not mean other methods are suddenly invalid. To say "you can use mind to do all the Time 1 stuff" doesn't suddenly mean that Time 1 can't do this stuff or that Mind 1 is better at doing this stuff than Time 1. Such exclusive thinking can only harm ones creativity as it denies the possibility of the unexpected.

                                                          If you're playing a game in which every spell needs to be taken from a source or double checked with HDYDT, then I'd say you're being far too rigid in your approach to this game.
