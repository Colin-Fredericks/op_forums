                    The Academy of St. Gabriel (https://www.s-gabriel.org/names/index.shtml) is a repository of data regarding historical names used by the Society for Creative Anachronism. Since they require that a name be documented at some point in history, this was a group of Name Heralds who compiled historical information on naming conventions in the period covered by the SCA (5-16th Centuries). It can be a fantastic resource for finding names for elders and methuselahs.

                                Those are their Magi names, adopted upon being acepted as full-fledged members of the House of Tremere of the Order of Hermes, like it was pretty much the norm for any character following the setting conventions of Ars Magica?

                                Kind of came naturally with the terrain to me, but maybe that's because i'm an old fart from before most of these links being shaved off and shoehorned in some version or other of Mage.

                                Grimgroth was a little nod to a forgotten, forsaken even, past, that somehow keeps on persisting and even being reused, all notion of incongruity involved utterly missed.

                                Goratrix might not be "a straight from the other game" port like Grim, but was definitely made with the "mix historical with some fantasy fancy/nonsense" naming conventions of the Auld Game in mind. Much like Meerlinda, Etrius & Abetorius, for that matter.
                                For me this always served also as a stealth hint of the age tiers of each councillor, separating the once mages turned vampires from those actually embraced into a full-fledged Tremere vampire clan, where such "shadow name" trappings were little more than relics.
