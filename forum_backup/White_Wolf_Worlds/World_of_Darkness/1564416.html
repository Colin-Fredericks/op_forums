      To be honest I'm not sure I udnerstand the question, but I hope I can answer

      In the World of Darkness vampires do not have a wide array of specialized anti-Fae gear because the two factions rarely interact.
      However, when a conflict does occur, vampires "rely" on a mix of mortal Banality, physical Disciplines, and Cold Iron.

      In WoD the Ultimate Anti-Fae Weapon is Cold Iron
      Pure, hand-forged iron completely bypasses all Faerie magical defenses and inflicts aggravated damage on Changelings.
      But "Cold Iron" is a bit murky, many say 'hand forged", - It must be created through traditional, low-tech methods (like a charcoal fire and manual hammering) rather than modern blast furnaces. In some interpretations, it includes meteoric iron that hasn't been heated by human agency - other sources say true cold iron must never be heated by human hands.

      Fae magic (Chimerical realities and Glamour) can easily trick a vampire's mind, but high-level sensory Disciplines can defend against them.
      Auspex Level 2 (Aura Perception) is a vampire's primary defensive tool to identify a Fae creature. It reveals the vibrant, ever-shifting nature of a Changeling's aura compared to the pale, stagnant aura of a mortal or the dead aura of a vampire.
      Mytherceria Level 1 (Folderol) allows a vampire to immediately detect any active Fae illusions or lies.
      (Vampire: The Masquerade 20th Anniversary Edition, p. 135 (Auspex rules) and Lore of the Bloodlines, p. 116 (Mytherceria rules).)
      Chimerstry illusion also "real" to the Changleings and the Fae - and lethal sometimes

      Because Changelings still possess physical, living bodies, they are vulnerable to a vampire's mental attacks, giving Kindred a major defensive edge if they strike first.​​
      Vampires can use Dominate or Presence to completely override a Fae's mind or force them to flee
      However, because Fae possess alien minds, a vampire must usually spend 1 Willpower point to successfully initiate mind-control disciplines against them. (This specific crossover ruling is detailed in World of Darkness: Outcasts (A Companion for Changeling, Werewolf, and Vampire) and reaffirmed in the Storyteller Companion crossover addendums.)​

      The greatest passive protection a vampire has is simply existing. Vampires are creatures of stasis, death, and routine. In the World of Darkness, they are walking, sentient anchors of Banality.
      Prolonged exposure to a vampire actively chokes out a Changeling’s Glamour, triggering the "Undoings" or forcing them into "Bedlam" (madness). Fae will actively avoid vampires because standing near one feels like suffocating. (Changeling: The Dreaming 20th Anniversary Edition, Chapter One (The Nature of Banality). )
      Tho Malkavians, Maeghar and Kisayd a bit exception... sometimes.... a bit complicated

      Thaumaturgy does feature specialized Wards and Warding Circles designed specifically to block and punish the Fae.
      Ward Versus Fae (Level 3 or 4 Ritual) This ritual allows a thaumaturge to enchant an object, space, or threshold (like a window, doorway, or weapon) to act as a lethal barrier against Fae entities (Detailed explicitly in Blood Magic: Secrets of Thaumaturgy, p. 95. Depending on the edition or specific printing, it is sometimes categorized as a Level 3 or Level 4 ritual)
      While a basic Ward protects a single discrete object or surface (like a doorknob), a Warding Circle secures an entire fixed geographic area​ - Warding Circle Versus Fae (Level 4 or 5 Ritual) (Appears in the compiled list of specialized circles in Vampire: The Masquerade 20th Anniversary Edition (V20), pp. 234–235)

      If your Storyteller allows older Revised/Second edition paths, there is an entire primary path of Tremere magic dedicated to security called the Path of Warding
      At lower levels, it alerts the caster if a supernatural entity bypasses a boundary. At higher levels, it physically locks down space or inflicts variable damage based on how many successes the caster rolled. (Originally featured in early Sabbat and Tremere player handbooks.)

      I have foggy memories about Akhu - Settie Sorcery have something similar protection, but not sure about that one

              As a rule of thumb vampires don't know shit about the Fae and have absolutely nothing to use against them specifically. You can use the Occultism Knowledge to remember some folk practices and the likelihood of them working, but is a wild guess at best.

              That's your main answer. As a vampire you're not prepared to deal with them, full stop, unless you specifically have Lore (Fae) or similar as an Ability.

              IF you have those, then it depends on how your ST runs Fae in Vampire. You'll still not have any tool designed to deal with them, but that will say what works in general, which is great info in itself.

              Cold Iron is a reasonable guess in English speaking countries, but there's no fixed definition of what's Cold Iron. It's not just that the ST has to decide, but the characters themselves would be aware of the ambiguity rather than the answer going only with Occult.

                                                      Actually hunting a "Little God" is highly frowned upon and considered a dangerous taboo within mainstream Kuei-jin society - also carries massive social and spiritual consequences
                                                      Hunting down and cannibalizing true celestial bureaucrats and minor deities—even exiled ones like the Hsien—is seen as a massive step backward into corruption in every Dharma
                                                      ...not to mention the Hsien might be trapped in mortal bodies, but they are still ancient elemental spirits/celestial bureaucrats capable of wielding incredibly potent Wu Tan magic. A Kuei-jin attempting to hunt even a lesser Shinma risks facing the collective wrath of an entire Hsien Court, who hold a deep, thousands-of-years-old grudge against the Hungry Dead for ruining the Fourth Age already...

In the Middle Kingdom a Kuei-Jin Court co-exist with the Hsien Court, the Sprit Courts of the Yang World, potnetially a local necropolis of the Yin World, be either the Court of the Dark Kingdom of Jade or locals who resist the Jade Emperor and even the Hengeyokai local beats court, with emsiasaries and ambassadors all around

                                                      No, not really

                                                      The 1st edition ST guide mentions the Shadow Court has secret, high-placed allies within the Sabbat, specifically referencing the Kiasyd and that Unseelie Changelings occasionally make pacts with Kiasyd scholars to trade forbidden magical secrets for protection or political leverage against the Seelie Courts
                                                      The enigmatic, 6th-generation Kiasyd known only as The Arcadian from Lore of the Bloodlines and the Changeling Storytellers Guide is canonized as having deep knowledge regarding the inner workings of both the Fae and Kindred politics.
