    Hunter: the Reckoning vs Vigil-type hunters in the oWoD

    12-18-2015, 09:19 AM
    I know that everyone is interested in the CoD, and that oWoD is not really subject to revisions (beyond minor flourishes that can be squeezed into a 20th Anniversary edition), but there is one aspect of the oWoD I feel needs a retouch: Hunters. Where to start? I love CoD's Hunter: the Vigil, and feel that there are elements of oWoD hunter organizations that could fit Vigil-style in an oWoD compilation of monster hunters.

    I feel that Vigil got everything right both in storyteller and a mechanics level. From the different tiers to the ways in which mortals get involved in monster hunting to the Endowments of the different conspiracies and how they get them. Everything, just everything felt right, made sense, and fit. Hunter: the Reckoning, on the other hand was the exact opposite. Everything about Imbued hunters felt awkward and clunky both from a storytelling perspective to a game mechanics perspective. The Heralds were too much of a Deus Ex Machina, some of the hunter splats didn't make sense (Innocents anyone?), the Edges were nerfed compared to the monsters that you hunted, the heirarchy of the Edges didn't make sense, and the way that you gained Conviction by gambling Conviction, particularly to go up in Virtue....it just didn't work. Add to that a lack of folklore concerning Imbued hunters and it just seemed out of place.

    That being said, there are already Vigil-like elements in the oWoD that could be used to make an anniversary book on oWoD monster hunters. Demon Hunter X gives us the Shih and the Technocracy-controlled Strike Force Zero, The Inquisition gives us the Society of Leopold and drops hints of other hunter organizations around the world that use Faith and/or Theurgy as their weapons, and there is even an Exorcist book detailing Demon (the Fallen) hunters. The Technocracy even plies its hand at monster hunting from time to time. I think there is enough material here to compile a comprehensive volume of monster hunters in the oWoD. One that may or may not leave out the Imbued.

      Yes, the last paragraph is on point. "Vigil-style" is what we would have originally called "The Hunter's Hunted". Reckoning is a very different game, meant to suddenly fling normal people who had been unquestioning of their lives into the deep end to see if they would sink or swim in a world full of monsters hiding their existences from public knowledge. Think They Live, but with an effect that can't be stolen or broken like the sunglasses, and instead of aliens, you're seeing shadows, and possibly even more weird details if you have the edges to differentiate between monsters.

      Edit: And the powers are kind of like the ones that Contractors would get in the closer to contemporary (and very much after the endgame runs for the lines) Darker Than Black. Usually, however, the triggers aren't quite as onerous.

          cWOD non-Imbued hunters already got an X20 book: Hunters Hunted II (though it doesn't cover all of them).

          On top of almost all the existing source material on them.

          Reckoning has made in the context of needing something that wasn't HH or HtV to bother making a full book about them. Otherwise HtR would have just been a collection of hunters from earlier books; not a full game line on its own.

          I've also run plenty of HtR, and I think your analysis of it is a bit biased. I'm not saying it's a game for you, if HtV is your deal, and the cWOD hunter groups that fit more into its style are what you want, game on. But the Heralds aren't really Deus ex Machina once you get into the lore of the game, some of the Creeds are hard to grok but none of them fail to make sense (if you want a perfect example of an Innocent in pop culture, Xander from Buffy the Vampire Slayer is one of my top choices), the Edges are what they are as HtR expects you to use its present monster creation rules to customize what you need (just like HtV does) rather than use the other cWOD supernaturals and you're supposed to be weaker than monsters so either lucky or smarts are your best weapons, and you can get Conviction in ways other than gambling.

          All in all, it sounds far more like you didn't really get into HtR's conceptual goal. Which is, again, fine, as it isn't something you have to get into. But a lot of what you're saying are bugs, are in fact features from the prospective of what the game is trying to do.

                  I'm a huge fan of both HtR and HtV. Comparing them isn't as helpful as it seems because they're really very different games. In the context of Vigil the hunters presented in Reckoning are always going to be playing somewhere between tier 1 and tier 2 in terms of scope, with the one addition of a fairly hefty Endowment.

                  I'm not really sure what you mean by the Heralds being a Deus ex Machina. I'll grant you that there always felt like a lot of overlap between Innocents and Redeemers, and that making Conviction simultaneously the thing you had to spend to use your Edges and the thing you had to save up to buy more Edges was probably a less than ideal design choice. The power level of Edges is spot on though. You're supposed to feel outclassed. Just powerful enough to play on the same field but nowhere near in the same league. Each time you reach a new plateau of power you discover just how much higher the mountain goes.

                  The lack of a well defined folklore niche is also intentional. The Imbued are something new, and that should be frightening. You're on your own and the biggest mystery in this world of darkness you've discovered is exactly what you've become.

                                                      It's not a thing in Werewolf; but Inquisition expanded the eponymous organization from “we fight vampires” to “we fight vampires and other monsters”, including werewolves. Since the Inquisition's primary supernatural weapon is True Faith (no Monty Python references, please), the book addressed how True Faith interacts with all of the supernatural types that were known at the time (I think; it's possible that they overlooked changelings).

                                                      Frankly, I find it to be rather curious that the discussion has turned to True Faith, considering that the Hunter Player's Guide suggested the possibility of reskinning HtR's mechanics as an alternate system for representing True Faith — a concept that Dark Ages: Inquisitor ran with. I'm not terribly fond of the “latter days” metaplot (which kicked off with the Year of the Reckoning); but the notion of recasting the Imbued as the Faithful is something I could get behind.



                                                      Getting back to the original topic, kind of: I find myself wondering what a Hunter Anniversary Edition would look like. It's been, what, sixteen years since the Reckoning? Hunter: Utopia's notion of getting people organized can't still be a thing yet to come, and something akin to Vigil's notion of Compacts and Conspiracies ought to be in a HAE core book. Hunter: First Contact detailed early interactions between the Imbued and the protagonists of the various HH/YotH books; by now, wouldn't the Imbued be frequently found in the ranks of these orgs? Which is not to say that all or even most Imbued would be found there; it would, after all, be an Anniversary Edition, designed in large part to address HtR nostalgia. But an appendix updating the First Contact information to account for nearly two decades worth of changes would not be inappropriate, IMHO.

                                                          If I were doing H20, I would rather see the emergence of Hunters moved up to 2012 or something similar. I've played in recent-ish HtR games where the Reckoning still happened in 2000... and the game loses a lot of spark, because the feeling of being thrust into the unknown without the centuries of mystic knowledge that the other games are built on loses a lot of edge if it's been 16 years since the first Imbued emerged; and somehow nothing has really changed in that time.

                                                          Hunter-net.org could also use a significant technological update.

                                                          If I wanted to make a 500-ish page tome (because lets face it HtR doesn't exactly have that much material as-is) I think using Utopia to have some organization building rules in the H20 book would be good because it's a nice support structure for a common game-arc, and it's a good tool for STs to make up their own thing for those that want to run with the Imbued have been around for 16 years instead.

                                                          It's also the first real chance for a second look at the HtR rules, and there's lots of stuff that could use a second look, some optional dials, and so on (ex: re-examining the automatic Derangements since the hassle of tracking 12+ possible Derangements is simply too much, compared to fewer individual mental issues, but ones that get worse with time; which is also much more how it was presented with the few super-high Virtue NPCs).

                                                          I don't think H20 is a place for shoving the YotH stuff, esp. with a very divided fanbase of people that don't want HtR to get YotH.
