    Skirting Around the Werewolves

    03-20-2018, 07:15 PM
    Alright, so I'm running a Mage game that will soon be adding a couple of Uktena, Nuwisha and (very confused, time displaced) Camazotz NPC's to complicate the cabal's mission.

    Here's the issue: anyone who bothers keeping track of the opinions of forum posters knows that my opinion on WtA itself is icy on a good day, and a lot of that comes from the fact that I think it's themes are flawed on a fundamental level. But I want to have shapechangers in my game. How do I integrate Fera and other assorted WtA ephemera into my game without dragging in all the attendant baggage and accidentally establishing the Gaian worldview as the "one true reality".

                  Ah. I suppose it was a little bit pompous of me to assume that people were already familiar with my opinions. I just wanted to avoid being "that guy" on the forums that constantly trumpets one opinion very loudly and frequently, y'know?

                  Basically, I think the whole shtick with the Triat is kind of problematically reductive. For better or worse, I also kinda' enjoy a lot of the Wyrm's trappings, particularly Pentex, which is such a cartoonishly nonsensical caricature it comes off as almost endearing.

                          Well, since you don't like some of the cosmology and the Garou aren't the PC's then just let the Garou be wrong. One problem that I see way too often in fastastic setting is that people (or creatures) who have some sort of belief system about how everything works turn out to almost always be right. In real life, people are wrong about how the world works all the time. You can have your werewolves have certain viewpoints about Gaia and the Triat that aren't accurate. They are rags filled religious fanatics after all, it's not as though they objectively access all of the evidence.

                          Make the truth what ever you like, the Garou culture doesn't have to know it.

                          Or, just change what the Garou believe. It's not like you're playing W:tA and owe some allegiance to the purity of that setting. In fact, even if you were playing W:tA you could still change not only the cosmology but the also culture and religion.

                          Feel free to change as much about W:tA as you like. It might actually make things more interesting for your players who would now be as ignorant of werewolves as their characters should be. I actually think that is a good idea to change the supporting game when ever there is a crossover, otherwise the players' OOC knowledge will affect their feelings and actions regardless of how hard you try to avoid it.

                                  Originally posted by The Nilbog View Post
                                  Ah. I suppose it was a little bit pompous of me to assume that people were already familiar with my opinions. I just wanted to avoid being "that guy" on the forums that constantly trumpets one opinion very loudly and frequently, y'know?

                                  Basically, I think the whole shtick with the Triat is kind of problematically reductive. I especially don't like the way that the Weaver is said to pervade all aspects of technology and civilization, reinforcing the obnoxious "science/technology = rigid conformity" theme that Mage has spent 4 editions trying to get away from. There's also the fact that the Gaia aligned factions seem to operate on a principle of theological voluntarism that is presented as the absolute, in-canon right answer.
                                  All members of the Triat have stakes in the world, technology included, since there are Wyldlings of advanced medical care, scary stories told on the internet, social outrage, superheroes (who don't even know that they're really spirits, sometimes), and the concept of "YOLO". There are also Weaver-spirits that are benevolent or semi-benevolent, such as spirits of mass transit or the joy of seeing a movie. Definition and routines can be alright; stringent control, however, is another part of the Weaver, and the Book of the Weaver mentioned that it was often at odds with manifestations of the Weaver that had anything to do with positives we see in life. You can make the effort to accept Downtown Bus but wrestle with the manifestations of the Patriarch; a lot of urban werewolves spend their lives doing this sort of thing.

                                  The complexities of the Weaver, however, mean that a lot of tribes just won't trust spirits in league with it, and they aren't really wrong for minimizing their associations with it when there are other spirits, particularly Gaian ones such as gridlock-beetles and crowdfunding-spirits, that can accomplish the same ends. This is particularly true if those Garou know of Drones and how they come about from exposure to the OneSong; that even makes most Glass Walkers wary of potential allies among the spinner's brood. Add on the apparent uncaring nature of werespiders (assuming that you're not dealing with a Hatar that hasn't managed to stay so impersonal), and you have a lot less certainty in trying to navigate the cosmolgy of the Triat. Their manifestations are beautiful, dangerous, and seductive, even more so for not being banes trying to honey up a potential mark.

                                  As an aside, I've also typically seen Mage as being stuck far more in problematic reduction for having technology be another form of magic. However, in this case, there's a rare instance of what's good for one being good for the other, as the dividing line of the Technocracy being Pattern because they're about control over discovery and application can also kind of work for discerning different types of Weaver-spirits, assuming that you decide to use W:tA's cosmology as-is or close to it.

                                  That said, the individual Tribes and Breeds do have some compelling stuff in them. Their histories and conflicts are pretty interesting and they certainly have some neat powers and abilities to showcase when it comes time for a gonzo action scene. For better or worse, I also kinda' enjoy a lot of the Wyrm's trappings, particularly Pentex, which is such a cartoonishly nonsensical caricature it comes off as almost endearing.
                                  If Pentex is cartoonish, then we're living in a world that's awfully close to Saturday morning fair.
