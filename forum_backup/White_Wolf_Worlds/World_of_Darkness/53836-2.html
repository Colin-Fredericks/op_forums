                        Personally, I go with the notion that they are all equally true. In my opinion, Mage is the lynchpin of the whole world of darkness. The main concept of Mage is that reality is, at least in part, defined by how living conscious minds perceive it. That's the perfect in for having all of the cosmologies be equally credible. Vampires believe that the world began one way. Werewolves another. Demons believe that they helped to create the universe, and that they were righteous in their decision to interfere in the development of mankind and rebel against a distant and neglectful God. Angels, if one chooses to include them in their game in any capacity, believe that they helped to create the universe and that they were righteous in their decision to remain loyal to God and defend humanity from a prideful and reckless group of rebellious Angels who would have sought to rule over humanity as mad tyrants. All of these points of view are equally valid. All of these creation myths and ancient cosmologies have a role to play in the future of the world of darkness. They're all exactly as true as the characters and the storyteller need them to be, because magic is just works that way. The general consensus is that the universe was created via the big bang, with some vaguely Christian trappings and notions thrown on top of that by the majority of people of moderate faith. So that's the primary way everything came to be. But all of these mystical shadow histories exist just behind the veil, all metaphysical echoes of one another filtered through the perception and nature of their adherents, and they can all play apart in your story if you feel the need.

                        At least, that's what works for me. I grew up on superhero comics, and that's how they usually handle myth and religion in their fictional universes, so it's the easiest for me to wrap my head around.

                            Originally posted by rderekp View Post
                            So, in the classic World of Darkness, there are many competing world-views as to what the truth of the world and cosmology may be. Is the Vampire myth the “right one”? The Triat? God as presented in Demon and Hunter? My question is, do you all decide what is “the truth” is in your world before you run it, regardless of whether or not your players will be interacting with the world on that kind of level?
                            The closest that the classic World of Darkness had to a Universal Truth was the Metaphysic Trinity of Dynamism, Stasis, Entropy, but the World of Darkness was too haphazardly created to implement any idea ubiquitously or even consistently.

                            The mage's Avatar Essences (Dynamic, Pattern, Primordial) correlate to the Metaphysic Trinity (with a fourth balancing Essence, Questing, being the middleground of the triangle), and the three antagonist factions of the Mage game (Marauders, Technocrats, Nephandi) also correspond to the Metaphysic Trinity. (Extremism=Imbalance; Moderation=Balance.) The werewolves' Triat of Wyld, Weaver, Wyrm is just a theistic version of the mages' atheistic Metaphysic Trinity. Apophis is the name that mummies (and Setites) give to the Wyrm, and "Grandma" ("Grand Maw") is the name that wraiths (and Nagaraja) give to the Wyrm. The changelings are spawns of Dynamism/Wyld. The three games which heavily employ Abrahamic mythology, Vampire, Demon, and Hunter, are the conceptual deviants here, but there is a Werewolf(?) book which suggests that Cain's immortality came from the Weaver (Vishnu the Preserver), and when Cain was abandoned by the Weaver he was then taken in by the Wyrm (possibly via a Wyrmish agent). This implies that the authoritarian Abrahamic god is either the authoritarian Weaver or an authoritarian spawn of the Weaver.

                            Admittedly even the portions of the mythologies which correlate strongly are still surrounded by numerous contradictory details, but there is no other concept in the classic World of Darkness that comes as close to a Universal Truth as the Metaphysic Trinity.

                            White Wolf's biggest mistake, apart from not ubiquitously and consistently implementing the Metaphysic Trinity, was hastily cramming the various supernatural populations onto a single planet and then clumsily backpedaling to erect and maintain extremely contrived separations that strain credibility. White Wolf should have either consistently utilized the Metaphysic Trinity as a background tenet for knitting all of the games into a cohesive whole (even if not done in a readily obvious way) or they should have utilized a multiple parallel Earths cosmology to sanely keep the games separate; instead they blindly took a middle road which resulted in a sloppily conceived world the whole of which can only be held in a schizophrenic mind. (In another post I will offer my resolution to the contradiction of having such powerhouses as the Methuselahs, Maruaders, Technocracy, and Pentex all on the same planet yet somehow incredulously not stepping on each others's toes way more often.)

                            Despite my complaints about the schizophrenia of the classic World of Darkness, the Triat is my favorite pantheon of any fictional universe.
