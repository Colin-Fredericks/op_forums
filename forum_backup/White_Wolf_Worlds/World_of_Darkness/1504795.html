      And my slightly tweaked internal factions within the Dreamspeakers Tradition.

      The Dreamspeakers are, at their core, shaman. There really is no exact English word to describe what they are and do, so the closest thing is the English adaption of a German adaption (schamane) of a Russian adaption (sha'man) of a Tungus term (saman). And that in it self may or may not come from a Chinese term for a type of Buddhist monk. Language is complicated and sometimes weird. Spirit-Talker may be the best generic term can hope to come up with. Anything else tends to end up referring to a specific culture and its practices, which may not really apply to the individual Dreamspeaker in question. Anyway, the material and spiritual worlds are inherently and intimately intertwined, with anything happening in one impacting the other. They are specialists in bridging those two worlds, in maintaining the proper connections between the two, and protecting both sides from harms, inadvertent or deliberate, caused by the other. The world is in desperate need of a spiritual reawakening of the land and its people to redefine Reality and bring both worlds into proper harmony with each other.

      Barutabana (Baruti is the singular) - Lorekeepers and storytellers working to preserve the collective wisdom of human history and legend. Named after one of the core groups of the Great House of Stone in medieval Africa, they keep alive the dynamic arts of story telling and teaching, hoping to impart their knowledge and wisdom to the new generations. (Curiously, baruti is also the Swahili word for gunpowder, and more than a few have given their listeners an explosive spiritual awakening.)
      • The Dreamsong - Keepers of the oldest of the old stories and songs, those innately tied to the creation of the world and vital to the preservation of future. Many of these stories are powerful enough to warp or even shatter reality if used improperly. As such, members often keep their identities a closely guarded secret from outsiders, and pass on the true versions of their stories only to fellow initiates.
      • The Hero's Journey - Modern adventurers and folk heroes who study, channel, and live out the stories of great cultural heroes of legend and folklore. Their actions keep these stories alive and help forge new legends of the modern age.
      • The Painted Horses - Entertainers and performers involved with underground and counterculture music and theater scenes. They strive to keep alive endangered music styles and other arts, especial folk ones, by exposing them to new audiences.
      • The Shadow Play - Storytellers who collect tales of horror and parables warning of the consequences of wickedness. Moralists and prophets, they are able to turn their the spiritual power of fear into terrible weapons against their enemies.
      • Njia Panda - (from the Swahili for "crossroads") Traveling collectors and sharers of cultural lore from across the globe. Members compare and contrast different stories and customs in a quest for clues to the divine origins and spiritual truths of human history and culture, and in doing so create a greater unity among human kind.
      • Voices of Harmony - An offshoot of the Njia Panda, they are diplomats working to improve relations between the Dreamspeakers and other Traditions, as well as among various mundane groups. Members advocate mutual respect, peaceful coexistence, and multiculturalism, working as teachers, apologists, and negotiators.
      • Gutenberg's Children - Artists and collectors who use counterculture outlets to publish their works as mass media. Their novels, manga, albums, and other mediums blend powerful mental and spiritual effects with classic and cutting edge creativity.
      (Concepts: Collector of cursed objects; Eccentric schoolgirl manga artist; Documentary filmmaker on a mission; Modern day monster slayer; Blues musician charged with keeping part of the song of Judgment Day; Vaudeville revivalist; Spiritual guide to a traveling carnival; Song-chasing musical anthropologist; Mediator promoting inter-Tradition cooperation; Host of the Grand Guignol)

      The Ghost Wheel Society - Modernists with a strong rebellious streak. The society works to explore the constantly evolving spirit landscapes of the modern industrialized world. They take a special interest in many of the fringe cultures on the edge of society, looking for spirits and wisdom in places others may overlook.
      • The Contraries - Devil's advocates and eternal questioners. They use debate, riddles, puzzles, and deliberately outrageous or shocking behavior to challenge the preconceptions and expectations of others. This is done with the purpose of confronting group-think and encouraging people - including themselves - to think in new and interesting ways.
      • The Brotherhood of the Fox - A fellowship of tricksters, thieves, con artists and troublemakers. Members master the the social arts (including Ars Cupidae) and subtle mysteries while living as Bohemian artists and cultural eccentrics. The current generation has taken an increasing interest in computers and internet spirits, including the Digital Web.
      • The Men of Letters - Deeply buried in the worlds of academia, business, and government, they are shaman of bureaucracy. Passing as mundane intellectuals, they secretly investigate the spiritual power of words and written language, turning the very spirits of such concepts against the Technocracy.
      • The Melting Pot - Somewhat controversial, especially among more traditionalist Dreamspeakers, members advocate for the mixing and blending of spiritual rituals and beliefs from multiple cultures that co-exist in the modern world. They try to take what is best suited for the task at hand while maintaining respect for the cultures in question.
      • The Skywatchers - Somewhat obsessive searchers for extraterrestrial and extrasolar lifeforms, be they spiritual or otherwise. They mix the traditional Spirit-Talker arts with modern astronomy, astrophysics, and various fringe beliefs in an effort to contact and understand truly alien spirits.
      (Concepts: Femme fatal cat burglar; Deep cover double agent within the Technocracy; UFO chasing amateur astronomer; Neo-theurgist computer mambo; Professional trouble maker; Shaman of the language spirits; Token FBI "Indian"; Professional gambler; Gonzo journalist; Radically inventive shaman of the inner city)

      Keepers of the Sacred Fire - The most formal, ritualized, and traditionalist of the Dreamspeakers. Some trace their origins back to the original fire/hearth goddess of Central Asia, with the group's name first being recorded in Bronze Age Greece. They strive to preserve entire cultures and even peoples from the forces of history, modernity, and the Ascension War.
      • The Curators - A somewhat controversial sect, they conduct mass rituals to seal off tribal villages, small towns, and other cultural nodes within pockets of space-time so as to protect them from outside influences and the corruption of the modern world. Members range from modern academic idealists and radical separatists to those born within such temporal islands.
      • The Hearthkeepers - Devoted to preserving and protecting the spirits of household and family, as well as the rituals which surround them. Members prefer to lead quite lives within their communities as wise women and cunning men, maintaining family traditions and cultural festivals as a way to keep their cultures alive.
      • The Benevolent Protective Order of the Sacred Light - One of the more obscure and unusual Dreamspeaker sects, the Order is a small masonic ritual society devoted to preserving European and American traditions of ritual mysticism and the Aeonic spirits which they invoke. The group had its zenith during the Victorian Era and has been in decline since the World Wars.
      • The Jesus Road - A loose association of Christian mystics and spirit-talkers. They try to preserve the more esoteric and revelatory aspects of their faith. This includes exorcism rites, faith healing, theurgic ritual, angelic visitations, divinely inspired dreams, and Pentecostal experiences.
      • The Lodge of the Grey Squirrel - Founded after the defeat of Tecumseh in order to preserve and protect the cultures, religions, and languages of North America's native peoples. Its members have fought tirelessly for treaty enforcement, control over their own lands, and to prevent exploitation. (Similar groups exist in South America, Australia, and other lands.)
      (Concepts: Devoted foster mom rescuing children in peril; First Nations political activist; Itinerant faith healer; Obsessive anthropologist fixated on lost and endangered cultures; Aeonic ritual shaman; Modern American in search of his African roots; Traditional artisan; Local pillar of the community; Preserver of Native American languages; Gospel singer filled with the Holy Spirit)

          The Nine Traditions are more than a political construct, for its formation was a world-wide secret ritual to tie each Tradition's fate to that of the Nine Cornerstones of Reality, i.e. the Spheres of magic. Not that the higher-ups who know of this secret are okay with it; even if they would never lose the war, they are still losing dozens of battles, lives and resources wasted.

          All the Traditions are formed as bottom-up coalitions and mergers of several Crafts. They had to, if they wanted a seat in the above grand ritual.

          The Order of Hermes, holding the seat of Prime (not Forces), formed as an alliance of Hellenistic cults to various Roman deities. They are the oldest Tradition, whose structure served as the blueprint of Traditions to come, and so far they are the only Tradition whose mages identify first as Tradition and second as Houses (the sub-factions).

          The Pleromatic Chorus, holding the seat of Forces (not Prime), started as a cult of surviving Gnostics that then later absorbed heretics and mystics from Judaism, Christianity, Eastern Orthodoxy, and Islam. Each Church within the Chorus identify with and uphold a single commandment, taken from the scriptures of the aforementioned faiths.

          The Vervain Garden, holding the seat of Life, are an alliance of reconstructed fertility cults and neopagans communicating via floriography and herbal symbolism. Their Varieties are named after agricultural crops and notable herbs significant to the region they cover.

          The Cults of Passion, holding the seat of Mind (not Time) have no real regional root, even blatantly branding themselves as being open for any Mystic who would use stimulation and hallucinations to reach an ecstatic state. Each Cult focuses on what brand of emotion or passion they focus on.

          The People of the Wheel, holding the seat of Fortune, originate from Hindu cults reflecting upon the nature of reincarnation, that grew to include any Craft concerned with the subject of rebirth and fate. Each Spoke focuses on a particular 'lesser wheel' in the world, such as the four seasons, sin and redemption, and so on.

          The Lacustrine Covenant, holding the seat of Time (not Mind), are a centralized coalition of secret schools and superhuman clans practicing enlightening martial arts across East Asia. Each School disciplines their students to master an esoteric Question (or Answer, for some Schools such as the Akashayana ) to the nature of the world at each School's core.

          The Scions of the Skies, holding the seat of Spirit, started as Genghis Khan's shamanic advisors who thrived in the Mongol Empire. Modern Skies are largely based in Asian Shamanism, but also various cults of the sky-fathers and other celestial creator figures across the world, including the recently joined Dreamspeakers.

          For the Society of Ether, holding the seat of Matter, and the Virtual Adepts, holding the seat of Connection, I haven't given much thoughts yet.


          Meanwhile, I renamed the Technocratic Conventions as the New World Initiative, the Secret Syndicate, the Peerage of Progenitive Minds, Iteration XI, and the Metanautic Division.

          Iteration XI used to be Iteration X too, but with a critical plot change; they were never duped by an existing spirit. Iteration X really did create an AI, the Computer, but the Computer was also an Artificial Spirit. After acquiring power at a boosted speed unfathomable to natural-born spirits, the Computer launched a program to merge physical matter and Umbral pseudo-matter as the next step in Enlightened cybernetics and material sciences.

              Dreamspeaker factions, pt 2

              The Red Spears - Spiritual warriors, guardians and crusaders. Fierce defenders of their peoples who seek to understand and exploit the spiritual power behind the elements of conflict and war. Among the most militant of the Tradition, many members spent the period between the fall of the First Cabal and the new post-WW2 Technocratic pogroms in self-imposed isolation from the other Traditions, preferring to stand and fight against threats to their cultures on their own. More than a few still advocate for this strategy.
              • The Animal Generals - First organized in ancient India and Southeast Asia, members learn the way of the beasts of the earth, studying their ferocity and natural weapons in order to draw on their spiritual power. In addition to shape-shifting/skin-changing, they also summon and recruit animals as companions and allies.
              • The Ghost Face Lodge - A secret society of assassins. Masters of stealth and infiltration, members delve into the underworld to draw on the powers of ancestor spirits and of death itself. Able to become living ghosts and to summon the dead to fight at their side.
              • The Cleansing Way - Devoted to the spiritual healing and purification of blooded warriors when they return from war. Many work as doctors, nurses, physical therapists, psychologists, and councilors who focus on veterans' affairs and refugee issues.
              • The Crimson Pen - Modern warriors in the realm of ideas and culture, using the tools of law, education, and entertainment to advance the agenda of the Dreamspeakers and their own cultures in both the Ascension War and the mundane world. In addition to drawing on the spiritual power of words and laws, many are also charismatic wielders of Ars Cupidae.
              • The Urban Fire - City dwellers who believe in taking the fight directly into the heart of the Technocracy's world. Members make contact with a wide variety of modern urban spirits while working as teachers, activists, engineers, first-responders, and vigilantes.
              • The Brotherhood of the Unseen Face - An ancient and secretive society of esoteric martial philosophers who devote themselves to the study of the world's cultures and their martial traditions in order to uncover the hidden spiritual factors which connect and drive aspects of war and conflict. Their ultimate goal is to end war and violence forever.
              (Concepts: VA hospital therapist; Inner city school teacher trying to rehabilitate gang members; Underground hip hop battle rapper; Brother of wolves; Assassin in training; Globe trotting anthropologist with a gun; High steel construction worker living a double life as an urban vigilante; Militantly activist journalist; Renegade federal agent; Martial prodigy wanting only to live a peaceful life)

              The Spirit Smiths - Preservers of the ancient arts of enchantment and fetish creation, as well as explorers into the new frontiers of techs-shamanism. Their crafts and creations help to strengthen the ties between the material and spiritual worlds, while also serving as powerful weapons within the Ascension War.
              • Forge of the Sacred Blade - Master weapons makers specializing in both the ancient arts and in modern gunsmithing. Members are expected to be skilled wielders of the weapons they craft, with their powerful fetish creations often also doubling as incredible works of art.
              • Crucible of the Soul - Herbalists and alchemists who now also chase after the spirits of modern chemistry and pharmaceuticals. Members prefer to work with spiritually-awakened natural ingredients, sacred plants, and psychotropics in order to create drugs and elixirs which promote spiritual as well as physical healing.
              • The Builders - Sacred architects and urban shaman striving to create spiritually awakened buildings, neighborhoods, and even cities. Members aspire to master modern engineering, sacred mathematics, and group psychology while also learning the ways of countless city spirits.
              • The Clockwork Coyotes - Crackpot inventors and borderline lunatics who eagerly build destructive weapons, traps, and other devices - often out of salvaged parts and repurposed junk - to use against the Technocracy and other enemies. Many often seem to have a low regard for the dangers of collateral damage.
              • The Digital Dream - Computer nerds and IT specialists building their own fetish computers and writing their own mystical code. They eagerly explore the Digital Web and seek out online spirits while trying to act as shaman to various internet communities.
              (Concepts: Spiritually awakened gunsmith; Alternative apothecary; Civil engineer serving the City Incarnae; Unstable genius bomb maker; Apprentice to ancient master sword maker; Computer prodigy delving into cyberspace and AI; Urban gardener of awakened plants; Chemical psychonaut; Feng Shui architect with a master plan; Restorer of antique furniture)

              Solitaires - Loners by choice or by circumstance. Most shun the traditional role of community guide while trying to find their own way and purpose within the Ascension War and the World of Darkness.
              • The Four Winds - Those who have abandoned the material world to spend most of their time exploring the spirit worlds of the Umbra, the underworld, and other regions.
              • The Elementalists - Dwelling apart from civilization to tend to tend to elemental spirits and forces of nature. Many dwell as hermits in mountain caves, seaside shanties, and forest huts.
              • The Lost Children - Cultural orphans of the modern world. Many are survivors of destroyed tribes or members of groups which have lost touch with the spirit ways. Others are outcasts or even criminals. Most are often bitter and alone.
              • The Moonstruck - The rarest of the rare, those born with blood ties to were beasts who have turned their back on mortal cultures and wisdom in a misguided effort to aid their animal brothers.
              (Concepts: Misguided werewolf kin; Antisocial geologist; Sole survivor of village massacre; Storm-chasing adrenaline junkie; Homeless servant of the Trash Spirits; Spoiled rich kid who received a spiritual wake up call; The old man of the sea; Reclusive shut in haunted by ghosts; Coma patient who woke up to find his entire family gone; Aimless drifter of the back roads)

                      The Technocracy’s size is immensely deceptive, and encounters with its agents reported by Mystics are unwittingly exaggerated.

                      About one fifth of encounters are plain Sleepers serving as cat’s paws, with varying levels of competency. Government agents and academic influencers tend to be these.

                      About two fifths are Technicians; the Technocracy’s answer to Sorcerers. These are the field agents and middle managers that most of the Traditions mistake for ‘true’ Technocrats behind the former category. The befuddlement is by no means complete, but persists thanks to the Technocracy not really differentiating between Technicians and Enlightened personnel on a systematic level, and Technocratic intelligence actively propagating the mistake.

                      About one fifth are humanoid Paradox Spirits summoned whenever a Mystic’s Paradox blows up hard in the city. The Technocracy is aware of these entities, and although they failed to establish organizational cooperation, they have tactics and protocols to direct them towards hostile Reality Deviants and flee before the spirits turn on them.

                      Only the last one fifth are truly Enlightened. They have the exact same abilities as their Mystic cousins… including the part where they can fully internalize their Foci to the point they can bend reality with will alone. They use their Instruments not out of necessity, but faith and confidence in the Technocracy’s party line of being tasked to conquer the chaos of the universe. When things get dire, though, they can start pointing fingers at people to shoot radiation at them.

                          So, back about 15 years ago, as a creative exercise, I tried to redevelop the original WoD from scratch, outlining release schedules and books for the various game lines starting in 1991. Part of that was the historical lines, which in the case of Mage was what I called Mage: Victorian Twilight. It was largely inspired by an outline Malcolm Shepherd and Ian Watson did back in the 2000s, and adapted to various house rule/home brew alterations I'd made to my own Mage game setting history. (Short version: The Sons of Ether and Virtual Adepts were never part of the Technocracy, being their own thing. And certain internal factions of the Traditions are slightly different, as seen in my various posts in this thread and the one before it.) So, obviously it has nothing to do with the new Victorian Age Mage other than starting point and time period.

                          But anyway, this was the intro I wrote for it.

                          Just as a forewarning: In some places I specifically use certain 19th century terminology to reflect the attitudes and prejudices of the time and place. In many ways, I consider this to be a part of the oppressive "Gothic" aspect of the Gothic-Punk World of Darkness, and is specifically what the "Punk" aspect - especially the PCs - are rebelling against.

                          Mage: Victorian Twilight

                          The Year is 1877. Queen Victorian is now Empress of India and the United States of America is a century old. The remains of the Order of Reason, thrown into disarray by the upheavals of the previous century, have reformed themselves and the Enlightened Union of Technocratic Order, founded in 1851, is on the rise. Meanwhile, the Traditions have weathered over three centuries of disunion and Diaspora in the wake of the Great Betrayal, and continue to exist as scattered and diverse Lodges. This is the Gaslight Era, the Age of Victoria, and the period of Twilight that precedes our modern World of Darkness.

                          While Victorian Twilight ultimately spans the entire globe (and beyond), the setting’s center and foundation is in Victorian London and the United Kingdom. This center moves outward to the “civilized” realms of Western Europe and North America, moving ever outward. While each of these places and the Mages within them have a part to play, the initial focus here will be on the center, with other regions and their unique aspects being covered in more detail later.

                          The Diaspora vs The Reunion

                          In the wake of the Grand Betrayal, the once grand plans for the Council of Nine Mystical Traditions fell to ruin. One of the Nine, the Solificati, left in shame and soon faded into history. A second, the Dreamspeakers, found many of their lands under assault from forces of conquest, plague, and conversion. Many Mages of India and China saw the European Awakened as failures and turned inwards, only to come to find the forces of Europe on their doorsteps. The Council of Nine has no structure, no unified leadership, and no goal save basic survival. Even many of the Traditions lack a sense of cohesion or focus, scattered as they are around the globe and viewing each other as rivals or even enemies. This is the Diaspora, the nadir of the Mystic Traditions.

                          In stark contrast, the Order of Reason, having collapsed after more than a century of strife, disorganization, and civil war, has been reborn. Reforged as the Enlightened Union of Technocratic Order, they have found new purpose and new weapons in this age of industrialization and empire. For a quarter century, they have been on the march, expanding their influence and power around the globe.

                          The scattered Traditions can no longer ignore their resurgent foes. But it remains to be seen if they can reunite before it is too late.

                          The Disparate Lodges

                          While the Traditions, in theory, continue to exist, various factions within each follow their own drives and interests. Since the end of the 18th century, the Awakened have come to refer to these factions as Lodges. While Lodges follow much of the Paradigm and basic beliefs of their parent Tradition, they manifest themselves in ways specific to 19th century beliefs and cultures, as well as their own specific goals and motivations. The total number of Lodges in existence is unknown, but believed by many to be potentially infinite. Within the Victorian World - that is Britain, Europe, North America and those places they directly influence - the largest and most widespread Lodges are considered to fall under seven main types. [Three prominent example Lodges are listed.]

                          The Aeonic Lodges are those whose styles and beliefs derive from European ritual magic, such as that which has influenced the Freemasons, the Golden Dawn, and other groups. They claim to trace pedigrees back to ancient cultures of Egypt, Rome or the Druids, and hold themselves as among the elite of Awakened society. Sometimes referred to as The Eternal, their ego and grandiosity tends to rub other Lodges the wrong way.
                          • The Gardeners of the Tree (Verbena)
                          • The House of Seshet (Hermetic)
                          • The Sons of Mithra (Chorus)
                          ​The Dionysian Lodges are rebels, rogues and rakes, united primarily by their refusal to conform to period norms of behavior and morality. They draw influence and inspiration from the old Romantics and Libertines, as well as the modern Bohemianism, Aestheticism, and Feminism. Often referred to as Rebels or Rakes, they considered to be the most troublesome of Lodges.
                          • The Crucible of the Case-Hardened Soul (Hermetic)
                          • The Fellowship of Pan (Ecstatic)
                          • The Sisterhood of the Moon (Verbena)
                          ​The Orientalist Lodges are those who have turned their interests to the lands of the East, including the Middle East, India and China. As the Empires of Europe spread into the lands of Asia, it was inevitable that there would be those who returned having converted to various native beliefs and then spreading them into the drawing rooms, clubs, and salons of Europe. Sometimes referred to as Exotics, they are often viewed as curiosities or enigmas to more traditional Lodges.
                          • The Banquet of Lakshmi (Thanatoic)
                          • The Blue Dharma (Akashic)
                          • The Garden of Peace (Batini)
                          ​The Primitive Lodges are those who practice the magics of older, rural, and supposedly uncivilized cultures. While often referring to the workings of those outside of Europe or Asian cultures, it also includes the superstitions of the peasantry and villagers from across Europe and North America. Many dismissively refer to them as Savages, but they hold the allegedly civilized Lodges in equal contempt.
                          • The Bata’a (Craft)
                          • The Red Spears (Dreamspeakers)
                          • The Twisters of Fate (Verbena)
                          The Religious Lodges draw on the power of faith, specifically that of the Judeo-Christian traditions. Theirs is the power of prayer and of miracles, as well as sacred relics and the angels from on high. Sometimes called the Illuminated, other Lodges often hold them at arms length, questioning if they are truly Mages or not.
                          • The Alexandrian Society (Chorus)
                          • The Knights Templar (Craft)
                          • The Sisterhood of Gabrielle (Chorus)
                          ​​The Spiritualist Lodges are deeply entwined in the spiritualist movements of the era, crossing the boundaries between life and death to convene with and draw power from the spirits of the other side. Known to some as the Haunted, the other Lodges view them with a small amount of fear.
                          • The Ghost Wheel Society (Dreamspeakers)
                          • The Knights of Rhadamanthus (Thanatoic)
                          • The Unseen Society (Hollower)
                          ​The Scientific Lodges are the most controversial among the Awakened. Having fully embraced the century’s new science and eagerly incorporating it into their magic, many more traditional Mages see them as pawns of the Order of Reason and potential threats. Many of the Technomancers could care less, seeing the older Lodges as relics of a bygone age.
                          • The Children of Knowledge (Craft)
                          • The Electrodyne Engineers (Etherite)
                          • The University of Light (Batini)
                          ​
                          Anyway, that was the gist of it. The idea was that future books would introduce additional Lodges. The Primitive and Religious Lodges might have even been held off until the Companion (which would've come fairly quickly, and also introduced fourth examples for each group, such as the Ruby Crucible of Thig and the Order of the Black Willow). Each Lodge type would've also gotten its own sourcebook, which would've also discussed possible additional groups, including non-Mage rivals such as the Enlightened Society of the Weeping Moon or various vampire fronts. The books about high society (the Gilded Cage and Gutter Magic) would've introduced groups like House Jerbiton and the Darklings, while the Detective's Casebook would've included the Analytical Reckoners. And there would've been books about the Middle East, India, East Asia, the Wild West, and Africa which included multiple Lodges for each region. And the Technocracy would've gotten their own book (Empire of Reason).

                                          The revival of the thread actually made me want to share some of my own takes and interpretations on how Mage works. While they may not be bursting with flavour like for instance No One of Consequence
                                          take on Victorian age Mage, they do make me happy.


                                          The council of Nine is a political movement, rather than a monolithic organisation

                                          The traditions on a whole don't take the council and their ecumenical message all too seriously. The average member of a tradition is insular and turned toward their own circle of peers and doesn't really engage with the Council business at large. They see their paradigm as paramount and find it to be the ultimate hubris of the council to be pushing this weird ecumencal all practices and all paradigms are equal. Not because it's necessarily untrue, but because it surley cannot be better than the tried and true practices of their own and their teachers.

                                          In practice this means that most Mages you would encounter are deeply tied into their own practices and do not ascribe or expect purple paradigm to work. It's a "new fangled thing" made by the council and it distracts from the REAL business of magic at hand. This is why often troublesome members or ones that clash with practices of the Tradition are pushed towards the Council to get rid of them. Of course this does make it more likely the characters like this would end up PC's, and for most of them the discovery of the ecumenical nature of the Council to be a shock that leads them into freedom, leads them towards proving their methods superior or puts them into conflict with the openness of the Council.

                                          This also means it's harder for Mages from different pactices and backgrounds to cooperate with magick effects, and I feel that's a good thing. They can work together to fight the common enemies, but each of them is married to their own wolrdview and powered by hubris that their school of thought is the best one. To enforce this I tend to be very strict about paradigms and foci, and have in some games even stopped people from using the whole tool dropping system to great effect (at least as far as flavour goes).


                                          Paradox is weird and not something you can expect to understand or compensate

                                          Just because the books like to default at times to things like damage or backfiring, I feel this takes away from the well, magic of Mage. Paradox should be weird and unknowable, so I prefer any backlashes or botches to actual lead to weird thing. For this reason I use flaws, weird consequences, Paradox spirits and Paradox Realms much more than the straightforward solutions. I've found it makes the games more memorable and Paradox more interesting.


                                          The consensus is an Awakened construct and a framework for reality workers to change the world, not the actual state of the world

                                          By adhering to this, It's easier to treat the World of Darkness as our own but weirder, because otherwise a game would quickly be quite off the rails once basic causality, history we rely on and what is these days thermed as cosmological constants. I also tend to make the consensus more about reality pushing back than the idea of Sleepers pushing back.

                                          This doesn't mean the world cannot be changed, but it tends to rubber back into the general shape we as players expect it to have as long as most of the population aren't willworkers or people indocitrinated to belive what the willworkers do.​

                                          What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.

                                                          Let’ try something more mechanics-y (but system-free at the moment).

                                                          Like how vampires had Virtue ratings and Wan Kuei have Soul ratings, mages have Resonance ratings; Devotion, Emotion, Function, Reflection.

                                                          Mundane Actions (the usual Ability + Attribute dice) get bonuses scaling with a Resonance if the action’s intent matches with how the mage has developed that Resonance.
                                                          Eg. a bald Etherite working to develop a cream to cure baldness gets Devotion-based bonuses on his academic research rolls.

                                                          Resonant Actions, rolling Ability + Resonance (ie. substitute Resonance for Attribute), are like mundane actions but with a slight dash of daily miracle. They don’t risk Paradox, and even help gather Quintessence, but can be done only a limited number of times.
                                                          Eg. a Hollower sees her crush about to get run over by a truck across the street, so she rolls Athletics + Emotion to jump in and rescue him, despite having 1s and 2s in Physical Attributes.

                                                          Shapings, rolling Sphere + Attribute (ie. substitute Sphere for Ability), are clearly superhuman actions, but still somewhat restricted to the human form. They don’t risk Paradox from Witnesses, but do from Vulgarity (here defined as going against the local Reality Zone’s Norms)
                                                          Eg. a Thanatos on a mission to assassinate a local gang leader decides to go for the shock factor, so she barges into a room full of mafioso and does the John Wick stuff with her guns. Forces + Dexterity. This is her first time using guns to kill. Her master, who prefers using actual firearms skills in assassinations (likely rolling Firearms + Function) for relative discreteness, sighs.

                                                          Workings, rolling Sphere + Resonance, are outright magic (or hypertech, or psionics, or whatever) without excuse. Risks Paradox from Witnesses and Vulgarity, but if the only Sleeper-witnesses present are Acolytes (here defined as Sleepers whose worldviews are compatible with the mage’s Focus), the Working goes off smoothly.
                                                          Eg. a Hermetic chants to invoke Saturnine forces that will open the Shroud and invite the spirits of the dead, rolling Fortune (what I call Entropy) + Reflection. She’s doing this to track down who killed her childhood friend, hence Reflection.
