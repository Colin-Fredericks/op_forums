    Can a super-trained human defeat a Garou or elder vampire barehandedly?

    05-23-2023, 05:20 AM
    A little homebrew: a mortal character ( no ghoul, no sorcery, no infernal investments ) trained to the human limit in several martial arts ( Combat Styles from Armory Reloaded, transposed in the Old World of Darkness ruleset ).
    If I remember well there was a rule for extra use of Willpower in the book Mirrors. I've to research it.

    Conceptually, the question is: without going into the blatantly supernatural, can the strongest human take on a truly powerful supernatural being?

    - - -

    So far:

    Strength ( heavy blows ) 5
    Dexterity ( close combat ) 5

    Dodge ( one-on-one ) 5
    Brawl ( boxing ) 5


    Merits:
    * Ability aptitude - dodge
    * Ability aptitude - brawl
    * Berserker ( V20 Black Hand )

    Flaws:
    * Stress Atavism ( Guide to the Technocracy )

                      Thanks Aahz.

                      So, the second problem is dealing enough damage. And the third is that those supernatural powerhouses have multiple actions every round ( Celerity and Rage )
                      Let's see.

                      Let's start with the basics: Strength 5 with specialization ("heavy blows" ) plus Brawl 5 with specialization ( boxing, karate, whatever ) and Ability Aptitude - Brawl. This gives us a base of 12 attack dice with a difficulty of 4, so roughly 8 successes. Problem is, it's bashing damage.

                      Then, there's this Merit/Flaw from Guide to the Technocracy called "Stress Atavism". It's common between cyborgs and genetical soldiers but it doesn't say it can't be taken by a common human.
                      For most characters it's something innate but I think that certain ecstatic pratices can allow a warrior to develope a similar "murderous istinct" by training and mental conditioning.

                      Stress Atavism (3 pt Flaw) Despite the ice-cold Technocrat stereotype, you go ballistic at any provocation. You revert to an animalistic state of mind, diving into fight-or-flight instincts. The good points: You become extremely strong and nearly invulnerable to pain. The bad points: You attack everything in your vicinity, forgetting small details like friendships, tactics or innocent bystanders. While this heedless mania makes you good in a fight, your comrades tend to wind you up and run. A common Flaw among genetically engineered constructs and cybernetically enhanced soldiers, Stress Atavism surges up during combat or other high-stress situations. If the Storyteller asks you to make a Willpower roll (difficulty 8) and you fail, the madness overcomes you. In your atavistic state, add two dice to your Strength, one die to your Stamina and three "Bruised" health levels. You also go completely apeshit, firing all weapons at full-auto until the magazines empty, then lashing out with hands, feet and anything else that seems convenient. Once you go atavistic, you fight until one of two things happens: either you are rendered helpless, or everyone else in the area is. Naturally, Control considers you an extraordinarily loose cannon. There's a termination order waiting for the day you outlive your usefulness....​
                      ​
                      So, we have an ( uncontrollable ) fighting frenzy that can somewhat make up for the Rage of a Garou. +2 Strength, +1 Stamina, +3 bruised health levels and immunity to pain.
                      We are on something but it's still not enough...


                      Question: the Merit "Too Tough to Die" allows a character who's normally unable to soak lethal damage with Stamina to do so.
                      But since mortals do not take aggravated damage, would that merit allow a human to soak damage from werewolf bite / claws?

                      -

                      Thanks again for the suggestion Aahz . So, with Berserk we have 14 attack dices ( 5 Strength + 5 Brawl + 2 specializations + 2 Stress Atavism ) at difficulty 6-3 ( Berserk ) -2 ( Ability Aptitude ): 2 ( minimum difficulty ).
                      An average of 12-13 successess... but still bashing damage, for now.

                                                      I think this needs to pump the brakes a bit.

                                                      What edition(s) are fair game? If we're just picking and choosing shit from all the books (including using 2e Merits with X20 base mechanics that have different versions of those Merits) and so on, it's just going to be a mess that generates no useful answers.

                                                      Like the whole Dex 5/Brawl 5 with applicable specialty in both... in no edition or iteration of the cWoD does that result in a dice-pool of 12 with a 0.5 average success rate (before Merits and all). We'd have to be mixing and matching at least three edition's definitions of things for that to happen.

                                                      And that's going to swing the other way to. Disciplines like Celerity, Potence, and Fortitude? Are we using the strongest, the weakest, or a specific editions iteration of them? Because that's going to matter a lot. Are we using 1e Werewolf's rules that have a glitch in them that make them impossible to actually defeat as long as they've taken 1 bashing damage?

                                                      If we want to be serious about this, we have to set clear ground rules or it's meaningless.
