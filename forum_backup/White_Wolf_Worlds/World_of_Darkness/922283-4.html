                            None of the major "canon" NPC's exist in my games, I'm mainly referring to the ones who became prominent in the Revised metaplot such as Vykos, Theo Bell, Lucita, Anatole, King Albrecht, and the like.

                            There are exceptions. Beckett exists, as do the NPC's found in Chicago by Night 1st Edition, Vampire: The Masquerade 1st Edition's corebook (Malcolm, Shelzza, and the Kindred of Gary), Milwaukee by Night, and the cast of Vampire: The Masquerade Bloodlines (though the Kuei-Jin have been changed to more traditional Cainite Kindred. Ming Xiao is a Tzimisce elder affiliated with the traditional Chinese vampire courts instead of the Sabbat). I'm on the fence about whether or not I should include the NPC's from Montreal by Night as canon or not. I like the book and it pre-dates Revised, but I don't know. I liked the characters, but they aren't as memorable as the NPC's in Chicago.

                            Also, since Sailor Moon and the fan-game Senshi: The Merchandising are part of my WoD, that means the Sailor Scouts, Tuxedo Mask, Queen Beryl and her generals, and some of the civilians (such as Naru and Umino) are technically canon NPC's.

                                            Interestingly, I do play a Post-Revised (basically the opposite of your world Camilla) game and started it before I played V20 and the other 20 games. To expand on what I posted earlier:

                                            1. The Withering Happened: I basically said that 1/3rd of the vampires of the Earth died in 2000 and it wiped out most of the vampires in South America, Russia, India, and other places the players would never go as well as most of the Sabbat.

                                            It also meant there were plenty of unclaimed cities in the Americas but vampires clustered together in New York, Saint Louis, and LA for the most part because of fear. The plague eventually stopped for some reason but the consequences still linger with the common beliefs being it was either a vampire disease, punishment from God, or the antedeluvians draining their prey.

                                            Mostly, I had this happen because it allowed me to dial down the power of the Sabbat and also vampires in general. It also meant that Neonates like the player characters had potentially far stronger ability to project force than in games where they were tripping over Methuselahs.

                                            It also helps that no one knows how many Ancients survived either. This is part of the reason why the Setites and Assamites are joining the Camarilla as well as why the Sabbat fell apart but is getting back together now that Ur-Shulgi awoke.

                                            2. The Forsaken War: I basically felt the Garou had something of an advantage in the games which needed to be curbed because they were such dangerous violent predators that it'd be interesting to see them warring against EACH other rather than everyone else. As such, I had the Forsaken event happen. The Garou discovered that Father Wolf (hypothetical totem for the whole of the Garou race) was corrupted by the Wyrm and had been for some time.

                                            One faction of the Garou wanted to cleanse Father Wolf while the other faction wanted to kill him to end the corrosive effect he was having on them. The murder faction killed Father Wolf before a cleansing could happen and in my games, this has actually had a generally positive effect on the Garou overall in terms of attitude but has also weakened them somewhat.

                                            It's also triggered a war between roughly two equal sides of the Garou which has more or less screwed their attempts to save the world even more than usual. This also has resulted in the Shapechangers coming out of hiding to basically form their own Alliance which is as strong as either of the garou factions.

                                            3. The Traditions and Technocracy are in an uneasy peace: The Avatar Storm happened almost twenty years ago and killed the majority of the Masters or trapped the others across the Gauntlet, turning them into various kinds of spirits. The Pogrom was suspended and the Technocracy focused instead of trying to deal with rebuilding its shattered power base while the Traditions did the same. Amusingly, this resulted in the Technocracy going to war with Pentex and managing to clean it out after almost a decade (and an economic crisis) while the Traditions focused their efforts on more traditional monster hunting.

                                            As such, this means that 20 years later, the current Masters are actually PCs from the 90s who HATE the Technocracy in an anarchic but now adult sort of way while the majority of newer mages have never been hunted by them so they don't see the big deal. The same is for the Union who have been at peace w/ the Traditions for 20 years and think of them as weird science-hating yokels but not an existential threat to reality.

                                            There are people who want to wipe the other out but they're extremists on both sides rather than the majority. The M20 Disparate Alliance also exists because the weakening of the Traditions and Union meant the Crafts and Independents could come out of hiding. Essentially, both of them saying, "Finally, Jesus, the 100 years war had nothing on you guys."

                                            It opens a lot of non-Ascension War games as a result.

                                            4. The Reckoning is really a status quo now: With a limited number of Hunters (1,000 at any given time), there's always Hunters present in the World of Darkness and they provide a bit of extra-oomph to groups like the Society of Leopold (who tend to classify Catholic Christian psycho Hunters as "Saints" and anyone else as Witches) and smaller Vigils. It makes it hard for the supernatural as a whole but not impossible. In addition, Slashers are what happens when Hunters act consistently evil until they have the equivalent of 0 Humanity and they develop the powers to kill regular humans in ways which seemed designed to send some sort of message. They also become damn near immortal.

                                            5. Demons exist but aren't really an apocalyptic danger: Demons didn't exactly change the world like they expected since the Hunter Baali joined the True Hand, the Traditions and Technocracy turned their attention back to the world, Hunters sense demons like lighthouses, plus demons turned out to hate each other as much as anyone else. As such, they've never really formed a society and while there's holes in hell, it's more or less a case of many getting summoned, killed, and going back to hell. The smarter demons generally run cults and are more humane than their fellows (albeit that's not saying much). They're also actively working to keep both the Archdemons and Earthbound asleep.

                                            Because my WoD isn't COMPLETELY HOPELESS, a few of these Cthulhuean entities have also been truly destroyed by both PCs and other heroes which has also made them cautious. The same thing happened with an Antedeluvian (Lasombra) and other ancient evils.

                                            6. Changeling: The Nightmare exists: The Formorians retuned and by returned, basically have taken over the Dreaming so that it's now a hostile and dangerous place no Changeling actually ever wants to visit. On the plus side, no Formorians actually want to venture into the physical world and just send their servants to kidnap Changelings to be their servants. Yes, that basically means the Formorians are the Gentry from the Lost. They just don't MAKE changelings in this world--at least that anyone knows about it.

                                            Changelings have, as a small-small-small compensation, benefited from their Arts and magic getting a power up which puts them roughly on par with Vampire Neonates as they can now use Glamour to heal wounds and don't get Banality from just existing--only showing Changeling existence off to regular mortals. Changeling society as a whole is fucked, though, and reduced to smaller courts and alliances than actual kingdoms.

                                            In short, my Post-Revised world is one where supernaturals have had the shit kicked out of them but are generally not interested in destroying the world so much as surviving it.

                                                One idea that I had was that Cain wasn't so much the first vampire as much as he is the most recent common ancestor of all currently active vampires.
