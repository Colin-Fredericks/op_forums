                        There are a lot of issues I had with C:tD, but I don't know if they can be addressed without starting from scratch.

                        1. What are the themes and the antagonists? As tv tropes put it, "It doesn't help that the game line itself has so many ways to go, you have little idea what the authors wanted—a fable on the death of childhood innocence? A game of raging against the dying of the light? An urban fantasy game about balancing the fantastic and the mundane?" Is it the lightest of the WoD games or the darkest? The core book has the primary struggle against Banality and it's agents, the Autumn People and the Dauntain. The trouble is that agents of Banality are by definition boring. So later books had the Shadow Court and the new Thallain as antagonists. The search for King David after being kidnapped by a Shadow Court member was introduced. Finally after the Week of Nightmares there were the Ahene and the Fomori Courts. Winter seemed to go from a general death of creativity, going out with a whimper, to a Garou style Apocalypse. Which of these will be in C20?

                        2. For a game emphasizing creativity, the players are limited to rigid, narrow Kiths. All trolls are honorable (blue) warriors, all pooka are liars, etc. There isn't even a "none of the above" option like Caitiff in Vampire or Orphans in Mage. They aren't really archetypal like the ones in DA: Fae and Lost, which led to Kith bloat. Again from tv tropes, "Faerie races include the European Kithain (16, plus 8 Thallain), Native American Nunnehi (14), elemental Inanimae (6), Pacific Menehune (4), East Asian Hsien (10), Arabic Djinn (a fan work endorsed by the writers; 6), and the dark Adhene (7). There are over 70 different splats, not counting the potential for Australian, South American, Indian, and African fae (beyond the eshu) had the line been allowed to continue, which might have pushed that number towards 100. Each race has their own sets of rules, which keeps them separate from the others (plus geography)." There's also 5 Inanimae and 13 Sidhe Houses. "A word of warning for completists: because new splats could pop up seemingly at random, collecting all available Dreaming splats requires 15 books and a fan web supplement (a 1e or 2e corebook, Immortal Eyes: The Toybox, Immortal Eyes: Shadows on the Hill, Immortal Eyes: Court of All Kings, Isle of the Mighty, The Fool's Luck, Kithbook: Redcaps, Kithbook: Eshu, World of Darkness: Blood-Dimmed Tides, The Shadow Court, Changeling Players Guide, Kingdom of Willows, Inanimae: The Secret Way, Land of Eight Million Dreams, Denizens of the Dreaming, and web supplement Djinn: Of Smokeless Fire)." Something with broad archetypes that could be customized, like DA: Fae and Lost mentioned above would be preferable, but then fans of the original Kiths would be left out.

                        3. The Eshu are problematic. They seem to be there to provide balance to the otherwise all European Kiths, but they're named after an African god, not actual African fae-like beings. They're also commoners, with the unfortunate implication that their natural rulers are the European Sidhe. Another book introduced the Oba as little known nobility of the Eshu, probably to fix this.

                        4. The Seelie/Unseelie dichotomy tends to turn into good vs. evil. Especially with the Thallain presented as really evil. It's presented as an innate characteristic, but only for the European Kiths. The Seasonal Court system or some other option would be preferable.
