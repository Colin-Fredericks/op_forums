              There really is a lot of potential in more Victorian Age settings in general.

              I'm really interested in seeing the Storytellers Vault do Victorian Age versions of Changeling and Wraith. A lot of the modern pop culture takes on faeries originated in the Victorian era and the era was also a time of renewed interest in spiritualism and a fascination with death on both sides of the Atlantic.

              Come to think of it, I always wondered what the setting for the planned historical prequel game for Changeling in the 90's would've originally been.

              I know Vampire got Dark Ages (and that later got turned into a whole line of its own in Revised), Werewolf got the Wild West, Mage got the Renaissance with The Sorcerer's Crusade, and Wraith got World War I. I'm genuinely curious what Changeling's original historical prequel would've been.

              I do remember writing up a concept for a "historical prequel" for the Street Fighter RPG set in Ancient Rome and focused on gladiators in the arenas and Shadaloo being a mystery death cult trying to destroy the Roman Empire from within.

                                                      I'm totally unfamiliar with the fan games, so I can't really say anything about them.

                                                      I think there's a lot you can do with the Revolutionary Era (and by that, I mean the period between the Seven Years War/French & Indian War and the battle of Waterloo). Adam Tinsworth, who did a lot of writing for Hunter, Demon, and Werewolf, once told me he had really wanted to do a Victorian Age Hunter book that would've focused on Hunters Hunted style characters who emulated the likes of Abraham Van Helsing and Sherlock Holmes, and I think that sort of thing can also work really well in the late 18th and very early 19th century. The 2001 film Brotherhood of the Wolf is a really great example of Hunters in that time period, with two veterans of the French & Indian War investigating the Beast of Gévaudan. Also Tim Burton's 1999 film Sleepy Hollow, with Johnny Depp (before heavy drinking turned him into a parody of himself) playing Ichabod Craine as an early forensic scientists. (There's also Terry Gilliam's The Brothers Grimm from 2005, which is perhaps a little too humorous, but still has a lot of interesting ideas.) This is also the period when Mary Shelly's novel Frankenstein is set, as well as works by other early Gothic writers like Anne Radcliffe, Sophia Lee, and the so-called Graveyard poets. And you can draw on things like Dennis Wheatley's Roger Brook novels, Bernard Cornwall's Richard Sharpe novels, and Robert Lee Hall's mystery novels featuring Ben Franklin during his time in England.

                                                      The 1920s is, effectively, part of the Wraith: The Great War setting, with the mortal war simply being the catalyst which leads to a near decade of upheaval in the lands of the dead. Humans who die during the early part of the Roaring 20s, the Jazz Age, Harlem Renaissance, Irish civil war, Années Folles, Weimar Germany, the Polish-Soviet War, the Russian civil war, the Soviet Famine, the break up of the Ottoman Empire, the Japanese colonization of Korea, US occupation of Hispaniola and Nicaragua, the Patagonia uprising, and other events are going to find themselves thrown into the events of the Stygian civil war and the Great Maelstrom. But, obviously, there's a lot going on in the living world to play around with. It's the start of Hollywood, which I think has a lot of potential for a Changeling setting.

                                                      The Baroque period has a lot to play with as well. Especially the time of the Sun King, Peter the Great, Carolus Rex, William and Mary, and Carlos II. If you've never seen it, Guy Davis (who did a lot of art for Vampire: The Masquerade during the Revised era) did a comic series called The Marquis, that is about a demon hunter battling demons in a fictional setting that is heavily based on early 18th century France, with a lot of Baroque influence in the architecture and clothing.
