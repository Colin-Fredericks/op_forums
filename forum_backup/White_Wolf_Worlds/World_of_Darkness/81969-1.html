    Couple of quick things while I'm taking a break from real work -

    Derzhuzad - I'm thinking I'm just going to lay everything out, then categorize. Get all the information down first.

    Phoebus - I'm not sure what you're trying to say. You would like a historiography to go along with it? Unfortuantely, I am not qualified to write something like that, and other people have written better texts than I could attempt. I am just trying to provide a list of resources.

    Talum - A free attack could mean life or death, especially when you add in all the modifiers (flanking, aimed, etc.).

    rderekp - Ah, I was trying to open it in a word processing program. That explains it. I'll pop it open in Google spreadsheet later. Thanks! (And looking forward to that gun list.)

    HOD - Some things here need longer explanations, so... Uppercut vs. Eye strike: The difference is in intent and training, where a professionally trained boxer is trying to knock someone out, while the drunk college kid is making a desperate attempt. There is also a bit of design wiggle-room for ST's, so that they may determine if they would like the strike to be Lethal or not. As always, I am open to suggestions either way. Accuracy: I am aware of the curve in the numbers regarding dicepools, but difficulty can not drop below 4, ever, for any reason. It's another design choice - giving players an option of adding more dice or lowering difficulty. Smarter players will, of course, lower difficulty any chance they get, but there are still some people that love rolling two handfuls of dice instead. Granularity: I'm not looking to divide, subdivide, further sub-subdivide... As I responded to Phoebus, I'm just trying to provide a list of resources for the players that say "I think an uppercut should do this!" or "I have a melee of 4 and spent 30 years in prison making shanks, of course I know how to use a katana!"

    killersquirrel59 - Thank you for the advice, but I don't personally care for nWoD. My troupe and I have extensively playtested this option every week for 18 months, and I just thought I'd share with anyone looking to make combat go a bit faster, smoother, and less math-intensive (because I have players that actively hate math, but will gleefully do it when it's simple subtraction and they're trying not to die). We've found that it's equally as "realistic" as any roleplaying system. Under the RAW, one has the chance of rolling 10+ successes to-hit, and then rolling 0 successes on damage, which is what prompted this whole thing months ago, and really just doesn't seem fair to me (or my troupe).

    harlokin - Okay.

    Seriously, thank you all for your feedback - every critique makes me rethink the process, reexamine the results, and see if maybe something couldn't be done in a better manner. I know that if I can't muster an adequate defense for a design choice, then it definitely needs to be reworked. Based on comments, I've already revised several things. However, I am also aware that I can't make everyone happy - and I have no desire to - but Golden Rule, right? If you don't like something, don't use it, or work it until it fits your game better. But if you find something you like, excellent! I'm glad to have helped. One gamer to another, I really do appreciate the time and responses, because time is one true non-refundable resource.

    Cheers!

    Edit: HOD - I think I might erase that sentence about the head and lethal damage. It's supposed to be just flavor-text anyway, and I'm inclined to leave it a little more ambiguous, more open to Storyteller interpretation. Unless empty-handed attacks targeting the head are still bashing? I don't have the books with me right now.

            Originally posted by Talum View Post
            Does anyone has a simple i guess "Hack" for improoving "sneak attacks"? just not getting the chance to abort your action so that then you can try to dodge/block/parry doesnt seem like what should be a huge advantage in combat
            Well, lets be fair here -- getting a round to attack someone in which they cannot take any defensive actions whatsoever, is incredibly powerful. Almost too powerful when you consider that, if all you need to land the attack is a single success (given the fact that neither a dodge nor a parry will be forthcoming) someone could make a called shot to the neck (which is a high diff), but spend a Willpower point to guarantee that it lands. If they can get three health levels past the soak -- that's a decapitation. That's some potent stuff.

            That said, if you feel ambushes should be deadlier still, V:tM (and only V:tM) indicates that, during an ambush attack, one ramps their excess stealth roll successes into their attack.

            For example: Guy is sneaking up on Cop. He rolls dex+stealth (7 dice) vs. Cop's perception+alertness (6 dice). Guy gets 4 successes; Cop gets 2 successes. Now when Guy ambushes Cop, he will add 2 dice to his attack pool.

            Just be warned that lots of things tend to get one-shotted when using that rule; but then, that's basically how it is in real life: if you've got the drop on someone, you have an overwhelming advantage over them.

                        Here's what I mean about armor. Certain types of modern armor only protect you from certain calibers of firearms. "Soft armor", for instance (you know the stuff police wear), won't stop a bullet from an AK-47. The ceramic plates that we wear in Afghanistan will stop such a round, but they are only meant to handle three such shots. Still other forms of armor claim to offer even better protection: Pinnacle's "Dragon Skin" armor, for instance, was supposed to protect from automatic fire/a far greater number of hits from an AK-47/7.62mm round.

                        That's quite a bit different from the standing rule, which is that the armor continues to function until the attacker scores a wound that causes a certain amount of damage.
