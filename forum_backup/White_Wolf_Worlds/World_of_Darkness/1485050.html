      Don't make sheets for them. Assign them a pool for tasks they're good at (I.e. Physical 6, Mental 4, Social 3) and give them any power that seems suitable to them as a character. The fine nuances and mechanics are irrelevant. They are there to serve a narrative purpose, not to force you to have a million extra sheets to bean count.

              Does it actually matter? It's your imagination and your world. Who knows better than you do what your NPC's are good at and what they are meant to do in your story.
              It doesn't matter if you decide on the fly their dicepool is 6,7 or 2.

              What about their health levels? Health levels are not affected by stats in the World Of Darkness. Most things in the World of Darkness have only 7 Health Levels, unless they're specialised monsters like in Werewolf or if you use some kind of rule for Extras. If there is actually combat I tend to just have a scrap piece of paper where I mark wounds for different characters, but it doesn't matter much unless they're important characters.

              Go easy on yourself and simplify things on the backend. There is more than enough work as a Storyteller to actually worry about stats meant to generate PC's and shape their advancement.

                  Which edition are you using, BTW? I find it does make a difference.

                  In V20, I just list a few key pools: Combat 7, Athletics 5, Subterfuge 4, etc. Then give them the normal seven health levels (or four for mooks).

                  Sometimes I'll assign a fixed number to their best pool/area of specialism, less to a secondary area of expertise and the least to their tertiary or least favoured area of competence. So 8/6/4 or 5/3/2, etc.

                  I sometimes also use the pregen character sheets from various adventures, and then use whichever one fits best in the circumstances, perhaps changing traits to better fit (so if there's a Tzimisce surgeon but I need a Toreador surgeon, I'll change Vicissitude and Animalism to Presence and Celerity, for example).

                  Children of the Night and books like that can be very useful. You just change names and things, and use whichever characters most closely fit the concept you need.

                  V5 suggests you do the Physical/Mental/Social thing with some key dice pools picked out, such as Athletics, Melee or whatever.

                  I also use online character generators to print off a load of NPCs, and just throw those in when necessary.

                  I try to keep it simple, so I use whatever is to hand at the time. If I've got nothing to hand, I just wing it, using the PCs' pools as a guide.

                          Remember, the NPC's don't need to have a bloodpool, or any other energy/power stat. You know how much they can spend and you know how much they can do. Celerity can be a bit of a pain, but my recommendation would be to use less characters with celerity just to simplify things for yourself. If you want to make it even more straightforward as far as the number of rolls go, you could even allow One roll to apply to all the actions done with Celerity in that round.

                          And you could also reserve Celerity as a special kind of opponent, one that's quite a bit more worrying, while any regular opposition you need to fight can just use other powers, or simple physical powers.

                          Oh, and most NPC's won't even need that level of detail, as you'll just be interacting with them, not going to fight them.

                                  My NPCs have exactly the traits I need them to have. And what they dont need, they dont have.

                                  Only BBB (big-bad-bosses) gain a sheet, and only if relevant. If for example my BBB used "summon ugly demon" on the players, if I plan for my villain to show up again, then I'll mark "ugly demon summoning" on the sheet. Otherwise, my baddies remain in a quantum state of uncertanty.

                                  What's more, mooks usually have the base average pool. The guard with the gun, 6 dices to shoot. That's his only purpose on life, to have 6 dices to shoot players that fail stealth. Likewise, all the other mooks that need to shoot will likewise have 6 dices to shoot... What a coincidence right.

                                  If the guard needs to have Will at all, his willpower will be 3. ALL guards in the entire game world have will 3.

                                  Other traits dont exist until they are relevant, and the observers collapse the quantum wave revealing them. Until they do thou, those traits remain under the quantum uncertanty. How many dots do my mook have in occultism? Dont know, dont care. Until such a day comes where this becomes necessary, his rating of Occultism exists in an uncertain wave where is between 0 to 5, all at the same time. Until such a day comes where the observers (players) collapse the wave of probabilities and determine that it's actually 0 or 3 or 5.

                                  The only thing important to note is that, once you've determined one trait for a NPC, stick with it if said NPC is bound to return. If not it doesnt matter.

                                      Glad it was of use. A long time ago I started out similarly to you, and only after running a couple of sessions did I realise that you don't really need that level of granularity for the game.

                                      You are the final arbiter of how any power works, not the player, so if you don't like their interpretation don't allow it. Heck, if you don't like certain powers feel free to limit them, just be clear and consistant about it with your group.

                                      Since your question about bloodboil made me take out my V20... none of your players interpretations are how the game works - Movement of the Mind cannot do damage to people except by tossing objects at them at Level 4+, it's quite specific about that. Also it speaks of objects as discrete units, which means that an "object" is a human being, not a pieces of it. There are rituals to enchant items and weapons in Thaumaturgy, however they are not as effective as full disciplines (they give minor buffs mostly to what the item actually does) and are quite costly to make.

                                      I'd like to leave you with some words of wisdom coined in the darkest days of Exalted when players were trying to be horribly abusive with how they used the mechanics:
                                      "Chicanery-No: If a player uses this Charm in an abusive or exploitative manner, the ST may punch him right in the goddamn face."
                                      -TheDementedOne (Robert Vance)
                                      (please don't take this literally, the idea behind this quote is that just because someone is being creative in an abusive way doesn't mean it's allowed or should be encouraged)
