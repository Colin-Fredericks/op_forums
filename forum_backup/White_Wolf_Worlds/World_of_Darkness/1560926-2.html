    Originally posted by Aleph View Post
    I do like this idea.

    A way it's to expand their inherent perception powers. Demons can see trough all illusions, and distinguish all kinds of magic for what they are ... but that's the extent of their "lore" about things, barring a specific power that allows to know more.




    It's not a bad idea, but it would need to be thematically different. Lores it's a wordplay because it's knowledge, but it's also that demons are the Lords of Creation. They're not an expression of another world that's alive and has an agenda. Their powers aren't something that can be "unleashed", as such.
    I think we have something to work with here.

    So, my thoughts for now, but I'll need time to revise some rules in several books:

    1. The Lores are applied through a number of distinct mechanics, as Knowledge, Awareness, "Unleashing", Arcana (the specific powers, borrowing the name from DD), Revelation (I'm thinking on redesigning the rules for Apocalyptic Forms and also refine those for collecting Faith) and Pactiser (borrowing from French, inspiration from another thread).

    2. Knowledge and Awareness are the baseline mechanic: a Fallen can roll Lores as they roll any Ability, but the information they can glean is preternatural. Each Lore will have examples, but include things like knowing all the weather patterns of the world in any age and sensing the weather well enough to predict a week ahead at least.

    3. "Unleashing" is a placeholder name. In C20 this rule gives them a way of treating Arts as if they were Spheres, delivering a freeform magic system without just repeating the mechanics of Mage. I agree it needs changes, but the idea is the same, with more control. A means to use a Lore roll similar to an Ability that encodes in a single roll the flexibility of freeform magic and the risk of Torment.

    4. Arcana would be static powers associated with the Lore. Here I'm thinking about using the V5 Discipline system: you have simpler powers, but they're several and you chose what to slot in. They'd have all powers of a given level, but "slotting" the power requires Faith Points and a roll.

    5. Revelation would be the roll to manifest the Fallen as an avatar of the Lore. It won't define stats for the Form, but how fast and impressive it is. It may be subtle, giving off sensations that cause awe even if there's almost no visible change. That way deciding when a target has faith in the Demon isn't too subjective anymore, it's always a Revelation Roll and, with success, taking Apocalyptic Form even if "subtle" (as in not Masquerade breaking, but definitely quite noticeable).

    6. Pactiser is the one I'm on the fence. I'm not sure if I want Lores to affect Pacts too much. I do think they need more weight, but I interpret that while the Fallen channel the power to make things happen, much of the changes the Faith cause come from the target themselves. But I can see a point in Lore use as a means to stylize Thralls for each Demon.

    7. Faith cost will be an important aspect of the uses. Knowledge/Awareness has no cost and no reliance on remaining Faith, the Fallen are always capable of it. Pactiser uses the Faith of the target already, so while it requires available Faith, it doesn't cost any. Arcana are above all energy savers, using them should be cheap, but changing would be costlier. Revelation will depend on the final mechanics, and Unleashing is the most expensive of the bunch.

    8. Pacts need more oomph, but that would first come from revising how much points we get to design the changes to the target. I think the base rules are serviceable: you can take up to half as toll, round down, use the rest to invest in them, and from afar Ravage them for extra dice. What we need is alternatives to channel this Faith, a way to build devices and ritual matrices that store and use this Faith, and/or the Ravaging. We also need rules for changing Faith over time.

    9. Just to be clear: I'm on favor of supernatural splats being able to make Pacts by default. It's ridiculous to block when this is already a thing in their own games. What happens to them in their society is another problem.

    10. Suggestion name for the project: Demon the Fallen 25th Anniversary Edition.

            Originally posted by Aleph View Post
            Maybe you could leave the system open ended, but give some cost reduction if it matches the nature of the demon (as expressed by Lores) and another one if it matches the nature of the thrall (as expressed by some sort of "ressonance", that could be related to Nature and maybe some elements or narratives we want to incentivize as part of the Pact).
            What about this:

            A Pact is divided in 4 parts: Desire, Demand, Accord and Fine Letters. This is less a change and more a formalization of how it already works.

            Desire is what the target wants, the intention. A Demon is free to favor the letter or the spirit of a Pact, but effects granted in accordance with Desire cost less.

            Demand is what the demon asks back and the target agrees, and Accord is any detail they negotiate to smooth things. They have no cost reduction, but the power of pure Faith gives the Fallen the ability to grant any effect that fulfills the Pact.

            Until here the key is: you can make anything if the target knows you're going to do something roughly in that line.

            Fine Letters are the changes you can add just because the target bared their soul to you and you can mess around. Those extras are limited only to not cause a break of the Pact, and they use your Lores as base.

            Other games have rules for infernalism where getting benefits through Pacts still costs XP. It is just a matter of keeping that: splats have to pay XP for Pact benefits. If you want to more freely empower your Thralls, then you need mortals.

            Even because splats may need custom options as they may demand other kinds of powers. A Fallen can concede infernal Rotes, teach Gifts, impart Dark Thaumaturgy. But they also live in societies where they're more vulnerable to get caught and executed. Dealing with splats is too different to be just worse or better, of changes a lot of things.

            Originally posted by Aleph View Post

            I'd say True Faith starts at Faith 4/5. It isn't just greater Faith, it's Faith under certain demands, at least the requisites for the Merit. And from their, despite the name of talks more about the dedication of the character than their faith.

            My suggestion: Faith 4 is a prerequisite to buy True Faith, Faith 5 is a prerequisite to raise it. True Faith is not the same as Faith, but it can be added to Faith for some effects.

            Beware: True Faith is both demanding and very powerful. The Pact itself must be done in a way that preserves the True Faith, and breaking it will make the Thrall lose the trait, not redirect to the Fallen. This Thrall also has more defense against the Demon than most, and the ability to hurt the Demon by default.

            What would we want out of True Faith? Should a Thrall like that give off more points? Less? Receive more powers? None? Just roll it with rituals like Summoning or Warding? Something else?
