    I find running a crossover game with the Ascension Cosmology to be the best option as consensual reality allows all of the different cosmologies to fit in somewhere. there are a few issues I've come across though.

    Mage magick isn't really OP when you take a few things into account. first in order to be really OP they need time. not the Time Sphere just good old standard linear time. Even with rotes a mage caught flatfooted is better off running. Second in order to affect other critters at full strength you require certain prerequisites. Spirit 3 Life 3 for werewolves, Matter 3 Life 3 for vampires, Spirit 3 Entropy 4 for wraiths. I do usually change that to Entropy 3 though. I also have a house rule that requires Life 3 Prime 3 for changelings, as they are effectively living magic. and of course, just hitting them over the head with Paradox is usually enough to give any Mage pause before doing something too big.

    Werewolves can be a bit much at first and getting the player to operate with the others is kind of difficult in game as they specifically hate vampires and have no love of mages. but that isn't going to be a major problem.

    Vampires vulnerability to sunlight is another hurdle, I do allow magical items that allow them to nullify that just for convenience sake and i don't find it too unbalancing. I do stress the rule that their die pool are capped by their humanity during daylight hours and their need to feed however.

    Wraiths are difficult but not impossible. Werewolves and mages and vampires all have powers that allow them to see Wraiths and I stress that the Wraith player should take Arcanoi that allows them to interact with the skin lands because none of the others are going to go anywhere near the Shadow lands with out lots of precautions. It should be noted that Wraith is the game I'm least familiar with my troupe just dosn't mess with them so I've been given a pass on them so far.

    Changelins I've had to tweak a little just so they can keep up. Mostly I've had to make Glamour much easier to get but and lower the standard banality of to around 6.

                    Poor Mummy =p
                    Well, mummies are interesting characters. No doubt about that. And they can make for cool antagonists, particularly in a campaign that spans centuries. Heck, in such a campaign, they can even (occasionally) be allies - memory can be so unreliable, even for immortals.

                    But having them in a mixed PC party . . . that can get messy. A lot of their (physical) power is tied up in their immortality - in the fact that while they can die, they don't stay dead. They don't get super strength or speed, they can't shape shift, and they heal human-slow. Balancing that in combat is . . . tough

                    Even while dead, and wandering the Underworld . . its still an issue. Mummies don't have Passions, nor do they learn Arcanoi. And their Ba (wraith-like form) can't enter the Shadowlands. So again, combat gets tricky - as does hanging out with their still-living colleagues.

                        Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post

                        The post was specifically about jamming everything into Mage. Which is being invested in one game over the others - Mage.
                        Again i must disagree, if your running a multisplat game you need to establish a common framework everything hooks onto so everyone can play together. Mage, with its basic setting idea that multiple contradictory truths have validity, or even parts of Demon, where reality exists at multiple levels and only some of it is perceived by any one group at any one time, is a better fit for a "somewhat coherent" shared setting.

                        The WtA and VtM cosmologies very definitely shut down the idea anyone else knows whats going on, and very emphatically shut down any alternate explanations. Mage is more accommodating, though that comes at the price of having to posit that no-one , not the vaunted Garou or the Fallen or even the Magi really know whats going on. Players and ST are then free to use as much of the respective "truths" as applies to the game and ignore the rest where it adds unnecessary conflict. Though i definitely do not recommend putting a Mage player in a mixed chronicle personally.

                        The themes of particular splat are guidelines in my opinion, not hard and fast conventions. Not every Werewolf game must be a doomed battle against an invincible opponent while lamenting the mistakes of your ancestors. Not every Vampire game must be a nuanced and soulful exploration of the soul-grinding horror of the vampiric condition. Sometimes you just want to run mixed chronicle in a shared setting and tell a good story and have some fun. Sometimes you want to heavily compromise a game's theme and play against type. That doesnt make one invested in Mage in my experience, it just means its easier to confabulate a mixed CWoD setting with Mage's setting assumptions, so long as you dont set them in steel.

                        Your Mileage May Vary, and all that.

                                        I'd say the key to Mage is to keep it focused on street level (to keep it from getting out of hand). Something that's also pretty important is that if a mage is going to be hanging out with a bunch of not-other-mages, those other characters are really going to make that mage think more in their terms than a traditional Mage game mage would. There are both advantages and disadvantages to that. A lot of conflicting Absolute Truths is probably going to severely hamper the mage's enlightenment, but at the same time it's probably going to give them access to a lot of things that no other mage can have or know and if they can handle it and endure, the pay-off will likely put them way ahead of most other mages who have not had that very uncommon set of experiences and revelations from the other player's characters.

                                        Alternatively, you could kind of mess around a bit with the idea of just who might cause Paradox. There are plenty of reasons why every other creature would cause some issues when they see the mage do something and then say "WTF!!! I don't believe it!!!"
