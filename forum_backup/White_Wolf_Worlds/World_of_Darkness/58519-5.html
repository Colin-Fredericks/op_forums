                Just recently started Kristen Painter's House of Comarre series, which plays out much like a Vampire, Hunter, and Demon crossover (obviously not all on the same side). The female lead, Chrysabelle, is a comarre - basically an incredibly valuable feeding slave, specifically bred/trained/tattooed to remind the elite of vampire society of the sun. But when her master turns up murdered, and she's the prime suspect, she flees . . .and becomes a key pawn in a scheme to free the Fallen.

                Originally posted by Amazon
                Born into a life of secrets and service, Chrysabelle's body bears the telltale marks of a comarré -- a special race of humans bred to feed vampire nobility. When her patron is murdered, she becomes the prime suspect, which sends her running into the mortal world...and into the arms of Malkolm, an outcast vampire cursed to kill every being from whom he drinks.

                Now, Chrysabelle and Malkolm must work together to stop a plot to merge the mortal and supernatural worlds. If they fail, a chaos unlike anything anyone has ever seen will threaten to reign.
                There aren't comarre in the World of Darkness, but the author's setting is similar, and with the occasional hint that she's familiar with Vampire: the Masquerade

                And on the live action front - TNT's Witchblade is gorgeous. Corrupt cops, an occult conspiracy dating back to Nazi Germany, demonic priests . . . Not to mention that most of the fights took place under the influence of the Witchblade, turning each battle into a surreal allegory.

                In this adaptation of the Top Cow comic book, Witchblade is the story of a New York detective, Sara "Pez" Pezzini, whose search for justice brings her into contact with the Witchblade, an ancient, intelligent, living weapon so powerful it can battle Earth's darkest evil forces. Week in and week out, "Pez" employs her skills as a police detective to fight crime. She finds she must employ the Witchblade to combat a much greater and frighteningly organized conspiracy of evil that threatens the very soul of humanity. As Sara tries to keep the secret of the Witchblade, do her job and have a personal life, she finds that her most formidable adversary may be the Witchblade itself.
                Spike TV's live action Blade tv series is excellent, too. They couldn't get Wesley Snipes to play Blade . . . but I think his replacement is 10x better in the role of the half-vampire. Much of the action revolves around Blade getting a newly turned vampire to "go undercover" for him, allowing the series to focus a lot on vampiric society

                    Welcome to Night Vale--It's neither a movie nor TV show, but a podcast pretending to be a community radio program. It is great fodder for any WoD game. It's not nearly as subtle (or serious) as the WoD, but the ideas can be tweaked and toned down. It's kind of like if all the weirdness in the World of Darkness was out in the open. Pretty much all the standard conspiracies are real in Night Vale, plus a whole lot of original ones.

                    It's free on iTunes and Youtube.

                    "The Summer Reading Program for children and teens has begun at the Night Vale Public Library. This comes as an alarming surprise, given that the program was abolished by the City Council thirty years ago. Though parents and teenagers have asked on several occasions to reinstate the program, the City Council has maintained its position, citing lack of taxpayer funds, the extreme danger posed by books, the peril of exposing children to librarians, and of course, the incident that precipitated the ban, which the town’s older residents will refer to only as “The Time Of Knives”."

                                                        Originally posted by CaptOtter View Post
                                                        Yeah, it was called Kindred: The Embraced, and it was terrible (thank Aaron Spelling--at least he didn't put Tori in it though). Vampires could walk around in daylight if they'd just fed (like, any vampire, not just vampires that should have Fort); the Toreador Primogen clearly had Protean 4 (which isn't unheard of, but, c'mon now); there was no mention of the Tremere or Sabbat whatsoever; Malkavians (as I recall) were treated the way mages treat marauders (i.e., hunted and killed as a matter of course); the only independent clan that ever made any appearance were the Assamites (and even then, it was basically limited to a tiny, lily white chick in a black turtleneck with a knife); the word Camarilla is never said..
                                                        As I understand it, a whole lot of oWoD Vampire canon/Clans/terminology was intentionally left out because Mark Rein-Hagen, White Wolf and/or copyright holders thus for Vampire: the Masquerade refused to allow Spelling to use them in Kindred: the Embraced. This is not meant to be hard detail, as what I know I picked up quite some time after the series came and went, but what I know was related to me by several local players in diffierent groups whose explanations matched evenly.

                                                        The series didn't even get to double-digit episodes, but for what it's worth I'm grateful the proposed inclusion of the Garou didn't come along. I'll take Bitten (in my hometown, no less!) over that any day, thanks.
