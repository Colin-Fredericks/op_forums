                                            I have sat down and read the core book in several editions plus numerous, numerous supplements and I can easily see how people could claim "the same claptrap". Not only are those things painted all over the game, many, many, many people play Werewolf EXACTLY that way. To which, I could add some serious reductionist animistic "kumbaya" mutualistic, self-reinforcing solipsism.

                                            That doesn't mean that Werewolf isn't anything else, nor that it was even meant to be more than that (or, at the very least, to handle the flip side of each of those things in a dynamic game setting... whether they entirely succeeded or not is up for debate). That doesn't mean it has it's own coolness for the values of coolness that it embodies but it doesn't mean that the criticisms of it aren't warranted. Particularly when Werewolf partisans can be particularly strident in slopping Werewolf's core mythology as the "One True Way of which all others are pale and mistaken imitations and reflections".

                                            My central complaint with Werewolf is how, unlike the other games (excluding Hunter for obvious reasons), it doesn't really even give a passing nod to the concepts of "werewolf-dom" that existed before the game. It gives a failed attempt at a gloss that is completely washed out by it's own conceptualizations. Without substantive changes you still can't even play many/most of the werewolf-genre stories within the context of the game.

                                            However, all that being said, it looks like the next iteration of Werewolf that is coming isn't going to address any of these concerns. And why would it? After all those things are already on a spectrum of "acceptable" to "what we play for". But it looks like it might dial up other aspects by several notches. I have the feeling that the whole "struggle against impossible odds" will skew past "let's alt least go down fighting" all the way to "we haven't committed suicide yet because we are too weak to admit it that we are just shit, so we lash out at everyone and everything".

                                                        As someone who has been to other Werewolf forums/sites in the past outside of White Wolf/Onyx Path itself, and I mean places that discuss werewolves and not W:tA:
                                                        I will always find this reasoning deeply ironic and just shake my head at it.

                                                        Originally posted by Ajax View Post
                                                        Particularly when Werewolf partisans can be particularly strident in slopping Werewolf's core mythology as the "One True Way of which all others are pale and mistaken imitations and reflections".

                                                        My central complaint with Werewolf is how, unlike the other games (excluding Hunter for obvious reasons), it doesn't really even give a passing nod to the concepts of "werewolf-dom" that existed before the game. It gives a failed attempt at a gloss that is completely washed out by it's own conceptualizations. Without substantive changes you still can't even play many/most of the werewolf-genre stories within the context of the game.
                                                        I've seen both Werewolf and Therianthrope communities commit to their own versions of the "No True Werewolf" fallacy (This is a variation of the "No True Scotsman" fallacy).
                                                        The people behind the Werewolf Horror Calendar even had a statement about it being against "Hippie Wolves", "Regular Wolves", and "Twilight Beefcake Wolves". Werewolf-News.com even joined in when promoting the calendar.
                                                        And yes, Werewolf: the Apocalypse is often derided as being part of the "Hippie Wolves" or "New Age Wolves" segment that Werewolf fans hate.
                                                        So a lot of these people practice the very same thing you are claiming W:tA fans do. Sometimes without realizing it.

                                                        I get really tired when the "W:tA doesn't stay close to actual Werewolf myths and folklore!" always gets brought up. Especially when some werewolf fans cite Werewolf movies as the actual myth/lore without much deeper insight into where certain ideas actually came from and that some of these ideas have no real connections (Silver, being bitten, etc.).
                                                        So what you are really talking about is that modern media werewolf-genre mainly influenced by Universal's The Wolf Man from the 1940's is taken as the true "lore" and usually outright ignoring a huge swath of much older stories about how varied werewolves can actually be.

                                                        Even the whole "Werewolf's bite infection" is suspect and cited as being a more modern idea that had hardly has any basis in the old lore.
                                                        And yet some Werewolf movie influenced fans get so unhappy whenever something doesn't have the "bite" in it.
                                                        These same type of fans were quick to support Forsaken when it came out, but quickly jumped ship on Forsaken as well when it didn't hold up to their other impossible standards of what "werewolf-dom" should be.

                                                        Getting back on topic:
                                                        I wonder if Paradox will break the W:tA video/computer game curse?
                                                        There was Capcom's Season of the Wolf for the PS1/Saturn that didn't come to pass once Capcom and White Wolf parted ways. And then there was ASC/Dreamforge's Heart of Gaia for the PC that became vaporware.

                                                        Looking through old Inquest magazines, I guess there was news going around at the time that some company was trying to make a Mage computer game as well and yet nothing came out of it.

                                                        I also wonder if Paradox managed to get any rights to the Rage CCG, Arcadia CCG, or V:tES. Would love to see a Print on Demand versions or apps/games in the style of the Yu-gi-oh Tag Force games.
