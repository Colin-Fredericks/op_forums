    Is there one World of Darkness or many?

    01-12-2015, 11:56 PM
    Just help me wrap my head around this. The various settings of the Classic World of Darkness stand alone mechanically, as they should (IMO), and because of that, and various other conflicting cosmological information between them, they should be considered separate worlds, or so seems to be the opinion of some on these forums. I agree that the various games set in the world of darkness tend to revolve around their own core themes, and it only muddies the waters of the games if you start trying to turn the World of Darkness into a RIFTS-style setting. None-the-less, crossovers were a constant fixture of the game until revised edition, and even then continued to have an impact on the greater World of Darkness, like the Week of Nightmares which affected Wraith, Vampire, and Mage.

    Werewolves do their thing, vampires do theirs, changelings do theirs, wraiths do theirs, and mages do whatever they heck they feel like, and because of that, all information that potentially links them to being in the same world should be ignored? It's just a multiverse with a bunch of coincidental crossovers that sound remarkably similar but really aren't the same thing at all? Is this the prevailing opinion on these things?

    Because we can find conflicting mechanics and cosmologies between the games doesn't mean the people at White Wolf weren't talking about the same place...they have far more in common than they have in conflict. Baba Yaga was a super-villain in both Werewolf and Vampire, and there is little doubt they are describing the same creature. The Shadow Lands as described by the vampires calling themselves the Harbingers of Skulls seem remarkably similar to the Shadow Lands described in Wraith. The Tremere (one of the core clans of Vampire) were once part of the Order of Hermes (one of the core traditions of Mage) and both settings mention one another. Many people seem to want to ignore these overlapping aspects the various games. I just don't understand why.

    Yes it's true that anyone running a Werewolf game is probably better off avoiding crossovers with the other settings at their gaming tables. Reconciling the mechanical differences between the settings is a pain in the ass, and little is gained by dipping one's toes into the other lines since the conflicting themes make for bad stories. Let's face it, Werewolf games are about entirely different things than Mage games and introducing mages to a Werewolf game (or vice versa) detracts from the core themes that make each of those games what they are. It still doesn't change that fact that the metaplot of the Classic World of Darkness included both lines in the ongoing and evolving events of a shared world.

    It sure stinks that Baba Yaga was killed on the Vampire end of things for the Werewolf fans, but she was part of that setting too. It's the Wraith players that really have reason to gripe; their game was essentially destroyed by events occurring in a Vampire book. Yep, people ignored it, and kept playing as though the 6th Great Maelstrom never happened, and who could blame them? I'm a fan of many of the game lines, and I also dislike how often they constantly screwed with one another like many other people do. I'm of the belief that these ongoing metaplot crossovers were a big factor in the decision to end the Classic World of Darkness and start fresh.

    I might just be some crotchety old fart that refuses to subscribe to the revisionist history others prefer despite the probability that it makes their games run smoother. Maybe I shouldn't stubbornly cling to past truths and embrace this new view of the old setting. I might have some sick attachment to the way things were because of my love of the old World of Darkness and the nostalgia I feel whenever I play it. Am I completely off my rocker, or are there other people on these forums that share my view on this?

          Each game is basically its own different take on the setting. Some games don't presume too much about the cosmology of their setting, and they can get along fairly well with others, like Hunter" the Reckoning, and to a lesser extent, Mummy, Wraith and Changeling. Others can't have any sort of accord of harmony between them, with the outstanding example of Werewolf and Mage. Vampire and Werewolf also disagree vehemently, and Vampire's crossover mechanics of "highest dot rating wins" could really be seen to universally be shit by players of all other games. Demon doesn't seem like it was every meant to be played with anything else.

          Some people will disagree and cite very early books. Generally, however, this was killed hard post-Chaos Factor and soulforged into an ashtray; by the time Revised came about, every game basically took its balls and went home. There was something to the effect of "multiple worlds" to explain many of the inconsistencies in Mage's capstone book Ascension, too. Basically, while there might be some major events that echo through every WoD, each WoD is its own and exists independently of the other.

                              I think 'Mostly Harmless' (the last HHGttG book - 'And Another Thing' is an abomination that I shall not acknowledge) posits a cosmology that best sums up the differentiation between cWoD game worlds - the Whole Sort of General Mish-Mash. Essentially the entire cWoD is a single universe but it is too complex to all be perceived simultaneously so it must be sliced to make sense of. The core game lines present relatively narrow slices with only the bare bones of other lines represented. Supplements show wider slices that may include some of the other lines flesh.

                              One of the interesting effects of this approach is it allows one slice (gameline) to be affected by something that doesn't really work within its own lore, allowing crossover madness such as Sam.

                                  Of course, there's only one World of Darkness
                                  all the beings are there (as you can see reading some books, and I'm not speaking stricly of the crossovers, but even certain splatbook, core books of the minor lines [mummy, kote, kotek]), but usually they don't interact too much...

                                  There're usually 3 major faults people point in this reasonment:

                                  1. The system are totally different
                                  2. In the books they say "other than what we're writing here, don't care of others line"
                                  3. The pantheon are totally different


                                  (1) and (2) got a simple interpretation, simply the authors don't want you to go around buying 500 hundreds of books, usually is specified that you need only the core book of that line to play (with the addition of the sourcebook YOU wanted to buy maybe and a couple books if the concept is quite advanced, for example the addition of VtM, Blood Magic for VtM, Blood Sacrifice). It's a clever choice, as minimizing the minimal requirement for playing let you enlarge the human gaming base (and even more clever if you want to "attack" a gaming base not really wealthy or already interested in some other game line [like D&D]). In the end, it's an unorthodox strategic move.

                                  (3) is relative to how the WW developed. At a certain time there were a lot of authors working on something like 5 o 6 lines, and sometimes even the material from the same line contradicted each other, because they were written by different individuals at different times, and they could not be expected to take into account of the entire amount of books written precedently written. Someone is saying VtM: Blood Bond or VtM: Diablerie Mexico are not canon because they treat blood bonds differently from the corebooks? Nope.
                                  The main point is, if you can't or don't want to device your own version of the cosmological situation, the real version is the one of the game line you're playing. They're explicitly opposed, each line had 2 or 3 main editions, if at a certain point they wanted to retcon all in a single origin, they would have done.
                                  You're playing Werewolf? Then the vampires ARE a vicious strain of wyrm-ridden corpses... you want to add the vampiric lore? of course, in the beginning there was only on vampire, created by 1 or 2 of the heads of the wyrm... he created others vampire and every time that the curse is transmitted, it weakens. Done, here's the whole vampiric cosmology retconned.
                                  All the events happen in the same cursed world, but the different beings see differt facets even when they call them in the same way. I remember even a curious statement in Gehenna (now, if you just count to seven, someone will jump out shouting "Gehenna is not canon!" despite the fact it was the conclusive book of a game-line :P ), it said something like "And what about werewolves, mages and all the other beings in the World of Darkness? They're occupied with their own apocalypses, don't bother with them if you don't want to throw them in the central stage of your own End of Times" [Mind you, it's just an outline of what I remember without recovering the book, and on top, it's translated from my own language; the actual English version may be slightly different].

                                  In the end, yes. Is the same world, but is the Storyteller the one that decide what is real

                                          The way I think of it is, each game line in oWoD is part of a single game universe--but as described by an unreliable narrator. Other systems are misinterpreted or neglected because they aren't so relevant to whoever's telling the story, or they are flat-out wrong for dramatic or ideological purposes. The best you can do is to write something that will keep the grumbling to a minimum.

                                                      In the end like I said in another thread there is more evidence on the side of the one world theory than there is against it. The conflicting issues are mostly mythological or naming issues easily explained by misunderstandings and point of view especially since most of those are related to issues no one can verify. Vampiric mythos is tied to Christianity yes, currently, but most pre-Christian elders might not exactly buy that line and there is precious little proof of it's validity, they are stories that is all.
