    All that is well and good. But the real beauty of the earlier suggestion is that it at least partly answers the question of “why can't Sleepers perform Technocratic Procedures?” They can; but only if they're guided through the Procedure by someone who knows what he's doing (e.g., an Enlightened Scientist).

    Then you get the follow-up question, “why can't Sleepers learn to perform the Procedure on their own?” That can also be addressed with existing rules, though not as gracefully: they can “learn” the Procedure, if the Procedure is turned into a Virtual Wonder and given to them. Think of it like The Matrix's “I know Kung Fu”: it's a way of inserting capabilities (in this case, Procedures) directly into someone's mind. The catch is that, per the rules as written, each instance of such a Wonder requires an investment of Quintessence; and it's implanted, not learned. Which is why I apply a fairly simple house rule: a teacher can gradually craft a Virtual Wonder in a student's mind without having to incorporate any Quintessence into it — if the student puts the appropriate effort into learning it and expends an appropriate amount of XP in lieu of the usual Quintessence cost.

    Et voila! A (nearly) rules-as-written means of addressing the most common critique about Technocrats not knowing that what they do is, in fact, Magick: their Procedures can be taught to and performed by individuals who are willing to put in the effort to learn, whether or not they're Enlightened — provided they have a qualified instructor; just like their Devices can be picked up and used by people who don't abuse them (read: Extraordinary Citizens: Sleepers with a sufficiently Technocratic Focus).

            No, I wouldn't limit it to the Technocracy. That said, crafting a Talisman (which is what is needed to allow a Sleeper to “learn” a Rote) requires the investment of a permanent point of Willpower. This limits its usefulness when teaching “acolyte magick” unless you can offload the Willpower cost onto the student as well (possibly in the form of, say, an 8-xp surcharge on learning the Rote). Note that, under the right circumstances (i.e., when using proper facilities), the Technocracy gets to waive that Willpower cost, making it a more useful option for them. But yes, it did occur to me that this could be used to provide a Sphere Magick substitute for Sorcery.

            I go into more depth on this in my Spellbooks hack.

                        And it's important to remember, from an OOC perspective, that the person who is calibrating and firing the weapon is being given that ability via the enlightened will of the Etherite. They are simply part of the instrument. Furthermore, unless you're using Correspondence you'll need to be present, and unless you're using Time it will need to be done within the Scene and not later on. It's also going to be roll for roll; if you want to give someone the ability to use these powers long term then you're going to be handing them a Wonder or making them into a Wonder, or trying to use magick to help them Awaken, or trying to teach them Sorcery, and all of those are quite complicated (especially the last two, since triggering Awakening is not easy/simple, and Mages aren't that great at teaching people static magic since they don't actually use it).

                        IC explanations of this could be quite meta "only with the oversight of my enlightened scientific paradigm can a regular sleeper like yourself perform such procedures" or more obvious "well you obviously got it wrong, these procedures are incredibly complex and I shouldn't have assumed you'd be able to do it without direct oversight".

                        But this sort of tool does give stay-at-home VAs more power; imagine a character... you know that Guy in the Show who stays at base and looks at the computer screens and tells people "what the hell they're looking at"? Your Chloes (24), your Arams (Blacklist). Well using Data such a character can perform effects from afar, via other people, using Management and Telecommunications instruments. All that matters is that they believe they can perform the effect; helping their agent at the scene blow a circuit to cause an explosion? Awesome, use Forces 2-4 and Data 2-4, possibly with some Prime. Making the agent create electricity out of thin air to throw at people? I mean MAYBE. Some VAs have technomagickal beliefs (the techgnosi merit covers this), but this is going to be a Vulgar / Working Without Usual Tools situation in most cases, making it much harder.

                            I’ve just been reading several of the technocracy books, most notably convention book $yndicate, and it makes it quite clear that the technocrats know they are forcing their will on reality, just as tradition mages do. Convention mages know they are fighting an ascension war. They know they have to be careful about paradox. Remember that the NWO is all about controlling the message, and the $yndicate loves to control the messengers. The terms that the technocracy uses are tools in their ascension war and shouldn’t be seen as strictly indicative of what the conventions actually believe. The recognition of this fact is why the convention book: $yndicate specifically calls out the “war of words” in which the $yndicate acknowledges the term “paradox”, but says they prefer the term “Market Correction” while the NWO and ItX call the same phenomenon “statistical inevitability”.. they then suggest that in mixed company a person should just say “paradox”. They see this war of words as an actual conflict with the NWO.
                            However, there are also reasons why the unenlightened wouldn’t be able to use technocrat items.. allowing them to do so would be dangerous. Certainly a MIB’s gun would be gene-locked or SMART-fire or something like that.. some people of these safety features might be overcome if the technocrat has time to explain it to the user. Also, there are some items,like the MIDAS card, that are specifically designed so the unenlightened can use them.
