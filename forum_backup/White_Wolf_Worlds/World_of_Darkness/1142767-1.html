                                            That the Fomorians are unpleasant and their rule over men would be a horrible time to be one does not change the fact that they still are a very primordial part of the Dreaming. The Tuatha, I believe, understood this and that is part of the reason why Fomorians and the Thallain were imprisoned instead of fought to extinction. Another part could be that the Tuatha did not wish to risk casualties and further war when surrender was offered but that is beside the point.

                                            Yes you can justify fighting Nightmares and yes some might want a life without being one, just like some humans would prefer to be something else, but it doesn't mean that there is, nor should be, a cure to someones basic nature. Besides considering that Legacies are a mechanical way to portray ones personality and since the Nightmare Legacy is always dominant in Thallain, though they also have a Seelie or Unseelie secondary Legacy which represents their human half (C20 p.370), it is unlikely that they would come to hate their basic nature.

                                            But incase there is a "cure" that can change the basic nature of a Thallain then there should exist a "cure" for the Kithain condition. If one can twist a piece of Fomorian Dream into a Tuathan Dream, as I assume most of you mean cure as something that would change them into their Kithain counterparts, then there should be a way to untwist a piece of Tuathan Dream back into a piece of Fomorian Dream.

                                            And admittedly there is a precedent for this sort of thing in the Sevartal birthright, but even that is not a permanent change, but I like to think it more as an exception that enforces the rule. Naturally YMMV.

                                            But in the end I am the last person to restrict how you want to play the game, if you gain enjoyment from a plot where a repentant Thallain seeks to cure his "condition" then by all means do. I'm just not getting the whole point of view where we look at something different and immediately think it should be cured from being itself. The general point of Thallain is that they like to be themselves, hence even mechanically having unique primary Legacies to represent that, no matter how we humans see them.
