    Originally posted by Thanateros View Post
    I think you underestimate the impact of implementing a truly ubiquitous, cohesive metaphysic and mythology;
    I don't think anyone is underestimating the impact. The general sentiment of the disagreement is that the impact is more harm than benefit to the enjoyment of the cWOD for most groups.

    I've been playing and running the cWOD for just under 20 years. The vast majority of games I've been involved with (say ~85%) were single game-line. All the PCs were the same supernatural type, and the game was run with only books from that type mattering. The existence of other supernaturals was always twisted to benefit the metaphysics, cosmology, and mythology of the primary game being played. What is the positive impact of an over-arching WOD cohesiveness to that mode of play?

    The rest of the games that were not single supernatural-types only, were almost always either convention one-shots (which meant there was never time to worry about such things), or larger format games (LARPs, online play). With that second category, there is a very common response of downplaying the relevance/power of the "global powers" of the WOD. This isn't even about having PC vampires, werewolves, and mages (oh my!). It's about having so many PCs, and often a large disparity of power levels withing each group, that you need to loosen the grip on the upper levels of power for the older PCs to have space to occupy so they aren't crowding out the younger PCs for niches to fill. When you're part of running a game with 200+ PCs spread across most of the major game lines, with everything from mortals, to support (ghouls/kin/etc.), to full supernaturals represented, you tend to need to modify the game in a fashion that inherently downplays the cosmological differences rather than trying to remove them through unification. The VtM players don't want to get shoved into a different box, they want to play vampires/ghouls in VtM just with more other critters running around the city. You end up spending far more time addressing system contradictions than setting ones; because you can design the local setting to facilitate this mode of play more easily.

    So, again, where is the utility of this impact? To that mode of play, what does this unification actually add to the experience?

    I was motivated to resolve the contradictions in the classic World of Darkness because the Triat is my favorite pantheon of any fictional universe, because I am a geek who simply enjoyed the mental exercise of resolving the contradictions, and because I was curious to see if anyone one else had dramatic solutions to the contradictions (especially the bigger contradictions involving vampire elders, Technocracy, and/or Pentex).
    Personally, I think any effort that starts with, "this part of the metaphysical/cosmological setting of the WOD is my favorite," is going to end up shooting itself in the foot. It starts you off with a bias towards options that put your favorite thing front and center over options that would resolve any perceived contradictions with as little impact as possible.

    When you hamper yourself by putting forward, "the Triat is now the grand metaphysical constant of the WOD," you end up adding work. The existence of Pentex, the Technocracy, and Elder vampires goes from something that either isn't actually a contradiction or a very minor patch (depending on your interpretation), into a huge effort because the Technocracy and the Elders were never designed to fit into the Triatic system.

                        I think you're missing my point here - in science we use predictive power as a measure of how good an explanatory theory is.
