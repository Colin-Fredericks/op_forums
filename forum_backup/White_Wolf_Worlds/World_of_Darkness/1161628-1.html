                                    Well, first of your friend really need to revisit this "don't house-rule" thing.

                                    Not because I think it is important or such on a general manner, but because M20 was specifically built that way. It is vague on some critical rules because it is meant to be fine-tuned for your chronicle, and be receptive for rules from previous editions. At least your friend should choose a previous edition to rule on such stuff, or he isn't actually using the rules on M20.

                                    Second, your friend could just look at it from out-of-the-box. Think about real life, and then think about someone just hiring a mad soldier as a body guard and arming the soldier with a sub-machine gun. Think about everything this person will have to deal with just because of it (and yeah, it isn't impossible to do). How the world at large see the PC? How people react? An extremely powerful character that stays out of trouble and plays with the group isn't actually that of a problem. But if this character is a trouble for people around, then those people will find a way to deal with it.

                                    Given, a character that pretty much became a threat on the level of a Master can easily attract the actual attention of a Master. Sanctums can be targeted by enemies. A character that heavily relies on a few resources can have those very resources targeted. Instead of thinking on how to rule out the advantages, let the NPCs do the hard work with everything they have.

                                    And if in the end it isn't possible to have fun with this guy, then get rid of the player altogether. The game is about everyone having a good time, and if a member needs to deny fun to other, they simply can't play together.
