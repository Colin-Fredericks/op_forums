                      It's a popular misconception that he was TRYING to get people killed, in an almost Hunger-Games-ish scenario. Really, survival of the fittest had absolutely nothing to do with it. Kayaba's ambition was to create a world, a true reality in the virtual plane. And for it to be *real*, there had to be consequences. There had to be DEATH. I took him to be a high functioning sociopath who didn't set out to get people killed in and of itself, but was too detached from the human condition to really understand that what he was doing was HORRIBLE. He had a dream, and he was going to achieve it.

                      Whether or not people died, that would be because of their own choices and actions. He simply enabled the option for death to be a very real consequence of those actions. Y'know, just like real life.

                                              I once suggested a game rather like SAO with MtAs and his historical counterparts but it never got past that stage and it was probably a little bit too ambitious.

                                              The backstory was that the 'Kayaba' behind this was an older mage that had started as Virtual Adept but defected to the Technocracy. However he had two constants in his time with both groups, his charisma and his obsession with the Digital Web, virtual reality and Ascension. He didn't create the virtual world of his alone and has with him a group of like-minded Technocrats and Traditionalists. But as far as they were concerned it was to be a safe testbed for experiments on Awakening sleepers and the like. Kayaba on the other hand, had privately come to the conclusion that an Awakening could not happen in a safe, risk-free environment and that only by struggling and risking life could one truly Awakening. As a result, on the day the game went live, he either murderer or trapped nearly all his comrades within the game.

                                              The virtual world would be the world of Mage: the Sorcerer's Crusade, albeit in a fictional world with the Order of Reason and the Nine Traditions set against each other. Indeed Kayaba would announce to the players that only way to win was for one group to win the Ascension War, freeing them and only them.

                                              The group would either be a group of players trying to survive and escape the game, or a group of mages and/or technocrats trying to uncover Kayaba's whereabouts and try to free the players.

                                              Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. They're indomitable. Indomitable.
