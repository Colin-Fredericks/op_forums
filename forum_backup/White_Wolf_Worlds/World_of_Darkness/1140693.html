    New Player Confused on v20 Things

    09-15-2017, 09:59 PM
    Hello! I've been playing WoD things for a while now, but I've recently decided to get into Changeling the Dreaming. I've got a question that has been nagging my mind since I got and had time to read the book: Why are there so many Kiths? There are 13 Kithain, 19 Thallain, 7 Adhene, 13 Nunnehi, 6 Phyla, and 8 more Kithain at the end of the book, and they all have character creation options to fit their different abilities and cultures. I'm not sure what the writers intent was in making so many kiths. Was it to give NPCs definitive and unique mechanics? Give players a lot of options so they'll never go unsatisfied? Encourage variety in motleys? The writers certainly earned their pay, but they've left newer players like myself a bit confused and a little overwhelmed.

    Another thing that I'm not too clear on is the distinction between dark glamour and normal glamour. Dark glamour is only really mentioned as a plot point in the book, with no real differing mechanics from normal glamour except its existence in the Autumn world brought back a few darker fae. If we're to play a kith that has been brought back to the world with dark glamour, are we expected to use dark glamour instead of normal glamour? If so, how do we go about getting that? Are there different ways to collect dark glamour vs normal glamour, or is it all the same in the end?

    I appreciate any and all answers and further discussion, especially if there's a question I didn't talk about here that would be important to mention.

      They were created in different books by different authors over the years, and then compiled together in one book for C20.

      Part of the reason to is because kiths are meant to be culture specific, so when you write about a new culture they get a whole new set of kiths.

      Mechanically I think dark glamor works like regular glamor. Although Kenning could show you what kind.

          You might be running up against the nature of the 20th anniversary editions. While the X20 books do their best to be accessible to newbies, they're also omnibus editions for the old fans. V20 collected just about every published bloodline from Vampire, and M20 revised some Crafts that hadn't even made it into the Revised edition. In short, it's a little bit fanservice, a little bit an effort to revise some stuff that never got past the old editions.

              Dark glamour is a plot device. There aren't any rules around it in the C20 core. The Thallain aren't really intended as player characters. There's certainly enough rules so folks could play them if they really want, but it would be about the equivalent of playing a Black Spiral Dancer or a Nephandi as a PC. They'd gain Glamour in generally the same way as the Kithain, only instead of being inspiring, they'd be horrifying.

                  The series of 20th Anniversary Edition corebooks began, with Vampire 20th, as a project to renew and reward long-neglected interest in the World of Darkness games with a heavy-duty collector's edition that compiled as many of the character option expansions that had been released over Vampire's lifetime into one big pretty tome. It was expensive and huge because it was conceived with longtime devotees in mind.

                  Since then, Onyx Path has broadened the aim of the 20th Anniversary Edition lines some, with new material and less of a collector-centric premium focus, but the WoD20 corebooks still inherit that format of being huge tomes that collect years' worth of supplementary material and additions to the setting into one dense place. Occasionally that also means straining to fit everything in the book, so some more peripheral character options that exist in their own context get less space to explain what they're about and what it is they bring to a Changeling game than their original books might have had.

                  TL;DR The book you bought is actually a decade's worth of books crammed together into one; the different Kithain and such were originally spread out over years of different publications, which might have been a more manageable pace but also required cross-referencing different books to use them with one another.
