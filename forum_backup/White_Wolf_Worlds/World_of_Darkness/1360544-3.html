            A large part of it has to do with my Protocols, which are sort of analogous to the Traditions in Vampire, with the major globals ones being established by the Grand Council of the Nine Traditions (or in the case of Tradition-specific ones, by the leadership of that Tradition), and then moving downward from regional groups like the Islamic Web of Faith or Africa's Great House of Stone then to more local ones by city or regional Councils or even individual Chantries (or in some cases even specific rules a Cabal would establish for its members).
            One of my main ones was the Veil of Secrecy, which was established both to protect the Awakened members of the Traditions from outside enemies and to protect the wider mundane world from the more dangerous aspects of what the Awakened deal with. It's part and parcel with the Traditions effectively being a resistance group against a global tyrannical conspiracy; they have to protect themselves and their activities from discovery while also protecting innocent bystanders from being caught in the crossfire of their secret war.
            The former part involves shielding themselves from Technocratic discovery and infiltration, corruption by the Nephandi, attacks by witch hunters, and getting entangled in the plots of other supernaturals. The latter involves making sure the ultimate truths about Reality and the Tapestry are only revealed to those properly initiated (usually through the various Gateway Arts each Tradition - and even Craft - have to introduce members and even acolytes to their world view and beliefs), that dangerous Mystic items and magic don't cause unnecessary harm to the unawakened masses that one dwells around, and that the invisible world of spirits, demons, and other beings don't cause damage to the material world.
            So, my Ascension Guardians of the Veil basically serve as the Nine Traditions' intelligence service, police force, and neighborhood watch. Various subgroups within it focus on infiltrating the Technocracy, keeping Talismans or cursed objects out of mundane hands, shutting down cults devoted to Infernalism, Spectres or worse, protecting the families and friends of Mages from being targeted by the Technocracy, investigating crimes within the Awakened community, shooing nosey bystanders away from Chantries or Nodes, and so forth.
            Basically, life the other three groups (Adamantine Arrow, Mysterium, Silver Ladder), I ended up incorporating them as something for when the game would grow beyond the local level and start getting into a more global scale, be it in the realm of military action, scholarly research, politics, or intrigue/espionage. Likewise, I incorporated the Free Council as a way for Orphan, outsider, and Hollow One centric chronicles to move up a level into regional or even global activities within the Ascension War.

                    So, my Mage: The Dark Ages (as opposed to DA:M) set up.

                    I prefer to set games in mid to late 12th century. This is mostly just because I prefer that period when Constantinople hasn't been sacked, the Third Crusade hasn't started, and Richard I, Eleanor of Aquitaine, Frederick Barbarossa, William the Lion, and Alfonso VIII are active.

                    I also have the Tremere outed as vampires already, and the Order of Hermes having begun a crusade/wizards' march against them. This is a shadow war, but other Mages and Vampires are already getting caught up in it as both collateral damage and from blowback by other forces. Those aware of this fight have started referring to it as "the Sorcerers' Crusade". One of the forces causing this blowback is the Sword of Gabriel, also known as the Sacred Order of Pure Thought and True Faith. This conflict has caused the various factions of Awakened - known as Fellowships - to tentatively ally with each other when their needs seem to align.

                    The period Essences are based on the four classic elements, Fire (Dynamic), Earth (Pattern), Water (Primordial), and Air (Questing). The Spheres are Connection (Correspondence), Fate, Energia (Forces), Nature (Life), Materia (Matter), Soul (Mind), Creation (Prime), Celestia (Spirit), and Eternity (Time).

                    My Fellowships:

                    The Craftmasons,
                    proto-Humanist artisans, artificers, and scholars formed during the Carolingian Renaissance by remnants of the Brotherhood of the Rule. (And no, they are not in any way tied to the Order of Hermes, whom they detest.) They seek to perfect God's fallen creation and help usher in a new golden age for mankind, using faith and reason to advance the lot of the common man and serve the common good. Medicine, trade, and sacred architecture all serve this end, as does education and the destruction of monsters and other demonic forces that prey on humanity and sew disorder. They work their arts through sacred mathematics and geometry, especially in the design of buildings and landscapes, as well as through alchemy, master crafts, and forceful logical argument. They focus on Connection, Creation (Prime), and Matereria (Matter).
                    The Craftmasons include several specialist Guilds, each with its own focus and concerns:
                    • The Bright Lions, alchemists and natural philosophers seeking to unlock the secrets of creation so as to perfect it and themselves. Their knowledge of herbs and poisons make them skilled healers and assassins, while their alchemical formula are able to produce elixirs and other objects of wonder.
                    • The Cosians, a collective of traders, merchants, healers, and diplomats, they originated out of the wool and cloth trades and are primarily made up of and led by women. They view themselves as the menders of a broken world and weavers of a better future, with a sacred calling to mend damages and bind together communities.
                    • The Iron Maul, warriors and hunters sword to use the tools of reason and faith to defend the helpless and destroy the enemies of humanity. Members wield masterpiece weapons of war and alchemical objects of destruction in their never-ending vigil.
                    • The Golden Pyramid, an alliance of merchants, traders, and guild masters who help fund the works of the greater fellowship. To them, wealth and commerce are tools that improve society and uplift the individual to greatness. They prefer subtle arts of diplomacy and manipulation blended with the best tools and tricks good coin can purchase.
                    (Concepts: Vengeful monster hunter, Disgruntled nun wishing to become a surgeon, Journeyman artisan striving to craft his masterpiece, Waldensian rabble rouser, Alchemist assassin.)


                    The Gardeners of the Tree of Life,
                    secret pagans who preserve the mystic arts and sacred rites of the Old Ways while acting as caretakers of the Tree of Life, that which connects and ties together every living thing in creation. Tracing themselves back by blood to the primordial Wyck, they continue to practice the secret rituals of the ancient Greeks, Germans and Celts. Through the use of herbs, chants, and sacrifices to forgotten gods, they help remind the world of its forgotten past and the power of Nature and the Wild. Most work their Art through the Old Ways of pre-Christian Europe, including herbalism and handcrafts, blood sacrifices, and pagan rituals. Others incorporate more mainstream methods such as astrology and folk charms. They focus on Energia (Forces), Fate, and Nature.
                    The Gardeners include traditional Branches, each with its own practices and rites:
                    • The Kindly Ones, secretive and aloof even from their fellow Gardeners, they are twisters of fate who punish those who transgress against ancient laws and customs. All are skilled in the arts of divination and cursing, and traditionally work in covens of three.
                    • The Kings of the Grove, scions of ancient bloodlines, many going back to ancient gods or to the Wyck themselves. They hold positions of power and influence in villages, towns and noble houses.
                    • The Oaken Shield, an order of knights who are secretly sworn to protect and defend the sacred places of Nature and of the Old Faiths. They wield powerful martial magics and even ancient artifact weapons.
                    • The Wild Heart, shapeshifters and skin-riders who spend as much time in the form of beasts as they do men. Each has a totem animal tied to her heart's blood. Many live as hermits or foresters in the wilderness, but others hide within the confines of civilization.
                    • The Poor Sisters of Eve, a heretical order of nuns devoted to the Mother of All, distrusted by many within the Fellowship. They practice the arts of herbalism, midwifery, and healing, as well as secret fertility rites and charm-making.
                    (Concepts: Wise woman of the woods, Secret power behind the Fief, Wandering troubadour searching for ancient tales, Noblewoman who secretly runs with wolves, Conflicted nun trying to reconcile her faith and magic.)


                    The Keepers of the Sacred Fire,
                    a secret society devoted to preserving and safeguarding the wisdom of the angels within God's Creation, including those found within the unconverted and the ancients of the world. A mix of clergy and layman, many of them are monastics, scholars, and cunning men and women who carefully collect esoteric lore, and have a reputation as exorcists and healers. The fellowship is believed to trace itself back to the Vestal temples of Ancient Rome, if not further back, having become Christian in the latter half of the Roman Empire. The Keepers are masters of theurgy and demonology, focusing their arts through rituals calling on the true names of angels, herbalism, and folk charms. They focus on Celestia, Nature, and Soul.
                    The Keepers include several dedicated Lodges, each with its own sacred calling and specialization:
                    • The Brotherhood of the Fox, wandering travelers, tinkers, and troubadours who lurk among the more disreputable areas of society, usually causing trouble wherever they go. Members argue that such disruption and change is necessary to prevent Creation from stagnating, but more than a few simply enjoy playing the role of trickster or fool.
                    • The Hearthkeepers, preservers of hearth and home, and of community and culture. Many are women, serving as village wise women, household servants, or nuns, working to preserve the unique wisdom and magic of women, including the rites of forgotten religions and goddesses. They view themselves as sacred guardians and healers devoted to the protection of their families and people.
                    • The Librum Numinia, a collective of scholars, philosophers, and artists dedicated to collecting the stories and lore of Christendom and beyond, scouring them for the hidden wisdom of the angels and insight into the will and plans of God. They consider all lore, no matter how heretical or even potentially blasphemous to be valuable in their quest.
                    • The Red Spear of Longinus, a martial order of knights, lay warriors, and crusaders sworn to the recovery and protection of sacred relics, both Christian and otherwise, and to battle the machinations of demons and other unclean spirits.
                    (Concepts: Young peasant touched by angels, Demon-hunting knight, Cryptic hermit who speaks with animals, Traveling seller of questionable charms and relics, Divinely inspired artist.)

                            My alternate Dark Ages Fellowships pt 2.

                            The Memento Mori, a mystery cult devoted to keeping the secrets of life, death, and destiny, with roots among Ancient Greece and Rome, as well among the pagan north. Hiding among various monastic and knightly orders, merchant houses, and noble families, they see themselves as agents and facilitators of God's divine plan and the hands of fate. When the world goes against the divine plan, the world suffers, as do the people in it. Members of the fellowship are as much healers and diplomats as they are killers and manipulators. They often work their Arts through old forms of necromancy and divination, often calling upon ancestor spirits, forgotten gods, and the darker angels for information and aid. Sacred oaths, backed up by the power of God and Fate, provide additional power. They also practice the sciences of alchemy and astrology, as well as the more common crafts of herbalism and physic. They focus on Celestia, Eternity, and Fate.
                            The Memento Mori include a number of hidden circles, each with its own mysteries and rites.
                            • The Golden Chalice, a secret society of courtiers, spies, and assassins originating among the courts of Constantinople. They mix alchemical arts with the powers of dark angels to subtly manipulate individuals and societies in to accordance with God's divine plan.
                            • The Knights of Nyssa, an order of crusader knights sworn to the protection of sacred sites and relics, both Christian and Pagan. Famed for the power of their sacred oaths and blessed weapons, they specialize in the destruction of wicked ghosts and other baleful dead.
                            • The Leaden Coin, a collective of merchant houses who trace their linages back to ancient families of power in the Mediterranean, Northern Europe, and the British Isles. They use the arts of divination and necromancy to make and grow great fortunes, funding the Fellowship and influencing society.
                            • The Rhymers, wondering minstrels, troubadours, and bards wise in the ways of Fate. They offer advice, protection, and prodding to to those who need help in reaching their ordained destiny, preferring the subtle arts of musical manipulation, folk charms, and rhyming spells.
                            (Concepts: Traveling troubadour helping a young knight become a hero, Clever daughter of a wealthy merchant, Lost crusader haunted by his past, Faceless servant who sees all, Mistress of the arts of death.)

                            The Sacred Passion, a heretical Gnostic-Christian sect based on a text called the Nine Sacred Passions, teaching that all emotions are a vital link between the human soul and the divine world. By pursing the passions of Joy, Love, Sympathy, Ambition, Grief, Fear, Envy, Hate, and Rage, one is able to surpass the limits of the material world and hear the heartbeat of God. The Fellowship has attracted an eclectic collection of nobles, clergy, artisans, monastics, and commoners, all of whom pursue their passions through prayer and meditation, ritual magic rites, alchemical and herbalist concoctions, theurgic summoning of angels and demons, and fasting and other ritual penance. The more powerful the emotional response, the better a focus for their Arts. They prefer to focus on Eternity, Nature, and Soul.
                            The Sacred Passion includes several Cults, each with its own methods and focus.
                            • L'Jongleur Amore, focused on Love and Joy, striving to bring beauty and romance into the fallen world through chivalry and courtly love. They are nobles, knights, troubadours, and poets who excel at the subtle social graces of Ars Cupidae. (Rumors persist of a rival faction devoted to Lust and Jealousy who work to cultivate pain and tragedy.)
                            • The Silver Ladder, focused on Ambition and Envy, striving for power and influence in both the mundane and mystical realms. Nobles, clergy, and merchants devoted to self-interest and personal advancement through the use of social manipulation, favors wrested from angels or demons, and alchemical elixirs (including poisons).
                            • The Studiosi, focused on Hate and Rage, an underground movement of radical Gnostic heretics devoted to the destruction of the Church Material that serves the Rex Mundi and of the very prisons of their own flesh through self-denial, flagellation, and violence.
                            • The Weeping Eye, focused on Sympathy and Grief, who believe they have a sacred duty to bear witness to the pain and suffering of the world. Largely a monastic sect, they draw strength and wisdom from seeing how others deal with misfortune and loss.
                            (Concepts: L'Belle Dame sans Merci, Priest experiencing sin so that he may repent, Itinerant healer overwhelmed by others' pain, Violent Cathar radical, Matchmaking noblewoman unable to find her own true love.)

                                My game is set in the years leading up to WWII and I'm focusing on the coming conflict, so I've made quite a few changes to both Traditions and the Technocracy.

The Elemental Dragons of Asia are largely their own masters.

This was the staging area for Czar Vargo, and his ideals are very much dominant for these Etherites. There's also a splinter group who left the Society and joined the Technocratic Soviets.

                                The Third Reich is home to a conspiracy of Technocrats, Traditionalists, vampires, and Garou. They operate under the Black Sun banner and are led by a secret cabal of powerful supernaturals. In the game, the Black Sun organization has fielded a mixed cabal with a Hermetic, an It X in a hardsuit, and a Get of Fenris, supported by Extraordinary Citicens and teleported in by yet another mixed cabal.

This division has divided houses and chantries, but the majority of the European chantries are controlled by the Goetheans.                                 The night-folk of both the Third Reich and the Japanese Empire are secretly led by a group of Nephandi, serving a European Earthbound and the Yama King Mikaboshi, respectively.

                                    • The White Doves, with the sacred calling to spread the message of the mercy and succor of the One True God, they serve the Fellowship as messengers, missionaries, apologists, and when necessary investigators and spies.
                                    (Concepts: Righteous crusader of mercy and redemption, Eternally questioning philosopher, Itinerant faith healer, Servant of the local bishop, Wise hermit of the forest.)

                                                    And my two Dark Ages antagonist sects (and some lesser groups).

                                                    The Sword of Gabriel, aka The Sacred Order of Pure Thought and True Faith. Founded on the divine revelation of One World, One God, One Church, they swear to uphold and protect the Church of Rome, to seek out and eliminate heresy, to convert unbelievers, and to defend the faithful from darkness and error. Those priests, crusaders, scholars, nobles, and laymen, known as the Sword-sworn, work their Arts through prayer, church ritual, holy relics, the true names of angels, and the power of God's Word. Healing and divinely revealed wisdom are just as important as God's wrath, but in the wake of their Illuminated Inquisition called to reign in the excesses of wizards and witches, many are starting to favor blade and fire over correction and conversion. They focus on Celestia, Energia, and Soul.
                                                    The Sword has a number of organized Cabals, each with its own views and methods of caring out their sacred mission.
                                                    • The White Tower : The World of God is a sacred thing, as is His Creation and the sacred revelations he has left for the faithful. Scholars, philosophers, and other learned men and women, they collect and study knowledge and lore, ensuring it is in agreement with the teachings of the Church before allowing it to be passed on to the masses.
                                                    • The Order of the Lion : Christ will return not as a lamb, but as a lion, and the members of the Order are sworn to be lions of God. Steadfast in devotion and relentless in the pursuit of evil, these crusader knights and lay men at arms, as well as those who sponsor them, bear arms in service to the Sacred Order and the Church.
                                                    • The Holy Banner : The Word of God and the reign of the Holy Church must be spread to all corners of the world. Heathen and heretic must be converted, while the lords of Christendom must be made to serve the ends of the Church. Through use of law, politics, finance, and conversion, these prelates, merchants, ladies in waiting, and seneschals all carry the Banner.
                                                    • The Razors : Sin and error are a sickness which must be cut out of the body of the faithful. To this end exist the secretive members of the Razors, walking invisibly through all corners of medieval society, even among Jews and other nonbelievers. Much of their time is spent gathering information and identifying potential threats or allies, but when called upon they also act as holy assassins.
                                                    (Concepts: Faithful lady in waiting, Fearless demon hunter, Stern abbotess with no patience for frivolity, University metaphysicist, Vengeful victim of magic misuse.)

                                                    The Pax Hermetica, aka the Order of Hermes. Considering themselves the only real practitioners of the True Art and all other practitioners and fellowships beneath them. Already having made no friends with their overbearing behavior toward the various Fellowships, their recent shadow war against the undead has brought nothing but misery and hardship to the mages of Europe. Save the lost Diedne and the now condemned Tremere, all of the founding Houses still exist, as well as the new Ex Miscellanea.

                                                    (Concepts: Relentless vampire hunter, Lord of the Deep Woods, Wandering trouble maker, Journeyman rune smith, Wealthy merchant prince, Rootless explorer seeking new wisdom, Half-mad mystic seer, Genius far ahead of her time, Arcane assassin.)

                                                    Artificers, aka Brotherhood of the Rule (which I'm not even going to try to translate into Byzantine Greek), eastern counterparts to the Craftmasons, they consider themselves the true inheritors of the ancient Roman Ars Praeclarus. They work in a much smaller scale than their "backward" Western cousins, creating wondrous automatons, ingenious mechanical devices, and alchemical marvels.

                                                    The Storm-Crows, a catch all term for number of mages who owe allegiance to no fellowship. They are a disturbing new phenomenon brought about by the upheavals of both the Pax Hermetica's overzealous crusade and the Sword-sworn's excessive retribution, as a number of long standing covens, chantries, and schools are destroyed and newer mages have no one to teach them properly.

                                                    The Web of Faith (Tahaluf al'Muminin?), the dominant alliance of magi in the Islamic world, from Iberia to the Levant and beyond. They are largely a mystery to European mages, but include the House of the Crescent Moon, the Darwishim, the Muktashfu al Daw' (Discoverers of Light), the Hadiqat al Salam (Garden of Peace), and others.

                                                    Those who are well read or traveled claim there are further alliances beyond the Islamic lands in Ethiopia, India, and China.

                                                        My own take on the Technocracy is slightly different than canon, drawing a lot on 1st edition presentation and real world technocratic progressive statism, the conspiracy genre, and some of the darker implications of some of the ways magic can be uses by unfriendly conspiracies.

                                                        The Technocracy is NOT based on or devoted to science and technological advancement; They are devoted to control and conformity of the masses. Science and technology are just tools to further this end, the same way religion used to be back in the old days. The Technocracy seeks to remake humanity and human society into something easy to control and shape in their preferred image, a Utopian society that places themselves firmly in charge and the masses as obedient sheep. The Traditions, with their belief in individualism and personal ascension, are a threat to this plan. These are basically the bad guys you've seen in things like The X-Files, 1984, Brave New World, The Manchurian Candidate, and The Trial.
                                                        Where my Mages have Gnosis (or Arete if you prefer), representing one's understanding of the greater secrets of Reality and Magic, Technocrats have Conditioning, representing their level of indoctrination and training in the Technocratic mindset and world view. And where my Mages have Wisdom as a morality stat, Technocrats have Obedience. And where Mages work toward Ascension as they increase in Gnosis (and Nephandi fall downwards), Technocrats go off on a tangent that makes them not human anymore. (And the details of which differ for each Convention.)
                                                        Low level Technocrats are usually recruited/scouted and initiated the same way Mages are, often appealing to their better natures of building a better world, advancing science, etc. It's only later as they go deeper down the rabbit hole that they become more and more aware of (and perhaps numb to) the darker parts of the Technocracy. The best hope for a Technocrat is to eventually realize that they are on the wrong side and end up defecting, usually to the Sons of Ether or the Virtual Adepts, but sometimes other groups.
                                                        (The Technocracy uses Sciences instead of Spheres. Spacial Science, Probability Science, Energy Science, Biological Science, Material Science, Psychological Science, Quantum Science, Dimensional Science, and Temporal Science.)
                                                        Historically, while there was an Order of Reason founded in the late Medieval period, it had largely fallen apart by end of the 18th century and the French Revolution and Napoleonic Wars were its death knell. The new Enlightened Society of Order and Reason was formed in 1851 in London. After the disruptions of WW2, they reorganized into the Enlightened Union of Technocratic Order, more commonly known as the Technocratic Union or Technocracy. This also began a series of pogroms against the Traditions and other groups, which coincided with the Virtual Adepts joining the Traditions.
                                                        (The Sons of Ether and Virtual Adepts have never been part of my Technocracy, instead being formed by those devoted to the free and open pursuit of scientific discovery and the exchange of information. So it's always been a cabal of five, even back with the Order of Reason.)

                                                            The New World Order

                                                            We do disagreeable things so that ordinary people here and elsewhere can sleep safely in their beds at night. Is that too romantic?Of course, we occasionally do very wicked things.
                                                            - John LeCarre, The Spy Who Came in From the Cold

                                                            The ultimately origins of the NWO begin with the Revelation of Pure Thought of God as Revealed by His Messenger Gabriel Archangel to Claudius Deditactus during the reign of Constantine the Great in 320AD. This dictate of One World, One God, One Faith and its idea of all humanity united under a single mono-faith empire was upheld and advanced by the Sacred Order of Pure Thought and True Faith through their infiltration of the Roman Catholic Church. However, the first major break among the faithful came with the rise of Islam and the breakaway sect known as the Righteous Brethren of Purity and Submission. While original Sacred Order has long since faded into history, the Righteous Brethren still exist in the Middle East as a persistent antagonist of the Web of Faith and the Ahl I Batin.
                                                            With the Dark Ages as the Order of Hermes starting their crusades against the undead, the Sacred Order started their own Illuminated Inquisition (not to be confused with the mundane Papal Inquisition or the Society of Leopold's Shadow Inquisition) against not only the Hermetics, but the heretical Voices of Mercy and various pagan sects. Eventually they allied with the Craftmasons and three other like-minded factions to form the Order of Reason in the 14th century. All of this soon lead to the Burning of Baerwald in Germany and Wyndgarde's March through Britain, as well as the betrayal of the Knights Templar.
                                                            The Reformation caused a schism within the Sacred Order, with a majority remaining within the Catholic Church and taking part in the Counter Reformation, but some taking up the banner of Protestant faiths. While the Anglican and Lutheran Orders ties with various crowns allowed them to carry on as normal, other sects found themselves hard pressed by their former fellows. Ironically, these groups would end up finding sanctuary among their former enemies of the Voices of Mercy.
                                                            By the time of the 17th and 18th century, British, French, German and other factions managed to reorganize themselves into the Bringers of Order and Light, blending together the new ideals of reason and the Enlightenment with that of a powerful earthly government and the Divine Right of Kings. Natural philosophy and logic became powerful weapons against witches, demons and heresy. In the wake of the French Revolution and the Napoleonic Wars, these Lightbringers became one of the founders of the new Enlightened Society of Reason.
                                                            The 19th and 20th centuries saw the rise of powerful national governments, including the belief in the unquestioned leadership of educated elites with the proper ideas and credentials and its natural expressions in Socialism, Bureaucratism, Communism, Corporatism, and Fascism. After WW2, the Lightbringers again reorganized into the modern New World Order. Under this guise, they carry out the Technocracy's pogroms against its enemies and advance their ultimate goal of One World, One Government, One Mind through the tools of social conformity, orthodoxy, and peer pressure, propaganda and information control, public education and mass media, public surveillance and data gathering, social engineering, psychological warfare, and indoctrination.
                                                            The modern Order has three methodologies. The Ivory Tower work in the fields of record collection, propaganda, reeducation, and information sanitation, ultimately deciding what the masses should be exposed to and what opinions they should be having. The Panopticon work in surveillance, data collection, internal security and threat detection, constantly watching the masses for signs of deviation and independence. And The Agency work in field operations, disinformation, external security and assassination, going out among the masses to deal directly with threats to the Order.
                                                            Among their forces are the Men in Black (faceless field agents who can pose as government forces and carry high tech gear), Greys (advanced agents able to blend invisibly into society to carry out their missions), Whites (aka Whitewashers, advanced agents who liaise with other Conventions, acting as spokespersons and troubleshooters who seem to never get their hands dirty), Big Brothers/Sisters (elite internal security specialists and loyalty enforcers), Sympathizers (interrogation specialists), Snowmen (liaisons with the mundane public who specialize in spin doctoring, disinformation and illusions), Re-educators (specialists in mindscaping, brainwashing, and reprograming), Censors (specialists in scrubbing unwanted date from public view and the minds of others), Gatekeepers (security specialists who restrict access to classified and unhelpful information), Collectors (data analysts who obsessively specialize in specific subjects), Talking Heads (mundane spokespersons to the masses disseminating the proper party line and talking points), Manchurians (brainwashed sleeper agents - including Mages - unaware of their conditioning until activated), and Watchers (engineered drones in the form of humans, animals, machines and other objects whose sensory input is recorded).
                                                            NWO strongholds are usually referred to as Academies, Media Centers, or Safehouses, depending on the methodology in charge. In addition, the have Re-education Camps and, worst of all, Memory Holes.
                                                            The ultimate end of NWO Conditioning is The Hive Mind. As you go above level 3 or 4, your mind becomes less and less your own. Eventually, you become just a mouth piece for a single collective consciousness with no real will or personality of your own.
