            I feel the sidhe come across as more or less the same. Broadly speaking, they're pretty jerky, a fact that is perhaps more apparent now there's more scarcity and noblesse oblige comes with more of a cost, but hardly at odds with the way they were portrayed before. It's easy to be chivalrous when there's no rush for the lifeboats. However, individual sidhe rulers are still described in varying ways in the setting and I don't think anyone would double-take if a game was set in an area with a more egalitarian sidhe noble in charge. If a player is making a sidhe character, the concern is the same as it always was - you're part of a noble social class and that may be a double-edged sword. In this case, however, there's actually more flexibility since a player can choose to play an autumn sidhe. In 2nd ed, the only way to do this was to be part of House Scathach. But an autumn sidhe is entirely decoupled from membership in any particular House (even if it's most common for Liam or Scathach sidhe), so, as I said, more flexibility.

            I'm also confused by Rabbit Pooka's belief that C20 is somehow painting the sidhe as victims. Is that something you feel C20 is doing, or were you just reacting to a previous post? Because I don't see that at all. The attitudes under discussion - while I would disagree that it represents a huge shift from the original game, or that it is presented as some monolithic, unyielding directive for every single sidhe ruler - seem pretty clearly presented as jerktastic to me?
