    Exporting S20 mechanics to V20

    08-04-2022, 01:08 PM
    So, here's the thing about me. I love Thaumaturgy. I've also always felt like it was basically Disciplines 2: This Time There's More.

    Now, I'm not going to say Thaumaturgy should just use Sorcerer mechanics through and through. That feels needlessly complex, and honestly Thaumaturgy seems to be more capable of firing off a quick effect and getting a consistent result than Sorcery.

    But that being said, Sorcerer seems to be way more effective at making you feel like a wizard than Thaumaturgy ever was. Which raises an interesting question: Is it possible to export some of the mechanics of Sorcerer over to V20 for use in Thaumaturgy and other blood magics?

    For example, one of my biggest issues with the way Thaumaturgy works is that there's no teamwork options. Granted, most of the time it's not needed, most powers are self contained one-and-done spells. But for, say, a group of Tremere casting a long and dangerous ritual to curse an entire clan so they stop committing Diablerie...

    Well. How was that done? Thaumaturgy rituals can't be done cooperatively, and Thaumaturgy as a discipline does NOT scale well into elder power levels, instead just letting elders be really diverse.

    So, basically, what do you think? Would that break the system wide open? Would it be pointless because of how self contained each power is? Would it finally make Tremere actually FEEL like they're doing magic?

      In short, yes. And I have long wanted to get an improved system by making it more diverse / flexible.

      And now in detail. Thaumaturgy inherits (in my opinion) a simpler and older system, where you just have to get a couple of successes and the effect works. That's the problem, magic rarely ever felt unique (other than a couple of rituals and special paths and a few narrative cases in stories in books). I would suggest adding a quick and long cast system first. In order not to break the tradition of a quick cast magic (1 success - the standard effect works), but on the other hand it would be nice to be able to control the effect. But it was with sorcerers in their paths, and with magi from the moment they appeared. Vampires have a problem with power level and their mechanics. It used to be in many disciplines of the elders, they were expensive and gave little benefit/effect. And sometimes they were too high level for their power.

      There have been official attempts to change this. Setites and Assamites were able to use Attribute + Skill for casting. Their rituals could include the preparation of mummies for cheapening, blood, and so on. It was similar with necromancy and its styles, but even there it was only about a couple of styles and rare rituals. But the magic was the same for everyone, and this simplification was sometimes sad. And even that they forgot in the future. Although there are pluses. Ritual magic among the Tremere in the Middle Ages was interesting (all those homunculi and artificial creatures). And in modern nights, it became possible to enchant a gargoyle.

      The situation is much better for the Tzimisce - their magic has unique mechanics, and the DAV version is even more interesting due to the Tzimisce-Spirits connection in their magic. But in any case, they all would do well to get additional abilities (like an assistant in the ritual) and the possibility of more control over power. Perhaps an assistant is not needed (or maybe not possible) for some rituals, but I would like to - because even earlier group rituals with vampires and magicians were mentioned.

      Actually about the conversion: This has already happened. Wizards have a unique rule in DAV20 to mimic vampire magic. But this imitation is only a complex mechanism / project of a particular occultist and cannot be taught to others - everyone has his own. There were also rules for necromancy in the old book, where mortals (like ghouls) could use repetition or imitation of necro with restrictions (they took longer to cast, even paths). And in Seawolves, vampires could get mortal sorcery (runes) with its mechanics (but blood instead of will). But this required connection with runes and the level was limited by the rune-wise skill.

      I look at my text, it seems to be crap. I remember the lost and not developed potential... sorry, but I can not yet offer complete alternative system.

              I generally like the Sorcerer 20 powers, but I still think the rules are a bit too complex and idiosyncratic. Some of the paths probably need folding into others (Necronics, Maelstrom) and some of the paths are probably too broad (e.g., Summoning, Binding and Warding). I would also remove any path that emulates another Discipline, out of principle.

              Aspects, though, could really help flesh out Thaumaturgy, while also clearing away the proliferation of overlapping and weird paths. In general, I think there needs to be a slightly more fluid system.

              I like some of what I see in the Decanter homebrew for Blood Sorcery, but it's a bit too bare bones in some ways. See here: https://blood-and-bourbon.obsidianpo.../blood-sorcery

              For me, the best system would allow for a number of broad paths, like Sorcerer, that are modified by your tradition, which adds in the flavour. Teamwork would be a standard thing for rituals and Tremere would get a bonus on this that works like Blood Sympathy -- i.e., the blood bonds between them strengthen their teamwork for magical purposes. Giovanni might get something similar for Necromancy because of their family ties. That gives both clans a reason for being the bosses in their relative fields.

              Paths would therefore be generic but traditions would add a rider effect that makes them unique, such as adding additional effects, adding requirements and limitations to the magic, and determining the base dice pools.

              The traditions could also gate which paths you can learn -- so users of Wanga no longer need to master both Thaumaturgy and Necromancy, and Akhu no longer needs to duplicate Necromancy powers, because users of those traditions just select the paths that fit their paradigm, regardless of whether they were historically and arbitrarily defined as Thaumaturgy or Necromancy in the past.

              There's no reason to duplicate powers with this system, so you would have a slimmer range of paths to begin with.

              So, let's say one generic path is the Path of Spirit, which allows you to summon and control spirits (binding and warding can be covered by a different one). If you use the Western Necromancy tradition, the rider would say that you can only command spirits from the Underworld, and this variant of the Path of Spirit is called the Sepulchre Path. It allows for greater control of wraiths, even if they don't want to help you, but coercive methods can result in hauntings and all your magic has the potential for sequelae as standard because it's entropic.

              But if you were an Ahrimane using the Spiritus tradition, your rider would instead allow you to command animal and nature spirits, in a sort of animistic/shamanistic paradigm, and you might call this path the Anima Path. This path would have an additional requirement that you treat your spirits with respect, such as making tributes and sacrifices to them, as this is on a more equal footing than, say, the coercive powers of the Giovanni. Animals and nature might abhor you if you don't uphold your bargains.

              Likewise, a practitioner of Abyss Mysticism would learn the Path of Spirit as, say, the Path of Abyssal Horrors, where you command abyssal spirits. Such magic might cause physical side effects, such as pitch black eyes, and have a vulnerability to sunlight.

              Setites would call it the Path of Duat, and it would function much the same as in Necromancy, but with some Setite-specific limitations (you must maintain a blasphemy shrine, etc). Those using Wanga would rely on the trappings of Vodu and similar syncretic religions -- you work with the loa, to whom you must maintain offerings.

              In this way, the Path of Flame (Sorcerer's Hellfire) could be flavoured as Lure of Flames or Fires of the Inferno, depending on your tradition. You could even make it cold black flames from the abyss, if you were an Abyss Mystic; barrow flame if you're a Necromancer; or balefire if you're a Kiasyd/Maeghar. There's loads of flexibility there.

              Players could learn multiple iterations of the same generic path, importing one or more variants used by another tradition, but at extra cost and time (use the V20 rules for converting paths as a guideline). So an Ahrimane could learn Spiritus paths as usual and Western Necromancy paths at greater cost, but without entirely locking them out of wraith manipulation just because they took animistic spirit manipulation earlier on. Maybe they take the drawbacks of both traditions when using those powers, though (e.g., using Necromantic approaches might offend your animal spirit allies due to the sequelae such magic brings).

              I would drop the rules for witnesses in most cases, using the loophole that, as creatures woven into the mythic threads of the cosmos, humans are subconsciously aware of vampires, or God makes their presence objective rather than subjective. You could always keep the penalty for witnesses for only the most brazen applications of magic (e.g., deduct your rating in the relevant Blood Magic tradition from the success penalty for witnesses, to a minimum of zero successes).

              I would probably also use Simplified Aspects and slightly simplified ritual rules. Additional participants would add a single die each, could contribute to the cost in vitae or Willpower, and would only extend the time if there are more participants than the leader's dots in Blood Magic, for example (and even then it'd just be a flat doubling of the usual time, rather than a scaling increase per person). You can also spend extra blood as if it were quintessence/mana, meaning that group casting could be significantly more powerful without adding loads of extra dice rolling or requiring loads of bookkeeping.

                      I've been thinking about this for years. I'll admit, I was partly inspired by Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay, where there are eight Winds of Magic (distinct schools of magic, of which you can only learn one, unless you're an Elf or a witch).

                      Rather than just repeating everything eight times, though, there are shared Petty Magic and Arcane Magic spell lists, the latter of which gain an additional effect from your Lore's rider.

                      Then each Lore has a set list of Lore spells that are unique to them, which would cover a similar space to unique rituals in the WoD.

                                                  Originally posted by Screeb7165 View Post
                                                  Would the riders need to be hand crafted for each path within each Tradition, or would they be broader? You mention specifically Spiritus and (Giovanni) Necromancy as possibly overlapping and being differentiated by their rider, but I'm not sure I see how that sort of rider would interact with, say, Lure of Flames, or the Path of Blood.
                                                  In most cases, no. My thinking was that the rider would work in tandem with the tradition to shape how your paths work, and it's the combination of these with a generic path that gives rise to unique variations. The rider would be a mechanical edge or benefit, and the tradition paradigm would set the context and limitations of your practice overall (so it would determine whether Divination was through astrology or seances, for example).

                                                  Individual paths, since they're generic, would also probably have a couple of brief lines about how they would work with three or four sample traditions as an indicator for how to use them, and those descriptions may enhance or replace the rider as needed. Sort of like seeming benefits in Changeling: The Lost, where each contract suggests three to four unique variations or bonuses for specific seemings.

                                                  But in most cases, you wouldn't need additional mechanics; you'd just need some fluff that tells you how the Sepulchre Path differs from the Animus Path (it might be as simple as saying it affecting wraiths v animistic spirits, and giving an idea of how that changes the mood/tone of the game).

                                                  In WFRP, riders are optional, so a Fire Wizard doesn't have to burn someone they're healing, but they may use the fluff of 'burning out' the injury. That's a good model, I think. The fluff is going to be as important as any mechanical edge, if not more so.

                                                  In the examples you give, Lure of Flames fits quite well across traditions, as I outlined upthread. So it'd probably be cold, black flames from the Abyss for Abyssal Mystics; or it might be faerie bale fire for Maeghar and Kiasyd sorcerers. It functions the same in both situations, but the fluff differs. There may be a slight mechanical advantage to some traditions over others, if fire is especially relevant to your tradition.

                                                  That said, I'm looking to make generic paths that can be modified by the specifics of a character's tradition, not specific paths that can be used generically. The examples I mentioned upthread were from Sorcerer 20, as I think they better serve as generic paths.

                                                  So there, Lure of Flames would actually be one version of the Path of Hellfire, where your focus is on fire rather than other forms of destruction and injury. But you can also see from the description of Hellfire that it just as easily covers Hands of Destruction and Levinbolt, too, and might also cover the withering powers from Thanatosis/Grave's Decay. I think those would be covered by the fluff of your tradition, and the rider may be relevant or not.

                                                  Trying to fit Path of Blood into multiple traditions would therefore be doing things backwards -- you'd need to make the Path of Blood a specific permutation of a generic path. In those instances where it becomes impossible to do that, I would suggest turning old VTM paths into rituals.

                                                  The Path of Blood would probably be a series of unique rituals that sit alongside Alchemy and Vitae (Quintessence) Manipulation. Or, if you're desperate to keep it, just keep it, as there's a good argument for adding a unique 'blood' path for vampire sorcerers specifically.

                                                  I also imagine that where a path and your tradition just don't fit, that would be a good sign to a Storyteller that the path can't normally be learned as part of your tradition.

                                                  Here's a model of a tradition and its rider, that I have given absolutely no playtesting at all:

                                                  Abyss Mysticism
                                                  Paradigm: Eldritch Fathoms
                                                  Abyss Mysticism plumbs the depths of the Abyss: an eternal realm of sentient darkness at the heart of Creation; a nothingness devoid of life but possessed of a cunning malice that spills into all those who touch it. In very superficial ways it resembles Necromancy, but it is something even worse. The Underworld ravaged by the Clan of Death is far more familiar to human minds than the Abyss revered by the Lasombra.

                                                  Limitations: As an Abyss Mystic, your paths and rituals rely on shadows and darkness. If you lack suitable natural darkness from which to exert your will, you must first create shadows with the Path of Obtenebration (Shadows). Abyssal Magic does not work in sunlight. All paths that target spirits specifically target Abyssal spirits only.

                                                  Purview: The Abyss Mystics tend to learn only the Path of Obtenebration and a selection of rituals that build from it. Other paths are rare because of the limited focus of Lasombra magic. Flames of the Abyss (Hellfire), Obscuromancy (Divination) and Shadow Step (Conveyance) function much the same as for other paradigms, but with flavour changes that reflect the role of shadows as the key component. So you may beckon cold black flames to destroy matter or injure your foes, read shadows to gain insight and forbidden knowledge, or teleport by walking through shadows, and so on.

                                                  When summoning 'spirits' with the Path of Abyssal Horrors (Spirit Summoning), Abyss Mysticism actually ushers in things that only superficially resemble the spirits of other traditions. Some Abyss Mystics believe these entities are the fleeting thoughts of the Abyss itself, as shaped by the caster's own desires and the laws of the physical world. These creatures abhor light in all its forms, and so take aggravated damage from both sunlight and fire, as if they were vampires. True Faith also injures them. This similarity between the Abyss's forms and the Cainite condition worries some vampire scholars, but not the Abyss Mystics, who just laugh.

                                                  Drawbacks: Abyss Mysticism taints the user in ways that may be subtle but are always disturbing: an unruly shadow that moves with a mind of its own, jet black eyes and lips, a voice that sounds like a thousand whispers at the edges of reality, and so on. [V20DA doesn't have these side effects; V20 does. One way to reconcile these differences is to have effects manifest only on a botch, or to limit permanent effects to one per dot of Abyss Mysticism, or to make them temporary effects that last until sunrise.]

                                                  Rider: Soulless Energies
                                                  When you use Abyss Mysticism, the air turns cold and lights flicker or even fail. You may choose to extinguish any manmade light source (candles, lanterns, strip lights, and so on, but not moonlight, starlight or sunlight) in line of sight, and/or lower the temperature by 10 degrees Fahrenheit for every dot you have in Abyss Mysticism.

                                                  Furthermore, mortals and most vampires are shaken by the effects of your magic -- they must make a Courage roll (Difficulty 4 + level of the power used) to avoid fleeing or succumbing to Rotschreck, as appropriate, the first time they encounter Abyss Mysticism.
