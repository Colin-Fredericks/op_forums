    How frequent should Enemies appear from the Enemy Flaw?

    08-27-2025, 08:27 PM
    I think almost all the games in all editions have the Enemy flaw. It's a flaw which you can take from 1-5 points, with one point being a single person near your own character's power level and five points being some of the most powerful characters in that game. The other points don't get described, but I generally see three points as defined as having the equivalent power of your coterie, pack, motley, etc., and you can figure out the other levels accordingly.

    However, what is the frequency that such enemies should happen to appear or intervene in game? Not having them show up at all in the chronicle is clearly wrong. The PC has freebies for nothing, and most players actually want their enemies to appear at some point in time. Likewise, having such enemies be omnipresent in the chronicle also seems wrong. That just hijacks the entire chronicle and likely interrupts the PC too much so he is unable to do anything else except deal with their enemy.

    So it is all about finding that sweet spot. I don't think the flaw should be something that is addressed and resolved in one single story. I think it's something that should keep making an appearance UNTIL the player finally decides to buy the Flaw off along with a proper resolution. Ideally, the ST should also present it in a way that until the end, it really bedevils that one PC and not the entire player character group (after all, they didn't get any freebies from it). However, how much time should it show up in game? What do people think?

    Is this something that might take up 10% of the total time spent in game on average? Less? More? Do you handle it in some other way than a time percentage of game sessions/time at table? If so, what is the general metric or guideline that you strive for?

    I understand there is no "one" correct way to handle this. I'm just interested in seeing how different STs approach this.

    BTW, I don't think the Enemy flaw necessarily means the enemy wants to kill that character, or at least that every time the enemy "strikes" it is to kill. I think that creates more problems than what it's worth. Such enemies need to be able to thwart, hamper, or harm the PC in other ways for them to become good long term antagonists. J Jonah Jameson is just as good an enemy as is Norman Osborn for Spider-Man.

      In my current Mage chronicle my wife is playing with this Flaw. In this case, the Enemy is actually the husband of her character, and he's a loving husband.

      So loving he's completely against her character endangering himself, so whenever the Enemy shows up his goal is simply to drag the PC back home to safety. The Enemy in question is also a Werewolf.

      What I did was to establish a base 10% chance per session for the Enemy to "show up" somehow, but once he does he'll be a nuisance for the rest of the story, unless the group arranges to get him off track somehow.

      For now it has worked and I'm happy with the characters and dynamic, but I have to say it mostly reinforced my dislike of this Flaw as a mechanic. Sincerely? It does just hijack the narrative, even if just for a while. If a player wants an "enemy", it's easier for me to just include this enemy in the story and bring them whenever it's organically relevant, than be forced to find a balance that justify a set number of Freebies and then keep a somewhat static status quo until the Flaw is bought back.

      And I'm not even talking about how hard it is to balance power and the level of interference of the Enemy, especially with how vague the two described levels are so described. I'd much prefer a CoD's Ally type of thing, where particular mechanical effects are given to represent the actual trait, and you're otherwise free to handle the story aspect as you see fit.

              I don't have a problem with characters being stronger, I have a problem with a trait that puts the responsibility of making it work squarely in my shoulders.

              Bringing any aspect of the game to the story is my job, but traits in general (of any type) have rules for two reasons: to ease my work on implementing them, and to guide player expectation on how they'll play out. On one side, rules are there to guarantee we're mostly on the same page about how things will play out and ground player expectations, so they reliably know what to expect. On the other, to help me adjudicate things and prepare, so I deliver my story in a way that's sufficiently consistent.

              Traits are basically rules under the player's decisions, they're a way to give players a degree of control over the story. It's not just about making what they want, it's about making their input work in a reliable manner, one I can implement with some ease and consistency and they know what to expect.

              While the system has many problems with traits, and rules in general, that don't work so well, Enemy is a complete failure as game design. It makes this story element more complicated instead of simpler. It interferes with my story without giving any directions to ease the process. It either hijacks my story or take out from my player any real autonomy beyond making the vague suggestion of a character, without delivering any consistency or reliability.

              Enemy isn't a bad trait because it's potentially "free points". It's a bad trait because we're literally better off just discussing the chronicle beforehand, it adds nothing to the process.

                  Originally posted by Aleph View Post
                  I say, let Enemies appear when it's organic for them to appear.
                  I don't think this really helps. It's seems tautological to me. It's another way of saying "Enemies should appear whenever they should appear."

                  In the context of an RPG, the group of PCs will interact with the setting according to the things they proactively do (we go into the dungeon) or inadvertently do (they kill a bandit, but don't realize it's not some random bandit but the lover of the Queen of Thieves who controls the Thieves Guild). The NPCs then act in response given their prior goals, objectives, and plans. They aren't necessarily interested in the specific PC or PCs unless they become an obstacle. The ST can adjudicate then what happens. Since it is generally the group of PCs who do this as a group, nobody is either left out or dragged in. That interplay can be "organic" because it is an interaction caused by their own choices and responses.

                  I think the Enemy flaw is different because one person (or a group of enemies) are seeking to harm one specific PC. That's their objective (although they may have more goals, that is irrelevant to the flaw at hand). They have to be proactive about it because they aren't reacting to what the PCs are doing.

                  "Organically" that could mean the Enemy is going to show up as often as they can. That can be easy to justify. However, I don't think that's going to be very fun for the PC. The player, typically, imagines that the Enemy will come into play every so often. Not that they are constantly fending that enemy off every game session or other high frequency. They want to be able to accomplish other goals and progress on whatever the main plot is. At a certain point, it is no longer fun, but becomes frustration instead even if it happens "organically". You want to avoid that as an ST.

                  So again it goes back to what is the "sweet spot"? You don't want the Enemy to show up too often, and you don't want it to show up too little. You also want the focus to remain on that one PC, and not have it interrupt the entire PC party or drag them into something they don't want to be involved in. (If another PC chooses to become involved, that's fine as it is their choice just like everything else in the game. However, I don't think the expectations should be that the flaws of one PC should become the flaws of all the other PCs).

                  Of course, the ST can always ask the player in question about the frequency. However, I've found that in many cases the players themselves don't have any idea. They'll just tell you about wanting it to be fun or interesting, but not hijack from the game. So such talks are often not helpful. Only after you run the chronicle for a while will the player often constructive feedback; i.e. "I was hoping to see my Enemy more often." or "I can't seem to get anything else done, my Enemy is really hijacking every game session." That feedback is going to happen at some point, but it can't help you for some time.

                  Again, I don't think there is "one" best answer. You can even start out one way (I'll try to include a mention of the Enemy 10% of the time), and it could turn into something else as the game continues (The other players seem to like dealing with Bob's Enemy. This is creating a lot of fun for them. Maybe I'll upgrade them to be a major opponent in the chronicle.) Something like that can happen "organically", but it's not guaranteed.

                  However, I do think it's useful to hear how other STs handle it. There might be an unexpected consensus. Or maybe there's a wide a wide variety of approaches, and hearing them may prove useful to other STs when thinking about how to implement this flaw. Having multiple ways to handle things gives more options to STs as not all Enemies act the same. You can try one way with one Enemy, and a second way with another. Or even change mid-streak if you see one approach doesn't seem to be working.

                              While not necessarily directly helpful - as I think the structure of the Flaw makes it impossible to be helpful with it - my feelings on this is that this should be negotiated with the player during character creation. Basically, I turn "frequency" into part of the overall threat that dictates how many dots an Enemy is "worth." So a fairly weak enemy that's a very regular presence in the game might be 2 or 3 dots instead of 1 to accommodate that.

                              The big point is that it just gets negotiated out with the player and the group so everyone's on the same page and that when the enemy shows up it's not derailing, or disappointing when they don't.

                              This is also helps avoid something like me having to accommodate an extremely potent enemy that's going to show up a lot. If I'm running VtM and you take the Prince of the city the game is set in as your enemy despite being a fresh starting level PC? The Prince is "present" sure, but the Prince has a lot more to worry about than some fresh neonate and can't afford to look weak by looking obsessed with one schmuck over their other duties. The Prince is not going to actively mess with the PC (and the rest of the coterie) with high frequency, but when the Prince does it's going to be a big deal. That just gets baked into the assumption once "frequency" is equated to the level of threat the enemy posses. Anything I would deem 6+ worth of enemy in both power and activity in the game gets excluded as an option for being too disruptive.

                                      Perhaps this goes a bit out of topic (or not?) but I've been thinking it could be useful as a tool to think a number of questions as a baseline to ask the player.

                                      I've been using standarized "lists of questions" as guidelines to generate my own NPC - and I don't see why the player couldn't use a similar list too on the Session 0 (well, I've seen players struggle with similar questions, so it's not really a solution, but maybe if we can think smart questions we can have a solid-ish starting point to get an intuitive grasp of how this is going to work out. At least more solid than the current "do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law").

                                      *How much pressence do they have on the life of your character? it's an interesting question

                                      Some more tentative questions:

                                      *What kind of threat do they present to your character (social, physical,...)?

                                      *How do they threaten your character (do they use influence to thwart your plans, kill loved ones, ...)? , roughly what's their modus opperandi and resources

                                      *What are their goals (long term) and how does your character figure into them? - this one may be good to keep as an ST secret, YMMV, but maybe there's a more nuanced way to seek player input about this?

                                      *What does your character think about all of this?, what do they want to do about this enemy?
