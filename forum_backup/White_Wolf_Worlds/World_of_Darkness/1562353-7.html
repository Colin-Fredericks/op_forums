                Originally posted by GreatHornedRatkin View Post
                This is true in a classical fantasy setting
                No, this is true in literature across the board, doubly so in RPGs, and IIRC is stated directly in most corebooks for VtM.

                Lestat, Victor Frankenstein, Axel and Otto Lidenbrock, Holmes, Watson, the Losers Club, the love triangle from Twilight. Even when such characters are conceptually common people thrown in exceptional circumstances, they're not really regular, anything but. What drives the story is how those extraordinary situations also reveal their extraordinary traits.

                This is not to say the PCs didn't had ordinary lives before. But they have never been meant to be average. Sure, they still have space to grow a lot, but they already begin as a cut above even among equals, in this case among other Neonates.

                Reasons for that abound, but most boil down to it being fun and easier to run. You want the PCs to succeed where their peers would fail, and to feel powerful and competent enough to do and see impressive things, even if their final fate is dark.

                Of course, stories of truly unremarkable people are perfectly possible and can be quite fun. But they're also quite more specific and hard to run properly, so very few RPGs focus standard char gen on them.

                You know two that actually do that? Paranoia and Call of Cthulhu. You know what they have in common? Both assume you'll start play with at least 3 extra characters ready, because they have extremely high mortality rates.

                                Sounds cool, and I guess this Authority has some shady stuff going on for them, too, given the obvious power play.

                                Is someone exploring exclusive control over the synthetic blood production? Or is it really distributed to ensure the new status quo?

                                Twilight has a number of cool ideas, as I said. It is more based in the idea of nomadic vampires, so they're fewer but far more well traveled, which means relevant interactions and conflicts aren't restricted by immediate area like a VtM city.

                                Instead of a Camarilla you have the Volturi as a coven that runs the world from a small town in Italy and enforce their Masquerade, but their method is pure brutality: if you're careless enough for the Volturi to know of a breach of their laws, they go to your place and annihilate everyone they're not satisfied with, vampire or not.

                                Covens are small bands. The Volturi are the largest with 30+ members total, the second largest was 7 before growing to 9 and the third a whopping 5. Most vampires are lone predators or covens of 2 or 3. It's a pretty different but interesting dynamic where you have small, tight local groups focusing on their own local stuff, but when vampire society is an issue it's very globally connected.

                                Powers are another curious take. Becoming a vampire absurdly amplifies personal abilities. For most it's physical stuff, and they're quite overpowered from the get go, but any small supernatural power increases several orders of magnitude. With a more variable approach to what kinds of powers one can have, this means vampires with special powers are completely unpredictable on top of rare.

                                Overall I think for real the bones can be used to build a solid setting. The story is trash, not that I have any problem with watered romance to be very clear, it's just the kind of annoyingly badly thought that you don't expect from a mediocre writer of juvenil literature, but from a mediocre 14yo. But the setting is creative and the ideas and concepts are pretty ok.

                                It's unfortunate that all the good bits take a back seat to the asinine plot, really.

                                    monteparnas Surprisingly, no, The Authority have genuinely noble intentions. They're the ones who invented the True Blood synthetic, and then had it mass produced around the world. Their leader was disgusted with the way vampires had been conducting themselves, and decided that he was going to force them to become more civilized, whether they liked it or not. He's even established alliances with the human military to create a new branch of law enforcement that deals specifically with out of control vampires.

                                    The reason why other vampires hate The Authority, is because their previous existence allowed them to avoid consequences for their monstrous behavior. They covered up evidence of murder, they could glamour people into becoming their slaves, real blood tastes much better than the synthetic stuff. They were free to engage in all kinds of hedonistic debauchery without anyone to keep them in check.

                                    But now that the truth is out, unlife isn't so care free anymore. Even worse, the federal government knows about their existence now, which means vampires have to do humiliating shit, like paying taxes again.
