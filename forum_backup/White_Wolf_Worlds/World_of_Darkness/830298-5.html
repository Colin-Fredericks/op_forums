                    Originally posted by Resplendent Fire View Post
                    The expressed disdain for urban fantasy is disappointing, to say the least.
                    I don't see disdain, he's saying that he believes that the World of Darkness' unique element among modern supernatural settings is that it was horror rather than urban fantasy. Now, you can argue that premise (genre definitions are tricky things and the lines are often blurry), but I think it is unfair to read into it that he's putting down urban fantasy. A good parallel, for me, is folks who don't like the new Sherlock Holmes movies because they are action films. This doesn't necessarily mean that they hate action films, just that they don't think the tone fits those particular stories.

                    In this case, I think he has a point. Stuff like Dresden Files or Buffy have horrific elements but the tone isn't horror. Buffy and Harry don't ever react in horror to dealing with creatures that prey off of humanity by consuming their blood and treating them like cattle, they fight them the same way that Indiana Jones fights Nazis. Part of the uniqueness of Vampire was that it acknowledged the horror of what vampires were and expected the PCs to do so as well. You aren't just superheroes that happen to get their power-ups from drinking blood, you are predators preying on humanity, leeches on society in a literal sense. Granted, not all games (hell maybe most games) didn't really explore this, but it was a unique aspect of the game. You are playing monsters, with the stress on MONSTERS. That's what I think he's looking to reclaim.

                    Anyway, just my opinion on the matter.

                    Onyx Path Freelancer
