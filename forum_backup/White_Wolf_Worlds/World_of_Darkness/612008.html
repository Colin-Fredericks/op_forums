    New ideas to counter well informed players

    08-02-2015, 06:17 AM
    One of my biggest issues with running games anymore is the fact my players are jaded and blase about what they encounter. If I desccribe a man who pulses and shifts into a gigantic man-bear they automatically know that its a Gurahl or if I'm being mean an elder gangrel.

    To counter this in my newest game (using nWoD rules and pick and choosing from conversion books), I am 1 making them start as mortals and not informing them which game line(s) I intend to use.

    I however would also like to give them new threats/beings out there to deal with.

    My first thought is a couple hundred years ago an archmage obsessed with werewolves and the fera got it into his head to start experimenting to create his own Garou, Bastet etc. Of course such a thing is virtually impossible and so he was only able to create individuals who could physically transform but had none or only partial access to the spiritual gifts of the fera (stepping sideways, gnosis, gifts etc). What he did not expect (or maybe he did he was crazy after all) was that those he gifted with such power would pass it down along their own bloodlines. However over the years lore was lost and not every child was gifted so the rites and rituals needed to awaken their powers were lost.

    The PCs of course would end up in a situation where they are forced to attempt to stop or inadvertantly reawaken several of these quasi-shifters.
    Thoughts? Suggestions on this idea?

    Thoughts on other new threats to lace into the World of Darkness? Id like things that bend but do not necessarily break or violate the various themes.
    My idea playing playing to Mage's ideas of consensual reality- the mages pattern tampering of these families so closely resembles the fera who are deeply seeded in humanities primal unconscious minds that paradox doesn't affect them in quite the same ways it would a true mage casting, however it would force certain issues- reduced regeneration, limited time in crinos, etc.
    For werewolf it is well known that mages stole the power of naming and routinely violate gaias balance and the weavers works with their art. This would be but one example of their blasphemy.

                                          I'd recommend going through things as regular, baseline humans for a while at first. Like a game of Call of Cthulhu, begin them as a group of investigators, slowly drawn into the world of the supernatural. Make them FEEL the fear of being confronted with monsters and dangers beyond mortal ken, without any of the supernatural advantages they normally enjoy. Describe the ferocious beasts in deep, descriptive detail, while refusing to give overt hints as to what they are. Push the bloody horror of a vampire supping the juices from a victim behind the alley in the players faces. Have a deranged looking person screw with a PC's mind, and let them wonder if its a vampire or Mage or unAwakened Psychic or Changeling, or something even weirder. Have their be...things just beyond sight that defy description.

                                          Make them fully aware of just how frighteningly ignorant they are. Challenge them on what they think they know, and don't be afraid to bend the rules, or present unorthodox "builds" of normally iconic splats. Is that a Malkavian with Protean? An old clan Tzimisce with Obfuscate? A Setite with Dominate? Is it a Marauder? Or maybe its a crazed hedge wizard with potent shapeshifting, mind control, and Psychic Invisibility? Or maybe it's a Wraith (or Specter, or Risen), or some kind of Umbrood, or a Fomori? Your players don't know, and you shouldn't give the answer readily.

                                          Maybe the group investigates a weird cult. But are they Acolytes armed with Charms and Talismans? Or do they all have Sorcery (there is a difference, but it can be hard to tell if you do it right)? And what are they trying to summon? A demon? A powerful bane spirit? Something from beyond the furthest ring? Might they be summoning something physical, and merely moving a threat to them? They might not summon anything at all, and just be a bunch of occultists barking up the wrong tree.

                                          After a while, when the players have started becoming used to the idea that you aren't going to make them all into vampires or whatever, start laying out opportunities to advance into the supernatural. Have them pick up journals filled with knowledge, allowing them to start learning hedge magic. Or make one of them start getting headaches, and hear voices, until they start developing Psychic powers of their own. Or plant clues that one character might be related to the Garou.

                                          Perhaps, in their desire to learn more magic, the budding sorcerer starts looking for teachers. And one offers them power - only for them to turn out to be a Tremere, looking to Embrace. Alternatively, the party makes an enemy of a vampire, but the Lick decides the only fitting revenge would be to curse them with undeath. So one of the characters wakes up in a ditch at night, with a grisly thirst and no idea who embraced him or what kind of vampire he is.

                                          Or if you want to drop magic on players out of nowhere, give them a book with occult knowledge in it. And when they finish reading it, surprise them by saying "Congratulations, your mind was completely blown by the revelations in this book! You no longer see the world the same way as before! Mark yourself as gaining one Arete!" Turns out the otherwise innocuous occult book was a Primer the whole time. And now the entire party (or whichever ones haven't become vampires or had their First Change) has the option of reading this book and becoming Mages. But do they do it? Cosmic power seems like a sweet deal, but if any of them do have Path magic, they might have reservations about suddenly making an enemy of reality in the form of Paradox.

                                          And if somehow the entire party dies, that's okay! You can either have a passing group of vampires (or the vampire enemies they were fighting) embrace them on a lark, or have them all manifest in the Shadowlands as Wraiths!
