    Originally posted by Bhurano View Post
    Kindred of the East was really good overall.
    Yeah, White Wolf botched on several cultural aspects and such... but honestly that was expected.
    The one thing I couldn't stand was the P'o... and giving that potentially to another player to basically play.
    That was not a a good move and is quite frankly a bad idea for several reasons... all player related.
    The idea of the P'o as an active adversary on the other hand was a good one.
    I just kept a lid on potential issues between players by acting as the P'o for each player character while I DM'ed.

    The whole thing with the Dharmas and them being capable/expected to climb the ladder to greater understanding and power... THAT was awesome.
    Overall, they should have been treated a bit different than just as vampires of the east, but that's not to hard to change.
    In most instances I found that their disciplines are weaker to a degree, not as flexible or have inherent drawbacks compared to those of the Kindred.
    Their lack of a distinct discipline enhancing their raw strength is putting them on the wrong foot against vampires and most shapeshifter.
    There is also the issue that they have to mix and match their Yin and Yang intake and where they stand overall.
    Overall, the Kuei-jin could be quite awesome, but I had the best results for my game when I rolled the Kuei-jin and Mummies basically together into one package
    and found that a more or less perfect solution.
    Thematically they are a PERFECT fit and quite frankly turning the Kuei-jin into immortal spirits fallen from grace and now trying to claw their way back, makes so damn much sense.
    Even better the expanded options aren't going to turn them into overpowered beasts.

    While I loved the concept of the mummy game line I found their different factions, unlike the general positive theme, not that evocative and rather generic,
    the Kuei-jin on the other hand with their dharmas and strange moral codes didn't have that problem.

    Now let's talk about the big one that concerns BOTH Kindred of the East and Mummy:
    Both were rather centered on their areas and that's that, this led to a kinda messed up naming convention in case of Kindred of the East and several other fumbles in regards to location descriptions, cultural stuff and so on... also once again the whole skilltastic catastrophe that is brawl vs martial arts. If there ever was bullshit in the games it is that one.
    Originwise it would have been better if they would have opened up the whole world for Kindred of the East gamers, instead of limiting it to most parts of Asia and a speck here and there.
    For the Mummy game line they did try to do that with the Player's companion, with varying success but at least they gave it a shot.
    If they ever modernize those lines or go for the whole 20th anniversary treatment, they would have to rewrite a good chunk here and there to make it palatable.
    It could be done and it wouldn't be too much work, since a solid foundation is already in place, still it would need to be adressed.
    How did you combine Mummy and KotE, out of curiosity? I've suggested it another thread and would be interested to see how others do it

    As for Mage versus Hunter cosmology: easily reconcileable. Hunters have existed at least twice before. The first lot became the Wan Kuei. The second lot were the Inquisitors of Dark Ages. The Messengers are, or are related to, the Psychopomps who used to match mages with their Avatars. The two Ministers who rule them are understood as angels but probably predate that paradigm. They may have been the beings sleeping beneath Enoch, and were thus reawakened by the 6GM

    My theory is that despite all the guff about the Imbued being human, they're just as supernatural as mages. The point is that just as werewolves' Avatars are connected to Gaia and changelings' are connected to the Dreaming, thus preventing those beings from Awakening (as they're Awake in their own unique ways), the Imbued's Avatars are bound up in their own duties, thus preventing them from becoming any other kind of Awakened being.

    If you look at Wraith, Mummy and KotE, every being does appear to have a multipartite soul. The Avatar is that god-shard or mystical element within the soul that reincarnates from life to life and which is activated or subverted (in the case of vampires) when that being is Awakened to some kind of supernatural existence. Hunters have one too, but it doesn't Awaken in the same way. Mages are Awakening to their potential divinity; other splats Awaken or have their Avatars enslaved to serve other purposes.

                The Imbued are supernatural creatures. Whatever force created the Imbued, the creation of the Imbued probably takes a lot of supernatural power. It is probably easier to mutilate an Avatar into the proper shape than it would be to create the Imbued from scratch, which is why I think that the Imbued are actually created from the mutilated Avatars of mutilated Mages. In fact, I think that they were created to destroy the supernatural creatures of the cWoD in order to prepare the Earth for the invasion of the elder gods (or whatever you will) by destroying the supernatural creatures who would be unwilling to submit to the Elder Gods.

                While it has been mentioned before, imagine a scenario where an alien force, like the Elder Gods, is getting ready to invade the Earth. The first step in any invasion is to cause chaos and confusion within the ranks of any potential defenders. The events of the Sixth Great Maelstrom and the Week of Nightmares could be seen as designed to cause chaos and confusion among the supernatural community. The creation of the Imbued would represent a fifth column that would attack the supernatural community during its confusion, preparing the way for the Elder Gods, and the Imbued could be implanted with a kill code by the Elder Gods to prevent them from being turned against their creators. Just a thought...

                        Originally posted by adambeyoncelowe View Post
                        How did you combine Mummy and KotE, out of curiosity? I've suggested it another thread and would be interested to see how others do it
                        It was rather easy, honestly.
                        I just took the parts of Kindred of the East and Mummy that I didn't like... primarily the limited origin options, due to geographical limitations and put my own spin on it.
                        The Kindred of the East had already the fluff piece going for them that they escaped hell or something along those lines... depending how one used that or which cosmology was overall in play.
                        Mummies had the whole "spirit seeks roomie to crash with" shtick going for them.
                        Throw in a dash of Demon... and voila.
                        In short: Wherever you hailed from it doesn't really matter, but you could be approached by a spirit/demon/whateveryouwanttocallit and you get a one time offer.
                        Offer some headspace, get free from a place of eternal torment or at least that whole boring as hell experience of being dead.
                        You get a second shot at life, awesome powers, even scarier enemies and some part of your psyche got fucked up royally by the P'o.
                        Or to be more precise a true demonic soul seeking to escape it's very own hell.
                        The KotE/Mummy mashup is now immortal... or at least can get better if killed.
                        They have to choose a Dharma, which can vary wildly depending where you return or who gets to mentor you.
                        Balance is gone, instead Dharma is the measuring stick... as well, as needing the opposing forces of Yin or Yang to keep going.
                        Also: rename Yin and Yang to something more approbiate according to region and time. A old germanic tribal warrior would probably just name those forces life and death and be done with it.
                        All Hekau Paths are usually inherently tied to Yang, with the exception of Necromancy which uses Yin and Nomenclature which can use both without issue.

                        Also, Demon Arts are frowned upon in certain cultural circles, but that's rather specific... and does not necessarily hold true for the more subtle ones.
                        And also the older bastards are not necessarily adhering to their own rules... freakin' hypocrites.

                        They also have all the shortcomings and advantages of typical KotE, which means Yin and Yang imbalance are in play, as well as the different conditions they can experience.
                        All disciplines and Hekau cost x7, because x8 is a bit to expensive. They have neither disciplines or Hekau that are easier to acquire, unlike KotE or Mummies.
                        I'll also note that I retooled Demon: The Fallen, as hinted at a bit up there, just slightly to get things in synch, but that's another thing.

                        Oh and the Akuma... those are the interesting ones.
                        They are only called Akumas - at least in parts of Asia - due to the fact that NOBODY likes them and wants them gone.
                        They exchanged their immortality for penance and immunity to the ravaging of the P'o, which means their monster can't rear it's head.
                        They are hunting down the escaped souls and returning them to their rightful place.
                        They are the Wardens and Headhunters send after all the escapees and some of the escapees even get turned to take up the gruesome task of dragging the guilty souls back,
                        even if they kick, scream and try their level best to get away.
                        Even better: They do loose their immortality, due to cutting a deal with some powerful entity, but said entity catches their soul every time they die and may send them back into the field.
                        And in best White Wolf tradition it is hard to determine who is right and who is wrong overall.
                        And the best part: Demons can now create them, although not control them, to use them as goons, allies or something else entirely.
                        Although they do need the names of some willing demons to do so, which adds another complication.

                        It produced a hell of a paranoid atmosphere in the games where I used these changes, because nobody knew for sure who worked for whom.
                        And cutting through all the webs, lies, deceit and intrigues on top of all the political stuff going on is just awesome.
                        Right now I am leading a campaign with this setup in roughly 900 AD in what will once be known as the HRE.
                        It's utterly hilarious, because the players are surrounded by human nobles, some werewolves, a bunch of rat kin that want to kill everybody just because and some mortal "wizards" - actually psions straight ripped out of Aeon. It's a political mess and utterly hilarious if looked at from the outside, in game everyone is playing for keeps.

                                                        Black Wind was designed with a very specific paradigm in mind - it was supposed to be like Celerity, but a little bit stronger (you could choose to do more than get extra actions), but you wouldn't have as much Demon Chi, so you couldn't use it as often, plus activating it could send you into Shadow Soul which was not a good thing. So a little bit better, but more dangerous and you generally wouldn't want to (or be able to) use it as often.

                                                        Then Celerity got nerfed, but there's not really an easy fix for Black Wind. If you nerf it the way Celerity was nerfed you'd basically break it. Very few Kuei-jin are going to have more than 3 or 4 Demon Chi (in contrast to pretty much any Kindred having 10 blood points) so spending 1 Demon Chi per Action doesn't work, plus Demon Chi is generally harder (and more dangerous) to regain, and you still have the whole issue with falling into Shadow Soul when you use it.

                                                        Rather than being nerfed or changed, Black Wind needs to be redesigned from the ground up. The paradigm it was initially created around no longer exists. It's far too powerful in its current form when compared to similar effects, but there's no way to really nerf it without completely crippling it.
