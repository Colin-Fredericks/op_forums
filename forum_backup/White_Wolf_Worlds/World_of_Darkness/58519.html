                                                  I keep watching the NBC Dracula like a train wreck with beautiful costumes and vampires. I think the acting & directing has gotten better over the course of it, but the characters felt rather cardboardy at first.

                                                  The pacing is uneven and unsatisfying. I think this is partly to blame upon the format of the show. American broadcast TV only has around 40 minutes of actual show for each one hour timeslot, and they need to time scene changes to fit in lots of commercial breaks. This doesn't lend itself well to a dramatic series. It feels like individual episodes and plot-points are rushed, yet the larger story is very slow and dragged-out because we get it in these tiny little bites.

                                                  This is a new-ish problem. The amount of time networks devote to commercial breaks has been steadily increasing for decades. Now that it's a third of the airtime, I'll only bother watching these shows on recordings where I can fast-forward through the ads. Cable networks like HBO are running the best dramas now, too, partly because they are well-funded and partly because they devote a hour of actual time to each episode.
