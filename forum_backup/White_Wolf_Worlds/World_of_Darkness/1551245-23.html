                            Originally posted by GreatHornedRatkin View Post
                            monteparnas The Tremere as a clan have only been around for 1,000 years, and created Thaumaturgy out of desperation. Also, Thaumaturgy is a style of blood sorcery, not the discipline of blood sorcery itself.

                            The Assamites have been using blood sorcery a hell of a lot longer than the Tremere, and Ur-Shulgi has been around for 12,000 years. I see no reason at all to claim he didn't have knowledge to some counter spell.

                            I mean, if Baba Yaga can just lock down the entire country of Russia, and her clan doesn't even have blood sorcery as an innate power, then why can't Ur-Shulgi be granted the same consideration?
                            It's more complex than this on several accounts.

                            First: the Tremere inherited traditions of Magic as old or more as Ur-Shulgi. Thaumaturgy isn't just the magic they created a millennium ago, it is the careful adaptation of accumulated magic knowledge from across epochs and places to the power inherent in vampiric Blood.

                            This comes directly from their origin in Ars Magica even before being restated in Mage. Do not underestimate their prowess.

                            Second: this adaptation was by a genius in magic (and Rein-Hägen personal PC and self insert). Lord Tremere was a superlative Mage among Archmages, already virtually immortal and so skilled that his least talented personal student was nonetheless able to replicate the fucking Curse of Caine in a bottle.

                            Third, Ur-Shulgi wasn't around for 12 thousand years. He was around between 2 centuries and 2 millennia, we're not sure how much, then he went to a nap. He's ignorant of everything that evolved in the world, including magic, in the last 10 thousand years.

                            Dude literally can't read because writing's just a new fad people got in during his sleep. Sure, he may have gotten up to speed since, but still.

                            Fourth and building on this, he had a ready, perfect counter for a spell whose fundaments themselves didn't even exist in his time? I don't buy this.

                            But the most important point: I'm not saying it is impossible or unbelievable. I'm saying it's a bad story. And all its aspects contribute to this, especially the fact there's no ritual or whatever, he just wishes the curse away. As storytelling it is lame.

                            And Baba Yaga isn't a good reference for well built and used Methuselah in VtM.

                            MarkK point perfectly demonstrates it. The problem isn't "Ur-Shulgi did". If it were a good story, then whatever. And that tiny retcon does make it more bearable, and only fail in making it actually good because it's so short, in passing and for such a small time.

                            Imagine the following scenario: for two months vampires and even some Mortals in Afghanistan notice a strange influx of resources to the middle of nowhere, as the Banu Haqim are pressed by the old monsters into diverting as much as possible into a build up of ritual materials to Allamut like never before. After that we have two weeks of eldritch energies gathering that in the last days has the castle glowing like a beacon while weird magical side effects take over the land, turning the desert into something more alien. At the climax all Banu Haqim are partially drained of something and each caste has a completely new and bizarre weakness for three days. Then it's gone along with the curse.

                            Now we have a rad story we can build upon, write tales happening during that time, have games happening in this window and witnessing those shenanigans, and all sorts of lingering results appearing in other games, like Vampires that still suffer the effects, magical anomalies, or just cities where the events left an impact that shaked the political landscape.
