          I think there's a bit of conflation of different Internet debates going on here.

          A lot of the division around the Nephandi has nothing to do with how playable they are, but how they got using in the Rev. and M20 metaplot.

          The "you should never ever play these" is also something that largely comes from specific Mage developers that aren't particularly big fans of playing Sabbat, Black Spirals, or other overtly evil characters types that were created solely to be the dark black to the PCs shades of grey. Which isn't nearly as common of a sentiment within the larger Mage community.

          The question of how is a Nepandus different from just an evil mage? The Nephandus is definitionally worse. In the race to the bottom where Nephandi have to be worse than just being evil, the interesting parts of playing an evil character gets lost in the transgressive high of being ever more atrocious; something that inevitably leads to someone's lines getting crossed and the game getting unfun.

          I would say the books have a similar stance on the Baali as not really being good player characters too for that matter.

          A lot of the evil-but-playable character types have some interesting themes and things to explore because they generally have a point, but their methods are both an internal and external source of diminishing what's right about their positions.

          The Sabbat, for example, aren't interesting because of the blood-rave-orgies. They're interesting because they accept the World of Darkness for what it really is far more than the other major vampire factions. They're interesting because of the contradictions and paradoxes that stem from how their founding ideals clash with the realities of being a group fighting a war over generations. They're interesting because in the end they really don't have any way to prove that just because they know that "truth" more than the others, that they've actually come up with a good solution to things; there is a pretty solid case that their methods are actually making the things they hate about the world worse, while the Camarilla's comforting lies might actually be better from a utilitarian standpoint.

          The Nephandi as PCs invariably kill that sort of nuance. The closest you get is different groups of them squabbling over the best way to destroy the world. There's no Code of Milan that creates philosophic struggles with real stakes within Nephandi groups. There's no unifying reason for Nephandi of differing persuasions to actually work together either.

          This leaves them as only really interesting to use as NPCs because as NPCs they serve to add drama and conflict to the story of the PCs. They don't have enough of their own to really last as PCs.

              In addition to Heavy Arms ' excellent treatment, it's also a matter of scale. Nephandi are not evil in the "I'm a selfish amoral bastard that does whatever he pleases and doesn't believe in scruples" kind of way. There are many characters belonging to all the factions of the different critters in the WoD that fall under this category. They're normal for their context even among the traditionally "positive" factions of the WoD like the Traditions and the Garou Nation.

              The trick with Nephandi is that they are not people with a lack of morals. They are a group of utterly insane deranged monstrosities that see horrifying deeds, corruption and debasement of people around them as light entertainment and setting the mood for a world either ending or being handed over to things from beyond. Worse yet, they chose this of their own free will and corrupted a part of their soul to go on and make everyone who comes after them take up their crusade against the state of Being.

              There is little to make these characters valid in play beyond the excesses the players can imagine them performing on others, because at the end of the day they will never evolve or care about anything other than suffering and the ultimate corruption of the world.

              What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.

                      Partly the Nephandi are considered off-limits by game designers who don't want to make "Nephandi: The Atrocity" a base playstyle. The Nephandi aren't "fun" evil - the kind that cackles "Mwuh Ha Ha!" and wears all black. Rather, they're the serious, "no one would have fun at a table where you do the kind of things Nephandi do recreationally" evil. (Unless the players were deeply, profoundly disturbed). The kind of shit that comes with a Content Warning is standard practice for them. Done for no reason but because they want to, or to please some entity from beyond the world.

                      It's not that you can't - no one can really stop you - but rather you shouldn't.

                      Nor is there any point or rationale for it, when it comes to Nephandi. It's my headcanon that Nephandi don't serve extra-planar beings as a goal unto itself. Rather, Demons or the Wyrm or the Great Ones are a means to an end. Not even necessarily the world's end. They're just shackled, emotionally, to Descent. They do it because, with their broken and inverted souls, breaking and killing and crippling and soiling and corrupting and making people suffer IS the point. They just like it. Anything else is an after-the-fact rationalization of a deeply irrational antipathy to Reality. If they can destroy or ruin it in a final way, they would. But they want, first and foremost, to spoil it all for everyone else.

                      That sort of brings it around to a very good reason Nephandi shouldn't be a player option: it's a deeply anti-social, unmutual path. Not good for a group play experience. It is the worst stereotype of Chaotic Evil play in D&D. It's disruptive. By definition, it promotes negative play experiences as the one and only playstyle.

                      At least with the Sabbat, there's in-built mechanisms within the narrative for the Pack staying together and collaborating. Sabbat players can have fun being monsters, without stepping on each others' toes. And at least the Sabbat's evil isn't JUST in service to their gratification. The sect has goals and fascinating philosophies about what it means to be a vampire. At least the Baali, while frequently working with or for Demons (or Sleepers), have a certain level of moral agency.

                      If you could sign up to play a Nephandus, you're playing the worst kind of evil, who has no choice in the matter.


                      And what are you getting out of a Nephandi PC, that can't arise from a PC that is "merely" a bastard? Nothing. You can just play a bad person, with selfish motives and delusions of grandeur. You can just play a "Dark Mage". You'll likely have way more fun, since you can define what being the antihero or villain protagonist means to you. As many D&D parties have shown, you CAN have a token Evil teammate, and still function as a play group.

                      It's when you use "Alignment: Evil" as a blank check to disrupt play that's the actual problem. By definition, Nephandi are disruptive. They HAVE to be, to be Nephandi.

                      Magestuck: The Ascension
