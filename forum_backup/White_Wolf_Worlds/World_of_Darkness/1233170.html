                                              Bonus trivia:
                                              If you weight a (dry!) log, then burn it up and actually manage to catch all the smoke particles and ash that gets produced, you will find that if you weight the stuff again it will weight MORE than it has before the log was burnt. Mind blown.

                                              This is because, while the matter of the burnt log has lost an absolutely miniscule amount of mass due of energy loss (Matter AND contained energy determine final mass), the matter the log consisted of actually has gained a whole pile of oxygen molecules Because, y'know, you need air to burn stuff for a reason. The oxygen binds to the burning stuffs (resulting in crispy things and ash), kicking other molecules out (smoke), and the change of the molecular bonds releases contained energy (as heat).

                                              So, if you subtract this mass of added oxygen, you will find that the remaining weight of all the ash and smoke is like 99.9999999% of what the log originally was. And that's it. And none of it comes from actual destruction of matter, just changes of molecule composition.

                                              There *is* a way to convert matter to energy (and vice versa), but this normally does not happen in everyday events. The most famous rare event of course being the Big Bang where a ton of energy got converted into the pile of matter known as the Universe. But they also converted matter into energy and vice versa in particle accelerators at an atomic level.



                                              TL;DR: Matter is essentially a form of solid energy, both matter and classic energy contained in it determine final mass, and normally there is no conversion between matter and energy happening in any way in everyday events. Also, neither can be literally destroyed, period. E=mc² is king.

                                                  Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                                                  I'm not even sure a nuclear blast can actually disassemble atoms.
                                                  No, no it can't...well, a nuclear blast is the RESULT of buhzillions of atom's nuclei disassembling at once, not a cause.
                                                  The mixed pile of protons and neutrons of a very unstable atom nucleus, as those in certain radioactive materials like specific forms of Uranium and Plutonium - strong radioactivity pretty much is the definiton of matter having unstable nuclei - gets hit with a neutron, splits up into two smaller nuclei, and the neutrons that are left over from that shoot out and hit other already very unstable nuclei around, causing the same reaction there. This is the Critical Mass's chain reaction that causes the explosion, releasing a HUGE amount of previously bound energy as all those countless unstable nuclei split nearly at once.
                                                  And even there, no matter gets technically destroyed, as far as I know. The pieces just get split up, and the resulting smaller nuclei collect neutrons over a long time to become stable again (i.e. non-radioactive for the everyday definition of that.).

                                                      Also, final 'Ambrosia does armchair science!' post!


                                                      If you really were to take matter and converted it into energy, 100% (which again, isn't happening in anything in the previous posts), that's where E=mc² comes in.
