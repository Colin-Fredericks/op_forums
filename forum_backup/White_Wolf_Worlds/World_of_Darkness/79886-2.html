                            Cisgendered hetero male.

                            I've played female characters as often as males since I started RPing. It began by realizing someone needed to play the romantic interests of the heroes my friends were making, and reading a lot of the original Marvel Excalibur run. Good characters in there, three of them female (Kitty Pryde, Rachel Summer, Meggan). I quite enjoy playing female characters, and don't shy from romance. My best male friends and I have played out several decent romances between our characters. Good fun.

                            Online, I've engaged in long term RPs as a female character, going into full sexual roleplaying as well. I've done the same as a male character. Doesn't bother me, as long as the person on the other end understood "this is play".

                            I have no issues with people wanting to play opposite gender characters when GMing, but have reserved the right to say "no" if they've proven to be poor at it (some people don't shift gender gears as easily). This "no" is relaxed every once in a while to give them a fresh chance.

                            I have no issues with cross-gender play in LARP as long as it's (a) tastefully done (b) honest effort is made to look/behave/sound like the alternate gender. I've dealt with it three times in LARP, ranging from a good performance to cringe worthy.

                            In my group males and female gamers alike dabble in the other gender, or play either equally, with only a few "sticking to their own gender". I think all gamers should take a stab at it.

                                        As a GM I've never given it much thought in the sense of seeing it as a big deal. I've been GMing games since the mid-90's (just a player before then), and in that time I've had to portray hundreds of NPCs (maybe more). Considering sex/gender is basically a 50/50 split, I've had to portray a lot of women, as well as characters of different racial or cultural backgrounds than myself. Comes with the territory. Now, a good deal of those female NPCs (I'm male) aren't highly detailed, due to being tertiary to the plot, and the roleplaying may be slightly glossed-over but that's no different than a good portion of the male NPCs who are equally tertiary to the story I'm telling. But if that female NPC is a main character, then yeah, I'll roleplay her fully to my players. In two cases, I've had players take the True Love Merit (one in a Dark Ages Vampire game from nearly 10 years ago, and one currently in a Renaissance Mage game) - in both those cases, their female love interests basically became GMPCs that feature pretty heavily in the campaign and so they're as "fleshed-out" as a long-term PC and I do my best to portray the actual love/romance between the characters. To my great satisfaction the Dark Ages Vampire game ran until Modern Nights (over 3.5 years of real-time) and so that relationship between my player's character and his love interest was portrayed over nearly a thousand years and when his True Love died during Gehenna, the player walked his own character into the sun during a wonderfully poignant moment (the player himself was actually weeping in real life [in a good way] - I consider it one of my greatest achievements in storytelling to date). So I like to think I do a decent job of portraying respectable and interesting female characters.

                                        I don't get a chance to play a lot of PCs since I'm the dedicated storyteller for our group so I make the important NPCs count. Out of the few PCs I have been able to run in long-term games (most of my PCs are usually only for one-shots), only one has been female. Though I have also played a gay male PC (I'm straight) in an ongoing Shadowrun game.

                                        As for the other subject that's cropped up on this thread - setting verisimilitude and roles for women - I'm in the camp of "do what makes sense for the setting and the players". So, pretty much all fictional settings I run are fairly equal-opportunity for women (I do have a female player in my group - she's been with the group since its inception in the late 90's). However, to use my most current setting as an example, I don't shy away from historical accuracy for my Renaissance Mage game. And my female player is completely fine with that and sees it as perfectly reasonable. The key is to not go out of your way to actively make the setting punish the female character (or worse, the player) or remove the character's agency. The setting might be oppressive to women but it's also a bunch of other stuff - treat the setting like you would a character - you wouldn't make a character whose only trait is being gay (I hope) like some awful sitcom, but you could make a character who also happens to be gay. It's the same thing for a setting, the Renaissance setting is oppressive but that's not its only trait or even the most defining - that would be the hope and opportunity for endless potential that the era brings: endless potential for war, technology (the sky's the limit), but also endless potential for women and everyone else. So my female player is having a wonderful time playing a 16-year-old recently Awakened Mage who is trying to navigate her new found power while also dealing with her family trying to marry her off relatively soon as said family jostles for political position in the Italian city-states. Her father is more willing to allow her the freedom to roam and be herself; it's the character's mother who is dead-set on women's roles and marriage (the character, consequently gets along with her father much better than her mother, meanwhile her brother is also a bit of a tool). Most of this set-up was the player's idea and I'm just rolling with what she gave me. In the long run though, if her character decides to seek more political and worldly mortal power, the player understands (and is completely fine with) the conventions of the time and that she'll need to go the Catherine de Medici route, at least for the time being. But those same limitations aren't imposed on her when seeking political power in the world of Daedalean politics (she's an Artificer). Consequently, for anyone looking to run a game in the Renaissance (be it Mage or anything else) that involves courtly drama, for a good look behind the eyes of a woman from the times (well, late Middle Ages, rather) I recommend the works of Christine de Pizan.

                                        Anyway, I hope my examples and experiences, illustrate my thoughts on the matter.

                                                White Wolf Games have always been (by Tabletop or LARP standards) very inclusive to varying sexual and gender identities.
