        This was a 7 years old discussion... wtf . Thread necro par excellence.

        From my POV, the Camarilla is not a Monolith. I have always felt like people overstate how “unified” the Camarilla actually is, especially pre V5.

        In practice, it is likely much closer to a loose aristocratic network than a centralized organization (similar to northern Italy during the renaissance). Every Prince runs their domain like a personal fiefdom and while there are higher authorities like Justicars and Archons, their influence is not constant.

        It is only logical, that in your night to night existence as a Kindred, the Prince matters far more than any global Camarilla structure anyway. The Traditions set the framework, but how they’re enforced (and how politics actually plays out) is highly local.

        That is why I tend to think of the Camarilla less as a top-down hierarchy and more like something akin to a “mob commission” model: there are powerful figures at the top, but real power is exercised regionally, and the lived experience varies a lot depending on where you are.

        Now lets look at Ashirra. I do not see them as just a “Muslim Camarilla” either, but as the actual dominant power structure in their regions. If you are operating there, that is the system that matters in practice, regardless of any broader claims. Similarly if you are in Capones Chicago, you are still technically in commission territory, but it does not have the exact same rules and structures as lets say NY. Technically same overall system, but in reality different.

        So yeah, shared rules, but not shared identity and highly fragmented execution. That tension is kind of the point from my pov.

                                    My take:

                                    - Camarilla: It's an understanding among Princes agreeing about the Primacy of the Traditions, the rules of the Conclave and the few cross-domain institutions, like Justicars and Blood Hunts.

                                    Kind of ends there. Mostly Princes are left to their own devices. They're also the highest in hierarchy: Justicars have global jurisdiction but not authority, depending on local recognition.

                                    The Inner Circle is the de facto conspiracy, but as such it's not a formal institution. It "doesn't exist". In lore that is a real but inconsistent depiction, in my games it's true.

                                    - Ashirra: they don't follow the Traditions, even if common sense makes them follow similar rules. What they follow is Islam, pure and simple. They follow very similar forms of Islam to those practiced by mortals, usually from their mortal days.

                                    Islam has no concept of intrinsic damnation. A nonhuman intelligent being is as capable as any human of following the Qur'an and reap the same benefits. This is in the book itself.

                                    "Choosing Islam" today isn't just pragmatism: it is the culture most of them grew within, even a Methuselah today was probably Muslim in life. That said Islam isn't monolithic and the Ashirra has sect interpretations that, while reasonably open, do not encompasses everything.

                                    The sect's territorial influence isn't the Muslim world or all the Middle East. They're strongest in Saudi Arabia and from there allegiances are always changing, although Afghanistan, Iran and Egypt usually have a number of aligned Sultans.

                                    While the Camarilla has the Justicars as a sort of international police, the Ashirra has Imams, scholars of religion and law, mostly of law. The real backbone of the sect are the main Imams of each clan, as they determine interpretation of the Shari'a to everyone else. The most influential Imams are from the Banu Haqim, Brujah, Lasombra, Nosferatu and Ventrue clans, and then above even those, the Imam of the Hajj, a small group of Nosferatu.

                                    - Hecata: a tentative new sect whose backbone are the Necromancer clans. Their goals are the most simple: resist harassment by other sects, help each other in research and make money.

                                    Their structure is based on family ties, be them by blood or adoption, and a common theme for members is to have a mortal family or group in the know who offers them support.

                                    Most of other clans and bloodlines who joined are Giovanni family members Embraced out of the Giovanni Bloodline. Sometimes that's the payment for a service, adding new skills to the toolkit, other times it just happens, but family is family and they "rescue" the Childe, appointing a foster sire.

                                    Adoption into the Hecata does happen, though, either of promising Necromancers or useful assets for the sect. Family unity is mandatory either way. Where other sects focus on general ideas and political structure, the Hecata have a strict demand for loyalty.

                                        - The Sabbat: formed in the fires of revolution, united by the Ritae and sharing culture through religion. The Sabbat is complex.

                                        What defines the Sect is the camaraderie, the pride in being monsters, the war against the Elders, individual freedom and faith. Yet each one of those is simultaneously absolute truth, full of caveats and ultimately kind of false, but not exactly.

                                        Camaraderie comes from the Vaulderie, but the rite isn't all powerful – that's kind of the point. Being a monster is good, but what kind of monster is an open question. War isn't suicide, so it's not a constant conflict against anything old, much less their own. Freedom is tempered by all levels of their society, being the most socializing sect. And while some are zealots, faith is a personal matter as long as you publicly espouse the party line and go to the Ritae regularly.

                                        In practice, the Sabbat is a different beast in each place, in accordance to the individuals there, their own interpretations and their Paths of Enlightenment, or sometimes their Roads. The only constant is the independent Packs and the Auctoritas Ritae.

                                        - Great Temple of Set: the Followers/Ministry are first and foremost evangelists of their god. Where they arrive, they erect a temple and start to seek converts both mortal and Kindred, and seriously any they can.

                                        This makes for a curious sect. Sometimes non Kindred get included in a sect for one reason or another. It's rare, but sometimes another bloodsucking creature is included for convenience, or a loyal retainer is revealed to not be a vampire, but still be loyal.

                                        Followers, though, have diversity in spades. In their temples everyone participate in ceremonies, mortals form the bulk of the Sect, and even their enemies the Serpents dare to invite and try to convert.

                                        Still, only the true Blood of Set can hold priestly positions, and the vampires of the clan keep command over all those who accept Set as the one to follow (whatever that means to a given group within the faith).

                                        - Special Cases: those are not Sects. Instead, they're broader groups covering large areas and partially tied by some cultural links, but not actual alliances. True Sects can form within them, though.
                                        -- Laibon: in Africa, specially south of the Sahara, many traits are common to local Vampires, and got intensified by colonial resistance. While white mortals controlled the continent for a long time (and in some senses still do), their institutions here are not under the command of European sects, but the Laibon who expelled the Camarilla and Sabbat with prejudice.
                                        -- Raktasadhus: in India the Vampires follow mortal culture way closer. The Embrace must follow Caste and Jati lines, the origins of vampires are debated in terms of the Hindu god or supernatural tribe who spawned them, and even without an actual sect, the Brahmins keep immense collective influence through religious authority and the magic of Sadhana. It's also the only place where the Ravnos are a local power and the Salubri exist in numbers without abnormal fear. As a curiosity, it's where the city of Golconda is found.
                                        -- The Courts of The Middle Kingdom: while the Kuei-Jin aren't the same species as Kindred, the similar niche puts them in frequent contact where they live. There is no hard and fast rules about what comes from their meetings, but in many cities there is either an alliance between leaders of some factions, or one species is de facto the administration of the area for all the undead.
                                        -- Independent Cities: those are all over the place. No Sect is really capable of keeping control over every city in the world – there's just not enough vampires – so when the local ruler decides to not follow any, there's little that can be done in the short term, and some can stay independent a very long time, longer than the current sects existed.
                                        -- Clan Membership is Not a Thing: that must be clear for all Sects. There's no such a thing as a member clan. Few clans have enough central structure to even ponder about the option of establishing a given Sect as mandatory or something, and the sects themselves don't work in this principle. For the Camarilla you're under their law regardless, for the Ashirra they're offering support to any Muslim vampire who accepts, the Setites will receive anyone they can convert. The Sabbat has the acceptance of all as a fundamental policy, and the Giovanni won't deny an opportunity. In the end, they all deal with individuals, not bloodlines.

                                                    Monteparnas’ descriptions above remind me of something I’ve been thinking about lately re: vampire, that being that most clans make more sense as cults (with or without unique forms of sorcery) than as ‘clans’ tbh. Now of course this is the same take as Requiem more or less and I still like the Masq clans and think that 9/10 times it would be too onerous to redefine them, even if it is something I entertain for fun.

                                                    Like, IMO, the clans that make the most sense as actual clans are the Brujah, Gangrel, Nosferatu, Toreador, Ventrue, and maybe the Ravnos (although I think making them a bloodline would also be fine tbh). That being said I don’t think making all the others cults is a perfect solution either - I think Malkavians make more sense as a condition (I’ve always liked the idea of Dementation as a psychic virus) and the Old Clan Tzimisce can easily work as a sub-culture of the Ventrue IMO.

                                                    Then what do we have looking at the rest?

                                                    An organization of hermetic mages who used magick to become vampires (Tremere), a group of vampires who have gained supernatural powers through some form of communion with a force/principle of cthonic darkness (Lasombra), a group who have done the same with a mysterious flesh-warping demonic virus - possibly spread via the vinniculum (Tzimisce), a group of diablerie-cultists dedicated to enforcing a vampiric legal code established by its founder (Assamites), death/necromancy cultists of one stripe or another (Cappadocians/Giovanni/Hecate/etc.), followers of a Gnostic anti-nomian sect with a heart-snake themed unique sorcery (Settites), and followers of an enlightenment-seeker Golconda-teaching visionary (Salubri).

                                                    Of those I think you could divide the above into two camps: A. Those who are part of formal and coherent organizations which can function virtually as micro-sects (Tremere, Assamites, Temple of Set, Giovanni/Hecate*); B. Those which make more sense as vampiric lineages descended from a Founder who carried out a transformative occult ritual and those who have been since inducted (Lasombra, V!Tzimisce, Cappadocians, True Settites, Salubri; plus groups like the Baali).

                                                    Added disambiguation: if you wanted to use this framework I do think the Death Clan and the Settites make somewhat muddled cases. The classic Giovanni and the V5 Hecate are essentially organized conspiracies/micro-sects while also simultaneously (under this framework) being members of a lineage created through the line-founder Cappadocious’ transformative Death ritual. See also the Settites, where the Church of Set can function as a micro-sect which can incorporate any vampiric initiates while being spearheaded by those of the lineage founded by Sutekh.

                                                    Using this Founding system idea, I also think it would make sense that sufficiently powerful sorcerers within these lineages should be able to induct members: whether that’s a heart-removal rite to become a True Settite or being Consecrated in the Abyss to become an obtenebration-gifted Lasombra.

                                                    I also think that if used many of these founded-lineages would not date back into hoary antiquity like clans currently do. For example whether the Assamites can arise prior to Islam is up for debate (I can see an argument for going back to Hammurabi and other bronze-age law givers), I can’t really see the Settites emerging prior to the emergence of Gnosticism in the Mediterranean world-system of the Axial-age or the Salubri emerging prior to the advent of Buddhism in South Asia (or the practice of Yogism in axial age South Asia at the earliest).

                                                    (Admittedly this is somewhat far afield from the OG question of V5 but it seemed in line with the discussion of sects in general and I felt like sharing this concept I was spitballing with).
