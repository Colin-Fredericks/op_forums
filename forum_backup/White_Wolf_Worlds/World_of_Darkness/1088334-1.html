                        Heya! I did some work on the design for Nightmare, Imbalance, and Bedlam in C20. I may be able to offer some perspective on how and why those parts of the game were changed.

                        Nightmare dice in C2e were a Banailty mechanic, giving the players a release valve, but 10 Nightmare would result in a permanent Banality. There was no way to get rid of Nightmare in C2e other than rolling ‘1’s or accumulating 10 point which converted to permanent Banality. Bedlam in C2e had no mechanical system at all (“The Storyteller has complete control over the process of Bedlam”).

                        One of the design goals for C20 was giving Bedlam an equivalent presence in the game mechanics as Banality (as a condition, not necessarily a temper). Additionally, Nightmare seemed out of place as pathway to Banality what with Nightmares being a type of dream. The perspective we took was basically that Nightmare as a concept felt more in tune with dreaming gone wrong (Bedlam) then no dreams at all (Banality).

                        All of this led to a design for the same type of accumulation system for Nightmare, but with the result of 10 points being Imbalance as a gateway to Bedlam Thresholds, removing the arbitration of Bedlam from simple Storyteller fiat. By introducing Nightmare as an element of the path to Bedlam, we could tie it into Unleashing and also give the players a choice to reduce the difficulty of cantrips by willingingly taking on more Nightmare dice.

                        As it stands, Nightmare is a slow(ish) build to Imbalances, which are a slow(ish) build to Bedlam. Bedlam, like Undoing, is a character ending event, so we made a conscious choice to have the systemic progression to Bedlam feel ever-present (Nightmare dice get rolled frequently) but to have the actual descent into Bedlam include some player agency (Unleashing, spending Imbalanced Willpower, pursuing Epiphany to mitigate Imbalance).

                        That's the reader's digest on the design process. If you and your group enjoyed Nightmare tied to Banality, and liked Bedlam as a pure narrative device, follow your dreams! Err, or nightmares I guess.
