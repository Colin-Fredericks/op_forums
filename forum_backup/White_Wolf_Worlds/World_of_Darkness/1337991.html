      These aren't necessarily all that flashy, many are basic but effective. Most of these have a success threshold of 2 unless otherwise specified.

      Direct Damage. All of these are Vulgar:
      Forces 2--Focus sunlight, static shock (using ambient static electricity) Sunlight burns do Agg damage, the static does Lethal if I remember right.
      Forces3, Prime 2--Lightning or fire or lasers. Some editions require spending a Quintessence. Agg damage.
      Matter 3--As long as there is some hard material near by, it can morph into a spike and stab them. Lethal damage.
      Life 3--Whether it manifests as nasty wounds or as turning the flesh into translucent necrotic gel, it does Agg damage.
      Entropy 4--Decay. Agg.

      Less Direct Attacks:
      Life 2--Tree attack. Nearby tree hits target with branch.
      Life 2--Beeeeeees! If there are bees nearby, they attack whoever is nearest.
      Life 3--Spasm. Cause body parts to seize up, temporary difficulty attacking and dodging or even missed turns.
      Time 3 or Life 3--Makes target go unconscious. Time does this by slowing the heart rate Depending on your interpretations of the rules this version might also require Life 2. Life 3 is obvious.
      Forces 2--Flare. Redirect light into the target's eyes.
      Mind 3--Pain. Cause pain, and thereby hinder effectiveness.
      Mind 3--Numbness. It's hard to do much when you can't feel anything.
      Time 3--Slow enemy.
      Forces 2--Suddenly the ground underneath the target has no friction. A bit juvenile but effective.

      Attack Enhancement:
      Correspondence 1--Increases the accuracy of attacks by enhancing spatial awareness.
      Entropy 2--Increases probability of hits.
      Life 2--Strength surge.
      Life 2--Speed Surge. Up to one extra action per turn.
      Life 3--Claws. Do agg damage. Vulgar.
      Life 3--Attribute Enhancement.
      Time 3--Better speed surge. Multiple actions a turn.

      Ensnaring:
      Life 2--Grass or bushes hold or hinder target.
      Life 3--Paralyze
      Matter 3--Turn target's clothing against them, turning their shirt into a straitjacket or causing their pant legs to fuse together.
      Forces 2--Dynamically bleed off all their kinetic energy as they try to move. Probably more than 2 successes, and higher difficulty without conjunctional Life 2.

      Deflection: Generally shields that only deflect ranged attacks are Coincidental, if they also block Melee or unarmed attacks they obvious enough to be Vulgar. Unless otherwise specified they add to the success threshold required to hit. More than 4 successes added to the success threshold is probably Vulgar. These are powerful, lasting Effects that require at least 3 successes depending on effectiveness.
      Forces 2--Deflects attacks kinetically. For it to follow the subject, some interpretations of the rules also require Life 2. Personally I just raise the difficulty by 1 if there is no Life.
      Entropy 2--Deflects using probability.
      Correspondence 3--Deflects using a Ban (a kind of ward). Technically requires Matter as well, but as with Forces and Life, I just add 1 to the difficulty without it.
      Matter 2, Prime 2--Causes projectiles to disappear before hitting the target. Vulgar.

      Toughness:
      Prime 2--Empower clothing to give a small Agg soak. Maybe Vulgar.
      Life 3--Increase soak, give self Lethal soak.
      Life 3--Increase health levels.
      Life 2--Ignore pain by turning off pain receptors.
      Mind 1--Ignore Pain by strength of will.

      Misdirection:
      Mind 3--Visions.
      Mind 3--Hearing things.
      Forces 2--Mirages. Forces illusions work similarly to Mind illusions, except there is not a higher success threshold for more targets. Maybe a higher initial threshold though.
      Forces 2--Illusory sounds.

      Firearm Neutralization (all Coincidental):
      Matter 2--Neutralize one bullet Only 1 success needed. Transform the propellant of one bullet into a similar but inert substance.
      Matter 2--Neutralize all bullets Requires 2 successes but is otherwise as above.
      Matter 3--Deform the firearm. Subtly changes the weapon's shape to make it dysfunctional.
      Forces 2--Butterfingers. The weapon (doesn't have to be a gun) suddenly has no friction.

          One of the best combat Wonders in the game is right out of Forged by Dragon's Fire. I'm forgetting the name of it, but basically it's a staff that can roll defense for you. Mages' access to multiple actions is fairly harsh compared to the other splats, and multiple actions are pretty powerful. Even the ability to have a bunch of free defense rolls from a magical item means you can focus on spell casting instead.

          Again, nothing super creative here, but some pretty useful (if high XP) effects that are pretty much all things to prep ahead of time:

          Correspondence 3/Time 4 to hang a teleportation spell, and reflexively escape combat.

          Life 2/Prime 3 (I think, I'm not sure if it needs revising for M20) to give yourself an auto-healing effect in case you take a big hit before you can do anything else.

          There's a few ways to do it, but any of the ways to pull a 'play possum' especially with the auto-healing is useful as people think they put you don, you're actually OK, but get a few free rounds of magic in before they realize it.

          Offense? Sure there's some creative stuff you can pull off, but the problem is that prepping offensive spells ahead of time usually means having to use aimed attacks and you can waste a lot if they dodge it. The most creative thing to do, is make sure all your mages can cooperatively cast. A mage that can throw a not so creative Forces 3/Prime 2 fireball is OK. Doing it with four mages that all have Forces 1/Prime 1 helping out... is just flat out scary. Getting everyone on the same page is the rough part.

              Heavy Arms brings up a point I forgot about, which is dodging magic. It's kind of unfortunate the way they handle it, either implying or stating that the difficulty is the same as for dodging other types of attacks, depending on the edition. It seems to me that magic should be harder to dodge--after all, it follows the caster's will and perception, not the predictable trajectories bound by mundane objects and forces. And of course the dice pools involved are usually quite a bit in favor of the dodger versus the Arete roll.* I usually houserule Dodge difficulties against magic as being 8, with the possibility of reducing that to 7 if dodging into good cover and the attack using some kind of object or force. Dodge attempts against direct attacks on the body like Life and Entropy would only benefit from complete cover.

              *Later editions add a separate attack roll for the caster, but adding more rolls is a bad idea, especially in combat.

                                  Originally posted by Trategos_Sol View Post
                                  Self explanatory. I’m running a Hunter game and the cell is going up against a cabal. I’d like to really showcase the creativity of Mage. What are your favorite rotes you’ve used, designed, or seen for combat and also escape?
                                  The game I'm currently playing has two Martial Arts mages, a cyborg daredevil (Frankfurt) and a "vampire" blood mage (Li). The first is an etherite/akashic nutcase, and the latter was an orphan that eventually got accepted into the akashayana for her (heretical) compatibility with their values and incredible power. Both are valuable instructors of various fighting styles for the Traditions, arguably the most valuable in north america.


                                  These are a few of their combat magicks, requiring varying degrees of mastery. They are mostly Martial Arts sometimes mixed with Cybernetics, Hypertech, and Alchemy/Maleficia (very much Blood Magic aesthetic). As with most magicks, more than one sphere configuration can pull it off, I will post the ones we have used.


                                  ------------------------------------
                                  A well-used defensive rote is the Meditation on the Invisible [Forces 2 or Life 3, Time 2]. Li muses and focuses on the wings of the hummingbird until she can picture each individual beating of its wings.
                                  In this state, she has certain foresight and perfected motion that allows her to dodge bullets as if they were as slow as a thrown knife or rock.
                                  Frankfurt uses instead Stochastic Overdrive [Forces 2, Life 3 (body) or Matter 2 (armor), Mind 1], enhancing his reflexes and motion to calculate the lines of fire and either dodge or block them accordingly.

                                  2 successes is enough to get this going for a few rounds. One can prepare with time to make this last a long while, but the mage is noticeably abnormal while it is up, at times very twitchy, at times moving like an accelerated video footage of a person larping slow motion movement.
                                  ------------------------------------
                                  Li is almost never involved in a fight without her Hungry Predator Sight active [Entropy 1, Life 1, Mind 1]. She homes in on heartbeats and fear like vultures to carrion, working as an alternative sensory input. Extra successes let her aim more precisely at juicier veins (a small bonus to damage). Correspondence 2 lets her extend this sight through obstacles in a short range.
                                  ------------------------------------
                                  Frankfurt is a fan of the Fire Cart Barrage [Forces 2]. A seemingly wild and incoherent series of punches, kicks and slashes that is, in truth, an elaborate kata that cannot be performed without near superhuman speed, force and precision. Deliver a single attack, to be checked against [successes] enemies in range and splitting the base damage uniformly among them. With Forces 3 and 1 quintessence, you do full damage to each of them.
                                  This one is vulgar if any of the targets is not within immediate punching range.
                                  ------------------------------------
                                  Against tanky enemies, Li uses the 8 Gates of Death. [Entropy 3, Mind 2] or [Entropy 5]. It is very much a rote for a Master due to its stringent requirements and the type of foe against whom this would be useful.
                                  The mage overwhelms a single enemy with an invincible battle formation that inevitably leads to her foe's death.
                                  For 8 consecutive rounds, you spend 2 actions, one in regular, aggressive combat against a single foe, and one casting the 8 gates of death.
                                  The 8th and final round is a single action where you deliver the decisive, inevitable attack, dealing all the accrued successes as damage at once.

                                  Frankfurt prefers the Thousand Fractures Atemi (informally called "argh, just fucking die already") [Forces 3, Entropy 1 OR Life/Matter 1], executed much in the same fashion, but instead of enforcing a fated battle formation you are attaching dozens of minute explosives to resonating weak spots on the target for simultaneous detonation. Third parties are advised to step back a little.
                                  ------------------------------------

                                  Another mage of that cabal, Galahad, is an enlightened prophet, adherent to a creed that posits a caste of revered leaders with empowered servants, and as such it is his station to command obedience and elevate his lessers. His Divinity protects him and scours his enemies. He's not a combat mage, but he makes do. His magicks are derived from his blessings and his unbelievable charisma (faith, desire and domination). Specifically for combat he has three bread and butter rotes.

                                  Radiant Authority [Mind 3]. It is not the place of the subject to question the master. Projecting confidence and control, even casting an aside glance is hard for his enemies. Weak willed characters cannot attack or otherwise oppose him, while others are penalized.

                                  Purging Flames [Mind 2] or [Spirit 3]. Galahad chants an exorcism of fire, a curse that flays the enemy's will to oppose him and his allies. The target must be on fire, and each round chanting the exorcism penalizes any action the target takes against him or his allies. The Spirit 3 variation of it requires an obedient flame spirit, and it can lit the target on fire on its own and also enact its powers through the gauntlet during the exorcism (it's also vulgar in most of the urban world!).

                                  Devoted Servants Never Fall [Correspondence 4, Mind 2]. A quick blessing is delivered unto an ally or believer, shielding them from both curse and stab. Penalizes attacks against your subjects for the duration..

                                  Check my Exalted homebrew!

                                          I think it does.

                                          Some ideas

                                          1º In general when one thinks on combat rotes one want's to think Coincidental, but when hunting mages it'f fun to note that their homes tend to have a...more flexible reality (more so in M20, with the Reality Zones rule). This allows one to pull all sort of nasty stuff in there and it's specially important for the Trads becuase their repertoire tends to include a ot of stuff that does look like magick.

                                          I'm thinking that the Chantry has, likely, a "public"area of low magick where mundane Acolites can work/live...and then there's the sealed place where they have all their horror-story experiments with the undead. Some still crawling down there (or could be released with a switch). I imagine they have a library, including occult texts revealing great deal about death and undead (not many Hunters would care, but maybe a Visionary could use/buy them...)

                                          I retain the idea a Ward against Guns (causing a severe penalty) could be thematic to have on parts of the Chantry (like, the sacred places where mundanes aren't allowed). It forces people to fight on a ground that many Traditions find confortable (Sword & Sorcery) and it's something that could work against many human treaths (and supers that use guns, that are many).

                                          2º Since they're "incredibly naive", I'm thinking of them as Vormass wanabee that most likely haven't reached anywhere near Mastery. At the same time, they need to be competent and knowledgeable enough to be able to hunt nigth-folk with decent chances of succeeding. I'm thinking a group of strong Disciples/low level Adepts would cut it and be decent antagonists. A lot of lvl 2-3 rotes and perhaps one or two involving a lvl 4 Sphere

                                          3º Ideas for Paradigm/foci.

                                          *For the Chorister I imagine a very hollier-than-thou Gnostic erudite. The World it's a Prision (M20) created by the Demiurge, one that one does not escape at death. Ascencion it's necessary, Death a blasphemy. Perhaps the Chorister can see the Underworld, the pains even faithful (heretic) Wraiths would suffer (perhaps a lover or friend that was very devot ended there, became Heretic, and got soulforged. Drama!), giving all the more reason to hate death. Faith/High Ritual as Practices to both have divine magic and incentive to do fankenstenian experiments with circles and all the cool magey stuff. Instruments/foci coul include prayers, angelic invocations, singing (very creepy when done to "test subjects"), arcane symbols

                                          *For the Euthanatos. This is interesting because they're supposed to defend the circle, not to break it. This one it's a barabbi, a traitor, even if the rest of the Tradition haven't discovered him/her yet. Could even be a survivor from the "consanguinity of eternal joy" (the Euthanatos faction that was led by Voormas, before the Traditions discovered their practices and hunted them all) that wentinto hidding. If you want a twist you could make this mage to be the "éminence grise" talking the Chorister about defeating death, which could in turn point to a larger conspiracy that wants to end death for a clifhanger. Albeit that could take protagonism from the Christian shtick of "death it's a blasphemy" as motivator.

                                          Anyhow, this one may well be the deadliest member of the Cabal, and the one that has the most to loose if the cabal get's disolved. Could believe in a Divine Order (a Dharma ordering the Earth...in this case a Dharma to usurp or break), usually represented by some sort of hinduism, but he doesn't need to be like that. Euthanatos are very diverse, could even be christian or believe in Santería (San la Muerte). Both would give good synergy with the Chorister. Weapons and/or martial arts to deliver the good death. Also rituals to incarnate the power of deities/angels/saints, scarification, bones because nothing says "death" more than bones (and yes, christians can use bones too).

                                          The Verbena it's also stepping in a dangerous zone by trying to end death, although not so much as the Euthanatoi. I haven't many ideas right now, but there are many ancient myths about immortality and death your Verbena could try to exploit. And blood magick, of course.

                                          For the other 2, I think the stereotypes work pretty well (mad doctor and evil wizard). It must be noted, though, that Hermetics can also be christians (historically, they kinda were)

                                          A bit of overlap it's good for Mages because it allows to do spells combining their power by combining their Instruments and approaches(albeit it does reduce diversity in approach).

                                          Necromancy.

                                          "Necromantic" classic effects require Life and Matter for the undead flesh, Spirit (maybe Mind under the Etheric Paradigm) to control the soul, Entropy because it controls decay. Time may be interesting too, because decay it's often related to the passage of time.

                                          Here's some experiments I think they could have been doing (and, maybe, weaponized):

                                          *Introducing ghosts into a dead body to reanimate it. This needs Spirit 4 (unless the ghost can do this naturally) on top of the reanimating Spheres. They could also have been trying to imprision ghosts into *other* stuff with Spirit 4 (giving a setup for a very, very, haunted house).

                                          *Awakening the spirit of the flesh: This is a very weird, very "dr. frankeinsten" approach to zombies. Spirit 3 can "awaken" inanimate objects, giving them conscience and limited movility. Soo...what happens if you cast *that* on a dead body (that counts as an object). I think that could create a shambling zombie (altrough not necessarily a loyal one)

                                          *Trying several methods against decay or death on living and unliving subjects. To then test them against several forms of death. Afterwards some of those may be used by the very cabal members:

                                          Heavy Arms mentioned the "auto healing" effect of Prime 3/Life 2, that could easily be one (and I think it should be). They could have more stuff like that.

                                          The Etherite could have a machine (a Charm/Gadget) that senses when you're dying, and then brings you back, healed. A Time 3/Prime 3 or 2/Life 3 Charm that "rewinds the time on your body 1 (or more) rounds" the round you died, very, VERY Paradoxical (and would work only once unless it's a Wonder, which would require more Prime) - but also fitting for an Etherite figthing death.

                                          A combination of Life 3/Matter 3 can give your body some of the properties of an undead's body (w/o becoming one), hardening the flesh and diminishing the need of vital function w/o erasing them. This allows to soack lethal and to halve some forms of damage like vampires do. Fitting for a group that studied undead.

                                          *Because Prime 3 can create Charms of inanimate objects and dead bodies can count as objects you could have all sorts of wacky stuff attached to certain valued animated dead bodies, similarly as we did for the mages. Skeletons that reform once or explode uppon destruction (that's a hermetic's favorite)

                                          Hunting

                                          *Having acolites with blessed weapons that cause Aggravated Damage it's just Prime 2 and would be capital if some of the captured nigthfolk get out of hand. This is irrelevant to Hunters (rarely relevant to mages), as only supernaturals are affected by Agg different than Lethal. BUT it could matter if the Hunters actually get to grab one of these weapons to hit the monsters and constructs that surely live (or un-live) within the Chantry

                                          *Sensory effects, of course, can't be underestimated about what they can reveal. They're often used to follow and research prey. While these are invisible, they can be showed off to the PC by the odd, perhaps uncanny, reactions of the mages to things that can't (or shouldn't) be seen.

                                          For instance, if the Chorister has Time 2, he could know Heroes (obviously misguided) are going to come to kick the door trough a prophetic dream, and introduce to the PC with this foreknowledge. That sort of things make mages feel more magical in my opinion (mages are people that "know things" nobody has right to know, after all)... in this case it could justify a degree of preparation for the conflict too, but I would minimize this, prophecy tends to be vague after all (not to mention self-fulfilling).

                                          A Danger Sense it's Entropy 1 and a must have for any Euthanatos (and other mages that do dangerous stuff, like hunting nigthfolk). Very good for a "martial arts" vive, where the killing intent can be sensed to avoid an ambush.

                                          Prime 1 it's used for all sorts of magical research, and can detect Edges being used. Remember that some can be countered, a mage that's being scried could sense this and close the connection with Corr 2 or scry back (Imagine the Hermetic reading at his book suddenly looking at the onlooker, doing a sign and canceling the scrying. Or perhaps trying to comunicate mentaly with the Hunter. Both can be scary).
                                          One thing to note it's that this "sensing when you're being watched" doesn't cover only magical sensing. Paranoid mages (and a cabal of immortality obsessed mages that hunt undead with a rogue Euthanatos should be) could have set protections against their Chantry being closely watched (or even themselves, when they're in a hunt). Mind 2/Corr 2 can be used to detect if someone it's watching you or an area (Perception it's related with Mind, Corr with the area and ranged perceptions), and who. This may or may not work in Hunters with Conviction active, it's not direct mindread but it comes close.

                                          *Restraining it's going to be very useful for this cabal because they want to capture nigth-folk, not kill them. I imagine they also dislike death, and thus will try not to kill (just as I imagine sentient nigthfolk captured by them will soon desire the sweet release of death)

                                          Forces 2-3 can be used to restrain someone by either sapping the kinetic energy or using a forcefield, pretty basic. I would rule it as a sort of magical grapple, with 1-2 strength per success. This can be represented all sorts of ways. I.E, the Euthanatos could summon shadows to grapple the target, the Etherite could have a "ray of immobilization"

                                          Life 3 should be more than enough to impair people. A rather peculiar rote I remember was one to make people puke. Because figthing while having the natural reflex to vomit active it's rather hard that would give a serious penalty. More straightfoward attacks could just cause violent pain (the Etherite could have a classic "ray of pain" even) or physical paralysis on the target's body.

                                          Mind 3 to make people sleepy, or more trusty (to command them to come or asleep instantly on a single turn you need 4).

                                          A real nasty if you have someone with Spirit 4: Have a bound spirit (maybe a spectre bonded to submission by the Chorister) possess the body of the target to figth for control.

                                          More mundanely (but very effectively), you can reinforce the power of drugs that are normaly used to incapacitate people using Life 3/Matter 3 (with the correct Spheres these may be made to work on undead too), and/or use Matter 3 to reinforce ropes, chains, gags (a Forces imbued gag that perfectly silences people could be useful for this team of kidnappers), etc. You could even add Entropy to lower the chances of the rope breaking. This could be represented as technomagic made by the Etherite, the Chorister blessing items so they work better or the Verbena creating powerful potions.

                                          Note that many of these won't work on a Hunter with Conviction, but they could be tried anyway because mages aren't likely to know how Hunters work (altrough this could change if a PC get's captured).

                                          *Setting ambushes

                                          Forces it's a very versatile Sphere here, from invisibility to magical silence and increasing darkness (like, breaking surrounding lights).

                                          Very subtly, Mind 3 can impel other people to leave an area alone

                                          Time 2/Correspondence 2/Entropy 1 to have a very good idea "when" and "where" to set an ambush.

                                          Matter 2-3/Prime 2 "Smokescreen": Create mist, smoke or another condition to impair the visibility of your victim and possible witnesses (remember to have sensory effects to see trough your own obstacle). This would actually work on Hunters, as much as they see trough all supernatural obfuscation, they don't see trough actual physical objects...while a Mage with a lvl. 1 Sphere active could. It may be Coincidental or not depending on how the smoke/mist manifests.

                                          Escaping

                                          Here there's nothing like Correspondence 3 teleportation. Vulgar, sure, but instant travel to your safe place (that may be at the other side of the planet, Corr doesn't care about distance but familiarity) it's hard to beat.

                                          A more subtle (and perhaps easier) approach could be to use Corr 1 to seek the best escape route. Entropy 1 to 3 to increase your chances of running away while deflecting enemy chances to catch you. Life 3 to be the perfect athlete. Time 3 to be faster. Forces 3 and/or Entropy 3 to hurl stuff on the path of your pursuer. If there are doors, Matter 2-3/Corr 2 to open or close locks on doors (add forces so the door "it's opened by a wind"). If there's people, Mind 3 to compel them to get in the way of your pursuers (if you're obviously under attack, and not obviously the bad/wrong guy, you could give them more courage to intervene in your favor)
                                          It may be tricky to Focus on the needed effects while escaping this way, but most would have something. It's just a matter of being creative. The Chorister and the Hermetic could use subtler prayers/inantations to get a better oportunity, the Euthanatos and the Vervena could have a symbol of a deity related to travel that's easy to carry (as small collar or jewel, tatooed in their body, woven in their cloth...).

                                          Matter 2/ Prime 2 "smokebomb". As "Smokescreen" above.

                                          If there's no Avatar Storm, Spirit 3 to enter the Umbra (Vulgar, but useful against enemies that can't affect the spirit world and when you don't have or can't use Corr 3). Depending on the Umbrascape this may be a bad idea (say, if the place it's full of dangerous spirits), but for the same reason it can be a good escape route (walls that don't exist on the other side allow to go faster, allied or enslaved spirits can lend their hand)

                                          Life 2: Faking dead. You can lower your vital functions until your body it's undistinguishable from a corpse. Unless your enemies are in the custom of burning the corpses of their enemies (which, to be fair, it's a reasonable thing to do if you're figthing supers), it may provide the oportunity to escape
