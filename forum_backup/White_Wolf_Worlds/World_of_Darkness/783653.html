    World of Dawn (fanmade supplement)

    12-10-2015, 05:23 PM
    Not entirely sure where to put this, so I'm putting it here. Hope that is okay.

    So a few months back I purchased Vampire The Masquerade 20th Anniversary Edition as well as Mage, 20th Anniversary Edition. Good books, like them a lot. I've always kind of wanted to play in a 'post gehenna' setting so to speak. So I wrote a bit on a what if setting. Figured I could put it up here.

    I've done some stuff on Vampire and Mage as those are the settings I know the most about. Maybe will write some more on it later. Here you go.

    For each age is a dream that is dying,
    Or one that is coming to birth.

    A world has ended.
    A final battle.
    In the cavernous bowels of the world. At the shining edge between the sky and the heavens. In the filthy alley ways of every city. In the highest towers, of glass, of dreams, of ivory. In the pitch black forests. In the bleak fields below the world.
    Screaming monsters fought horrors from beyond. Cackling madmen threw themselves into a sea of souls. Cursing youths and ancients beyond reckoning struggled. Fire and blood and pain and desperation ruled.
    And the world ended.
    And from the ashes, a world was born.
    It was Gehenna. The Apocalypse. The end of the Ascension War.

    The World of Darkness died.
    The World of Dawn is now.​

    What is this?
    This is a fan made supplement to the popular roleplaying game setting of the classic World of Darkness. You should know what the World of Darkness is. If you do not, google “classic World of Darkness” and you should quickly get the gist of it.

    The central premise of this supplement is that all the rumored end times of the old world of darkness lines occurred in the early 2000s, but that the world itself did not literally end. Rather, trough an epic night of battles and adventure and lots of rather brutal and tragic death, the various factions and people of the old world of darkness somehow managed to persevere. In the wake of these battles however, the world has permanently changed. This is not the old world of darkness. Many factions have suffered severe damage and losses, many old status quo are now straining and breaking apart, and the world as it was is simply no more, in both a material and metaphysical sense.

    This supplement deals with this new world. It asks one simple question, the answer to which is entirely up to you.

    They say the world was saved.

    Now what?

    Chapter I Part I: The Vampires.

    The Dead are dying.

    Dawn is upon us.

    When we fade, who will remember us?

    Will it truly be like we never were?

    Gehenna, the long rumored apocalypse of Vampiric lore arrived almost thirteen years ago. To this day, no one has a complete grasp on the exact course of events during that final night. What is known is that several massive battles took place, involving not just vampires, but every kind of supernatural, and that the end result was the defeat and presumed destruction of the antediluvians, and the survival of the vampiric race.

    Whatever the true course of events, it is an undeniable fact that the curse of the Damned has weakened. The unfortunate creation of thin bloods no longer seems constrained by generation, and even elder vampire finds some of their old powers failing. Kindred society itself is strained, not just from their own weakening blood, but from the losses sustained during the final night, from the cracking masquerade, and from their own internal strife.

    Themes and Moods
    These are new themes and moods a storyteller can rely on while crafting a vampire chronicle using the World of Dawn.

    Death – Things are ending and changing in irrevocable ways. The world is not the same today as it was yesterday. Inevitable, some things will be lost. Chronicles focused on this theme could play up the weakening of Kindred blood, and the strain within the organizations that make up Kindred society.
    A New Age – An Age has ended, and a new one has begun. There is a certain vibrancy to things. Old traditions are changing, old organizations are strained and forced to change underneath various pressures. While the future can be scary, there is possibility in it.
    A New Horror – While there are bright spots in the World of Dawn, there are shadows as well. Many old evils still remain walking the earth. There is no guarantee the future will be a bright one.

    Melancholy - Many lost friends and lovers during the end. Many lost integral pieces of themselves. Many believe vampires are slowly passing from the world. There is certainly room for sadness and reflection within this new age.
    Nostalgia - Returning to old places, meeting old friends, reminiscing about the past. It may not be for every group, but nostalgia is certainly a fitting theme.
    Hope- The dead may become living, the end can be survived, names can be taken, asses kicked, those still alive can scream it to the world. The new world provides hope for many vampires.
    Fatalism- There are things that are inevitable. Unavoidable. Shit will burn. Friends grow apart. Things fall apart. It cannot be stopped. At best you can choose how you deal with the realization.

    Rumours
    While the overall details from the final night is unknown, there are some rumors that keep popping up.
    It is rumored that Caine himself walked the earth, that he fed on his children, and ultimately, that he choose(and was granted) final death in lie of devouring all his kin.
    It is rumored that an antediluvian has survived, though which one differs greatly from tale to tale.
    It is rumored that by eating the caitiff one can keep ones powers.
    It is rumored that by eating your childes one can keep ones blood potent.
    It is known that the antediluvians rose up, and that several of them were killed in titanic battles.

    A masquerade ending:
    In the wake of the end of the world, a bandage has been ripped from the eyes of humanity. Most mortals now know there are things out there, in the night, though they may not know the details. More than that, the ubiquitousness of cameras and information dispersal in the modern age is making the Masquerade increasingly difficult to maintain. Exactly the extent to which the Masquerade has been breached is up to the Storyteller. Here follows some suggestion.

    * A masque cracked.
    The masquerade is failing, albeit slowly. Many mortals know there are unnatural things in the night, though most pretend like nothing. Millennial of encouraging a world-view of what constitutes a normal world will not be overturned overnight. Most mortals know instinctively that speaking to loud of that one thing they saw is... unhealthy. The fallout of The End has been excused as natural disasters, terrorist attacks, and a crisis in the stability of the world. Even if mortals know there are supernatural things out there, they are unlikely to know what these things are, or for that matter know that vampires specifically exist. It is still only a matter of time until the masquerade falls apart. This is the default assumption of this fan made supplement.

    * A masque broken
    The masquerade has failed. An antediluvian did not just raise a few buildings in downtown Manhattan in some freak earthquake, there were tentacles the size of skyscrapers going on. A bunch of vampires in northern Europe fought and ate a monster on live television. Werewolves tore into what looked like Men in Black shooting bolts of plasma downtown. Someone saw a ghost. Worms the size of buses burst from underground to feed on people. Several cities and at least one mountain are gone. People know and are talking about the supernatural. Everyone wants more information. Many people are panicking. Supernatural factions all across the board are racing to do what damage control can be done. This is a good choice if you want a chronicle in the immediate aftermath of The End, possible centered around staving off a new Burning Times.

    * The party goes ever on and on
    The masquerade never broke. Millennial of carefully maintained secrecy and manipulation of society has engineered a robust veil of secrets. People in the old World of Darkness do not know and do not want to know what lurks in the night. They have enough of their own shit to deal with. Cameras on smart phones? Those don't exist. The Internet being a free repository of information? All major sites(facebook,. Youtube, google, etc) are controlled by shadowy cabals with a vested interest in keeping things hidden. The damage of The End, well what end? There were some earthquakes and a bunch of coordinated terrorist attacks sure, but well, the world is still there isn't it? Nothing has changed... has it?

    Part 2: The New World

    Nor, in thy marble vault, shall sound
    My echoing song; then worms shall try
    That long-preserved virginity,
    And your quaint honour turn to dust,
    And into ashes all my lust;
    The grave’s a fine and private place,

    The New World – Conflicts and Pains
    The World of Darkness ended in a very real, if not quite literal, sense. None feel this keener than the Kindred. The End has changed the vampiric condition, perhaps irrevocable. More than that, the old sects, pillars of the societies and culture of the walking dead have been dealt a massive blow. The Kindred as a species suffers a real danger of extinction. As a new world dawns, the Kindred is slowly dying. Only time will tell what will remain, and how they will deal.

    What follows are some information on the new issues to deal with in the World of Dawn.

    During the end:
    It is difficult for the kindred to know exactly what went down during Gehenna. At the time, most were concerned with their own survival, or going out in a blaze of glory. No one were everywhere and no one had any real overview of the course of events. Cataloging and figuring out who did what, and just... what happened has become an obsession of many kindred trying to deal with lost friends or the new world. What is known, is the fallout.

    Not Wholly Damned
    Something has happened to the curse that binds all Kindred together. It has resulted in a weakening of the blood. This has had several effects.

    Thin bloods. The creation of thin blood has increased. No vampire seems to be safe, regardless of generation. The chance of creating a thin blood differs greatly between individual vampires, though none can be certain they are exempt, and generation still seems to play a role.

    Here follows some suggestions for the risk of creating a thin blood.
    Generation >7 20%.
    Generation 7-11 50%.
    Generation 12-13 70%.
    Generation 14 100%.
    A caitiff with a vampiric master fail to embrace a mortal 50% of the time. A caitiff with a caitiff master fail to embrace a mortal 90% of the time. The result is just a corpse.

    These are only guidelines, a storyteller is free to modify them as he sees fit for individual vampires.

    Ghouls – In several recorded instances, the blood bond of a ghoul has weakened significantly. In some, it may have disappeared entirely.

    Echoes of a thing since long forgotten – Some kindred have felt pangs of emotion or sensations they have not felt since their mortal days. True love, the taste of food, the fear of someone alive.

    Death – Some Kindred just... died. Whatever animating force kept them moving left, leaving only the corpses they were behind.

    The Stepchildren
    With the weakening curse and the many kindred who suffered final death at the end, the clans has found themselves forced to accept Caitiff into their ranks to a far greater extent than before, an odious and distasteful state of affair for many kindred. A policy of 'adopting' caitiff as clan members proper are in effect with most clans.

    Friends Forged in Fire.
    A new phenomena has arisen after The End. During the end, everyone fought. Everyone. Vampires and Werewolves and Ghosts and Mages and Changelings and stranger things still. While no larger organization may have joined rank with any others, on the front lines, you took what allies you could. This has resulted in a new tendency of certain vampires to cooperate more readily with other entities than was the case prior to the end. While this tendency is in a minority, it does exist. Many older vampires warn against or actively fight this trend.

    On a higher level, the Camarilla has set a precedent of taking some, exceedingly cautious, diplomatic contacts with other supernatural factions. This has been due to simple necessity. Directly after the end some limited cooperation had to be allowed to conduct damage control, define new territorial boundaries, and as quickly as possible return to something akin to the old status quo. No further inroads have been made by the Camarilla since then, and the whole situation feels fairly uncomfortable for them.
    Storyteller note: The splats (the different game lines all set in the world of darkness) are not really designed for cross overs. Attempting to cross over different splats with each other can easily result in unbalanced game play or odd effects. Not only mechanics may differ, but you may find yourself facing heavy thematic clashes, where the themes of one splat just doesn't fit at all into the themes of another, or where one factor makes another irrelevant due to odd rules interactions. As such the possibility of crossovers are only included here as an advanced option for storytellers that wish to try out such a thing. You should feel free to ignore it.
    A Lack of Control
    The new world has found the Kindred lacking somewhat in its capacity to exert their usual control on certain aspects of mortal society. It is not just the end itself, albeit the massive losses sustained certainly did not help. Rather it is a combination of factors, including not just lost operatives and infrastructure, but also the weakening of their damnation, the increasing difficulty in keeping the masquerade maintained, the internal struggle within the various sects, and the inevitable sense not even the most ancient elder can deny of living in a new age. The end result is that the Camarilla, nor the rest of the Kindred, cannot exert quite as much control of mortal society as was once the norm. Other supernatural factions are suffering trough similar problems.

    This is not to say that Kindred society is completely helpless. They can still exert a lot of influence, as can many other supernatural factions. It has merely gotten more difficult.

    Part 3 Of Sects and Clans

    The Sects

    The Sabbat
    The Sabbat was hit hard by Gehenna. In the end, the sect survived but at a terrible cost. The Sabbat has sustained heavy losses. This is not their greatest problem. The remains of the Sabbat is suffering trough a civil war. The pawns have turned on the masters. Gehenna was everything the Sabbat feared and fought against. During the Final Night, the rank and file of the Sabbat were... disappointed in their elders. Certain details came to light that has caused rebellion to spread like wild fire trough the sect, helped by the need of many Sabbat members to find a new great enemy to fight. The conflict is mostly directed from younger Sabbat packs towards their elders, and from elders towards their young. The Camarilla has so far kept outside this conflict, busy rebuilding their own organization, content in letting the Sabbat tear into itself. .

    The Camarilla
    The Camarilla has found itself lacking manpower. Many positions filled for centuries now stand empty. The sect find itself hampered by internal strife. While no civil war wracks the Camarilla, the sect is paralyzed by endless debate on every little issue. Some argue about the Sabbat, believing that the time to strike against their hated foe is now. Others reason that providing the Sabbat with an external enemy only risks uniting them in the face of a common threat, and that the Camarilla is simply to wounded to take such a risk at this moment. On the Masquerade most believe it can still be upheld, while others are desperately laying the ground work to minimize the damage once it falls apart. Some believe the kindred are dying, and drastic measures are required to ensure their survival, others think there's not much that can be done. There is also the jockeying for position and petty politics that has arisen as power vacuums have arisen all across the sect with so many vampires dead.

    The Anarchs
    The Anarchs, like all other factions, suffered heavy losses during the end. The aftermath has been kinder to them than most. The Anarchs have received some defectors from the Sabbat. They have also been slightly more willing to accept and integrate Caitiff into their ranks. With many princes dead, and other territories open, the anarchs have been able to make inroads into several cities. There is a concerted effort within the sect to mature into a real alternative to the Camarilla and the Sabbath, to deal with this new world as it arrives. Right as their kind seems doomed to extinction, the Anarch sect might fulfill the promise they have so long struggled for. Or fail in every way predicted.

    The Inconnu – Many of the Inconnu is no longer sure what to do. The end came. The final night. The ancients of old rose up to devour and feast, just as foretold.

    And the world did not die.

    There is an uncertainty among many of the Inconnu on the future. Certainly while the cainite condition is weakening, there should be many decades, even centuries before vampires as a whole is lost from the world. What should they strive for in the coming years?

    The Caitiff – The Caitiff have become a necessity in the new world. To many lost, and the thin bloods are to common. Most clans have accepted a policy of adopting Caitiff into their ranks. When possible, these are selected with even more exacting standard than normal childes, either for wisdom or for pliancy. These caitiff tend to be folded into the clan proper, though they can look forward to being held to much stricter standards than normal Kindred. Those who fail to measure up are killed or abandoned, often with their memories altered.

    The Clans

    Assamite
    The Assamites, what is left of them, is currently undergoing something of a religious crisis. While the details are unknown to most outsiders, the clan seems to have splintered into several smaller cults, arguing amongst each other. This is not to say the clan is actively fighting itself. So far the arguments seem to have been limited to mostly yelling at each other and the occasional murder. Those few who have dug into the issue has heard only one thing. 'The Old Man is Gone'.

    So far the bickering has been kept to just that, and the Assamite clan has quickly united when any external threat has presented itself, if only long enough to make it go away. Apart from their internal issues they seem to be sabotaging Tremere attempts at rebuilding the Tremere clan.

    Brujah
    The Brujah fought and died on the frontlines more so than any other clan. As a result, almost all of them are gone. Attempts at rebuilding the clan has been helped by their acceptance of Caitiff into the ranks. While they were not the first to suggest the policy, they quickly took to it and put it in practice. With the clan stretched so thin, individual Brujah members enjoy more freedom than before. Unfortunately this has also resulted in difficulty pushing their own interests within the Camarilla and the Anarch sects. Especially with the Anarchs, the Brujah has lost a lot of influence. Most Brujah that held some prestige within the sect have died. As many of their current members are young and inexperienced the clan has started getting a bad reputation.

    A subset of the clan has sided with the argument that the masquerade will inevitable end and that it'd be better to end it on the terms of the kindred than have it fall apart on its own. This to has not helped their popularity.

    For their actions during the end at least, they are respected.

    Followers of Set

    The new world is certainly no less immune to corruption and decay than the old is, and the followers of set is ready to serve. Far from the apocalypse causing them to question their beliefs, it seems it has only caused the Followers of Set to be strengthened in their convictions. Now more than ever it seems, is the time to ensure the slow decay of this new world.

    Gangrel

    Right before the end the Gangrel left the Camarilla and struck out as an independent clan. They weathered the end better than most, with many of the clans member hiding in the wilds of the world, though many also raged and fought. The clan have good contact with many members of the Camarilla, and after the end still often cooperate with the sect. One thing that hinders the clan in the new world is the Werewolves. They seem to have started to hunt and target Gangrel specifically, sometimes even letting other prey go, should they sell out the outlanders. As if clan Gangrel has committed some grand slight towards the skin walkers. What this slight would be, most Gangrel members do not seem to know, and the skin walkers are certainly not telling.

    Giovanni
    The Giovanni are having problems. Something has happened to the underworld, and whatever the true case is, it has affected their necromantic arts. While the clan tries to keep quiet about it, for the last thirteen years, they have only had access to ghosts already bound to their service, or those they can trap at the moment of their deaths. Their situation is not helped by many of their elders being dead, saddling younger necromancers with ghosts they may not be quite powerful enough to handle.

    Lasombra
    The Lasombra is currently engaged in the Sabbat civil war. Not all Lasombra is on one side or the other. While most elders are fending off their erstwhile pawns, many opportunistic Lasombra has joined with the insurgents. In a very real sense the clan is at war with itself. While several members of the clan has taken on a policy of using Caitiff recruits, mostly as cannon fodder, many others refuse to lower themselves to such standards, preferring instead to devour any failed embrace.

    Malkavians
    The Malkavians seem to be playing around with the Masquerade. Mostly they seem to be trying to keep it up. It is uncertain. Quite a few times during the past years a Malkavian have created some larger Masquerade breach, but in almost all cases the breach has ended up either spreading massive amounts off misinformation on the vampiric condition (including one incident ending up seemingly proving the existence of aliens), or discrediting future masquerade breaches. It also has led to increased hunter activity targeting vampires.

    The clan still seems to be suffering from insanity, and due to the difficulty in discerning any real focus or pattern in their activites, the Camarilla has been unable to do much but execute a few individuals. Certainly the other clans are far from trusting of the Malkavians, and the clan is under intense scrutiny.

    Adopted Malkavian Caitiff has an interesting relationship with their would be clan, as they do not suffer of the weakness of insanity that plague the Malkavians. How they are actually treated wary widely from case to case. Many Malkavians try to embrace the already mentally ill, some ensure their Caitiff become 'Real Malkavians' after the fact, some relish in having someone a bit reliable to rely on, some play around with the sane people, some throw them out on the streets.

    Nosferatu
    The Nosferatu believe their Antediluvian to be dead. They seem quite happy about this fact, even as their blood fails them. Aside from this, not much has changed for the sewer rats. Whatever ails the blood of Caine that has given rise to the increase in Caitiff, it does not seem to have weakened the disfigurements that all Nosferatu suffer from. The clan conducts business as usual, trying to raise as little notice as possible. Trying to lick their wounds. Not quite as easy a task as they would like. The truth is, the Nosferatu is severely understaffed for all the things they would like to do. Keep tab on the Sabbath civil war, keep tabs on the Camarilla, keep tab on everyone and everything, try to rebuild their nests, try to figure out what will happen in the future. This is not helped by the controversy of adopted Caitiff. While the Nosferatu has accepted Caitiff into their ranks, many Nosferatu despise their thin-blooded brethren, whose curse is lighter than theirs. Causes of abuse is not uncommon.

    Like many others, the Nosferatu can see Kindred society starting to fray at the edges. They are not sure what to do about it.

    Toreador
    The chef interest of the Toreador is the weakening off the vampiric condition. Reactions among individual members range from a kind off manic glee in reliving mortal passions, to a kind of slow panic of the prospect of their ultimate extinction, to a deep seated worry on the current and future stability of their society. As a whole however, the clan is struggling with their failing damnation. Some wish to find a way to stave off the current curse of events, others merely wish not to be forgotten when things finally run their course.

    Tremere

    The Tremere barely survived the end. The clan is still in disarray, for reasons unknown to most outsiders. Many clan members seem disconnected from the rest of their clan, their once tight organizational structure cut apart by losses sustained. This is not all. A new curse has fallen on the Tremere. They are unable to blood bond others. Their blood simply taste disgusting to others, and those bonds there are are fading. While the details are unknown, they seem to be blaming the Assamites on this course of events.

    Many Tremere have become nearly independent from their clan, keeping small cults of their own, or devoting themselves to research. Some are still trying to keep the clan together, with great difficulty. There is a concerted effort ongoing to reconnect and rebuild the clan, though this is stymied by Assamite interference.

    Storyteller note: (The normal weakness of the Tremere has been replaced. They can no longer form blood bonds. Bonds made before the end can be kept up, but only partially, and even these are weaker than they should be. In addition, their blood tastes disgusting, and anyone attempting to drink it must pass a willpower check or retch it back up again. A small consolation against hypothetical diablerists).
    Tzimisce
    The Tzimisce stand united against the petulant rebellion of their servants and would be allies. They categorically refuse accepting the likes of Caitiff into their ranks. Rather they have engineered an entirely new flesh crafted design, using Caitiff flesh. They quite enjoy releasing this predatory horror against their traitorous brethren, as it craves vampire blood. While there are few Tzimisce remaining, those that are are certainly formidable, be they old or young.

    Ventrue

    The Blue Bloods are suffering trough annoyance, and are currently busying themselves with saving the Camarilla. While the losses during the end was great, and many critical positions within the sect were slaughtered and devoured or otherwise put to final death, the remaining Ventrue argue that this at least provide some space for capable individuals to advance. While the weakening of the cainite curse certainly is undeniable, the Ventrue argue that's no reason to let proper decorum slip. Why by careful management, it could be centuries until such a thing becomes a real problem. They are hindered in their designs by many factors. Due to the losses sustained during the end, many clans and individual vampires have wanted to embrace mortal kin, leading to a surplus of thin bloods. Younger vampires are also more likely to want to embrace companions of their own, making the issue even more problematic. In an attempt to 'move with the times' it was the Ventrue that first brought up the suggestion of adopting Caitiff into the clans proper. This policy has resulted in most Ventrue being pickier than ever before in who they choose to embrace, reasoning that in case the result will get them stuck with a caitiff they might as well make it as able as it can be.

    More critical, to the Ventrue at least, is the failing masquerade, and they are one of the clans working hardest to maintain what can be maintained.

    Ravnos
    There are less than a hundred Ravnos remaining in existence. Many of these are dispersed across other groups. There are slightly more Ravnos within the Sabbat antribitu, but even there the clan cannot be said to exist as an organized entity anymore. There are a few holdouts comprised of a dozen or more Ravnos, but these are fairly isolated and difficult to reach. Ultimately each Ravnos is on her or his own, forced to rely on what allies and resources his own wits can bring.

    Part 4: Miscellanea

    New Merits and Flaws

    The beauty... (Merit 5)

    The curse of the vampire has weakened significantly. She may be outside in the sunlight. The vampire takes only bashing damage in full sunlight (going to lethal and then aggravated once the wound track is filled up). She may be outside during overcast weather or while keeping in shadows and take no damage. However, her paleness, cold and lack of breathing all stand out a lot more in the sun. All attempts to discover her true nature or there just being something wrong with her is at 2 less difficulty.

    Renegade Ghoul(Flaw 1-5)

    One of the characters ghouls has had his blood bond dissolve. Perhaps it happened over night, perhaps the ghoul managed to keep it hidden. He has in any case become immune to the blood bond from that vampire, and abandoned the character. How bad this situation is depends on the rating.

    A flaw of 1 means the ghoul has little interest or capacity for revenge, and the vampire mostly risk a loss of reputation and face if this got out. At a rating of 2-3 the ghoul wants the vampire to hurt, and has some capacity to do so. Perhaps they have some allies, or they just know sensitive personal information on the vampire that could be dangerous. At a rating of 4-5 the ghoul should be a significant threat. Perhaps he's a very wealthy industrial magnate, or a very capable street level ganger. If the character also has the flaw dark secret, perhaps the ghoul knows of this. It is up to the player and the storyteller to hash out the details.

    Weak Blood (Flaw 3)

    The blood bond of the vampire is weak, and has a tendency to fail. The vampire must spend some effort in keeping the blood bond going.

    Ghouls of the character have some more independence than is normal for ghouls. They have an easier time leaving the character, questioning him, refusing orders, or betraying him than is normal. If this got out, the character is likely to get a worse reputation among other Kindred who might get scared he'll somehow be contagious. He might get refused the right to embrace, with the reasoning it will most likely only result in Thin Bloods.

    Mechanically, the blood bond of a ghoul always counts as one step less than it actually is. Any attempts the ghoul makes to break or go against the bond is also at -1 in difficulty.

    Tremere cannot take this flaw, unless the storyteller prefer to keep their original weakness.

    Chronicle and Session Seeds

    A mask slowly taken off – The players are a group of vampires with a particular interest in the masquerade. The details of this interest should be hashed out between the players and the storyteller. In The World of Dawn, many vampires believe in the inevitable decline of their kind, and of the equally inevitable fall of the Masquerade. These vampires often band together to try and preempt this fall, preparing things so that when the mask finally falls away, and the world sees them as they are, they will have some reason not to burn the kindred from the face of the earth. Such vampires often attempt to do good deeds, or at least set things up so they appear to be doing good deeds.

    Conversely the players could play a more traditional group, tasked with ensuring the masquerade is kept up. While such a group might be locked to a city, they might also have reason to travel around to various crisis areas. The players could do anything from spreading misinformation, to taking control of media, to guns blazing clean up troublesome canite masquerade breakers.

    As another alternative you could have a particularly nihilistic or crazy group of kindred attempt to break the Masquerade deliberately. Such a group would no doubt be hunted, not just by vampires, but by every supernatural faction, and would be unlikely to survive for long. (This could be a fun thing in itself).

    You could also elect for a broken Masquerade, with the supernatural known. The players are tasked with public relations, trying to downplay the horror of kindred life, and play up the romance. The vampires then attempt a new masquerade, keeping the darker aspects of the undead hidden, while showing off the sexy glamor people often associate with vampires.

    Caitiff – The players are in charge of a group of neonates characters. Most of these neonates are caitiff.     While the design varies there are some commonalities within the Igazi. They are a vampiric creature, abomination though they may be, and so they still crave blood. They have been infused with a terrible blood lust for other vampires. Each Igazi crave the blood corresponding to the clans the caitiff used in its construction originally hailed from. While they will feed on other vampires, they will always prefer the blood of those clans. Ironically, this makes them somewhat safe to common mortals whom they avoid feeding on unless starving.

    Aside from their bloodlust, each Igazi have a number of common special powers.

    * Each Igazi can recognize a member of a clan they wish to eat using smell alone (Succeeding on a Perception + Alertness check of difficulty 7).
    * If a vampire attempts to feed on an Igazi, they are subjected to a dizzying sensation, not unlike the Kiss a mortal feels as a vampire feeds on it. Each vampire feeding from, or being fed on by an Igazi must clear a self control check or be paralyzed in pure bliss. The Igazi Emberi itself is resistant to the bliss of being fed on, and may conduct a self control check to being able to act as normal.

    * The Igazi is incapable of diablerie. They have only seven blood points, though they can still drain a vampire dry. The extraneous blood is simply wasted.
    * Igazi are not subject to Rötschreck or Frenzy, aside from when they see vampiric blood while not feeding on it, in which case they may Frenzy at the sight of it. Otherwise, the beast within it seem dead. It is claimed some Igazi have willingly crawled trough fire, as if it was not there, to feed on their victim even as they burned.

      Almost thirteen years ago now, the world ended.

      Yes, yes, we all know it's still out there, with sunshine and grocery stories and... small animals. You'll never quite get it unless you were there.

      It was a horrid battle. Traditions and Technocracy fought, against the Nephandi, against the Marauders, against the Vampires, and the Werewolves, and the Ghosts of the underworld, and the Umbrood of the pit, and against each other, and worse things still. The world bled.

      A New World

      Old Enemies - Our conflict with the Technocracy has slowed down, as both our organizations have been trying to rebuild. Even now, we are more evenly matched than either of our groups are... comfortable with. An uneasy stalemate has coalesced over the decade, often broken by intermittent skirmishes. The danger of a full scale Ascension War still looms.

      Calm seas - The avatar storm has splintered and dissipated. Intermittent manifestations still occur, shutting of access to the umbra for people in its general area and shredding particularly powerful avatars into pieces. But it is possible to reach the Umbra from earth.

      New People - At the end of the world, mass awakenings occurred at several places on earth, and both the traditions and the technocracy has raced to pick up these strays before they go marauder or worse. While the same type of mass awakenings has not happened since, the ratio of annual awakenings is larger than at any point in the previous century. Whether this trend will continue only time can tell. Currently there has come a large influx of new recruits, of all ages and dispositions. The Traditions have allocated more resources to training mages. It is not uncommon for a master to have several awakened disciples and new chantries have been funded solely dedicated to teaching new mages. The Order of Hermes especially has spent a lot of resources on this. While the influx of new mages is not extreme, it is still to this day unusually high.

      Maps Redrawn - The Tellurian network of nodes and leylines have shifted, seemingly permanently. Many formerly sacred sites are now as mundane as rock and dirt, while locations prior steeped in the mundane are now infused with power. Much work is spent trying to find powerful new sites, racing to get in control of them before opposing factions or even allies can get there in time. These alterations has not been limited to just the end, but on several occasion in the years after, nodes have either coalesced at new places of powers, or dried up completely.

      Horizon ascendant - The nodes have become integral in the reconstruction of the Horizon realms. An ongoing effort to reconnect, restore and rebuild the legendary Horizon realms by hunting down presumable stable nodes and claiming them for oneself, or trying to redraw global leylines. Not only nodes are required, but high magic, and lots of grunt work within the realms themselves, as many of the realms were left devastated and needs to be cleared out.

      Storyteller Note on Horizon Realms:
      In the original Mage the Ascenscion the Horizon realms were amazing otherworldly realms where the Traditions and Technocracy kept settlements of their own, from science fiction like space stations, to an idyllic countrysides, to bustling waystations. Within the Mage metaplot, these were cut off from earth and ruined during the Avatar Storm event. While the exact course of events is up to each storyteller, here follows some suggestions.

      * Not as bad as it seemed
      While the avatar storm occurred, and access to the umbra was cut off, the Horizon Realms where not totally devastated. Not all masters died, becoming an umbrood was not as certain as depicted in the original books, and not all umbrood necessarily became completely delusional. The avatar storm started to weaken some time before the end, and by the current timeline contact with the Horizon Realm has been reestablished. Much of the realms have weakened and been devastated, but they are not in ruins, they can be rebuilt. This is the default assumption of this fan supplement.

      * In Ruin
      Events occurred more or less in line with the metaplot. Most of the Horizon realms were cut off, most of the masters are dead, it is left to those of the new world to reconnect with the Horizon realms, and rebuild or scavenge among them.

      * Pristine
      The Horizon Realms where never cut off. Most masters are alive, and their only real problem is that many of their old connections to Tellurian nodes and quintessence have weakened or dried up, resulting in the need to find new nodes.
      All I know is I know nothing - Many sleepers have become aware of the larger world around them. Many are, if not actively investigating, at least keeping one eye open. Whether this is a good or bad thing is up for debate. Paradox still plague us, and the Technocracy is still there for any mage who gets to overt.

      This is a new Age in the world. Some claim the world has been made lesser, others that this is a new beginning.

Whether that is breaking the cutting edge of genetic research, or simply having an easier time diverting more funds to their more eccentric projects without being noticed, they are doing what they can to ensure their projects are realized or provided time and resources. Our intelligence tells us the Convention has grown rather cut throat, internally. Externally we know they have gone trough some joint projects with Iteration X, whose trans humanist philosophies mesh well with current breakthroughs in the field of human genetic engineering.

      Iteration X
      Iteration X seem to be cut off from their main base of operations, or at least this is the impression we get from them. Even with the avatar storm dissipated, with the leylines rearranged and so many things thrown into disorder it is not too surprising. They have been spending significant resources trying to reconnect with this off world construct. As far as I know they have met with about as much success as the Euthanatoi has in their jaunts to the underworld. Aside from that they are putting a lot of work into rebuilding other Constructs both on and off world. They also appear to have released a new Hitmark version. It seems less like a heavy hitter and something more intended for mass production, though it remains quite dangerous.

      As mentioned earlier they are cooperating with the Progenitors within the field of genetic engineering on the human body. It's not surprising. Genetic modifications appears more coincidental than overt cybernetics. Don't get me wrong. They still use plasma guns. They're iteration X, of course they do.

      Moving on.

      The Void Engineers
      And then there's the Void Engineers.

      They were almost completely wiped out by the end. While the exact course of events are interesting, this is not the time for it. Suffice to say, very few Void Engineers remain. Most of the remnants have defected from the Technocratic Union and joined us.

      Not something we saw coming.

      Because there are to few left to form a proper Tradition, (and I believe due to political reasons) the remnants have for now been folded under the Virtual Adepts, as a part of that Tradition. On the technocratic side, much of the responsibility (and infrastructure, Constructs and technologies) of the Void Engineers have been handed over to the Iteration X, along with a handful of Void Engineers that remained with the union, and has been melded into a subsection of that convention. There is an ongoing problem with trust and differences of perspectives between the Void Engineers and the rest of us. While accepting the defectors of the Technocracy is certainly not without precedence, both the Sons of Ether and Virtual Adepts came to us at a time we were desperate for allies, and brought with them powerful masters and resources. Conversely the Void Engineers arrived at a time where, while we certainly could use the manpower, we also needed space and time to rebuild, and they themselves where almost crushed.

      I am sorry to say we probably could lend them more support than we currently are.

      It is up in the air what will ultimately happen to the Void Engineers. Perhaps they will fade away, become more integrated into the Virtual Adepts, defect from us as well and become an independent organization, or, fairly unlikely but still a possibility, rebuild and join us as a full tradition. Time will tell.

      Storyteller Note:

      The above is a resolution of a metaplot point first brought up within the Void Engineer convention book. There, the Void Engineers grow estranged from the rest of the conventions, who start to distrust and marginalize them. The Void Engineers leaving the Union was a possible conclusion to that plot line. This is wholly optional. While it is brought up here as a default, the Storyteller has several alternatives to what can have happened:

      * The Void Engineers remained with the Union. They are assisting with the reconstruction of of world infrastructure, and with protecting the solar system versus umbral invasions.
      * The Void Engineers left the Union without joining the Traditions, now working as an independent group.
      * The Void Engineers joined the traditions, but were not as devastated by the end as portrayed here. Through some high level politics they became the tenth Tradition.
      * Something completely different occurred.


      Storyteller Note

      It is important to note that there are several variations on the Technocracy.

      This was not uncommon for the old White Wolf roleplaying games, not just in Mage the Ascension, but also in Vampire the Masquerade, Exalted, and others. These games often allowed for multiple takes on various factions within the game, both as a result of a game line developing over time, adding more details and content as time goes by, but also by intent to allow groups to make their own 'canon' that fits their game the best.Roughly, the most common interpretations of the Technocracy can be summarized as follows:

      * Dark: The Technocracy is a cold, faceless, monolithic organization, bent on total control of humanity. Extremist authoritarians, they desire nothing less than the death of magic, progress and dreams, leaving humanity as a stagnant species, never changing, improving or dying, with them in total control. Dystopic stories such as 1984, 414 Fahrenheit or Brazil are all good inspiration for this kind of Technocracy. This is close to the original take on the Technocracy.
      * Dark Grey: The Technocracy is cold and ruthless, but they ascribe to an ideology that makes some degree of sense. Officially they strive to protect and improve humanity. While the higher ups in the Technocracy are corrupt, lower ranks may sincerely believe in their cause. This is the most common intepretation of the Technocracy, though exactly how dark they are portrayed can vary. See light grey.
      * Light Grey: As above but even more benevolent. Even some of the higher up may be genuinely idealistic, clashing with the Traditions more due to ideological differences, than because of self serving or hypocritical reasons. A light grey Technocracy may also downplay their ruthlessness, trying to play them more as coldly pragmatical. In the Dark Grey and Light Grey versions, the Traditions and Technocracy are often portrayed as respected enemies, someone that while you might fight and try to kill most of the time, if the situation truly calls for it, you may put your arms aside and cooperate with temporarily. Or at least try to exploit each other rather than outright kill.
      * Light: In this view, the Technocracy are genuine good guys fighting to protect humanity from all manners of existential threats and inhuman monsters. With this version, the Traditions are often portrayed as unambiguous bad guys to provide some balance and a clearcut villain to fight.

      This fan made supplement assumes either a Dark Grey or Light Grey Technocracy as a default. Of course, nothing stops the Technocracy from being portrayed different. Perhaps they have only been licking their wounds for the last decade, preparing for a new Progrom. Or perhaps they are throwing as much as they can spare behind improving the lot of humanity.

      Part 4: Of Madmen and Villains
      From what we can tell, the Nephandi were devastated by the end. Plots they had been stewing on for years or decades, possibly centuries, finally came to fruition. The fallen threw everything they could into the end of the world.

      We killed them.

      It was not easy.

      We lost a lot of good people beating them back.

      Beneath a building in Copenhagen, there is still the remnants of an umbral labyrinth present.

      The entire thing is a localized avatar storm. It is only the smallest fraction of the wounds our reality sustained during that part of the conflict.

      ...I will speak no more of this. Suffice to say, any remaining Nephandi are either very powerful, very skilled, or very desperate.

      Conversely the Marauders have had an upswing, if you can call it that. As the ratio of awakenings have increased, so has the number of Marauders. Though I have heard claims that even accounting for the increased awakenings, Marauder activity is unusually high. I cannot say. Demographical analysis is always tricky with these things, even when assisted by magick. Whatever the case may be they remain a threat.

      Part 5: Themes

      Soldering On
      Before the end there was the dream of global ascension. A future where humanity, as a whole, ascended. The exact form of this dream differed dependent on whom you asked. For some, it was a global awakening. For others, the realization of some reality changing project. Regardless, the end came and this did not occur. It is a theme then within the World of Dawn, that there are no catchall solutions. No grand scheme that will solve everything in one fell swoop. Rather the road to any form of global Ascension, will be a long and ardous one, quite probably stretching on for centuries or more. A game focused on this theme would have a focus on difficult solutions to difficult problems, or problems that have no real solution, save for careful management and hard work over time. Slice of life campaigns, or highly political campaigns could both be a good fit for this theme. Any campaign where the players work at a problem that will take years or more, such as the rebuilding of the new horizon realms, or the slowly shifting global consensus could also work.

      New Blood – While the exact ratio of annual awakenings is up to the storyteller, within The World of Dawn these have increased. Coupled with the losses sustained, there is a lot of new people within the Traditions (and the Technocracy). This can function as a source of themes. One could center a campaign around themes such as inexperience, dreams, expectations, discord as new meets old, or even something as simple as possibility. Character focused campaigns might be a good fit for these themes. It is worth to note that there are both spontaneous awakenings, from people just living their lives in the world, and awakenings within the traditions themselves, as particularly gifted acolytes or potentials manages to awaken.

      New and Old Conflicts - The new age is awash in the old, given new form. Campaigns using this as a source of themes should play this up. A focus on ascension war veterans, a possible new full scale conflict between the traditions and the technocracy, nostalgia, feeling out of place, or a new start could all work very well. Action packed campaigns might fit this theme very well, as could campaigns focused on more immediate problems that needs to be resolved fast.

      Campaign Seeds

      Prospecting
      The players are in charge of finding and securing nodes, preferable major ones. They need to travel the world, investigate possible sites, hide or protect them from the technocracy or other conflicting parties, or otherwise ensure they do not end up in the wrong hands. This kind of campaign are rife for possible adventures. A node may be tainted by nephandic influence, or already claimed by werewolves. The player characters may find a node at the same time as a technocrat amalgam, with both groups ending up fighting to get in control of it. A node may be in a very public place, or conversely a very remote and hard to get to place. Players may need to spend some work on not just finding the node and calling in reinforcements, but actually going trough evaluating nodes, try to guess at their stability, estimate their strengths and difficulty in keeping hidden or secured, and provide the rest of traditions recommendations and reports on their efforts. This provides a source for though choices. If you find a major node you could not possible keep, would it be right to destroy it? It is certainly something the technocracy tend to do.

      School Life
      The players are teachers on a chantry dedicated to teaching those recently awakened. Each player has their own student (some may have more than one) whom they are responsible for. Adventures may focus on specific things the players need to teach the students, not just in using their spheres, but also in good sense, how to deal with the technocracy, or higher academical studies such as theories on the consensus reality, avatar, avatar storm, the umbral realms, tradition history and organization, tradition law, or even mundane things such as history, ancient languages, scientific theories, how to handle fear, or similar.

      In this kind of campaign, you can even have the odd adventure when the players send out their students on a mission, and then take over and play as the students.
      Alternatively the player characters are the students of a chantry focused on educating those recently awakened. This is a good campaign for slice of life adventures. The campaign could focus on the players slowly developing their own paradigm, or finding their place within the traditions. Just like above, the characters may end up conducting missions on their teachers behalves (or in spite of their warnings and requests not to).

      High and Low
      An organization composed of lower ranking tradition members have started to gain some traction. Working over tradition boundaries, they are hoping to bring about certain reforms within the traditions, and work as a counterweight to more established individuals. The player characters are either people behind the movement, working to ensure its goals, or outside it, investigating whether they have reasonable points, or are disorganized and more likely to do more harm than good. The truth of the organization could be anything from genuinely idealistic to that of a nephandic plot to sow discord and chaos.

      Rebuild and Grow
      The player characters are masters working with the rebuilding efforts of the old horizon realms.

      Astronauts
      The player characters are playing as technocrats in charge of an off world construct. A small space station within our asteroid field. Originally a Void Engineer construct, abandoned before the end, when the avatar storm still raged, they need to bring the station back into working order, clear it out, and keep it maintained. They are joined by one of the few remaining Void Engineer loyalists. They'll have to deal with extra dimensional threats, dimensional anomalies, the malfunctions on the station, coffee breaks, research, maintaining communication and supply lines with the rest of the Union, and possible etherite activity in the vicinity.

      Adventure Seeds
      A hidden home belonging to a long since dead master has been found. The player characters need to investigate the place, either to loot it or to hinder it being looted by others.
      A player character who is a member or have contacts within the celestial chorus is contacted by a mysterious individual who wishes to join. That individual is a vampire.
      The traditions believe they have found a lead to one of the few remaining Nephandi, and intend to go out in force to confront him. The players are asked to join.
      A new person has awakened, and the technocracy are already en route. The players are closest, and are asked to bring him in before he is taken away by the technocrats.

                  --- Part 6: The Living City

                  This is a fanmade setting detail that can be used in any Mage the Ascension campaign. While it is written here as part of the World of Dawn fan made supplement, it is not tied specifically to that setting.

                  The Living City - Introduction
                  While it has no known name, it is usually referred to as The Living City, or Paradise. The Living City because it moves across the Tellurian seemingly at random. And Paradise, because the Marauders find it to be so.

                  Its origins are unknown, but rumors and reports of it stretch as far back as right after the end.

                  It takes the form of a city though its exact architecture and layout is unknown and vary dependent on where and when one visits it. While it most commonly appears within the penumbra, or sometimes inside realms within the middle umbra, this is not a certain thing. It has been known to visit Boston, London, Moscow, the Macedonian Desert, and, according to at least one Order of Hermes initiate, the bottom of the Atlantic Ocean (he claims to have been inside the city at the time). To this day, no real pattern has been discerned in its movements. It commonly shows up at the sites of larger cities, but sometimes it does not. When it visits a city, the gauntlet grows slightly thinner. Portals to and from it starts to appear, most of them hidden unless you know the right trick. In extreme causes parts of it might integrate into the visiting city, a door not leading where it's supposed to, a street petering off into strange and unfamiliar blocks of buildings, buildings appearing where they shouldn't.

                  Those mages who end up within the living city, whether by accident, intent, or due to the interjection of a third party, quickly find their craft grown much stronger than before. Reality bends at the mages fingertips, and even the clumsiest, slowest of initiates may wield magic on the level of a master like nothing. Paradox do not occur, and every paradigm is welcome within the boundaries of the city. This power comes at a price. The avatar of a mage within the city is in grave danger. The more powerful the mage, and the more magic cast, the greater the risk. Each time a mage casts magic within the living city, his avatar is put under immense pressure. This may wound or ultimately break it, shattering the mages perception of reality.

                  The reason the living city is also known as Paradise, is that the marauders created from it have named it so.

                  Most of the city is abandoned. Few mortals take up any permanent residence within the city. Not only is there the danger of the city shifting to some uninhabitable place, there is also the danger of turning umbrood within it. Instead large parts of the city is inhabited by spirits, while other parts remain almost wholly abandoned.

                  The Layout of the Living City
                  The city itself is a dazzling array of buildings and streets, with their style and architecture often changing. Sometimes dependent on what part or time a visitor observes them, sometimes completely at random. There also parks, small rivers, trees, statues, everything you might find inside a city. The city is rife with warped spaces, and even without that it is fairly large. It is easy to get lost within it. Most of it seem abandoned, desolate streets stretching on, with only the odd spirit to keep one company. Those mages who manage to find their ways deeper inside the city will find more spirits, many who have been driven delusional by their stay inside the city. Eventually, the Ravine will be reached. This boundary circles the city, marking the line between the city proper and the inner city. While its name is a reference to its true form, that of a large abyss stretching down into the void, it takes many shapes. Sometimes it is a large river, at others a huge wall, or a thick clutter of apartments, hindering any passage. In all cases it forms a barrier that must be penetrated in order for someone to reach the inner city.

                  Within the inner city, things change. There are more and higher buildings here, and the streets are more narrow. While most spirits within the city proper are fairly neutral, spirits here seem actively hostile. The deeper someone penetrates into the city, the more warped space arises, until the city becomes a non euclidean labyrinthine mess. Theoretically, if someone could pierce this, they could reach the center of the city. What might be found there, none knows.

                  Theories behind the Living City

                  While it is not common knowledge, the phenomena of the city is well known enough for theories to have arisen as to its origins. Here follows a summary of some of the more popular ones.

                  A detached umbral shard realm
                  This theory holds that the city was once a shard realm within the middle or high umbra, detached sometime during the cataclysmic events of the end. According to most versions of this, the city would originally have represented the lost cities of old, Xanadu, Rome, Carthage, before broken and twisted into its current state. Some claim it is instead a broken paradox realm, originally intended to trap a mage within his own hubris, before the end came and set it loose from its home in the deep umbra.

                  A mad dream.
                  The mad has been known to alter reality around them, letting their delusion seep out and manifest in reality. This theory claims the city derives from one such marauder, his delusions grown so powerful so as leave their original mage behind. As the madness of the marauders infects those around them, so then does the city infect its visitors.

                  Nephandic Trap
                  This theory holds the city was somehow crafted by the fallen, to prey on and break unfortunate mages finding themselves within it.

                  A spirit court
                  This theory is close to the one that the city represents a shard realm, but rather than being broken or an accident, this one holds there is intent behind it. The theory goes that during the end, a spirit court fled their place within the high umbra, bringing their home with them. Whether this court has passed from the city or remains deep within it, those who ascribe to this theory cannot say. However, it is common to mention the possibilities of vast treasures left behind by its originating spirits. Whoever can pierce the heart of the city will surely find something of value, whether its treasures or spirits, or so the adherents of this theory say.

                  The Truth Behind the City

                  What follows outlines some of the true secrets and origins of The Living City.

                  Storyteller note: While this is presented here as the true origin of the city, it is left completely to the storyteller if it will be so in their chronicle. If you as a storyteller likes another explanation better, feel free to use that instead.
                  The Living City is named the Living City not because it moves around within the Umbra, but because it is alive. It is the culmination of a lifelong dream.

                  In the 20th century there lived a Dreamspeaker. Jacob Tackett. Jacob Tackett loved cities. His career within the traditions where not to unusual. What was unusual was his views on personal ascension, and the unconventional and often radical Dreamspeaker philosophies he ascribed to.

                  Jacob Tackett wanted to turn himself into a spirit.
                  Jacob Tackett wanted to turn himself into a city.

                  And so he did.

                  It took him almost a century of work, possible more. Even with that, his research and personal magics where not enough. The ritual which would spell his doom, and give rise to “Paradise” required not just himself but several powerful mages. Pulling on his oldest chantry mates and allies, he managed to get them all to agree in making the attempt sometime right before the end. The result was both a great success and a horrific failure. It did transform him into a living city, but in the process his avatar was shredded to pieces, and he himself became quite, quite insane. The current state of this city, its erratic behavior, the weird phenomena within it, the way it interacts with awakened mages, are all a result of this botched ritual. Most of those present at the time are dead by now, as the end came and people died. Only a handful remains who knows the truth.

                  The Purpose of the Living City.
                  The original intent of Tackett was to turn himself not just into a city and its own spirit, but to do so with his avatar intact. His ultimate goal was to as, a living city find and guide worthy sleepers, hoping to test and help them in awakening, not unlike certain grimoirs has been known to do, but at a far more advanced level. This is why the city provides visitors with the ability to alter reality within it in almost all the ways they please. It is an echo of the original intent of the city, to guide and test sleepers in the hope that they may awaken. This was the view of ascension Tackett had, a personal transmutation that might help other people to reach some form of personal enlightenment.

                  It all went terrible wrong.

                  Dangers of the City

                  The Living City is rife with dangers for the unwary traveler. What follows here are merely some of the more common threats within it.

                  Cosmic Power - Any awakened mage that finds themselves inside the city has reality at their fingertips. Any mage inside the bound of the city can be seen as having a rating of 5 in all spheres, and an arete of at least 5. Paradox do not occur within the city at all, and no characters gain paradox from any effects completely within the city. Any effect or object created within the city that is brought outside the city, brings paradox on it as normal. At the storytellers discretion characters may or may not require foci to cast spells. Rather than the effect of the city just being a matter of sphere ranks, the storyteller should play up how everything is just so much easier within the city, how reality changes and alters almost without the mages needing to do anything. Freeforming is encouraged. While the storyteller is free to impose limits on the characters, the focus should be on the capacity to pull off mad stunts, and effects beyond their wildest dreams. If a character wants to raise a building, let him! If he wants to create a living artificial intelligence from the stuff around the city, allow it! If a player tries for something really big, impressive or just downright cool, grant them bonus successes, or a higher arete!

                  When a character uses magic inside the city, the avatar is strained. The more powerful the mage, the greater the risk. Two systems to represent this is presented here. Either of these can be used, or the storyteller can make one up on his own.

                  <These haven't been play tested! Use with caution!>
                  1. When a player casts uses awakened magic, roll 1d10. On a roll of 1 something really bad happens. If the players start to really cut loose, you can slowly increase the chance of something really bad happening.
                  2. Whenever a player casts a spell, roll a number of dice equal to their real arete, vs a difficulty of 6. For each success, that characters avatar take 1 “damage”. When it receives damage equal to his rating, something bad happens and the damage is removed. It doesn't immediately break, but something goes wrong.
                  <Freeforming is also encouraged with this, if you think you are up to it>

                  Degrees of madness – While the city is meant to be dangerous, instantly turning a character marauder by a bad dice roll is a Bad Idea. As such here are a number of less instantly fatal side effects for slinging magic within the city.
                  • Cracked avatar – An avatar receives a wound within the city. The exact nature of it is up to the storyteller. It may be permanent or temporary. Perhaps the mage become unable to improve or use a certain sphere, the avatar becomes branded, the way the avatar interacts with the mage is altered, or the very nature of the avatar changes, such as a questing avatar turning primordial. Note that, in contrast to paradox, this is not some punishment bestowed upon the mage by reality. Rather it is the mages avatar breaking under the strain. Avoid poetic justice or some fitting themes to this kind of avatar wounds. Rather try to keep them chaotic and erratic. Less a logical consequence, and more something gone wrong with the avatar.
                  • Too good – The effect of the mage succeeds to well. A mage trying to raise a building raises an entire city block. A mage trying to create a robotic android as a companion accidentally creates one with an awakened avatar of its own, adding complications when they return.
                  • Wild Magic – The mage starts having difficulty not casting magic. Just thinking about an effect becomes enough for it to start to manifest. Spontaneous phenomena starts to occur around the mage, fitting his or her paradigm. Will power rolls may be in order not to cast magic, and she should be very wary what she thinks or say.
                  • Partial Quiet – Something about the mages perception of reality changes. He enters a partial quiet, which persists even after leaving the city.
                  • Quiet – The mage enters an actual quiet, either within the city, or as soon as he leaves its boundaries.
                  • Temporary Marauder – the mage goes insane, either temporarily or with the option to save him. He gains some form of delirium akin to that of the mad. The extent of the madness is up to the storyteller and the player.
                  • Marauder – While it should be avoided for player characters, going marauder is a risk within the city, and may be a fitting consequence at certain times. This could be at a campaign finale, with player character risking it all to pull of something truly crazy, or as a result of someone blatantly ignoring all warnings and consequences for extended periods of time. It could also be something that happens to an NPC.


                  As a storyteller it is up to you how dangerous it is to cast magic within the city. If the effects are usually temporary and takes a while to pile up and grow truly dangerous, you risk the effects becoming little more than a glorified form of paradox, but the players are allowed to play around more, which can be a lot of fun. If it is extremely dangerous to cast magic within the city, the players may try to avoid doing anything unless it is a truly big, necessary effect but in this case they may be to cautious to even try.
                  Your last option is to keep things unpredictable. Nothing happens at all most times a magic casts magic, but the times it does it is scary.

                  Other dangers:
                  Hostile umbroods
                  While umbroods are rare in the outskirts, and remain neutral to visitors even as they grow more common as you travel further inwards, visiting mages can certainly earn their ire, by for example accidentally blowing up their house, breaking obscure and nonsensical laws they follow, insulting or attacking them, or similar.

                  If any mage manages to reach the inner city, the umbrood he meets will be hostile, seeking to protect the centre of the city, either by intimidating, threatening, fooling or attacking the mage outright.

                  Marauders
                  Marauders enjoy and often traverse the city. The place ironically make them more lucid. Their insanity does not affect the city itself or inhabitants within it. If an old marauder imagines everything around him to be a movie set, while he may still think so, the city will not change, and people he meet will not suddenly believe themselves to be actors or movie crew. Most marauders seem less upset about this than one might think they should be. The city then becomes a place where a marauder may engage the players in idle conversation as soon as attack them. They are still quite insane, and any mage dealing with them should take great care. Their avatar already broken, it is not in as much danger as others.

                  System: A marauder within the city do not gain a rating of 5 in all spheres. His insanity constrains him. Rather, all spheres count as one higher for a marauder than it would be in reality back on earth up to a maximum of five. In addition, marauders do not risk breaking or wounding his avatar while within the city.

                  Repercussions at home:
                  While the city is not exactly common knowledge, it is known, and any mage who returns from it may find themselves distrusted and under investigation by the traditions. Mages visiting the city knowingly may find themselves punished by their tradition. The Traditions do not wish their members to go marauder after all, and so tries to dissuade those who visits the city from returning to it.

                  Umbrood creation:
                  A person remaining for several days or weeks within the city risks becoming an umbrood, as if they were in a horizon realm cut off from the tellurian. This transformation can be staved off. Dreamspeakers in particular stands a good chance of surviving for extended periods within the city, not so much because their ranks in the Spirit sphere (most visitors are going to have an effective rating of 5), but because they have the knowhow in how to deal and pact with spirits in the city to show them how to remain human. Even then though, the avatar will slowly start to crack.

                  Shifts:
                  While the city tend to remain in place for a few days to a few month, it does move around. Anyone within the city when it shifts will be brought with. If a mage enters it in London and the city shifts to Tokyo, the mage will exit it in Tokyo. Worse still, the surroundings within the city may change. If the city enters a place such as the bottom of an ocean or the depths of space any mortals within may find themselves getting flooded by water or lacking air to breathe.

                  Aside from this, the player may encounter any danger one might expect within a spirit city. Finding cursed objects, getting attacked by birds, bad weather, buildings falling on them, getting lost.

                  The last danger of the city is the temptation to use it to solve any problem the troupe may find themselves with. It is possible to affect things outside the city from within the city. In this cause the effect brings with it paradox as normal, in addition to putting the avatar under immense pressure.

                  Leaving the city
                  The easiest way to leave the city is to blast open a portal. This is a correspondence and spirit effect that affects the outside world, and is likely to be vulgar magic at that. (In the real world, extra dimensional portals just do not suddenly happen). This risks both the avatar and paradox.

                  The other way is to find a naturally occurring portal or path out of the city.

                  The farther in a mage travels, the more difficult it gets to leave the city just using magic (effects start requiring more and more successes). The Ravine itself can be seen as an enormous correspondence ward, and any attempt at crossing it using magic is likely to fail. A mage that manages to reach the inner city must first cross the Ravine before he can get out of the city using magic. (Unless again, he finds a portal or path out of the city. There are almost always shortcuts, and indeed spirits may try to fool mages into taking one, if it gets them away from the inner city).

                  The inner city is a mess of non euclidean space, something that might take a master of correspondence years of work to set up. There are paths trough it, but they are difficult to find and follow.

                  Returning to the City
                  Once a mage has visited the city once, it is up to the storyteller how difficult it is for that mage to find a portal to it again. It might be as easy as an Intelligence+Occult or Awareness roll, (assuming the city remains within the same city or general area of the mage), or it may be used more as a plot device, appearing as and when the storyteller require. Mages may try to gain entrance to the city by finding someone who knows a path in. This could end with them having to deal with anything from a spirit to a marauder to a werewolf.

                  Using the Living City within your own chronicle.
                  The Living city makes excellent fodder for a chronicle. Here follows some ideas.

                  Event Seeds
                  A player character has his avatar manifest within the city and speak to him directly.
                  The players need to get away from pursuers and are forced to take a shortcut trough the city.
                  The characters meet a fairly lucid Marauder who engages them in idle conversation.

                  Adventure Seeds
                  The players find themselves within the city in the course of a normal session. They may accidentally stumble upon a portal or a path in, or be brought there by some third party. This is a good idea for players who do not know anything about the city. Care must be taken to feed them neither to little information or to much. When they return and report what they have seen, they may find themselves under investigation even by their own masters, and banned from returning to the city. The storyteller can then make it necessary for them to return to the city a few sessions later.
                  The player character must find an item or person believed lost within the city.
                  An avatar has found itself stuck inside the city after its mage died. Gaining some degree of autonomy, but without the capacity to leave it seeks to find a way to escape. It tries to lure in people lacking in an avatar, to grant them power and itself freedom.

                  Chronicle Seeds
                  The restoration of Jacob Tackett – The player characters are charged with tracking down the lost avatar pieces of Jacob Tackett, with the ultimate goal of restoring his sanity. This task could be provided by one of the participants in the ritual that originally created him. If any player has a particular powerful mentor, or there is some established npc that would be a good candidate. Otherwise the storyteller can simply create one.

                  Alternatively, the player characters may figure out part of the truth on their own, or stumble upon a few of the pieces during their normal adventures.

                  Note that the storyteller is under no obligation to tell the players what the pieces they are finding are. If he wished to, he could keep it mysterious, with the players only knowing they are some kind of very potent magical item.

                  Where the pieces are is also up to the storyteller. They could be hidden within the city, each piece requiring some test or challenge to overcome. They may instead be scattered across the Tellurian. This would provide an excellent reason to traverse the cosmology and world of Mage the Ascension with the player characters needing to do everything from explore ancient Martian ruins to bluff themselves into the cellar of a bar in Liverpool.
                  Be careful so these quests do not become to much of a routine. You want to avoid the players feeling like they are going on an endless parade of fetch quests.

                  If the pieces are gathered his avatar can be restored. This may require the player characters gaining access to an archmaster of prime, or a final quest into the center of the city, to find and talk to the last remnants of Tackett.

                  Should they succeed, and Jacob Tackett have his sanity restored, the traditions will have gained a powerful ally. The mages within the city will no longer be empowered, but neither will their avatars be in danger, nor will mortals be in danger of turning umbrood.

                  While he is unlikely to allow himself to be bossed around (he was an archmaster of spirit after all) he is likely to try to assist (and influence) the traditions. In addition to his powers, Tackett sits on a massive amount on lore and knowledge on ephemera, the spirit stuff, and the avatar. Tackett will likely start working on his original intent, finding worthy sleepers he may test and instruct in an attempt to make them awaken. For all intents and purposes the city becomes a Genius Locci, an intelligent (and awakened place).

                  No exact stats will be provided for Tackett. He is an archmaster of spirit and a master of correspondence. He has a rating of at least one in all spheres. He has the skills that would fit the head of security for a prominent chantry, in addition to a vast array of theoretical knowledge on matters arcane. His personality is rather easy going and polite, but he priorities his own interests first, and takes most things in stride. He is rather old at this point, and a veteran of the ascension war.

                  The insane Tackett is an umbrood, the spirit of the city. He is not awake, though a person reaching the center of the city has the option to awaken him as any spirit can be. He is unlikely to be lucid, and his psychology and perspectives will be alien.

                  Other campaign seeds:

                  Nephandic plot -
                  There's a nephandic scheme to corrupt the city. Maybe a nephandi have started to gather the avatar pieces, in order to taint and corrupt Tackett. Or a nephandi is working to take Tacketts place as the guiding will behind the city, not unlike gutting someones soul and possessing their body. Such a nephandi may be a recurring villain the player characters have already come into conflict with. It could also be one of the original helpers within the ritual that created the city, who then became one of the Fallen, in the events surrounding the end.

                  Technocratic plot
                  The technocracy try and destroy the city. This can be a good plot if the player characters are part of a technocratic campaign. The technocracy are likely to know even less about the city than the traditions, other than it being an abnormally large dimensional anomaly. If the player characters are tasked with the city's destruction, this leaves a lot of options available for possible adventures. Hunting down and interrogating dreamspeakers or tradition mages who may know more than the technocracy. Conducting survey expeditions into the city proper. Fighting off marauders or mages who try to stop what you are doing. Dealing with suspicions from higher up, or possible forced to deal with a higher ranking technocrat who did visit the city and went marauder(without anyone else noticing). Slowly formalizing a plan for how to best eliminate the city, then enacting it in some grand finale.
