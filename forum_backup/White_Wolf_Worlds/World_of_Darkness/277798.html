      One of my principle villains was Akna, the Balefire Phoenix. She was a fallen firebird spirit that devoted herself to Eater of Souls after she betrayed Falcon thousands of years ago.               Most of the villains I've run weren't the "big bad" types. They have mostly been the PCs' peers, meeting them at cross-purposes due to differences in philosophy and/or conflicts of interest. Sometimes it's not always clear who the villains really are, and I've given my players plenty of chances to make friends or enemies out of a variety of NPCs. They've surprised me a few times by talking their way into a loose alliance of convenience when I expect a slash-and-burn approach, and by outright killing people who I thought they'd find useful.

              One of the few characters I used who was unabiguously a villain when I wrote him was Bishop Isadore Durant. He a Malkavian antitribu in power in Berkley, the city the PC's pack called home. He had maintained a secret alliance with the Camarilla for years. Each side fed the other empty "victories" and pre-arranged peace so the respective leaders could reinforce their power. He was known for a quirky and enigmatic personality which, combined with certain stereotype about his Clan, helped to avert scrutiny. Like many longer-lived vampires, Durant's only true loyality was to his own interests.

              As a flavor note, I think having a Malkavian constantly manipulating city politics to keep things convenient for the Sabbat gives tidy explanation of why Berkley, CA is such a ridiculous place. It certainly makes more sense than some of what actually goes on there sometimes.

                  I've got a few go-to villains.

                  Alexander Drake is my master planner/magnificent bastard. If he's a vampire, he's a Ventrue or a Setite. If he's a mage, he's NWO or Syndicate. He always wears a suit. He always has a plan. He does not get caught and if by some chance he does, not only is he not worried, but he has a plan for that too.

                  Jake Malcolm is my boisterous brute. As a vampire, he's usually Gangrel or Brujah. If we're playing Changeling, he's a Redcap. He's big, loud, fights a lot, shoots first and might ask some questions later if he thinks about it.

                  Zax Raven is my all-purpose con-man/trickster. As a vampire, he's Ravnos or Toreador (no, really, it works!). As a mage, he might be a barrabi VA or C of X, maybe not barrabi at all. He's trying to get something out of everybody and he doesn't care how he does it, as long as he doesn't have to work too hard for it and it won't seem like he worked to get it at all.

                  The Professor (I change the last name around) is my generic mad scientist/creator. For Mage, I've made him an Etherite and a Progenitor. As a Wraith, he was an Artificer. He makes things, people, etc into whatever he thinks would be a better use of their materials or talents. He doesn't like the way the world is and so he's going to change it, one thing at a time if necessary, into the world he thinks it should be.

                          I have the Hag of Winter (aka the Stern Lady). It is a once-female 7th Generation Tzimisce embraced about 250 years ago.                               After writing this I realize all my recent villans have a distinctive mage-y touch to them. I will make sure to bend that trend!


                              Three from my current game, far different "story elements" with each.

                              First - The "Far Beyond Redemption" Villan

                              Father Donovan, Barabbi Celestial Choir.
                              He is a legendary Nephandi hunter, known to use brutal tactics and have no compassion for the twisted mage's souls. What most don't know is that he shares that same disregard for all souls. He believed (before the Cauls, because of the shit he saw) that we were all fundamentally tainted and union with God was only a pipe dream. Since the Cauls he takes pleasure in proving that even the most "pious" or well-meaning people have twisted souls. He works to caress this darkness out of them one small step at a time.
                              He would rather die than admit what he truly is, and his conviction is hard to tell apart from a TV Evangelist.


                              Second - The "Good Intentions" Villan

                              Dr. Jonathan Jones, Progenitor
                              He is a pharacopoeist who grew up on the streets before using his amazing intellect to work his way into an ivy league college. He spends his time now "perfecting" street drugs to remove all the "unwanted" side effects, and replace them with his own subtle social engineering-based side effects. Instead of meth making your teeth fall out, it makes you a better employee.


                              Third - The "Redeemable" Villan

                              (Name withheld for now)
                              A hedge magician - alchemist with the OoH - who is beginning to get old and has lost hope of ever Awakening. He has lately turned to much darker research, and depending on how things go in the next few parts of the story could very easily fully fall into Infernalism. He isn't there yet, but he has made contact with a patron and done some minor thievery to further his own research, based on "information" provided by the demon.


                              From a game long past, Dark Ages Vampire (with a splash of Mage and Werewolf).

                              The Pot-Belly Merchant, Orphan Mind Mage, Infernalist (not Nephandi)
                              His name changes frequently, and his only concern is making his life nice and comfortable. He was working with Lord Vanis because it was profitable, and he didn't give a shit how things turned out. His job - which he took very seriously - was to procure children and make sure they proceeded through the 'process' quickly.

                              Lord Vanis, Tzimice 6th Generation, Infernalist, Rampant Diablerist
                              An old world style ruler, and a master of fleshcrafting AND koldun. He was different in that he had a good deal of mage and spirit lore. He was able to blood bond a spirit mage, and incorporate this ability into his process. The process was simple, procure child -> torture child -> blood bond child -> summon and force bane possession of child -> Fleshcraft and train child to be elite (but prone to wild frenzies and the inability to feel good emotions) warrior.
                              Lord Vanis would never fight directly and was deeply involved in Infernalism. He was exceptional at fleeing, and he could customize his minions to attack his enemies weaknesses. He didn't have many demonic investments, instead valuing knowledge and respect above all things.

                                  Madam Deveraux (The Stern Lady, The Hag of Winter) appears in cities across North America, particularly among the southern and port cities. She is immensely charismatic, possessed of a refined manner and exquisite decorum, dressed in beautiful but understated clothing which accentuates her beauty. Typically, she prefers etiquette and polite conversation when first engaged. When enraged, her zulo form combined with her Protean and physical powers make her a terrifying opponent. However, this is merely conjecture, as there are no existing survivor accounts. She is rumored to be associated with various blood cults past and present across the American South, and has extensive contacts in human trafficking.
                                  Background:
                                  Embraced from among the mulatto results of the African slave trade, the Kindred later known as The Stern Lady has been intimately connected with New Orleans and its history of slavery and spirit magics for centuries. Heir to a mortal magical tradition centered on the spirit world, she was brought into darkness by an unnamed vampire. Asserting control of several slaving ships with her emerging powers within a few years, she entered the slave trade as her own Mistress, embracing a variety of tutors to expand her knowledge. As her knowledge of blood and blood magic expanded, she found uses for the kine beyond merely serving as sustenance, binding them to her in chains of blood and secrecy for their vitae and other uses.
                                  The Stern Lady has the vision of becoming an antediluvian in her right, fueled by secret diablerie and the power of the kine souls she consumes in her pursuit of apotheosis. This steady stream of cultist souls has enabled her to grow in power to rival vampires greatly her senior. Through study she has realized that she is approaching the pinnacle of what her current generation is capable, and once more seeks an older cainite to diablerize to continue her ascension. Luckily she never shows the signs of diablerie!
                                  The Stern Lady is served by an elder Malkavian who possesses the Restructure power. This power is used in conjunction with Presence or Dominate to “recruit” individuals from homeless shelters or as a result of human trafficking into Soldier, Follower, Celebrant or other Natures for inclusion into their blood cults, creation of servants or agents, inclusion into special training or possibly childer.

                                      In my troupe’s Wraith games, we had a few villains (in the “Big Bad” sense). We had one reoccurring character that was the troupe’s primary antagonist without (necessarily) being the “villain.”

                                      Sergeant Bigsley, Centurion of the Grim Legion and foil to our characters (of the lesser-evil variety). What we knew about him (or at least what the troupe put together) is that he’s been dead for less than a century, is a protocol-driven military mind, and constant pain in the Corpus for the players. To him, we were:
                                      • A borderline Heretic trucker probably in league with those shady Ferryman (“Only the Ferryman could have forged that rig!”)
                                      • A reckless, vengeance-seeking outlaw on the verge of becoming Shadow-eaten. (“Anyone with that sorta grudge that crosses the Shroud that often has gotta be up to no good.”)
                                      • And a Doomed conspirator who is obviously a traitor to Stygia. (“Why else would anyone associate with the riffraff that he’s constantly seen with?”)

                                      He’s usually portrayed as a determined blend of Fidel Castro and Homer Simpson; a well-used field uniform (that we always thought was WWII-era), a pot-belly, a thick beard, and – yes! – a cigar. His legionnaires would harass us every chance they had, at one point arresting the troupe after a very unfortunate misunderstanding.

                                      As a plot device, I loved this character. He wasn’t evil, he wasn’t corrupt. He just took his job seriously. It just so happened, to our misfortune, that our characters’ version of “serious” was not in-line with his. He was a great antagonist that a story could be created around that didn’t end with some Big Nasty Nephwrack/Onceborn Cult/Pasiphae/etc.; we could save those for the stories that needed a touch of Oblivion-tainted Horror (capital "H").

                                      Sgt. Bigsley, I salute you!
