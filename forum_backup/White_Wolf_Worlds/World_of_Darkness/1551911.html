    Mummy/Exalted Crossover

    06-29-2025, 05:53 AM
    A Revised Mummy Setting

    I know I’m not alone in regarding Mummy 2nd Edition as the highpoint of the Mummy sourcebooks line. That said, some of its mechanics were either clunky or not very fleshed out. 3rd Edition fixed most of these issues, but at the same time it threw out the baby with the bath water by trashing the setting almost entirely. Now you had to play as someone with a serious mental or moral flaw and then have your own personal Jiminy Cricket stuffed into your head while dealing with the constant scrutiny of 42 Big Brothers who were eager to dissect your every word and deed and gleefully describe all the ways you were always failing to live up to their standards. (Just when you thought you’d finally escaped your abusive nagging parents…)

    Another issue was the Spell of Life being far too available in the world and for far too long. Seriously, this thing would have been the hottest spell in the history of humanity and every magician would have been cheerfully willing to commit murder to get ahold of it. The end result would have been legions of mummies wandering around the world by now, which isn’t desirable as they were designed as this niche addition to the World of Darkness and there weren’t all that many of them. So, knowledge of the spell and the ability to successfully cast it needed to be locked down. That said, these restrictions still needed to allow for the possibility of new mummies being created in the modern age.

    And finally, with the changes I’d be making the Cabiri couldn’t realistically exist as there was no written version of the Spell of Life for them to find and copy. However, I’d recently discovered World of Darkness: Immortals and this struck me as the perfect crossover. The Purified are clearly an homage to the World of Darkness mummies, though their form of immortality is decidedly inferior. But, that makes them the perfect choice for a “Lesser Rite” companion to the Great Rite.

    Here’s an overview of the setting and revisions for the Reborn:
    • This version of Mummy could be best described as “3rd Edition going on 2nd Edition.” The setting is from 2nd Edition, but the rules, Backgrounds, Merits, and Flaws are from 3rd Edition. So, there are no “soul fragments” flying about looking for hosts, no Judges of Ma’at constantly looking over your shoulder, and Amenti is still very much intact in the Underworld. In addition, the newer version of the Spell of Life from 3rd Edition was retconned as having been that way since the beginning.
    • There are no Wu T’ian and no Teomallki. The Cult of Isis have the only magicians capable of successfully casting the Spell of Life upon mortals to turn them into mummies.
    • There are no Bane Mummies, either, as Set would not have been able to cast the Spell of Life without the consent of Cytheria (see below). His followers must make do with creating Asekh-sen as shock troops instead.
    • Upon their First Death, the new mummy does emerge in the Underworld, enveloped in a Caul like any other newly-dead Wraith, but they’re strangely ignored by the inhabitants of the Shadowlands until they're drawn back to their body by the Great Rite. Some mummy scholars have posited that Anubis protects them during this vulnerable time, but this is mere speculation.
    • Upon their First Rebirth, the new mummy’s body is returned to the peak of health and fitness and appears to be in its early twenties.
    • The Shemsu-Heru are those mummies who swear fealty to Horus. The Ishmaelites are those mummies who were members of the Shemsu-Heru but later defected, or mummies created in later centuries who declined to join his crusade. For his part, Horus despises the Ishmaelites and sees little difference between those who abandoned his cause and those who never joined it in the first place; he has a very “you’re either with me or against me” attitude.
    • Mummies aren’t bound to the Web of Faith and there’s no such thing as semektet (a weakened state for 3rd Edition mummies when they had no Sekhem remaining).
    • Mummies regain one point of Sekhem each day at dawn along with an extra point if they’re within the Lands of Faith. If within a Node / Caern / Dragon Nest, they can spend a full day there meditating to recover Sekhem; the roll is Wits + Meditation (difficulty 6).
    • The Balance Trait no longer exists. Use the new Sahu Trait instead for any rolls that would involve Balance in canon (see below).
    • Mummies gain the following Supernatural Merits for free:
    1. Blessing of Ra (7 points – Mummy: The Resurrection Players Guide, p. 115; this Merit has been altered, see below for details)
    2. Burning Aura (1 point – M20 The Book of Secrets, pp. 68)
    3. Gift of Ma’at (3 points – Mummy: The Resurrection Players Guide, p. 115; this Merit has been altered, see below for details)
    4. Immunity to Disease (3 points – Mummy: The Resurrection Players Guide, p. 115)​
    5. Immunity to Poisons (3 points – Mummy: The Resurrection Players Guide, p. 115)​
    6. Spark of Life (5 points – M20 The Book of Secrets, pp. 78)
    7. Unbondable (2 points; this Merit has been altered, see below for details)
    ​The reason for this package of Merits is a replacement of sorts for the Judges of Ma’at. Instead of a stable of gods constantly judging you in an effort to keep you on the straight and narrow, now your very nature ensures that you can’t join up with the Forces of Darkness™. Vampires and monsters such as Banes, Fomori, etc. will always feel unnatural and wrong to you. And if they even come near you, your Blessing of Ra will instantly kick in, lashing out with a Forbiddance attempt. You’ll certainly never be welcome at Elysium. Like it not, you’re on the side of the angels, and with your Burning Aura Merit, you can’t really hide, either. (The Blessing of Ra and Spark of Life Merits make your aura even harder to hide on top of that.)
    • Contrary to 3rd Edition canon, mummies can’t just conveniently opt out of their immortality at the drop of a hat via “no will to live” or “self-destruction” (as per Mummy 3rd Edition, p. 137). The Spell of Life is pretty much a one-way trip. You’re going to be around for a long, long time…
    And for the Cabiri:
    • These immortals were created via the Elixir of Life, whose effects qualify as a second-rate version of the Spell of Life. This “Lesser Rite” transforms mortal humans into creatures that superficially resemble true mummies, but their version of immorality is not as resilient.
    • The Elixir of Life is a highly-advanced potion, requiring either Alchemy 6 and Enchantment 6, or Alchemy 6 and Ephemera 6 to make. It also requires numerous rare and expensive ingredients, including several deadly toxins and blood from the person who will drink it. Actually brewing it requires a full lunar month, during which time the alchemist is more-or-less confined to their laboratory as they monitor the process every waking moment. The person who will drink it does not need to be the person who makes it, but they’re literally trusting their life to someone else if they’re not the elixir’s creator.
    • Upon drinking, the elixir takes one minute to suffuse itself within the drinker’s body, then takes effect as a Rating 7 Toxin. (See the Toxin rules in M20, pp. 441-442.) Barring extraordinary measures, this will see the drinker dead in short order. Over the next three days, their mind and soul will slowly complete the transformation into an immortal spirit within the Penumbra. Once this is complete, the new immortal must find their way back across the Gauntlet to their body. This requires an expenditure of Willpower and/or Gnosis points equal to the local Gauntlet rating (see M20, p. 484). If this is not feasible, they must travel the Penumbra to find a Node, Caern, Shallowing, Haunt or other location where the Gauntlet is thinner and try again. Their spirit form is assumed to have Rage equal to their Strength and Gnosis equal to their Intelligence. They can regain spent Gnosis points by meditating quietly for an hour with Wits + Meditation roll (difficulty 8).
    • Back on Earth, with a successful Perception + Awareness roll (difficulty 6), they can follow a silvery cord back to their body’s location; this is their immortal spirit’s bond to their earthly body. In their absence, their body does not rot and requires neither food nor water. If examined by a doctor, it would appear to have an exceptionally faint and slow heartbeat and to be in an exceptionally deep coma. Once the drinker manages to return to their body, it immediately comes back to life and during this process returns to the peak of health and fitness in its early twenties. From that point on, their body heals more rapidly than normal and completely ceases to age. They’re also immune to diseases and poisons.
    • The new immortal retains the ability to use Sorcery, but loses the potential to Awaken. If already Awakened, they lose that status and are now treated as a Sorcerer instead. They’re also unaffected by vampire blood and thus can’t be Embraced. If they had the Ghoul, Garou Kinfolk, or Kinain templates, those are lost as well.
    • Their newly immortal body isn’t any more durable than a normal human’s, and can die just as easily. If and when this happens, they’ll be forced out of it into their spirit form and find themselves once again back in the Penumbra. This is a vulnerable time for them, as in this state they can meet Final Death. If slain in the same manner as other spirits – losing all of their Essence and then being harvested for Gnosis – that’s a permanent end for them. In addition, if their physical body is somehow completely destroyed, they’ll be trapped in the Umbra as a spirit. However, if they can once again make their way back to Earth and then to their body, they can repair it by spending Willpower and/or Gnosis points to heal Health Levels on a 1-for-1 basis.
    • The requirement of achieving arch-mastery of two Paths is the reason there aren’t more Cabiri in the world, as Sorcerers with that powerset don't exactly grow on trees. Awakened Mages could pull this off with Prime 5 and Spirit 5, but that would be their last act as an Awakened Mage so even those masters of magic that know of this path aren't keen utilizing it to prolong their lives.


    Prehistory – The Exalted Age

    Cytherea

    In the wake of the Primordial War, having narrowly escaped the fate of the Neverborn or the Yozi, she retreated to hidden realms of her own creation in the Wyld. Throughout the rest of the history of Creation she periodically interfered in the schemes of the Gods and Exalted alike, but always from behind the scenes. She continued her self-imposed exile into the age of World of Darkness, along with her predilection for interference from the shadows.

    In the early days of the land of Kemet, she foresaw their evolution into the great empire of Ancient Egypt and was moved to guide their development as their coming civilization reminded her of the golden age of Creation. In the guise of Thoth, she taught Isis and Nephthys the secrets of magic and later guided Isis into crafting and casting the Spell of Life to help purge the forces of Apophis from the land. Millennia later, it still only by her will that High Priests and Priestesses of the Cult of Isis can enact the Great Rite upon mortals to transform them into the Reborn; when the faithful pray to Isis, it is she who hears their prayers. She crafted the Reborn as replacement of sorts for the Exalted, but instead of creating nigh-invincible powerhouses she focused on creating agents whose greatest strength was their ability to ultimately ignore any defeat and return to their mission time after time. This approach, she feels, has avoided many of the problems that arose with the larger-than-life Exalted whose great levels of power ultimately contributed to their corruption and the fall of Creation.
    ​


    Prehistory – Ancient Egypt

    The History of the Reborn

    This history of the ancient world and how mummies came to be is how it was described in Mummy 1st Edition (pp. 58-65) and Mummy 2nd Edition (pp. 16-21). This story was also neatly recounted by Lazar of Stygia here on YouTube.

    The Creation of the Artifacts of Ma’at

    In the waning days of the Ancient Egyptian empire, beset by foreign forces that were increasingly eroding their culture and sovereignty, the priesthood of Isis and those remaining priesthoods of the other old gods assembled at a hidden temple in a last great conclave. Their civilization’s end was likely at hand, but their crusade against Apophis remained. It was paramount that the Reborn would continue to have support and that new worthy Reborn would continue to be justly selected in the ages to come.

    For the first concern, the most essential priests of Isis would entrench and go underground, prioritizing the security of their most important temples, the ones where the Great Rite could be enacted. These would be obscured and hidden to the best of the Isis priesthood’s ability while their allies in the other priesthoods would carefully work to erase any evidence that they had ever existed at all.

    But for the problem of selecting new Reborn, they debated for some time and finally decided on a novel solution to a very long-term issue. The priests of Ma’at and Shai would work in conjunction with the priests of Isis to create seven sacred ushabti. Into these would be bound seven wise and loyal spirits who would take on the duty of selecting worthy candidates. The magics enchanting them would both protect them from harm and see to their serendipitous movement around the world as fate directed them to those whom they would evaluate. Unbeknownst to them, Cytherea quietly guided their efforts as she approved of their plan.

    This great effort took over a year and when it was at last complete the priests of Ma’at and Shai departed, taking with them the ushabti to quietly send out into the world. Long after all of them had departed the Earth, these immortal agents would continue to scrutinize humanity to determine those who would join the ranks the Reborn. And thus it has been for millennia.



    Prehistory – Ancient Greece

    The History of the Cabiri

    In the sixth century B.C., a Greek magus traveling in Samothrace met with a man named Cabirus, who claimed to be an immortal. He credited his immortality to a powerful rite performed by the high priest of an Egyptian cult, and claimed to know the secret of making others like himself. The magus, versed in alchemic and necromantic magicks, recorded all of the knowledge of the rite imparted to him by Cabirus and then returned home to Thessalonica. He eventually became the first “lesser immortal.”

    His notes came to be known as The Secret Writings of Cabirus and would eventually attain the status of the most sought-after grimoire of the ancient world. Horus was greatly displeased when he learned of it, and what happened next was a seemingly endless cycle. A copy of the Writings would languish for years in some remote location, be rediscovered by some mystery cult, and eventually a new immortal would emerge. Agents of Horus would learn of this and then track down the copy and destroy it (along with the new immortal, if they could find them). But it was never the last copy, no matter how many times they tried. Horus persists in his seemingly-futile crusade to expunge every last copy, but with the advent of the Information Age this has become a Sisyphean endeavor.
    ​


    Cosmology

    Amenti – The Dark Kingdom of Sand

    Contrary to some rumors, Amenti, the Dark Kingdom of Sand, is still very much intact the Underworld and provides a place of refuge for all of the Reborn in between their lives on Earth. It remained untouched by the Oblivion Storm [this was the Avatar Storm in WoD canon] as it’s protected by Cytherea (see above) in her various guises as the gods of Ancient Egypt. Normally, only the Reborn may enter Amenti and even finding it in the Underworld is impossible for most Wraiths. But, from time to time some Wraiths or other Umbral travelers of good character have been permitted find it and enter this unique realm. (The bonded familiars of mummies are always welcome to accompany their masters into Amenti.)
    ​


    Organizations

    The Cult of Isis

    The priesthood of Isis have (barely) survived into the modern era and maintain a small network of hidden underground temples throughout the Middle East. This is a testament to both their tenacity and that of their hidden patron, Cytherea, a surviving Primordial from a long-lost age (see above). She’s assumed the guise of Isis for millennia now and is the one answering their prayers. It is only by her will that the Spell of Life can successfully be cast, as even her high priests and priestesses don’t realize that this epic magic is beyond mortal ken. All of their surviving temples are at least rating 5 Nodes, which is a necessary component of the Great Rite. The spell itself is actually an invocation to plead for her intercession and the conduit by which that can be enacted. As such, these hidden temples have heavy magical wards and a plethora of modern defenses as well; local officials are also well-bribed to stay out of their affairs.
    ​


    Notable Relics

    The Artifacts of Ma’at

    Millennia ago, these seven ushabti were crafted and sent out into the world where, through seeming fate and happenstance, they end up in the possession of those who are good candidates to become one of the Reborn. Each ushabti is inhabited by an ancient and wise spirit who spends years evaluating a given candidate. During this time, the spirit both tutors and subtly tests them to evaluate their character and abilities. If the candidate is deemed acceptable, they’re directed to one of the hidden temples of the Cult of Isis within the Lands of Faith. If they fail the evaluation, the ushabti disappears back into the world in search of the next candidate. Physically, each ushabti is about 10” tall, delicately carved from hard stone and exquisitely painted. They are all of the goddess Ma’at, though each portrays a differently dressed and posed version of the deity.

    Merits

    Blessing of Ra (7 points)
    Imbued with holy wrath, you can easily turn away the foul minions of Apophis. Reduce by two the difficulty of any Forbiddance attempt (Mummy: The Resurrection, p. 142). Creatures successfully banished by you suffer one level of aggravated damage as your righteousness sears their tainted soul. You also make reflexive Forbiddance attempts whenever a malign entity even comes near you. Since this reaction is automatic, it doesn’t require an action. In addition, you have a golden nimbus surrounding your aura.
    REFERENCE Mummy: The Resurrection Players Guide, pp. 115
    ​
    Effortless Resurrection (7 points)
    Unlike most of the Reborn, you needn’t sacrifice a point of permanent Sekhem or Willpower in order to resurrect yourself. However, if you die yet again and attempt to resurrect yourself within a year of your last resurrection, you must spend permanent Sekhem or Willpower as any other mummy would.
    REFERENCE: This is a custom Merit.
    ​
    Gift of Ma’at (3 points)
    Attuned to the ways of cosmic balance, you can naturally sense the presence of those who serve or oppose the cosmic order. Whenever anyone with a Corruption rating enters the range of your natural senses, your Storyteller should secretly roll your Humanity (difficulty 4). Success reveals the presence of an unrighteous being and you will know which being that is. Any such beings feel unnatural and wrong to your senses.
    NOTE: All vampires have a Corruption rating of at least 1, which is very likely to increase as they age and their Humanity score decreases.
    REFERENCE Mummy: The Resurrection Players Guide, pp. 115, 198-213
    ​
    Resilient Khat (5 points)
    Even if your khat is totally destroyed, it will slowly reform as a pile of ashes in the nearest safe and likely-out-of-sight location from the point of its destruction. This takes a lunar month and even then your body has a Health Level of Dust/Ash, so resurrection requires six successes. However, even if your body was blasted to atoms in a nuclear explosion, you can still come back.
    REFERENCE: This is a custom Merit and was priced as per the Supernatural Merit: Unity of Being.
    ​
    Unbondable (2 points)
    This rather infamous Merit has been extensively revamped…pun intended. If you take this Merit, you’re effectively opting out of Vampirism and Ghouldom. You’re simply unaffected by vampire blood. You can’t be Embraced and you can’t become a Ghoul, either. The reduced cost reflects your inability to “double-dip” on powers, gaining access to vampiric Disciplines in addition to any other supernatural powers you might have.
    REFERENCE: M20 The Book of Secrets, pp. 75

          Originally posted by orathaic View Post
          I have been looking at doing something with the Purified for a CofD like setting, and heard it was inspired by the WoD Mummy line, so this is great considering i can't get my hands on Mummy.

          Can you explain what the theme of the game is? How you decided on those? Or anything else which went into you design goals?
          Well, this is a bit of a story. I've been gaming the World of Darkness on and off since the 1990s. Mummy was a niche setting addition, but it always fascinated me. Think WoD Sorcerer, but they can't die, at least not permanently. They’re the Energizer Bunnies of the World of Darkness – they just keep going and going and going…

          The theme of Mummy? That’s up for a bit of debate. They’ve been called a good character for a mixed supernaturals party (see this video from Mr. Welch). Alternately, you can run their canon description straight, and then in that case their schtick is “they’re the good guys in the World of Darkness.” All the other game lines are gloom-and-doom, but they’re supposed to be hope for a better future. That alone makes them stand out, and considering that I’ve always been fascinated by Ancient Egypt, that sealed the deal for me and I found myself coming back to them over the years.

          As for my design goals, that’s also a bit of a story. Most Mummy fans I’ve known have liked 1st and 2nd Edition better than 3rd (Mummy: The Resurrection). This is because they threw out the baby with the bathwater when they updated the setting for 3rd and basically got rid of a lot of what made Mummy fun in the first place. Going from playing an immortal sorcerer to playing some schmuck with needs-therapy-level of issues who’s had their own personal “spirit guide” shoved into their head while also now having to deal with 42 nagging parents who are always eager to tell you all of the ways you continue disappoint them…yeah, that was a bit of a letdown. That said, the mechanics of 3rd Edition were a welcome facelift from 2nd Edition. So, what to do?

          This was my attempt to take the fluff of 2nd Edition and mix it with the crunch of 3rd Edition. In the process, I also wanted to address what I saw as a huge plot hole for Mummy. Namely, the availability the Spell of Life, which is what transforms regular humans into mummies. If you go by canon, it seems that nearly any magician can grab a copy and promptly achieve immortality, but for some reason there just weren’t a lot of mummies wandering around the world. This boggles the mind. A spell of guaranteed immortality would be the hottest piece of magic in all of history and there should be millions of mummies wandering around by this point!

          Access to that spell needed to be locked down, but at the same time I needed to leave wiggle room for players in the modern world to create new mummy characters. My solution was to retcon the Spell of Life as “epic magic” beyond the ken of mortal magicians. Even if you’re a master magician, you can’t just whip out this spell and become a mummy. Instead, only duly sanctified priests of the old gods could successfully cast it, and they would only do so for those mortals they found worthy. The Cult of Isis is about the only Ancient Egyptian priesthood still around in the modern World of Darkness (and Isis is both a goddess of healing and magic) so they were my designated group to control access to the Spell of Life. And as a further access control, it could only be successfully cast in one of their temples within the Web of Faith.

          All well and good, but, upon further reflection, I had another problem. There weren’t really any gods hanging around anymore to actually facilitate this. The closest thing the modern World of Darkness has to all-powerful gods would probably be the Celestines, but they’re…not really what I needed here. I needed some old gods (or at least one old god) who was still hanging about. That got me thinking, while Exalted isn’t canonically the long-lost past of the World of Darkness universe (though it was originally going to be), many fans still run with that, including me. Doing some digging, I found a Primordial who could conceivably fit the bill, with just a bit of backstory fudging.

          And this actually ended up being a better concept than I intended. Immortal champions created by a goddess to be her agents on Earth? Sounds a lot like the Exalted themselves, so I ran with the idea. The mummies being “Exalted Lite” not only solved all my access control issues, it was another link to the Exalted setting, which I’m all in favor of.

          Though there were some unavoidable repercussions. One, the Bane Mummies couldn’t exist as my new background story for the mummies would completely preclude that. Still, the Asekh-sen are pretty nasty already and the servants of Set can create those whenever they feel like, so they’ll do as stand-ins for the Bane Mummies. Two, it would also preclude the Cabiri, but I liked having them around as another occasionally-seen-in-the-shadows faction, so I found a way to include them. Having just recently got around to picking up World of Darkness: Immortals, the Purified struck me as the perfect replacement for the Cabiri. These immortals were clearly meant as a homage to the Mummy sourcebooks, but they were inferior immortals when compared to the true mummies. But, since the Cabiri were said to be the creations of “a bastardized variant of the Spell of Life,” that actually worked out even more perfectly. I just needed to make the casting requirements to become one of these lesser immortals difficult as well in order to justify their rarity, and there you go.

          For the true mummies, however, I wanted to stress what I believe they ought to be doing, namely combatting the darkness in the World of Darkness. This appears to be what the 3rd Edition authors were (clumsily) attempting to do via the Judges of Ma’at, but I wanted a less Big Brother approach to steering mummies towards the straight and narrow. Hence, the package of Merits that ensure they can’t miss the monsters lurking in the shadows, and that they can’t try to make friends with those monsters. No nagging parents looking over their shoulder, but their new nature is just fundamentally incompatible with evil, so this should eventually clue them in to what side they’re on.

          Anyhow, hopefully this answers your questions, but I’m happy to clarify any points.

          P.S. If you want to explore further links between Exalted and the World of Darkness, I recommend this not-exactly-official supplement to the World of Darkness by one of the former Exalted developers.

                      As for my design goals, that’s also a bit of a story. Most Mummy fans I’ve known have liked 1st and 2nd Edition better than 3rd (Mummy: The Resurrection). This is because they threw out the baby with the bathwater when they updated the setting for 3rd and basically got rid of a lot of what made Mummy fun in the first place. Going from playing an immortal sorcerer to playing some schmuck with needs-therapy-level of issues who’s had their own personal “spirit guide” shoved into their head while also now having to deal with 42 nagging parents who are always eager to tell you all of the ways you continue disappoint them…yeah, that was a bit of a letdown. That said, the mechanics of 3rd Edition were a welcome facelift from 2nd Edition. So, what to do?

                      This was my attempt to take the fluff of 2nd Edition and mix it with the crunch of 3rd Edition. In the process, I also wanted to address what I saw as a huge plot hole for Mummy. Namely, the availability the Spell of Life, which is what transforms regular humans into mummies. If you go by canon, it seems that nearly any magician can grab a copy and promptly achieve immortality, but for some reason there just weren’t a lot of mummies wandering around the world.
