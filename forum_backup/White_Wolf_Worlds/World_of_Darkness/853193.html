    Looting And Fencing.

    03-25-2016, 06:31 PM
    The Abstract nature of the "Resources background" is, rather blatantly, designed to de emphasize looting, which plays a prominent role in Dungeons and dragons.

    I get the desire for a change of theme, and the emphasis on either trust funds, playing the stock market, or a day job.

    But I find it hard to believe, that when a Garou pack sacks a pentex installation their not going to
    loot it

    likewise when some hunters slay a vampire, why not ransack his stuff?

    If your a Nagah, by taking his valuables, you both A: Fund your nest, and B: Potentialy throw hostiles off track, and get them thinking you were a mundane robber who got very very very lucky...

    How difficult should fencing be?

    some jewelry any pawn shop should do

    specialized computers, scanners, like pentex DNA Stark Psychiatric institute or the technocracy have, are best sold, if at all to hard to find speciality markets,

    well thoughts on the idea?

                          some jewelry any pawn shop should do

                          well thoughts on the idea?
                          I personally don't think that the abstraction of wealth is designed to reduce looting anymore than the abstraction of movement and combat is designed to reduce dungeon crawling, its just a favor over narrative terms rather than book keeping or simulation, but...

                          Characters can absolutely steal from those that they have fought with or destroyed. Looting is a fine practice in a fantasy game or post apocalyptic setting, there's no social consequences (usually) from taking things through force or violence. Looting in today's world (and in the World of Darkness) is burglary, armed robbery, grand or petty theft, and potentially homicide if your player's characters leave a body behind.

                          I think the difference here is the weight of consequences for those kinds of actions bring, particularly if they're something that happens over and over again. Fencing specialized equipment is the type of thing that a fence remembers, and when a group of 'ecoterrorists' are under investigation by Homeland Security (or SAD), and they discover that specialized gene sequencing computers, the type that could make biological weapons, well they might grow concerned. Or if a hunter cell has murdered a witch and steals and sells her valuables, well homicide is going to look into that and the valuables might not be worth very much if they're particularly well known - that makes them harder for a fence to sell. Even without police involvement, word could get out, selling a copy of the Book of Nod or stock bonds worth hundreds of thousands might raise the attention of a dead Vampire's coterie, clan, sect, ghoul(s), or mortal relations.

                          At the very least it is painting the picture to whomever the pc's are selling to that they are thieves and possibly murderers (did characters check that expensive rug for blood spatter when they sold it?) which might even set them up to be robbed themselves.


                          As for mechanics, Streetwise represents knowing who to speak to and a certain connection to criminal activity, you might need Wits + Etiquette if they're not familiar with a particular city or scene to establish themselves, if they are established what the characters want for it has to match what's realistic. A shady pawnshop might take any electronics but won't touch firearms, likewise bundles of cash need to get laundered to avoid drawing attention from the IRS or FBI.

                          The value is going to always be far less than the retail price for stolen goods because the seller takes on risk if they're caught, items that are traceable are going to be worth less.

                          Contacts (criminal) or Perception / Manipulation + Streetwise - locate a fence
                          Manipulation/ Charisma + Streetwise/ - make a deal
                          Intelligence + Finance - launder money
                          Wits + Law/Science - hide evidence

                          I would scale the difficulty based on how obscure or 'hot' the things they were trying to fence, and play out a scene anytime it would be enough liquid cash to increase a character's effective resources score. 8 for something difficult to sell like expensive equipment, 9 for anything that would require a specialized buyer like pieces of art or highly valuable goods. Further I'd keep track of how many times the pc's rely on fencing goods, if it was enough to raise a resources dot, I'd make them turn to money laundering or else face the consequences of having an investigation opened on them.

                          If you're looking for inspiration the tv shows Better Call Saul and Breaking Bad both deal with criminal processes and the problems that crop up dealing with illegal goods and services.

                          Hope that helps, if you're playing a more cinematic kinda game, then I'd say cut it down to a simple roll and reward the characters with a little bit of 'spending money' or a new piece of gear on a success and a minor wrinkle if they don't.
