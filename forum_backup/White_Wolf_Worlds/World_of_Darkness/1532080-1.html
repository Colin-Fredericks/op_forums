            It's not particularly surprising that gnosticism pops up everywhere in pop and counter culture. The broad strokes of gnostisicm - that the creator of the physical world is suspect, that there is wisdom to be found in rebellion and transgression, that a small group of of people are aware of 'the truth,' etc. - are a writer's gold mine. It's a scaffolding for writing stories that feel metaphysically or spiritually deep without actually needing to be deep, and for writing stories without the constraints of more traditional moral systems or lessons. A 'civilians are expendable" mentality doesn't fly in most stories, but with a gnostic framework it becomes the foundation of a cinematic blockbuster, and outside of that same framework the words "there is no spoon" would likely fall flat.

            Gnosticism is also an attractive spiritual or metaphysical framework. In the broad strokes (again) it is dualist, justifies a certain amount of rejection and transgression of mainstream moral and ethical systems, has an understandable answer to questions about the existence of evil, etc. So of course it keeps popping up throughout history in a variety of ways that look broadly similar until you get into their deeper beliefs.

            All of which is to say, I don't think the presence of gnosticism in a modern work, be it the World of Darkness or the Lego Movie, is an indication the writers are well versed in the beliefs of the Gnostics, the Albigensians, or the Theosophists. Some may be, but others are only be passingly familiar with them.

                There are many things that overlap between Demon and Mage Lore. Especially Old Mage (where "The One" wasn't just a paradigm thing but the OOC explanation). But not in a single way

                For instance: The Pure Ones were described as "shards of the One", but also as independent and "non-human" entities that created the cosmos and it's most basic laws (and also shattered, creating the Avatars).

                The Pure overlap very well with Demons in their role (even if they don't define themselves as "shards"), and this angle was even explored in Demon's own fiction: IIRC, there's a Demon that "sees" a mage using magick, and perceives it as the mutilated "body" of an angel providing the power - the Avatars being dead angels does correlate with them being shards of the Pure, but clashes a little with magick bing purely faith.

                Interestingly, if Angels were "The Pure" that means "the Wyck" (the mythical ancestors of Mages acording to the Verbena and old OOC lore, lore that's still reffered on M20, btw) were half-angels. The Wyck would be what Demon calls the Nephilim. Which doers correlate with Mages being the remanants of the Babel Experiment (as Demon ST handbook suggests), but in a more involved way than what one could fathom from that alone.

                *Then again, when talking about the "truth of the universe", remember than mages should be allowed to understand it however they want when making Paradigms and such. I would make so there's enough leeway and obscurity to believe, at least, in the "core" philosophies of each major faction.

                Remember that while Demons come integrated with a set of "truths", these are obscured in the very book trough Demons having lost a lot of memories, and having their very essence warped by the Abyss. So not everything has to align to a T

                Meanwhile, as I see it Mage doesn't place much weight on what the truth is, as long as it can be reinterpreted in many different ways, and as long as "humanity" has - at least potentially - a say about how the world looks like.

                I would recomend to think about how are you going to relate the splats at the level they're going to interact

                *What about The Revelation?, how is that going to work on Mages, taking into account that "belief" it's very important?. The way I handled that it's that Demons look as "something very important and holly" allrigth - but that something it's to be interpretted within Paradigm. it could be Angels, it could be Aliens

                *How does True Magick(tm) affect Demons and their powers?. There's a lot to unpack here, from what Spheres affect Demons to if Faith can be "translated" to Quintessence. One thing that could be very important to make both Demon and Mage antagonists it's wheter mages can destroy, or at least imprission demons. Demons with healty faith networks are very hard to get rid off, so this is going to come out

                *The Umbra it's barely touched on Demon. To the point one can safely ignore al of it but the Dark one. Now you have 3 layers of Penumbra + the DigiWeb + deeper worlds. What do the Demons do with all of this? Maybe the "layers of reality" thing didn't ceased to be, after all, just are on a different place ... Moreover, from a mechanical pov, if Mages are going to actively interact with multiple layers of Otherworlds Demons may want to follow

                Narratively, let's find commonalities (common antagonists, as proposed before, are a good idea):

                *Earthbound are each one a "Cthulhu cult" unto themselves, and one could explore how that interwoves with how mages try to cultivate beliefs in their area

                Îf you want to explore The Lore, it also offers interesting things like exploring the remanants of ancient cults and civilizations, that probably had their own magick and inklings on the workings of the cosmos

                *Raveners, some Earthbound, and Nephandi have a lot of overlap in their goals.

                It could also be interesting to think about if the "friendlier" factions of Mages and Demons could share some goals.
