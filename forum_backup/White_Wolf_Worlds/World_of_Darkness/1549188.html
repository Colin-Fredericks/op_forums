    Dark Ages high-powered character and splat combination for solo play

    03-12-2025, 10:49 AM
    In the last few years, I have been involved with Jumpchain CYOAs. For those not in the know, it is a kind of extended solo game where your universe-hopping avatar experiences many adventures (Jumps) in a lot of different fictional settings of your choice, both generic genres and specific franchises, collecting powers, skills, items, and companions along the way, until the character ultimately transcends as a multiverse-roaming godlike entity. In-game justification is a godlike entity (the Benefactor) supports the character's interdimensional adventures for their own amusement.

    Some time ago I added Ars Magica, Mage the Ascension, and 'omnibus' versions of the nWoD/oWoD settings to my Chain (sequence of Jumps), since there were a few of the many fanmade Jump scenarioes that covered them. According to my playstyle tastes, I preferred to do so with a high-powered hybrid of various supernatural templates. Recently a Jump scenario has been released that covers the Dark Medieval setting, so I am going to add that Jump to my Chain too. Ofc I am going to take my builds for ArM, MtA, and the 'omnibus' modern oWoD as inspirations. According to the latter, my character was an OP mix of Kindred, Fera, Mage, Changeling, and Kuei-Jin in modern nights, with enough freebie points to be the equivalent of an Archmage/Methuselah in each splat.

    I am considering to do something quite similar for the DA setting with the same OP standard. Ofc the cornerstone of my build shall be a combo of Mage, Fae, and Fera to begin with. Their metaphysical natures, abilities, and goals seem to mix well with relatively little effort.The coexistence of Awakened, Fae, and Fera natures in one being does not seem much difficult to justify and manage once Jumpchain fiat removes the 'only one major supernatural type' limitation. According to my preferred version of Jumpchain rules, my DA build is going to created from scratch at insertion as a kind of supernatural chimera with the Jumper's mind plugged in, with no established background, same as in the ArM, MtA, oWoD, and nWoD Jumps.

    DA Mage brings a lot of versatility, sheer power, and overall potential to the character, esp. if you choose your Foundation(s) and Pillars wisely. My by far preferred option here is a combo of the Old Faith and the Order of Hermes in paradigm, mindset, and power terms alike. I tend to assume that to pick more than two Fellowships would be more distraction than it is worth for diminishing power returns. Otherwise, my likely third option would be the Spirit Talkers.

    DA Fae are more or less the equivalent of mages in terms of sheer powers and versatility, and orders of magnitude better than modern Changelings. Solstice is probably my preferred Court option to be neutral about the Court-rivalry drama. As it concerns type, I am more than a little uncertain between Firstborn and Changeling. The former fits my tastes better (a more powerful Fae nature that is more congenial for Unleashing) but maybe its combination with the other splats is more difficult to justify in metaphysical terms. The latter makes sharing a body with the other supernatural natures somewhat easier but I am less enthused about its limitations. OTOH, a sizable part of those limitations can be overcome with enough freebies/Xp.

    Being a Fera is excellent to compensate for the physical limitations of the other splats. Unlike mage, fae, and vampire powers, I mean to rely on Fera inborn abilities rather than Gifts and rituals for this component of the build since I mostly deem the latter a waste of my time. A combo of familiarity, metaplot relevance, and preferred mage/fae options drives the build to be mostly European in character except as it concerns a possible Cathayan component. This in turn mandates the Fera part of the build to be a Garou since my other preferred options (Mokole and Bastet) are almost unheard of in DA Europe. If those affiliations instead were mostly East Asian, Zhong Lung or Khan would have likely been my preferred option.

    Kindred is a quite possible and even likely but slightly less certain addition to the package. It comes with a balanced mix of inborn abilities and specialized powers. It seems slightly more difficult to justify in metaphysical terms, but not overly so. Since Jumpchain character creation involves in-game backing by a godlike entity, we can certainly assume such a divine fiat forces the Avatar, the Fae soul, and the Fera essence to co-exist with the Curse of Caine w/o any long-term negative effects (take this, Blood Treachery). The analogy here would be with an optimized Lich ritual or a complete success for what the Tremere were trying to do. Low-Generation Caitiff would be my preferred option here since I do not want to be bound by Clan limitations. In terms of preferred Disciplines, however, this component of the build is going to resemble a mix of Brujah, Toreador, and Ventrue. Since I greatly dislike the limitations of high Generation, I am going to make it as low as possible short of burdening the build with Methuselah's Thirst. Say a 4th-6th Generation that was recently spawn into being. Since I dislike playing a high-Humanity or religious character, my preferred Roads are Beast, King, or Sin in this rough order of preference.

    Cathayan is the last potential addition to the admixture that I am contemplating. Its power options seem worthwhile. OTOH, I am concerned I might complicate things overmuch by adding this component from a wholly different metaphysic to an otherwise European set of options, esp. in a premodern setting. If this gets done, since the Kuei-jin are a specialized kind of Risen, nothing short of sheer Benefactor fiat seems up to justify it.

    Demon seems far too much trouble to try adding it to the mix, in the Dark Medieval and modern settings alike. The only partial exception might be to pick some Thrall trait that allows the character to be empowered by sin (Lust, Wrath, and/or Pride are the options I fancy). The other splat options do not interest me and/or do not have enough coverage in Jumpchain terms or for the DA setting.

    A major advantage of doing the oWoD the Jumpchain way is that the character, being an avatar of the player, usually comes with a complete set of in-game metaknowledge for any fictional setting they visit, barring amnesia Drawbacks I tend to shun. Given this and my playstyle and metaplot preferences, the by far most important concern for my Jumper shall be to prevent the decline of magic and wonder, and the rise of the Technocracy, Paradox, Banality, the Masquerade, and an hostile Consensus by any means necessary. The Craftmasons, Gabrielites, and other precursors of the Order of Reason are going to be crushed with extreme prejudice if my Jumper has a choice. The Shadow Inquisition is going to have the same fate, and Jumper is going to try and lessen the power and influence of the Church as much as possible. Although they are not theoretically hostile to the survival of the Messianic Voices in a diminished, more tolerant, and less overbearing version, much as it happens in the Glastonbury Compact but with a much wider reach. Other relevant but less pressing concerns involve containing Wyrm corruption (not much of an issue anyway in this age), and minimizing the vicious and self-defeating squabbles of the various splats (such as the War of Princes and the War of Seasons) to a degree that does not threaten Jumper's other objectives.

    One obvious possibility is to make the Craftmasons at Mistridge lose and be crushed. In all likelihood, it would cause the faction to be disbanded or destroyed, and without their role, chances are the Order of Reason never gets organized. Another possibility is to bomb the 997 Gathering of the Square that set up the Craftmasons and their nascent alliance with other proto-Order of Reason groups in the first place, although that seems less easy to accomplish. Much the same way, the Gabrielites have to be purged as well.

    Yet another option is to have House Tremere destroyed before or soon after it turns to vampirism. This might happen because their attempt to destabilize the Order of Hermes with the Schism War is discovered and they are singled out as the rogue House instead of the Diedne, and Marched. From my PoV, this would be optimal since it means the Diedne thrive and stay an integral part of the OoH, leading to improved relations with the Old Faith, other 'pagan' Fellowships, and the fae. Alternatively, their turn to vampirism is discovered before they had a real chance to adapt Awakened magic to blood sorcery (or for Tremere to Diablerize Saulot), allowing the OoH to destroy them with ease.

    In all likelihood, if the OoH does not have the face the major distraction and attrition of the First Massasa War, they would notice the potential threat of the Craftmasons and crush them with relative ease. OTOH, a Cainite society without the Tremere Clan may evolve in ways that I find difficult to fathom.

    To crush the Muslim and East Asian pro-reason sects, such as the Court of Sacred Sciences and the Chinese Dragons may well be warranted in order to prevent the Technocracy from rising all the way again, just with Muslim or Chinese trappings in place of European ones.

    Not directly related to the Technocracy issue, but it might also be good for Tradition mages to find ways to tone down inter-Fellowship hostility and achieve some kind of cooperation between Fellowships not unlike the Council of Traditions even without the Order of Reason threat as a unifying element, devise appropriate magitech replacement for advanced technology, and prevent Commoners from becoming a serious threat to supernaturals. For the purpose of this scenario, supernaturals staying or becoming the ruling elites of the world is supposed to be a good thing from Jumper's PoV, within reasonable limits of course. Something like a repeat of the Impergium is bad, but having a functional and benevolent magocracy or necrocracy is fine.

    As it concerns the fae, I suppose to prevent a restart of the fratricidal War of Seasons is likely warranted. Getting rid of the Technocracy likely does away with the rise of Banality, although it may not suffice and to weaken the Church considerably may be needed as well. I am not sure what vampires or werewolves may need.

    Yet another potential flashpoint to wipe out the Technocracy may be to destroy the Convention of the White Tower in 1325, although I am concerned by then the nascent Order of Reason had already got too momentum by then for its rise to be disrupted.

    Comments? Opinions? Suggestions?

              *Do you gain the weaknesses of the Splats alongside their powers?. I'm particularly eyeing at vampires being weak to sunlight, sleepy at day, and having to contend with the Beast - if the most important thing you get from it it's immortality, you could gain that via purchasing the Unaging Merit instead. I'm not sure the other powers are worth the disadvantages, considering you already have a massive amount of powers at your disposal

              *I'm uncertain at what point it's "difficult to justify in metaphysical terms" a problem, but let's think a little because it's fun: A werewolf-mage-vampire it's already possible. Not just by Sam's example, but also because vampires of the Black Hand can borrow an Avatar trough Disciplines or being given one trough Pillar magick (and if a vampire can an). The "trick" seems to be that the Avatar and the Vampire soul never really reside on the same host. Tha Avatar it's either cozy in the body of their mage acting as a conduit, or inside a legendary artifact. And if it works for vampirism, it would probably work for Faes and pure Fera too. It never happened, it wasn't tried either, but it doesn't seem like these splats are *worse* for Avatar's health.

              So, a "metaphysicaly more acceptable way" could be the Benefactors crafting an item, or a place, to hold the Avatar. A problem it's that these "options" don't allow one to further advance as a mage. Maybe not much of a problem if you start with enormous freebies. But still requiring consideration

              *Fera-Vampire combinations are horribly cursed. Abominations are trully worthy of their name.

              You're half-spirit, but now you have most of the spirit world as your enemy (well, that kinda comes with being a vampire, but it's worse if you actually care for the Umbra).

              Also Abominations don't get to chose a neat Road and call it a day, they loose themselves and need to follow the Wyrm to avoid it. Now, maybe the Benefactors can get rid of that problem, again, I don't know how much "difficult to justify in metaphysical terms" really matters.

              *Some Gifts can be very practical. And with Gifts you need to think that you don't need to go trough a tree of potentially useless stuff to get them (assuming that, just like you don't need a mentor for your other powers, you get to cherry-pick here too)

              For instance, Spirit of the Fray gives such a massive bonus to Initiative that you almost always act first in combat. I'm not sure you can easily replicate it with other powers w/o wasting resources that could go elsewhere. And could be combined with simple divination magicks that prevents surprise attacks.
              Even if you plan to avoid combat like the plague (the wise path) it could happen (esp. since you plan to get into a lot of trouble), and acting first it's never not going to be good unless your prep it's so OP that you erradicate the opposition before the turn begins

              This is especially true if you decide that ditching "vampire" it's tempting, since some Gifts could provide alternatives to the benefits of those Disciplines you don't want to waste your dinamic powers replicating. But even if you don't, some could be of use.

              For the killing spree:

              Terminating the Tremere it's overall a good idea if you can do it. I'm positive the world it's going to be a better place. They didn't just reinforced the Order of Reason, they also manipulated the Inquisition against their vampire enemies, partially provoking it's rise in power. Even if you get rid of the Craftmassons in a different way, the Tremere are going to find some other way to cause trouble.

              Probably your idea of changing the outcome of the Schism War event it's the easiest way of getting rid of that plague before they become too powerful.

              Either way, to deal with the rise of the Technocracy there is a named character you may want to get acquainted with: Wolfgang Von Reismann Not much it's said about him in the Wiki, unfortunately. But this guy founded two Factions. And not just any factions, but the Craftmasons and, after founding the OoR, the High Guild that were it's main sustainers. Not a small feat. And he did it w/o being too powerful a mage himself, at that - pure ambition and social manipulation.

              This guy has an obsession: immortality. That, and not altrusim like you could be led to think, it's his main motivation. In a world where, much as Tremere suspected, magick was becoming harder, Reismann sought all the forces of science he could find to curb death.

              If you could convince him that a world of technomancy ain't the way to do it (in fact, it wasn't: He dies before the propper founding of the Technocracy, passing the mantle to Reginald Proctor, the founder of the Syndicate, if I'm not mistaken), you could actually *curb* the forming of the Technocracy in a much more holistic way, deviating it's interest in science for misticism, or maybe a more balanced view of science and magick.

              That may be overall better to balance the world of wizardry that just killing him. And may be of help in the case merely ending the Technocracy's career doesn't happen to be enough to prevent the "fall of wonder", that's your main goal, and AFAIK was started much before the Technocracy was formed (since the discovery of Iron?) and has other factors going on for it that may be harder to tackle (such as Lucifer wanting to spread reason because he tought that was going to help figthing the Earthbound, if I'm not mistaken)

                  Theoretically speaking, I found another possible way to combine supernatural templates gainfully. In this case, to stack a minor one (Demon Thrall) with several major ones (Vampire, Mage, Garou, and/or Fae). Rather than being part Demon, which seems more trouble than it is worth, to be a Thrall seems a viable and gainful option, esp. if you make good use of the Merit that lets you be w/o a patron, b/c the demon lost interest in you, got banished back to Hell, was destroyed, or released you. Alternatively, you can get close to the same result to a lesser degree with the other Merit that makes your patron deem you too valuable to abuse or waste you.

                  This seems esp. true for Vampire and/or Mage, because you can harness a considerable amount of additional power through appropriate Arcana to supplement your Foundation, Pillars, and Disciplines at no real harm for your Awakened powers. Demonic powers often can replace or stack with Disciplines and considerably increase physical abilities or lessen the frailty of the human body, nullifying one of the main vulnerabilities of a mage. Admittedly, much less so for age, where Arcana can just give a thrall a brief respite.

                  Vampires and mages can also buy Discipline, Foundation, or Pillar dots as Arcana. Moreover, there is a special Arcanum that can make the vampiric Beast wholly tamed and passive, except when purposefully roused by the demonic patron. If one effectively has no patron thanks to the appropriate Merit, that is close to 'Dark Golconda' with no arduous quest to achieve it and no harsh Humanity requirements to keep it. Sure, you shall have to get extra Taints and/or do nasty deeds to pay for the stuff, and manage your relationshup with your patron, but this is par the course and seems viable, esp. for characters and players that are uncaring of moral issues.

                  On the other hand, to be a Thrall seems more troublesome for a Garou, since they usually register as Wyrm-tainted to other Fera. Although this seems less of a problem for a hybrid character that does not seek to be a part of Garou society. It is a much more serious problem for a Fae, since demonic powers (Arcana) proportionally nerf and diminish Fae ones (the Mist rating) in a changeling. There seems to be no effective min-maxing loophole around this incompatibility issue, short of creating a special ad-hoc houserule to lessen, counter, or remove it by Jumpchain means through the fanwank leeway that Jumpchain players ultimately have from the system. Otherwise, I tend to assume it would be a bad choice, since Fae abilities are among the most powerful, versatile, and useful in the DA setting. To nerf them for the sake of demonic powers would be a very bad bet.

                  Everything good (physical enhancement, removal of human weaknesses, emulation of Disciplines) Arcana can do for you, you can get just as effectively by combining vampiric, were, and/or fae abilities to appropriate degrees, including access to immortality by being Kindred and/or Fae. Cheap access to vampiric and/or mage powers is not that valuable if Jumpchain rules allow you to tap effectively unlimited freebie points for any supernatural splat. Admittedly, tamed Beast for a vampire is one heck of a boon.

                  DA books give no hint whatsoever about how to manage the case of being a Kuei-Jin thrall, likely because the two splats belong to normally extraneuous metaphysics barring Jumpchain fiat to combine different plats or the unlikely but possible case of a Cathayan and a Demon meeting thanks to travel.

                      Lots of good ideas, suggestions, and remarks, thank you. This was the kind of constructive feedback I was looking for. Let's address a few of your points.

                      Yes, it seems you do get the splats' weaknesses alongside their powers, unless there are Jumpchain Perks that do away with specific ones. E.g. in the Dark Ages jumpdoc there are Perks that restore knowledge of how to craft fae Gold Oaths, make a vampire look fully human, and prevent a kuei-jin from suffering imbalance or having their powers vulnerable to Age degeneration. The MtA one has the equivalent of Unaging, Perks to make physical, mental, and social abilities peak human ones or somewhat better across the board, and a Drawback - a prized Perk in my eyes - that makes the Consensus accepting of low-level sci-fi, urban fantasy, and horror stuff at the price of making the Ascension War Hot. The Omnibus oWoD one has Perks to resist supernatural insanity, confusion, or fear, lessen supernatural weaknesses, make the mind resilient to Karma-meter degeneration, and make all power pools naturally regenerating.

                      Alternatively, sometimes one splat's powers can counter another one's weaknesses. E.g. the physical resilience of a vampire or a were can counter the human frailty of a mage or a changeling in human form, or the immortality of a vampire or a fae can counter the mortality of a mage or a were.

                      Yes, to import certain M20 or C20 Merits in the DA setting can be an effective stopgap to the physical frailty or mortality of those splats. For that matter, however, DA fae are immortal by default, so to be part one can be another valid solution besides being part vampire. That is going to be the case until and unless rising Banality forces them to retreat to the Dreaming or undergo the Changeling Way, and if my Jumper gets their way, that shall never happen. Admittedly, the disadvantages of vampiric nature you quote are among the reasons I am less than fully persuaded and enthused about including the Cainite or Cathayan components in the splat combo.

                      Broadly speaking, I am very reluctant to use the 'Avatar in a phylactery' trick to ensure the coexistence of mage and vampire natures, b/c I'd be extremely paranoid about the item getting damaged, stolen, or lost, and I am rather sceptical about its necessity for various reasons, the Black Hand precedent notwithstanding. E.g. Jumpchain has plenty of Perks to mandate theoretically incompatible or conflicting supernatural abilities coexist w/o issue, and I see no good reason why they shouldn't cover the case of the Avatar and the Curse of Cain. As a rule, Jumpchain rules often allow to sidestep canon setting constraints for a character easily, either by default or through Perks. Moreover, to be a Mage Lich seems very close to be a mage-vampire, and that state is sustainable w/o any need for a phylactery. Yes, Foundation(s)/Arete get frozen, but as you point out, that is no issue if mage powers get maximized from the beginning thanks to effectively unlimited freebies. Last but not least, it was not necessary for Sam. I am entirely confident that the Avatar and Fae/Fera natures can coexist and stack within a person w/o any issue thanks to Jumpchain fiat, also b/c the relevant metaphysics seem less inimical than the mage-vampire case to begin with.

                      I may need to give further thought to whether I deem adding the vampire component to the mix is worth the effort, or the mage-fae-garou combo with a few appropriate Merits & Perks is sufficient and optimal. You may have a point about Abominations.

                      You see, as it concerns management of supernatural abilities I greatly favor systems that allow to do almost everything with a few freeform and versatile powers, such as Mage Pillars/Spheres, Fae Dominions, Ars Magica spontaneous magic, etc. Granular spell-list systems with many narrow power tricks such as Fera Gifts, Mage Rotes, Fae Cantrips, ArM formulaic magic etc. bore and annoy me to no end. I shun them and reliance on them in gaming as much as possible. This is the main reason I could never get myself to touch Exalted and its Charms with a 10-foot pole no matter the hype and my love of OP games, and the skill-tree nature of the system only made things worse. My sceptical to negative attitude to Fera Gifts comes from that. Ofc, this does not mean I cannot accept a few exceptions here and there if there are a few special tricks that are too godd to pass, but they need to be exceptions.

                      No, I do not mean to have my Jumper go out of the way to avoid combat, b/c it would be no fun, it seems futile given my objectives, and it would be inappropriate for Jumper's personality. Rather I mean to be so OP and versatile thanks to the combo of splat powers that combat is no big issue, barring exceptionally powerful enemies or very difficult circumstances.

                      We seem in good agreement about the opportunity of offing the Tremere and giving the Schism War a pro-Diedne outcome. I agree that steering Von Reismann on a different path might be another valid approach to prevent the rise of the Craftmasons and the OoR. Quite possibly, my Jumper is going to stack several approaches about this goal just to be sure. You are correct that in the end, to eliminate the Technocracy is just a means to the end of preventing the fall of magick, mysticism, and wonder. My Jumper is going to deploy any available means necessary to achieve this end, maybe short of large-scale culling the human population back to prehistoric levels. Going too far back in time, say to the transition from the Bronze Age to the Iron Age, seems excessive as well for various reasons. OTOH, moving the point of insertion in the DA setting about 300 years earlier to the early-mid 10th century, to enact the other changes we discussed, seems quite gainful and entirely feasible.

                      Jumper shall have to address all the factors of the 'fall of wonder' within that timeframe as best as possible. E.g. perhaps Lucifer can be persuaded or strongarmed to adopt a different strategy to counter the Earthbound. For the record, my Jumper and I have no issue with a gainful coexistence of non-exclusivist science and reason (of the kind the Techie Traditions shall eventually embody) with magic and mysticism in a science fantasy setting, quite the contrary. Not Jumper's preferred way of doing things for themselves, but YMMV. We are just determined and ruthless about preventing the future the Technocracy would bring by any means necessary. If to have powerful and reliable magic and lots of supernatural wonder means the world stays somewhat scarier for Muggles and the supernaturals stay in charge openly instead of behind the curtains, so be it, ten times over. There is no good reason why mysticism and science cannot be combined to provide a semi-utopian lifestyle that is better than the best of Technocracy's deal. This ofc short of science fantasy hazards, but then too much cozy and peaceful an existence is boring and harmful if you ask my Jumper.
