    A Thanatoic dominated world would be potentially interesting, at least from a story perspective. Everyone is perpetually reincarnating as they slowly work their way toward their potential fated destiny. And the biggest obstacle to doing that is the personal guilt, pain, and other karmic damage one might build up during their lifetime, which will weigh them down in their next incarnation, taking them that much further from their destiny. So the upper ranks of society all around the globe are going to be a class of psychopomps whose sacred duty is to help guide others through any existing trauma so that they can continue to advance. I'm not sure how good of an RPG setting you can make out of that, but it would certainly offer an interesting setting for fiction. Stuff in the vein of Quantum Leap (at least the original; I've not seen any of the remake), the K-drama Tomorrow (about a group tasked by Heaven to prevent suicides), or maybe Person of Interest (just with fate magic instead of super computers).

                    Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post

                    I'm not really an expert on Ars Magica. Most of the books I have are stuff I picked up looking for more information to mine for the Order of Hermes and for general Dark Ages geography and culture.

                    I do know that 5th edition has a book called Rival Magic, which included four new local mystical factions that could be rivals to the Order in certain areas. They were, I think, the Amazons (based on the Greek myths and located on the island of Amazonia in the Caspian Sea), the Muspelli (Nordic Jotun worshipers), the sorcerers of Soqotra (isolationists living on an island in the Arabian Sea), and the Augustan Brotherhood (a secret society of court wizards who practice their own style of Roman inherited magic and have the ultimate aim of trying to reform the old Roman Empire).
                    If you wanted to, you could certainly try to tie said groups to the Sisterhood of Hipolita, Nordic Infernalists/Nephandi, Taftini or Ahl i Batin, and the proto-Order of Reason.

                    The 4th edition book Ultima Thule: Mythic Scandinavia goes into detail about Scandinavian and Norse wizards, including some stuff about Finnish practices.

                    The 4th edition book Kabballah: Mythic Judaism covers various forms of Jewish mysticism, especially in Iberia.

                    The 4th edition book The Dragon & The Bear covers Russia and talks about Slavic, Siberian, and Mongol magic.

                    There are four books about the Islamic world. 4th edition's Blood & Sand (the Levant), and 5th edition's Between Sand and Sea (North Africa), Lands of the Nile (Egypt, Nubia, and Ethiopia), and The Cradle and The Crescent (Mesopotamia and Persia). Each talks about certain mystical traditions native to their regions.

                    And the 5th edition book The Contested Isle covers Ireland, and has a little bit of stuff about druids and other Irish mythic traditions.
                    Thank you for this, Once I will have enough time and finances then I will certainly check out Ars Magica books, and the mentioned chapters and descriptions. I had a definite feeling that his game and setting would have some unique and particular groups, guilds, and factions.

                    It is nice and curious to see the Amazons and citizens of Scandinavia having definite and clear representation and portrayal.

                                                It's also something pointed out in Guide to the Traditions: while they started out as a highly fractious lot, the Traditions have cultivated a “principle of harmony” over the centuries, and are now the primary proponents of the notion that all kinds of Magick are just different flavorings of the same thing. This gives them common ground that the disparate factions lack, while similarly avoiding the conformity that the Technocracy imposes through its Conditioning.

                                                At least, that's the Traditional narrative. What hasn't been tested is what happens when and if the Union is taken out of the picture. Do the Traditions continue to uphold their harmonic ideal? Or do they gradually fall back into petty squabbling? If the latter, does that eventually turn into a power struggle among the Traditions, with the possibility of one emerging as the dominant force?

                                                There are those who argue that the whole “we're all doing the same thing” notion is an invention of the Order of Hermes, and that accepting that premise is essentially subsuming the other Traditions under the Hermetics; that what's needed is for each Tradition to seek out its own authentic truth and to go its own way. Such attitudes are very similar to the views held by the bulk of the Disparates, which is why in my version of the setting they're a minority view among the Traditions; I like to keep the Traditions and the Disparates distinct from each other.

                                                But I could definitely see a possible future where the Traditions slowly fall apart, and eventually they all end up joining the Disparates. That wouldn't be “the Tradition in control”, which is what this thread is supposed to be about; but it would certainly enhance the possibility of one (former) Tradition gaining dominance.

                                                My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
