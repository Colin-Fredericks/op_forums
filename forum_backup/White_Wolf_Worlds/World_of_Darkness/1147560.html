    Time Magic/Paradox Question

    10-06-2017, 04:21 PM
    In our on-going campaign a player sped time in their sanctum so that it passed within at an increased speed (60 times normal). The effect only lasts for 1 month (divided successes). Since this causes five years to pass within the span of one month my thoughts are that any person residing within when the effect ends would be subject to massive paradox. However I do not want to be too harsh and so thought I would ask the community. What are your opinions?

                  I think that a person entering a building and exiting a month later 5 years older is within the realm of Coincidence, since that's a time frame that isn't as noticeable in people. Some people can end up looking years older or younger because of changes in their stress levels, lifestyle, health issues, or even how they apply make-up. Unless we're talking about child characters - for whom 5 years can mean the world of difference - a Hypothetical Average Observer might not notice how the character has aged, or might easily rationalize it if they did. It's been a month, after all. Anything can happen in a month. The only hitch I can think of is that if a character lets their hair grow out for all that time, it might raise questions. So the characters should make sure they've cut it to a length that's reasonable for one month's growth, or just make it shorter and imply a haircut.

                              Well.

                              For the mage, that are still five years passing. This is important to remember. From their point of view, they are spending *Five years!* in that sanctum.
                              I'd go for some serious willpower rolls if they are even remotely social, to see if they last that long in there. And even if they manage to stay, five years of isolation changes people. Probably for the worse, too.


                              I also hope they have enough food in that Sanctum, and as I said enjoy not having contact with the rest of humanity for that time from their point of view. I'm sure the boss will want to know where they went, too, for that whole month.

                              Really, this only works for antisocial characters that have no ties to anyone or anything except maybe their cabalmates (if they are also present), don't want to interact with sleepers, have no obligations to their traditions they need to take care of, hold no strict job, have no mundane belongings that needs regular tending, and..well. Are pretty much utter hermits. Life of the party. Last but not least they need to be lucky for the Avatar not to scream utter rage into their ears.

                              And speaking of Avatars, gaining Arete or Spheres is not a function of simply time passing. It takes enlightenment. Study. Mentoring. Revealed knowledge and trials.
                              And all of that often enough hardly works in isolation, nor is pulling away from the world enlightening in most cases.

                              EDIT:
                              To add, this is the kind of stuff Archmages pull off that have lost their ties to most of the world already, and are urgently trying to solve deeper mysteries without the world continuing to turn. There is also a reason Archmages are often considered too narrow-minded and focused on a thing they obsess about, to actually head for Ascension anytime soon.

                              That said, slowing down time in a Sanctum *Can* be a great tool to solve a problem that has a very very narrow and hard time limit. It's a good idea for that.
                              But spending five years in there is..not something I see most willworkers willing (heh) or able to do.

                                                      I agree with Aleph. Characters - not just Masters, but any character - gaining greater understanding by retreating from the world is a venerable meme, in many different magical styles.
