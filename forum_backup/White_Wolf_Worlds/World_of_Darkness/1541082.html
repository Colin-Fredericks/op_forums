    Dtf Revamp(redemon) ideas

    08-27-2024, 05:08 PM
    A lot of ink has been spilled over the idea that Demon: The Fallen while considered a good game by many simply does not fit into the larger World of Darkness.
    I'm making this thread to ask you guys how'd would you revamp the game to fit in with the rest of game lines.

    ​​​You can share any ideas; I only ask you folxs to stray too far from the Abrahamic themes of the game in your modifications. Otherwise you should go crazy fixing stuff your thinking it's wrong with it

      Originally posted by Nicolas Milioni View Post

      ​​​You can share any ideas; I only ask you folxs to stray too far from the Abrahamic themes of the game in your modifications. Otherwise you should go crazy fixing stuff your thinking it's wrong with it
      I'm assuming you meant not to stray too far, because we can always go... further afield.

      First thing's first, layered reality didn't help in trying to play with others. As a concept, it just felt like trying to cram everything together in a vain attempt to not step on any toes, but the demons did that anyway by claiming that they made everything. It's going to need to own its BS, just like all the other game lines did.

      Secondly, the Middle and High Umbrae exist, and D:tF's gotta deal with it. Else, it's just going to make it look even more like the demons are primarily just filtering the outlook of their hosts, who are cut off from most other Umbral sides of things and preoccupied heavily with the weight of human mortality.

      Thirdly, you're going to need to dial the Earthbound back a fair bit. Definitely from that whole "responsible for all other beliefs that are not Abrahamic*" thing, also likely from the standpoint of being the end-all, be-all apocalyptic threats. Grandmother Thunderwyrm could eat their vessels without realizing it.

      * Given the specific fallen angel conceit that D:tF uses, it's not even broadly Abrahamic, but specifically Christian. Very, very Christian, and from the depths of Paradise Lost over anything explicit in writing from the Bible to boot.

                          I think some of the problems are more from the lack of further material than actual aspects of the game. Not that they couldn't or shouldn't have been addressed in the core or any of its few other books, but they're more a matter of unsaid stuff.

                          The big problem, though, is how to reconcile its Christian take with the broader setting and a more open interpretation.

                          Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post


                          First thing's first, layered reality didn't help in trying to play with others. As a concept, it just felt like trying to cram everything together in a vain attempt to not step on any toes, but the demons did that anyway by claiming that they made everything. It's going to need to own its BS, just like all the other game lines did.
                          The idea behind the layered reality is already similar to how Mage works with the Umbra. I think instead of getting rid of it, it could be a better idea to get rid of the notion that their existence comprised all of them.

                          So the angels could have been the creators of a world, but definitely not of all of them, and maybe they can't even tell apart which is which.


                          Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post
                          Secondly, the Middle and High Umbrae exist, and D:tF's gotta deal with it. Else, it's just going to make it look even more like the demons are primarily just filtering the outlook of their hosts, who are cut off from most other Umbral sides of things and preoccupied heavily with the weight of human mortality.
                          Here's one point where I think it has more to do with things going unsaid than being actual aspects of the game. As far as I understand it, it's less that they don't deal with the Umbra, and more that the book doesn't.

                          It isn't new. Vampire has Auspex 5 and Spiritus as examples of powers that deal with the Umbra, but never actually call on it even in later editions. It's easy to assume that Demons can interact with the Umbra even if their core book never calls on it.

                          By the way, not addressing the Umbra was an error. It was an error for Vampire and an even bigger one for Demon.

                          Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post
                          Thirdly, you're going to need to dial the Earthbound back a fair bit. Definitely from that whole "responsible for all other beliefs that are not Abrahamic*" thing, also likely from the standpoint of being the end-all, be-all apocalyptic threats. Grandmother Thunderwyrm could eat their vessels without realizing it.
                          Even naming the generals by well known "demons" may have been an error, as most or all of them are actually gods being badly understood by Hebrews and then demonized by Christians.

                          It plays a lot of nice buttons for familiarity, but maybe sticking to made-up angelic names would have been a better idea.

                          From that, it would be a matter of making their cults just demon cults, and later godly identity theft shenanigans, like the vampire "gods".

                          Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post
                          * Given the specific fallen angel conceit that D:tF uses, it's not even broadly Abrahamic, but specifically Christian. Very, very Christian, and from the depths of Paradise Lost over anything explicit in writing from the Bible to boot.
                          Definitely. In fact, for at least some of those that like it, like me, this is even an integral part of the experience. There's something cathartic for me in playing a rebel against the Christian god, even not being a Christian myself.

                          But it has nothing to do with the bible, or with other Abrahamic faiths.

                                  In Houses of the Fallen, on the page 65, in the Chapter Three: The House of Fire and Stone section there is this cute little line:

                                  "While they [the Artificers] did speak with the spirits of the stone and earth, of electron and molecule, they did not create those spirits. They simply allowed them to form naturally around the material.
                                  While the other Elohim worked in the spirital realms, the ethereal layers of reality, the Artificers stay in the material realm to fashion the stuff of Creation"

                                  The Artificers, the Angels of Fundament were aware of the existence of spirits, even were able to speak with them!
                                  They were probably not really bothered to "notice" other spirits, just the ones that were connected to their work and area of expertise (and later, as the chapter said "...gluing together hydrogen and oxygen to privide the Angels of he Deep with oceans and rivers" they probably noticed the spirits of these watery elements too)
                                  Even with the strong abrahamic fundation and background of the game the Umbra and Spirits were/are present in Demon: The Fallen!
                                  Just never really mentioned
