    "Crack" Speed: Additional Action through stimulant use

    10-05-2019, 01:56 PM
    At a minimum, in both Revised and M20 (as well any number of the other storyteller games), stimulants such as cocaine, meth, crack, etc., have had the practical in-game effect of giving its user an additional action per turn--the downside being bashing damage, impaired judgment, etc. However, the fact remains that this is a non-magical means of getting an extra action, and therefore has two pretty serious implications: first, that operating at a speed that provides an extra action is something the human body can just do; and second, that if this is something that (unfortunately) plenty of people get into, it would probably be coincidental to take a single additional physical action every turn.

    Unless we're going to posit that there's something inherently magical about cocaine (and I'm sure we could find someone who would agree with that lol), the fact is that the drug cannot by definition allow a human being to do something superhuman. This raises the question: why couldn't you just use Life 3 to make your body do this without having to do the coke. In other words, what stops a mage with Life 3 from simply making an effect that reproduces the specific cell signaling that gives the extra action, but without having it addle the brain and so forth? Certainly if you can grow claws or wings with Life 3, making the body do something it already has the inherent capability to do should be far far easier. Having said that, I could see the taking of bashing health levels as part and parcel, if you consider that perhaps pushing human metabolism in this way would be inherently damaging to the body, but the impaired decision making and so forth is almost without question attributed to the psychological and neurological effects of flooding the brain with dopamine--and this Life 3 effect would circumvent the need for the dopamine dump by simply signaling along the necessary pathways directly (in overly biological terms--so if you're a Progenitor doing his--rather than the brain signaling the metabolism and extremities to "push" themselves, the magic would simply trigger the effect that would have been created by the cell signal at the sites of the needed boost). Sort of like how you can turn off the light in your refrigerator without closing the door by simply pushing down the button that typically gets depressed (thereby turning off the light) when the door closes.

    The downstream consequence of this is of course that this is unambiguously a form of extra action that is purely physical in nature... and Time 3 unambiguously provides extra actions by manipulating time--i.e., your body doesn't actually move faster, you simply move faster relativistically because of the manipulation of the flow of time. In other words, if you're employing both effects, it follows that you would double the benefit of Time 3 time dilation.

    I'm curious to know how other people handle this; I don't realistically expect that anyone wouldn't house rule this away (at my table, even attempting this unravels a mage's pattern instantly!), but I think this interaction of the rules as-written is sound.

      Any mortal can take additional actions if they divide their dice pools. You can shoot twice in one round, you'll just probably miss.

      "Extra actions" are good because they let you do lots of stuff without penalties. So getting "coked up" before you decide to shoot some Vampires COULD be a good idea.

      Regardless, I'd definitely say that 1 extra action per round is coincidental. And depending on the method you might get away with 2; if you use a visibly extremely fit body as a focus, perhaps throw in dramatically snorting some cocaine with lots of drunk witnesses, well it's probably coincidental to do 3 actions in a single round ;-)

      As for replicating the effects of cocaine using Matter or Life or similar, it comes down to an issue of Sphere division. You could argue that with Forces you could accelerate your body to incredible speeds and thereby gain extra actions, with Forces 2 only. It's just that it isn't allowed, because "I'm using Forces to take extra actions" is actually a sort of mistake on the player's part. What they're actually TRYING to do is use Time to take extra actions, and the flavour they're giving the effect is making it look like Forces. They still need Time. There are probably Mages who don't conceive of a separation between Time and Forces, and Mages who don't think of a separation between Time and Correspondence, but the distinction in Spheres on the sheet represents a devotion of study. Maybe they are one and the same, but if they are one thing it's a thing that has multiple areas to study.

      I digress. Using Life or whatever to replicate the effects of cocaine on your body to take extra actions is coming back to this Time vs Forces issue; you do need Time to do this specific effect. However the justification within a Mage's paradigm can be very "life-y". The Life-focused Verbena is more likely to talk about awakening an inner beast that dances faster than the eye can perceive than they are to talk about slowing the hands of time. An Etherite might say that they reverse-accelerated the chronosphere to devolve their body into a dinosaur form, but that doesn't mean they can do Life 5 using Time 5 if that makes sense.

      If in doubt, refer to A Bit of Spirit from the (mostly) good How Do You Do That. The Shaman doesn't think he's using Life to heal; he thinks he's using Spirit. Everything is Spirit. Summoning fire, healing people, turning back time. But he doesn't have a single Sphere that covers everything. He has many Spheres which cover those different effects. There is some nuance; he can use Spirit 5 to try (haha) to force Wrinkle to reverse time, if he's utterly insane. But if he wants conventional time-reversal he's stuck using the Time Sphere, which to him is a sort of "Spirit Sphere but just for controlling the Spirit of Time".

          The hard line vulgarity of extra actions was always a bit questionable.

          That said, if you want to replicate the distinction between Life and Time, you can leave full extra physical actions to Time (oddly, Mind allows limited extra actions as well), and allow Life (and maybe Mind depending on the nature of the action) to reduce the penalties of multiple actions; though that was easier in Revised than M20.

          A fairly basic sounding effect that would certainly be coincidental and not rely on Time? Using the higher pool before splitting rather than the lower pool.

          If you want to value the Sphere distinctions here, it seems simple enough to get close to the same results without actually overlapping here.

              Note, the ruling in M20 regarding extra actions (as in, Time 3) is more lenient when it comes to vulgarity, at least according to HDYDT:
              It pretty much says "One or two extra actions may be passed off as coincidental bursts of speed; three or more extra actions per turn move the mage into vulgar magick territory."

              Which makes sense, really. It's not extra actions per se that are vulgar, sincs high-skill splitting dicepools or the use of stimulants pretty much can give similiar results..but 3 or more extra actions at full dicepool starts being just unnaturally fast and good.

                  The problem is that it's very... murky... regarding the idea of running the game as HAP based. People can't see your stats or your dice-pools. They have no way to tell if three attacks are a mix of excellent skill and a bit of luck, or magical powers letting you take each shot as if you had three times as much time to shoot.

                  I've been at these games long enough, where I've seen characters no using extra action powers to spam as many attack as possible as desperation tactics only for it to end up dealing massive damage (hell, one time I had a character in a fencing duel to first blood that was out-classed but one initiative, so I spammed attacks and ended up doing 6L on a lucky hit... or well... not so lucky for the context), and I've seen characters with 8 dice and three extra actions whiff four attacks.

                  How does a HAP know the difference?

                  I can buy some arbitrary idea that Time based multiple actions get obvious at 3+ actions because of the amount of distortion, but why it's 3 instead of 4 or 5 or whatever is hard to make any specific case for; even as a balance issue.
