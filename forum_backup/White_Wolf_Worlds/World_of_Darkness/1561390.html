    [20th Editions] Which buffs can become permanent and ideally "free"?

    07-06-2026, 02:19 PM
    Greetings,

    I am trying to find out, for all gamelines, which buffs can be made permanent at no ongoing cost, for example:
    1. Vicissitude 1 / 2. V20 Core p. 241
      Vicissitude 1 for the user, Vicissitude 2 for others. You can get Appearance up to your maximum rate (perhaps beyond that? it is not clear for me if you can get 6+ if you are not a low generation vampire).
      Applicable to anyone I think.
    2. Elixir of Youth. M20 Forgotten and Forbidden Orders p. 109
      Unaging merit with 5+ successes.
      This mainly makes sense for Mages, but maybe the effects can be applied to Changing Breeds? Changelings? Ghouls? Kinfolk?
    3. Rite of the Silver Forge. W20 Core p. 331
      Frenzy on 6 successes rather than 4, +1 permanent Honor renown.
      This only makes sense for Garou, and perhaps Changing Breeds?
    4. Light within Shadow. V20 Lore of the Clans p. 126
      Controllable Night Sight merit. Risk of getting the Uncontrollable Night Sight flaw on a botch.
      Vampires only. Maybe Ghouls with Obtenebration?
    5. Occhio d’Uomo Morto. V20 Core p. 179
      Permanently gain the Shroudsight power of the Ash Path. +1 difficulty against powers requiring eye contact. Appearance is reduced by one dot. Perception rolls are at +1 difficulty​.
      Vampires only. Maybe Ghouls with Necromancy?​
    Any others from any gameline?

    Note: I do not count Claiming the Dark (V20 Lore of the Clans p. 126) as it can be disabled at will, and it cost extra blood points while active.

                  So... the WtA Gift lists are absolutely full of Gifts that are some form of buff either more traditional buffs that are always on (or selectively on) and boost some set of actions, a ton of things that grant you stuff you can't do otherwise but require a roll (though not spending any points), and so on. Finding all of them is a bit of a pain because there are so many. I'm not sure I'd treat any Rite that results in temporary Renown as a buff since you can lose Renown before you can get it rolled over into permanent Renown (and there's no mechanic benefit to having temporary Renown sitting on your sheet besides working to more permanent or making sure you don't lose permanent if you get hit with Punishment rights/do stupid things).

                  In VtM you're jumping right past Potence and Fortitude always having always on buffs with latter additions of spend blood for more, and Celerity eventually getting its own passive buff as well.

                  While not a traditional buff, mages that temporarily buff their Attributes can pay reduced XP costs to permanently raise them with some increased Paradox risks during the process. Nothing technically stops this from mages casting the same buffs on their allies to help them improve their mundane capabilities either (even if the books don't really talk about it that way).

                  You'd also want to decide where magic item crafting falls as a "buff" as most of the game lines that include it have ways to get always on (or very easily activated) ones even if the bonus might not be as big as ones that aren't free to use; but items can get taken away from you.

                                          Not all Fetishes require a roll to activate, or at least have passive benefits all the time and you roll to get more (ex: grand klaives are always functional blades despite being made of silver and always do Agg to anything, you roll for whatever additional power they get).

                                          Non-Gnosis possessing characters types that can use Fetishes consistently use Willpower instead (eg: Spirit mages and vampires that having the right sorcery for dealing with spirits).

                                          The thing with magical items is that in every game where making them is a thing the players are expected to be able to do (esp. if it's a core book thing and not a fairly rare supplement thing like VtM), the books are just examples and characters can move away from what the books look at as "normal" for the default way those splats work for whatever makes more sense for the game being run. Characters in a game with open magical buff trading is a thing are going to develop more stuff that works for any customer even if it's not as good as what they could get for "native" users in most cases because you're moving pretty far from the default as it is and thus priorities are going to change.

                                              Anyway, Mages have too many ways of doing that, and too many buffs to offer. The main problem is that you're looking for a list of options in games made to give you tools, a few examples, and let you build your own things.

                                              Let's see: a Mage offering such a service won't be in the street, will work in the lab under ideal conditions. You can expect it to be a Chantry or Reality Zone where their stuff is Coincidental, period. They also have all their Instruments, all the tools and time and stuff. You can safely assume they work with a -3 Diff modifier.

                                              That's Difficulty equal the Sphere level of the effect. Diff 5 for the most powerful magics. A Master has at least Arete 5 and Willpower 5 (it defines how many rolls in a ritual), that's 25 dice total if they manage at least 1 success per roll. Reasonably easy to get 5 to 10 successes in a ritual.

                                              Hardly a permanent effect will require more than this, at least not simple modifications.

                                              Just to be clear on costs: Mages can give buffs whose cost is making you a Thaumivore. This usually comes along with a natural ability to consume the Quintessence source by yourself, so some people would still be able to use such effects. That gives you a way to access even more powerful effects. A thaumivore can be a vampire, for example, or drain Wonders with a touch.

                                                      Just remember another one that greatly fits the bill, here: Demonic Pacts.

                                                      There are plenty of entities that are demons for all purposes in the WoD, and many can offer a buff or more as a Pact.

                                                      Most rules for that are in Vampire books, there are all sorts of different rules for what a Pact does.

                                                      Fallen from DtF take you Faith Score (which is not the same as True Faith) and turn directly into Bonus Points to buy things, I don't remember the exact rate now.

                                                      It's up to the ST which splats can enter Pacts, but overall it's not hard to justify. Very versatile, and a good contract can be quite safe with the Fallen compared to other demons.

                                                      A Fallen that runs this as a business would be even more likely to play nice for reputation. No real soul is exchanged, most benefits they get don't harm the Thrall, despite the name there's no real obligation to serve unless the Pact says so.

                                                      Your major risk is that the Fallen can drain your Willpower points, but that's not exactly regular. It can possess you without a roll, but must still be near you when without a host (Willpower drain has no limits). Can use some powers on you or near you, but nothing that harms you. All effects are on the table to negotiate, too, since what they want the most is the Faith Points you generate and do nothing with, which means the regular Fallen actually has an invested interest in the wellbeing and long life of their Thralls.

                                                      Of course things aren't usually that simple. But if the point is to have a market, making it that simple there wouldn't break rules or lore, or be too weird as a curious exception.

                                                          Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

                                                          Just to be clear on costs: Mages can give buffs whose cost is making you a Thaumivore. This usually comes along with a natural ability to consume the Quintessence source by yourself, so some people would still be able to use such effects. That gives you a way to access even more powerful effects. A thaumivore can be a vampire, for example, or drain Wonders with a touch.
                                                          I'll add that this is complicated by the type of buff. The subject will need to be a thaumivore if they have to supply quintessence for their buffs and that need comes from two sources. There's quintessence used as fuel and quintessence caused by pattern bleed. The former powers special abilities that could reasonably need a fuel source, like firebreathing or an on demand (as opposed to generating at the same rate as a snake's glands) venomous bite. The latter comes into play when there is a significant deviation between the subject's body and their current pattern.

                                                          I bring the distinction up because M20 says the pattern bleed goes away if the subject's pattern is permanently altered to match their new body, and Life 5 is specifically stated to be capable of permanent changes.

                                                          Of course there's also the Permanent Paradox cost of outlandish changes (which only matters to mages) as well as the effect of unbelief and rejection.
