                        The reason I'm interested in a smart use of Traits is that I actually think this leaning into Clan archetype is excessive. I actively dislike it.

                        I lean heavily on the idea the individual Vampire is first a person with zero reason to follow the culture of the abomination who turned them. Your society not having a place for you doesn't change it is the framework you have for reality and that goes deep.

                        So the whole Toreador are artists, Brujah are thugs and Ventrue are power players for me is a huge ball of bullshit. And that IS the fun, to be thrown in a reality that empowers and curses you into a path that's not your nature.

                        But the archetypes exist for a reason. What so happens is that this reason usually isn't culture because there's little real common culture in a Clan to begin with. My take is that this is the result of details of the Curse that nudge things and then create the space for a stereotype to appear.

                        Exceptions exist, there's nuance, but that's the baseline. Most vampires aren't an expression of the archetype: they're in friction with it, but in different ways. The Ventrue Neonate pay lip service to a cult of "lineage" of a curse, but they are in real danger to life if they don't learn the spiel. A Toreador can't help but be stuck in the spot admiring things they legitimately find boring, and reach others who share in this type of experience to learn coping strategies.

                        My preferred take is that. To ask how does a mundane mind react in response to the curse and its alien "physiology". It so happens that to me a direct change in personality – be it explained as a Beast or as Clan culture – is the least interesting exploration, a blunt tool to turn the vampire into a Planet of the Hats kinda thing that's shallow and asinine.

                        Clan Traits have a chance of doing that since as powers/mutations they're less prescriptive of who you are and more of what you've been empowered to do.

                        What most players do with VtM, in my sincere opinion, is if anything more appropriate to the Japanese take on Vampires: a blood sucking Youkai that has never been something else. A race proper, from crib to final destruction. And I don't think this is a bad take, it's just not what VtM describes and the transition breaks my immersion.

                                GreatHornedRatkin

                                Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                Exceptions exist, there's nuance, but that's the baseline. Most vampires aren't an expression of the archetype: they're in friction with it, but in different ways.
                                Let's break down my take on the matter:

                                1. The core books state repeatedly that kindred are not consistent in Embraces. It's true that as far as they can, the Ventrue will chose the best. But it's also plainly clear that their actual ability to enforce this or make the best choices is limited at best. That goes for most.

                                2. A lot of clans don't do that at the same level, or at all. Those who do either have a limit or go through a excruciating process that still gives us clear friction points and costs. It's not about Clan culture being real or not, but how deep it goes, which isn't a binary.

                                3. "Some of their qualities" and close to their stereotypes are two different things. I lean a lot more in the former than most.

                                4. Low vs High Clans is the single stupidest thing in VDA. Full stop. And this is also false: Toreador, Malkavian and Gangrel royally don't care, Nosferatu are very selective and involve a whole Warren. Tzimisce followed family lines but stopped at that, and the Cappadocians are literally the clan that most Embraced whatever and only barely changed ways after the Feast.

                                5. Friction is the key concept: what you were is in conflict with what other factors press you to be. My interest is in exploring this conflict. You are the nexus of many independent influences and will respond to each while still feeling the others. How that generates idiosyncrasies is what I like to see. In this sense, Clan influence is crucial, don't get me wrong, it just has to not be the end of it.

                                6. A lot of players go strictly Clan stereotype, from Fledgling to Methuselah, that's stupid. But I must add that, for me, the idea of "their mortal days were so long ago they don't play a part anymore" isn't just arguable at best, it is just an uninspired excuse to flatten the character.

                                7. As I'll show, how far a clan goes to make the members fit shouldn't be incidental, but rather a defining trait. That should show up in many more ways than simply being effective.

                                8. Ventrue are not grueling. That wouldn't allow for the infiltration they favor, as they need to be able to incorporate new elites and knowledge constantly. They're also capable at hiding their indiscretions from each other. Ventrue ideal is to become the best of the best. Ventrue reality is that most of them are at best midway to the ideal, and frequently those who best perform the archetype are the farther from its values. I take it from knowing corporate elites and how much their competence is smoke and mirrors.

                                9. Banu Haqim are gruesome. They're also zealots that live collectively in a palace, not a warren for those in the city, a single castle for the whole clan. Or at least did so until recently. They also frequently ask permission to their prospect for the Embrace, they follow dozens of strict rules, and they actively hunt those who don't fit. Not destroy if convenient, hunt. Even then there's plenty of space for differences to appear.

                                10. Giovanni is a prime example for what going through pains to ensure conformity means. It's so strict you're watched by the collective Clan since you're a child. Not Fledgling, a turns-milk-into-poop child. Your sire may even have been your babysitter for a night or two in the past, a monster you met every Sunday at family dinner and in holidays. Even if the Embrace was not a choice, it was family duty rather than external predation. And they conciliate the friction between vampire society and mortal society making both the same god you, there's zero "unlearning" involved.

                                11. Followers of Set were turned into Ministry precisely because they had more schisms than anything and that was not actually v5 invention, it has been in the making since 2nd Edition. And since cults are a real thing that affect real people, I can also point you to the fact that the indoctrination has limits and cult escapees are a thing. By the way, their stories are underused in VtM, and I imagine many from the clan being such people.

                                12. Lasombra Social Darwinism is philosophic trash as it has been repeatedly demonstrated with Social Darwinism in reality. The books do a poor job demonstrating that their spiel does not really correspond to their reality, with many authors believing this bullshit, so let me be clear: if they were competent at doing what they claim, that itself would undermine their uniformity. For them, the stereotype itself is a logical fallacy.

                                13. Tremere don't have such spells, to begin with. Otherwise wouldn't be the most infiltrated Clan in canon. They have strict vigilance, which is key to their concept, but it has serious limits, which is also important. It also doesn't ensure archetype, it ensures obedience, which is a very different thing. It should be where the struggle is the most intense, because it's the Clan that enforces conformity most directly and rigidly through imposition.

                                Final words: I'm not saying my take is the most correct. The books allow many takes, and in fact it is impossible to be 100% true to them as all possible interpretations of canon also require you to place distinct weights in each part.

                                My points, thus, are:
                                1. The books allow the above take fully within canon;
                                2. Many popular takes actually conflict with canon, as it is meant to be nuanced;
                                3. I don't avoid full archetypal integration because I find it unofficial, I avoid it because I don't like it. I like the conflict better. Everything else is accessory to my preference and you should favor yours.

                                                    As for villains, that depends a lot, and it is too easy to look at the matter with a biased selection.

                                                    But the simplest reason why most memorable villains are performative is because they were accessories to another person's story and this accessory has to stand out in the time they get in the spotlight. This convention is mostly for cinema and to a lesser extent TV, as the media favors simplifying narrative elements to convey the message within seconds.

                                                    Most villains we don't even know if they have a justification, there's this tendency of assuming that not being told something in a story means it didn't happened. But when sequels and what have you come, they either avoid talking about the villain or eventually make the sympathetic part, because its lack was actually a narrative need, not a point.

                                                    Genre plays another spin on this, so you'll have a pretty different experience depending on the movies and media you like, and... Gothic is all about sympathetic villains. The genre is built upon the premise that the villain could have been better, and is the ultimate cause of their own tragedy. So VtM actually comes from a tradition of sympathetic villains, and the game is very clearly built to support them.
