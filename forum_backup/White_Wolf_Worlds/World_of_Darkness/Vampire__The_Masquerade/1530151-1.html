                                                    Originally posted by James_Willoughby View Post
                                                    The fan content license is, if I remember, fairly specific, so would not generally apply, for instance, to pictures published on a blog or similar.
                                                    Eh the Dark Pack redux was hugely flawed because it was meant to be one of three different content licenses to cover different kinds of content, but they only did the first and thus all fan content would have to fall under it or not take part in it; which is part of the reason so few fans used it.

                                                    But the fact that the art is specifically referencing the intellectual property of Paradox would make a fair use argument pretty strong if the matter ever went to court, or, indeed, another form of adjudication.
                                                    There's nothing in IP law that states that derivative works are inherently less protected when the owner of the IP derived from is involved.

                                                    If I make a fan documentary on the World of Darkness, and I monetize it on YouTube, and then Paradox reuploads it to their YouTube account, or streams in on their Twitch account, the fact that I made a film about the WoD has no bearing on a claim of fair use by Paradox. In the eyes of the law it would be no different if my subject matter was the Pokemon franchise and Paradox did the same thing. Paradox's fair use claim would have to be justified under the principles of fair use on its own merits.

                                                    Sure, because IP law is very judge dependent some judges might give weight to the IP owner regarding a dispute over fan materials because there is very little restraint on IP judges, but it's not a stronger case on the letter of the law (or even the spirit of it).

                                                    The only stronger position Paradox has as the IP owner is if they want to stop a fan work; which is not the case at hand.

                                                    Originally posted by James_Willoughby View Post
                                                    Yes, there is a simple answer; it falls under the criticism and comment area of fair use as defined by section 107 of the US Copyright Act.

                                                    (There are actually quite a few avenues for fair use, and a transformation is only one.)
                                                    This is completely incorrect.

                                                    Transformation (or the four considerations listed in section 107 as more codified tests of fair use) is how you tell if something is validly one of the kinds of fair use. There is no other "avenue" of fair use.

                                                    If I posted a video of me "criticizing and commenting" on a full length feature film where I show the whole film and then at the end I say, "I hated that." I would most likely lose utterly in a fair use defense. I did not transform the content in any meaningful fashion. Yes, I criticized and commented, but not enough to claim my use of copyrighted material was fair use.

                                                    If I recorded a rolling commentary of everything I hated about the movie with detailed explanations and examples over the entire duration, I'd actually have a case (though not a slam dunk case, it would at least be a case the court would take seriously). Even better if multiple aspects are relevant (cinematography, CGI elements, audio, etc) to further make the case that the whole thing was important to transform with my voice over.

                                                    Just asserting that what Paradox did falls under criticism and commentary is not sufficient to call it fair use. That's why section 107 immediately proceeds to how to adjudicate the grey areas of how much transformation is enough.
