    In my setting a group of vampires (supported somewhat by a few of the movers and shakers in the Camarilla) created a popular television drama that airs on Omnivision with with novels, movies, video games, etc., called "Midnight Blood" as a way to help uphold the Masquerade. Midnight Blood has a lot of similarities to the in-setting reality of the Camarilla, with different clans of vampires (some of which are pretty similar to the actual clans), a religious sect of dangerous marauding vampires, etc. It's got a really rabid fanbase, conventions, cosplay contests, a big MMO and so on. If someone were to find out about the Camarilla and the Masquerade and then try to come forward and present their findings to the masses, response from most people would be to say, "What? Camarilla? You mean the Council of Fangs in Midnight Blood? Dude, that's a fantasy television show, it's not real. Stop being crazy and get help."

    The result would be It would be like someone in our world insisting everything in the Harry Potter books was 100% real and that there really were wizards and witches and a school for magicians in Britain. Really! People would just roll their eyes.

    Of course, older kindred don't really understand the "hiding in plain sight" concept so they view Midnight Blood as a profound Masquerade breach. And at the time it came out there was some major intra-sect conflict. At this point however it's out there, the damage is done, it's not like they can make everyone forget about the series and the Masquerade didn't explode.

                    Originally posted by Black Flag View Post
                    It’s pretty much what Dracula did in the official setting. Apparently pissed some people off at time, but in the long run it seems to have worked.
                    In my campaign, the publishing of Dracula and other vampire fiction had a number of unintended consequences for the vampire race as a whole.

                    * The first one was the fact that it changed the way the Masquerade was enforced as now many more people knew what vampires were but they believed they were fiction and that was harder to believe when people claimed they were real.
                    * The second one was the fact that Caitiff and Thin Bloods dramatically increased in number. 9/10 before, when a vampire was Embraced and abandoned by their sire, they died in short order because it was an impossible to understand sanity-blasting experience. The ubiquotousness of Dracula meant that these people now went, "Oh, I'm a vampire." This moved it from 9/10 to like 5/10 and thus it became an actual problem.
                    * The number of independent vampire hunters began to steadily rise. Things like the Inquisition were the only game in town but now it was possible for someone like the Frog Brothers to survive a successful hunt. Gradually, these people formed their own groups and gangs. It's been a slow burn but it has killed thousands of vampires over the years.

                    Generally, my view on the Masquerade is the fact that it is something that is hard to DESTROY but relatively easy to INJURE. One of my book trilogies is the Red Room saga (Esoterrorism, Eldritch Ops, and The Fall of the House) which is about how a magical Illuminati-esque group keeps the supernatural secret from the public. The House is hard to stop because it controls governments, media, and has, well, magic.

                    So various groups want to destroy it and come up with increasingly severe and horrible plans to break the Masquerade (called "The Big Lie") permanently. This includes a vampire-blood based zombie plague and trying to summon a Godzilla-sized Elder God at one point. Other things can be covered up, even including the destruction of a small town.

                        In my eyes the Masquerade survives because the people at the top end of the various organizations that hunt vampires realize vampires for the most part adhere to secrecy. Once you make their existence public the reason for them to maintain secrecy disappears which causes a number of undesirable outcomes.
                        • Casualties will skyrocket. The more violent vampires will no longer hold themselves at bay instead going on killing sprees. Any important public figure will be a target (basically picture Russel Edgington's TV attack and multiply that by a large number, what he does is not beyond the capability of a young vampire in VtM).
                          • They have the casualty numbers among their own organizations to attest to what vampires being assailed on what are considered advantageous terms can inflict.
                          • Related to that is they no longer get to fight them on mostly advantageous terms. When vampires go on the offensive it means that these organizations now have to play the reactive role. There's an awful lot of people and cities to try and protect.
                        • Not everyone is going to want to attack vampires. Some will argue that they're simply suffering from a condition and try to help them. Others will want to take advantage of their capabilities.
                          • Vampire blood can quickly become one of the biggest tools on this particular facet of conflict.
                            • It halts the aging process. There are any number of wealthy, powerful, and influential people who would gladly pay and work with vampires to insure they never need worry about death's specter.
                            • It contains healing properties that make it better than many, if not all, the medicines we have available.
                              • Imagine the argument that can be made "Do you have a loved one suffering from cancer? What would you give to make them better, right now? We can help you. You don't have to watch your loved ones waste away any longer. Call the Camarilla and we'll make sure your grandparents get to see their grandchildren, parents can watch their children grow up, and couples can enjoy spending their lives together. Camarilla - we're here to help."
                              • Modern medicine has long dealt with treatment regimens for chronic conditions. The addictive nature of vampire blood is about the only side effect of vampire blood, pretty much everything else is an upside. Just frame it as long term treatment and the pharmaceutical industry either needs to get with the blood or go out of business.
                            • From a government perspective vampires could quickly end up being treated like nuclear weapons. Whether or not you use them, you assume your foes will. Thus you have to have them or you put yourself at a tactical disadvantage.
                              • Essentially every Discipline has direct military application. Vampires could easily become a sort of special forces in the military, used for the toughest missions or to counter their enemies' equivalent.
                                • Ghouldom could become compulsory. Imagine a group of soldiers devoted to their commanding officer. They're stronger, faster, and better than a human. It wouldn't be that difficult to imagine the WoD military selecting their best officers to become vampiric commanders, not only do you never lose their battlefield experience but they make sure their troops follow orders to the letter.
                              • Even beyond legitimate political entities the idea of a rogue nation or terrorist group working with vampires could easily sway military minds into taking advantage of vampires of their own.
                                • You can't hope to outlive a ghoul or vampiric terrorist leader, you have to eliminate them
                                • Your forces are only as secure as your ability to monitor and counter Disciplines like Dominate, Presence, and Obfuscate
                                  • Its almost impossible to consider an area clear since there could be someone with an assault rifle waiting to ambush soldiers with Obfuscate
                                • Animalism turns IEDs into guided missiles. Suddenly every stray might be a squad killing explosive
                                  • Or they might be one of the enemy taking the guise of an animal to get into killing range
                                • Celerity makes facing them in direct combat factors more deadly. Even low level abilities like Rapid Reflexes and Blink mean a squad will probably face casualties before they can even engage
                                • Wards and Warding Circles make assaulting territory much harder. You never know when you're going to run into a barrier that inflicts casualties before you even close with the enemy
                                • There's a host of Discipline applications for the field of combat, these are just a few examples of the headaches that a commander would face fighting a vampire backed foe
                        • The legal ramifications could choke the legal system for years
                          • Something as fundamental as "is a vampire still a citizen?". Similar to the "they just have a condition" above there could be legal arguments that they should still hold legal rights.
                            • Aside from a condition they're possessed of their mental faculties and capable of making rational decisions. They could argue they should not be considered deceased, they still move and function just fine.
                            • Trying to argue that they lack certain functions only opens up avenues of attack
                              • Vampires can't have children - So sterile people and those who've had procedures that removed or damaged reproductive should lose their citizenship?
                              • Vampires don't eat - Blush of life from a number of vampires quickly disabuse this notion. Arguing they have trouble eating opens up assaults from the view of any number of dietary troubles
                              • They can't go out into the sun - So the person with serious life threatening allergies ceases to be a citizen?
                              • Their organs don't work - Plenty of medical conditions end up with organs not functioning properly or needing to be replaced with artificial ones, do they stop being citizens? How many prosthetic parts does it take before someone is not a citizen?
                            • In a twist of irony animal rights organizations could become their closest allies
                              • They literally talk to animals, you only need a few facts that only an animal might know or have experienced to eventually confirm this. How much better do their arguments become if a vampire can confirm what they say.
                          • Many organizations and individuals will be faced with either working with the vampires or suffering
                            • How many corporations are going to find they have vampiric investors and shareholders through shell corporations and holdings? Suddenly every business is going to have to devote massive resources to checking every one of their records for problems.
                              • This only multiplies when you start looking for ghouls
                            • Many will be faced with the revelation of their darkest secrets or crimes
                              • This is the World of Darkness after all, we know there are corrupt criminal businessmen all over the place. To say nothing of their own personal sins
                        • Religious organizations are upended by proof of the supernatural
                          • The moment the term Cainite and the origins of vampires starts to come out non-Abrahamic faiths will have to respond. Either they need to find and adapt similar stories or face the argument that there is living magical proof that they are wrong
                          • A pall of doubt on any number of miracles could start to crop up as the obvious comparison to Disciplines and the powers of the blood
                          • The accusations and arguments over why vampires exist could divide various religious groups further. Each blaming the others sins for the existence of vampirism
                          • When the SI's activities become public (an active vampire foe with little to lose pretty much insures it will) there will be a lot of questions of oversight. To say nothing of concerns about authority. Just look at the worries when Kennedy was running for president that the United States president could be subservient to the Pope. Now you have evidence of a papal backed hit squad.
                            • Any mistakes or false targets they took out become arguments why fighting vampires is a losing proposition. If the best experts can screw up identifying a vampire versus a person, are they really that different?
                        • There's the fundamental personal problem that many people will know a vampire
                          • There's plenty of discussion around Touchstones but it pretty much insures that a host of people will know a vampire personally. While some would abandon the vampires, others are going to stick with the friend or even more their family member
                          • Which leads into a big one, most people aren't going to want their family members being executed because they turn out to be vampires
                            • Imagine when a political figure finds out a niece, nephew, uncle, or even direct family member is a vampire. You don't think some of them are going to use all their influence to help out their family? As more and more vampires are discovered it becomes harder to claim conflict of interests and recusal.
                          • Even without vampires voting, countries with elections could find vampire friendly governments being elected. Suddenly legislation is being passed to restore their rights and figure out the legalities of their conditions. In the meantime their foes are facing down legal challenges to their activities
                          • Trying to accuse vampires of murder because of feeding becomes fraught with problems. The SI's actions pretty much prove the point that if they tried to seek help they would be hunted and destroyed

                        Basically vampires going public has every chance to blow up in the various hunter organizations faces, with them being portrayed as fringe religious lunatics who want to kill people's relatives because of a dietary and allergy condition. If nothing else it makes their activities much harder because they no longer have the nebulous oversight they do now, instead they'll be under a microscope from liability and litigation.

                            B: Apocalyptic war.

                            or some mixture thereof.

                            But I think, ultimately the primary stopper for the masquerade break is the actual process of a masqurade breach

                            Inciting event occurs>No vampire intervention>Witness must correctly identify the attack as vampiric and not rationalize it as something else> Witness must decide to take action despite consequences-

                            A- Witness goes after the vampire themselves. Not a problem.
                            B- Witness decides to tell the media>Reporter must believe the story> Reporter must decide to take the story further despite consequences>Editor must believe the story> editor must decide to take the story further despite consequences> No vampire intervention> Media outlet must reach a wide base of people> public have to believe the story>story has to spread unopposed.
                            C- Witness tells police> Beat cop must not die of laughter> Beat cop gets homicide specialists/forensics involved> nobody is influenced by vampires, vampires do not intervene> murder specialists do not rationalize situtation and agree with the vampire cause> Vampires do not intervene> Superiors do not intervene> police allow the media to get involved. See B

Getting people to practice plugging minor breeches that would otherwise fizzle out is important. Just having the vampire populace on their toes is enough.

                            Most player characters are morons because they are controlled by players are playing a game. The setting is usually a city during turbulent times, because the players are playing a game. But most cities aren't going through great turbulence, most characters aren't controlled by PCs (or are written like Storyteller PCs, though those are plentiful). Most characters adapt to undead life and aren't a walking comedy of errors,Most cities are slow burning; Plans that last centuries, political rivals already defeated, surrounded by cities of the same sect, city comfortably corrupt. There's little Masquerade risk. On the otherhand the places where shit's going down and the pc's are going wild a masquerade breach needs to compete with all the noise of the usual crime waves and bankruptcies that ensue during such times.

                                                What people don't understand about the masquerade is that it's not really a necessity, but a convinience. Think about it, if vampires suddenly decided to come out in the open, then they'll have to start obeying human laws. What happens when you accidentaly drain a vessel, or go into frenzy and kill a bunch of humans? For vampires this is natural behavior, an inevitable part of being a cainite, but to humans this is murder and it needs to be punished.

                                                How about ghouling and blood bonding? Is it legal to drug someone into loving you? Into having sexual relations with you? To make them a into a slave for the rest of their (supernaturally extended) life? Of course not, the courts would throw the book at you! But again, for vampires this is just a part of being kindred.

                                                So you see, vampires and humans are really not the same, nor are they equals. The societies they build also have to be very different, because if the Camarilla decided to, for example, make killing humans a punishable offense, pretty much every vampire would have to be punished.

                                                Vampires and humans are totally incompatable, and frankly, living openly alongside humans in an equitable fashion would be a real drag for the average bloodsucking monster that can tank bullets and kill people in a single blow. That's the real reason the masquerade exists. Because the alternative is either enslave the humans openly, which is an entierly superfluous option to any camarilla ancilla and upward, or be enslaved by them and have to follow their laws, their customs ect. Just a waste of time either way.
