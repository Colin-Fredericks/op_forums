    Methuselahs in the New World

    07-14-2021, 11:00 PM
    Something that's always bugged me. What were Methuselahs doing in the New World so early anyways? Menele, The Wanderer, whoever is responsible for the Tlacique...all long before anyone had any right to be there (outside of maybe Drowned Legacies, and the indigenous population). Like Menele's story claims he did it to find a place where Helena couldn't find him, but given how dangerous those early sea voyages would be (not going to discuss Finnegan's Flight, that's just ridiculous), it really seems strange that old vampires would just randomly decide, hey, let's go see if there's another continent out there.

          Oh well, Kharnov finished first, but here it goes anyway.


          - Menele, according to some bits in the backstory of Critias (who actually unknowingly enabled it), came to the Americas sometime in the 1200s-1300s, so before Columbus but definitely a few centuries after Leif Erikson's journeys & discoveries. Same goes for Bjorn Garinson, the Brujah primogen in Washington DC, mostly.
          Also, i'm pretty sure CbN implies Menele already had some evidence such a land existed - from Auspex, from time-lost punic chronicles about accidental discovery by sailors following the african coast, or a combination of the two - before actually making the journey.


          - The Wanderer, if you check his backstory in Diablerie: Mexico, is an actual native american embraced in what would become British Columbia sometime in the 1st century AC. His nameless sire is the one with supposed old world (possibly roman or gallic origins, if a "before Caesar crossed the Rubicon" commentary serves as indication) origins and based on location, might have come by foot (or paw & wing), crossing the Bering Strait.


          - The Tlacique i know little to nothing about, but even their Wikia entry says their common origin is contentious and association might owe more to politics than actual blood relations, so everything should be taken with a grain of salt. Specially considering that two of their common disciplines are also shared with the Baali, that definitely had a pre-columbian presence in the region acording to the books (though even then some might be less reliable than others).
          And if their founder was indeed a rogue setite and not a rogue Baali or something else altogether - well, the Sahara is a fairly large place potentially bridging the space from Egypt to Atlantic seaboard in a way not unlike the Great Steppe/Silk Road did for lands between Europe and Southeast Asia.

          Just some thoughts, hope this helps.

                          Obviously some of this is simply a matter of personal taste. If you don't like it, then it seems silly. If you do like it, it adds another layer of mystery. The idea of certain vampires making their way from the Old World to the New World isn't any crazier than all the real world theories of pre-Columbian contact (some of which are silly, some are plausible but without evidence, and others unlikely but very cool).

                          We can divide these into several groups.

                          The first are ancient vampires who seem to have crossed the Bering Strait. I would include all the vampires described in Diablerie Mexico here - the Slumbering Giant and the unnamed Nosferatu. Their clans (Gangrel and Nosferatu) are the ones established as the most likely to survive in the wilderness so it isn't much of a surprise that some could find their way to the Americas. Both could easily have reasons for wanting to flee and having come to a remote spot on the Chukchi Peninsula realized there was something on the other side (perhaps as a result of Auspex, an occult vision, pure guesswork, or anything else you see fit). This was a very early sourcebook so intentionally including these vampires here could have been a way to allow STs to have their own small, secret lineages of these clans if they wanted.

                          Menele's voyage to the New World is in a different category. He fled across the Atlantic Ocean only a relatively short time before Columbus. This would have occurred at a time when the Europeans knew well that the world was round and that they were just beginning to have maritime technology capable of surviving such a voyage. As long as we accept that Menele somehow knew that there was land overseas (through some occult means available to him as an ancient vampire), then it is feasible for him to have arranged it. And as to the dangers of sailing the ocean, simply having a power like Weather Control or Auspex (to detect storms before they happen), such dangers could be reduced heavily. After all, these vampires just wants to get here, they don't care about the poor mortals who have to make their way back (and likely don't want them to). Again, CbN was one of the first sourcebooks for Vampire, so we know this was one of the original conceits of the game. One thing that does surprise me, is that there's never been (to my knowledge) any vampire who entered North America through the Viking settlements of Greenland and Vinland. That would fit this category of "Atlantic crossing only when European shipbuilding technology became good enough".

                          Then we have the story of the Baali Huitzilopochtli in Chaos Factor published in 1993. That doesn't fit either category. I don't have this sourcebook so I don't know the details of how he was supposed to have travelled to the New World. But depending on your depiction of the Baali and Infernalism, it could be justified through some kind of magic. 1993 is still very young in WoD publishing terms and the Baali as a concept had already been introduced in the first Storytellers Guide. So it seems to support a general idea that the creators of the game were purposefully positioning Mesoamerica as a center of vampire activity in the New World. Given that region's association with blood with human sacrifice and vampire bats, it makes a thematic sense. But it still seems unclear to me how the Baali reached there. Given nothing else, I'd likely put it as a result of the fabled navigations of the Phoenicians and Carthaginians making them a third point of possible contact. We know these peoples were skilled navigators and there are unproven theories of possible Phoenician contact with the Americas. That's interesting enough pseudohistory for me to say it could happen in the WoD. And it works better than the other two contact points. Furthermore if there were vampires who transited to the New World at this time, it gives credence to Menele finding such records and convincing him to make the voyage as well a thousand or more years later.

                          The Tlacique are more problematic because they weren't an original part of the World of Darkness, but a late edition in the Revised era, presumably to help expand the Setites. As such, they are a late addition to canon and don't fit into the original intentions of the game's creators. Rather than make yet another origin point for their arrival in the Americas, it would make better sense that they came from one of the established points, like the presumed Phoenician/Carthaginian point of contact. That lends itself to the idea that the Tlacique were part of the Baali transfer to Mesoamerica. Or perhaps pursued them to the Americas.

                                                          Writers, and historians for that matter, tend to write about things that are interesting. The "completely normal" isn't that interesting. This makes it seem like a bias where you've got so much emphasis on the few Methuselahs shown off in the new world and nobody writes about the presumed myriad of Methuselahs chilling in the middle east (there are of course, other factors like familiarity, but this is a pretty big one.)

                                                          there's this perception that people in early-modern england emptied their chamberpots out the window, pooring their shit on the street. In reality, that happened a few times, it was newsworthy, and people were very angry at the fool who threw their shit out the window. Most people would take such waste to the downstream part of the river or give it to the people who process this stuff (it's chiefly great for tanning, fertilizer, and gunpowder). But somehow, the exceptions to the rule became sensationalized and now most have this idea that throwing shit out of the window was normal (This was kinda aided by victorian-era historians wanting to make the past barbaric so their own era seemed better).

                                                          Point being, most princes likely have fairly stable domains, they rule well, and vampire plots take years to achieve. But for the purposes of Drama we need shitty, awful princes with domains on the verge of anarchy. We need methuselahs in the USA because they're interesting and the US is unstable, but we also want some ancient methuselahs that came to the USA well before they really should have because it's intriguing to have methuselah from pre-urbanization of the USA; that helps reinforce how ancient and unfathomable they are.

                                                          In-universe? I'd say Methuselahs moved over because they were the losers or were otherwise threatened and felt the best thing to do would be to create some distance. Perhaps they spent a lot of time flying around the globe with astral projection.
