    Thoughts regarding Neonates not exploiting crime to obtain power/resources?

    06-24-2023, 10:14 PM
    In VTM, a common trait is that the Elders have all the power/money and the young have none. My issue is that very few Neonates seem to capitalize on their inherent supernatural abilities to gain power/resources using crime.

    Presence and Dominate can be used quite easily to form a criminal organization/gang, force mortals into giving you what you want, and to scare mortals into not moving against you. Auspex can alert you to the movement of law enforcement and victims, as well as help you obtain important information like the code to the safe where jewelers keep their most valuable items. In fact, every discipline can be used in some way to easily commit crimes that mortals would have serious trouble pulling off.

    However, you almost never see neonates capitalizing on it.

    Why is that?​

      This might be an unpopular opinion but the key issue is the Players themselves. You can give a less than creative player all the mechanical advantages in a game and nothing will happen, but if you give a creative and somewhat unorthodox player a multi-tool, a flash drive, a smartphone and a data plan.... nations will fall. It takes a certain amount of either "big picture" thinking or "outside the box" thinking to create the sort of plans that will generate real power in a setting like VtM. Not all players have that or if they do, not everyone has the energy to plan such things after getting done with classes or their 9 to 5 job.

      For example, due to how much of an XP sink Thaumaturgy is, Tremere generally don't have functional power within a coterie until you pass the 75+ XP range, by that point most other PCs have more or less completed their build and are working on flavor stats or filling in gaps. So when I play a Tremere I usually have them use level 1 Auspex to hit up the local gambling dens and spend the evening playing against other gamblers rather than the house. In this way the Tremere basically became the bankroll for the coterie. Hit a different casino or den each night and you are looking at Resources 3 to 4 quite easily, and none of that is money that can be easily traced.

      But what do you do now that you have an untraceable monetary fund? Invest in properties so that your gambling money farms even more money, then purchase and upgrade multiple havens. So now you are the owners of multiple havens, own many businesses which generate passive income, you own controlling stock in corporations around the world, each member of the coterie is rated at Resources 5 individually.

      But now what? You have enough money to influence politics, manipulate the markets, hire mercenary companies, create entire fake companies to protect your interests or get you new toys. Power in the vampire world is a tricky thing because while you might have insane wealth, the humans you use are only could for a limited amount of time. If you ghouled the US president, at best they are only in power for 8 years, and anything you would want to have them do would have to be done carefully and quietly so they could only do you one or two favors in that time period.

      As you can see the scope can be grand, but due to the mechanics there is a degree of diminishing returns. Especially if the ST doesn't have a lot of storylines where you offer to pay off a neonates mortal families almost defaulted mortgage in exchange for them teaching you a rare discipline.

                  Creativity is indeed a key factor here
                  (Or, I dare say, background knowledge, may God have mercy of the soul of a poor ST who have players with knowledge and background and let say path of conjuring or alchemy... )

                  And of course the fact, Vampires rarely Embrace from that area of life - mostly to gain knowledge or influence from the said area - , and vampires inherently avoid unfamiliarty themselves and will not deepdive to learn the ins and outs of such things
                  Not to mention, most of the vampires were... well... normal Humans. And we grew up learning Crime is a sin (if you come to a relegious background) or - obviously - against the Law
                  Vampires were people, humans too, most often than not, good people, not criminals and part of the clinging to their Humanity is to try to behave like Humans and avoid thing humans would consider crime; murder, stealing, robbery, theft, burglary, arson (especially arson, Fire BAAAD), assault and abuse of any kind
                  Neonates are - most of the time - try to remain Human and fit in to Humanity
                  Sure, some of them jumps in the opportunity or were criminals to begin with, they obviously have an advantage, but I think if the vampire in question wasn't embraced exactly becasue she was already familiar wiht Crime, most of them will avoid it
                  In time, thanks to the web of Gifts and Favors , aka "Boons" that tie the Cainite society together (Favors/Boons are the currency of the undead and agreeing to do or owe a favor is a big deal among vampires, even a neonate could and probably should and would eventually aquire a few) she will learn the nature of these things and slowly tries those with humans
                  And the next step?
                  Well, she will understand, when the Corleones make you an offer you can't refuse, you literally can't refuse it.
                  It's a warning to do what they say, or else.

                  Experience, paried with the slow errosion of Humanity will in time demolish those mental blocks that could prevent you to be a bad person and eventually you will come to a situation like this:

                  Clara: You're scared.

                  And

                  Clara: I don't know.
                  The Twelfth Doctor: 'm sorry?​

                  Usually when in my games we reach such or similar conversation even with a friend, ghoul, anyone or anything thats is the truning point
                  Either the vampire continnue trying... or stops trying

                  And good people will avoid crime like before... bad people?
                  Well...

                  The good are innocent and create justice. The bad are guilty, which is why they invent mercy.

                          I've done it. I can think of many reasons why it's not the go-to

                          1-We're talking about blue collar crime here right? White collar crime is just better. Safer, invisible, more lucrative etc..
                          2-Dealing with criminals exposes you to a lot more risk. Both in that they can try to kill you or in that you might wake up to find half your organization arrested and police looking for you.
                          3-Large Criminal organizations will operate beyond the scope of the kindred's domain and other vampires will not appreciate your agents in their territory. With smaller groups, given gang rivalries, dealing with criminals will earn you the ire of other vampires who may also be involved in criminal doings.
                          4-A lot of big money items like drugs or human traficking require complicated networks.
                          5-Big Mafias have their own internal hunter teams because they're aware of the BS.
                          6-Camarilla Kindred don't want to look aggressive or ambitious. They're the sect that promotes quiet friendly gatherings, peace and prosperity, and the road of humanity. Most don't want to befriend a thug they can't control.

                                  I think Sect and Clan also factor into it. In most cases a sire doesn't kick their new childe into the streets to fend for themselves, but will tutor them until they feel they are ready.                                   Most Giovanni and Setite characters I have met had at least a toe in large scale drug dealing and money laundry, while Ventrue and Lasombra stuck to white collar stuff. Tremere tended to access resources of their Clan, their idea of resources is a bit different. If they needed a quick fix of money, they usually sold magic favors to the moneybags.
                                  The most street level crime I've seen came from Caitiff, Brujah, Tzimis, and maybe somewhat surprisingly Toreador.

                                                  The don't draw attention to yourself is common sense, but truth be told I have seen several STs jump the gun on what attracts attention.

                                                  Some STs are almost impatient for the PCs to attract attention so they can start their preplanned story, rather than letting the players dictate when things kick off.
