Vampires can get away with picking on low level Mages, but high-level Mages are terrifying, and to the extent that Vampire society knows about Mages, the general attend tends to be "do NOT fuck with them unless you have a damn good reason."

                                            It's actually pretty common sense in World of Darkness that just picking a fight out of the blue is often a very bad idea, because the world is full of terrifying super-monsters hiding behind the face of Joe Average. Vampires are more aware of this than most because most Vampires can't even tell the 'power level' of their own kind just by looking at them. Attacking without having extensively scoped out a target first is potentially inviting a situation to blow up in your face, because you underestimated your target or you underestimated their connections. Of course, Vampires do have to attack random people out of the blue more than other supernaturals, and statistically that does get messy sometimes when they end up trying to jump another supernatural for blood. I imagine Bone Gnawers are a source of urban horror stories for Vampires.

                                            And to be clear, weakness inspiring aggression is likely going to be a factor in the instances where Vampires do try to blood-bond a Mage (I'll go into this a bit more below).


                                            Originally posted by HorizonParty2021 View Post
                                            If the Mages have low Arete, they are limited to 1 success on their sensory roll, on average. With respectable Arete, they would have the jump on the Vamps. I think we both prefer the latter.
                                            If sensory abilities is all those Mages have, they're going to have every reason to try and get the most out of it. Which means using every possible method of lowering the Difficulty of their casting. They'll be limited to one success no matter what, but there's no reason to believe they'd be completely incapable of using their magic when they really need it. I don't know much about ritual casting, so maybe that's not possible at such a low level, but using a ritual to set up the Effect before they start their day/night may be an option. Though that may make them more susceptible to detection by Auspex.


                                            Originally posted by HorizonParty2021 View Post
                                            I will keep this in mind in any future discussions where someone casually says, "Mages? Just blood-bond them.".
                                            There are some legitimate reasons why blood-bonding is a common suggestion Vampire players might make for handling something like Mages. It's the one method that nearly every Vampire possesses, no matter how lacking their are in social Disciplines, for turning a potential enemy into a minion. But there are limitations. In particular, the fact that a full blood-bond takes at least three nights to set up. Even if a Vampire manages to run down a Mage and force them to drink the first dose of vitae, that first-stage bond isn't going to be strong enough to compel the Mage to let the Vampire give them the second dose. They will have an entire day to respond, whether that means trying to run, trying to fight back, or seeking help from allies. Unless the Vampire kidnaps the Mage, in which case any allies they have will likely have questions about why they haven't heard from them for three days. Or the Vampire might be in the position to slip doses of vitae into the Mage's food or drink, but that could require a fair amount of effort to set up, especially if the Mage in question is appropriately paranoid.

                                            Generally speaking, the most likely Mage to be targeted by Vampires for a Blood Bond is an isolated one. And a Vampire that has the means of determining whether or not a Mage is a safe target for an attempted blood-bond and then successfully dosing the Mage three times is not going to be a neonate, probably not even an ancilla. It's going to be an Elder or something more powerful, that has carefully vetted the target before making a move.

                                            Which isn't to say that neonates trying to jump a Mage isn't a thing. It's just something that ends badly for the Vampires more often than not. Mages make the best detectives, and they can operate during the day.

                                            Again, going off half-cocked in the World of Darkness is almost always a bad idea, because there is always a Bigger Fish.


                                            Originally posted by HorizonParty2021 View Post
                                            I was actually saying that the Coterie/Pack would have someone using Presence or Dominate and another with Celerity preventing the Mages from running away. Toreador and Brujah have Celerity and Presence as Clan Disciplines. A single Vampire could chase and Entrance.
                                            A coterie of Vampires versus one Mage is likely to go pretty bad for the Mage. But a coterie versus a cabal becomes much more of a dice roll due to the potential builds, as I noted above. A starter Mage with Forces 3 and Prime 2 is an extreme example, but even Forces 2 with a pre-existing source of flame would be a source of Rotschreck. Mind 1 allows for the creation of mental shields that would aid in resisting Dominate or Presence. If the Vampires don't subdue the entire cabal in their first strike, there's a lot of potential for chaos. Which again invites the question, does the coterie really want to risk everything on a crapshoot? And if they've spent the time and done the work to make sure that it *isn't* a crapshoot, I'd argue that they deserve it, or conversely that the Mages deserve it for not covering their arses better.

                                                    I think it is a good moment to explain more about the Blood Bond for HorizonParty2021 and anyone else that may want this info to use vampires in interesting ways. Going as RAW:

                                                    The first thing a Vampire must do is make the target drink the blood. Any amount of blood does the trick, but sometimes it ends up being a full Blood Point, which means a loss for the Vampire and a gain for the Bonded.

                                                    With this, two effects happen to a mortal, including a Mage: first, the First Level of the Bond sets in. This is a weak emotional effect, the Bonded will think about the vampire frequently, even if they never saw the Kindred before, but that's it. There are no rules, the bonded acts on these flourishing emotions as they see fit.

                                                    Second, and this is important, the victim turns into a Ghoul. Even without a full BP the victim immediately gains a full dot of Potency to play with. Any full BP can be used for healing and stuff.

                                                    No amount of blood will affect the strength of the Bond in a single night, no matter if concentrated or spread through several doses, so only from the next night on the Kindred may try to increase it to Level 2, in the exactly same way. A Level 2 Bond does cause strong emotions, enough for the Kindred to become an important person in your life. Mechanically, though, it is only two effects, the Kindred have -1 dif on social rolls against you and you must roll Willpower if you want to directly harm the Kindred. But otherwise you're free to act as you will, and the emotions are strong but not dissociated of context. If you're bonded after being physically assaulted, there is a good chance that you won't be happy with the Kindred.

                                                    Finally comes the Level 3 Bond. It is an extremely strong love. In fact, emotionally speaking it is among the strongest forms of love possible. Everything else in your life becomes secondary to it. The 3rd level Bond immediately shatters every other lesser level Bond you have and immunizes you not just against other Bonds, but against anything that tries to interfere with this emotion. By RAW, not even a powerful Mind effect can force you to act against the Bond, it would have to Break the Bond itself to do so.

                                                    Otherwise, though, the Bond have few hard rules. Your regnant have an easier time using Dominate on you, which is neat if they have Dominate to begin with. No need for eye contact and you get a +2 dif to resist if you try to. The main rule, though, is that to act against the Bond you'll have a pretty hard time, a Willpower roll dif 8 just to get the right to try, then spending willpower to go through it, with the level of "treason" defining how much time you can act against the Bond for each WP, going from 1 turn (direct attack) to 1 scene (send a letter to a rival).

                                                    And that's it. The secret lies in the lack of details. What consists on "acting against the Bond" is undefined by design. It is a strong emotion, unnaturally strong, but actually quite natural on how it evolves. The Bond is a relationship, and nothing stops you from hating at the same time that you love. The only one able to dictate how you'll act when in love is yourself, and the Blood Bond doesn't actually makes it less true, so much so that even for common mortals the rules strongly recommend for the ST to take in consideration the Nature of the Bonded.

                                                    Vampires that take no care with the Bond may end up with thralls that hunt and kill the Kindred's other servants out of jealousy, or protective lovers that sabotage their plans and communications "for their protection and happiness". A Mind Mage could reasonably decide to use Magic to make those feelings be reciprocated, and a thrall that hates you can easily wait for the right moments to make those WP expenditures for maximum effect.

                                                    Blood Bonds are the most powerful readily available weapon in a vampire's arsenal, and one of the few potent enough to threaten anything else as its formation can't be resisted. But without a lot of care this weapon backfires. A lot.

                                                            One thing I forgot to mention, the Will roll to get to expend WP at a Level 3 Bond must gather as much successes as the times you fed from the regnant before. V20 don't mention more, the rule used to be different with the number of times you feed affecting difficulty and V20 kept it as a typo, so there is a limit, but undefined. Without looking at old rules I would recommend 5 successes as the limit, unless you decide that it can be done as an extended action, which then could have a far higher limit.

                                                            In the end, ways of getting rid of the Bond exist, but are rare and powerful. Bringers of Light is actually a Camp, the only one that can learn the Gift Saur Ops Specialist mentioned, Purity of Blood, that not only is Level 5, but must be taught to a member of the Camp by an avatar of Grandfather Thunder in person.

                                                            For Mage I'd rule something with high levels of Mind, Entropy and Prime, at least one at 5, Quintessence expenditure and lots of successes (at least 10, preferably 15+), and even then only by a Mage that went through the Bond once, so they know what it is really like, or some other event-like requisite.

                                                            The Bond can be broken with a lot of effort, usually by abstaining from contact with the regnant for years, which in itself counts as going against the Bond. The rule of thumb is (12 - Willpower) months for each stage, but a Full Bond retains full effect until the last stage is broken, so three times that amount. A Willpower 10 Mage will still have to withstand 6 months without contact with the person they love the most in the world. Poignant, but perfectly doable.

                                                            Why I wouldn't recommend making it easier to resist or break? Because that would take out the sting completely. With the actual level of freedom the Bond offers to the player of the victim, the thing that makes it interesting is the very notion of this emotional nuclear option, messed up and chaotic, but unavoidable as a force of nature, and so sensitive to how you manage to cause it and to whom. Dominate is more reliable at every turn, even if easier to break, heck, offering money for service is more reliable than the Blood Bond. If you don't want the unstoppable Bond in your game it is better to just use Disciplines and scrap the effect completely.
