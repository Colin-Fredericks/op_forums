            His Church certainly tried contacting him, and in the old Gehenna book there were two wildly different scenarios when they succeeded, which in light of the notion of Set and Typhon being 2 different Cainites that ate each other, suddenly make a lot more sense:

            1. The Setites contact Set in the Underworld and try to bring him back... and fail, for he is deader than dead. He does, however, wish for his Followers to join him. The vast majority of them [are compelled to] commit suicide right then and there, and IIRC those that are prevented from doing so are horrendously depressed and listless. From the idea that this is a 2-man show, this is probably actual Set, severed from his own body by [Typhon].

            2. The Setites, led by at least one of their Methuselahs, manage to bodily resurrect their founder... who looks upon his 'followers', promptly declares them unworthy, and starts eating any he can catch. If one of these two had to be [Typhon], the metaphorical serpent devouring their own tail here sounds the most likely.

                    I just noticed something else: In her early days in the Children of Osiris, not to long after her embrace, Kheminitri interacted with a vampire called Serethor. This would have been around the twelfth century B:C. Serethor is described OOC as "A Fourth Generation vampire who belonged to the Children of Osiris and the childer of Khetamon"

                    Now there are several ways of reading that. By far the most probable is that Serethor was a diablerist. Presumably before joining the Children of Osiris. It could also be that the childrer of Khetamon was a sub-faction within the Children of Osiris. Or a nod to the info we've seen about Khetamon masquerading as Osiris and founding the Children of Osiris.

                    But if we do read that as a straight lineage with no diablerie... Khetamon was the childe of Osiris, and Osiris the childe of Typhon, the serpent. So that would mean that Khetamon was of the Third Generation, Osiris of the Second... and that the real identity of Typhon was Caine! Who seems to have had a "serpent in the garden" thing going on , and was stirring up trouble for the Antediluvians towards the end of the Second Citys lifespan.

                    The name Serethor historically belonged to a concubine of the First Pharaoh, Menes, who joined upper and lower Egypt, so the time fits.

                            Originally posted by Trollroot View Post
                            It is as people note, possible that there are more entities named Apep or Apophis. But on the “What would inspire an Antediluvian to formulate a code of worship?” I would ask “What would inspire an Antediluvian to name his code of worship after an older vampire?”


                            Egyptian magical theory is all about heka or names of power, to name a thing is to know a thing is to control a thing. Perhaps it is absolutely part of the 'plan' of the entity known today as Set/Typhon/Sutekh/etc. to muddy and obsfuscate his name and nature, to make it harder for his magically-potent foes to get a grip on him in their divinations and workings, and, in the process, to 'steal' the credit for as much other stuff as possible.

                            Random other thoughts;

                            1) In some older book, the Jupiter Celestine was a big deal in Chicago, IIRC, and that made precedent for the notion that various gods of myth and legend can form potent entities in the spirit world (and chicken-or-the-egg discussions about whether the mortal faith created the spiritual entity, or the spiritual entity inspired the mortal faith).

                            What if there was a Celestine in the deep umbra based off of the god Set in ancient Egypt, and said 'god' was on the outs, kicked out for the whole feud with Osiris thing, leaving the Set Celestine wandering and vulnerable to predation by a fledgling 3rd/4th generation vampire who got sired much later than his peers, and was seriously underpowered/inexperienced compared to the other Clan founders. But if he somehow conflated his own reputation and name with that of the 'god' Set, and, over centuries, somehow tapped into, and eventually absorbed, the power of that waning Celestine, he could have boosted himself up to more of a match for his (much older) 'siblings.' And also driven himself completely round the bend, absorbing another much older spiritual entity and all of it's anger and hate and past grievances with the Celestines that kicked him out into the spiritual wilderness into himself, but gotta break some eggs or whatever.

                            2) Set's a redheaded warrior-god of storms. So is Thor, fated to die fighting Jormungandr. So is Quetzalcoutl, the feathered serpent. There might be some fun parallels to play with there. Not that Set *is* Thor or Quetzalcoutl, but that Setites are deliberately muddying the water further, drawing attention to superficial surface similarities to make Set seem more ubiquitous than he really is (and drag folk who honor either towards more Set-friendly practices, by polluting their beliefs and lore with Setite lies).

                                        I took it in the more Biblical sense. There's a moment in Genesis where God is cursing the snake and says, "And I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will crush your head, and you will strike his heel.” Which would be talking about humans in general having the abity to choose good and ultimately crush and overcome evil.

                                        Some translations however completely change the meaning of that moment by capitalizing "He" and "His" in which God is referring specifically to Jesus.

                                        So in those versionsof the Bible, God is saying that the serpent (Devil) would strike Jesus' heel but get its head crushed. Under that interpretation, with Set being a stand-in for the Serpent if Eden (which kind of fits with his whole philosophy of opening people's eyes to the truth of good, evil and the world) then Set would have been defeated (maybe permanently?) by Jesus.

                                        I don't think the Setites would care for that interpretation though.

                                                            One thing I don't like that much is that the authors basically advanced the Setites as a Clan and the Setites as Sutekh's followers on two completely dissociate directions. For the Dark Ages and, to a lesser extent, for the modern clan they went as far as possible into Gnosticism and making their religion complex and nuanced.This part I love.

                                                            But for the Ante itself and the Special Egyptian Background™ they fully embraced the Thulsa Doom-style cult of EVIL. Even when they did improved the depiction of the other factions and events during the Year of the Scarab, for the Setites they just deepened that shit. That's kind of boring, despite how fun Super King of Evil Suthek may be in a more Pulp take of the game (or just played for laughs).

                                                            Two small corrections, although I won't remember the sources and may be wrong, but I do remember reading it:
