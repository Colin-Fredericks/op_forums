                        Originally posted by Morbus View Post

                        ...That's a rather weird way to see things...Given the initial depiction of the Ashirra as an Arabian only sect, it's rather clear what the real inspiration was. It's not islam per se.
                        That's rather the point I'm getting at by pointing out the seeming contradictions in the sect, the Ashirra is the product of complex social, political, and cultural factors. They only seem to be contradictions if you're not looking at the whole picture. Like it or not, religious, sectarian, imperial, and ethno-national conflict would weigh heavily in the sect's creation and sustenance, particularly in light of the events of the 20th and 21st Centuries. If someone is looking to point to any single factor which might have informed the Ashirra's creation or evolution, they're not going to get a proper basis for holistic analysis.

                        I'm not going to mince words. You can't examine this through a predominantly Western lens, just as you can't examine South or East Asian cultures through a predominantly Western lens, and expect sensible conclusions. You're talking about a region that is so culturally, regionally, linguistically, socially, and economically diverse that, for some, Islam is and has historically been the only source of cultural cohesion. And that's a circumstance that's only been aggravated, not palliated, by inter-war partitioning of the Ottoman empire, post-war decolonization, Western attempts to enforce Western ideals of nationalism in the Arab states, and resistance against irredentist movements and failure to recognize stateless nations atop permanent war for empire, oil, and drugs.

                        And just like every other sect of vampires, the Ashirra absolutely would -- and should -- adapt in response to changing regional and global circumstances, particularly the upheavals of the later 20th and 21st Centuries. The rise and fall of Arab nationalism, the inter-war partitioning of the Ottoman empire, the post-war era of decolonization, the rise and decline of pan-Arabism, the rise of pan-Islamism, and the conflict between Arab nationalism and pan-Islamism, should absolutely factor into what the Ashirra is in the modern nights setting. Because what else would, say, a Gangrel with a Bedouin herd, a Kurdish Brujah, and a Ventrue with influence over the House of al-Sabah remotely have in common?

                        To say otherwise is orientalism in and of itself.

                            It's worth noting that the Camarilla, and the Ashirra, have never been perfectly aligned with any mortal empire. The Camarilla was never limited by the bounds of the British Empire and the reach of the Ottoman Empire never defined the Ashirra. The Camarilla and the Ashirra are simply a pair of sinister, and malicious secret societies protecting a race of antisocial personality disorder parasites. Inside the territories each assumes to be its own, its membership possess more in common with each other than they do with membership in the other sect. But neither sect is going to really represent the local mortal population in terms of religion, politics, jobs and so on. They might represent the local population in terms of ethnic demographics. But the average Ashiira will not more be a good Hindu or Muslim than the average Camarilla will be a good Catholic or Protestant.

                            Vampires are twisted mirror reflection of people and what people do and what people are and how people behave. That is true of the Camarilla and the Ashirra.

                            I think the Ashirra make sense and are fine on both a Watsonian and Doylist level because I am not one to tell people they are having fun wrong, because I am not going to deny people representation, because not everything needs to be Camarilla and because have the Ashirra provides chances for narrative and drama in a game that would not be present in a setting without the Ashirra.
