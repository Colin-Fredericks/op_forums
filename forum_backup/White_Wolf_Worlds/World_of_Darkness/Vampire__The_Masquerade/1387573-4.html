The original Tremere Elders were part of the Order of Hermes and lived in a weird bubble different from the rest of the world with many female wizards working alongside men as equals (or closer to it). Modern Tremere are more used to it as well with Monica Chang (Anarchs Unbound) and other educated women. The Middle Tremere are those Tremere recruited from the 700 or so year period of the Clan's history where women struggled to get education in the sciences or academia.

            So, there's a huge number of Tremere embraced in times of great sexism that won't shake it off like in other Clans. Other Clans like the Toreador, Brujah, Nosferatu, and Ventrue are not going to Embrace exclusively from men because they're not Embracing purely for history in the (very-very sexist) academia and sciences where they'd be banned from among mortals.

            Even then, there would be women Embraced during these time periods by being recruited from either supernatural groups [witches, wizards, cults, psychics] or trained by the original Elder Tremere as apprentices outside of traditional misogynist European society. These would be, maybe, 1/4th of the Clan instead of 1/2 though or maybe even lesser. So in a weird way, yes, I can see the Tremere being objectively more sexist than other clans do to the Middle Management being recruited from Europe's male-only academic network.

            But at the end of the day, it's really just how you want to play the Tremere. My take on the subject is that there is a strain of sexism in the Clan but it's by profoundly petty and small minded men and not the movers and shakers of the Pyramid that are beyond such things [and have aged out of viewing human gender as mattering].

                My take on the Tremere is focused on the idea of "the Usurper". This term doesn't just imply that one is hungry for power, or has achieved power. It means that one has acquired (or attempted to acquire) power which was not theirs by natural right. But, what are the Tremere usurping? In one sense, their vampiric existence is a usurpation. More specifically, it is their seizing of rights and prerogatives usually preserved for High Clans.

                Each clan has socio-historical themes which inform their cultures.

                The Ventrue, Lasombra, and (especially Old Clan) Tzimisce hold power by right of inheritance, landed nobility, and working within the legal/social system into which they were born and later embraced. They are, at the bottom of it, children of privilege. If they manage to retain the glory of their ancestors, and perhaps extend it a few inches in one direction or another, they will be judged a success.

                Toreador and (traditional Idealist) Brujah hold power by virtue of their personal excellence. Intelligence, charm, bravery, talent, and, in some cases, refined tastes are the elements they combine to rule. Oh, they may have humble origins on paper, but at no time in their lives were they overlooked or thought of as being without potential.

                The Giovanni hold power by a mix of inheritance, nepotism, and cronyism. It doesn't matter how innately skilled they are. If they have the right last name and aren't total F***Ups, they may very well be ghouled or embraced.

                These are High Clans because no one is surprised when they attain power. Their success is barely noted, as it is the natural order of things.

                Then, on the other hand, there are the Tremere...

                The Tremere were never expected to succeed. They were outsiders. Commoners and craftsmen. Scribes and academics. Crass moneylenders and eccentric old crones. They dragged themselves up from obscurity and barged into the halls of power when no one... perhaps, least of all, they themselves... expected them to succeed.

                Tremere are identified with the rising bourgeois middle-class of the middle ages and the renaissance. They held no titles. No prerogatives. They had little more legal rights than a peasant. But, they, as a class, created the urban, intellectual, free-labor economy of Europe.

                Starting with craft guilds (and a heavy dose of stadtluft macht-ing frei), they brought in the best specimens of the humblest classes. Their (admittedly imperfect) meritocracy pushed the most ambitious and capable individuals to leadership, at a time when the aristocracy was so inbred some royals could barely eat food or form words. The burghers invented trades, exploited loopholes in the noble's traditions, and amassed fortunes. Making themselves and their cities too profitable for the nobles to dismantle, they demanded and often received rights and privileges formerly held only by feudal lords.

                They eventually fomented revolutions, which remade society and politics for the benefit of the bourgeois.

                In practical terms, this theme of talented but underestimated individuals coming together to increase their position is seen in all of the templates in the clanbook. These are not people anyone expected to succeed. They all pushed into realms they should have been barely aware of, and -by hook or by crook- made a place for themselves there.

                Now, they are being given the chance to do the same for Clan Tremere in kindred society.

                    My take on the Tremere, by contrast, is exactly the reverse. The Tremere are beings who don't think of power the same way as the other clans. Power is not money, power is not fame, power is not titles, power is POWER.

                    The Tremere venerate your ability to enforce your will on the world and respect it most when you are able to do it by direct command of others through magic, Dominating them into submission, sensing weaknesses, or the fear that people have of you because your powers.

                    If I were to summarize my opinion of the Tremere, they are best defined as a cult. The cult is full of rituals, titles, ranks, and privileges wholly outside of the vampire race's normal secret society. They are a secret society within a secret society.

                    The Tremere who founded the place were an aristocracy among aristocracies by being recruited form mages. Anyone could be Enlightened in those days. Nicolai, as we saw, was a turnip farmer's child when trained by his master.                     In a way, being a vampire diminished their off-brand oddity because they gained the ability to bestow "greatness" on anyone they wanted with the Embrace. Librarians, laywers like Abraham Dusable, scholars, and college administrators that would otherwise live mundane lives.

                    Part of what I liked about The Politico, The Professional Witch, The Psych Major, and The Conspiracy Theorist is they're BAD Embraces. They're people the Tremere recruited and misjudged their supernatural arete. It's an irony that someone like Erichtho was Embraced for her beauty and to be Nicolai's consort (eww) but is probably one of the best Tremere they could have recruited.

                    It's interesting to speculate, really, just how many Tremere kind of...suck...but survive because the Pyramid is about loyalty as well as subservience.

                            Ambition has always been at the heart of the Tremere, even in the original Ars Magica days, with their founder and the founder of House Tytalus having the same master and holding a lot of the same attitudes about the virtues of doing everything it takes to win. Their co-invention of Certamen and the encouragement of its use to settle disputes within the Order was motivated by the idea of giving them an edge in solidifying their overall influence in the group, and turning themselves into vampires was a way to hold on to power in a world were the traditional Hermetic arts were growing weaker. But once they because vampires, they couldn't hold on to the old Hermetic attitudes that mundane human society and its halls of power were largely irrelevant, nor to the idea that things like physical combat could be left solely to faceless Grogs. They had to start embracing people for reasons other than mystical aptitude, including merchants, diplomats, and their Wardens in order to build up a power base among the Kindred and defend themselves from the Tzimisce (and later the Order of Hermes). But the people they tended to embrace for these roles were ones who were ambitious and power hungry enough to think that being a vampire was a perfectly acceptable trade off for the chance at personal power and advancement. The Politico was embraced by one of those type of Tremere because he's exactly like the sort of political animals the clan has been embracing since the Dark Ages. The Conspiracy Expert's ambition is largely centered around his own desire to understand the secret masters of the world, and in stark contrast to the Malkavian Conspiracy Theorist who wants to destroy the conspiracy, he just wants to learn more about it and, in all honesty, finds being a part of it kind of cool. While the Professional Witch may have some latent psychic abilities - ones which Auspex have probably developed much further than she ever would have as a mortal - what ultimately makes her a perfect Tremere is that she was raised to view everyone else in the world as marks for her to take advantage of for her own benefit. (Honestly, I'd love to see her in a Boston based chronicle in the same coterie as the Brujah Elder Representative and possibly the Malkavian Mesmeric Manipulator as a rival or frenemy.)

                            As far as the issue of sexism in the Tremere goes, there was kind of a theme running through the early WoD stuff of having a group of righteous underdogs vs what was essentially "The Man", representing all the sexism, racism, and every other ism that cool GenXers were against. In Vampire's case, you've got the rebellious Brujah and outcast Nosferatu vs the snobbish Toreador, elitist Ventrue, and scheming Tremere, with the mad Malkvians and loner Gangrel sort of in the middle, but more often than not leaning toward the underdogs. This carried over into Werewolf, with the elitist Silver Fangs and scheming Shadow Lords as "The Man" compared to, say, the Bone Gnawers, Black Furies, or Wendigo. The Order of Hermes played "The Man" role in Mage, with the Celestial Chorus and sometimes Sons of Ether backing them up. Wraith just went for a full on Renegades vs Hierarchy thing. Thankfully this kind of faded away. Vampire: The Dark Ages even pointed out that certain gender attitudes were a little impossible to maintain when that frail and weak looking noblewoman could very well be a 200 year old Brujah with Potence 5 and able to tear your arms out of their sockets if you pissed her off. (This is one of those reasons I always wished there'd been an Ashen Lady book about women in the Dark Medieval world, as the reality was far more interesting than the stereotype and there's all these interesting areas were vampires, werewolves, mages, and the like don't have to and aren't going to follow "the rules" unless they damn well feel like it.)

                                            Clanbook: Ventrue (in prima parte)

                                            Hear ye, hear ye. Let it be known to all that this missive shall instigate discussion and judgment upon that folio known to all as The Book of the Clan of Ventrue, and to those Lords thus demonstrated therein, as chronicled by Master Andrew of Greenberg in anno Domini Nineteen Hundred and Ninety Four and illuminated by Joshua Gabriel of Timbrook.

                                            And that's enough of that. This is the last of the original seven Camarilla clans, and somewhat curiously written by the gameline's Developer, which is sort of unusual. (Richard Dansky once told me that anytime he actually wrote any material for a line he was developing, he'd have to get one of the other developers in the office to look it over as it would be missing one of the usual steps of quality control and oversight otherwise. Usually if you see a Developer writing a book for their own line, it was because it was an emergency fill in - such as Ethan Skemp co-writing the original Silent Striders or Phil Brucato co-writing the original Euthanatos - or a passion project on a subject they really love - Phil Brucato doing the original Cult of Ecstasy book.) Our cover is, in my opinion, a lot better than the last two. Even with shaved head and dark sunglasses and leather jacket, this guy radiates a level of cocky self-assuredness that you'd expect from one of the Ventrue. It's dynamic and kind of hip in a way that isn't ostentatious. The back cover image is of a black man with a cigarette and a sort of world weary look in his eyes as if to say, "what are you rebelling against this week?" I like that.

                                            The Anarch Wannabe
                                            Damn it. I rather dislike this character image. I've expressed my dislike for "wannabe" anything as a concept before, and the image of a slightly effeminate young man in designer leather jacket and pre-ripped jeans only adds to that. (I think the idea that the effeminate aspect is maybe supposed to make him look less serious or respectable only makes it bug me even more in hindsight.) And that is a shame, because otherwise, this isn't a bad character. The intelligent and well educated child of wealthy privilege, he's been forced to conform and live up to his parents' expectations and plans of a perfect coastal elite upbringing. The best pre-preschools to get on that track to the Ivy League at age 3, various tutoring and extracurriculars, elite prep schools, that sort of thing. Even after graduating at the top of his class from Yale and starting at a big time financial company, he was still under his parents' thumb. And then he got embraced, and suddenly what they want doesn't matter anymore. Even if he's still somewhat beholden to the clan, he relishes his new found sense of freedom and doesn't want to lose it. That's why he heavily sympathizes with the Anarchs, even if he isn't exactly a member of that sect. He doesn't mind shouldering the responsibilities that come with being a Ventrue, but he only wants to take on the responsibilities that he wants to rather than those other people try to force on him. And he's rather torn between his duties to his sire and his desires to go full Ararch. I really enjoy that sense of conflict and competing desires and responsibilities. He's Mental primary, with a 3/4/3 split, and Social secondary, with a 3/2/3, making him pretty smart and personable. Ability-wise, he's got three dots each in Etiquette and Finance, with twos in Bureaucracy, Investigation, Linguistics, Politics, Drive and Leadership, and then a couple of ones to round it out. Disciplines are the one dot in each with an extra dot in Fortitude set up. I really think he could've used those seven points more effectively for abilities and backgrounds, probably dropping the dot in Dominate or Presence, depending on how you wanted to play his personality. Contacts are spread evenly between Contacts, Generation, Herd and Resources. I'm a little surprised he doesn't have a Mentor. Humanity and Willpower are both at eight, neither of which bothers me over much. I like this character. He's got an interesting and sympathetic backstory that sets him up for a potentially dramatic conflict as the chronicle goes on, and he's got a fairly well rounded ability set. I just wish he was called an Anarch Sympathizer rather than a wannabe.

                                            The Born Leader
                                            I like this one. The image is pretty good. Young, somewhat arrogant looking, very sure of himself. He could almost be the guy on the cover, just with hair. (And just from an artistic standpoint, I appreciate the use of very simple lines and minimal shading on an almost all white image. It's kind of a nice contrast to a lot of the darker colored artwork uses in a lot of Vampire art, especially with the leather jackets and the like. This sort of clean simplicity is something I actually miss a lot from the early 90s era of RPGs.) As far as the write up goes, this guy is a little bit more of a blank slate than the previous character. This is not necessarily a bad thing, as it allows more room for a player to fill in the spaces, but this does make him stand out a little less than other templates might. He's the sort of person who has always known the best way to do things - or at least thought he did - and could usually convince people to go along with his ideas. I think. It's actually a little contradictory about that, at first saying he had difficulty doing so at times, but later saying he was specifically embraced for his ability to sway others to his way of thinking. I wish this was a little more clear. But he rather enjoys being a vampire, especially the supernatural edge it gives him in convincing people that his way is the right way. He's very arrogant, and wants to be the center of attention, to the point that even some elders find him a little overbearing. Ironically, this apparently makes it easier for him to work with other clans, even Anarchs at times. Common enemies and all that. I kind of want to throw this guy into a room with the Tremere Pyramid Climber and see what happens. His Charisma is maxed out at five dots, and he's got three each in Manipulation, Perception and Wits. Abilities include three dots each in Leadership and Investigation, with twos in Acting, Empathy, Intimidation, Streetwise, Firearms, Bureaucracy, and Politics. (I kind of wish his Leadership was one higher, but his Charisma kind of makes up for it.) Disciplines include three dots in Presence and one in Dominate. I have mixed feelings about that. I could really see those seven points going a long way in boosting abilities and backgrounds; Alternately, I can see a good argument for having two each in Dominate and Presence. Backgrounds are all one or two each in Allies, Contacts, Herd, Influence, Resources, and Status. Humanity is eight and Willpower seven, which makes him a little less willful than the previous character. So, overall, I think this is a pretty good character and potentially fun to play.

                                            The Capo
                                            A bit of a throwback to the 60s, 70s and 80s, but even now still has a certain place in certain cities. (And this character certainly works perfectly well as a different ethnicity, such as Russian or Japanese.) He grew up in the world of organized crime, thinking of them as his family in every way that mattered, and willing to do anything for the betterment of the family. Apparently even killing his uncle. One of the true powers behind the family admired his brutal ruthlessness and dedication, so made him a ghoul and eventually a vampire. (This is one of the handful of characters specifically mentioned as being a ghoul.) Now those attitudes he had for the family carry over to the Ventrue, and he'll do whatever it takes to advance both the interest of the clan and his own place in it. The image is of a pretty slim and wolfish looking classic mobster in pinstripe suit. I'm especially glad that he doesn't look like any of the characters from Goodfellas. (In fact, he looks like he would pistol whip Joe Pesci within an inch of his life the second he got out of line with that irritating schtick of his.) Physicals are primary and Socials secondary, but he does have an extra dot devoted to Wits. He's got most of the abilities you'd associate with a criminal enforcer, including three dots each in Brawl and Intimidation, and twos in Dodge, Streetwise, Subterfuge, Drive, Firearms, Melee, Stealth, and Security. Disciplines are one each. Backgrounds are ones and twos in Allies, Contacts, Fame, Influence, and Resources. I feel like a few of these could stand to be a little higher. Humanity and Willpower are both seven. (And if you've been keeping score, then this means that the murderous mobster has more Humanity than the Brujah Skinhead. For reasons, I guess.) Overall, I like this character. He's a little bit of a stereotype, but one that I think fits well with the clan, especially the idea of the parallels between the mob family and the Ventrue, and how this character approached both. So, continuing a pretty strong start.

                                            That's all for this entry. Next will be part two of Ventrue, hopefully managing to fit four of them in.

                                                    Clanbook: Ventrue (in secunda parte)

                                                    Continuing.

                                                    The Corporate Executive
                                                    Essentially, this is a refinement of the original template from the Players Guide. A fast track ladder climber with an MBA (from Harvard, no less), on the way to being a CEO. However, the character was being groomed for all of this by their soon to be sire, and was embraced before that happened. Though somewhat regretful at the fact that they'll never get the public recognition of their talents they've always craved, the challenge and perks of being a vampire help make up for it. The clan, and to an extension the Camarilla, is like a huge shadowy corporation, and they can't help but imagine what the combined power and influence could accomplish if Kindred actually worked together. Especially under her leadership and guidance. While the write up doesn't specify (save for a mention of business dress under equipment), the character image is a woman. (The list of equipment also includes a portable FAX machine. How 90s.) She's Mental primary and Social secondary. However, she's got a two in Charisma and three in Appearance, and I can't help but feel like that's mainly just because she's female. (This is one of the reasons I'm glad CoD got rid of that trait.) Given she's described as the classic iron fist in a velvet glove personality, I'd think she'd have those two switched. She's got three each in Finance and Etiquette. (I think I made a quip about Brujah being issued three dots of Politics and it feels sometimes like Ventrue are done the same with Finance.) Bureaucracy, Investigation, Politics, Alertness, Empathy, and Leadership are all two each. I think there's an argument that one of those dots in Empathy could've been better spent raising her single dot of Intimidation or Subterfuge to two. Disciplines include two dots in Dominate and one in Presence, which feels very fitting. Backgrounds include four in Resources, and two each in Contacts, Generation, Herd and Mentor, and one each in Influence and Status. I'd probably move one of those Herd dots to Influence if I were playing this character. Humanity is eight and Willpower is seven. Overall, I think this is a pretty good character. It's a classic clan archetype, and done fairly well. While I have a few quibbles about some point placement, overall, it's very solid and great for one of those games involving a lot of politicking and intrigue.

                                                    The Counselor
                                                    I remember back when the book first came out, it taking me a few rereads to actually get this one. The name is basically a play on the fact the it's used to refer to both therapists and lawyers, and while the image looks like that of a therapist, he's actually a lawyer. (Though, really, I think the idea of a Ventrue therapist/psychologist would be kind of fun.) He's always been a really good listener, and at telling people what they want to hear. This is something he does more for his own benefit and advancement than out of any sense of loyalty or altruism, which seems perfectly fitting for a vampire. He opted to go to law school instead of medicine or psychiatry. One of his clients apparently enjoyed his advice so much that he ended up embracing him. Now, he's somewhat terrified that other vampires might see through his act, but Kindred seem to love having their egos stroked even more than the living. He's Social primary, with four dots in Charisma, and Mental secondary with three each. He's got three dots each in Alertness and Empathy, and twos in Acting, Subterfuge, Etiquette, Bureaucracy, Investigation, and Politics. But wait, you might ask, I thought he was a lawyer? And yes, he only has one dot in Law, which feels like a major oversight. Even if he's not a very good lawyer, I'd have expected him to have at least two dots in the skill (and preferably more, as he's the only character in the book whose focus is law). Disciplines are two in Dominate and one in Presence. Backgrounds include a four in Resources, two in Allies and Herd, and one each in Retainer and Status. Humanity is nine and Willpower is six. Overall, I like this character, in spite of the Law ability issue. He's got an interesting hook of being a bit more "weaselly" than your typical Ventrue, and feels like he'd be fun to play in a game focused on intrigue and the Elysium.

                                                    The Lover of Unlife
                                                    In some ways, the previous character could almost be seen as someone who blurred the lines between Ventrue and Tremere. Similarly, this one blurs the lines between Ventrue and Toreador. Born to extreme wealthy and privilege, she's never wanted for anything and has had every opportunity to hone her natural talents in whichever way she wished. She is perfect in every way, and will never let you forget that fact. Now that she's a vampire, she's pretty much the same, except possibly even more arrogant. She epitomizes the Ventrue in their role as pillars of culture and respectability, and I rather love her for that. The artwork is especially nice. Like the Born Leader, it uses a lot of strong lines and white space to great effect. She's Social primary, of course, with a 3/3/4 split. I don't really begrudge this one having Appearance as her highest trait, as it seems to fit the character. Physical is secondary, with 2/3/3. I found this a little odd until I saw she had two dots in Athletics and realized that those natural talents probably include one or more sports. I admit that I kind of like the idea of a Ventrue who tries to play golf, tennis or the like whenever she can. Abilities include threes in Acting, Etiquette and Music, and twos in Empathy, Leadership, Subterfuge, Drive, and Linguistics. (She has a single dot in Firearms, which I probably would've given her in Melee, just because Fencing seems like something she'd be into.) Disciplines are, again, two in Dominate and one in Presence. I feel like maybe that is backwards, although I also think this is a character where Fortitude wouldn't be at all out of place. Backgrounds include four dots each in Generation and Resources. (I'd have expected that last one to be five.) Also twos in Herd, Mentor, and Retainers, and one in Status. Humanity is seven and Willpower six. I rather enjoy this character, and recall doing so when the book first came out. I think my favorite bit is the comment that people feel inferior around her because, well, they are. This is another one that I think is a great choice for chronicles that focus on Vampire as a game about intrigue and social conflict.

                                                    The Military Man
                                                    A somewhat older character. Went to military school, graduated West Point, served as an officer in Vietnam and later more covert work around the world, and finally Grenada. Then he was drafted into the Ventrue as part of their ongoing war against the enemies of the Camarilla. At first glance, this looks like a little bit of a one note stereotype. The image is clearly derived from a late 80s Arnold Schwartzenegger and the character quote is adapted from Apocalypse Now. However, the character does play into the clan's ideals of actually caring about the people serving under him, as well as trying to be fair and work with everyone toward the common goal. He's Physical primary and Social secondary. His abilities include three each in Drive, Firearms, Investigation, and Linguistics, with twos in Athletics, Brawl, Leadership, Stealth, and Medicine. This feels somewhat insufficient for a character described as "knowing all the tricks of leadership" and, well, generally being a military field commander. I feel his Leadership should be higher, and he could benefit from some Intimidation. And if any character was ever going to have the Military Science secondary ability from the second edition Players Guide, this would be the one. And it is at this point that I notice he's only got ten dots in Knowledges (and nine Skill and seven Talent). So I'm guessing he's actually supposed to have three dots in Military Science and they were left out. Oops. Disciplines are two in Fortitude and one in Presence. Backgrounds are evenly spread between Allies, Contacts, Generation, Resources, and Status. (One book from around this period, either Elysium or DSotBH, I forget which, introduced a Military Forces background, mainly for elders, but I could actually see it fitting with this character.) Humanity is seven and Willpower nine. As an effort to create a physically and martial focused Ventrue while still holding to the clan's modern day image, I think this is a good idea. However, I think the execution is lacking in terms of traits, or at least in regards to editing.
