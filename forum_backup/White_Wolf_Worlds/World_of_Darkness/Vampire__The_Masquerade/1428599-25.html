                    Originally posted by DrHappyAngry View Post
                    Sorry missed the last part, that's what I get for trying to read the forum in between working.

                    You can't entirely separate the two since they're two sides of the same coin, but I can give some examples of diplomatic shifts with long term consequences. From the top of my head, the formation of the modern state of Israel, the Syracusians switching to backing the Carthaginians, the Persians switching between backing the Pelopennesian League and the Delian League, Nixon going to China, Ben Franklin's trips to France for support, NAFTA, the formation of the European Union and the Catholic church arbitrating the dispute over the new world (and Africa and Asia) between Spain and Portugal. War, or the possibility of war, is tied up in a lot of these, but that's because you cannot separate it from diplomacy.
                    I am talking about sudden diplomatic reversals that aren't just tactical in nature and last for a long time. Thanks for your reply, but I don't think your examples qualify unfortunately.
                    • Not sure what you mean about the establishment of Israel. There were lots of things against its establishment, but the creation of Israel was a process that took decades and WWII to set the conditions for it. If the Arabs of 1947 decided a Jewish state was great, recognized its borders, and established normal relations with it - then it'd be a sudden reversal.
                    • Syracuse was a heavily divided city. It's not surprising that it would support one side versus the other depending on what temporary event put one faction in charge instead of another. An example of what you would want would be if Rome decided Hannibal was its best friend.
                    • Persia was the enemy of Greece. It used divide and conquer and tactics. It supported one over the other simply based on what would weaken the Greeks. What you actually want is something like Alexander the Great deciding to be best friends with the Persian Empire and not invading it.
                    • Nixon going to China was an expedient move to split the Communist Bloc. Mao all of a sudden didn't end the Cultural Revolution and support everything the US did in foreign policy, and Nixon didn't give the go ahead for China to invade Taiwan. Relations were often strained, and there was no formal alliance between them. These were limited tactical moves at a time when both sides saw the Soviet Union as their main threat, not a rapprochement similar to the century long politics that saw the UK becoming allies with France and the US by the early 20th century. This was simply the start of a process that might have seen Chinese and US become good friends and full allies by now - but we'd likely need some alternate history around 1989 and after.
                    • French support for the US also not surprising since both were enemies of the UK at the time, and of course an ongoing anti-French bias in the US as a result of them being culturally English meant the US immediately counterbalanced its French ties by being extremely favorable to Britain after the war. The US even abrogated its alliance with France as a result of the Jacobins killing Louis XIV.
                    • NAFTA doesn't represent any sudden reversal at all. US and Mexico were never enemies except when territorial dispute of the old Texas border lead it to war; Mexican insecurity at US dominance not withstanding. Closer economic ties in the form of an FTA is simply something that was part of normal politics of the time depending on domestic and international politics.
                    • The formation of the EU took place several decades after the first Treaty of Rome which in turn was over a decade after the end of WWII. It was a gradual rapprochement between France and Germany as a result of both of them being big losers of the war and needing a forum by which both can regain their power. In Germany's case, it was also an essential diplomatic move given that their country was divided between two hostile blocs, occupied, and they accepted they had lost two great wars in a bid to overcome the Western powers and weren't going to have a third shot. If the French Third Republic embraced Wilhelmine Germany after 1871 and decided to become allies, then you'd have that scenario.
                    • The Pope acting as mediator between Catholic countries is normal and routine. Neither Portugal or Span were hateful enemies of each other.
                    You are right that one can't separate war from diplomacy. But you're still misunderstanding my point. Sorry if it hasn't been clear.

                    With the possible exception of France and Germany in the EU - which I've already explained required complete ruin and defeat in war and took decades to achieve - none of your examples deal with hated rivals suddenly (and without long preparation time and changes) becoming allies and being permanent.

                    You can get temporary alliances based on immediate needs that resort to the status quo ante, but that is not the same. You might also get one side decisively beating the other in war, and one is forced to abide by the decisions of the other. But that's not the same either.

                    Something like the Sabbat Lasombra joining the Camarilla en masse would be like Rome becoming friendly with Hannibal, Alexander the Great choosing not to invade Persia but become friends, France teaming up with the Habsburgs; or the Pope joining forces with the Ottomans.

                    When we do see things like two bitter, historical enemies joining forces and that works out long term, we see the change happen over a long time and long term trends explain why these states now have common interests instead of being rivals. It's not sudden. It takes place over time so attitudes change as common interests converge.

                    In order to see something like huge numbers of Sabbat Lasombra abandon their sect and join the Camarilla (even handwaving the Vinculum issues - which we shouldn't do), we'd have to see that there was a huge division within the Sabbat Lasombra occurring over a long time so that such a betrayal makes sense because it completely destroys Lasombra prestige, clan pride, brood loyalty, etc. We'd need to see the reasons for such a cultural revolution. We don't because this is something new invented for this edition of the game, and it doesn't have the proper support in the setting history.

                    Maybe if the old Lasombra clanbooks had gone into detail about how the clan was bitterly divided between Lasombra holding to the old anarch ideals, and Lasombra who joined the sect afterwards merely to advance power, and that these two groups - old foes from the Anarch Revolt - were in constant turmoil despite being in the sect, then we'd have something like that. And maybe we're also told how some Lasombra AT were still in contact with one of these factions (or a part of them) and could act as a means of assisting such a betrayal. Then we could see how one faction decided to bolt (though here too, we'd need to address the Vinculum) and be successful given a sufficient pretext or proximate cause. But the old books never gave us that. The Sabbat Lasombra appeared unified without severe tension between its members, and always tried to kill their antitribu. We're not given any previous conditions that would justify it - hence a comparison to a Professional Wrestling story instead.

                    On the other hand, let's look at an alternate scenario - the Followers of Set join the Camarilla. This would have made much more sense given the lore of the setting. We know the Camarilla actually offered the Setites membership at the time of their founding. We know the Setites are enemies of the Sabbat because of the Serpents of the Light (and that sect's general hatred of antedeluvians). And the clan has been developed much further from its roots of Wyrm minions who twirl their moustaches and do evil. So if we're told that clan joined the Camarilla in some alternate-V5, we already have long standing historical elements that could explain why, and it'd be much easier to justify (though it would still take work). That would be something much more believable - prior ties, common enemies.
