    Originally posted by Hello View Post
    In the dark ages salubri clanbook, we learn that the salubri and tzimisce have a very close relationship. In fact it is stated that warriors and healers of clan salubri were able to learn more about their powers by watching the tzimisce torture their victims. So why is it that a clan that's supposed to be filled with paragons of justice are willing to consort with such sadistic monsters?

    It becomes kind of funny when you realize that they talk bad about almost every other clan, but one of the clans they seem to have only good things to say about are the tzimisce. Looking at this, its easy to see why the other clans were willing to turn their backs on the salubri, you cant preach justice and at the same time hangout with tyrants that enjoy torturing their subjects.

    -You can find it in Salubri Dark ages clanbook page 35

    Are you intentionally exaggerating for effect on this "they talk bad about almost every other clan" thing? I ask because in the clanbook you're talking about, the Healer Salubri providing perspective feels they legitimately wronged the Assamites as a clan in supporting the Crusades and regrets being unable to find forgiveness from them, the Warrior Salubri respects them as warriors and notes having been happy to leave a battlefield without killing one, and the third person objective narrative notes that the Salubri look at their former stance overall with embarrassment. They regard the Brujah as friends. A healer Salubri sacrificed their lives to save a Gangrel from being made into a Gargoyle, with several other Gangrel coming forward to note having received similar defense and the Salubri are noted as regarding the clan overall with respect. They speak of the Malkavians and Nosferatu well.

    That's.. *counts* five clans. Of the remaining 8ish, their main note on the Ravnos is being confused by them, they're obviously not going to like the Tremere, nor the clans actively handing them over to the Tremere, nor the Setites and the Baali. It otherwise notes that before the Ventrue started going "nah, kill em, that's fine", they had a positive opinion of the Ventrue that was let down by the whole "nah, kill em, that's fine" thing. Why would they not "talk bad" about clans actively engaged in making them dead? (You can score some points for inconsistency off their take on the Cappadocians I suppose, if that's mostly what this whole thing was an effort to do).

    It's less "kind of funny" and more, you seem to be leaving a lot of context out and exaggerating some content over others. Yeah, they have a close relationship with the Tzimisce. Yeah, it's not great. It's as though all clans are flawed, even ones ostensibly trying to strive for some measure of decency. And that the book presented things as being more complicated than "they had it coming for the Tzimisce thing".

    But otherwise

    It becomes kind of funny when you realize that they talk bad about almost every other clan
    Their opinions on other clans and the context these opinions exist in are more nuanced than you seem to be willing to acknowledge.

    I get that twitter style "thank you for coming to my TED talk" provoke people hot take posts are fun, I guess? Like trying to be that "debate me!" meme guy or something? But the game at least tries for some nuance when presenting things. This instead has a pretty solid Matt Bors "We Should Improve Society Somewhat" comics vibe honestly.

                                It's also pretty true to life that people will rationalize and accept all sorts of awful things when their lives are at stake, or even just when they can get something out of it. The healer notes he disagrees with what his host is doing, but we see he's ultimately decided survival is more important than his ideals, as did a variety of the Salubri who tried to shelter with the clan. The clanbook notes some not to fleeing to the East at all and instead stick to their principles/focusing on fighting the infernal/continuing to tend to people about them/trying to hide within their existing communities/etc. On the other hand, we as readers know they are going to die for that choice (and also most of the ones in the East later on, but that's not especially tragic). But Vampire tends to make grim statements like that about what ideology and cleaving to it can get you.

                                It's funny if you want to act like it's the only thing in there, I guess? For me it makes them more real. Beliefs get compromised, beliefs get followed imperfectly, or even badly. People who strive for virtue are very often the most blind to their own failures of living by that virtue. Even and especially glaring failures.

                                And yet, just sometimes, some of such people actually manage to pull off living by their ideals to a way that can make them seem worthwhile. Even dying by them for the sake of another.

                                Both these concepts are in the clanbook, and it's better for it that they both are, for my own experience. Your own reaction is apparently vastly different. Which again, sure. You've not especially replied to anything I brought up and instead just keep repeating the thing about the Tzimisce.                                 I mean we could also go beyond the clanbook to places where various Salubri had a far more negative view of the Tzimisce at the time, or that some of those who took shelter with them tried to make sure they did it with such handful of non grotesque Tzim as there might be, but it doesn't really feel like you want to discuss that, given the state of replies to others bringing up "well there's a lot of inconsistent setting writing on the Salubri". Which, there definitely is. So, clanbook it is I guess?

                                Also while we're there the idea that the DA Tzimisce were especially worse than the other clans doesn't really bear out in the overall setting material, or even one of the overarching themes of the Dark Ages era across several lines. They all have terrible crap they've been party to. The Ventrue alone committed some breathtaking atrocities during their wars with them in the Dark Ages period. The entire Shadow Reconquista was in many ways pretty unpleasant as multi clan wars go. Vicissitude just let the Tzim be more gross about what they do comparatively. This is otherwise the era where roads like the Road of Sin are major roads with significant multiclan devotion. The coarse, very often cruel unsubtlety with which vampires as an overall whole generally treated humanity is part of what ultimately leads to the Burning Times.

                                You seem to want to have some kind of statement of definitive absolutes here. I don't really feel like it bears out. Otherwise I guess I'd say something like "do you think the various Gangrel for whom various Salubri died then went 'yeaaah, but the Tzimisce tho!'" and act like one cancels out the other. I feel like it did a pretty solid job of that.

                                Also worth noting, I say "objective third person narration" at one point, but that itself isn't accurate on my part especially. Except for the rules writing and sample pc writeups, the entire book is always from some character's perspective, the narrator is himself another vampire. People tend to miss that about these things and thus leap to a definitive take on things when it's all.. not that. This was a problem with a lot of material from this era of White Wolf and fan reaction to it. From Changeling to Vampire, a lot of stuff got written in character, not out of character, and thereby marrying to a view of it as final, it has, you know, problems. Hence some of my qualifiers like "the book at least seems to want to try to say..."
