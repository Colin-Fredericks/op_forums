                He didn't allow it. He demanded it.

                My theory on Cappadocious goes something like this:

                Cappy had a clan, obsessed with dead stuff and mold and graves and such. Mostly pretty literal in their thinking. Maybe one of their main duties in Enoch was clearing out the dead bodies, and they grew to like their work a little too much. They were gravediggers, morticians, funeral pyre builders, the guys who drag around a wagon calling "bring out your dead!"

                Cappy set his sights higher. He wanted to really understand death. To grok it. So, he began embracing folks who had some real insight, at least for the times. Professional mourners, morticians, probably some mummifiers (is that the word?), priests of death gods and psychopomps, etc. Thinkers and mystics, rather than simple disposal technicians.

                But, he still had all these, well let's call them what they were, illiterate death fetishists and random hangers-on filling his payroll. So, he had the Feast of Folly to clean house. He found the low class grave diggers obsolete and so just... disposed of them. Cold. Out with the old clan, in with the new.

                Now, that seemed promising for a time, if unfocused. Then he met the priests of a certain religion whose deity (some guy named Yahweh, from some crappy backwater in the Levant), incarnated, died and returned from the dead, then went to the Other Side again. Oh, and His religion used blood to ritually transfer his divine emanations. Cappy figured he hit the motherlode. God or no god, if he came to earth as a human, he had a human soul. That means when he dies, he creates a wraith. If his followers believe in the power of His blood, then in the spirit world, that should be a way of siphoning his power. And, that is something, with the right disciplines, a 3rd gen can eat!

                But, how? He needed somebody better at dealing with the other side of the shroud than he was. I mean, when it came to animating corpses, controlling dead bodies through their blood, and spreading rotty stinky diseases, Cappy was the best of the best. But, on the other side of the Shroud? And going there to make wraiths not just do what you want, but think you are worshiping them in the process? He was a rank amateur. But, hey isn't that what Italian ancestor worship is all about! Hey, those guys up in the swamps of the Northern Adriatic seem pretty good at that stuff.

                So, he embraced Augie. But... the problem was the Giovanni were no one's tool. So, Cap needed a few things. He needed to clean out the losers on his payroll; Mortis was old hat. He also needed to establish a power base to fund his research. Oh, and he needed a more tractable clan. This business of Japheth and Lazarus and those losers having to be asked nicely every time Cap wanted them to do something small (like slaughtering the old clan, for example) was just... frustrating.

                Cappy already had high levels of Fortitude and Mortis. He just needed to learn some Dominate, which he did. Keeping in mind how Dominate lets you possess people, Fortitude keeps you whole and intact; and Mortis lets you think of the rules of physical life and death as vague suggestions... how powerful of a combo discipline would you need to create a failsafe where anyone who diablerizes you gets possessed instead. Permanently. Not a rhetorical question. Dominate 7, Fortitude 8, Mortis 8, maybe? Maybe with Auspex, which Cappy had, thrown in to cinch the psychic contest? It would take a century (or three) to design and learn, but, hey, Cap's not in a hurry.

                Poor old Uncle Augie was drinking from a firehose and got drowned. Kaput. Out like a light. And Cap had no doubt at all what the outcome would be. Cap was now not only in Augie's body, he had a sympathetic blood connection to every single dead body with blood related to Augie. Yep, he has a direct sympathetic connection to the entire clan, through which to use his Dominate and Auspex. The other Antes with that combo are Malkav and Tzimisce; think of the highly networked, non-focal existence they built in their bloodlines.

                That's the Giovanni today. An entire clan (and family) of blood puppets to an antedeluvian they thought they destroyed centuries ago. He's just more subtle than Malkv or Tzimisce. He doesn't feel the need for their attention seeking behavior.

                Clan Giovanni built a powerbase, worked to tear down the shroud, researched Necromancy and rituals that will empower them on the Other Side, and maintained a presence in the church of that blood-death god that piqued Cap's interest. They were even nice enough to take out the garbage, which was his old, now obsolete, clan. They did everything he needed to prepare to diablerize a god.

                Oh, and while most folks think he's insane for wanting to diablerize God, let's remember Cappadocius "knew him when". Back in the day, on the old block, Yahweh was just one of many in a pantheon of Semitic tribal spirits who managed to build up a following in the Neolithic Levant, roughly equal in power to, say, Set or a maybe a second gen. Cappadocius believes (and in the WoD cosmology might even be right) that Yahweh managed to leverage sleeper belief to catapult Himself into the top spot in the spirit world during the Age of Faith. But the Renaissance, Enlightenment and the 20th century knocked him down a few pegs, mojo-wise. Most sleepers either don't really believe in Him or see Him as Morgan Freeman in a white suit. Yahweh is slipping from lack of belief, and soon, maybe, will be vulnerable. Then Cappadocius will feed on the divine soul of Yahweh, Himself.

                And both Clan and Family Giovanni are built to help him do just that.

                Every few months someone on the boards posts a topic asking what theme songs apply to the WoD. My nomination for Cappadocius:

                "He was born on a Christmas Day, when the New York Times said God is dead and the war's begun..."

                    I've always been torn on Cappadocius. On the one hand it seems preposterous that an upstart like Augustus could consume arguably the most mystically accomplished Antediluvian. On the other hand I also kind of hate the meme that the Antediluvians are impossible to defeat to destroy no matter what you do. The possibility ought to exist that Cappadocius just done fucked up.

                    I split the difference. In my own canon Cappadocius foresaw the Giovanni's treachery and rather than quash it chose to try and turn it to his advantage, using the Usurpation as a new Feast of Folly (he never seems to have liked his own clan all that much) and using Giovanni's diablerie of him as a sacrifice in a terrifying ritual that would allow him to achieve even greater power. Except something went wrong. He missed something. Augustus consumed him and the Usurpation purged his clan almost completely. Some say his soul is still out there somewhere. Not a Wraith, not a Vampire, not a Spirit, but something else. How could this be if Augustus is indeed now of the third generation? That is something truly known only to the most ancient necromancers.

                    What to me stands in for the Giovanni's "Antediluvian dread" is the Premascines. They isolate themselves from the rest of the family and nearly all interactions with them turn hostile if prolonged. Why then does Augustus allow them to be venerated? Why the pomp and offerings? Why are not those family members with the Sanguine Incongruity destroyed as abominations? Because Augustus is afraid. He could take one or two of the Premascines on his own no problem, but he's isn't sure that on his best night all the Premascines couldn't roll up on him together and murder him if the spirit suddenly took them.

                                    I acutally ran an entire Orpheus campaign based around the notion that Cappadocious had succeeded in diablerising the Elohim. Things had gone well for him at first but within years the power started to eat him from the inside out because it was too much for him to control. Although all other vampire clans were wiped out come 50 years of Godhood he simply expired and the power remained as a burning, untapped ball of burning white potential in the spirit world. Anyone who tried to control or absorb it was destroyed utterly by it in seconds or less. From then on the power of God was controlled by nobody, could be controlled by nobody, and the universe started to die.

                                    I started the players thinking they were in a perfectly normal modern day setting and then occasionally fed them little cards with answers to questions that would come up later. 'When did World War 2 end?' turned out to be '1962 with the dropping of a nuclear bomb on Berlin'. Add to that the cold war was still going on in 2013 and various other enticing 'this world is noticeably worse' pieces of trivia. By the time the game began the spirit world and real world were getting closer because the entire world was circling the drain and about to be subsumed by another reality. In essence one universe (my take on the mysterious known in-game as 'grandmother') was consuming another, and everyone would be lost to the worse-than-hell non-existence of being diablerised. There was no way to reverse the process and the campaign just got bleaker and bleaker as they tried and failed to restore any hope.

                                    I never really gave the players, (all humans that could step in to the spirit realm) a solution to the problem, the end was the end, but they found an interesting half-victory. The final Cappadocians left were busy escaping to the new universe that was consuming their own dying one, (to a universe in which Cappy failed) and showed the players the way, but to pass through a person needed to be undead, and to have undergone a transition to escape the spectral hive mind and regain their humanity. They needed to become what was known in Orpheus canon as an 'Orphan-Grinder'.In an unexpected final move a PC, a young Hindi physicist named Devi Nagra (who had been through the worst of it all including learning that her faith was, apparently, false) absorbed what was left of the power of the Elohim and used her last thought before being obliterated utterly to kill the entire world and force them through the harrowing transition. Being a spectre in an alien reality, she reasoned, was better than being compressed in to nothingness.

                                    As the storyteller, I was floored by this move.

                                    Her moment of godhood lasted less than a second before her body and soul were destroyed utterly by the untamed power but she saved potential billions from a fate worse than hell in doing so. As the previously living found themselves flushed in to the spirit world the first thing they saw was the burning white figure of Devi as Kali the Destroyer pointing their way to the new world.

                                    The moment was so palpable and poignant that, I kid you not, two of the players were actually crying.

                                    Anyways, the typical canonical approach is that both Cappadocious and Augustus failed. Cappadocious' soul escaped diablerie, (as is laid out explicitly in the Giovanni chronlices) and most of what was left, (though not all of him) was destroyed in the 6th maelstrom. I like this solution best because it both avoids the 'you can't ever beat an antediluvian' trope but also shows that Augustus wasn't up to the task. It also puts an interesting spin on the Giovanni - that Uncle Auggie has to play the part of a 3rd generation vampire but simply lacks that kind of power and so his bluff has been getting thinner and thinner over the centuries. That by modern nights his own clan is starting to suspect he is weak and desperate.

                                                I prefer the idea that Augustus didn't diablerize Cappadocius at all. And that the purge of the old clan didn't happen as such, they just sort of waned while the Giovanni waxed.

                                                Way I see it, Augustus probably wanted to diablerize old Cappy, and took steps towards that goal. He had his more loyal childer take gangs to strike out at the other fourth gen Cappadocians, eliminating his competition in one fell swoop. Some perished, others survived by going into hiding. At the same time, Auggy came to Cappadocius, seeking to eat him.

                                                As he was in position to do the deed, Cappadocius woke up and smiled at him. He said, "Augustus, I already know you planned to devour me, body, mind, and soul. I also know you seek to gain control of the Clan for yourself." He then rose to his feet, patted Augustus on the shoulder, and took off a piece of clothing that Clan Cappadocius identified their founder by (a scarf or amulet or something). "While I'd hate to disappoint you, lad," Cappadocius said, "take consolation in knowing that one of those things will happen tonight."

                                                He then handed the piece to Augustus, and walked away, never to be seen again. Auggy, absolutely terrified at this moment, could do nothing but stand frozen on the spot for a minute. Then, regaining his composure, he donned the item, walked out, and began telling everyone that he totally diablerized Cappadocius, and had all the powers of an Antedeluvian. Any of the old clan who protested this usurpation were hunted down, while the rest of the Cappadocians decided it was easier to keep their heads down. The Ventrue and Giovanni had their war for a while, and then have their peace agreement.

                                                And from there, the Giovanni simply out-bred their Cappadocian cousins. This being because the Cappadocians, ever since the Feast of Folly, were deathly afraid of inviting their founder's ire by embracing too wildly. No one wanted to start a new Feast, so they took childer sparingly. Even after Augustus claimed to have diablerized Cappadocius, not all of the Clan necessarily believed Cappadocius was actually gone. Either because Augustus was lying, or because the diablerie wasn't the end of old Cappy. So even after Augustus's ascension to head of "Clan" Giovanni, the old guard decided it wasn't worth taking chances.

                                                Centuries of time, accident, the Anarch Revolt, and simple attrition wore down the numbers of the Cappadocians, until they were little more than a bloodline. Meanwhile, the Giovanni took childer freely from their mortal family, and proceeded to amass a great deal of temporal and supernatural power. Some Elders of other Clans remember the days when the Cappadocians were not just a Clan, but a High Clan (at least in most parts of Europe). But most of the younger generations have never heard of the gravediggers, let alone seen one. Only a few Camarilla domains have Cappadocians in them - insular and paranoid broods whose antipathy towards the Giovanni upstarts is outweighed by their paranoia of old Cappy. Meanwhile, the Sabbat has a slowly building Cappadocian Antitribu population, who understandably take a militant attitude towards Cappadocius and his fellow Antedeluvians.
