                                V5 is very much about the simplification of the gameline. You can see it in everything from the way the Sabbat were treated to the way Disciplines were written.                                             Throwing my hat in on how to handle Eastern Vampires.

                                            Hungry Dead are, contrary to popular belief among Western Cainites, a unique clan of Kindred that have most of their Strongholds in East Asia. Their exact clan of origin is difficult to determine, both because of forgotten pre-history as well as the alterations of bloodlines produced by their unique process of Dharmic initiation. Most theories either hold them as being connected to the Tzimisce or the Cappadocians (due to their mutable natures and their associations with mortality) while other theories suggest that they developed as a unique Caitiff line. The Hungry dead generally reject the Caine myth and so do not put a lot of stock in pondering any prehistorical connection with Western Vampires. Instead they hold to their stories of the Yama Kings, powerfully ancient divine beings that punished the first of the Hungry Dead, dragging arrogant mortals into hell for their crimes. However these progenitors were not willing to accept the torment and fled from their imprisonment back to the world of the living, but not before the Yama Kings marked them with their curse, forcing them to live in shadows and subsist upon the lives of others to keep them from the call back to hell. Hungry Dead soon after learned that the curse could be passed on to others, tainting them with their impurities. Some spread their curse out of spite for the living, some out of a twisted sense of justice towards fellow sinners and criminals, some out of a pursuit of self-indulgent hunger for power, some simply to alleviate the loneliness of their cursed unlives. Whatever the reason, they spread throughout most of Asia and remain the most prolific clan there. One of the quirks of the blood of the hungry dead is that it does not appear to pass its potency as easily to childer. Newly embraced childer are quite uniformly low in potency, a side effect of which being that most arise from their embrace in a state of frenzy, a state that most Hungry dead claim is the result of one's soul temporarily passing into the torments of the Yama Kings in Hell before being brought back to the land of the living. As a result of the lack of pedigree that is offered by one's sire, as well as a mythology that holds the first among their kind as the first of a line of sinners rather than any great personhood, and the concept of Generation, as it is traditionally held among Western Kindred, is largely disregarded among the Hungry Dead.

                                            However what truly marks the hungry dead as distinct from Cainites are the Dharmas. Part blood sorcery, part spiritual philosophy, part social unit, the dharmas are a process of internal alchemy whereby the Hungry Dead begins a process of spiritual and supernatural enlightenment. Many Western Kindred have remarked upon the idea that the Dharmas are a form of Golconda path, and indeed, some kindred historians have pondered upon stories of an individual named by the Hungry Dead as Zao-Lat as one of those who helped standardize the five Dharmas within the Supernatural society of East Asia.

                                            Whatever the case, functionally the Dharma path is a a long process by which a kindred is able to change themselves by holding to a disciplined mindset and regimen that is said to bring spiritual insights that in turn change their bodies. This not only seems to compensate for the difficulty of many Hungry dead face beginning at uniformly low blood potencies, it also seems to bypass many of the limits that generation might often present. Theoretically, any kindred following a Dharma could improve themselves without the limits of birthright, the upper limits being unknown but often spoken of in legends. However following Dharmas are a difficult path, and it is often easy for even the most dedicated adherent to even momentarily stray and ruin centuries of progress.

                                            While Hungry Dead are the most common variety of vampire in East Asia, they are not the only one, other clans have come to settle in Asia throughout history. Many among them have fully embraced the Dharmas, both out of an appeal to such methods as well as cultural pressure. Within most supernatural societies in East Asia, adherence to Dharmas is considered an essential moderating factor upon the often wild and disruptive actions of Kindred. Not only do the philosophies provide a motivation for a kindred to hold themselves to a higher purpose, but the teachers of Dharmas in a community are usually responsible for the actions of their students, and thus help keep them in check. By contrast, kindred who do not follow Dharma are usually considered dangerously unbound, regardless of their clan of origin, and such individuals are usually encouraged to move on their way.

                                            While Western Kindred have been blissfully ignorant of most of the details regarding their Eastern counterparts and vice versa, modern globalization has torn down those barriers and forced the two societies to confront one another. While many traiditionalists would prefer to continue ignoring one another as they stick to their own traditional hemispheres of power, others have been making moves, either diplomatically or with hostility. Cainites rode the ships of colonizers and established bases of power in many places such as Japan, Hong Kong and the Philippines, while Hungry Dead attempted to establish their own toeholds in California and further afield. Not every interaction has been a bloody one however. While it is not common, Western philosophically minded Kindred have been initiated into the the Dharmas before, while on the other side, ambitious hungry dead have found the politics of their Western counterparts more reactive and to their liking than the often glacial pace of a society rooted in the passive adherence to philosophies dictated by ancient beings more interested in their personal enlightenment than on the unlives of their followers.

                                            So yeah, that's my basic headcanon. Hungry Dead are a clan of Kindred, but they reject the Caine myth even if a lot of Cainites might think that all of these Yama Kings sound like some kind of Antediluvian and/or their Methuselah childer. And more importantly they still tie themselves to Dharmas which would mechanically tie their generation and blood potency to their morality bar (which would have its own differences from humanity based on their Dharma), as well as granting them some more unique abilities (Discipline powers, as well as some of the bits like the ability to feed on breath or life force instead of blood as one advances). As mentioned, other clans could join a Dharma, but I'd imagine that would effectively alter them to some extent, like changing in-clan disciplines or even their curse to some extent.

                                                    Originally posted by AkatsukiLeader13 View Post

                                                    While I understand where this is coming from, I have always found it head scratching that so many fans insist the WoD vampire origin myth means that Christianity is objectively real in the setting. Regardless of the origin myth we are told, there is absolutely nothing that establishes the Abrahamic religions are real or correct.

                                                    When I first read the second edition VtM corebook many decades ago, I was already familiar with the idea that vampirism has its origins in the antedeluvian world as a curse with a founder among vampires. Why? That was basically the same story Marvel Comics used to explain vampires in their setting except that the antedeluvian world was Atlantis, the founder was Varnae, and the curse was a result of them being sorcerers using the Darkhold. Much later, its essentially the same story Mike Mignola used with the Black Goddess and Hyperborea in the Hellboy comics.

                                                    The mentions of Caine, God, Nod, and everything else are just a patina that can be easily scrapped away. You can replace them with Chthon, Nyarlathotep, Tiamat, Atlantis, Hyperborea, Mu, and lots of other things.

                                                    Do I think the vampires at the time of Alexander of the Great or Julius Caesar believe the eldest was Caine cursed by the Jewish Jehovah? Absolutely not. They most likely associated the antedeluvian world with the Flood of Deucalion and had some other explanation as to why vampirism existed (in my chronicles I tied it into the Orphic tradition of the Titans dismembering and eating Dionysus, his ichor mingling with others like how vitae mingles with human blood). Likewise the vampires of Ancient Sumeria wouldn't use a Jewish tradition that was not going to be used for thousands of years in the future! They'd be talking about the flood of Gilgamesh and might be referencing the antedeluvians as the Anunnaki.

                                                    As so much that the origin of vampires is true as portrayed in the books, we only need to take seriously the core story. That there was a world before this one. The origins of vampires occurred there. That world was destroyed. And there were 13 survivors of that world that were the progenitors of all vampires today.

                                                    Everything else is just details interpreted by vampires using their own cultural knowledge from their mortal days to understand what they've been told. The reason we have the Biblical centered myth of the corebook is because much of the world was ruled by either Christians or Muslims four two thousand years. I'm sure that any vampires originating in China likely use their own Flood traditions to explain the previous world, its destruction, and the vampire founders who survived. Same with other cultures. There just needs to be enough points in common so that the essential story is preserved, not the specific details.
