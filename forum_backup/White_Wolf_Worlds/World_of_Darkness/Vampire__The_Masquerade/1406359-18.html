    I initially approach the Sabbat from a meta perspective. What's their purpose in the game? I think it's to serve as the home for the evil vampires in pop culture depictions pre-Anne Rice. They're the Satan worshipping vampires from Hammer Horror; Kurt Barlow causing the entire town of Salem's Lot to become vampires; the heartless vampires who turn a variety of victims when they appear and torment their friends and family like in Count Yorga. But they do it without the traditional cape and evening wear that had been cliche since Bela Lugosi. They're the boogey men. But they're updated for the modern age like the Camarilla was.

    They originally lost to the Camarilla because they're already obsolete, but they can thrive when society becomes decadent.

    As an ST, I think they're a great tool. I liked early edition Sabbat which introduced the various Paths of Enlightenment (though I think the Revised rewrites are better) and depicted the sect divided by a variety of means (the various Paths of Enlightenment; Loyalists versus Centralizers; Inquisition versus Infernalists; various clan feuds (Panders, Tremere AT, Tzimisce, etc.); and even Nomadic packs versus Founded Covens. I found them very compelling.

    I didn't like the Revised metaplot which tossed all of that aside just so there could be a Crusade and the introduction of even more bloodlines just because. It became less interesting, and I didn't find the handwave explanations compelling. But there were some good ideas even in all this dreck.

    I like the Sabbat as a glorious mess because it gives me lots of tools as an ST, and if I wanted, I could run very different Sabbat games depending on what elements I wanted to focus on the local city. I could have fairly high humanity (for Sabbat) Loyalist Nomad packs; murderous founded covens ruling the ruins of Detroit; or devotees of the Path of Night terrifying mortals, but never quite killing them because that'd miss the point.

    I never ran Sabbat games, but as the occasional NPCs in my chronicles, they really caused a lot of problems and introduced confusion to the PCs because they were never consistent. And uncertainty produces fear.

    I like that within the Sabbat, the various factions prevent unity - at least in its original depiction. For every Lasombra ultra-conservative wanting a centralized structure and soldiers that obey orders without question; there's a charismatic ductus of a Nomad Loyalist pack undermining that archbishop's authority. Whatever is true, is only true locally.

        What Ever Happened to the Red Question

        Adeyemi returns with a profile of the Red Question, the collection of Anarch hackers who targeted Camarilla vampires. The name of Red Question as a group itself is a reference to what they hoped to be an armor piercing question, asking young Camarilla vampires why they believed in and obeyed Camarilla elders.

        Adeyemi discusses some of the things the Red Question did (including revealing secrets and gutting elder finances) but also spends a lot of time on the titular question; where did they go and what happened to them?

        This is a section that disappoints me on a meta level, or out of character level. It is not necessarily badly written but it should have been thought through better. Every section of the book should provide fodder for games and opportunities for players. Saying the Red Question, an Anarch vampire version of something like Anonymous, is simply gone ends too many game possibilities (it is suggested they were wiped out by the SI). Anonymous has returned, and so of course can the Red Question – but that is beyond the scope of this section.

            Revelations of the Dark Mother

            This is a good section in the book, as it provides insights into the beliefs and methods of the cults of Lilith, the Bahari. The short version is pain, in all its forms, is central to what they believe and how they operate. This section of the book includes statements from three vampires, including Agata “Outstar” Starek and Rachel Dolium, who compiled the “Revelations of the Dark Mother” book. The Bahari are usually vampires so it is terrifying, if you are paying attention.

            Pain is a teacher, and arguably everything we learn is related to pain on some level. Maybe it is physical pain (don’t stick your hand in a fire) or maybe it is social pain (you should be embarrassed by the “F” you got on the test), but pain plays a role in learning, and learning plays a role in growth, development, and evolution. The relative power of vampires means it is harder for them to experience pain, so it is arguably harder for them to grow, develop, and evolve. Likewise, pain can teach empathy – so the same relative power of vampires means it is hard for them to experience empathy.

            On a related note I doubt Tzimisce (a clan almost synonymous with the infliction of pain) are often members of the Bahari – pain is for other creatures, not for the Tzimisce. It’s been a thousand years and the Fiends are still pretty salty about the Tremere inflicting pain on them, after all.

            It is also interesting to compare the Bahari with the Church of Caine. The Bahari are considered a group of cults, while the Church of Caine gets to call itself a church. The term “church” carries social clout and respect, while the term “cult” conjures suspicious and a lack of respectability. As described in the texts, the Church of Caine is a structured organization providing rhetoric to justify its members’ behavior and structural methodology. By comparison, the Bahari have little formal structure and little formal rhetoric. The Bahari would be uncomfortable to be a member of while the Church of Caine would be a comfortable one to be a member of – and what does comfort teach?

            In the Chicago Folio members of the Cult of Lilith catch an interloper and potential spy and saboteur. They stake him, step him up in front of a mirror and then skin him – noting he gets to watch as his skin gradually grows back. If they had been really angry with him they would have set him up in front of a wide-screen TV and made him watch the 2015 Fantastic Four movie.
