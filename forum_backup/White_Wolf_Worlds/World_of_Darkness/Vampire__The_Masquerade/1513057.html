    How do you deal with 'invincible' characters?

    05-28-2023, 06:53 AM
    So some combinations of powers make individuals virtually unkillable and yet they remain super dangerous. How do you deal with that? Claws, fangs and burning blade work on everything? Wards? The enemy mysteriously getting a shipment of holy hand grenades and sunlight bombs? Do you see nigh-invincible characters as a bug or a feature?

    Mist form + damaging thaumaturgy
    Incorporeal passage + damaging thaumaturgy or mental disciplines.
    Elemental mastery (turn yourself into a small steel statue or ball with power 4, animate yourself with 3. With such dense steel, you should be virtually impervious to the bulk of small arms. Also, as far as I know, there's no rules for how damage to your form relates to your health) Even if we decide on rules like "well you're steel but given your size we should reduce your strength to 1" there's still things like being allowed to use Potence in the new form.
    Bloodform+Acid blood (+ fire immunity.)

    Also, Donning the Mask of Shadows RAW won't end when you attack or interact with the world, so while you're not truly invincible it could be said that this power lets you breeze through everything except for very specialized preparations (which most characters won't reasonably have, unless the ST is being a dick) Not to mention you can use this power on the rest of your coterie. perhaps something like a bloodstain on the weapon could be a still-difficult to spot giveaway, but even then you'd have to consider things like blind fighting rules..

                              Take it as a feature.

                              "Invincible" character builds are for the most part only possible by spending large portions of their XP and Freebies on things that are combat focused.

                              Classic WOD is rarely so focused on combat that this is a major problem. Sure, your tenebrous form thaumaturge character can mow down the average ancillae, but that doesn't protect your character's friends and family if you piss off the wrong person, it doesn't prevent you from degenerating your humanity score if you go on a murder spree, and it sure as hell doesn't get your character the things they want now that they gimped their social skills to become a death machine.

                              Honestly, "Invincible" characters tend to be the most vulnerable at the tables I've seen.

                                                          I deal with invincible characters out-of-character by talking to the player and figuring out what they want from the experience, explaining what I want from the experience, explaining how they might be disrupting other people's fun, and trying to come to a resolution. Mechanical tricks and tactical sleights-of-hand are fun, definitely, but they absolutely need to seem like natural outcroppings of the plot and not vindictive lightning bolts from above.

                                                          I say this because the power balance in Vampire (and especially other WoD splats) is more an out-of-character thing than an in-character thing, more a meta-idea than one codified by the system itself. The books have never been shy about telling the players that they're going to be crushed and that they're always going to be weaker and This Isn't D&D, What Do You Mean You Want the Cool Powers, Fuck Off. The power balance is meta because since the books encourage a less-empowered player experience and a more "heavy-handed" ST approach (as opposed to a "challenge rating" system-based approach), when the ST takes a heavy hand, that hand can take many forms. Often, it takes the form of some unfairness (to be fair, this could be real or perceived unfairness), and then the true balance of the game is revealed: the ST controls what happens at the table. The Players control whether they are at the table. If they don't like how the ST balances the game, things change drastically in terms of power balancing when they realize they can walk.

                                                          All of a sudden, sure, Elder McFuckOff killed Jim's hypersoldier, but now Jim's gone and he ain't coming back anytime soon and the nearest replacement is two states over, or is on a server so now we're playing at least semi-remotely instead of in-person and using dice bots, or can't make our usual schedule for whatever reason, or is a much bigger asshole, or wants to play a different tone of game, or will not stop hitting on the female players...you get the idea. So either the ST makes concessions to replace their player (and the incoming player gets to learn exactly why a spot's open), or the party is now a vamp down - and while The Game's Not About Combat, sure, it's still bad to be down a whole character's worth of disciplines, skills, connections, and abilities, even if they were 90-100% "kill anything that moves". Suddenly your new party of Party minus Jim is in this world where either Elder McFuckOff just vanishes and doesn't punish Jim's known associates, indicating that they were a targeted strike on Jim's ego, or Elder McFuckOff does stick around and puts their boot on the remaining PCs' necks, in which case they probably saw Jim walk out the door and are wondering if they can make it through before traffic backs up. That's not even taking into account whether or not Jim owns the house everyone plays at, who drives whom, who lives with whom, who brings the tasty snacks to sessions...there's a lot of out-of-character politics that can very quickly come into play.

                                                          Just talk to them. Good players are as rare as Salubri - treasure them.
