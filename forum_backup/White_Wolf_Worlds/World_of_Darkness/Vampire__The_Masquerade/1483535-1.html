            In general the problem with True Faith is its specific set of associated powers.

            Faith is a complex sentiment, and can be tied to anything. A religion, philosophy, group, idea, it doesn't matter, and in its original write-up the True Faith trait recognizes this. Being positive or negative is also an extremely narrow view of theology. To me that should put the Society of Leopold right away without TF, because I can't conceive their Fire and Brimstone style of Catholicism as positive. And there are people with pretty humanitarian views through the veneration of demons.

            But the actual powers of True Faith given are all heavily based on Christian imagery and stories. They make perfect sense from a Christian POV, but for other beliefs they seem really off, you either need some (or a lot of) effort to even justify them, or they simply make no sense for that belief system.

            So, while for the concept I think it is a perfectly acceptable option and may be quite thematic depending on the chronicle and the character, in practice it is the rules that may fail you and give inappropriate effects. You would either have to carefully consider this faith for the powers to make sense for the character's belief, or you would have to tweak True Faith itself to offer more adequate benefits.

            And just to be clear, it has nothing to do with the "positive belief" nonsense. Take for example the very first power, the ability to ward off Vampires. A believer In Candomblé would possibly make a ritual offering for the Orisha to request protection for the rest of the week. A Muslim could recite the word of Allah to force the Kindred to follow suit and respect them as Faithful, but for neither does it make any sense to brandish something and just pray to make vampires step back, it just doesn't make a lot of sense for them.

                I believe some attempt at emulation important in the World of Darkness. It is supposed to be our world through a mirror darkly. So if you depart too much, it's not the WoD but some kind of fantasy world only vaguely similar to ours.

                In my chronicles, I only allow True Faith for the monotheistic religions, although I would allow something similar for Buddhism that is flavor texted appropriately. There's enough real world folktales and legends that show Buddhist monks and such doing similar enough things.

                For the historical pagan religions I'd allow appropriate Paths of Sorcery to reflect what myths and legends allow for them. If you want Isis to protect you, better find an actual priestess of Isis who knows the Path of Enchantment so she can craft a protective amulet for you.

                I'd never use it for any kind of demon worship. That's we why we have Infernalism and their infernal investments and appropriate Dark Thaumaturgy and Sorcery.

                Almost every religion has some tradition of bestowing the blessing or protection of the divine against supernatural evil. It's just a matter of what best emulates it.

                When it comes to religious "magic" or miracles, I don't believe in doubling up. If the person's "powers" are best emulated by something like a Path of Sorcery or some other Numina, you don't get True Faith. I even hold this for the monotheists. If a Christian NPC has some kind of Theurgy, I don't think they should have True Faith. If you are attempting to control forces beyond mortal ability, you're not allowing yourself to become a vessel for the Almighty.

They're not special techniques where faith plays just a minor role in, they're not secret knowledge or the power of an initiate in the mysteries or in the priesthood. They're legends about the power of the divine when channeled through conviction by the faithful, regardless of education, holiness or caste.

                                                A note about true faiths effects, the level one power doesn't rejects enemies condemned by the faithful(if that were true there would be a lot of minorities suffering from magic splash damage).

                                                Faith seems to mainly Affect creatures that the God Itself would have issue with. The heavily christian-centric true faith presented in WOD repels vampires, demons, and ghosts that refuse to pass on, but it doesn't actually harm mages or werewolves for example. One of the books has it that True Faith prevents Garou from using Rage or shape-shifting, but Gnosis gifts are fine. People with true faith can resist or dispell True magick, but the mage themselves is left untouched and they don't suffer any paradox related effects.

                                                This might suggest that the god of the true-faith user only intervenes to protect their faithful most of the time, and only goes out of their way to hurt things that are already on it's kill list. A non-abrahamic faith might not be harmful to demons(though still protecting the faithful from demonic magic like vs a sphere mage) simply because that god doesn't recognize demons as "evil" so much as part of how the world is.
