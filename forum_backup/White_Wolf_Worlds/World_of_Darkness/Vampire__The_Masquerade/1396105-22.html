    Originally posted by Pleiades View Post

    first, Mages are not a good example, because they do have two different splats, one of which represents traditional mages (hedge magicians)

    second, I'm not against vampires not conforming to legends (hungry dead don't conform either),
    what I'm against is making hungry dead and other "exotic vampires" into other clans/bloodlines,
    we already have 7 to 13 clans, which are already hard enough to lump in together due to how different some of them are,

    if we need more "exotic vampires", we should make them into different splats,

    and by exotic, I don't mean "Asian" or "African", I mean flesh-eaters, Risen, soul suckers, xenomorphs, blood mages, or anything that would be sufficiently different from a cainite

    if there's flesh-eater vampires that can't bloodbond, let them be a different splat rather than a clan that has an antideluvian that likes steak

    just my two cents
    I feel that's needlessly complex when the central appeal of V:TM at least at the beginning was each clan allowed you to play a different kind of vampire. You want to be Nosferatu, you play Nosferatu. You want to be shape-shifting monster Dracula, you play Gangrel. You want to be a Lost Boy or Near Dark, you play Brujah. You want to be Scholomance studying Satanist wizard vampires [which were a thing] you play Tremere.

    And so on.

    I feel like they didn't go nearly far enough, though. Why not reveal dozens more Clans of local vampire legends?

                Because they weren't going for local vampire legends.

                Vampire is a satire.
                The core seven clans are each making fun of something in normal mundane life, in particular society's elite parasites; The Ventrue that owns everything, The Toreador is a vile socialite, the Brujah is the guy that owns some and is mad at the elite that he doesn't own more, or he's a union boss, the Tremere are a professional firm, The Gangrel is a rural type, the Nosferatu is the unpleasant guy they deign to work with because he gets them what they need and the Malkavian is the lunatic who could be any of them.

                They were never meant to meet some vampire myth, they were meant to take the piss. They were an exaggerated mockery of people in the real world. That's the core seven. The other clans that came after were half overlapping with the seven, half crude caricature of foreigners from an anglo-american perspective.

                        Actually, I’m fine with there being stuff that doesn’t fit into the playable character types. Nothing wrong with pulling the rug out from under what players think they know, and the setting should never feel completely filled in.

                        My main problem is when there’s a double-standard and people insist the exotic critters have to be Asian etc. A core principle is that the Kindred are not specifically European and never have been. Non-Kindred critters shouldn’t be restricted to non-European locales or ethnicities, and we have to take care not to fall into the trap of othering and exoticizing those places and people (which WoD unfortunately has quite a history of).

                        ​At the same time, it’s not wrong to create types of Kindred based on worldwide vampire legends. That’s what Kindred are, essentially: a critter that incorporates elements of various sources but isn’t beholden to any of them.

                                Originally posted by Black Flag View Post
                                Non-Kindred critters shouldn’t be restricted to non-European locales or ethnicities, and we have to take care not to fall into the trap of othering and exoticizing those places and people (which WoD unfortunately has quite a history of).
                                To be fair; given their metaphysics, I’ve always thought of the Wan Kuei as a type of Risen, with the abilities gained their spiritual paths (the Dharmas) being the primary distinction between Eastern Risen/Wan Kuei and Western Risen.

                                Given that Golconda is believed to be a variation on those paths and allowed access to supernatural disciplines beyond generational limits (i.e. even a 16th Gen could theoretically gain “Plot Device/Rank 10” in any discipline if they achieved and maintained Golconda) it doesn’t seem out of place that a Risen following the origins of that path might have similar supernatural gifts.

                                In this case the “exotic” isn’t the creature, it is the specific spiritual beliefs of a culture that makes them exotic... and if you really wanted to emphasize that, introduce a Western Risen who has become a Wan Kuei by adopting one of the Dharma paths.

                                Side-bar: Original Recipe Mummies could also be seen as variant Risen; they just had a True Mage cast a spell permanently linking the Wraith to its corpse and enabling it to rebuild said corpse using spiritual essence built up in the Dark Umbra. Exotic again being the particulars of how a specific culture interacted with the spiritual concepts of death and resurrection.

                                Anyway, if they really are starting work on a Wr5 to coincide with development of the Wraith video game, perhaps the solution to handling the Wan Kuei in an appropriate manner lies in changing the game line they are associated with and making the implied connection to Risen canon.
