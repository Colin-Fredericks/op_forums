    V20 - The Alexandrian Kraina

    02-08-2026, 08:13 AM
    Below is a first draft of a "Library Kraina". Would be open to any feedback! The level 4 power is definitely the weirdest, and while I and my friends like it a lot, I want to be sure it's as succinct and well-written as possible, given how complex it is.

    The Alexandrian Kraina

    Developed in Prague by an Empiricist Tzimisce of the dawning Renaissance, this Kraina relies on connections with spirits of knowledge and the written word. Named for the "Great Library of Alexandria", the 'homeland' of this Kraina exists wherever books are gathered in a place of learning.

    Each power below is rolled at a difficulty of the power's level +4, -2 if it is the Koldun's Primary Kraina (as standard). Each power has a blood cost associated with it, however this cost is waived if the Koldun is inside a library of some kind. Please also note that a Koldun's rating in this Kraina can never exceed their rating in the Academics Knowledge.

    • Native of Alexandria

    At first contact with the spirits of libraries and tomes, the Koldun learns to access knowledge held in many different spaces, guided by knowledge from the other side. This might permit the Koldun to study a topic while sat in a tent with no access to their tomes, or to find their way through the cryptic maze of an ancient library as if they had walked it for a lifetime.

    System: Spend 1 Blood Point, then roll Intelligence + Occult. On a success the Koldun gains the benefits of the Library Background, with a rating equivalent to their level in this Kraina, for one scene. If cast inside a Library, instead gain the full benefits of that Library (navigating it with a practiced ease and familiarity which might shock an onlooker), and access a Library rating equal to your rating in this Kraina +1, or the Library rating +1, whichever is higher. Note that while this ability can draw the Koldun towards a particular tome within a library, they must still make their way to it; the spell simply guides them along the best path. Furthermore while using this power can grant temporary knowledge, for instance helping a Koldun to plan a battle strategy, it will never reveal to them secrets which are unknown to them and to which they have no means of access; for instance a Koldun might uncover advice on siege warfare while sat inside a tent, or wandering a library, but they will not divine the contents of a letter to an enemy which would provide great insight into a siege.

    •• Revoke the Punishment of Babel

    It is thought in multiple religious and occult traditions, including the Abrahamic, that man once spoke a single language. The truth of this is unclear, but spirits of words and meaning are capable of bridging such divides, as they have done for spirit-callers and theurges since time immemorial.

    System: Spend 1 Blood Point, then roll Perception + Occult. On a success you may read and write any language for one scene. Note that this does not permit the Koldun to speak or understand the spoken language; when they look upon a scroll written in a foreign tongue, the 'true' meaning of the words is conveyed to them in terms they can understand. If this ability is used on a cypher or coded message of some kind, the Koldun will realize that it is a code on a success.

    When used against a cypher or other code, the number of successes required (which can be accumulated via repeated rolls) to pierce the false meaning and access the true message will be determined by the number of successes rolled by the person who wrote the hidden message, or must exceed the character's rating in the relevant Knowledge (for example, a fiendish missive written by a foe with a Cryptography Knowledge, or equivalent, of 4 would require 5 successes to understand).

    Finally, attempts to read any languages which are forbidden and grave even to the world of the spirits, at the Storyteller's Discretion, will add +2 to the difficulty of using this power.

    ••• Flood of Knowledge

    Though a Koldun and spirits associated with this Kraina tend to be of a scholarly bent, every Vampire has its fangs, and every spirit its rage. Unleashing the power of whatever minutiae they have studied, the Koldun can overwhelm an assailant with ideas and facts which they may struggle to comprehend.

    System: Spend 1 Blood Point, then roll Intelligence + a Knowledge of your choice, chosen whenever you use this effect. The victim may resist by rolling their Intelligence + the same Knowledge chosen by the Koldun, at a difficulty of 6. Note that they may still attempt the roll with their Intelligence alone, if they lack the relevant Knowledge.

    The victim is stunned for one turn per remaining success. On a failure nothing happens, and on a botch they become immune to this power for the rest of the night. While stunned by Flood of Knowledge, the victim may only stammer out single-word statements and stagger around a space, taking no actions. This effect will end if the victim becomes clearly endangered.

    •••• Prison of Parchment

    While the secrets of the Umbra are largely beyond this Kraina's reach, a Koldun who reaches this level of mastery might invoke a strange realm which houses books, ideas, written words and knowledge. With this power a Koldun might dive inside the pages of a book of Fairy Stories, or force a prisoner to confront the Apostles within the pages of a Holy Bible.

    System: Spend 1 Blood Point and 1 Willpower, then roll Strength + Occult. On a success you may grasp a willing, restrained, unconscious or otherwise helpless target, and force them bodily into a tome, which may be no smaller than a journal. Alternatively, you may target yourself. The individual entering a book arrives in an abstract and ever-shifting realm with rules and conventions determined by the book in question. To arrive on one page, a Cainite or Mortal might find themselves being approached by Zeus, or watching Jesus minister to his Disciples. Such characters have varying levels of self-awareness determined by their character and how prolific they are in the world of writing. While Little Red Riding Hood would be very upset by a stranger telling her she isn't real, Christ would most likely invite you to sit with him, tell him your story, and ask if he can help you get home. The traits and roleplaying behaviours of 'characters' within these tomes, be they a recipe or a crop record or a God, should be determined by the Storyteller, and should follow the logic and sensibilities of both the book and the spirit world.

    The victim does not age, hunger or thirst, if that would typically be a concern, and can attempt to wander between the pages of the book, encountering different 'scenes'. They retain their real traits, but a great deal of problems they face (such as leaving one parable and reaching another) must be navigated using their Knowledges. The language used to write the text remains relevant, which may prove tricky given language barriers.

    Note that while this power is exceptionally potent, it has significant limitations. A specific tome can only ever hold a single victim. A tome which holds a prisoner, such as an inn's ledger holding the innkeep, will be altered visibly to any who study it; for example a monk reading a Holy Bible might be surprised to see mention of a "terrified Hussite" in a Parable written more than a thousand years prior. These changes may be noticed, if a character attentively reads the contents, with a difficulty 6 Wits + Academics roll, though Academics may be replaced with a relevant knowledge such as Theology (a holy text), Seneschal (a ledger) and so on, as appropriate. Any supernatural or supernaturally touched character may note there is something 'off' about the tome in question by passing a Wits + Awareness test, difficulty 8. On a success they simply feel a strange suspicion towards the tome, perhaps noting the touch of the spirits if they are familiar with such things, which may prompt them to read the book.

    Escaping imprisonment in a tome is possible, but prisoners will be held for a minimum number of hours equal to the Koldun's successes, unless the Koldun ends the effect early while touching the tome. If the Koldun does not end the effect, a PC may escape through narrative means; this should be difficult, but not impossible, and is best handled by the Storyteller. NPCs who the Storyteller judges would struggle to escape might simply remain in the tome until freed, while a roll is available for when the Storyteller wishes to randomly test the abilities of a prisoner. This should be an Extended Intelligence + Academics (or appropriate Knowledge) roll, with the victim freeing themselves once they have accumulated a number of successes equal to the Koldun's Willpower. Each roll represents a night's work. Destroying the tome will also free the prisoner.

    Upon being freed, a victim will find themselves leaping from the pages of the tome and landing nearby. If the book is enclosed, they shall emerge in the nearest available space.

    ••••• Biography of Blood

    With this ability, the Koldun finally masters the connection between the essence of the writer, their blood, and the books they write. Through this power they might pen a long missive by pressing their blood against the paper, or force a prisoner's life story onto the virgin pages of a waiting tome.

    System: When used on another creature in possession of blood, the Koldun must spill their blood upon an empty page, journal or tome and declare to the spirits what they wish to be written from the creature's knowledge. Once this is done, the Koldun must spend 1 Blood Point and roll Manipulation + Occult against a difficulty of the target's current Willpower. On success the content requested is written, immediately filling the empty space.

    A single success is enough to fill a small journal or less, while documenting a Methuselah's life in exquisite detail would require 5 or more successes and a huge quantity of empty books. Multiple rolls may be made to accumulate enough successes, but each roll demands the Koldun spill more of the target's blood upon the page, and further expenditure of blood. Botching any of these rolls ruins the process and renders the target immune to Biography of Blood for a month.

    Though less impressive to the uneducated, but still immensely powerful, a Koldun may instead mark a page or tome with their own blood (spending 1 Blood Point if they are not in a Library), roll to activate this power against the default Kraina difficulty, and scribe what they intend to write from their own memory or knowledge immediately, making them a sort of unliving printing press.

    There you go! This is version 1.0 as it were, and I'm looking forward to making a bunch of cool changes and improvements!

      Things I'm looking to add or clarify in the next version:

      - benefits of using Prisoner of the Page for study, such as it helping you to find the secrets of a tome or learn something within, plus edge cases like using Facsimile-Jesus as a Mentor on Road of Heaven or equivalent etc.

      - might be worth mentioning that someone stuck within the pages of a book could use Presence 4 to try and summon help, and that generally speaking you can use your powers within the book (e.g. using Dominate or Animalism on a character in a story), but they won't be leaving the book (the book doesn't have arms or legs or eyes, which rules out most powers) unless it fits into an exception like Presence 4

      - maybe a note on Cosmology? It feels like the book-prison of level 4 would be accessible by Werewolves and Changelings, which might matter if you have certain NPCs or a crossover game etc.

          •••• Paper Prison

          While the secrets of the Umbra are largely beyond this Kraina's reach, a Koldun who reaches this level of mastery might invoke a strange realm which houses books, ideas, written words and knowledge. With this power a Koldun might dive inside the pages of a book of Fairy Stories, or force a prisoner to confront the Apostles within the pages of a Holy Bible. On the other hand, they might allow a grieving lover to enter the diary of their late beloved, or permit a Malkavian to enter a prophecy and examine it from the inside.

          System: The Koldun must spend one turn preparing the prison, and then force the victim into the tome with a separate action. When attempting to force a victim into the tome, spend 1 Blood Point and 1 Willpower, then roll Strength + Occult. On a success you may grasp a willing, restrained, unconscious or otherwise helpless target, and force them bodily into a tome, which may be no smaller than a journal. Alternatively, you may target yourself. The individual entering a book arrives in an abstract and ever-shifting realm with rules and conventions determined by the book in question. To arrive on one page, a Cainite or Mortal might find themselves being approached by a randy Zeus, or watching Jesus minister to his Disciples. Such characters have varying levels of self-awareness determined by their character and how prolific they are in the world of writing. The traits and roleplaying behaviours of 'characters' within these tomes, be they a recipe or a crop record or a God, should be determined by the Storyteller, and should follow the logic and sensibilities of both the book and the spirit world.

          The victim does not age, hunger or thirst, if that would typically be a concern, and can attempt to wander between the pages of the book, encountering different 'scenes'. They retain their real traits, but a great deal of problems they face (such as leaving one chapter and entering another) must be navigated using their Knowledges. Their supernatural abilities, such as Disciplines, may be used within the prison, but the effects will generally remain within the prison realm, with rare exceptions such as Presence 4 (Summon), which would allow the prisoner to call for help in the real world. The sunlight detailed within a book does not harm or intimidate a vampire. The language used to write the text remains relevant, which may prove tricky given language barriers. Damage taken within the realm of the tome is real, but will at worst cause the character to fall unconscious or into Torpor. Any psychological damage, such as Derangements, should be determined by the Storyteller.

          Note that while this power is exceptionally potent, it has significant limitations. A specific tome can only ever hold a single victim. A tome which holds a prisoner will be temporarily altered in a way which is visible to any who study it. For example a monk reading a Holy Bible might be surprised to see mention of a "terrified Hussite" in a Parable written more than a thousand years prior. These changes may be noticed, if a character attentively reads the contents, with a difficulty 6 Wits + Academics roll, though Academics may be replaced with a relevant knowledge such as Theology (a holy text), Seneschal (a ledger) and so on, as appropriate. A character with the Awareness Talent may note there is something 'off' about the tome in question by passing a Wits + Awareness test, difficulty 8. On a success they simply feel a strange suspicion towards the tome, which may prompt them to study the object.

          A prisoner will be held for a minimum of one sunset per success on the Koldun's roll, unless the Koldun ends the imprisonment early while touching the prison. If the victim is incapacitated (staked, in torpor, chained up by a fictional villain) they will be unable to leave. Destroying the tome will also free the prisoner instantly. Upon being freed, a victim will find themselves leaping from the pages of the tome and landing nearby. If the book is enclosed, they shall emerge in the nearest available space. For example, a Cainite imprisoned with 4 successes would emerge immediately after the fourth sunset following their imprisonment, assuming they did not become incapacitated in some fashion while trapped inside the book, in which case their emergence would be delayed until they left Torpor.

          Note that exposure to such a realm need not be breakdown, it can also be breakthrough. An adherent to the Road of Heaven might, for instance, find great strength in what they learn from speaking to a facsimile of Christ. The impact of such interactions, such as mentoring for Roads and various other Traits, should be left in the hands of the Storyteller.

          A Note on Cosmology: Those imprisoned within written works by this power may be accessible via travels through the Dreaming or Umbra, at ST discretion.

          ••••• Biography of Blood

          With this ability, the Koldun finally masters the connection between the essence of the writer, their blood, and the books they write. Through this power they might pen a long missive by pressing their blood against the paper, or force a prisoner's life story onto the virgin pages of a waiting tome.

          System: When used on another creature in possession of blood, the Koldun must spill their blood upon an empty page, journal or tome and declare to the spirits what they wish to be written from the creature's knowledge. Once this is done, the Koldun must spend 1 Blood Point and roll Manipulation + Occult against a difficulty of the target's current Willpower. On success the content requested is written, immediately filling the empty space. The content is truthful and has a level of accurate detail requested. Retrospective falsification and amnesia, natural or otherwise, will be represented with passages with missing words, jumbled prose, and unconvincing accounts. The Biography is a peerless insight into the subject, but even the simplest life can create a puzzling reading experience.

          A single success is enough to fill a small journal or less, while documenting a Methuselah's life in exquisite detail would require 5 or more successes and a huge quantity of empty books. Multiple rolls may be made to accumulate enough successes, but each roll demands the Koldun spill more of the target's blood upon the page, and further expenditure of blood.               Since I love studious powers for vampires, the idea of a Kraina for libraries hits a soft spot with me. So far I like where this is going, but I have one nitpick: Krainas emphasize a Tzimisce's bond with their domain, and maybe it is implicit, but I feel it should be a requisite for the Koldun to own a library. Maybe it doubles as their Haven, but it should be something that is their domain, and theirs alone. They need a place of commune and power, a heart of the Kraina if you will.

              In order to use the Kraina in foreign places of knowledge, they would then have to metaphysically 'extend' their domain to these places; in most cases it wouldn't be practical to outright buy them, so something symbolic like placing a book from their home library in the shelves should do, or an index card written in vitae. There is also some potential to 'annex' newly visited to their home library that way - if they can establish a strong link, the spirits in the home library may be able to snatch a book or knowledge from the linked places. Maybe consider creating some complemetary Rituals that formalize this process, with varying levels of access to the annexed place - even something like physical travel between the 'extended' library wings could be fun at higher levels.
