    Originally posted by Blind Dragon View Post

    3) (The bit about the Sabbat that never made sense to me) How does the Sabbat's modus operandi of Mass Embraces and flouting the Masquerade to embrace their inner Beast work, especially in a post-SI world? Is that addressed at all?
    The older editions explained this quite well: the Sabbat don't actually break the masquerade in their own territories. Instead, they commit minor breaches of the masquerade in enemy territory, forcing the Cammies to scramble to fix things, thereby exposing themselves and their resources.

    The thing about sieges is, it's always been a problem for both sides to find each other. It's basically 20 guys in a city of millions laying siege to 20 other guys hiding in a city of millions.

    So here's what you do: you embrace a group of homeless people or whatever. You send them to attack a location. Being newborns, they flaunt their new powers a bit, but they barely have powers so it's nothing that isn't fixable. The Camarilla scramble to fix this, exposing their resources and minions in the process, and maybe even their faces. They likely slaughter the shovelheads, but who cares, they are shovelheads. The true Sabbat watch from the shadows and use this intell for quiet, targeted assassinations.

    In other words, the Sabbat breeches of the masquerade never amounted to anything that couldn't be dismissed as some homeless people on drugs going crazy in enemy cities.

    It's only in this edition that the Sabbat suddenly became idiots blatantly violating the masquerade in their own cities.

            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
            However, getting back on topic (SHOCK - I am terrible at this), The Paths replacing Clan is something that I actually really like and am glad that the Sabbat are going for. In a way, the Lasombra and Tzimisce ANtitribu elements don't really matter because the "New" Sabbat are a group that is fully molding into a single post-clan sect. Being Lasombra as a concept no longer will matter in the Sabbat because all of the traditional Lasombra power structures, cultures, and beliefs will cease to exist.
            This isn't a bad thing per se, I actually tend to agree with you. The problem is historical context. As much as it makes sense, the Sabbat Lasombra never did this before, while the Camarilla ones did. Why would they change attitudes now?

            I do get that as much as the Sabbat had some of this tendency before, the two founding clans were exceptions. They had pride in "completely" subverting their old institutions and now being fully Sabbat instead of needing to be Antitribu. Now even them are pressed to set clan aside in favor of identifying as Sabbat above all else before being of clan X or Y.

            But then again, why bother with the moniker? They can still rub in the face of others they are the clans that fully converted. They are the leadership of the sect. Unless both of those two things changed, they have no reason to embrace the moniker of Antitribu.

            Well, I for one don't care if they're in such situation. I am a fan of both clans, but never liked their too firm association with the Sabbat. I also think the Sabbat works better without them as so obviously at the helm. I think both making them present elsewhere and making the Sabbat more independent from them are good things (coupled with the bad things of making the badly written defection, that I can easily change in my games, and going-to-Hell Sabbat, which is less simple).

            Yet, the change of the moniker just for the sake of it (yeah, I know it is to make things simpler, actually) is something I think doesn't add to the setting without consideration being paid to its history.

            Or even, if they really wanted to do it, this is the one single thing they could just retcon and it would make more sense. Would be bad, but would be less bad.

                            Here are some lines from The Player's Guide to the Sabbat, a book that long predates both Revised and Justin Achilli's presence, in Vampire the Masquerade.

                            When hunting humans for blood or sport, Sabbat will
                            not hesitate to kill their prey. They love to drink victims to
                            death and will even drink them dry when they do not need
                            any blood. However, they usually kill only in cities they do
                            not control. They know killing on a regular basis in their own
                            cities would lead to trouble
                            The book goes on to introduce the idea that the Sabbat are in the end like most vampires, hypocrites, because while officially they ridicule the Masquerade, unofficially their leaders are engaged in regular efforts to cover up the crap their younger or more dedicated members engage in. That while *less effective* and subtle at concealment than a sect that, say, openly champions the Masquerade, like the Camarilla, they nevertheless do successfully in the end cover things up and work to do so.

                            For instance, on Sabbat games:

                            The Sabbat have a number of games they enjoy. They
                            play them mainly in enemy territory, but occasionally within
                            their own.
                            As though the Sabbat actually does have an understanding that there are limits to the crap they can get away with on their own turf. Certainly missing persons cases will ramp up when the "ocassionally" circumstances happen, or the loyalists slip the leash of control, but even these Sabbat are noted as understanding that there have to be what are referred as "cool down periods".

                            The idea that the Sabbat get up to most of their nonsense in *other people's territory not their own* is introduced because...

                            Sabbat are known to
                            stir up a great deal of trouble, especially when they are not in
                            their own territory. That way someone else will have to clean
                            up the mess.
                            Now, certainly, some of the other ideas and writing in the book will contradict these things, even shortly within the writing of others. I won't argue this was the best written book evar as far as being entirely coherent with itself, much as I wouldn't argue a lot of early VtM is what I would call well written. But to suggest that the ideas that the Sabbat in fact understood they needed some measure of restraint and self control in their own territory are retcons and the sole responsibility of Justin Achilli is..... interesting. The ideas existed well early in the existence of the Sabbat as a playable entity and certainly under different writers/developers. People thought these things long before revised as far as "how does the Sabbat work then?" and the books offered material that supported doing so.
