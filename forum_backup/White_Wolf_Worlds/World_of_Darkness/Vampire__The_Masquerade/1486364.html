    [Setting Change] There are no Anarch Domains

    07-13-2022, 05:47 PM
    So, not wanting to derail either of the other Anarch domain related conversations, I had a thought about changing the Movement a bit that addresses certain things brought up in both of those thread:

    The Anarch Movement doesn't seek to establish Domains. That's why there's no idea of what an Anarch Domain looks like, or any uniformity to those that might qualify for that idea. The Anarch Movement simply seeks to establish local rule over Sect rule, where every local Domain's relationship with other Domains is a negotiated affair instead of part of some massive organization that robs local vampires of their power.

    The ideal "Anarch" Domain is actually an Independent Domain. Domains with no allegiances to anyone or any group outside their Domain unless they choose to; including any fidelity to the Anarch Movement if that's how things play out. Independent Domains that operate like Camarilla Domains but don't answer to the Camarilla are not a fail-state for the Anarchs as long as it's still self determined by the locals to work that way. The Free States being a thing is similarly within ideals because it's a mutually agreed upon alliance, not lasting is actually to the Movement's preference even if Independent Domains being taken over by Sects is not.

    As I see it, this offers the following benefits:
    • Independent Domains are already the thing in the setting, and this expands their role as part of the bigger picture instead of largely being a fluke of authors wanting to make the Caribbean, southern Central American, and South American cities flavorful but without the knowledge base of how. STs are empowered to create quirky cities that operate on their own systems, with inherent conflict between the Anarchs that helped "free them" into this state and want to keep it that way, the Camarilla that doesn't want to lose their ability to send in powerful vampires to exert control, and the locals that don't want to bow to either.
    • If you're using the V5 setting changes, this helps explain the draw of the Anarchs to the Ministry and Tzimisce: both are heavily related to regions known for Independent Domains; backing the Movement helps ensure many of their older holdings get to stay the way those Clans like things and less beholden to playing by the rules of a larger organization.
    • There is inherent narrative conflict among the Anarch Movement on how interventionist to be. Factions are inherently going to form over whether the Anarchs should come in uninvited, and how long they should stay after any Sect loyal opposition is dealt with under the idea of making sure the new local government is up and running. This also provides a lot of places to explore some real world debates around different types of libertarianism and anarchism that makes the OOC name "Anarch" relate more to what they do IC, as well as other political topics like interventionism, colonialism, and so on.
    • This also creates a narrative space for the Movement that gets them out of the "but if they win they just turn into the Camarilla-lite" issue. The Anarchs can be anywhere, and everywhere, because winning for them isn't about being a "proper" Sect but about fundamentally challenging the validity of having Sects in the first place. The Anarchs don't consider things a win unless their efforts prove to the locals that they don't need anyone above them to run things properly for themselves. All but the most dedicated Princes and Bishops are going to be at least slightly tempted by the subversive idea that they could be the true top and not have to worry about a Justicar or Archbishop looking over their shoulder.

                          Well, to the real world comparison: the big powers are generally too busy with each other. Sometimes big powers do just steamroll small powers (Tibet isn't a country any more), sometimes they setup proxy governments, but the Cold War ended with a lot of countries that aren't under the sway of any specific larger power because they weren't important enough for anyone to put in that effort by pulling away resources from more important battlegrounds. IRL there are currently ~20 countries (depending on how you want to define things) with no military... but nobody is rushing to conquer them. What major power thinks it's worth it to go take over Uruguay?

                          Then there are, of course, lots of countries that are willing to make deals with the big powers to ensure neutrality. It's easier to have Switzerland take no sides than expend your forces to conquer Switzerland to make sure they don't side with your enemies. And the Swiss have spent a long time being happy to be that way.

                          Or one can look at Afghanistan and it's long history of just being an epic pain in the ass to conquer.

                          So yeah, there are avenues of claiming territory despite being a severe underdog on the global scale. Global power doesn't mean easily exerted power everywhere.

                          The baseline setting of VtM is actually in the Independent Domains' favor. Vampire territorial expansion is hampered significantly by their small numbers, reliance on very long term planning, and need to wage proxy warfare. The Camarilla and Sabbat have enough trouble exerting military power over Domains a few miles away from their nearest powerful Domain, let alone hundreds of miles away. It was an intensely massive military campaign in Revised for the Sabbat in southern Florida to push the Camarilla out of Georgia, and the Carolinas, before the issues with the Regent led to Virginia being a stalemate.

                          How are they going to manage to take over a city in Brazil from Mexico City? Or the Camarilla taking that city from Texas?
