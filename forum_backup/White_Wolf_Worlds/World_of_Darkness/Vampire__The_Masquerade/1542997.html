    The original Kolduns

    10-02-2024, 05:37 AM
    Good evening Class!

    It is more like a curious musing than anything and of course I am interested about your take and opinion!
    Especially from those who love crossovers - but of course it not necessarily have to be a crossover
    Regardless the Tzimisce and the Shadow Lord history are intertwined - not unlike the Bubasti and Silent Strider (and lesser extent the Mokolé from Sobek's side, who ended up as an Abomination) with the Followers of Set
    The books focusing on Shadow Lords almost every time always mention the Fiends and their eternal feud with the Tzimisce, like how the supplements talking about the Fiends never forget to mention the Garou of their ancestral homeland
    Now I always liked to toy this idea I will detail here and I'm curious about your thoughts about the matter

    My theory is: the Shadow Lord Kinfolk were the original Koldun
    A simple yet bold claim
    But I think have a lot of support
    Koldunism is elemental and spirit magic. With rituals and gifts, haggle and bargain the Koldun make deals with spirits of every kind
    Familiar, isn't it?
    The Garou also work this way; you can't really force a Spirit to aid you (well you could, but with consequences), you have to do what the Spirit ask, bargain, haggle, make deals, go on spirit quests and you can earn the help of a Spirit and it eventually helps you, maybe even allows you to use it in a Fetish, teach you rites and Gifts
    Unlike Necromancy or Thaumaturgy (Dark or regular) Koldunism works because you know the local area, the spirits, and earned their cooperation - this sometimes even reflected in a dice penalty when you work your magic with an unfamiliar land with spirits you don't know
    The aforementioned ways of magic uses blasphemy shrines, dark rituals, fetters and brute force to make the Wraiths, spirits and even Demons obey
    Koldunism uses gifts, sacrifice, deals and oaths - it is a mutually beneficial relationship
    Back to my Theory, the Shadow Lords - like all Garou - main goal was to keep their land clean of the Taint, serve Gaia and honor the spirits
    It is not that farfatched idea I think to come to the conclusion Kinfolks with proper connection and training helped them - and this is where Koldunism comes in the picture
    I believe it was the Shadow Lords themselves or mighty Spirits who originally tutored the first Kolduns who were Kinfolks - or maybe even regular humans
    It is not enough to cleanse the land, you have to keep it clean, you have to reinforce the ties with the spirits and respect it - logical to assume, since the Garou's numbers even back in the ancient days were limited and thinking on long terms, they basically enlisted the local Kinfolk who shares their blood and belief to keep their home clean
    Especially because of the entity Kupala!
    Now what is Kupala is always a good question from Demon to a Claw of the Wyrm itself , corrupted nature spirit or the land it self, he could be everything, yet there is one constant in the sources: Kupala's servants (also called Kupalas) are Banes or demonic spirits - the two actually the same in this regard
    Lore and legends tell us the Shadow Lords after a long and bloody struggle pulled the Earth apart with their claws, threw the ancient spirit Kupala in and slammed the Earth shut (forming the Carpathian mountains)
    This is actually very similar to the capabilities of the Way of Earth!
    In later supplements we learn of the rite called:

    Kupala's Night
    The pagan ways are the old ways of the Earth, and sometimes Christians fear them with good reason. Just as there is light in the world, there is darkness, and during the festival of Kupala's Night, the force of darkness is undeniably strong. Tzimisce mystics and feudal lords often travel to the knezates of other respected rulers at this time, setting aside their feuds long enough to commune with the ancient spirits
    Mortal pagans practice Kupala's Night on June 22nd. They also revere Kupala as a benevolent god. Tzimisce, of course, know both suppositions for the lies they are. The true rituals of Kupala's Night are performed on a different night each year. Only Tzimisce astrologers can discern the most efficacious day for performing the violent ceremony. The veil between the land offlesh and the realm ofspirit is easierto rend on this night, and those who revere the old ways can see the very taint of the earth. Horrible apparitions infect the land, and where the soil has been corrupted, the hideousforms of the kupala can be seen by moonlight. They thrash and gibber, contorting their bodies into impossible displays of physiological degeneration.
    The land and the ruler are one, and so the Transylvanian Tzimisce show their reverence for the land by sharing their blood with each other and the earth. With the assistance of mystics, participants enact an ancient rite involving the sharing of blood, A silver cup containing the vitae of all in the ceremony is passed around to be consumed and the last few drops are poured on the ground. This not only demonstrates their alliance, but it also fortifies them for the task at hand.
    The screams of the kupala stop as terror seizes the spirits.
    Then the slaying begins.
    Vengeful Tzimisce distend their bodies and sharpen bloody claws. The spirits cry for mercy, yet none is given. Freely killing the maddening evil in the soil, the Fiends purify the land in obeisance to their ancient pact to protect it. Their fervor escalates into all-consuming frenzy much too often, and nothing in the vicinity is safe.
    By dawn, the madness fades and the evil of Kupala is weakened once again.
    Hospitality is extended to guests who assisted in the reaving the following night. The truce between collaborators lasts long enough for travelers to return to their fiefdoms. in a week or two, the fierce fighting for control of the land renews, but by then, the greatest of the clan's enemies has been grievously wounded.

    The Tzimsice actually enter the Umbra and technically hunt down Banes and other nasties on their domain, putting the Werewolves to shame

    (On Kupala's Night, the Gauntlet in areas infected by the kupala drops to 2. Tzimisce koldun enact a Level Five Thaumaturgy or Koldunic Sorcery (see Libellus Sanguinis 1 : Masters of the State] ritual allowing the Tzimisce to enter the Penumbra and battle the spirits.)

    Later we've actually discovered that there were Koldun who performed rituals around the Cathedral of Flesh to keep the taint of Kupala from spreading and infecting more of the land
    And those rituals worked!


    Several rituals deal with the spirits

    Lv1.
    Hospitality: The Koldun awakens the spirits in an area attuned to him, like his Haven, animating the objects and granting them limited sentience. The objects speak if spoken to and sometimes even when the Koldun does not command, though always with deference and fear. (Players Guide to High Clans)

    Lv2.
    Service for Souls: The Koldun summons the spirits of nature and mystically "ghouls" them by offering them his blood. The bound spirits remain with him until their task is fulfilled. (Sins of the Blood)

    Withering Agony: The Koldun draws the spirit from a sick tree out and puts it into another body. The now infected creature suffers from physical debilitations. (Sins of the Blood)

    Lv3.
    Raze the Lélek: The Koldun destroy a physical object by rending away its inhabiting spirit. (Lélek means Soul in Hungarian) (Sins of the Blood)
    Conjure Spirit: The Koldun summons a greater nature spirit to negotiate the task the spirit shall do for him. Most Spirits take offerings and oblations in blood, but others are more picky. (Players Guide to High Clans)

    Lv4.
    Ties that Bind: The Koldun spills blood on the earth before him. Then, he ritually eats the blood-soaked earth to reconnect with the land, gaining a greater connection to its spirits and more power for his sorceries. (Blood Magic: Secrets of Thaumaturgy)

    Merging of the Spirit: The Koldun sends a nature spirit to inhabit the body of one of his Ghouls by feeding him extra blood intended to attract the spirits. A ghouls body can take at least three spirits before it warps into something inhumane. (Sins of the Blood)

    Lv5
    Elemental Servitor: The Koldun summons a spirit to assume corporeal form to fight for his summoner. (Sins of the Blood)

    Conjure Demon: The Koldun summons one of the spawns of Kupala. The Koldun may barter with the demon for favours. (Players Guide to High Clans)

    Nemesis of the Living Earth: Beginning a long malediction lasting from midnight to sunrise, a koldun may name a target as an enemy of nature. If successful, the victim finds that the elements turn against it to harm it. (Players Guide to High Clans)


    Lv6.
    Embracing the Demon: The Koldun may summon a demon into the corpse of a childe-vessel previously put under the blood bond. A demon bound in such a way will serve the Koldun faithfully. (Sins of the Blood)

    Storm Hound: The Koldun builds a fire out of dried herbs and wood from lightning struck trees. Sacrificing a puppy and vitae to the Slavic storm god Perun in the flames, the Koldun creates a storm that can be pointed to any person in the world, as long as the Koldun is in possession of a piece of their body (like hair). The storm will find the target even if it is at the other end of the world. (Lair of the Hidden)

    As you can see there is a very clear distinction of Demons/Banes (kupalas) and nature spirits when it comes to Koldunic Sorcery - actually the rituals are negotiations, not brute force, and only at the very end you start to deal with "demons" (or Banes)
    The deals and pacts between spirits of Nature are not something new in WoD when it comes to the undead
    The werewolves of the west rarely care to differentiate between a Kuei-jin and a vampire, the Hengeyokai are often forced into contact with them. The Beast Courts keep up formal, if not warm, relations with the Kuei-jin; although these walking corpses are certainly vile, they do have their purpose under Heaven
    The Nushi are similar to Garou's Totem, but can be shared by members of a Wu (Pack/Coterie) of Kuei-Jin (or other oriental Shen.)
    More so, the editions can't really agree the Tzimsice hace Koldunic Sorcery or not before the Eldest made some deals with Kupala (and handed the furry behind of the Shadow Lords to them)
    Clanbook Tzimisce Revised goes to the point it states they indeed had Koldunic sorcery but after the pacts with Kupala (and actually it is NOT the curse of Caine, but the side effect of this pact and/or Koldunism) the Tzimisce are tied to their land) it became stronger

    Here is a funny Comparison!
    Gaia's Vengeance (Rank 5 Gift)
    The Garou calls to the spirits of the surrounding forest to attack intruders. The terrain responds as best it can: Rocks roll and smash, vines trip, and water sucks victims under. An avatar of Gaia herself teaches this Gift.
    System: The player spends one Gnosis point and one Rage point then rolls Charisma + Primal-Urge (difficulty of the local Gauntlet). The exact effects depend on the terrain and are left to the Storyteller.
    ​
    Gaia's Vengeance​, the Rank 5 Gift of the Garou and Nemesis of the Living Earth, the level 5 Koldunic Ritual... is basically the same


    Now the Conjure Demon level 5 ritual follows upon the principles of Conjure Spirit though with greater rewards for success... and greater peril
    The summoned Demon/Bane is stated never shows fear and perhaps need never show Fear for its powers usually exceeds the Koldun by far
    Yet... the Demon / Bane called in this fashion is stated remembers the Kolduns who came before and humbled them with Mighty curses and enchantments.
    Out of memory of those ancient pacts and fear that those pacts might one night find renewal they treat the Koldun with respect as equals!
    This is a very interesting passage because it says that while initiate practitioners can only manipulate spirits of nature masters of Koldunism, like the Kolduns of old can bind demons and they humbled demonic spirits with mighty curses
    I cannot help but think of the possibility that those Kolduns of ancient times and their rites might have existed before Kupala's imprisonment (or even his corruption) and are probably the very thing which bound this entity into the Carpathians and decimated his brood

    And who else could have done this back then than the Shadow Lords and their Kinfolk?
    I belive the original Kolduns were the Shadow Lord Kinfolks and when the Eldest arrived to the land he embraced a few of them and "stole" their knowledge
    And the spirits remember - remember the service, remember the intention and to be fair the Tzimisce of Old (or sometimes, new) still practice these rites
    And history actually repeated itself!
    When Count Florescu experimented on the Shadow Lord Kinfolk again and he created the Danislav Ravenant family, the spirits stayed with them, more so, Grandfather Thunder himself took the Danislav under its dark wings and his spirits - and other spirits - are/were willing to teach the Danislav Shadow Lord Gifts up to level 3!
    So this kind of assimilation is not unheard of, nor the spirits willing to work with Vampires

    Nos this is just a theory, nothing more
    And I admit have little use in Game - tho' it is amusing to explore this kind of things - but one might wonder: maybe the Tzimisce are so inhumane and far from Humanity because of this ancient connection to the Garou and the Spirit world...

                      It's a plausible theory, but there are plenty of other explanations. As for me, I think the Path of Spirit Thaumaturgy predates Koldunism, and the koldunic paths are likely specialized versions of that. Perhaps based on very specific pacts made by the path's progenitors that are baked into the rituals and paths themselves (even if as flavor text we the players don't see it). In the World of Darkness, much spirit magic follows the same sort of template regardless of whether it comes from werewolves, mages, sorcerers, or vampires. It's simply the way spirits behave in the setting. The information necessary for koldunism can come from several different sources. Kupala might have been the only being needed for the Tzimsice to develop the koldunic paths.

                      That is not to say that Garou rites haven't influenced it. They very well could have. Vampires could secretly view Garou practices, and the concentration of Tzimisce and Shadow Lords among the Carpathians could have lead more to that. Whether Shadow Lord kinfolk had a role is more nebulous.

                      Rites are not normally something kinfolk can learn. It's not one of the Backgrounds available to them for kinfolk character creation. The Pact that allows rites to be powered do not seem to extend to them. And the vast majority of kinfolk do not have Gnosis (and none have Rage) that are prerequisites for some rites. And they don't learn Gifts except in some extraordinary situations. In the original Kinfolk character sheet the Rituals knowledge is NOT listed as an ability (I don't know about the W20 version).

                      Furthermore, the Shadow Lords are extremely hierarchical and secretive, usually treating their kinfolk as some kind of favored slaves (this does not mean they are not cared for or loved). Their kinfolk are supposed to be submissive to the dominant Garou. Kinfolk are supposed to know their proper place, and their proper place isn't trying to learn Garou secrets or do things that Gaia created the Garou to do. I think only in exceptional circumstances would a Shadow Lord kinfolk learn anything useful that might be used later to develop a path of vampire blood magic, and even more unlikely such a kinfolk would become a vampire. Not impossible, but the odds are heavily against it.

                      If Shadow Lord kinfolk do have similar powers, it would be through paths and rituals of sorcery. However, if that is the route, there isn't much advantage to go with kinfolk specifically as opposed to non-kinfolk. Baba Yaga obviously had a lot of magic power as a mortal, but I don't think she was a kinfolk despite her ties to the earth mother. Almost all the early magicians of Stone Age humanity was some kind of earth mother worshippers (proto-Dream Speakers and such) per the setting.

                      So in my chronicles, I probably would not go with this, but I wouldn't object either if an ST revealed this as some secret lore of her chronicle setting.
