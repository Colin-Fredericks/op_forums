    Originally posted by MarkK View Post
    I don't really think they care too hard about the pre-existing fanbase at this point
    They probably don't, and arguably shouldn't. If the game stands on its own, then our grognard hearts will just have to accept it.

    But otherwise, time still goes on, and they're still subject to it.

    Most of their mistakes as we perceive it are things that many others did in the past, and fans sooner or later reacted to. Think about Paths: in 1st ed core they didn't exist, an when they first appeared they were clearly meant to be just very crude justifications for the Sabbat, not really meant for a "serious" game. It was time, and the same fanbase that never saw it before, that demanded from them a more serious and nuanced work on Paths, and eventually the creation of Roads for DAV.

    As time goes on, their current fanbase will grow in their understanding of the rules and make more complex demands. Just as we did a long time ago, so we currently have several editions and in-edition variations instead of just 30 years of supplements to 1st Core. Maybe their future demands won't be the same we make. Maybe some will, as they have access to previous editions and may go for them between V5 publications, as several forum members already did. They're maybe not a majority, but a significant number of the people here began at V5, and this number is growing by the day without that keeping them out of V20 discussions.

    Either way, their mistakes will come to them sooner or later, as they did the people that designed RPGs for decades before them, including White Wolf.

                            I wouldn't go too far either way for pre-V5. There is satire, it just isn't the whole of the game. This is specially true as time goes on, because this is natural: the more time you expend in any fictional setting, the more this setting naturally deviates from original intentions and inspirations to pursue its own logical conclusions.

                            But even if not always satirical, the WoD had always been commentary-friendly: it is our world but darker, isn't shy of having bad things and even letting you play those things, the authors tacitly accepted (to varying degrees) the idea that characters of every ideological leanings could and would exist and not always suffer the consequences for it they personally thought they should.

                            ​And I completely agree that satire itself must be updated, and I think Heavy Arms didn't meant by that that ditching the Sabbat was a good idea, just that it must be analyzed from the right perspective. There are several valid reasons we discussed in the proper thread why it isn't a good idea, but the reason isn't just that the game should remain the same. Actually the Sabbat does need some update precisely for the reasons of updating satire, just not in the way it was done and definitely not by just ditching it.

                            The idealization of 1st edition is a given at this point, and it has the same problem with idealization in general: it misses the mark far more than it realizes. It ignores 1st edition also had thematic contradictions, broad usage, not always had a clear idea of what it was doing, had good writing and bad writing, it was an inspiring amount of interesting ideas thrown together in a messy way and bad rules, by people that had artistic experience in the field, but no professional training or technical knowledge, because professional training and technical knowledge in writing RPGs weren't a thing at the time.

                            Honestly I'm not against ditching the whole "Our World But Darker™" spiel, I've actually done this in my own games a long time ago. Our world but weirder? Sure, although our world can get pretty weird sometimes (I mean, aside from being pretty fucked up and sad for its many bad consequences and "small" tragedies, three years ago I would outright say that an invasion on Capitol just isn't credible if you're not running a Gehenna scenario, much less an invasion by a bunch of mostly incompetent lunatics). But changing this doesn't have to mean "I want my WoD game to be a safer place where the bad people are easily recognized as such.

                            ​For the Oblivion part of it... I think Ragged Robin is right. There certainly is an idea behind it, but by itself it was just a design gaffe. It happens. Whatever the intention was, the rule just failed at conveying it and working for the final product, and it isn't a particularly bad problem, just a small mistake.
