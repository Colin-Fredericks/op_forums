    [WIR] Children of the Blood

    01-31-2024, 04:13 PM
    I've mentioned it elsewhere but I genuinely love Onyx Path Publishing's take on Vampire: The Masquerade Fifth Edition. More than anyone, I feel they had a clear and consistent vision for what the game was about and how to marry ease of access for new players with a fidelity to the lore that came before. So, obviously, I was going to try to do as many of their books as possible. This one is going to be about CHILDREN OF THE BLOOD.

    I hope people will read along with me and share their opinions on the subject.

              Children of the Blood part 1

              Children of the Blood comes from the long tradition of books like Children of the Inquisition, Children of the Night, and Children of the Revolution. They are NPCs that are usually tied to a specific place but can be inserted anywhere. Generally, these books are some of the most informative of the books. We learned much about the origins of the Camarilla from Rafael de Corazon's biography. We got a lot out of Dracula's backstory. We figured out how the function of the Camarilla military occurred from Karsh's write-up.

              COTB seems to be far more on the lower end of the power scale. Part of what 5th Edition has laser focused on the idea that the power level of V:TM was way too high in previous editions. I think this is a smart move but you need to have a balance between the powerful Elder types and the incredibly weak ones. After all, coteries tend to gang up on their enemies when and if they decide to attack someone.

              I'm very excited about these as I think V:TM is primarily a social game and every single character is a potential series of adventures by themselves. Most of these NPCs are also tied to Cults of the Blood Gods since, well, it is a supplement tied to it the same way that Let the Streets Run Red or The Chicago Folio is tied to Chicago by Night.

              Opening Fiction: Second Best Smile

              ​Summary: Second-Best Smile is an interesting story about the character Baptiste, who is a member of the Nephilim cult. A Tremere belonging to the [b]Church of Set/b] is chasing down his ex-ghoul.

              Analysis: This is a pretty good opening fiction for Children of the Blood. It shows a rather petty bit of politicking between two members of cults and yet still pretty interesting in how it's done. In the end, the cultists are all looking for a sense of community in the World of Darkness and that means that the actual beliefs of the sects are less important than their ability to sell themselves to the locals. Thus, a member of the Church of Set can end up joining the Nephilim just because he's looking for his ex-girlfriend. We also see just how sick and twisted the Nephilim are as Baptiste skins her for a tattoo to frame.

              Because ART baby.

              I should note the Nephilim have really managed to take off as a concept as one of the Renegade free adventures, Midnight Kiss, is based around them. Given Michael and Mary the Black are acquainted with Baali in Beckett's jyhad diary, you could easily tie to the cult's resurgence with the now-no-longer-being-mentioned Bloodline. After all, what is more Baali-like than covering the face of utter evil in great beauty?

                                  Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                  The larger question is, "Does this make the game more fun for players?"

                                  Which is not being sarcastic but simply asking the Doylst answer that whether or not it is better for any changes to the campaign world in how they impact player enjoyment and agency. It was actually addressed in ANARCHS UNBOUND that the Red Question made it impossible for the government to track the Anarchs on the internet using magic. It was a hand wave but one designed to deal with the larger issues of changing technology.

                                  Other solutions include maybe not including the Delerium or that vampires now all have the Blush of Life due to a changing of the blood.

                                  What is the best way to keep up with changing technology and assumptions without compromising the game?
                                  The simple answer is modularity rather than strict adherence to out-dated design concepts. This would allow the most amount of people to run games that suit their preferences without needing to home-brew or house rules huge swathes of the material.

                                  Consider how vampire societies are portrayed in other media/settings. Seraph of the End portrayed an underground vampire city which the humans had no clue about with a full hierarchy and laws. Conversely the movie Priest had vampires living in hives more akin to xenomorphs. Warhammer Fantasy has whole undead nations to play with.

                                  VtM largely suggests that every city that has vampires is owned by a faction, who have a diverse population representing most if not all clans. But never really accounts for the fact that vampires are supposed to be a low population critter, like a major city has one or two coteries worth of vampires. Also why in the world would a clan stake a claim on a city and then let others setup shop even if they belonged to the same faction?

                                  A city run by Nagaraja would probably have a vampire society more along the lines of the movie Midnight Meat Train than something VtM considers a normal city. So much of VtMs vampire society is made to create conflict, it is intentionally designed to create storyhooks, rather than creating functional vampire societies that are distinct from each other.

                                  As technology advances, vampires and their society will have to adjust, which in turn creates new things to squabble over and new factions to form. In an advanced technology culture do vampires have to retreat to third world countries or rural areas which have that level of infrastructure? Alternatively do the vampires stop trying to hide in human society and simply create underground cities with the factions being more political than armed forces? All of that can maintain the same themes that VtM has and falls in line with its history as already written.

                                                      Children of Blood part 2

                                                      Introduction

                                                      The introduction to Children of Blood more or less just explains what the book is and isn't. It is a supplement to Cults of the Blood Gods but not all of the characters are cultists. In fact, quite a few of them were actually characters that weren't fully detailed in Let the Streets Run Red and Chicago by Night. I don't hold this against the book at all but am glad that the people at OPP managed to get the room to be able to detail all of the wonderful NPCs as well as characters they wanted to detail even if they had to spread it across multiple books.

                                                      It also says that you have permission to change these characters up as you see fit for your chronicle. This shouldn't need to be said but, well, a lot of Storytellers are antsy about breaking canon and this works well for it.

                                                      The first section of the book will be the main part of the series, Believers and Charlatans that contains our collection of oddball NPCs. BTW, they're called SPCs in the book and while I understand brand identity, I just can't get my brain to switch over and call them the new adage.

                                                          Children of the Blood #3: Florian Ribiero



                                                          Type: NPC

                                                          Summary: A mathematical genius who sees God as a fundamental pattern to the universe and was Embraced by a Toreador. He was shattered by the existence of the supernatural, in part because it threw off all of his equations, and has since sought out Roger de Camden for secrets of the Mithraic mysteries.

                                                          Analysis: Personal anecdote: I am a religious person but if you asked me to describe my religious beliefs, you would swiftly go down a rabbit hole starting with Star Wars' The Force, then being influenced by Hinduism, pantheism, and Spinoza's concept of the universe as a fundamental collection of infinite substance that we are all a part of. For my belief system, I jokingly refer to myself as a Christian Jedi and for a more serious belief system, I believe in a unification between reality and the divine that defies conventional explanation. If I wasn't so shit at mathematics, I would have probably been a decent Order of Hermes applicant but certainly would be able to slide into the Celestial Choir but they'd probably kick me to the curb for the Hollow Ones.

                                                          Which is to say I fucking love Florian Ribiero. He's a fantastic character and if he was written by Matthew Dawkins The Gentleman Gamer (which I strongly expect to be the case) then he's my second favorite character in the book before his OTHER creation that I know was by him. This is notably higher praise than it sounds due to the fact that I made a couple of characters in the book. He's a character that fits perfectly into the World of Darkness but could have very easily have fallen into any number of other groups or Clans. Had circumstances been different, he might have been Embraced by the Tremere or Awakened as any number of other Traditions. Dude would have probably been an annoying addition to the Arcanum and having to deal with the fact he's surrounded by people who believe in, SCOFF, ghosts (and annoyed they exist).

                                                          Fundamentally, Florian wants the universe to make sense and the fact is that the World of Darkness doesn't make sense. The real world doesn't make sense either, which is why real science is based around coming up with theories to explain away the parts that show why our current theories are inconveniently wrong. It's a matter of faith for him but he has no belief in a personal God and would probably explain why such an idea is ludicrous to a guy who can maybe talk to Gaia. He's joined the Cult of Mithras to try to work out some of these issues but I fear that it is the absolute worst sort of place for him because he's seeking a way to depersonalize God and reality in a group that very much has a personal relationship with their god. I fear he has to get out of Edinburgh before Mithras shows up (or is resurrected) and he gets himself killed trying to tell his fellow cultists the dude is "just" another vampire.

                                                          There's a lot of places Florian's story could go from him getting deeper into the mysteries of Mithras, deciding he really wants to be something like a Nephilim instead (which is a later move since Michael is also a real being--and possibly Baali now), or trying to learn Blood Sorcery. He would either suck royally at magic or find the kind of power/insight that would potentially make him a massive threat to others. It would really depend on whether or not he learned it from a Banu Haqim or Tremere mentor, really.

                                                          Also, I appreciate the fact that Florian is a gay man in a relationship with his sire with no sign of the angst that other games would normally put on it or big to do. Florian isn't even THAT in love with his sire or traumatized by events. They had a relationship, it was good, and now they've sort of drifted apart. It's a nice aversion to the usual V:TM melodrama.
