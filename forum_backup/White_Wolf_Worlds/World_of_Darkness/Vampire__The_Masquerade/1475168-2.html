        Originally posted by The Players Guide to the Sabbat, page 95
        The Path of Harmony developed in the late 1700s in the New World colonies. The Path enjoyed a great deal of growth in its early years, but its numbers have begun to dwindle because of the renewal of constant Jyhad. Most of the Sabbat have become too jaded to accept the ways of this Path. The Path of Harmony synthesized the various philosophies of the Romantics, including American ideals and transcendentalism. It put as much emphasis on emotion and divine Providence as it did on the natural law established by God.
        Not that anyone here will care, I suppose, but the above excerpt is what the actual Second Edition text says about the history and development of the Path of Harmony (emphases mine, of course). "Native American beliefs" are not mentioned even once in the writeup, nor are Harmonists identified with Native Americans anywhere in The Players Guide to the Sabbat.

        The entire Path of Harmony = Native Americans thing seems to be a product of the Revised Edition version of the Path of Harmony in the Sins of the Blood supplement (pages 35-37). The Revised version is also responsible for the Path's "protect the environment" angle that is absent from the original.

        This has to be one of the most hilarious self-owns by Revised proponents I have ever seen on these forums. Should we qualify the assertions earlier in this thread by stating instead that this horrifyingly racist revision of the Path of Harmony is a prime example of the kind of nonsense that Revised Edition is infamous for?

        That would also be a tiresome argument, but at least it would be a more accurate view of this particular development within the gameline.

                                Thanks for looking the original sourcebook. It's easy to forget details. And it caused me to look again to see if my own memory was wrong, or was using Revised era wording.

                                There still seems to be a connection between the Path of Harmony and Native Americans, but it's a bit hidden. Throughout the Players Guide to the Sabbat, we're told that many Native Americans are part of the Country Gangrel and in fact make up most of the Country Gangrel recruits, and even those that aren't share "certain" Native American beliefs. And then in the Gangrel antitribu section, we're told that almost all Gangrel follow either the Path of Harmony or Honorable Accord. So this is where the connection is made.

                                Other clans mentioned as following the Path of Harmony include "some Brujah", that Harmony along with Honorable Accord and Caine are "common" among Nosferatu, "a few Panders", "most Ravnos antitribu", and "a few" Toreador.

                                So overall we can say that the followers of the Path of Harmony are mostly within the Gangrel antitribu, Nosferatu antitribu, and Ravnos antitribu with only a few others elsewhere. This is probably a good indication this is a Path mainly followed by Nomad packs operating mostly in the wilderness, and not urban-based covens. It makes sense that the Path originated among Native Americans embraced by European "country Gangrel" in the early days and then spread elsewhere to other Nomad pack types. But that is interpretation, not explicit information.

                                We're also told that one of the allies of the Sabbat are a few small groups of Native American shaman societies who provide safe havens and information in exchange from protection by Gangrel antitribu who also participate in mystic rites and offer advice. These have to be vampires who were Native Americans prior to their embrace. It doesn't make sense that they are from another race or culture. It makes the most sense these are Country Gangrel on the Path of Harmony who were Native Americans prior to their embrace and retain some connection to their old religion. This doesn't sound like Gangrel who would be on the Path of Honorable Accord which is heavily based on the Code of Milan (who by default are most likely to be City Gangrel who were driven out of the wilderness by the Country Gangrel - since we're told).

                                It's not clear if these shaman societies realize they are dealing with vampires, or if they think they are some other kind of supernatural creature. But we are told the Native American Gangrel take refuge on the land of their own people. We are given the names of three of these societies, one of which is later described in the Book of Chantries in the Mage line.

                                You're right that the description of the Path doesn't mention anything about Native Americans. But when combined with other information in the same Players Guide, I think the connection is solid.

                                As written, all the talk of "harmony", "strong consciences", "most humane", and living with nature roughly matches how many white Americans thought about Native Americans at the time as a result of the movie Dances with Wolves which was HUGELY influential in how Americans began to think about Native Americans in the nineties. It became the new stereotype. I would guess it was an attempt to make a humane path along the lines of Humanity but "more appropriate" for how they imagined cultural differences between European settlers/modern society and traditional Indian cultures.

                                It also means that the Country Gangrel aren't your typical Sabbat and likely have few dealings with it. They're most likely Loyalists who are part of the Sabbat simply because it was the Sabbat who arrived in North America first when that sect was very different from the one described in the modern nights - one more about Sabbat freedom from the tyranny of the elders than a monstrous bunch of inhuman vampires under the control of a centralized hierarchy. Freedom, in this case, being "leave us alone with out own people, we don't care what the rest of us do, but if the Camarilla vampires attack our people, we'll help out the other Sabbat." My interpretation of the facts.

                                Obviously since the Path is also held a lot by Nosferatu and Ravnos antitribu, it has a following beyond that. And these variants of the Path are probably more universal and generic, while the Country Gangrel utilize more specific references of their own people. So it isn't solely based on Native American belief. It is just open to including those beliefs because that was likely the need of the original followers.


                                Incidentally, the information found here means we have to translate "the Sabbat purged the Path of Harmony" to mean the Sabbat slaughtered almost the entirety of the Country Gangrel and the Ravnos antitribu, and a third of the Nosferatu antitribu. There is no way the Bishops could have done this without provoking a Fourth Sabbat Civil War. I just don't see the other Nosferatu antitribu turning against their kin, or the other Loyalists and Nomad packs agreeing to do this or tolerating it. In any case, it wouldn't have been easy or quick to do. So the offhand comment in Revised Sabbat that they were purged simply isn't compatible with early information. Once again, we're in an Earth-One and Earth-Two scenario.

                                                        Which includes the fact that the Cam has always had Path vampires in it, it's just currently a dirty secret sort of thing, rather than something openly acknowledged and used in order to minimize the disconnect from the meta. The early writing for the Cam has them opposed to Paths because Paths are a Sabbat thing. It's not like purely Humanity vampires never have ideological struggles, and the Cam's Humanity only public position results in a kindred society without in-fighting. Many of the Paths that would make sense for the Cam to acknowledge (and to never have really lost) would inherently decrease in-fighting because in-fighting is something those Paths don't like (one of the better reasons for the Cam leadership to acknowledge those Paths, and create structures that influence otherwise troublesome vampires to join them).
