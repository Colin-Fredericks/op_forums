    Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
    The bane of "modern technology doesn't work" is pants-on-head when you realise vampires are meant to be ancient, unchanging creatures and clan Lasombra has been around for a very long time. Like, was that weakness waiting around going "Some day I'll be useful" or was their problem with tech relative to the times? Did they have trouble working with belt buckles when they were introduced?
    From the book:

    Similarly, modern technology relying on touch or other forms of direct interaction — such as voice activation or use of a stylus — tends to glitch or simply act unresponsive to Magisters
    Emphasis mine. In other words, it's an extension of the broken-reflection thing, with your image on a camera being symbolically equivalent to a "reflection". Their bane in earlier times was presumably the same as it is today:

    Anyone seeing the reflection or recording (live and otherwise) of a Lasombra vampire can instantly recognize them for what they are, provided they know what they’re looking for.
    That's the very first sentence of their bane in the official writeup. The "roll to use tech" thing is a side note at the end, to incorporate the Bane Severity mechanic (like how the most debilitating part of the Tremere bane is the inability to bond other vampires, and there's a small side detriment involving Bane Severity added on).

                                    Originally posted by Taggie View Post
                                    He's right about the touch screen thing, otherwise stylus and the posh gloves would not work.
                                    If I remember right, capacitive touchscreens by design only detect big conductive things—it's how they avoid being set off by drops of water. Styluses are generally too small to activate screens on their own, so they're designed to connect to your body and use that. Same with gloves with capacitive threads in the fingertips.

                                    Now, my previous snarky post aside, I actually don't know if cadavers are very conductive. Most of the papers I've found about that are about e.g. ways to estimate time of death by checking the conductivity of muscle tissue, not about the skin. But as you've mentioned, I know reasonably-sized sausages do work; I'd allow a Lasombra to make a Crafts roll or whatever to connect a stylus to a piece of metal in their sleeve or something like that, at which point the stylus works fine, since it's not relying on anything symbolically associated with their shadow or reflection.

                                            That's one take on it; I prefer the take that it comes down to their lack of reflection (and photographs are symbolically reflections, and phone calls are symbolically echoes, etc).

                                            But I did invent an Oblivion power for the Dresden take, since I know some players like that:

                                            Jinx

                                            All Lasombra are intimately familiar with how Oblivion interacts with technology. Most of the time, it's nothing but a headache, turning a simple text message into a minutes-long struggle to make the keyboard respond properly. But some Lasombra have found ways to twist this trait into something more useful against the SI.

                                            Cost: One Rouse Check.

                                            System: The Lasombra pulls on the Oblivion-energy in their blood, drawing it into their mouth and lungs, then exhales it as a faint black-and-red mist that disperses into the air. If they succeed at a Resolve + Oblivion roll (difficulty 3), all complex technology within five meters per point of margin jams, malfunctions, or shuts down. On a critical win, the devices burn out and cannot be reactivated without extensive repairs. “Complex technology” generally includes all electronics and any mechanical device with at least five moving parts.
