            Because like everything you broached in your post, facets in the Middle-East were already "been-there-done-that" so to speak. The Camarilla would be in no true position from then on to really subsume regions that didn't have such similar upheaval or regions with organizations already having a structure far older and experienced.

Actually in the fact of the mortal parallel of the West gaining imperialistic ground in the Middle-East, I would be of the thought the vampiric parallel of the Middle-East collective Vampiredom being on high alert and on extensive Jyhad to ensure their parallel hold of influence isn't subsumed; like how their Middle-Eastern mortal counterparts had been.                     Originally posted by CTPhipps
                    Mithras is the biggest Prince of them all and he HATED the Camarilla for undermining his sovereignty.
                    Exactly. The Camarilla is just another body of authority, and the Ashirra or otherwise another theoretical Middle-East Sect wouldn't just fall into the Camarilla at all; especially since the latter existed far longer and presumably founded under different contexts entirely. Why should such an organization recede their sovereignty onto another one automatically, without any point of dashing in some interesting Jyhad powerplay and intrigue? Especially more awkward when there is a massive start-up difference to the one attempting to claim suzerainty, versus the one that is more older and more younger (points I've discussed before).

                    The common excuse is that just because the Camarilla's mortal parallel successfully colonized the Middle-East, it should be handed all influences of it outright entirely.. But that's lazy and seems to even forget that fact that Vampires, as part of the setting by itself and by nature, are at odds with their host parallels; Vampires are parasites and the relations they have with their mortal counterparts isn't symbiotic; though maybe semi-symbiotic if some count the use of Ghouls, but then again Ghouls aren't humans anymore.

                    The Camarilla barely and were entirely kept at bay by the Sabbat for a long time. The Anarchs were always there, and not for the majority of the time not being heavy-hitters, still kicked "ass" so to speak. How do you expect them to even control or take advantage of the Ashirra? The background context of the Camarilla in the first place had many of the Elders perish, due to the nature of the Anarch revolt and how it operated. Such a revolt never happened in the Middle-East to begin with; so not only is the Ashirra older and more experienced as an organization; they also have older and more experienced Elders who have a better dynamic with their own Neonates ostensibly. Going back on the Mithras point of things, the Elders of the Middle-East would definitely NOT want to align their interests with an upstart Sect of things and lose powers to a inner body of a new authority they won't care about.

                    It already is an in-universe feat that the a Vampire parallel of a society who's Human counterpart has been subsumed from Imperialism/Colonialism can survive, via the Laibon. The Camarilla can only go so far using mortals to a point for their battles, but they can't all the time and that's the reason why the Laibon could still remain off of Camarilla dominational-influence. It also wouldn't make much sense for the Camarilla to go on such cost-effective ventures in the form of possible Final Deaths, wasted resources, money, etc. when they're already under constant endemic warfare from the Sabbat, along with the Anarchs, also fighting them.

                    To come back to the point encompassing the Ashirra/Middle-East region specifically, direct or full colonialism by the "West" in the Middle-East only happened within the 1900s (I don't account for the Crusades, because those events were conquests, not structured imperial colonialism per se). The 1900s.... even with their mortal counterparts finally establishing a foothold, on the flipside of Vampire power dynamics, it takes centuries to fully set-up a full deep-state-like or wide purveying control of a region. I'd actually see with the Ottoman Empire's collapse, the assorted Ashirra would fortify and go on the defensive, fully expecting the Camarilla to try somethings after taking heed of events that happened within the "Kindred of the Ebony Kingdom" timeframe of events; in relation to the Scramble of Africa mentioned in the book and how the Laibon reacted to it.

                    It'd be another extensive shadow "front" (denoting how the endemic conflicts each of the western Sects have are "shadow wars") where the Camarilla are dealing with alot of things. It's just isn't realistic and especially with homeground advantage (which again, is the exact reason how the Laibon survived against the Camarilla. And more especially so, considering how out-in-depth would a Cammy Vampire would be in the Ebony Kingdom) the Ashirra would have, the Camarilla would at best have maybe some important footholds in specific areas; but not wide swathes of any influential-jurisdiction.

                            I think that ultimately having the Camarilla be a western European organization that really only holds sway over Europe, the Americas and Australia was kind of a mistake.

                            The game ended up focusing almost entirely on Europe/America while leaving the rest of the world largely unknown. Like, India is doing its own thing, but what's going on there? The gameline never really explored that part of the world in depth. We got a few NPCs and a few groups, but we don't know the ins and outs of what sort of an organization keeps watch over the sub-continent or that sort of thing. The same is largely true of the Middle East. With Africa we did get a World book going into more detail, but it's still very bare bones compared to the treatment we got on Camarilla territory. We certainly never got anything like "Guide to the Ashirra" the way we got a Guide to the Camarilla or Guide to the Sabbat.

                            So personally, I think it would have been better if the Camarilla and Sabbat had been the two world wide factions, or if the game was really intent on having other comparable sects, for the gameline to have actually devoted time and word count to properly exploring them. The non-European/American sections of the world ended being entirely side-lined and playing almost no role in the game, whereas if they had been a part of the Camarilla or Sabbat at least everything Camarilla and Sabbat related would have also applied to them.

                            Hopefully V5 starts to seriously explore the Middle East, India, Africa, etc, the parts of the world that were never really bothered with before.
