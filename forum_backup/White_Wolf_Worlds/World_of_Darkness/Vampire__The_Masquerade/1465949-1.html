    To be honest, I think clans are something that is too often emphasized in games. Obviously clans provide a lot of the culture of the vampire, and some clans have more rigid structures than others. But I've seen too many games where the politics of a domain are driven almost entirely by clans as if they were some group that commanded absolute loyalty by its members, and I don't see that. That was a real problem in some of the LARPs I've been in, and I always disliked it because I felt it tended to hijack the game rather than contribute to it.

    I think they are best seen as large, extended families. You have some obligation to others of your blood, and you see each other during family gatherings, but otherwise that's it. Actual politics are much more personal. You can have rivals and enemies inside your clan just as easily as those outside of it. In so far there is any true loyalty of those of the same clan, it's more likely to between broodmates and sire-childer - the immediate family of a far larger extended family with various prominent lineages and distant branches often being separate families. The smaller the group, the more likely they have common interests.

    Even for the more centralized clans, that structure is there for vampires who want to raise their status or influence within the clan. No vampire must to do that. You can be the black sheep of the clan and don't have anything to do with that. Or be the person who shows up, but contributes nothing. Even for a clan like the Tremere, which is the example of centralized internal control, we had the immediate examples of Garwood Marshall who didn't contribute anything to the local chantry. And both Nicolai and DuSable seemed to disobey the Pyramid on occasion. They don't get hunted down and destroyed for disobeying. They just don't get to rise within the clan hierarchy or benefit from that social solidarity. It's more about peer pressure, not getting supported later should you need it, and the fear whether you'll be mentioned in the will. Now that doesn't mean you can't have some local chantry of board or salon or whatever hunt down and destroy a vampire of that clan for somehow not towing the line. But that's because the head vampire is some kind of psychopath or tyrant. It's not part of the clan. Only if someone is seen to have truly betrayed the entire family might something like that happen.

    The other thing about clans is that they become more important the older the vampire gets. A neonate still has many ties to the mortal world. They have friends and family members from the time they were still living. They have interests and passions from their mortal dies that still motivate them. The world around them is what they know and understand. However, as time passages, their ties to the mortal world passes. Not only their old friends and families, but even things we often think as more permanent like social institutions, countries, religion, and more can either end or change into an unrecognizable form sharing only the name. But the clan remains the same. The elders continue to live, and their culture survives. As influence among mortals are seen to fade and end, power and influence within the clan gains greater importance and seen as something more permanent. So clans are much more important to the elders than to neonates.

    As for the individual clans themselves, almost anything important about them can be found in the typical two page clan write ups in the corebooks, plus the few pages given in the Players Guide. The clanbooks typically provide more details, but aren't actually needed. The unique information there is more like trivia to namedrop, than something that should cause you to run things differently from the basics provided in the corebook and Players Guide.

        How do I roleplay the Clans 4#: Malkavians

        As a general rule, I think Malkavians are people who should have understated insanity. It's not that it's not there but I think a good more than half of Malkavians are perfectly high-functioning and able to fit into Kindred society just fine.

        I think I used the description, "The majority of Malkavian derangements are narcissism, megalomania, blood obsession, reduced empathy [roll Humanity for 2 stains instead of one], berserk fury, paranoia, and sanguinary animism [Your Beast talks to you]." In short, the vast majority of Malkavians are people who have only slightly exaggerated or perhaps even precocious versions of what Elder vampires already have out of habit.

        I tend to suggest Malkavians tend to have lower Humanity than the majority of Kindred in the Camarilla but they tend to stabilize a lot faster. So the majority of Malkavians you'll meet are around 5-6 Humanity as Fledglings and Neonates. They are removed from Humanity in the same way the Tzimisce tend to be, though on a different path. One player of mine came up with a very interesting idea to separate the Malkavian derangement from "mortal" derangements as well by taking some elements from the Daughters of Cacophany. All Malkavians are hearing SOMETHING all the time in the background of their minds. Words of Malkav or other Malkavians perhaps but develop their frailties as a means of trying to cope with the
        concept.

        I do think that the "Trickster" archetype is alive and well among Malkavians with the fact they are people who very often have questionable desire for power and prestige compared to other Kindred. As such, they are often people who enjoy bringing down the haughty and prideful for no other reason than they can. These aren't the majority of Malkavians but it's a tradition in their clan and they have a decent chunk among the Anarchs unlike what V5 says.

        The use of Auspex, Dominate, and Obfuscate also means that Malkavians are able to gain insights into what other Kindred are up to better than people think. Unlike the Nosferatu, they aren't generally information brokers but still gather secrets as a way of forwarding their own plans or sabotaging the plans of others. Generally, other Kindred underestimate what Malkavian spies are up to because they either believe that the Kindred are receiving "signals" from their abilities or because they don't think Malkavians are capable of the long-term planning that other Kindred are.

        Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

            Toreador

            Embrace: More than most other clans, especially with so much prestige, the Toreador are likely to be extremely personal on their Embraces. But it isn't that the Clan won't judge it, quite the contrary. They'll definitely judge a member's choice and procedure. Still, that only means localized social pressure, instead of any organized standard.

            This makes the Toreador an extremely diversified clan. That may be one of the things that keep them at least decent at socializing, as they have to adjust to what is actually a pretty diverse family. It also means Childe are quite affected by their interpersonal relationship with their Sire, as other clan members and greater clan culture intrudes little. After that they're shaped by their interactions with the local Kindred society, including clan members.

            Structure: the clan has little in terms of structure, instead focusing on just keeping in touch and sharing interests. Their strength and their weakness as Kindred is their ability to exist in society, so they must have theirs in some form.

            This makes them used to deal with diversity and dynamic interactions, more than actually forces them into any form of conformity. Except, of course, that it also exposes everyone among them to their best bullies. In such a situation no Toreador can be indifferent to the opinions of their peers for long, and most sharpen their senses to deal with it.

            Some may actually retract and try to not get a lot of attention. They may be numerous, but by virtue of their behavior they also don't weigh much in the clan's reputation, so their actual numbers don't matter.

            Weakness: beauty is in the eye of the beholder, but it doesn't change that the clan weakness makes every Toreador keenly aware of how it affects people. After all, they may be more susceptible, but no one is actually immune.

            It also creates a survival pressure few Kindred comprehend. Toreador go to great lengths to fill their havens and lives with beauty because they will die if they don't. Why? Simply put, if they don't inure themselves by excessive contact with beauty, the very world around them becomes a life-threatening danger.

            Have you ever had a moment when you found out of a sudden that a little thing you usually take for granted is actually very beautiful? Or found a beautiful image, art piece or person, when you simply weren't expecting? A Toreador isn't actually more likely or interested in such things, but they can become entranced by such fleeting moments all the same regardless of how much they really want to pay attention.

            So it is better to be surrounded by beauty and raise your standards as much as possible, so you're not likely to freeze in place when you definitely shouldn't. And if the other clans think low of you, fuck them, they don't give you blood, keep your roof or pay your bills, do they?

            Disciplines: Presence makes you the most beautiful thing around. With all you learn from your weakness, it's logical to learn to use this aspect of the Discipline. Even because you know very well that it only goes so far into shaping emotions, the other half is always in the beholder's eye again, so you're kind of stuck with it, like it or not. You know beauty, live surrounded by beauty, and other Kindred probably only see you as that. It's not as if any of them will remember you by your effect on mortals...

            Auspex is almost as much a curse as a blessing at first, as the Toreador have to bear an even more colorful and detailed perception of reality. But it also helps in seeing hidden ugliness as much as beauty. As it grows in power the Kindred acquires an excellent window in the emotions of others that goes well by itself or in tandem with Presence.

            Celerity is extremely versatile ass it helps you to be where you want when you want, be it at Elysium or out of Dodge. Of course, its uses in combat also mean a Toreador can be a capable duelist (great for dramatic flair in court) or a very capable fighter when they decide the other stuff isn't really their thing. A diverse physical Discipline for a diverse clan.

            Culture: Toreador culture is more informed by their shared experiences in Kindred society than by shared goals, dogma or what have you. Beauty is always in their talks as much because they learn to appreciate it as a way for them to share survival strategies.

            Outside that, though, it isn't a club, a cult or an enterprise. It is a society. They have little in common except their need of socializing for the sake of self preservation and no pressure to isolate. And while it isn't a safe place in most reasonable standards, it is still a place where being emotional and vulnerable is too common to be a problem.

            Characters: A Toreador can be almost anyone and anything, with a twist of being outwardly an hedonist, or just not be seen at all as a member of the clan.

            Seeking beauty in safe circumstances becomes quickly second nature after the first time that you lose hours of your night out of a sudden stuck in place. This is a very scary situation, the sensation of loosing yourself.

            Otherwise, socialization informs a lot of those characters in very different ways. Many learn to derive power from it, many just feel better within any social gathering. Sometimes it is just their yearning for company, but at other times it is the safety of having someone nearby that can get you out of a reverie.

            Those who avoid large gatherings may still seek groups they feel safe with, and they quickly become the social glue of those. Others may avoid socialization entirely, as their reputation is actually far out of proportion.

            What they can't avoid, though, is the impression they leave in the other Kindred and the feedback loop it creates.

                    Tremere

                    Embrace: more than Ventrue, the Tremere are a tightly controlled structure. Way before you earn the right for Embrace from your Prince you must earn it from your clan. It is a proposal for an initiation. Your chantry and its peers will discuss the candidates, evaluate, compare.

                    Which Embrace has something to add to the Warlocks? Even an approval is less about you being rewarded with the right of siring, and more about being recognized as the one who brought the most promising apprentice. They are not your childe more than a new member of the clan.

                    This selection process is long, structured and pragmatic. Observe and take notes about candidates. Write a thorough report about their strengths, their benefits to the clan, weaknesses, verifiable knowledge, ending with a pitch on why they should be brought in. Add to it regularly, with new observations and test results on the candidate.

                    Candidates may be chosen for any reason. The ability to learn and develop the arcane arts of the clan is highly desirable, but not always required. They may have valuable knowledge in areas the clan still needs, talent acquiring material resources, sometimes a new perspective that can provide insight in the development of new Paths of Blood Magic, or just be well positioned in a number of ways. But the clan will hardly suffer stupidity or incompetence if a candidate can't hugely compensate this in some way.

                    Structure: the Tremere can barely be called a clan in the more familial sense of the word. Anyone that can't fit in the hierarchy isn't just a pariah, but a full liability to be brought down. They are an occult order and an army. You have a position in the hierarchy, benefits and functions, a superior to answer to, a number of responsibilities.

                    Obedience isn't an option. You don't belong to your Sire or to a culture. Your personal beliefs are irrelevant as long as you comprehend loyalty isn't in any way a choice. You're watched and evaluated, and non compliance or lack of value isn't just grounds to removal of benefits, but to outright termination of your "employment" and benefits, including the benefit of living.

                    Being a Tremere isn't only enslavement, of course. With the same amount of exigence come the benefits. What the clan require, the clan provide the means to achieve. Even a small chantry is also library, university campus, fortress, communication point and shelter. Most likely an armory. Your superiors are demanding, but they are also very clear in their demands, as Tremere have no use for wordplay and pretense among themselves.

                    And finally, as long as you meet basic expectations and don't put it at risk, the clan won't care about your personal affairs. Culture and belief are not of their concern. You may benefit greatly from your efforts to grow in the Pyramid if you have the ambition for it, but you're also left to your devices if you just want to fulfill your quota and go home.

                    More than many other clans the Tremere knows what it means to be more of an organization than infiltrating them. And as such, they know ambition plays a huge role in their midst and must be rewarded, but so does simple conformity. Ambition will be rewarded, though, and they have many ways to do so, from bits of knowledge or special work or learning opportunities, to an endless ladder of positions in their occult hierarchy when a member prove themselves and outsmart their rivals.

                    Weakness: in a sense, there is none. The Tremere were self-Embraced and no such a thing exists among them as understood by other Kindred. They're as perfect as a powerful Caitiff can be. The Pyramid certainly has no weakness.

                    For individual Tremere their weakness is the clan. Whatever version of their weakness you use, they individually pay for the practices of the clan to control them. The leaders of this big scheme don't trust their underlings in any meaningful way and use their magics freely on them.

                    Your blood is in their possession. Literally. At least a few drops are safely guarded at secret locations where some of your superiors may access it to work their sympathetic magics on you. If you're weak against Blood Bonds it is because they made you so to have you pliable. If instead you are incapable of Bonding, it is because they took it from you so you can't ever use this against them. If they didn't did anything like that, then they at least started Bonding you already. And if they didn't case your weakness on purpose, it's still a side effect of their magics on you.

                    My personal preferred version is that the clan became incapable of Blood Bond recently, as some in the Council of Seven finally achieved to enact a ritual to this effect. They had been victims to it for about a thousand years, and have no love for this ability. The Bond clouds the mind with unhealthy emotion, is unnecessary when they have your blood and their magics and above all else, this ritual freed them finally from Tremere, the Founder.

                    Disciplines: it is hard to say when their signature Disciplines came to be that. Was it by design, accident or through effort? Only very few among them can surely know the answer, but it is nonetheless as set that serves well their organization.

                    The Tremere's approach to Discipline use is open and direct. They're not a manifestation of personality or anything, just straightforward tools. You are a student and researcher, and so expected to gather information. You will not suffer resistance to the clan's orders, Dominate ensure obedience. Thaumaturgy is the Great Work and Tool of the clan, questioning it isn't a point.

                    What's more interesting than their spread is the unique relation they have with their powers. They covet their secrets, but freely offer their services. Clan members are encouraged to exhibit some powers freely so everyone knows what they can offer. They're maybe the most unashamed users of Dominate, and they don't pretend to not make free use of their Auspex.

                    Not that they're careless about this, they pretty much have to be. Instead, while other Kindred use their Disciplines to grab influence and power, the Tremere as a clan use theirs as bargaining chips and arguments.

                    Of course, they have secret plans, but that's the gist of their relationship with their supernatural powers. And how you use yours on your personal life won't change how you're required to do your actual job for the clan.

                    Culture: as an academic and militaristic organization, the Tremere have a strong internal culture with not as much pressure for a member to belong. For those who dive in, it is a world in itself that greatly rewards ambition and intellect, and impressing your superiors is of utmost importance, while surpassing your peers is an absolute necessity through either hard work or sabotage.

                    For those who abstain from it, is at the same time a source of security and oppression. You have obligations to fulfill and superiors measuring your performance, but you also have this same structure to give you stability. Luck have a role to play, as you may be positioned in a key station where your challenges are big and constant, or may have just a few obligations beyond weekly reports and annual tests.

                    You are never invisible to the system, though, and that guarantees every member develop some sort of paranoia.

                    Characters: as members from a driven organization with a global strategy and structured work, the Tremere are varied but enslaved to a will above their own.

                    It is less that they have a lot in common and more that they have to play the part. Some relish it. Some resent it profoundly. Some are good at it and others don't, the clan doesn't always require that you pretend to like it in front of others.

                    Many learn to act in the shadows, where the clan can't see them (or at least they think it can't). Some do so to rebel, although not always in a big way. Many just want to sabotage internal rivals. Others want to "breath" a little, only. Even the most loyal learn to hide failure and problems that the upper echelons may worry about out of proportion. The clan, of course, is completely aware of this fact.

                    But in the open the Tremere make sure that your individuality simply don't matter. You are a servant of the Pyramid and act as its eyes and hands. Anyone who thinks otherwise is a fool and they know it as well as you do, regardless of what you want.

                        Malkavian

                        ===============

                        On the matter of Madness: It is easy to play madness for laughs. It is also easy to condemn the practice. What isn't so easy is to decide what to do with it and how it should be played.

                        Some may avoid "overt" derangement altogether, thinking they're silly by nature. I don't think many of the actual people that suffer from them would agree. But then, it may be just a matter of comfort zone. That's ok, but limiting. In the end I can give some advice, but it's hard to define and decide.

                        My favorite NPC Malkavian is the Voerman "Sisters". A very overt form of madness not being played for laughs or for gore. Both personalities are competent and interesting characters in their own right, and their changes in control aren't random, either.

                        The advice I can give is this: if you want to portray a person with mental health issues, ask an actual person with similar issues. We are not in the dark anymore in those days. Many of us have normal lives despite our neurological and/or psychological differences, and many became bloggers, vloggers, etc, talking about our conditions on the internet. You will find out how it really is like to live with things like Schizophrenia and Dissociative Disorders.

                        Also learn a little about some definitions on disorders, but a quick primer on it, though:

                        Madness - is primarily a social perception. Although many people think that it equates to "mental disease", this isn't actually true;

                        Insanity - is actually a legal term. Isn't about how much a person needs health care or anything, but just about how much the person can be deemed guilty of a crime. Voerman isn't insane, not even when she killed her father (although she was a child, so not guilty by default);

                        Disorder - is the actual medical term, and comes in many forms and variations. It is based on adaptability, though, so many weird behaviors that would be promptly regarded as madness aren't disorders, and the opposite is also true. I have Autism Spectrum Disorder;

                        Randomness - is a common misconception about mental health problems. People seem as Mad are frequently regarded as incomprehensible and random by observers, but this is mostly born out of a disconnection in perception. As a rule of thumb, Disorders are far more marked by a difficulty in breaking patterns than in following them, even if not the patterns that people expect.

                        ============

                        Embrace: no clan-wide rule or expectation exists about embraces in the clan. The few things it expects in a new member will be brought up by the process anyway.

                        Many Embrace while at the mercy of their Weakness in some way. And many pretend to have done so. In fact, Malkavians aren't the only clan without any rules for Embraces, but they're the most likely to ignore the Domain rules and get away with it. It's so the de facto most diverse clan around by far.

                        Structure: one change in Revised isn't an actual change, as it was there in their description since always, the Malkavian Madness Network. The clan have a loose structure where very few demands are thrown on its members, but they have more contact than any other clan.

                        The nature of this structure is mostly social, with a heavy exchange of news and support. Malkavians know each other and exchange news on their side of the world, some may have an alliance and share particular pieces of information, and everyone is welcome to just hang around at a safe place.

                        If there's one rule it's this: the Network is a safe place. Everyone have issues, everyone needs a place where they can speak out their thing without being judged. If the clan can't provide, they'll be overwhelmed by other's prejudice and collectively weaken. You don't have to like your clan mates, but respect them in the Network.

                        What makes this structure special and baffles the other clans is the fact that Malkavians interact in their own minds, either because their Ante became a hive-mind of sorts, or just because some members use their own Auspex to set up their meetings.

                        Weakness: the seeds of madness lie in the minds of everyone, and the Malkavian Curse makes it bloom in its most absolute glory. That is an unsettling experience as your own mind craft your customized condition just by growing something out of proportion.

                        Out of the deal comes overblown prejudice and new understanding of the human mind, especially your own.

                        In the end, though, the prejudice part is what makes this weakness be a social glue for the clan. The inner experiences are too unique, as are the individual reactions to them. But the outside experiences of how the others react to the mere mention of your clan's name, that's the way the other clans show to you where your home is.

                        Disciplines: it's complicated to separate the Clan Disciplines from the Clan Weakness with the Malkavian. Auspex gives access to information outside the perceptions of other Kindred, Obfuscate make other look the other way or let you be whomever you want to be, Dominate and Dementation mess with the minds and emotions of others.

                        But being mad makes a bad night do pretty much the same things, in a way. At least that's what most people think, and isn't completely untrue.

                        What can then be said for sure is that those powers work even more to set the Kindred apart from its "normal" peers, every use of them being either an alienating experience or the forceful imposition of yourself on others. It's also a great way to delve deeper on one's own problems.

                        That belies, though, one of the most powerful Discipline spreads when used together. Combined those three Disciplines strengthen each other to astounding degrees, as they cover each other limitations.

                        Culture: with a common experience of alienation and contact through the Madness Network, Malkavians tend to have a strong sense of community and a somewhat supportive attitude towards one another.

                        They meet in mind or body, they talk about their stuff, share information, find others with similar experience or at least find people that won't look them with fear or pity in the eyes. This tends to be an important fixture in their lives, so as much as they're not of a single purpose, few among them won't fight to keep things that way.

                        Keeping the workings of this community from being understood by other vampires is also a common cause, as any Malkavian that could care to make it clearer also understands the advantage of not doing so. Only a Malkavian can truly participate directly and without risking their own mind, so they're not only an excellent way to keep people guessing and nervous, they're also excellent to discuss important stuff protected from prying eyes.

                        Characters: Anyone can become a Malkavian. But no one can make it unscathed.

                        Building a Malk demands a deeper than normal examination of the character's psyche. Legend says that only people with already a foot in madness is chosen for the Embrace, but truth is that "madness" is just a little twist in normal human experience. You can't be human and not have a foot there.

                        So the process comes down to examine the character and find the seed you'll use to build their unique mind, then choose the way it will go from "normal" to "mad". Then, as you revisit the character's story through the lens of the new madness, it becomes easy to reinterpret their lives as unhinged from start.

                        What else informs a Malkavian distinctly from a random character is the "Malkavian treatment". How people around you deal with your condition? How did other Kindred reacted when learning your clan? How did you dealt with the Network? The experiences brought by the mere fact of being a Malkavian may bring succor, resentment, sometimes even be more traumatic than any cause for their specific madness.
