    The Bone Path: Bonefidly boneheaded.

    10-04-2022, 11:17 AM
    I realize it's supposed to be terribly out of fashion in modern nights and you don't want players thinking they should be Von Carsteins with such a power, but it's a frustratingly terribly written path even when you take into account that it was probably made to suck to discourage player use. Reading V20, which really should've been the edition that irons out messes like like this. Take a moment to admire how arbitrary and broken this whole path is.

    Things in general:
    Zombies do not have mental stats. While we can explain away a lack of intelligence and wits there's a glaring issue in that they do not have a perception stat. Either they sense nothing or they have sensory omniscience and the stealth of a tiny magic octopus hidden beneath the sand is as difficult to find for them as a two-year old failing to understand how hide and seek works. I suspect sensory omniscience is the way we're going... but isn't that so strong that zombies would be used a lot more often? Perhaps setting the zombie's perception level to the user's path rating would make sense?

    What's the range of this path? Is does this power need touch or is sight enough? It doesn't say. I assumed touch at first and it would be more ritual-like, but reading into it, sight seems to be the default. I don't have a problem with either, but not saying either seems pretty lax.


    Tremens
    "under no circumstances can a corpse animated by Tremens be used to attack"
    But can they activate a booby trap? It's well within their capability to pull a trigger, but they can't do that for a shotgun pointed at the door? They can't give someone close by a push? Maybe drop a bowlling ball on someone? knock over a domino to set off an overly elaborate trap? This is a completely arbitrary caveat that leaves me with a lot of questions and there's no justification for it.


    I would get rid of the inability to attack, and instead set the zombie's strength to 1.
    The more suc-cesses that are achieved, the more complicated an action can be affected in the corpse. one success allows for an instantaneous movement, such as a twitch, while five allow the vampire to set up specific conditions un-der which the body animates (“The next time someone enters the room, I want the corpse to sit up and open its eyes.”
    They've detailed one and five successes but not three, the default for -good success-. That's a potential headache for players/storytellers disatisfied with the other's interpretation of how complicated something is. More painful is that the example given with five successes isn't complicated at all. The difficulty for the roll is 6, so 5 successes should be impressive, like being able to make a programming tree of conditions and possible movements.

    1 success: crude instant movement
    2 success: crude movement on a condition being reached, or complicated movement now.
    3 successes: complicated movement on a condition being reached. or precise movement for a turn.
    4 successes: Two commands are possible, or one command with three conditions is possible.
    5 successes: Whatever you want.


    Apprentice's brooms & Shambling hordes.
    Is it me, or is a level 2 power significantly more impressive than a level 3 power? I mean level 3 gives you pain resistant but barely competent animals who follow a single simple command, while level 2 gives you functional automatons that can build whatever you want so long as they're following instructions and don't have to make choices, but arbitrarily can't attack (I mean they can shoot targets, but not people. They can cut down trees but not humans, they can administer vaccines but not poison... Perhaps you could have them hug the enemy while strapped with explosives...)

    Soul stealing & Daemonic posession.
    I fail to see what either of these has to do with controlling corpses? Soul stealing is an expensive trick to incapacitate mortals and Daemonic posession has far too many caveats to be a level 5 power. Both would be better as rituals.



    This is messy. My rewrite draft for simplification/thematic unity:

    Level 1: Tremens.
    Level 2: 2 strength*, simple tasks, dies after completing task or decomposing. 1 blood per casting, success number of zombies.
    Level 3: 3 strength, complicated instructions, continues to serve even as bones.
    1 blood per zombie. 1 willpower if giving an advanced instruction
    Level 4: 4 strength. obeys the creator without the use of the power. Has the user's talents and skills. (4 max) The body is preserved without need for another power and appears more lifelike with more human movement (Appearance rating of the corpse). Regains health by eating flesh.
    1 wp, 3 blood per zombie.
    Level 5: 5 strength. Has some autonomy. Can instruct level 4 zombies. Starts with 1 dot in charisma, manipulation, intelligence and wits. gains attributes and regains health points by eating flesh (max of 5 or the maker's attributes).
    2 willpower, 10 blood per zombie. Willlpower lowered by 1 per level 5 zombie active.

    -Adjustments: Because allowing players to create undead mice or sparrows or rabbits that can bench 300kgs is a little too terrifying, the Strength levels used here apply to human adults and anything larger. For anything smaller the level should be adjusted to be relative: Level 3 should be a typical fit example of the species, level 4 would be an exceptional example, and level 5 would perhaps be a little beyond what can be expected since animals tend not to push themselves spending time at the gym and especially not with with copious exogenous hormones in their system. For many animals such as the aforementioned mouse, the 1-5 dot range the strength isn't even going to meet what would be considered subpar (1) for humans. A 35kg dog 's 5 would probably translate to 3 dice of strength.
    Whether empowering a tiny animal (<1kg) with level 1 or 2 path of bones should max them out in strength or keep them weak is up for discussion. I would posit that making a large zombie (let's say a bear, or human mass monsters like this happy man would probably just yield more health levels rather than more strength, because muscles mean little to an undead's strength; it's all in the force that's animating the body).


    Thoughts/suggestions?I was wondering if the higher level zombie should have different names:
    Ghoul and wight are taken terms, draugr isn't, but such characters were spectacularly ugly/violent and although VTM's naming sense can be a bit daft, I would prefer to go more in the direction of Masquerade-friendly/actually usable than just monstrously powerful.

              I wanna put some respect on Bone Path 5.

              Daemonic Possession was my favorite power when I played a Giovanni. It allowed me to gain faithful spirit servants by the dozen.
              Allow the wraith to possess a prepared body, and give them a chance to do what they could not in death. Hug their children one last time. Have a beer at their favorite pub, or get a chance to beat up their widow's new boyfriend.
