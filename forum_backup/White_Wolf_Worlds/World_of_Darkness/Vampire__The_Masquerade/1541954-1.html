                            Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post
                            It's more compelling a setting if the damn things can actually die.
                            I agree that I prefer a setting where some antediluvians are actually destroyed. It makes the Jyhad real, and it lends itself to some cool distinctions between clans. A good mix of founders actually destroyed, believed destroyed but actually not, and survivors can make for some interesting scenarios and legends about Gehenna.


                            However, in terms of Lasombra himself, I am of the mind that he has survived. He was never diablerized. IMO, the entire thing about Gratiano is that he was the ultimate test. We know from Children of the Inquisition that Lasombra had destroyed all his other childer by the time he embraced Montano because he always doubted their loyalty. So it doesn't make much sense that he embraced Gratiano in the way he did. He embraced Gratiano precisely because he was someone who already betrayed his own side. And almost immediately upon embrace, Gratiano began plotting to usurp his sire and take his place. Do we really believe that the Lasombra antediluvian as described in the Montano chapter is the same guy who couldn't see past Gratiano's sycophancy? His constant testing of the real limits of Lasombra's commands? Who couldn't believe Montano's own warnings? Unless he went senile during torpor, I cannot believe that.

                            To me, the most likely scenario is that Lasombra used Gratiano to determine the true loyalties of his entire clan, most of whom were descended from the 4th generation childer he had already destroyed for failing to achieve his standards before embracing Montano. CotI says Lasombra "craved faithfulness even beyond his final annihilation" which means he wanted childer to be loyal to him even after his destruction. (That's an odd quote on its own unless we're supposed to realize it's a clue.) So what better test of his clan than to present them with his own destruction? Who remains loyal when that happens? Who turns out to be false?

                            In-character descriptions in WoD sourcebooks always need to be taken with a grain of salt, examined critically, and compared to others. The chapters on Gratiano and Montano describe very different antediluvians. They are not the same character. I am pretty sure this was done on purpose to provide clues to the readers. We get Gratiano's chapter first in order to impress in our minds that Lasombra was just some fool who couldn't see the obvious in front of him. We're then given lots of information to the contrary in Montano's chapter, and I believe some critical clues that reveal the "real story". But we ignore their implications because we're already used to the Gratiano version of events.

                            I could be completely wrong as to the actual intent of the authors of CotI, but this is what I use for my chronicles as ST. Lasombra is very much around. Maybe he's in the Abyss or not, but he definitely has a physical body, and the Sabbat Lasombra are going to have a very big surprise for them when Gehenna happens. (One of the secret ironies in the WoD is that the Sabbat is wrong about almost everything. They didn't kill the two antediluvians they thought they did. Vestgeir almost certainly was not Caine. And much of their leadership and effective packs are members of the True Black Hand.) Later sourcebooks written by different authors under different developers had other ideas, and that's fine too if you want to use them.
