            I just want to pipe in and say how much I enjoy the New Black Hand Book.

            I particularly like the cleaned up motivation of Old Clan Tzimisce. Previous books have always presented a sort of weird twisted connection to Kupula. In one instance they worship him for providing them with their Koldun sorcery and on the other actively work against his corruption of the land. I was never really sure how the Tzimisce felt about Kupula as the material was contradictory. When Krainas were first presented in V20 Dark Ages I felt the change was unnecessary and jarring. The new fluff, Kupula having corrupted Koldune, fits great with the setting and ads a bit of motivation and mythology that keeps me reading and enjoying VtM.

            The evolution of the Molochites was nice. The text kept to motivations first expressed in DA Clanbook Baali. I've always taken from the Baali Clanbook the reason the Baali keep the Children sleeping was more so they could extract power via Daimoinon without paying the consequences. Their motivation was self interest rather than any form of altruism. With the Black Hand: A Guide to the Tal'Mahe'Ra the Cult although still monstrous, becomes more sympathetic and righteous. They are an Inquisition against Infernalism. (Even though they sacrifice to demons they do so for righteous reasons) An interesting shift since the loss of Moloch.

            I liked the over all tenor of the book. The idea of a Cult made of Cults tenuously held together by their common goal to serve the powers that sleep in the crypts. Entities on whose identity they cannot agree and if seriously debated could split the tenuous alliance. This common theme threaded its way well through most of the book. The story at the beginning of each chapter worked well to highlight the material and brought what was always represented as a Sect of elders to the level of a neonate.

            The weakest portion of the book for me was the inclusion of the Megher, Golconda and Diablorie. Positively, I liked the expansion of the system for both Golconda and Diablorie for those STs who wish to include either in their game. Until the rules section, however neither Daiblorie nor Golconda had previously received a jot of attention. Their inclusion just felt out of place. As I said I liked the write up for both but thought they would have fit better in a different book.

            As for the Megher I just felt they were superfluous. Unlike the other clans and bloodlines they weren't as entwined within the overall text and reason for inclusion was never really expressed in the overall narrative. It was nice that there was a tie in to V20 DA but they may have better served as a template like abominations rather than as a full bloodline.

            As an aside I hope the Bloodlines book ties in the fluff of Lamia surviving from V20 and their recreation (re-embrace) in Tal'Mahe'Ra

            Great book with a lot of material to digest extract and use. I'd like to shout out to Obsidian and the writers for providing a great book.
