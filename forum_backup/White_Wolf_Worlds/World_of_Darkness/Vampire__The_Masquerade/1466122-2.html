                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                    I feel like the Feast of Folly has been incredibly overstated in the lore.
                                                    Some key aspects to understand the Feast of Folly:

                                                    1 - The Cappadocian was the biggest clan at the time. Affecting any percentage of them is a bigger thing than affecting the same percentage anywhere else;

                                                    2 - It is also a very organized clan for the time, but also very quiet. Even if irregular purges were a thing in other clans, the Feast was unprecedented for them. This story is about their perception, so that's what matters;

                                                    3 - About half the attendees are reported as affected. This' a big proportion for any purge, so pretty much an unprecedented purge when we combine those first 3 factors;

                                                    4 - Cappadocius called the clan to their seat of power and most sacred place at the time, so it isn't just a culling happening at a random place. And the place itself is lost to the clan as it is turned into a prison. This adds a lot of significance to the fact by itself;

                                                    5 - No killing is reported before the closing. That means that instead of "simply" a purge what happened was that a huge amount of Kindred for the time got themselves entombed alive in a sacred labyrinth, left to their own devices.

                                                    But what really makes the event relevant is it causing splits in the clan that had no splits before. It isn't a sign of Gehenna or anything, but the event in their history that defines the origins of Infitiores and Harbingers, maybe more. It is the earliest schism in the Cappadocian, and if nothing else is exquisitely executed.

                                                    You shouldn't claim it got rid of losers, though, as this was not quite the intent. No one involved describes the Feast as that. Cappadocius had an arcane set of requirements related mainly to their ability as researchers, which gives credence to AkatsukiLeader13's affirmation, at the very least the Feast victimized members whose main assets were to give support to the clan outside doing research, including soldiers, assassins and spies. So at the very least the victims were "losers" only as defined by a very unhinged guy.

                                                    Maybe there was other reasons behind the decisions or the entire endeavor. It is a very strange occurrence, an extremely specific way to get rid of excess baggage when most Ancients are far more straightforward about it. Even if not, the result is a bloodline entirely comprised of elders or, more probably, Methuselah with an extremely close relation to the Underworld.

                                                    Just to have a notion of the numbers: it's reported only among attendees more than 12 thousand Cappadocians. By all accounts this should be more than the entire Kindred population at the time. If you go by the set standard of 1:100k Kindred-to-Human ratio, they would need a population of around 1.2 billion humans. At the time of Augustus' Embrace, an unspecified amount of time after the Feast, the entire world population is estimated at 310 millions, less than 26 thousand mortals in the entire world for every Cappadocian that just appeared at the Feast.

                                                            The inordinate focus on the Feast of Folly is just a symptom of an issue that has plagued the Cappadocian concept from the beginning: an excessive focus on the clan founder and metaplot developments at the expense of player-facing material about the clan. Who thought that giving a clan of reclusive scholars and mystics the most active, the most meddling, the most micromanaging founder to have ever unlived was a good idea? Achilli's Clanbook: Cappadocian is more interested in the personal history and psychology of Cappadocius than in the history and evolution of the clan. Cappadocius is supposed to be a genuine Antediluvian blood god from prehistoric times, yet we know more about his personality, his wacky shenanigans, and his kooky plans than about the goings-on of the youngest clan founders, Tremere and Augustus Giovanni. Nothing could kill all sense of mystery about the Antediluvians more effectively.
