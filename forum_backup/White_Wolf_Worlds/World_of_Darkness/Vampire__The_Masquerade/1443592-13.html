    Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
    While it might be an exaggeration to ascribe everything to malicious and intentional -shallNotBeNamed- , I do think V5 does a significant job in lowering the scope of genres available, and emphasizing some aspects of the game at the expense of others; The moves towards simplified, cinematic combat also remove the more simulationist possibilities that could've been undertaken in v20
    Honestly, I think the biggest benefit of V5 is the fact that it dramatically broadens the scope of underutilized genres in the vampire game. Not only is combat and mechanics more simplified but it really does give a lot of incentive for doing street level gaming that didn't exist there before. I used to be very hostile to MAGE REVISED due to the extensiveness of use that I made of the Umbra in my games, almost to the point of being Nobilis, but when I actually tried out a street mage game, it proved to be incredibly fun.

    losing control all the time eliminates the possibility of playing a cool vampire, at least in my view. Clan compulsions... I wanna be in control, and the idea that I can do absolutely nothing to mitigate the prospect of losing control (other than murder people before I want to do something) is problematic both on a personal level and on the level that it effects the setting and actions of
    everyone involved.
    Honestly, I think perception is the cause of the problem here because if you play the rules as is, the V20 vampires are the ones that should be frenzying all the time. I believe that a lot of Storytellers got out of the habit of calling for frenzy checks when in danger, when angry, when there was any fire larger than a cigarette, when injured, or the scent of blood nearby. The rules make it very clear that this is meant to be an extremely common thing. I note that I actually think that the ability to manage the Beast goes up with the fact that the mind is "quieted" when you kill someone in the game. Plus, Compulsions mean that you are LESS likely to go on a killing spree but just have your behavior altered.

    But there are ample ways of managing the Beast in-game, not the least bit keeping that Hunger at 1 and only having to deal with the threat if confronted with great provocation. Let's not pretend that V20's rules don't make it clear that if you spend that last Blood Point, you won't carve up your own coterie and the nearest baby. When you don't, that was because the ST made a decision to not do so because it wouldn't be fun. V5 has solidified that lessened tendency to go on a murder spree into the rules.

    Even the Bestial Failures have been designed to move AWAY from random murder.

    RANDOM COMPULSIONS

    1-3: Hunger
    4-5: Dominance
    6-7: Harm
    8-9: Paranoia
    0: Clan Compulsion*

    (There's also a degree of agentic shift here; If the Beast fucks shit up, and I can't do anything reasonable to control the Beast, then I'm not responsible; my loss of control is what bothers me, not that the Beast used my body and killed a baby or whatever. End of discussion.)


    I don't think that in the entirety of the genre of vampires has ever made this an attitude of a protagonist. Vampires may be cursed in the literal sense with an uncontrollable lust for blood but when they do something horrible under duress they still carry that traumatizing memory. I once got a 1-star review by a poor woman who was HORRIFIED that there was a scene in my STRAIGHT OUTTA FANGTON book where the protagonist was locked up in a basement with a little girl to be starved until he fed on her.

    I'm like, "Yes, it's meant to be horrifying."

    It obviously traumatized the protagonist despite having no control.

    I don't feel "political horror" works so well in a setting where the Vampires are so deep in "personal horror" that they struggle to feed each night and don't have the time to fuck up the planet or arange schemes that can't be destroyed by their own Beast rebelling against them or their touchstones going out of whack. Political horror also doesn't work so well when the gap between prince and hunter has been shrunk to an extent that the coterie can wipe the city through teamwork and a semi-good plan and we don't have to overmuch worry about the possibility that one old sixth gen can wipe the party before they can even make a proper move. Also I can't dominate the president into executing order 66 because dominate doesn't work the way it used to.
    Totally disagree.

    Political horror is something that I think has been modified primarily for this game based around the decades of lessons of LARP and material. The loss of Elders and focus on jockeying for power has taken front stage so we do have people like Kevin Jackson actually able to move into the big seat. However, it's because the power vacuum that currently exists low-wise is open to them. It's not like there's more frenzy opportunities than Elysium than there were before either and when you frenzy, you might NOT rip out the throat of Annabelle as you would before but instead just be a real asshole.

    I'm not entirely down with that but let's not pretend the Compulsion system doesn't downgrade the dangers of frenzy.

    As for Princes and Hunters, unless you use the overpowered "Wake with Mornings' Freshness, virtually any vampire caught with hunters in the daytime will be only able to flee. Its why minions exist.

    Gaining power (influence resources, domain, retainers etc) is hampered by my own inability, while gaining personal power (disciplines) is a dead-end where once it could be a goal in of itself. I can't be a wizard anymore/wizards have had their powers reduced and their beliefs largely invalidated.
    Frankly, I feel like Rituals as a replacement for Paths make vampires stronger not weaker.

    The Sabbat, and their themes, literally left the game.
    Yes, their absence was a mistake and I'm glad they're finally "coming back."

        I also recall V5 openly says something like " If you consider yourself alt-right go home and rethink your life, this game is not for you"

        I would probably be considered "left" enough to be put on a list, but you simply can't have the kind of attitude to write something like that in something that's A: A commercial product that you hope will be successful and B: a satirical work of literature that you hope will bring people around to your point of view. That "my way or the highway" attitude is really significant enough that it's effects are felt on the rest of the work. Even if you don't mean to hurt "political horror" with "personal horror" the fact that you've taken such a strong stance with who can't play your game makes it feel like it's not such a stretch that you're taking a strong stance there too. Hell, the reduction of strong hierarchies and power disparities (IE Generation isn't that desirable and humans are a credible threat), which are important aspects of fascism, kinda gives rise to the idea that the previously exaggerated gaps in power have been reduced in order to make sure that such hierarchies aren't legitimized and everyone feels equal so that the game doesn't support fascist ideas (which is, to me at least, obviously counterproductive since those hierarchies were exaggerated for the purpose of satirical ridicule, but it's not unknown for supposedly liberal narratives to backfire and become counterproductive, like how the "strong women" in recent Star-Wars movies have been an object of some scorn. If someone's game enough to write "fascists stop reading" on a product you can expect little good)

        In short, if you're dictating who is allowed to play it's not much of a stretch to believe you're also going to dictate how they should play. I'm not saying they are engaging in a 1TW approach, just that it's understandable that some people have suspicions.
        Yes, that certainly made what could be called a "bad first impression" on me. It seemed counter to the idea of mature games for mature players or the idea that real world issues could be addressed with WoD games.

        Of course, looking back, WW had all kinds of crazy politically charged and biased stuff in their games through the decades. I think when I was younger it didn't phase me quite as much as it would (and does) now.

                    I have no idea where you're getting this from. V20 still has automatic success rules, which STs are more than free to apply to Frenzy/Rotschreck checks. So cutting out the low level triggers when it isn't a dramatically appropriate scene is 100% how the rules are written.

                    V20 is very clear that vampires feel these urges commonly, but the vast majority of the time they easily keep the Beast in check. For example, hunger checks only set in if your character has too little vitae in their system at all. It doesn't mean the sight or smell of blood doesn't rouse the Beast to feed, it just means that unless your low, and have relatively low Self-Control by PC standards, vampires can control themselves without even needing to roll.

                    To cite, V20 page 299:

                    "Relatively innocuous stimuli, or stimuli directly under the character’s control, are unlikely to induce Rötschreck. For example, a character who sees a lit cigarette in a nightclub, or a screened-in fireplace in an ally’s home, might grow uneasy, but is unlikely to succumb to the Red Fear. If that same cigarette is pointed threateningly at the vampire, though, or the fireplace suddenly flares up…"

                    Vampires, in V20 rules, do not flip out over things just because they're there. It's established that a certain level of narrative escalation be present.

                    I note that I actually think that the ability to manage the Beast goes up with the fact that the mind is "quieted" when you kill someone in the game. Plus, Compulsions mean that you are LESS likely to go on a killing spree but just have your behavior altered.

                    Let's not pretend that V20's rules don't make it clear that if you spend that last Blood Point, you won't carve up your own coterie and the nearest baby. When you don't, that was because the ST made a decision to not do so because it wouldn't be fun. V5 has solidified that lessened tendency to go on a murder spree into the rules.
                    Um, you seem to be skipping that V5 still has Frenzy rules in it on top of Compulsions, with pretty much the same provocations as V20 as (if adjusted for things like blood points vs. Hungry levels). Compulsions from Bestial Failures might not have a high risk of random murder, but they have a risk of that which adds to the risk of flipping out via Frenzy. That doesn't really paint a picture of more "calm" vampires. A Hunger 5 V5 vampire is extremely at risk of murdering people for blood, and isn't likely to care who.
