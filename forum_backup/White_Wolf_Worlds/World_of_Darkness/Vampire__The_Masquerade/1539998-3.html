    Originally posted by Knightingale View Post

    At least, that's how V5 defines Predator Types.
    No, it expressly is not. V5 corebook, page 175, emphasis mine.



    ...Your hunting preferences feed your Blood, and your Blood shapes what Skills and even Disciplines you develop as a vampire. This modus predationis is how you usually hunt. Your Predator type shows your reflex or habit; it doesn’t mandate your behavior in the game.     Yes, it's supposed to be a preference and habituated activity. Vampires in popular culture and folklore have always been depicted as heavily habitual, to borderline/outright compulsive.             Originally posted by Theodrim View Post

            Except, is it?

            I could break down V5 versus previous edition character generation step-by-step, but in the end V5 has one fewer step overall by way of rolling backgrounds, merits, and flaws together, and replacing freebie points with predator type. Freebie points were by far the most problematic step of previous edition character generation -- freebie point-buys had wildly divergent experience cost discrepancies, were the biggest decision paralysis invoker and stumbling block for newer and less rules-fluent players. The difference between an experienced player's, and a newbie's, starting characters could be massive to the point of disrupting game balance for easily the first hundred experience points' worth of play.

            That's without going into the storyteller headache resultant from potential merit/flaw munchkinism, having to audit at char gen to make sure someone didn't accidentally spend too many or too few freebies, and checking for odd, inappropriate, or hard-to-justify freebie expenditure. That's without assuming negative player intent -- sometimes a player just gets overexcited, loses focus during character generation, suffers from decision paralysis, or slips into old habit. I do it too -- a recent V20 character I made was a Nosferatu ancilla recently out of a long torpor, and I as someone who likes to play characters with the drive skill, had to consciously remind myself this is not a character on whom points in drive would be appropriate.

            Can you justifiably call something unnecessary bloat, when it overall reduces the number of steps to character generation and directly replaces a far more problematic step? Commentary thus far is just restricted to character generation.

            In play, predator type recommends predetermined dice pools for feeding. That's one fewer decision point for a storyteller to make, while establishing a player expectation of how they might be able to approach a circumstance outside character specialty or expertise. It amounts to less time and mental bandwidth to be spent on game mechanics over the course of a session, story, or chronicle, which can then be spent on story and narration -- the things that really matter in a narrative-focused game. And most importantly, it reduces the potential for squabbling over which dice pool might be most appropriate for a given situation by keeping everyone on the same page.

            Is it perfect? no. But "imperfect" is not synonymous with "bad", least of all when the systems replaced are even more problematic.
            Once again your framing it in the context of edition comparisons. All you actually need as presented in v5 to replace it is a single dice pool dependant on how you hunt correct?

                                        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

                                        Yeah, but which pool to use as rouse rolls?

                                        Overall the idea of substituting bookkeeping for random chance is pretty interesting to me.
                                        You roll a number of dice equal to the cost in vitae. So 1BP = one die in your rouse check. Because there's a 50% chance of success (ignoring 1s and 10s), having up to 5 Hunger Dice is the same as having 10 BP. If you have more than 10 BP, it gets a bit trickier but if you increase the blood pool by 2 per Generation instead of 1, it works okay again.

                                        Alternatively, as I suggested when V5 first came out, you can just give Hunger Dice whenever characters fall below 5 blood points, but that makes Hunger much less of an issue. You can tweak it by saying that the first Hunger Die comes as soon as you're at 9 vitae, the second at 7, the third at 5, the fourth at 3, and the fifth at 1. Or even do 9/6/4/2/1 or something.

                                        Here is a thread on Reddit with some more ideas: https://www.reddit.com/r/WhiteWolfRP...dice_into_v20/

                                                            Originally posted by adambeyoncelowe View Post

                                                            It's way too complicated for what it is, though. It needs to be streamlined massively. The BP table shouldn't be much more complicated than the VTR2e one. The problem, I think, is they're trying to make it work with rouse checks instead of blood points—when the latter is far simpler.
                                                            Additionally... the complexity undermines the idea of having stronger rules for VtM vampires having darker appetites as they age.

                                                            In pre-V5 the rules (which did exist) where generally hand-wavy and/or optional, but at least could be applied to make it happen to any vampire that clings on long enough.

                                                            By meshing Generation and Blood Potency, V5 results in high Generation vampires that will never inherently get into really morally dubious areas of thirst (a 12 Gen V5 vampire born in the 1000s never goes above BP 3 unless they lower their Gen below 12), where a Gen 4 V5 vampire is created already in pretty dark feeding habits off the bat (min BP of 5).

                                                            I don't entirely agree that rouse checks are at fault here. They don't help, but the bigger problem is that the game decided to express more supernatural power inelegantly: essentially trying to represent older vampires as having larger stores of vitae in four different methods that don't directly scale together. You can simplify the V5 BP chart immensely by having all of the five middle columns (no reason not to toss Bane Severity in this) by having everything be BP/2 round up and then tack on an exception that Superficial Damage healing can be used to heal 1 even at BP 0. I'm not sure most people would notice the difference, and "BP/2 round up" is a lot easier to memorize than a big chart that's close to but not exactly BP/2 sometimes up sometimes down.

                                                            There are other options that keep the idea of a rouse check and not go back to blood points (I'm not personally sold that random is better for this, but lots of people like it so no reason not to have good rules for it), without ditching the idea of older/low Gen vampires being more powerful. As a starting point, you could look at something like advantage/disadvantage in new d20 systems. Weaker vampires have disadvantaged rouse checks, grow into normal rouse checks, and powerful vampires have advantaged rouse checks.. and you'd maintain the larger conceit in a much simpler fashion; though three steps might not be enough to make it feel right. There's also things like how Storypath will sometimes represent characters that are inherently more powerful as having a reduced target number for their rolls. You could modify rouse checks the same way where instead of everyone operating on 50/50, the odds get better towards passing your check and not getting hungry as you go up in power. Either approach means you can keep the impact of rouse checks the same, but skew the math so that stronger vampires fail checks less frequently and thus can do more without getting hungry.
