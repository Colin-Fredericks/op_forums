                Eh, attributing "wisdom" to Caine is a bit of an iffy proposition. But yes, the most common conception is that the limits of Generation were Caine trying to prevent something like the murders of the Second Generation from happening again. I've tended more towards an indifferent or uninvolved Caine in my versions of events, which is why the Antediluvians being behind the diminishing power of newer vampires made sense to me. The only flaw is that it mostly only makes sense if you view all of the Antediluvians as self-serving assholes, which gets complicated if you want to leave room for genuinely good(ish) ones who wouldn't kneecap their own descendants out of fear. Also, you start having to try and figure out Blood Potency as a connected but largely distinct system; from what little I know about V5, I don't entirely like the way that they do it, but maybe I should take a closer look just to get ideas. And what about the Panders? Does their neat little anti-Clan ritual remove the limits of Generation as well, and there just hasn't been enough time yet for the rest of Cainite society to realize this bombshell?

                Also, the Erciyes Fragments are an incredible source. It's been a while since I read it, but IIRC it's one of the best pieces of lore for getting across what a monumental ASSHOLE Caine is, IMO at least. But the fact that it's explicitly written from an IC perspective makes it less binding or credible as a source, not more. I'm pretty sure that the introduction or side-notes make it clear that there are some issues with the history of the Fragments that make them a bit suspect as evidence. But again, that is the post-modern chaos of WoD lore; whether or not the Erciyes Fragments are a genuine example of Caine speaking for himself, or just another case of old vampires manipulating history for their own benefit, is entirely up to the Storyteller.

                Also, whether or not Caine makes an appearance during Gehenna is a bit back and forth. It's definitely a prominent part of the Noddist eschatology, but 2 out of 3 of the scenarios in the sourcebook barely reference Caine beyond a nod. You can run a Vampire game without having decides on the answers to all the mysteries of Noddism, but you probably need to have the answers ready by the time you're playing out Gehenna, because at that point you really have to decide whether or not the legends are true.

                                        Caine's story is very tragic, pretty much in the most classic sense of the word: the source of his tragedy is his own flaws.

                                        I want to make something very clear: Caine Raped Zillah. Forcing her to "love" him through use of the Blood Bond was a violation of her identity and her body. (A part of me feels like I read somewhere that Zillah's Embrace was also against her will, but I'm not sure about that.)

                                        I don't think that anyone is trying to make the argument that Caine is an idiot or incompetent. Rather, we're pushing back on the idea that any of his decisions are the result of "wisdom." And maybe part of the problem is that "wisdom" is a bit of a fuzzy term.

                                        Using the example of Caine's rulings on the Embrace, Caine could have prevented the existence of the Third Generation simply by using Dominate to forbid the Second Generation from Embracing anyone. If he knew it was trouble, why did he wait for the problem to start getting out of hand before making a (seemingly purely verbal) ruling on the matter? For that matter, looking at the power that Caine seems to have over his descendants, using the Clan Curses as an example if you accept those as Caine's work, then it seems entirely possible that Caine could have made the vitae of his childer unable to pass along the Embrace, given how it's a flaw in the Blood that pops up all on its own from time to time.

                                        The Curse of Generation probably serves as a good example as well. It's hard to argue in hypotheticals, but it seems pretty reasonable to posit that having each vampire's power be capped unless they're willing to commit diablerie against one of their elders seems like something that would incentivize violence and conflict between Cainites. A world where not every vampire can achieve Level 10 Disciplines is probably better for humanity, but that's never seemed to have been Caine's motivation behind cursing the Antediluvians. It's all just spite.

                                        It only makes sense to consider Caine "wise" if you think that the entire terrible mess which is the history and mythology of VtM is all going according to plan from his perspective. If there was anyone with enough power and awareness to solve some of the problems created by the Jyhad, it would be Caine. And maybe he does in your version; maybe the sorry state of the World of Darkness is in spite of Caine's best efforts to try and fix it, if you want to give it an interesting spin. But the version of Caine we get in the books is more like an absent and abusive father.
