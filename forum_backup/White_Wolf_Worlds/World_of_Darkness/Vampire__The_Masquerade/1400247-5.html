                Without trying to get into the whole psychology of why humans like horror as a genre in the first place:

                It's important to remember what personal horror is as a subgenre: horror that stems from the characters' internal conflicts in response to the world around them, rather than from external factors.

                One of the key things about playing the supernatural monsters of horror fiction is if they are to be monsters, and also be sympathetic to us as the players of the game, then personal horror is essentially inevitable. A monster that never faces internal turmoil over their actions just turns into playing evil characters for the lulz. A "monster-in-name-only" means you avoid those internal struggles, but then you're also not playing much of a monster.

                Making the PCs supernatural monsters allows the game to compel the characters into moral, ethical, etc, dilemmas, then face the consequences to their own senses of self. Even things like the powers, politics, and so on are geared towards that end. The WoD posits supernatural societies where being a loner is too dangerous externally to really get by on, at the cost of toxic social systems that provoke moments of personal horror. A vampire needs to get entangled in vampire politics to survive, but vampire politics are built by the game to cost your a bit of your soul (or a lot of it) in the exchange. Supernatural powers in the WoD are, generally, very cheap in practice for how much damage (not just health damage) they can do; esp to normal humans. They are a constant temptation to approach the situation as a monster and risk your character's integrity. A vampire with the strength to rip a man in half is a character that has the gnawing in the back of their head that it's hard to consider themselves human in a meaningful way when they're that separate from humanity.

                Even games that sell themselves as urban fantasy instead of horror are generally going to have a good deal of personal horror that crops up in the game. It's pretty hard to play something like the Dresden Files and not have your characters do questionable things that make them question their moral compass. The WoD just chooses to lean into the personal horror harder.
