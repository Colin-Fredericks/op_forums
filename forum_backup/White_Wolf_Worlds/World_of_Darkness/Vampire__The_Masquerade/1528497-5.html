                        It has no relevance to the artistic merit and whenever it comes up in these discussion, it doesn't actually change anything.

                        What? No. Paradox licenses out the WW IPs. Other people pay Paradox to make these things. Renegade pays Paradox to make WoD books. Onyx Path pays Paradox to make Exalted books. That revenue stream is part of what makes shares in the company valuable, and thus is important to shareholders. Paradox has a fiduciary duty to tell it's investors (aka shareholders) what it's doing with the stuff it paid a significant chunk of change for.

                        So a better question is what does Renegade's shareholder reports say.
                        Renegade Games Studio isn't a publicly traded company. There are no shares to buy, thus there are no shareholders, or shareholders reports. This is why we know so little about the TTRPG industry: most of the companies are private and thus have no responsibility to publish certain information.                                                         Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                                        I mean, it's absolutely irrelevant to quality and I don't see what the point in speculating on it is either.
                                                        Sadly, things like this need some sort of quantitative analysis and sales figures are about the best (really, only) available. And given game companies are generally profit-driven, sales dictate whether products receive post-release support or supplements. Case in point, how V20 was originally going to be a one-and-done commemorative release with as much content packed into it as possible, and (as far as I know or have read) it wasn't anticipated to single-handedly revive the intellectual property.

                                                        In the bigger picture, frankly Paradox and RGS would do well to take a lesson from WotC or Black Library's handling of Forgotten Realms' and Warhammer's metaplot: provide a stable world-state for playable content, publish city or sect content intended for play within the confines of that world-state, and leave metaplot advancement to separately-published fluff. Advancing metaplot by way of pre-published module is just dumb, and has never really worked in practice the way designers seem to intend.

                                                        Case in point, Fall of London. I think it might have been more interesting and less divisive had it been a dual release: one being a canon novel that details the metaplot events, the other being a city book that represents the city before and after.
