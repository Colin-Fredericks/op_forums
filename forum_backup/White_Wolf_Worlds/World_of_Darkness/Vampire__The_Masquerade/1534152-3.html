        I swear that "1/100,000" thing is the single most harmful bit of fluff -- yes, fluff -- White Wolf ever put to paper.

        It doesn't account for variations in population density. It doesn't account for local and regional economic development, or local culture. It doesn't account for surveillance, law enforcement, samaritans, or hunters of any stripe. It doesn't account for other fatsplats, if and when present.

        But most importantly, it doesn't account for clans in the area. It doesn't account for their organizational structure or ideology. It doesn't account for feeding discipline, influence, or ability to maintain the Masquerade. It doesn't account for age or generation. It doesn't account for predation pressure between vampires.

        The books go out of their way to point this out at nearly every opportunity, but it gets ignored in favor of the number as if cWoD is a crunch-heavily tactical/strategic RPG. It'd be better if whichever publisher owns the right to the IP this week disavows the number and puts "IGNORE THIS" in giant bold text up front during the worldbuilding chapter (like a lot of these people read that one). People need to stop fixating on this ridiculous, arbitrary, number and instead focus on building organic cities with vampire populations it can support without breaking suspension of disbelief. Y'know, the point of this thread in the beginning.

        Hell, Nashville came up. Nashville's greater metro area has a population not of 700,000 but 2,000,000. That's the number you look at -- the greater metro area population -- not just the urban core or suburbs within city limits. Uniquely American urban sprawl means a constant flux of commuters, whether that's work or weekend fun.

        "By the numbers", Nashville "can" support 20 vampires. Will the city in the heart of the Bible Belt with a heavy law enforcement surveillance framework, multiple federal law enforcement agencies' field offices, within close proximity to multiple military bases, which is also one of the country's centers for health care and health care technology research and development, support a population of 20 vampires? Not bloody likely, unless each and every one of those vampires is at least an ancilla with strong feeding discipline and considerable experience covering their tracks.

        In this chronicle I'm currently running, I did a quick write-up for Nashville since vampires from it may factor in. You know what I put there? A Tremere chantry, two or three Toreador, and a Ventrue. That's ten or so Camarilla vampires, all of whom from clans noteworthy for both feeding discipline and ability to maintain the Masquerade. They're the ones able to exist under the Inquisition's nose after the city was swept and cleared.

        And they currently consider Nashville overpopulated. To the point they don't honor the Tradition of Hospitality, unless visitors are established Camarilla vampires on official sect business with writ from another city's Prince, or an Archon or higher.

            Originally posted by Theodrim View Post
            I swear that "1/100,000" thing is the single most harmful bit of fluff -- yes, fluff -- White Wolf ever put to paper.
            If you think about the ramifications of about half the fluff in VtM, it breaks the setting. The 1:100k thing is just the sprinkles on top of much worse things.

            There isn't any kind of unified vampire society aspect, like what the Blade setting has with a language, writting system, and organized feeding areas. The VtM clans generally aren't cohesive enough to rule like the great houses do. Further more the fluff keeps pushing diverse bloodline cities, when no clan would allow such a thing in their city.

            The fluff says that there are the Traditions, but in truth there is no real way to enforce them or penalty if they are ignored. The same thing happens with Boons and the half assed concept that the harpies somehow enforce boons by gossiping.

            Vampires are supposed to be rare, but there is no mechanical reason for it. It's not like you have to get to a certain age, essence level, or blood potency level before you can sire a new vampire. So there is really no reason why the VtM setting doesn't turn into Stakeland or the Vampire Hunter D setting. Mages are limited by the rarity of awakening and shifters are restricted by the fact that they have to wait a normal generation for a fresh crop of kinfolk to be born and grow up before they have a chance at becoming a full shifter. Vampires have nothing keeping them in check other than theoretical attrition.

            Vampires are supposed to be automatically afraid of fire, but they are nocturnal creatures which can't see in the dark without very specific disciplines which most don't have. Before the invention of the electric lightbulb, how did they function?

            Vampires are supposed to be the masters of the city and human civilization, kept hemmed in by shifter death squads roaming the edges of the wilderness. Yet several shifters make the city their home and there is very little to no mechanical support for why a vampire would stay in a city as opposed to well stocked doomsday bunker or castle.

            The fluff assumes that vampires default to hunting random mortals, rather than having a form of Herd despite the latter option vastly cutting down on the potential for being ambushed, caught by hunters, or just stuff going wrong. The Anita Blake book series had all sorts of vampire society rules, titles, and other supporting concepts that surrounded the mortal followers of a vampire. VtM just kind of stares blankly at the idea from a vampire society standpoint.

            The fluff acts like the VtM setting is the undead form of authoritarianism that everyone wants to rage against with elders and high councils etc., but it is simultaneously the Lost Boys where the vampires are beholden to no one and roam the streets as they see fit.

            The problem is that if you are focused on good world building, you need consistency and logic. If you are making a game, you need variety and flexibility so that the gamers can have whatever they want. To use a food analogy, good world building is a five star restaurant that specializes in a specific cuisine, a game on the other hand is shopping mall food court which offers something for everyone, even if none of it is particularly good on its own.

                1:100.000 vampires per human can make sense in a "global scale" as some cities could not have vampire in it due to the presence of dangers like hunters, werewolves, mages or being dominated by a tighly united pack of vampires who do not want to share.

                However the truth is that in local or city level settings this makes the game unplayable because there is simple no vampire society to interact. It is because this simple truth that supplements tend to ignore ratio saying X city is an exception like Chicago which is overpopulated, but if you take into account that this happens mostly with every supplement then you start to realize that exception has become the rule.

                Why? Because as much as we love TTRPG and specially the vampire genre the Truth is that the masquerade is very poorly designed from a mechanic perspective.
                • The embrace has no cost of yearly limit which could lead to vampire becoming a Zombie problem especially with the sabbat over there.
                • Everyone talks about vampire population but ghoul and thrall population is also another problem due to no limit to Vinculum or apropiate cost.
                • Vampires cannot feed from humans without keeping the masquerade because their kiss is "Memorable" instead of mind wiping and only 3/13 have Dominate.
                • Vampires cannot "see in the dark" without a protean 1 or serpentis 2 power which makes 11/13 clans blind for most of history as they are scared from fire.

                Personally my advice is to use the books as a suggestion and tweak the setting to something more consistent because masquerade vampire never made sense as the game mechanics where always contradictory, the thing is that the everything else from the political aspect of the game to the katanas and trenchcoats had enough cool factor that we just choose to ignore it in pursue of our happines.

                I am a lucky GM who has a reliable group of childhood friends who are interested in board games so I can use my own rules, but for someone else who has to seek groups online a possible solution is to port some rules from Requiem 2ED using masquerade as a base, it is not perfect but it fixes many stuff like all vampires having night vision or the embrace having a cost (I use XP, but Requiem cost is 1 point of humanity, i do not like it because it feels a very high price but at least is a solution).

                    Everyone talks about vampire population but ghoul and thrall population is also another problem due to no limit to Vinculum or apropiate cost.
                    Back in the day, I ran a game where the Players were split into two teams, one team was a Nosferatu Warren who controlled a town, while the other team was some Tzimisce who were trying to take the place over. The Nosferatu focused on their Spawning Pools to mass produce armies of mutant animal ghouls, while the Tzimisce focused on flesh crafting servitor ghouls. It was an experimental game to see if one could pull off an RTS/TBS style game using the WoD setting. What we found was that there isn't enough blood or people to make it game breaking at scale.

                    A ghoul servitor is great as an administrator for daytime operations or day light security. But once direct combat starts, a ghoul burns through vitae at the same rate as vampires, especially when they get injured. Thus if you want to keep your ghouls alive, you can burn through huge amounts of vitae after every fight, let alone a prolonged battle or siege.

                    If memory serves it worked out that even a Background Herd rating of five (60 vessels) was barely enough to keep a 20 ghoul squad functioning, with very little left over for the vampires to consume. The reason being is you have to put the vessels in rotation so they can be fed on every couple of nights. The vampire master pretty much just turns into a Blood to Vitae conversion machine.

                    The flip side of the healing doctrine for ghouls is a pure cannon fodder mentality, which works well for animal ghoul hordes, but when it comes to human ghouls, you can depopulate an area to the point of a masquerade breach in surprisingly little time. This is also assuming you don't require a specific skill set from your ghoul and are just using them like vitae boosted pokemon.

                    Don't get me wrong, I have made some really scary combat groups that leaned heavily on ghouls, but after the 10th ghoul or so you are spending more and more of your time seeing to their vitae levels for diminishing returns. If you aren't really rigged to support a large ghoul force, then the fall off number is closer to 5 ghouls rather than 10.

At some point, data points must by necessity be arbitrary just for the sake of applying those models.

                                                        I grant with what we can presume from game mechanics someone could actually do that modeling, and it would surely be a fun thought experiment. It's explained in the fluff, by high muckety-mucks simply not giving a shit until the little vamps start fucking with their schemes.                                                         In the end, is it really worth it? I'd honestly say no.

Immortal tape worms of society which despite the minority of them killing for fun or when feeding, actually fall into one of the more symbiotic sub-categories of parasitism. You can take your pick of aspects which are deemed beneficial, the fact that any vampire can heal mortals with vitae, the fact that Dominate is a therapists dream ability, the fact that vampires are living time capsules of knowledge and experience that society often forgets and loses.

                                                            Indoors, sure, different story. But here game mechanics save us: Rotshreck really only rears its head when the fire poses risk (or is a surprise), and most vampires are going to have enough dice pool (even in older editions) to just auto-succeed the miniscule difficulties associated with candles and other similarly-sized fires. They won't like it, sure, but the idea of any and every vampire Rotshrecking by the mere presence of a candle is just an absurd prospect, not even supported by game mechanics as-is.
                                                            V20 page 299
                                                            Provocation Difficulty
                                                            Lighting a cigarette 3
                                                            Sight of a torch 5
                                                            Bonfire 6
                                                            Obscured sunlight 7
                                                            Being burned 7
                                                            Direct sunlight 8
                                                            Trapped in burning building 9​
                                                            The first four examples present no direct danger, and specifically the "sight of a torch" is particularly damning since there is no range requirement/limitation. This makes for the action of walking down a hallway lit by torches mechanically speaking problematic. This is the same concept of rolling dex every time you tie your shoelaces, it is silly and time consuming, but eventually the RNG will strike and now you have to explain why a vampire who has run their own blacksmith shop has absolutely lost it due to someone lighting a candle in their vicinity. If the intention was to not bog down play with such things, then the rules would be more along the lines of "when vampire is set on fire, roll to avoid Rotschreck based on how big the fire is"
