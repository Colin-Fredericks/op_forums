                    Pretty much what the title says, I've watched a couple of the playthroughs and have made a summary of the events as well as list for what the differences are. You can judge what is important and what is not from what I've seen.


                    * Phyre wakes up in torpor in Seattle but wasnt in America when they were transported. They kill the person who awakens them and another security watchmen. The tutorial consists of also evading the cops after your initial murder.

                    * They discover they have Fabian the Malkavian Noir Detective in their head.

                    * Phyre is a pseudonym they pick from a Seattle grunge band poster and they are the Nomad.

                    * You've been in torpor since the 1920. Fabien was embraced around then and has never left Seattle. This will become important because Fabien and you will be doing different parts of the game. Phyre will be doing the politics and beating while Fabien will be doing the flashbacks and investigation.

                    * There's actually no mystery for Fabian or it's solved quickly, Fabian was the one who awoke Phyre and they diablerized him. You find Fabien's corpse soon after, which is lore breaking because it should have disintegrated at Fabien's age.

                    * Phyre is aware that Fabian still existing in their consciousness is weird and describes how diablerie normally works. There's a symbol on Phyre's hand that doesn't seem to be overtly magical at this point.

                    * Phre has an encounter with the Sheriff, Benny Muldoon, who is a psychopath who is on a crusade to kill every Thin Blood in Seattle. He hates manners and is a Brujah I assume. Phyre can stand up to him or try to be polite.

                    * Phyre goes to present themselves to the Prince but Fabian warns against that. Instead, they decide to visit the ex-Prince, Lou Graham, instead. Lou abdicated being prince, which Pyre and Fabian both find unusual. However, the new prince is her childe so it shouldn't be too surprising to seasoned VTM players.

                    * There's a massive snowstorm that has isolated Seattle for the events of the game.

                    * Phyre heads to Lou Graham's hotel and has a flashback to the 1920s when she was Prince and Fabrian was newly Embraced. This is a hint that, in fact, you are going to be playing Fabien as much as Phyre.

                    * Phyre meets Lou who has just slaughtered a bunch of Anarchs who attacked her haven.

                    * Lou will comment if you're not changed to nicer clothes after waking up from 100 years of torpor.

                    * The Prince of Seattle, JJ Campbell, has been murdered by the "Anarchs." Lou isn't too worried because she's installed her other childe as Prince. You piss off Lou if you question her in anything but a flattering way. She's described as a narcissistic psychopath--so a typical Elder.

                    * The new prince is Ryong Choi.

                    * The game is reactive to your Clan and if you're polite and differential to Lou but a Brujah, Fabien will comment on it.

                    * You head to the Prince's skyscraper and find out it's under assault by the Anarchs too. Phyre proceeds to lay the smack down on them. So I don't think you'll be very friendly with the Anarchs. Fabien comments on how the Anarchs aren't normally dangerous and them agreeing each other other, let alone coordinating to kill the Prince is insane.

                    * Along the way you meet Tolly killing a bunch of Anarchs and their ghouls. The Nosferatu talks like a flamboyant stereotype, which is an odd juxtaposition. He's not the Sheriff but one of the Deputies like Fabien was.

                    * You meet Safia the Tremere in a computer server room as you climb your way up an elevator shaft. She's a super Nomad fanboy and you have the option to flirt with her or scare the shit out of her.

                    * You meet Ryong interrogating an Anarch Thin Blood who knows nothing that Ryong kills. Ryong is a weak prince and needs to punish you for the tutorial but doesn't have a sheriff. You aren't made sheriff immediately but it's pretty obvious she's alreadying thining its a compromise. Ryong is just Lou Graham's puppet anyway and mentioning her makes it clear Ryong feels manipulated.

                    * You head back to Fabien's apartment and finds its a hellhole that is covered in police tape. You go to sleep...and find yourself in a vision of a sunny garden where someone is talking to you.

                    * You flashback to Fabien's past in 2024 as a police detective vampire working to keep the Masquerade.

                    * You learn how to wipe minds in-game. You use it to wipe the memory of a police detective you owe $30, which shows Fabien is a dick.

                    * Fabien then investigates a masquerade breach with a pretty young ghoul who may be using Elif's model. Sadly, she's killed by the Second Inquisition before the end of the flashback. The mystery is determining who sent her to the club since it was to pass on a message to you. You can speak with her ghost. She's a dumb valley girl but I feel like it's a waste because I wanted to keep her as my own ghoul.

                                            Regarding the name "Bloodlines 2", I thought this interview on Gameinformer with Creative Director Alex Skidrow was interesting:

                                            When I ask creative director Alex Skidmore why the game is called Bloodlines 2 instead of something else, he thinks for a while, then starts his answer by saying, "It's what we were asked to do." It's an honest response. Notoriously, Bloodlines 2 was originally being made by Hardsuit Labs and got far enough in development to be shown at E3 2019, but the next two years were filled with delays, departures, and ultimately, the decision to remove Hardsuit Labs from the project entirely. The Chinese Room took its place, and since it was working on a project that had already been announced, it didn't have much of a choice – this game would always be called Bloodlines 2.
                                            So, according to this, the series of events appears to be: The Chinese Room wanted to make a VtM-game. They got approval from Paradox. They started to work on it. Then Paradox approached them and told them the game they're working on would now be called "Bloodlines 2". I wonder how much the trajectory of development was impacted by Paradox deciding that the Chinese Room project would be called "Bloodlines 2"...

                                            Also, it will be interesting to see how far they're willing to go in addressing the fan-feedback regarding the paywalled essential content.

                                                            Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                                            may disagree with ratings like 6 out of 10, but that's still a game worth playing, and worth buying at some price.
                                                            One of the things I hate the most about videogame-critique/journalism is how outlets like IGN or Gamespot popularized the idea that anything less than an 8 out of 10 is an expression of "I hate this game". And you see that in the industry as well. Because there are companies out there that tie paying a bonus to developers based on Metacritic scores. The most infamous example is, of course, Fallout: New Vegas. There, the bonus was tied to a Metacritic score of 85 - but the game got an 84.

                                                            I always thought this feels a bit narrow-minded. At that point, why even bother with a 10-point score? And then I can understand the people who feel that not using scores at all is the better option. Especially when they're used like this.

                                                            To me, 5 out of 10 means average. There's good stuff, there's bad stuff - but overall it's just a "take it or leave it" shrug. 6 to 7 is "I like it - but there are big problems here that we need to talk about". 8 means "I love this game". And 9 to 10 is "Not only do I love this but this is "Best of the Year"-material" with a 10 maybe being even more than that. Anything below 5 is where you get to "I hate this". 4 means "The bad clearly outweighs the good here". And with 3 or less, it's games where something's fundamentally wrong, stuff like sections that are literally unplayable.

The thing that still baffles me is the Paradox-con last year when you got these comments like "Bloodlines is a dead end" and "If we're gonna make a Bloodlines 3, we gotta work with somebody else.". Who knows how much acrimony there is between Paradox and the Chinese Room at this point? And I'm just thinking back to their relationship with Obsidian and how that turned sour. Paradox really has a gift in pushing developers away after they've worked with them. And it's all simply because these developers didn't fulfill their unrealistic expectations. Not unrealistic in the sense that it was technically impossible but unrealistic in the sense that Paradox isn't capable of steering/managing projects that capably.
