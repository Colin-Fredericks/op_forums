    Originally posted by marin View Post

    The Soul Eater thing was replaced with Asakku. It is explicitly called out that Asakku's reality is optional, and that it may be self-delusion on the part of its Kindred investigators that perhaps others have taken advantage of.

    So what is Asakku? A small group in the Black Hand has noticed that when some vampires study Vicissitude, something makes contact with them, offering them power in return for the ability to manifest itself. If they accept, they lose their identities to it, becoming its vessels.

    The two most prominent theories the group has are that either it's a form of madness, the vampire coming to identify with the Discipline to the extent their original consciousness is obliterated, or... it's a manifestation of the Watchers, the Second Generation, seeking to return to the physical world. Misapprehension about the first time the group discovered Asakku resulted in the Soul Eater idea popping up - that is, the belief Vicissitude was contracted from a spirit, etc. - but this is explicitly not the case, as it's established the Tzimisce Ante had Vicissitude from the earliest times.

    (If you know your WoD cosmology, reading about the first case of Asakku might get you wondering about connections to the Neverborn Malfeans instead...)

                                    I see your point and why the sandbox starting approach of V20 can be problematic for players who consider themselves "less creative". Which I find dubious, because if you go the RPG way, you have to have some creativity, otherwise you would not be playing rpgs.
                                    Sandbox and Depth of background stuff are mostly exclusive, since to give the Sandbox players their room, you cannot go too deep into the matters. This is good for players like me and my group, since we can finetune what we have without problems and build on this with our own stuff and playstyle. I see why that can be chaffing for players who like an extremly deep background world to start with, especially if they have not much time to prepare games.Here in Germany, the rpg "DSA" is one with an extremly developed background, but while that is a great thing if you had few time to prepare a gaming evening, it can become a burden too. I only came back to DSA with the new edition, since they lightened some things.

                                    But back to VtM: I recognise the appeal Revised has for players like you. It went deep into the various areas of play (and I have to say that some stuff dealing with the periphery like Havens of the Damned was good) and it could have been a great version of VtM if not for the fact that Revised screwed over a lot of the canon of 1st/2ed. The writers back then tried to press all players to play in the style "personal horror" to the exclusion of all else. "Blood treachery" is one example where they went overboard ruleswise to make sure that only their own interpretation was playable.
                                    But the crowing of this was how the writers reintrepreted the various Clans and Bloodlines. Several became unplayable and more the changed fluff made the survival of those clans and Bloodlines illogical, since so much infighting and strife would have meant that many Clans would have fallen and never reached the Now.
