      Despite my dislike of the Kuei-Jin/Wan Kuei, I actually haven't ever removed them from my games because some of my players like them greatly.

      Instead, I use these tools:

      * Wan Kuei are actually welcome at Elysiums of the Camarilla if they announce themselves and agree to abide by the Six Traditions. They tend to look over ethnic neighborhoods as their domain but nothing keeps them from participating in vampire society as normal. A formal truce was established in 2004 despite attempts by MacNeil to disrupt it.

      * The Anarchs have many allies among younger Kuei Jin and don't really begrudge ones who join the Movement or form their own gangs. The fight over Los Angeles and the New Promise Mandarite ended a long time ago.

      * The Wan Kuei are the Eastern version of Risen or the Risen are the Western version of Wan Kuei. The Mummies are another variation of them with Osiris substituting for their gods in bringing them back to life. I also have Cappadocias' ghost doing the same thing under the guise of the Grim Reaper.

      * There's actually a fairly massive number of Cainites living in China and other Eastern territories. While there's been many conflicts with the Wan Kuei, they tend to stay out of each other's way. About 40K or so total, not counting India where the Ravnos used to hole sway.

      * There's a massive social conflict among the Wan Kuei because more "neonate" members of their race exist than have ever existed in the past due to population growth.

      Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

              The important thing is they're related as to create a Grand Unifying Cosmology of the WOD. They're about as related as Nunnehi and Hsein to European Changelings.

              Another bit of headcanon I've only used once in my game that I think people will like.

              The Origin of Caine

              Some of the missing pages of the Book of Nod deal with the fact Caine lived to a ripe old age and died a mortal death after his curse by God. He then passed into the Underworld and made a pact with one of the spirits there that helped him return to life. He was supposed to fight the demons that had escaped from Hell with Lucifer's help but chose instead to make a dark ritual that allowed him to keep others he loved from dying with the help of Lilith. Caine is thus one of the first Risen but refused to abide by his chosen task and instead focused selfishly on never going back to the Underworld again. Caine is thus why there's no Western Wan Kuei.

                  For me personally, I also tend to use Wan Kuei/Kuei-Jin (I'm going to lean towards the former term) as a distinct but present aspect of the World of Darkness.

                  •Kindred (in the classical VtM sense) have existed in Asia as long as they have anywhere else. Historically they existed similarly to their counterparts elsewhere as loose territorial collectives. I tend to have them steadily developing regional structures to fit their surroundings and human cultures, like the kindred of europe did during the War of Princes. These various domains are considered, as a whole, to be the Black Courts.

                  •The Black Courts benefited from not having to deal with the Inquisition prompting the creation of sects. The sects appear though alongside European imperial expansion and their arrival has caused something of a loose confederacy to form causing the Black Courts to transform from an umbrella term to describe politics in the area to a loose network of alliances amongst asian vampiric organizations.

                  •The Wan Kuei have co-existed alongside the kindred of the Black Courts in the same way that the changing breeds, will workers, ghosts, and little gods have. They have their own unique court structure and political politics which sometimes makes contact with the Black Courts.

                  •Kindred of the Black Courts are more likely to ascribe their origins to more appropriate regional folklore, mythology and mysticism while the eldest and factions of occultists practice a form of Noddism. This however is something very similar to how I handle kindred in general with Noddism being rather occult.

                  I sometimes go back and forth between the idea of having beings such as the Wan-Kuei appear globally as well as in Asia. While these beings would likely distinguish themselves via developing their own regional beliefs, they would still be the reincarnated vessels of semi-divine shepherds of creation who fell to corruption, with traditional Asian Wan Kuei forming a role akin to Middle Eastern Cainite-Noddist kindred.

                  Alongside this I am currently considering something inspired by what CTPhepps has been saying to have almost an undead trinity form globally representing Kindred (Vampires), Wan Kuei (Reincarnations), and Revenants or some such term to describe dead bodies reinvigorated by the souls of the dead, which may or may not be distinct from the Risen or Arisen.

                      I always held that the metaphysics and origins of the Wan Kuei are the same, but its a matter of geography, not ethnicity. Some of the Elders left and a few along with a bunch of the young ones remained. Now both Kindred and Wan Kuei are attempting to rebuild.

                              My head-canon for them comes from the description of them under “Other Clans” in the 1e rulebook.

                              They’re another branch of Caine’s family tree descended from an Antediluvian everyone believed perished in the Deluge, but actually just took the opportunity to run off and form their own society far from their kin.

                              It’s children turned early to paths of enlightenment that became the Dharmas and mastered the arts of Chi Manipulation (Thaumaturgy), Spiritual Sight (Auspex) and Shintai (Vicissitude).

                              These paths were also what Saulot learned of his travels east that became the foundations of Golconda, but whereas Saulot’s path was all or nothing; the Dharma paths unlocked that state more gradually (hence they can slowly learn disciplines above 5 dots as their mastery of a Dharma path grows).

                              The downside is their ancestor passed on their subconscious guilt and self-loathing to all their kin. This results in their having to make Frenzy checks whenever they fail a Conscience roll. It also results in their being unable to Embrace except while in a fit of Frenzy. The notion that Wan Kuei arise naturally from sinners is a lie they tell themselves to deal with having afflicted mortals with their curse just about every time they lose control of themselves.

                                      It sort of depends.
                                      I haven't really done much with KotE in the past twenty years. If I ever do, I'm very likely to pick a specific country - most likely Japan - and focus exclusively on it, ignoring a lot of the existing books' Sino-centric focus to do stuff that draws heavily from J-Horror and various urban-fantasy style anime, mainly dealing with those from the past 50 years or so, and elders and their plots only coming into the picture much later. Alternately, I might consider some setting like Hawaii.

                                      But for most of the past twenty years, I've adapted most of the standard 13 clans to East Asia. I tend to do them on a country by country basis, rather than broad "All Asian Toreador are X."
                                      In Japan, for instance, I have the Brujah and Toreador as the two dominant "noble" clans, effectively acting as widespread and interconnected clans-in-the-human-sense networks of political, social, financial, and criminal interests. There's plenty of infighting within both of these two groups - poetically referred to as the "House of the Burning Lotus" and the "House of the Crimson Chrysanthemum" - but they tend to close ranks against outsiders out of obligation if not actual loyalty.
                                      The other major clans in Japan include at least two Ravnos Jati (one of which are traditionally viewed as ninja, more or less), Gangrel who are traditionally viewed as akin to yamabushi, Nosferatu oni, Malkavians, Setites, and surviving Salubri and Cappadocians.
                                      My Japanese Kindred refer to themselves as Jikininki/Shokujinki, or "human eating ghosts" (which aren't exactly vampires, but the modern post-Stoker idea of vampires don't exactly conform to ancient Eastern European and Baltic legends either). Elders will often disparagingly refer to younger vampires as Gaki, much in the modern sense of it being a euphemism for spoiled brat. Neonates will usually refer to elders - behind their backs at least - as Odokuro, after the giant blood drinking skeletons from assorted folk stories.

                                          I have two ways of dealing with them: the easy way, and the hard way.

                                          I use the easy way when they show up incidentally in a game among western vampires. The hard way I wrote up on a boring evening; it will likely never get used.

                                          Easy way:

                                          In Asia, all the "Cainite" clans have a degree of representation. Some are lines going back centuries, like the Lasombra who followed the Silk Road, and the Gangrel who looked at the Great Wall and said "Shape of a bat!" Some are the remnants of 18th and 19th century colonialism, like Ventrue who see Singapore as a place they can get in on the ground floor of a new civilization, or the Brujah who followed communism to China, Viet Nam, and Korea. Most of these have embraced mortals from Asia, and are --to varying degrees-- becoming "Asian bloodlines".

                                          There are a number of Cainite lines which seem to have originated in Asia. The Danava, the Anda, the Nagaraja, and the Gaki all have anatomy and physiology clearly tying them to "Cainites", but tell very different stories of their origins. Some claim vague connections to Caine; some claim an ancestor who gained immortality on their own. Who is right? Who can say?

                                          Then there is the great big elephant in the room. They constitute over 90% of the vampires in East and Southeast Asia.

                                          The Jiang Shi are not so much a clan as a sprawling alliance of hundreds of minor bloodlines, all with a common weakness. Politically they are organized in Courts, each about the size of a single province or small nation. There are two main classes: the Gentry and the Commoners. The Courts used to be led by an Emperor and local Zong Du. In most places, they are now lead by Yihui, local councils.

                                          Jiang Shi in V20
                                          V20 Disciplines:                                           Whichever three disciplines a fledgling Jiang Shi learns first are thereafter their in-clan disciplines. Commonly, they learn the same as their sire. All of the common disciplines of Cainites are available and widely known to Jiang Shi.

                                          Instead of Thaumaturgy, they have a similar discipline called Wuxian. It has many similar paths and rituals. Learning this as an in-clan discipline... that is to say early in one's unlife... is the main dividing line between Common and Gentry social classes. The Gentry value it as a sign of their superiority.

                                          V20 Clan Weakness: Viewing themselves as patrons of their communities, they can only feed from, ghoul, and embrace their own mortal descendants. If they feed from someone who is not their mortal descendant, they vomit up all the blood in their blood pool. If they attempt to ghoul a non-family member, the attempt fails. If they attempt to embrace a non-family member, the fledgling is twisted into a horrific monster with a Humanity of 0 and the Repulsive flaw. The poor creature looks like a rotting corpse, covered in tattered skin.

                                          This is quite debilitating for fledglings, and those who have no mortal children are incapable of surviving on anything other than the blood of other vampires. It is said some of the most ancient Jiang Shi are ancestors, in one way or another, of virtually all humans alive tonight.
                                          Jiang Shi in V5
                                          V5 Disciplines:                                           Whichever three disciplines a fledgling Jiang Shi learns first are thereafter their in-clan disciplines. Commonly, they learn the same as their sire. All of the common disciplines of Cainites are available and widely known to Jiang Shi.

                                          Instead of Thin Blood Sorcery, they have a similar discipline called Wuxian. It has many similar paths and rituals. Learning this as an in-clan discipline... that is to say early in one's unlife... is the main dividing line between Common and Gentry social classes. The Gentry value it as a sign of their superiority.

                                          V5 Clan Weakness: Viewing themselves as patrons of their communities, they can only feed from, ghoul, and embrace their own mortal descendants. If they feed from someone who is not their mortal descendant, they take a penalty on all dice pools equal to their bane severity until they reach Hunger 5. If they attempt to ghoul a non-family member, the attempt fails. If they attempt to embrace a non-family member, the fledgling is twisted into a horrific monster with a Humanity of 0 and the Repulsive flaw. The poor creature looks like a rotting corpse, covered in tattered skin.

                                          This is quite debilitating for fledglings, and those who have no mortal children are incapable of surviving on anything other than the blood of other vampires. It is said some of the most ancient Jiang Shi are ancestors, in one way or another, of virtually all humans alive tonight.

                                                  I use KoTE in my San Francisco chronicle where there is a detente/cold war style conflict between them and the Camarilla. As they are only NPCs, I simplified a couple of things about their Chi - basically eliminating Temporary Chi imbalances and making Yin and Yang more like virtues rather than separate Chi pools. And I conveniently ignore some of the more unbalancing Discipline powers (I'm looking at you Cry of Blood).

                                                  Further, I was inspired by a fluff line in the KoTE Companion about Kindred blood drinking being seen as fouling the Chi to the Kuei-jin, and built on it to help explain the geographic division. As I do it, in numbers the Western Kindred's feeding spiritually pollutes entire populations. In the concentrations found in the Dark Ages and ancient times this basically makes it next to impossible for Keui-jin to process any anything other than Demon chi and prevents their rising in the spiritually polluted human populations. I use this to partially explain the geographic boundaries of both groups: as vampires spread out of the Middle East into Africa, Europe and India they naturally wiped out the Keui-jin there. In Eastern Asia, the Keui-jin were organized enough in early population centers to blunt the spread of western vampires into their territory. I don't think it's a full or perfect explanation, but maybe better than nothing.

                                                  In the modern era the Keui-jin are finding that the Camarilla levels of concentration are more manageable to exist in, and they can manage the impact through maintaining herds and western-Kindred free or highly limited domains. Western kindred always existed in Keui-jin territories, just in very small numbers that didn't trigger the wider spiritual pollution effects.

                                                  I also develop the idea of Vampires as spiritual polluters in to Werewolf by making their feeding a 'natural' Formori possession vector, and that an extended vampire being present in a Caern will start to foul it. I like the idea of their being a game mechanic reason behind the conflicts of the groups and that it would be completely undetectable to the Vampires so they are oblivious and dismissive.
