            I think there's a data point worth considering here: LARPs.

            While it's hard to speak to any specifics (I can't find any records of this left over from the mid-90s), the large fan VtM LARP organizations all drifted towards Cam vs. Sabbat before the books did. The problem was that Cam vs. Anarchs didn't work for the LARP format. This was a big place where the "Camarilla-lite" attitude snuck in. Anarchs as-written weren't good for LARPs (and personalization wasn't the problem). LARPs work better with the Cam as the PCs (organization multiple local events under one larger organization is much much much easier to do with the Cam than the Anarchs), and the Sabbat work better as the antagonists to Cam LARPs (as the Sabbat has a lot more too offer than just a bunch of angry young vampires with a smart leader).

            The TTRPG books followed the trend far more than the other way around. LARPs were more Cam focused. The big event LARPs at conventions and so on followed suit. The visibility of defaulting to Cam PCs grew, and the more able Sabbat antagonists grew with it.

            It's far more likely the oWW was following the players' lead more than the other way around here. The supplement treadmill needs people to buy buy buy, and you need to sell to what they're playing more than you get to direct popularity via editorial decisions (the oWW walked back a lot of 2e and Rev. metaplot stuff because it was unpopular and thus wasn't selling as well)..

                Ultimately, the lore of WoD is not realistic in an overall global sense; it can be used as is for local stories, but the moment you try to apply it on a global sense, it makes no sense.

                What I mean by that: I did once tried to "run a simulation" of all the different groups - a simple "division of labor" of the massive multitude of groups, based on the "official" numbers of supernaturals that supposedly inhabit the world.

                Just you guys try to do that, and you'll perceive how that doesnt lend itself to the stories being told. For example, the number of Cainites in the world is suppose to be at 40.000

                Now, try to divide that in at least 15 - 13 clans, plus a number of bloodlines and caithffs. Lets round those 40k to 60k for the math, and also because that number was from the 90's. That gives roughly 4.000 vampires to each clan - now now, I know they dont all have the exact same numbers, but that just adds injury to the insult. And, we'd get also 8000 ones to divide among the numerous bloodlines, caithffs and possibly antitribu.

                Ok, now divide that among the specific inter clans political/philosophical divisions.

                Now, try to divide that between generations, while quickly realizing that generational numbers add up upon one another exponentionally. So, aside from the 13 third generations, we are left with 9 generations to divide that - and there are also thin bloods. So, that gives almost 400 of each generation for each clan, which wouldnt be as such due to the exponential progression of it, but it does give an idea.

                Now, for vampires that is still somewhat viable, althought unlikely given the stories told. But the case gets far worse for the other splats.

                The worse offender by far is Mage. Mages "official" number is 1 in a million. If we are generous, that gives the impressive number of 8.000 mages globally.

                Now, between Mages we have basically 5 major "factions": the Technocracy, Traditions, Disparates, Nefandi and Marauders.

                Let's be ultra generous and round them to 9.000 and divide by 3, with the Disparates taking one of the portions along with Nefandi and Marauders, since those are obviously smaller. And lets wave the fact that probably the Technocracy have far more enlightned people than the Traditions, but lets just equalize the numbers. That gives 3.000 mages for each faction.

                Ok, now let us divide those 3.000 Mages between the Traditions, for instance. That will gives a number of 300-350 Mages in each Tradition, and only if we are been ULTRA generous.

                Now, divide those 300 mages in each tradition between the several houses of the Order of Hermes, and you'll realize that the might and powerful hermetic houses are actually a rabble of a few dozens of secluded weirdos in a very very localized ponctual way.

                Now, let us think about how many of those are truly on Earth. Because you know, there are several places in Umbra to stay, Horizon, Doissetep, the Digital Web... And all of a sudden, the "house Flambeau" constantly "waging war" against the Union becomes just a pathetically small gang fight of that one single guy that was left on the material plane.

                And if you try to think how many masters there are for each group, it gets even worse! It's no more than half a dozen for each.

                Not enough for be building an armada fleet of starships, or armies of clones.

                So, all the lore must be considered locally for making enjoyable stories, but is not realistic in a global sense

                        There's also limits to what's practical in any given medium. The number of "main" characters on-screen and in-script have some pretty well trod estimates of how many you can have before you start losing the audience in a film; whereas books tend to be able to have much larger leading casts as there isn't the expectation of being able to digest the whole story in a few hours. Older films tend to have way less extras than new films because of changes in technology. Movies like the LotR series and 300 use digital technology to turn a few hundred extras into thousands of individuals on screen: something older war films couldn't do and used other methods to imply the massive size of groups.

                                                    Think about the fact that the average Attribute value for a human being is 2 and that 5 is world-class. The average mortal would be built with around 9 dots total to distribute among Attributes, equal chances for more or less (because it is a Normal Distribution). A hunter PC under the worse rules has 13 dots. A supernatural PC has 15. On Attributes alone we're talking about an extremely low percentage of the population even at the bare beginning. Each aspect of a PC is increasingly putting then at the very top.

                                                    Our starting characters in WoD aren't even close to average people. They're over-the-top flukes that managed to become a part of their new over-the-top secret supernatural societies and survive their beginnings. Another characteristic of many splats, turnover is high. The challenges of the first nights for vampires, exposure to Paradox and dangerous phenomena for Mages, the sheer brutality of the War of Apocalypse for the Changing Breeds, people die a lot. You can meet 100 vampires in a city through the course of a year and yet it had no more than 30 at any given time, some left, a lot were Fledglings and died. Not necessarily standard, but can happen.

                                                    We are talking supernatural societies whose bottom lines are already apex monsters compared to normal humans and a part of the hidden conspiracies controlling the world. The Neonate angry because the Elders have all the good things and they can't control more than a couple night clubs, a small gang and one officer at the precinct, from the POV of a mortal investigator is a secret shadow that ties those unrelated things, toying with unchecked power behind the scenes, and serving directly the masters of the world. And this mortal is actually right. Millionaires have trouble pretending to the level of power this Neonate have, and need to employ it far more judiciously. You're not the bottom of society, you're the bottom of the masters above even the elites.

                                                    Consider now all those individual characters as some of the most powerful assets of their groups, not the bottom line, and review the numbers. The setting is not meant to have a representative of every small faction in every city with a million inhabitants, even if your chronicle may have it, because your chronicle is, by definition, special. A special moment in the lives of special people, your PCs. For many a Bloodline you don't need more than a dozen members around the world. You don't even need to have member of every Generation. A single immortal monster can be the sole representative of their culture for centuries before passing it down, because they're immortals. Mages and Werewolves have humans to carry on the bulk of their teachings and culture while they personally go out to be the legends those followers will sing about for generations.

                                                    Are Generations exponential? I'm not sure. Most vampires Embrace slowly, a lot never do. Werewolves live a dangerous life where a lot of cubs don't make it alive through the Rites of Passage and many Cliath won't make it to their third year after Change. A single Mage isn't just a person that believes their Paradigm a lot, they're a genius of their paradigm that is transcending limitations and braving new possibilities since their awakening. And all of those have the resources and raw potential to cause such an astounding impact on the world that only their few equals can stop them from simply taking it all over.

                                                    Sometimes sure, the games make things seem like they need more people than they give, maybe far more. Maybe. But I don't think the numbers are so lower than they should have for what they're really trying to represent. And the mortal "companions" aren't all that big of a problem, if every splat has 1 million "human supporters" and we consider VtM, WtA, MtA, WtO, CtD, MtR, HtR and DtF, that'l make 0,1% of the entire world's population. It is half the number of millionaires the world over.
