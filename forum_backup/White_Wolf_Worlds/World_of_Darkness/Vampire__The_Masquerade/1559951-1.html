                The Sabbat were said to have fought the Gehenna War under the cover of the Afghanistan and Iraqi wars, so those two places are likely included. But I'd also add in the Levant and Iran, and probably a few other places around there.

                Per the Book of Nod, p. 100:

                "On the second day, Caine will return And call his Children to the meeting place
                on the site of the First City he will beckon
                them, sitting on his basalt throne."

                He goes on to judge those who are unworthy (including diablerists), and then kills them. This seems ironic, given that diablerie appears to delay the Beckoning. So maybe the elders aren't running to Caine, but to his grandchilder? (Page 99 says they will eat everyone.) Maybe those who stay behind will actually be the ones getting judged?

                My understanding is that those who are immune to the Beckoning are so because they have no Antediluvian. There have always been hints that an Antediluvian has the power to call/summon/affect their entire clan, even if some of them seem able to resist. So this seems to be a "sympathy of the blood" thing—just as Tremere was able to call the Tremere antitribu to him (per Nights of Prophecy) and Ravnos' descendants went mad when he apparently died.

                The Feast of Folly might be an older example. The Family Reunion itself may have been something similar (but perhaps weaker, since it was only Fourth Generation elders responsible), and that may be the real reason everyone turned up and played nice. It also required the Old Man in the Mountain to agree to the Treaty of Tyre to curse his entire clan. And the Malkavian Methuselahs behind the Great Prank may have been doing something similar, too.

                tl;dr: It works through blood sympathy. The eldest can draw their descendants to them, or even affect them at large given enough oomph to do so.

                                                            Nope.

                                                            I use as principle that with time most vampires notice their powers are blunt instruments, and careful application of lesser ones or just skill is more useful in the long run, and a more worthy challenge.

                                                            Also that the Antes have less grandiose goals most of the time, because at the level of power they're in, their usual interests are either utterly mundane, alien, or both, while the big ones require too careful action for displays of power to be worth it.

                                                            Think Saulot in Lair of the Hidden working as a humble servant, because the low profile while simply being there is way more effective to get what he wants.

                                                            Overall the secret to have high power NPCs in game without breaking everything is to have them under constraints: abusing power is easy, but won't get what you really want. Also never let any explanation of what they want land as a trustworthy conclusion.
