                    Predator types as implemented suffer because they have massive impacts on character creation (and not balanced ones in terms of relative XP costs), and then have no substantive impact in play. They're nice as both player-to-ST communication (it's a quick way to say a lot about your character's night-to-night activities) and they're nice RP aids, but there's nothing stopping players form picking a type for the mechanics and never really feeding that way in play; there's no carrot or stick about it.

                    If I was going to just port them into V20, I'd do something like have them replace Demeanor (a trait that's basically fluff as it is) in character creation (so players are thinking about it, and it does do the communication part effectively), and then have some small bonus like +1 vitae per scene or per downtime feeding roll if you feed appropriately to your type. All of the char-gen Discipline, Humanity, and Background/Merit/Flaw adjustments really don't help them do their job in the game. It also lets them be more customizable. There's a big different between a Nosferatu or Gangrel that feeds on animals by default out of practicality vs. a vampire that does it for moral/Humanity reasons when they don't face any particular issues being around normal humans to hunt.

                            Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                            Predator types as implemented suffer because they have massive impacts on character creation (and not balanced ones in terms of relative XP costs), and then have no substantive impact in play. They're nice as both player-to-ST communication (it's a quick way to say a lot about your character's night-to-night activities) and they're nice RP aids, but there's nothing stopping players form picking a type for the mechanics and never really feeding that way in play; there's no carrot or stick about it.
                            Of course they have substantial impact on character creation -- it's a part of it, incorporated with backstory, character typing, and theme. It should.

                            Predator types with highly niche feeding styles -- farmer, graverobber/bagger, grim reaper, consensualist, trapdoor, blood leech -- lean more heavily into facilitating those styles while enforcing restrictions on characters who choose them. I don't see how there's no carrot or stick, when many predator types heavily restrict potential vessels, up to and including nominal inability to feed (or extreme cost of feeding) on mortals at all. Those also happen to be the predator types an enterprising player might select for mechanics, since they grant dots in uncommon disciplines (namely, protean and oblivion) or an additional blood potency at character generation.

                            Are other predator types less restrictive? yeah -- they're expressly intended to be. It's the distinction between generalism and specialty. The benefit to an open-ended predator type is adaptability -- both graverobbers and baggers have iron gullet and prefer cold blood, but the former will have difficulty feeding in a traditional rack whereas the latter would not.

                            Which by extension raises the Humanity spectre -- by RAW player characters cannot raise Humanity at all, it's an optional rule for ST's to allow it...either through extensive, dedicated, and borderline flawless roleplay, and/or with experience at new rating times ten. The difference between Humanity 7 and 6 may not be huge, but far more substantial than any edition to date. And, that difference already has major implications for certain predator types (most notably, siren).

                            Blood leech is the most munchkin-friendly predator type in the game thus far. It offers highly-valuable specialty in one of the two more valuable abilities in game, your choice in one of the two stronger disciplines in the game, an additional blood potency...at the cost of an Humanity, and two nasty flaws one of which is Prey Exclusion (mortals). And that one puts you 50 experience in the hole, as it costs 20 to buy BP 2 but would cost 70 to buy Humanity 7 (if the ST even allows it).

                            Sure, there's also crossover between predator types, and which predator types are more appropriate based on character can be questionable. I ran into this issue myself creating a Toreador NPC for my most recent chronicle, who's a BDSM sex worker that does exclusively incall and porn. Was it more appropriate to make her a siren, scene queen, or trapdoor? any of the three would be equally valid choices. In the end, it was absolutely a mechanical choice: trapdoor's specialty, backgrounds, and flaws were more appropriate to the character, even if protean or obfuscate were facially unorthodox.

                            Other than that, as background costs have been normalized (it's just three per dot now, unattached to current or new rating) the biggest swing one could get from a dot in predator type is...ten XP. The biggest value-add in terms of experience is starting with level 3 in a clan discipline (15 experience), and the lowest is starting with a third clan discipline at level 1 (5 experience). Value-add from starting with a first dot in an out-of-clan discipline is nominally 7, but in practice is all over the place and unquantifiable, depending on the discipline in question, its comparative value in accordance to table and type of chronicle, and whether or not that discipline dot is a prerequisite for amalgams a player may desire for their character.

                            That's an amount of experience points that normalizes inside a single story's worth of gain. It's still a far cry from freebie points, that can be gamed to put a character up to a hundred experience ahead of another, with savvy distribution and merit/flaw selection.

                                It doesn't matter that the predator types have less negative impact on PC XP-value balance in V5 than V20 already has in the BP/XP divide; they introduce even more as written if you add them without modification. V5 could actually claim to have an XP balanced starting spread compared to all previous VtM editions (though VtR 2e beat it to the punch) if not for the predator types. Predator types also aren't an exhaustive unalterable list. While the list is fairly comprehensive, there's nothing stopping the ST and players from modifying them or creating new ones if something else would work better (and not just Rule Zero).

                                As for carrots and sticks, I mean things outside of the specific pool of mechanics they offer up. If you take two characters that have to different types, but then the course of the story pushes them in the same direction and then end up with identical sheets... all differences regarding feeding are essentially erased. It's not like they offer exclusive traits or something; even if V5's high XP costs and low XP hand outs makes every extra dot at creation that much more impactful.

                                Compared to other Vampire editions, the predator types are just taking what would normally be points to flush out a character to the player's desires, and putting them in a "feeding" bucket. It's extra scaffolding in terms of game design. It's not "necessary" but largely serves its purpose of giving more direction on what's important to think about when assigning values to your character's sheet. In V20, you can informally just ask players to explain how their character normally feeds and see if their sheet really meshes with that, and end up with largely the same outcomes; it's just not as baked in.

                                I personally find the "should" statement rather odd given that V5 encourages much fresher vampires than older editions did as the default. If one of V5's goals was to make the default closer to newly embraced vampires instead of vampires with a decade or more of being undead under their belts, it makes less sense for the characters to have settled into feeding strategies so much that they can have such a drastic starting impact.

                                Also, frankly, if you're going to try to edition war and write fanboy screeds at people every time V5 gets criticized, people aren't going to care even when you make good points. You're just sabotaging your own credibility to discuss these things reasonably. Even more so since the mods just asked us (the forum at large) to tone things down in this regard.

                                                Predator-Types always felt like a bit of an overreach to put it at character-creation this prominently. These muddled heaps of dots in Skill-specializations, Disciplines, Merits/Backgrounds and Flaws act far too much like a locked-in choice. I think it's entirely dependent on what sort of people you have at the table whether focusing on feeding to this degree is important. Predator-Type only matters if the player in question cares about it. For a player who doesn't care nothing about the more restrictive Predator-types would be interesting but that doesn't equate to them simply having to take a less restrictive version giving them something of equal value compared to the person who really cares. Rather, in my opinion, there are plenty of players who don't want to play VtM with their PCs having locked-in feeding-strategies from the beginning. It's addressing a character-question that is interesting to address but not necessary. Predator-Type makes it a necessary question, though, and now a firm answer and categorization are needed where before this was something you could figure out during play.

                                                The Predator-Type should accommodate the character-concept and not the other way around. And so I think flexibility and minimalism in how impactful picking a Predator-Type is are preferable. The way Predator-Types are presented, though, (unless (somehow) the character concepts fit the confines of how much is stuffed into these Predator-Type-packages) they end up feeling more like a confining option than something that improves the character-creation-process.

                                                            Originally posted by Manfr View Post

                                                            Something I'd add, is that in the typical Vampire city, with no huge numbers of Kindred, Predator Types are also a social scene of sorts: you meet people wheew you feed, were they other Kindreds, other Supernaturals or the kind of people you aree feeding from, of course.

                                                            It also bears mention with the changes to V5 metaplot and city politics, the "Rack as social space in addition to Elysium" isn't the default or the expectation. It's more akin to Dark Ages, where every vampire has their feeding ground(s) to do as they will (within reason), and if/where/when those overlap it's either claimed by a coterie, shared between allies, or cause for potential conflict. Vampires may not necessarily share predator types to inhabit the same spaces, and to a certain extent it's more cause for harmonious interaction if they don't since they're not necessarily feeding on the same herd.

                                                            Case in point, that Toreador trapdoor I mentioned in the chronicle I'm running. She's in a coterie with a scene queen Tremere and a siren Gangrel. Their shared feeding ground is the local university, where the Tremere has her fingers in sex-positive campus groups and non-profs, and claims a "content" house with a protest group front as her domain. The Tremere's into new agey free love bullshit and has the influence to keep things moving, the Toreador handles the money and does "content", and the Gangrel gets free "meals" while keeping things safe and consensual (i.e. she's the muscle). They don't feed on the same mortals, but there's enough cross-pollination between their respective herds -- not to mention synergies in their influences -- they accomplish more working together than individually.

                                                            But, onto nitty-gritty...

                                                            Originally posted by Knightingale View Post
                                                            ...These muddled heaps of dots in Skill-specializations, Disciplines, Merits/Backgrounds and Flaws act far too much like a locked-in choice.
                                                            Most of that framework exists to justify how the character feeds (and how feeding preferences would influence dot allocation in the backstory phase), while providing a baseline ability to feed in the first place. The quiet part out loud here is predator type doesn't just influence disciplines, advantages, and flaws -- players still have to allocate attributes and skills before predator type, and once they've put everything together they need a respectable dice pool in their feeding roll of choice, and in the case of some predator types and clans a discipline dot to supplement feeding strategy.

                                                            That's all there to ensure players don't feel they must optimize for feeding, and are freer to allocate dots elsewhere as appropriate to the character. If, for example, a player wanted to run with an alleycat Tremere, it's a pathway to the dot of Celerity or Potence they'd need, as well as contacts or a relevant specialty that might otherwise have to be bought, to justify the feeding strategy.

                                                            I think it's entirely dependent on what sort of people you have at the table whether focusing on feeding to this degree is important. Predator-Type only matters if the player in question cares about it. For a player who doesn't care nothing about the more restrictive Predator-types would be interesting but that doesn't equate to them simply having to take a less restrictive version giving them something of equal value compared to the person who really cares...
                                                            See earlier commentary about experienced players and the difficulties of running feeding scenes with them.

                                                            Rather, in my opinion, there are plenty of players who don't want to play VtM with their PCs having locked-in feeding-strategies from the beginning. It's addressing a character-question that is interesting to address but not necessary. Predator-Type makes it a necessary question, though, and now a firm answer and categorization are needed where before this was something you could figure out during play.
                                                            Except, with only a handful of predator types are characters mandated to feed a certain way. There's nothing to stop a siren from starting with (or gaining) fame/influence and feeding scene queen or osiris-style, or just having good combat dice pools enabling alleycat or pursuer-style feeding. A Gangrel or Nosferatu trapdoor can still build up or start with Animalism, and supplement trapdoor feeding with farming when the mortals just aren't around. Graverobbers might be obvious predators, but that flaw only applies when attempting to feed through social wiles -- they can still bag it, or go alleycat or sandman.

                                                            A case in point is a snippet of a scene from that same chronicle, when the PC's were at a party being held by the aforementioned NPC coterie. The Brujah PC was a little peckish, and his player asked to hunt at the party. I said yeah, but since it's a college party he can't exactly beat the piss out of someone, start a fight, or extort them for blood, and the easiest or most obvious way would be to seduce a mortal. He consulted his dice pools for siren-style feeding, realized his character sucked at it, and pointed out something I hadn't considered: it's a college party, there are going to be people passed out drunk or high off their tits, he could sandman it.

                                                            I was more than happy to oblige, and after the disclaimer it may result in his character getting drunk or high, he was able to feed just fine despite having a predator type not at all suited to college parties. Worked out better for him in the short term, since the difficulty bonus against frenzy from weed came in handy a scene later when he had to resist it.

                                                            None of this "locks in" anything, it just establishes a baseline to justify how your character doesn't just starve into torpor or wassail.
