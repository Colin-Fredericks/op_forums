        Originally posted by The Gentleman Gamer View Post
        I am interested to see what people think of the Drowned Legacies as written (in brief) in BJD.
        - Aztec symbolism has layers of meaning. "Cipactli" is the primitive water monster, or a caiman. As a sea monster, is wasn't different from a Tiamat. As a caiman it was a symbol for the primitive earth and for the founding of dynasties. I'd describe the Legacy not as reptilian, but as monstrous, and related to power. It wasn't bad written, and I know the writers didn't had enough words to developed the legacies.

        - Karai Pyhare is the "Lord of the Night" from the Guarani speaking peoples of South America. It's a night spirit, and thus any vampire (or non-vampire) could be translated as the "Karai Pyhare".I don't know why there were described as "bestial", and I'd like to develop them as tricksters that use local lore to trick the humans into fear or submission.

        - The Kalku from Mapuche lore are witches that deal with evil spirits. It would be interesting to have a new spiritual blood-magic.

        In developing the Legacies I'd consider the following principles:

        - There was shared cultural identity between large areas of Mesoamerica and South America before the arrival of the Spanish and Portuguese. There were large linguistic groups, but even within each linguistic group there were different agricultural and material cultures, imposed by the environment. In the western half of South America, for example, you had the Tupi (and others) living from the rain forest to semi-deserts.

        - Current native lore isn't the best guide to develop the legacies, specially for South America. The peoples from Central America had a writing system, but most South American groups didn't had written languages. It means it is possible to understand the Mexica and Mayan worldviews, but it's almost impossible to know more about the Guarani religion in the 16th century. For example, Guarani lore from Argentina had the word "Luison" for a kind of monster, but this word is just a misappropriation of the Spanish word for werewolf. It's impossible to know (to my knowledge) if the Argentinian Guarani adopted European folklore (as it happened in my own country, Brazil) or if they started using an European word for their own monster.

        - Studying the local culture and conditions if pretty important. It's impossible to use the same developing guidelines used for Kindred of the Ebony Kingdom to develop South American legacies. The African peoples were able to preserve their culture and native religions and languages in a greater degree than Native American peoples, and most Native Americans right now have a mixed heritage. Aboriginal or Amerindian Brazilians, for example, are only 0.4% of the country's population, but almost all Brazilians have some remote (16th-18th century) Amerindian admixture. Our language is European, and our religion is mostly European. Mostly importantly, our worldview is mostly influenced by Southern European/Catholic culture, specially in the Northeastern portion of the country. So, in portraying the "aboriginal legacies", it's important to study the local peoples before the culture melange the country became after the 16th century and specially after the 17th and 18th centuries. It's the only way to distinguish between truly native influences and foreign influences (foreign being a relative term, since most locals consider Catholicism and Roman languages as their own native heritages, specially when compared to the Protestant English-speaking North).
