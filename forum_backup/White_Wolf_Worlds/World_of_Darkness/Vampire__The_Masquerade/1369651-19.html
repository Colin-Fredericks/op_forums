    Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
    I think most of the mid-ranking members of the Camarilla (that is those embraced before 1950) probably have some fairly good ideas about the Sabbat, know those guys are dangerous, and either have their own stories or know other vampires who have been hurt or killed by them. And ~50+ year old vampires are the ones who hold more of the power in any given Camarilla city than vampires embraced less than 20 years ago.

    I view it kind of like if the US government had granted amnesty to half of Al-Queda members, then invited those Al-Queda members into the US, gave them citizenship and then they started taking positions of political leadership. Everything else aside, I think a lot of Americans (even people traditionally liberal) would find that to be a pretty questionable and concerning policy and there would be a ton of pushback. Younger people born after 9/11 might not care all that much, but most people older than that would be very weary of that. And for most people in the US, the Al-Queda/US dispute only goes back around a quarter of a century. Meanwhile the Sabbat has been a fanatical fundamentalist organization that's been at the throat of the Camarilla for a few centuries.

    I have a hard time imagining that just because the Inner Circle of the Camarilla said, "These guys are cool, trust us!" everyone else in the Camarilla would just nod and go along with it.
    I'd argue that if lower-ranking Al-Qaeda members offered up the higher ranks of such that they'd definitely take that deal. I remind you we have OPERATION: PAPERCLIP and other examples of enemies being brought in exchange for concessions or wealth.

    Whether they intend to fulfill their promises or not, the fact is that the Camarilla is not an organization that has any professed ideals. It helps things considerably in terms of real politic that the Camarilla propagates vampiric supremacy, power, and a gerontocracy rather than making nonsense promises of being for any higher ideal.

    It's not like the Camarilla disapproves of mass murder, lies, or torture.

    What they disliked about the Sabbat was they were rivals for power and prestige. Power and prestige which the Lasombra will bring while it is greatly weakened from losing so many members to the Beckoning, Anarch Revolts, and Second Inquisition.

                            I think the answer tends to come down to "the people who actually live in those places that might want to buy the product otherwise aren't interested in having those superficial (if not dangerous: see above post) stereotypes fisted into the textbook description of the part of the world that they would presumably be playing in." And combined with those of us that think "that's a reasonable thing to want," the business tries to decide which group will earn them more dollars.

                            The thing to remember when complaining about pop culture infecting the "Western" depictions of vampires in Vampire: the Masquerade is that the authors are "Western." They recycled Western tropes for Western audiences, having some understanding of the context of what they were influenced by and what they were creating. And even that doesn't save them from occasionally fucking up horribly (WoD: G****s). But we are all fallible humans, learning everyday, and hindsight is 20/20.

                            To the extent that pop culture "anime-esque" tropes are not necessarily harmful, I don't think that removing them from the Wan Kuei, or removing the Wan Kuei over them, is absolutely necessary. But I think that fans who actually live in those places have as much, if not more, right to have their opinions and feelings considered by the writers as the fans who don't live in those places but have enjoyed the superficial portrayal of those places up to now. Ultimately, the writers themselves have the right to decide how they might want to update their setting, and the business will consider the decision that they think will produce the most profit.
