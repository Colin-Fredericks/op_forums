          It's probably a bit reductive to say that they taste "the same," but rather that they taste "close enough" that you need an expert to pick apart the difference. I think it's also less a question of literal "taste"; I think this matter tends to come up more when Thaumaturgical testing for lineage occurs. And since the Tremere are already supposed to be this weird slurry of Tzimisce and Salubri, it's probably easier for Baali to pass themselves off as some unusual off-shoot of the Tremere than it is some other unknown Clan. Especially since plenty might have access to Dark Thaumaturgy to imitate Tremere Blood Magic.

          Also worth noting that this is one of those "kinda common OOC knowledge, but very obscure IC knowledge" topics. IIRC Aisling Sturbridge offers a side-note accusing Vykos of being a liar when the theory of Tzimisce blood being using to create the Tremere comes up in BJD.

                              Another possibility is that the "Tremere blood tastes like Baali blood" is an in-universe bit of propaganda, spread by enemies of the Tremere to literally demonize them.

                              After all, the Tremere spread vicious rumors about the Salubri being soul-stealers. Turnabout is fair play.

                              To be fair, the Tremere ARE pretty evil, all told. They have many enemies, and not just for petty reasons. It's just that them being blood-related to the token Infernalist bloodline doesn't necessarily need to be one of them.

                              It might be a malicious lie by one of the Tremere's enemies. Or something that a vampire who had an opportunity to drink both (or thought they had) convinced themselves was true. Like, they fed on a Tremere and a Baali (or someone they believed to be a Baali, or just a random Infernalist), and it tasted just similar enough (in the sense that any vampire's blood tastes similar to any other) that they jumped to conclusions. This accusation then gained momentum over time, while also degenerating (as rumors often do). Until "Tremere blood tastes like Baali" just became accepted fact (at least among the minority of Kindred who heard of it).

                              After all, for many vampires, "Infernalist" and "Baali" are one and the same. The distinction is unknown or lost to them. Heck, maybe Tremere blood does have a whiff of the demonic, but only because it descends from Tzimisce, who have a long history of dealings with Kupala and other lesser demons.

                                          Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post

                                          I like that theory. They are the result of multiple clans becoming infernalists. While not all infernalists become Baali, some Infernalists got a special deal back with the Devil back in the day, resulting in multiple Baali founders. I wouldn't be surprised if such deals were fairly common back when 4rth and 5th gens were neonate trash in a world where werewolves were much more common, and mages suffered far less paradox. There may be literally hundreds of Baali founders, most of which have long since been destroyed.
                                          Specifically, the version of that origin story in Black Hand and other V20 books has nothing to do with the Devil or bargains. Indeed, most Baali don't really truck with anything as mundane as the Devil, but rather older, more eldritch things, which predate the Abrahamic religions.

                                          Basically, the idea is that it's the Well at Chorazin that makes Baali. So any vampire thrown into it, or Embraced within it, emerges as Baali. This is the basis of the spawning rite used, for instance, to make Apostates (those who convert to Baali from other Clans and Bloodlines).

                                          The origin myth of the three 'founders' states that Saulot (or another) punished the mortal infernalists at Chorazin by attacking them and throwing them into the Well. He then poured some of his vitae in afterwards, for reasons unknown (Transylvania Chronicles suggests Saulot wants to become the Demon Emperor, so perhaps that's why).

                                          The three who crawled through the mass of broken bodies and drank this vitae emerged as the three Baali founders, known as Moloch, Nergal and the Unnamed.

                                          Thereafter, the Baali would often make sacrificial wells that, through such grotesque acts of evil, effectively become Nihils or similar (i.e., shallow points between the Skinlands and the realms of the dreaming Neverborn, who may or may not be the same as the Children).

                                          It's very close to the idea of Nephandi being made through a caul, which I think is intentional. The story as told in more recent supplements hints that multiple Antediluvians may have come across the Well, rather than just one, meaning the founders may have been created at separate times (or perhaps multiple Antediluvians travelled together and punished the human infernalists).

                                          Now, I think you may be referring to the lore as told in Chaos Factor (now retconned), where a band of Methuselahs from the times of the Book of Nod followed Shaitan in offering themselves up as servants to a demon called Baal, thus becoming Baali.

                                          I think the V20 version of events is probably an attempt to integrate the conflicting lore somewhat (the buried and entombed bodies of the Children are implied to be physical manifestations or shells of the Neverborn, in a nod to the Qlippoth of Mage, who must be kept aslept lest their dreaming minds and entombed bodies reunite and awaken in the Skinlands).

                                          I also think it links back to the apocryphal history of ur-Shulgi which was removed from the Revised Clanbook Assamite. In that book, ur-Shulgi is hinted to be a Widderslainte, who is thrown into the Well by Haqim, emerges as some sort of Nephandi/dark blood mage, and rallies the Baali to make them easier to strike as one. The Baali are thus defeated and Haqim proceeds to tear the demon/inverted Avatar inhabiting ur-Shulgi's body and Embraces him as an Assamite. In this way, Haqim might be a third Baali progenitor.

                                          The complicating factor (and these always exist in the WoD) is that Lore of the Bloodlines represents yet another story, where the Children are the First People, which potentially makes them Fallen rather than manifestations of the Neverborn. But it might also be true that the Neverborn are just the souls of a primordial race who strode the cosmos before the VTM Yahweh decimated/usurped them.
