        Originally posted by Gryffon15 View Post
        This is somewhat tangential to the core of the thread but with enough connections to make it worth including imo.

        I’ve never really understood the rationale for having the New Anarch Revolts (here referring to the ones which produced the West Coast Free States rather than the First Anarch Revolt, the revolts that produced the ‘Brujah Council’ of the USSR, or the contemporary revolts of the early 1990s) take place in the 1940s rather than the seemingly natural pick of the 1960s. Maybe for Gen Xers in the 90s it being so natural was the problem, it was too obvious. But I mean, it would be natural for a reason.

        Southern California in the 1940s, to play Devil’s advocate, does have some things going for it tbf. You’ve got the waves of unrest produced by the Great Depression which certainly produced political ferment in real life (you had Upton Sinclair’s EPIC program, but also groups like Ham and Eggs & Technocracy Inc.). You had waves of Oakie immigrants who (of the Steinbeck tradition), alongside the early Chicano’s, could possibly provide a base. You had the New Deal programs which finally ended LA’s tenure as an anti-labor ‘Open Town’ and which gave the left room to breathe thanks to the Popular Front. You had Japanese internment which could have functioned as a ‘barrack of revolt’ hypothetically.

        Yet on the other hand this is still coming in the wake of the put-down of organized labor in Southern California post-WWI and in the 1920s. This is also post the Water Wars which helped to entrench the ruling powers and the rise of Hollywood Land. Furthermore imo the conditions of Southern California as a major military camp and manufacturing hub would dampen the potential for revolt - Military MPs everywhere, anti-saboteur vigilance, the anti-strike pledge from labor, and the generalized atmosphere of paranoia (see the Battle of Los Angeles) would imo strengthen the Camarilla during this period by making clear the absolute necessity of upholding the Masquerade. Hell that’s part of what makes Hollywood so valuable - it was, alongside the news network hubs back east, a crucial wing of the state’s propaganda arm. If the toreadors couldn’t thrive in such an environment they wouldn’t be worth their bulls.

        The humans would simply be too edgy to risk much going on outside the shadows. Besides that the war economy was a boon to organized crime, who were virtually given a blank check to manage the black market, so long as they kept saboteurs off the docks. In this atmosphere I can’t help but see the Camarilla thriving; fattening themselves on internment profiteering, war profiteering, and black market profiteering.

        Then what about after the war ends? There was a bit of a post-war slump after V-Day, that’s true, so maybe you put the revolt there. But I still don’t really see it. The slump ends pretty quickly once the Korean War gears up. Besides that the post-war economic engine gets running pretty soon. The suburban sprawl in Southern California takes on new life in this period and the automobile nation proper is born. Plus the Military-Industrial Complex takes root and really makes cities like San Diego come to life, not to mention the sci-fi wonders coming out of places like Cal Tech. Then you get into the era of the Second Red Scare, from McCarthy & HUAC to the anti-Beatnik hysteria to the teen delinquency-hysteria, all the way to Goldwater and the Orange County suburban warriors coming into self-consciousness.

        For me the Southern California of the 1930s through to the 1950s (including perhaps the 20s & early 60s), especially LA, is the prime real estate for Noir. IMO that works best, in vampire terms, by having the Camarilla still running the show.

        I do think there would be a ferment however, the seeds of what would become the new Anarch Revolt. The old-old left in Southern California may have been beaten down after the era of the First Red Scare post-1917 and through the 20s, but there would be survivors, especially amongst the immortal. You had the Steinbeckian figures of the 1930s alongside the noir anti-heroes, the flakes of the nervous breakdown of the middle classes, the rising old left of the popular front and their liberal counterparts in the New Deal tangling with the rise of the Californian answer to brown shirts while exiled European intellectuals sought security in Hollywood. You had the rise of Noir proper with the 1940s and the question of the post-war order, amidst even further booms in population under the War economy (concentration that, even if they had accepted War-time Masquerade protections measures, would certainly have felt tensions building). You had the post-war answer in the Second Red Scare and HUAC which forced the comrades and fellow travelers back underground (those who didn’t flee to Mexico City or Moscow at least). You also had the run-wild paranoia and hysteria of the era produce more moral panics to provide anarch inspiration: hipster anarchs from the anti-Beatnik hysteria (paging Nick Cave), hotrod Anarchs from the anti-youth delinquency hysteria, and perhaps most significantly anarchs drawn from the rising discontent of a black and latino population chafing under Anglo rule and channeled through the rising civil rights movement.

        If you combine all the potential anarchs created by the past 50 years with the booming of the population in the 1960s as the children of the war are finally grown up then I think you have a much bigger recipe for a New Anarch Revolt than you do in the 1940s. Combine that with the waves of migrants called in by 1967 with the inauguration of the Summer of Love, the international ripple effect from the Cultural Revolution in China in 1966, and the possible catalyst (or at least mask for the spark) which can be provided for LA by the Watts Uprising of 1965, then I think you’ve got a strong case for post-dating the New Anarch Revolt forward two decades.
        Looked at from another direction, you could make a case for the Second Anarch Revolt being triggered in part because the LA Toreador are doing well out of WWII whilst almost nobody else is - this would certainly be in keeping with Don Sebastian’s demonstrated tendencies (A mistrust of non-Spaniards, a pronounced tendency towards tyranny and a somewhat fickle competence - when he was good he was extremely capable, when he was bad he was appalling).

        If nothing else, it’s worth considering that the Camarilla’s influence in LA was weak enough for the Prince and his cronies to hazard the Masquerade to the point of chasing starlets stark naked through the streets of Hollywood.


        It’s also interesting to wonder if the greater openness of the 1960s could be depicted as the fruition of efforts by elements within the Free States to create an environment in which their revolution could finally get out from under the human authorities as they had thrown off the old vampiric regime (I do tend to think that vampire history should sometimes mirror, sometimes prefigure and sometimes be dragged along in the wake of human social & political developments - with the Second Anarch Revolt being a case where the Free State vampires were a bit ahead of the curve).


        In any case, it’s not as though Film Noir vampire-style is completely off the table in Los Angeles and the Free States, whether pre-Revolt or even afterwards (The Free States are a big place, after all - it’s not impossible some areas were business as Humphrey Bogart even while others were preoccupied with Big Political Developments).


        Also, Black Fox, I for one would be sorry to lose Christopher Houghton, since the most powerful elder in the Free States (Or at least in Los Angeles) being a sort of Shakespearean Peter Pan explains so much about their unpredictability and violence, whilst also allowing PCs to mostly go about their business without having to worry what he’s doing at the moment.

        Also, I suspect there’s a good deal of potential pathos in the character, as a boy robbed of both his childhood and of his chance to grow into manhood (Due to having been Embraced at a horribly young age in more ways than one), who finally escaped the domination of his Sire at the cost of his dignity, then sought a place where he could finally make something of his own dreams come true.

        Even if they were Cruel Dreams.

                This is not directly relevant to the Anarch Council, but thought it was the best place to go. It's something the Anarch Council might export as part of its ongoing support to the Anarch cause in other domains.

                The Anarch Cookbook has an extended section on "Libertas". It is mentioned as a counterpart of Humanity. The idea is that just as Kindred have varying amounts of Humanity, they have varying amounts of Libertas. It's a kind of gnostic philosophy in that the more one "frees" themselves from control of other beings, the greater one's consciousness is. Things like Dominate, Presence, and the Blood Bond degrades one's Libertas or at least can be seen as evidence of it. Freeing oneself from control of others increases it. Mortals suffer from this too, but the act of embrace ends up "freeing" the mortal slightly because of their liberation from "Libertas-draining institutions of mortal society." Gaining a new perspective as a vampire does that. Now vampires just need to increase that higher perspective.

                Eventually, the adherents of Libertas believe, this will have physical effects (just like Humanity does). Physical effects will be things like vampires overcoming their vulnerability to wood (meaning stakes through the heart), fire, and even sunlight because they'll be free from such external controls/limitations.

                It sounds batshit insane, but that's because we players know there is no such mechanic. (Although it does sound like some things that the Ordo Dracul might believe in Vampire: the Requiem.) However, in character, who is to say there is anything wrong with the idea other than it hasn't been proven or disproven yet.

                This entire section reminds me a whole of lot of Philip K. Dick's works that were heavily infused with a gnostic worldview of reality not being what we think it is, and that we're being manipulated by higher powers wanting to keep us ignorant. It builds on a lot of pseudo-gnostic philosophy built into the World of Darkness, and can also be seen in the Malkavian clan and eventually Mage.

                The philosophy behind this is not the sole philosophy of the Kindred of the Free States. Indeed, it seems to be a minority view, but it seems the writers of the Anarch Cookbook intended this to be an important element.

                We don't see this at all in the LABN sourcebook. Other than a brief mention in the Status Prefectus, it doesn't get addressed at all. In fact a lot of things mentioned in the Cookbook and other very early material concerning the Free States seem to get dumped by the time LABN came out. If Andrew Greenberg was no longer Developer at that time, I would attribute it to different tastes of the new Developer. But he was still the Developer at the time of LABN so I wonder if he simply had second thoughts on the direction of the Free States. Maybe there was fan feedback pushing back on it.

                So I'll be introducing this concept into the chronicle. It's novel enough idea, and I think it deserves some effort of spreading to anarchs elsewhere. It transforms mere politics into a spiritual crusade. Its actual true adherents are a relatively small group and spread between several coteries. But a a substantial minority will at least make reference to it, even if they really don't do anything about it.

                I'll just need to get some cliff notes on Philip K Dick, Colin Wilson, and William S Burroughs so I can condense this into some kind of coherent thought good enough to explain in an RPG and perhaps not sound completely nuts (only partially nuts). I'll probably base any exponents of the philosophy in very Burroughs-like beatnik tones.

                I already intend to have Dr Raoul King (the obvious Hunter S Thompson homage in the original Clanbook Gangrel) show up to the occasional Rant, so why not a Burroughs analogue?

                        The Anarch Council (not actually mentioned in LABN but in the Brujah Clanbook) seems to be the Comintern of the Anarch Movement. It doesn't rule the Free States, but supports the anarchs in other domains in their attempts to free themselves from the princes. As such, understanding their successes and failures can be important especially because a common question from PCs could be, "What exactly are they doing?"

                        This could just be an honest question. The PCs might just want to confirm the Anarch Council is doing something. However, one of the worst things that can happen is when PCs think important NPCs are idiots or losers because they can't accomplish anything and need the PCs to do everything. This happens, of course, because the ST/GM simply wants to give players something exciting to do, but doesn't consider how their presentation might undermine the reputation of the NPCs.

                        I also think its an important question as one reason many players lost interest in the anarchs is that they just seemed to be losers who got lucky once. Showing there is some kind of struggle with both setbacks and victories shows the anarchs are doing something, and makes them more attractive. My players in this chronicle won't want to feel they are losers for being anarchs, but that MacNeil and other anarchs are making a difference.

                        "Support" in this case probably being smuggling weapons, sharing advice, helping anarchs make their own connections, and sending militant anarchs to places where they can help in any "big push" to overthrow the local prince. Any specific ideas on what else they do would be appreciated.

                        So what has the Anarch Council be doing since 1945? Thankfully we do have some examples of anarch activities in other parts of the world, and we can easily hook these examples back to the Council. I'll explain in the following posts.

                            First, let's look at some regrettable failures.

                            The Anarch Cookbook describes how a conflict between the anarchs and the prince in Detroit escalated into a five faction war (Anarchs vs Prince vs Primogen vs Setites vs Sabbat) that saw the Sabbat takeover and massacre everyone else. We're not given other information, but I would set this at the same time as the 1967 Detroit Riots. That makes the best sense to me. It makes a good example of why the anarchs need to be careful in their attacks on the Camarilla and understand how other parties might take advantage of the work they put into it and get a lot of anarchs killed. If PCs push too hard or complain the council isn't doing enough, this is a good story to tell them as a cautionary tale. I'll probably have as a local NPC someone who is a lone survivor of the Detroit anarchs.

                            We also have the infamous Night of Rage during the 1968 Democratic Convention in Chicago. Chicago is basically the capital of the Camarilla in North America. The largest city they control and home to several powerful and influential Kindred of multiple clans. Taking the city would have been a huge victory for the anarchs. Instead, it became a terrible defeat. "Never before had the Anarchs experienced an assault of this massacre." Over a hundred vampires from all over the world disappeared in one night. The anarch leadership of many cities had been wiped out. "Princes across the country were free to crack down with brutal vengeance. Anarchs everywhere were on the run: an age of repression had dawned." I imagine a lot of success stories that MacNeil and company had obtained over the past twenty years were wiped out. This can be a powerful lesson at being too obvious or too direct. If the elders and archons can concentrate their forces even large numbers of anarchs can be lost. Of all the defeats the anarchs suffered since 1945, this should be the most painful.

                            Defeats are important because they are remembered and often used to fuel grievances, memorialize fallen comrades, or as part of a story that ends in eventual victory. Slogans like "Remember Detroit" or "Never Forget '68!" can provide a sense of history of the struggle important to LA anarchs even though those are far away places.

                                However, we can also point to some successes.

                                The Anarch Cookbook mentions Perth as a domain that was recently liberated by the anarchs and that many of the Gangrel "princes" of small cities/towns are either anarchs or sympathetic to them. This is repeated in A World of Darkness 2e which adds that the prince who was overthrown was Lumley of Clan Tremere. I'm going to call this the Perth Free State. NPCs will announce its "liberation" at the first Rant the PCs go to, and make sure some Aussie anarch reps thank MacNeil and the Council for their support. I might have Germaine and some other well known Anarchs come back with them, fresh from helping liberate Perth. That way the PCs can see the movement succeeding and that MacNeil and the council are doing something.

                                VtM Corebook 2e mentions that Sabbat recently lost Miami, and Anarch Cookbook reveals this was mostly due to the anarchs, and that the anarchs mostly fight Sabbat there and not the Camarilla. While there is a Camailla declared prince in the city, I am going to hold that the anarchs actually hold most of the city. Everyone "knows" the prince doesn't really rule, and that it's more important for the anarchs to fight the Sabbat there than the Camarilla. It's not exactly clear when the anarchs took control, but it intersects with the time of Daliah's embrace in 1982. I am going to say the anarchs made their move soon after the 1980 Mariel boatlift of Cuban refugees and managed to "win" just before Miami Vice TV show came on the air in September 1984. Miami at this time had the combination of prosperity and high crime that LA was also known for. A potential Miami Free State on the east coast would be a huge win for the anarchs, and I imagine a lot of help is being sent this way. Miami would seem to be a big draw for Smiling Jack as it is near his old Caribbean pirate grounds.

                                A World of Darkness (both editions) mentions that the anarchs are strong in London, and that Manchster/Liverpool (one domain since the '70s) is ruled by a Brujah Prince. There was an attempted anarch uprising in Birmingham in the '70s that was ruthlessly crushed. Toreador-rule Edinburgh allows anarchs to reside openly, but it is assumed the Toreador there could crush them if they wanted. The Anarch Cookbook mentions Liverpool is a "strong haven" for the anarchs. They are probably protected (and cynically used) by the Brujah Prince. I imagine Robin Leelend is active there. Belfast is mentioned as a contested city between anarchs (Brujah, Gangrel, Malkavian) and Ventrue. Supporting their brethren in the British Isles is probably an important agenda for the Anarch Council even though there is no Free State there. If they are ever able to establish a full blown Free State in the British Isles, it'll open up opportunities for them elsewhere in Europe.

                                Anarchs are mentioned in Paris, but don't seem much of a threat. Assuming these are local anarchs with little help from the Council. The greater potential in Britain probably appears as a better bet. There is an anarch insurgency in Scandinavia, but this is probably more to do with the Valkyries rising from torpor, and while they do not approve of the Camarilla, they really aren't anarchs. Anarchs are mentioned in Spain, but don't seem to be doing much. It is often mentioned in Europe that many Sabbat disguise themselves as anarchs. The main story in mainland Europe seems to be that the anarchs are present, but is a problem that is being controlled.

                                The Anarch Cookbook mentions a recent upheaval in Panama and a three way struggle between anarchs, Camarilla, and Sabbat fighting it with their retainers. It is probably a reference to the the 1989/1990 US invasion that ousted General Noriega. Needless to say control over the territory around the Panama Canal would have a lot of positive impact especially in terms of support from LA to the US east coast and Europe.

                                There isn't much talk of anarchs elsewhere. That doesn't mean there are not young vampires who are classified as anarchs. Just that they are probably weak and powerless.

                                Still, this gives us a good outline of the Anarch Council's strategy. Continue to disrupt the West Coast. Establish a foothold in Australia that might one day liberate that continent. Support the establishment of an east coast Free State around Miami. Help the anarchs in the British Isle. Try to control Panama so they can better support the anarchs around the Atlantic. PCs who want to hear that the anarch leaders are doing something will be gratified to learn that they are.

                                                Plans for the actual chronicle are near culmination for me. Final character creation and preludes are coming soon. So I am focusing more on actual chronicle plot needs and such than laying out the sandbox. One player has stated he wants to be involved in the Movement itself which is not surprising since the focus is on the Anarch Free States. However, there is a huge difference between a PC dwelling and doing things in the Anarch Free States and a PC being involved in the Anarch Movement. The Movement has already succeeded in LA so there is little to do there. The Movement is mainly concerned with helping anarchs in other cities - places where the characters are not at. I'm not running a chronicle in some other city, but in LA.

                                                So I need to figure out opportunities for this character while keeping the game in LA. Since the Anarch Council is the organ that supports the Movement, I am putting it here on this thread.

                                                I already have some ideas in mind.

                                                I'm obviously going to ask the PC how he thinks he can contribute. He'll have to sell himself to the important anarchs on the Council. Hopefully he can come up with some ideas of him being in LA and still being useful. There's always the possibility the PC can make use of certain Backgrounds or Disciplines or backstory that can be done "out of area". I'll have to see if that comes to anything.

                                                The Anarch Council aren't going to utilize just anyone. They'll want people who show they are competent and loyal. So the PC will need to spend some time showcasing they are and providing bonafides. Eventually he'll succeed. While I will be putting most of this burden onto the PC, I will probably need to provide some OOC advice. So if you have any suggestions, I'd love to hear them.

                                                I'm also going to utilize the Vampire Club in San Francisco extensively. It's one reason why it exists and I created its own thread. It won't be hard for the PC to visit and the other PCs can easily piggyback on the visit so the entire player coterie is together. It's an ideal spot for meeting other characters, making deals, and collecting information. It won't put PCs into a dangerous spot as if they visited other domains more hostile to anarchs. Whether by planes, trains, or automobiles, they can leave LA, arrive there, and make it back over a weekend (or perhaps even one night). I have some intrigue already in mind, but if anyone wants to add any "one game session plots" I'd love to hear them.

                                                There are also things to do in LA itself with the "maintenance" of the domain. Spies, interlopers, raiders/saboteurs from the Camarilla, Sabbat, or even Independent Clans are a present threat. This is complicated because the Free States are not about having enforcement. You can't hook up with the Sheriff, Harpies, or Scourge because there are not any, and any attempt to do their tasks openly would not be acceptable to the anarchs. However, there is always opportunities for low key, subtle, and unofficial actions. Whether the player realizes the hypocrisy in that, I don't know. Again, if people have ideas, particularly of the "one game session plots", I'm listening.

                                                There can also be things that are less obtrusive or less like enforcing the Six Traditions. New anarchs show up all the time in LA, often with zero knowledge about the city. In the setting itself, there is something like a welcome wagon at A Taste of LA. This is fairly bare bones though, and possibly the PC could do better.
