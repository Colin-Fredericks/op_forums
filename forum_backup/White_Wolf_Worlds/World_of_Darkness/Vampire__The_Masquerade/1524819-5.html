    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    With the "voice of a thin-blood stuck in your head" thing for helping to handle exposition, I think fans of the BL1 Malk play would be really disappointed with Malks in BL 2. Either they lose uniqueness because it's basically the same but the Malk is less certain the voice isn't just something they made up, or they get overburdened by trying to have both said voice and other things that demonstrate madness in some fashion.
    Yeah, the presence of a voice in your head that is objectively real (because it appears in other playthroughs) makes having Malkavian-specific delusions/hallucinations very weird. And not in a good way.

    Like, how does it even work? Is the Thin Blood in your head also cognizant of the Malk PC's warped perceptions? Will there be lots of incidents of the Thin Blood acting as a minder for the PC, keeping them on task and yanking them out of fugue states? Is that even desirable for a Malkavian playthrough, since the big breaks from reality were a core part of what made BL1's Malk playthrough so fun? At least when you're alone and mad, there's lots of interesting ways to mess with the player that can't really be objectively refuted. The subjectivity is part of the draw, and what makes a Malk game unique.

    If I had to guess, though, I'd say if the game DID get a Malkavian option, the Thin Blood's dialogue would almost never reflect the change (because implementing such radical narrative differences would take lots of development time the game obviously doesn't have). The game would treat it as if it's a normal playthrough, aside from very specific moments. Which misses the point of having a Malk option in a Bloodlines game.

    Either that, or it'll be a DLC that is a very short, contained experienced. Simply to avoid having to remake the main campaign from scratch. Which ALSO would be missing the point of having a Malk option in a Bloodlines game.

            The next trailer will be revealed on January 31st.

            But before that I think the latest Dev-Diary gives us a good insight into what kind of game Bloodlines 2 actually is.

            First, it mentions these three playstyles. The examples it chooses are a bit... wild but okay.

            The first is "Strategic Stalker" which mentions Dishonored and doing stealth-builds in Fallout or Elder Scrolls. The second part is an interesting choice because stealth sucks in Bethesda games and is tied to very rudimentary systems (stealth-damage, detection-reduction... stuff like that). Dishonored meanwhile is an actual stealth-game based on sandbox-maps where special powers are available to the player to take out guards in multiple ways, find multiple ways to traverse the map and there's a bit of an expanded stealth-system. But I guess the main-takeaway here is that the game has a stealth-system. We'll see whether it's closer to a Bethesda-game or Dishonored.

            The second is "Action Brawler" and the games that are mentioned are God of War, Shadow of Mordor and Elden Ring. Hmm, now those are three very different types of action-games... Who knows what type of combat-system it exactly is but of course the first issue is definitely that all three of these examples are third-person-perspective games while Bloddlines 2 isn't one. And then there's of course the question of just how many tools you get to use in combats like moves, powers and whatnot as well as how much there is to the parry/dodge-mechanic.

            The third type is about narrative and the only thing that this sentence kinda sticks out: "We envision that a core of the Bloodlines 1 fans identify with this playstyle and also fans of TCR's titles to date (and a lot of the developers working on the game), so we’re making sure the gameplay pillar doesn’t add action at the detriment of these important players."

            But I think the most revealing bit comes at the very end:

            So, how do we go about achieving this? We focus on immersion over complexity: A streamlined control scheme with actions that are easy for all three playstyles to pick up and play. We then build depth on top of these to give longer-term mastery for the players that want it. For example, the defensive mechanic is a dash, allowing players to dodge incoming attacks or reposition. If dodge is used towards an enemy performing a melee attack, it becomes a counter, staggering the enemy - it is fine for players to never do this move, but for those that want a higher skill-ceiling, it is there. How we use abilities is also streamlined, to maintain immersion. Abilities are mapped to the face buttons (on gamepad), limiting the player to 4 available at one time. This is a small enough amount that a player can use them without breaking their flow, but also deep enough, through the different ways you can combine and use them that it keeps combat engaging and fun.
            "Immersion over complexity"... Hmm... Also, this indicates there's an expectation that you're gonna use a controller to play this game.

            And later on, I thought that was a bit of a weird thing to put in there: "As an Elder vampire, Phyre has become desensitised to violence, and we want the same to happen to the player..." You... do?

                                The gameplay-trailer is out. It shows once again the Willhem-scene but now it's the full scene and not just the dialogue. What it certainly emphasizes is that what we've seen before was indeed pre-alpha-footage.

                                The gameplay looks okay. And we've finally got to see the UI! But it doesn't look good that we're still returning to the same scene that has been shown as a "reveal" three times at this point.

                                Some other comments:
                                • Considering this is the Chinese Room we're talking about and the illusion-stuff in that section and the main-villain being a Malkavian... I wonder just how often this game will turn into these types of walking-simulator-horror-games with simple puzzles and reliance on these visual effects where you go into a room and when you turn around the room changes, that sort of stuff.
                                • The animations don't look great in combat. Also, I wonder with movement-abilities like the dash, first-person-perspective always feels bad when you get swarmed by enemies. It doesn't seem that outside of powers you get anything more than light and heavy punch and if combat only happens in restricted small areas like in this preview I guess, it won't be that big of an issue.
                                • At least it doesn't seem like you can get interrupted when you do these Feeding-scenes because of the way the abilities and health work, it seems like you will be doing quite a few of those in each combat. Hopefully, there's some variety to the animations.
                                • The new dialogue shown off with Willem where he has a bomb strapped to him seems like a typical dialogue-tree-puzzle where you have to choose the right option a couple of times based on some social deduction and stuff you learn in the sequence before to convince Willem not to blow himself up.
                                • Phyre was described in the interview afterwards and there was again this talk of "leaning into the legend, lying about it etc." but honestly the descriptions just reminded me of how Shepard and their backstory was treated in Mass Effect. It's this cipher of a character that while treated like someone who already is part of the world, the emphasis is far more on what the character is doing after the game has started. I guess we'll see when they talk about what they call "social feeding" and how complex the roleplaying gets. Otherwise, you really should just pick the options in the Willem-scene that convince him to not blow himself up.

                                Overall, I feel better about the game now than before but I'm still not convinced it won't have some adverse effect on something in this game that this is supposed to come out in less than a year. In a certain way, we've been following the active development of this Willem-scene as the dialogue-scene gets better facial animations and gets integrated into an actual game-sequence. I know, game-development is cutting it really close these days (lengthy crunch periods, day-one patches, misleading trailers etc.) when it comes to release-dates, though. Hopefully, this won't be one of those games that is launched in a broken state and instead of working on the promised DLC, the Chinese Room studio first has to fix all the problems and issues with the game (because releasing the game was deemed more important than properly finishing development).

                                        I think the only thing people may not like is that this section didn't give off major RPG-vibes. It was essentially a linear section with some atmospherical walking-sections, some combat-arenas, a basic puzzle and then a dialogue-tree-challenge that decides whether Willem blows himself up or not.

                                        As someone who's a videogamer and plays a lot, there was nothing in that trailer that impressed me. That atmospheric walking-stuff and environmental storytelling-stuff... neat but I wished it would then lead to something more involved than that sound-puzzle they have there. I mean, Arkham Asylum came out in 2009. I get it, Detective Sense is a neat little thing but at this point I want to see something more interesting than running around a room chasing a sound. At least, they didn't make it worse by having enemies attack you. But it's still just boring busywork to repeatedly have to run around and press F on that grammophon. Just do a cutscene instead where the character relays his backstory instead of me having to move around that room chasing that music. There isn't even interesting environmental storytelling happening like changing the room to reflect parts of the story. But of course that stuff would require time to do that - which Chinese Room don't have.

                                        Combat similarly... not impressive. If light punch, heavy punch, dodge and feed is all I'm doing this is gonna get boring fast. Rewatching it, I almost felt like I would've preferred to see more enemies plus setting the abilities on a time-countdown rather than being tied to feeding. Because there was nothing tactical going on there (obviously since there are so few tools available and the enemies were all melee-oriented), it's more about the spectacle than anything else and so at that point why not have the character do these dashes every couple of seconds, for example? And you are playing an Elder after all with additional special abilities like telekinesis.

                                        Another big problem, though, is that this game is absolutely not reflective of what V5 focuses on. So an issue I see here is people who buy this game and think this is what it's like to play V5 and then they join an online-group or pick up the corebook and then find out that the TTRPG is very different.
