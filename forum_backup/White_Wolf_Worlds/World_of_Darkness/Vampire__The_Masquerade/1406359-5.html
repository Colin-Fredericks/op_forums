            CT Phipps on Soviet Russia

            In Soviet Russia, Brujah oppress Ventrue!

            I feel the need to take a moment to share my "Ten Tips for running the Anarchs in V5" list. http://unitedfederationofcharles.blo...g-anarchs.html
            One of the things that I talked about there was the fact that I believed there was a substantial number of Vampire: The Masquerade players who disdained the Anarchs because they were the "good" faction in V:TM. One of the chief appeals of this game is the option of playing a "bad guy" with options ranging from being an Anti-hero like Wolverine and Ghost Rider to being a full-blown villain protagonist who eats humans like Pez.

            The Anarchs are not the good-guys of course, though you're probably going to find SLIGHTLY more High Humanity vampires among them than in the Camarilla's upper echelons. However, what makes the Anarchs interesting is that they're just as likely to be scumbags that terrify people as they are to be Maldavis Civil Rights activists. The Lost Boys are often talked about as a Sabbat pack but they're really just as likely to be Brujah Anarchs, riding up and down the boardwalk and murdering at will because they don't respect the Prince.

            The use of the Soviet Union as the ultimate example of an Anarch Free State is a very good choice because it nicely shows that a bunch of Anarchs are interested solely in making themselves the top dogs of Kindred society versus any societal reform. Even then, I think that may be overstating things for the article. The Soviet Union was a lot of things to a lot of people and while as a Christian anarchist, it was pretty much my idea of hell, I imagine the Brujah of it all had various ideas for what it should be.

            * Brujah who wanted to use it as the basis for a equalized post-capitalism society across the world.
            * Brujah who saw it as a way to establish vampire superiority over Kine.
            * Brujah who saw it as a nightmarish Empire and in need of overthrowing, especially Ukrainians.
            * Brujah who might give two shits about Kine but want a equalist society among vampires.
            * Brujah who saw it as the ultimate piggy bank for funding overthrows of vampire societies across the globe (mortals as well).

            Mind you, the 21st century makes it seem like the Brujah Council has basically reformed itself in the former Soviet Union and is back to its old tricks in the Russian Federation. I'm imagining that the "New" Anarch Free State probably has a few more Ventrue, Toreador, Tremere, and the like but they're all publicly Anarchs. Just the oligarch police state kind that are different from the Camarilla because, "fuck you, we say we are."

            And if you think they'd join the Camarilla because of this, I'll laugh at you because the Brujah Council ruled as much territory at its height as the Camarilla ever did.

            As for Baba Yaga? Well, would YOU admit that 80% of your society was wiped out by the Wicked Witch of the West? A children's story?

            This sounds like an articulation of the view of many Camarilla elders and leaders, and even the philosophy of the characters of LA by Night, given their involvement in the nightclub massacre.
            Victoria Ash was having a bad day it seemed.

                    The Nights that Shook the World doesn't really say how much freedom, necessities and amenities the kindred of the Soviet Union had, or how they were organised. If we take Domasheva's word for it, it might have been a fair and plentiful society for kindred. She might have been part of a more priveliged class within that society if it wasn't. For a start, she evidently lived in Moscow, which has to be preferable to gulags, even if gulags have more accesssible food per kindred.

                    I'd love to see a book about this or even a series. I've considered:
                    • New Wave Masquerade. Sort of like New Wave Requiem, it would be an immediate prequel to the first VtM books. I picture it more focussed on America, but this could be when the Brujah Council is really falling apart.
                    • A Cold War book about the time when there were effectively four sects: the Camarilla, Sabbat, Anarch Free States (as opposed to Anarch subjects of princes) and the Brujah Council. This could start in 1940s, possibly covering the early Anarch Free States of California and post-war Europe. Maybe it could feature the odd cross-sect nuclear apocalypse conspiracy.
                    • A book starting with the birth of the Brujah council. This could show the Brujah Council when some of them gave a damn about kine and how they overcame the old regime of the Camarilla, Sabbat or whoever else was there.
