                            I haven't read this book and don't read/play V5 so take all my comments with a grain of salt. But it sounds like this is typical Justin Achilli, who is an interesting writer in some respects (he understands the characters are vampires, shows a flair for the gothic and horror, and adds enough nice touches) but has a big problem of getting the subject matter wrong by reducing a complex subject to a single thing that interests him. Thus his Giovanni clanbook took a family of decadent Venetian merchants and necromancers (modern Borgias as someone once put it) into a bunch of Sicilian mafioso goombas because he really liked Goodfellas. When he took over Vampire in Revised he turned everything into one big sect war. And he could only think of the Sabbat as a monolithic terrorist vampires, which he's repeated here, because that is what fit his sect wars concept. I think he'd be a much better Storyteller for an individual chronicle than a gameline Developer or writer. He tends to have some good ideas, but just discards or ignores anything that he isn't into. That's fine when STing a chronicle. It's bad as a setting sourcebook. (To be fair to Justin, this book is likely work for hire so he's working under the editorially mandated direction of someone else.)

                            I think there are three aspects of the Sabbat that need to be addressed in any new sourcebook. And that is understanding the difference between founded covens in a Sabbat domain, the role and nature of nomadic packs, and the Black Hand.

                            If you want to tell a story of the Sabbat as a group of ideologically committed inhuman terrorists who embark on Sabbat Crusades, it's essentially a Black Hand chronicle. That's the group within the Sabbat that fulfills that function. And it's probably the major reason non-Sabbat call the sect itself by the name of "Black Hand". That's based on their own personal experience with Sabbat invasions and think what they learned about them applies to the whole sect. But the rest of the sect is not that. You don't need to turn the rest of the sect into that in order to run such a chronicle or have that element be present in the setting.

                            Founded covens is where you have a more typical domain where there is a ruler (Archbishop) of a city ruling it over other vampires. But instead of the Traditions, you have the Code of Milan, the various ritae, and other guides on how the sovereign can rule. This is where we'd see the Sabbat as a ideologically diverse sect going over conflicts based on rival Paths of Enlightenment, discontent over the sect's political direction as Loyalists clash with Moderates, Ultraconservatives, and others, and the fact that a sect founded about freedom of elders are now ruled by elders since the original anarchs are now centuries old and the war of ages is taking place here as well. Sabbat domains should be darker than Camarilla ones, but there should still be a great amount of diversity of how that is implemented to reflect the personality of the archbishop and the various politics and Paths of the vampires and covens in the domain. Yeah, covens may stand up and travel to another city when the conditions are right and a grand Crusade is called. But they normally don't do that and aren't trained for it any more than any other vampire.

                            Lastly there are the nomad packs which have always gotten a short shrift. These are the Sabbat vampires other vampires are most likely to encounter and survive. Non-Sabbat don't travel to Sabbat domains. And if the Black Hand shows up, they'll likely destroy you if they encounter you. But a nomad pack temporarily visiting a place and leaving might have any number of encounters with other vampires - Camarilla, Anarch, Independent. Wonderful tool for the ST. I imagine this is where the bulk of the Loyalists lie as the vampires here mostly operate outside the strictures of the Sabbat hierarchy and offers the best opportunity for expression of Sabbat freedom for a vampire to do whatever they want. They should have a very different culture than the founded covens. And a lot of the oddball concepts of the Sabbat likely fit here. These are often the fiddly bits Justin Achilli purged in Revised and V5 seems to repeat. All those Sabbat from the Native American community? Likely here. Ahrimanes? Likely here. Adherents of the Path of Harmony (or even Humanity?) likely here. At the same time, you still have nomad packs of all Paths and political persuasions. But a conservative Nomad pack whose members may be on some of the darker Paths are still differently from a founded coven because of the freedom or the road and the challenges all nomad packs face - the danger of travelling through unknown and hostile territory filled with Camarilla archons like the Wolfpack, Lupines, and local hunters of vampires. This has to forge a different Sabbat culture than the other two groups. They'd likely be the most common reinforcements that arrive into a city when a Crusade is called because they normally do travel and have probably honed combat skills as a result.

                            The original Sabbat books did not go into these details, but you could tease them out and figure out how to do it on your own. But it's been woefully neglected because Justin decided for whatever reason that he wanted to turn the entire sect into dedicated Black Hand packs who only lived to produce Crusades.

                            I would love to see a Sabbat book that treated this differences seriously and gave STs a toolkit on running different chronicle styles for their players. This would leave a very different kind of game than one centered around the Camarilla or even Anarchs.

                                            As much as I want to state that I am disappointed at the length and simplicity of this book (it would be 5 stars if it had included the complexity it deserved), I feel like you are inaccurately describing Justin Achilli and how he treated both the Giovanni and the Sabbat. Justin Achilli is the guy for which the Giovanni were stated NOT to be the mafia. Indeed, Justin Achilli said that the only Italian gangsters in the Clan were the Pisanob and didn't even want to include them but felt like he had to for the sake of those who enjoyed them.

                                            (If you read CLAN NOVEL: GIOVANNI, Justin has the mafia Giovanni openly mocked as a moron who is only playing at being evil compared to Isabel and the other "real" Giovanni)

                                            As Justin said something akin to, "Why would the Giovanni, a multibillion dollar banking clan, want to focus their efforts on a multionaire organization like the mafia?" He created the subfamilies of the Giovanni and expanded them beyond a single family line of Italians into a vast multiracial network of businessmen, criminals, and more.

                                            Indeed, the big thing that Justin Achilli did was that he was an early pioneer of BLACK DOG style writing at White Wolf. Specifically, Justin believed the Giovanni (and later Sabbat) should not just be "Bad" but genuinely evil. Part of what Justin Achilli's design ethos was, was the fact that he felt Vampire: The Masquerade had gotten way too tame. It had been a bit like Warhammer 40K in that it felt like it was appealing to teenage boys and adolescents rather than the dark adult audience he wanted.

                                            His fingerprints were all over Revised (no kidding)

                                            * The Camarilla being shown to be every bit as vile as the Sabbat in many ways. Scourges, persecuting Thin Bloods, and so on.
                                            * The Sabbat being Low Humanity as a way to emphasize they were rotten bastards. Having them go from amorality to overtly claiming to be evil monsters.
                                            * A focus on the gross incestuous, child-abusing, monstrous nature of the Giovanni.
                                            * Making the Thin Bloods the "only" vampires that could actually be "friendly" good guys.
                                            * Showing the Anarchs to be their own organized and hypocritical sect.

                                            I feel V5 is very much something that was influenced by his take on Vampire and Revised in particular. Except it decided to dial back the Sect Warfare element significantly and go more like Requiem and 1st Edition.
