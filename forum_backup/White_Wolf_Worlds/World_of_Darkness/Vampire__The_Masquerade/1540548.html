    [V20] Mixed feelings about a house rule - Special Ammunition deals Lethal

    08-16-2024, 11:58 PM
    So!

    With the subject of my previous post still ongoing, for a long time, I come here to ask for opinions on a house rule that I found... confunding. Tbc. I am not searching for affirmation directly, but there is something about it that feels off.

    Context: The game this is a rule in is very high in xp count. (Few characters have less than 200 XP, more than a few have 300-400 XP), some of which are explicitly combat built. The opposition amounts to various elders, including a voivode and salubri warrior who has fought alongside Adonain and is 6th gen etc.

    Essentially, it has come to a point where most combat encounters are over quite quickly because most people roll 10+dice to hit and nearly all combat characters have access to claws or similar.

    The house rule in question:

    "
    Non-Standard Ammunition

    Staff is ruling that incendiary ammunition will not deal aggravated damage upon successful impact. Instead, it will inflict Lethal damage.

    "

    Within that context seems incredibly odd to me.

    Opinions?

      Off the top of my head, the damage potential was nerfed so that the players had a higher chance of survival while engaging in heavy combat, which in turn means they can survive long enough to accumulate that much XP.

              On the other hand, a 50 BMG round more or less decapitates with a headshot, regardless of if you are using non-standard ammunition. So, I would be willing to bet that not only the game mechanics have been nerfed, but combat has been scripted in such a way that it isn't as dangerous.

              A few ear marks of this sort of thing are:
              1. Combat area terrain doesn't factor into the fighting
              2. No one uses snipers or remote-control bombs
              3. Long range combat is being at opposite ends of the same room rather than being in a different building or city
              4. Combat feels more like a cinematic moment of badassery rather than a tactical slaughter achieved by teamwork
              5. Enemy NPCs always fight to the death, rather than running away and creating counter measures for next time
              6. Enemies that require specialty disciplines to deal with aren't really used (Ghosts & Necromancy)
              7. Enemies rarely if ever use non-combat strategies to deal with the PCs despite direct combat not working out for them
              8. Mortals have yet to figure out that Amazon.com exists and thermal sensor attachments for smart phones are only about $300
              9. Conveniently mortals aren't around during combat, so everyone can go full fangs out, rather than the edge going to whomever has the best subtle or stealth combat build.
              10. The ST runs VtM with a battle mat despite being Theater of the Mind, they use gummy bears or candies to represent the enemy NPCs, when you kill the npc in game the player gets to eat the candy IRL. This is very fun, but can backfire horrendously.

                                              Without getting too technical (or gross because terminal ballistics is a messy subject, no pun intended) the objective wounding ability of bullets depends on the physical projectile crushing/tearing tissue with its mass or permanent cavity, and possibly some related effects from temporary cavitatoin (the 'shockwave' for lack of a better term although that isn't exact terminology). There's more things that could contribute but are products of lengthy and contentious debates that really won't matter to many supernaturals (hydrodynamic shock may or may not be a thing, for example. You can get into long flamewars over that alone.) For most non deforming bullets the dimensions of the bullet determine permanent cavity either diameter (if it doesn't tumble) or length (if it does - some rifle bullets tumble.) Hollow point/expanding munitions can expand up to several times the diameter (or more, alot of it depends on the design and balance between depth of penetration and wound size.) You can also have bullets that fragment by design (either intentionally or unintentionally... 5.56 NATO rounds for the original M16 unintentionally fragmented due to peculiarities in design and the velocity of the round) Fragmenting rounds tend to create the largest and messiest wound cavities (lots of small fragments lacerating flesh at high speed, akin to a shotgun blast)

                                              Bullets will not always be instantly fatal for a person but they should be potentially incapacitating or nearly so (a person WITH a gunshot may not instantly stop for various factors, but probably will be incapacitated in a short time - due to blood loss.) - game rules should simulate this accurately for all mortals (you might houserule that mortals hit by such ammo get -1 or -2 to dice rolls to simulate expanding or fragmenting rounds being more effective at causing injury, thus more damage successes) Anything similarly organic or humanoid is probably similar, although other mechanics could make them tougher.

                                              With Vampires I'd say it depends on hit location (heart or head) to be lethal rather than bludgeoning, OR using certain kinds of fragmenting ammo (again shredding huge holes in the torso shotgun-style.. expanding rounds may not make holes as big as a fragmenting round.) Unless a vampire is wearing body armor (its pretty easy to get body armor that can protect against most FMJ rounds that might fragment inside the body) Incendiary could be aggravated in the sense that 'fire hurts vampires' so a burning bullet might inhibit healing, but typically most incendiary bullets IRL don't have enough burning material for that to be damaging in and of itself. Even the so called 'dragon's breath' shotgun rounds are grossly exaggerated (look pretty, very little fuel in the shell to burn stuff.. burning is a very energy intensive process) If you allow for a degree of greater tech advancement over modern (not unreasonable) you might be able to handwave incendiary as being more 'burny' than they are IRL though and I've always tended to assume that.

                                                      Given the Technocracy is a thing (even to an extent in VtM) you could surmise that there is a degree of more advanced 'incendiary' technology that can burn alot more severly than RL incendiary bullets. Maybe some sort of super-indencidary or a miniaturized thermobaric (which part blast AND thermal, but I digress.)

                                                      Another RL possibility is that they're using High Density Reactive Metal bullets of some kind. Basically its possible to use HDRMs as structural material for some kinds of munitions (like missiles and artillery shells) and they add extra energy to the payload rather than inert metal - although that energy is more thermal/incendiary than blast. That probably would work here. IIRC HDRMs might be several times the (non-explosive) energy of TNT or equivalent and if you have a 10-12 gram bullet (around 10mm or .45 ACP) that is over half HDRM it would be equivalent to maybe 10-20 grams of thermite or so (these are off the cuff guesstimates mind, I'm not doing thorough research to back it up).

                                                      Another contributing factor (at least for vampires and other undead) is that they may be more 'flammable' due to a lower moisture content - part of burning/igniting the flammable parts of a person (like fat) requires boiling off alot of the water content and the less water there is in the body, the easier it would be to ignite/burn. Same principle with trying to set wood with a high moisture content on fire vs wood that is dried out, now that I think about it.

                                                          All the talk about how the ammo could be made Agg means nothing if the ST already decided that it will cause Lethal. And irl incendiary rounds really don't burn a lot besides themselves, so there's no arguing for realism.

                                                          What you're facing is a general problem with Vampire, and to a lesser degree Werewolf, where the system pays a lot of attention to melee combat and nothing to firearms.

                                                          Sincerely, what you need is a healthy talk to your ST. You have to point how you're feeling about the effectiveness of your approach to combat, and that you wish you could be more effective without just becoming one more melee brick.

                                                          For how to materialize a more effective firearms combat, I suggest you go after magic. While VtM never put in place actual rules for making magic items, it's implied they exist in the setting, and other Splats do have them.

                                                          You can search for a fetish, a wonder, even the odd Treasure that deals Agg. The search may be an interesting story on its own. But your ST have to be on board.
