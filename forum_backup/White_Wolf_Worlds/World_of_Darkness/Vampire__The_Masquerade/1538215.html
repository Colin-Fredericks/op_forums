Unless we are modeling Strahd using Van Richtens guide to Vampires for the "elder" vampire stats and powers which is a whole another kettle of fish.

          It is worth noting that Vampires range in power depending on edition of D&D used. For instance, back in 2nd Edition AD&D, vampires had a touch attack called "level drain" which permanently destroyed xp and lowered your level per touch attack with no limit of use. The only way to get it back was to either get a specific high level priest spell cast on you or you had to re-grind all the xp to regain the levels. Being an undead hunter in 2nd Edition was bit more hardcore than other game systems.

          Off the top of my head, a VtM version of level drain would probably be something that causes permanent Willpower loss on top of ability/attribute dot loss. Something along the lines of "Rune of Singular Hate" from the exalted sorcery comes to mind.

                  Strahd Von Zarovich is a very terrifying character in D&D. After looking at his stats throughout the editions of the game, I've come up with the following notes:
                  • Strahd has around 4 dots in all physical attributes; regardless of edition. Likewise his Intelligence is straight up maxed to 5.
                  • He would possess 5 dots in Protean, at least 3 into Animalism and Presence, and at least 2 into Dominate.
                  • Dude is a 9th-level spellcaster in nearly all editions; meaning he would possess several dots in various forms of Thaumaturgy, Necromancy, and other forms of blood magics.
                  • He would have flaws relating to his weakness to invitations and running water. The water in particular would be similar to being in sunlight for him as is the case for all vampires in D&D.
                  • Dude is straight up an Old-Clan Tzimisce, as he is bound to Ravenloft like Tzimisces are bound to their place of death or birth. That said, Caitiff also works as he doesn't have the traditional need to sleep in dirt.
                  While vampires in previous editions have "energy drain" effects, this doesn't translate well into VTM and was more of a mechanic rather than traditional lore. Considering he is not a descendent of Caine, he may not have a traditional Generation stat. Due to how the Shadowfell works, Strahd is both ancient and young at the same time. For him, only a few centuries have past; to the rest of the multiverse, millennia have gone by. As a result, any Generation actually works (although I do suggest it be as low as possible).

                                                          Mechanical conversions of Strahd seem boring to me. D&D and VtM have very different mechanics, and what you would give a vampire meant to be the final boss in an adventure in D&D is very different than what you would give an elder NPC for VtM.

                                                          What is more interesting is deciding how to make him fit into the setting which is very different. What's cool is his motivations and personal history. That's why people like the character. He has gravitas and he's tragic despite being an absolute badass and monster.

                                                          First, let's acknowledge Strahd is just Dracula by another name (and purposefully so). He was a nobleman who lead his people in war against an invading enemy and later cursed to be a vampire. That wasn't a problem for Ravenloft. But the World of Darkness already has a Dracula so much of his backstory is already taken. I'd probably just make him a member of the Wallachian boyars who served under Vlad and fought for him against the Turks. Vlad is probably his sire, giving him the embrace soon after he became a vampire so that he had a trusted ally.

                                                          Second, we can't use the name because it's nonsense. Strahd is made up. "Von" denotes a German noble, but Zarovich sounds like something Slavic. Perfectly fine nonsense name for D&D. Doesn't make sense in the WoD which is our world. The best thing is probably to just create a brand new Romanian name that passes the smell test. However, I could be fine with the character having a "vampire name" like "Strahd". After all, what kind of name is "Lodin"? But if I was ever going to introduce this character into a chronicle I ran, I wouldn't use that name at all.

                                                          Third, the key to Strahd is the origin involving his younger, handsome brother Sergei and the young girl he loves, Tatiana, and Strahd being a middle aged man who spent his entire life in war now wanting the youth and love he gave up. That can be kept entirely with little change. Except that after killing his brother, declaring his love for Tatiana, she committing suicide in grief, and getting killed himself, it was Vlad who turned him into a vampire because he just couldn't see his friend die like that.

                                                          Fourth, he obviously needs some kind of Flaw that includes the ghost of his brother and his search for the reincarnated Tatiana (which may simply be a case of a lookalike rather than actual reincarnation). He desperately wants to find a new "Tatiana" and embrace her so she'll love him and be his for eternity. And he is continually thwarted by the ghost of Sergei helping hunters protect "Tatiana". Whether "Sergei" is actually a wraith or something else, I'll leave to the ST.

                                                          Fifth, in terms of sects, he's mostly likely a member of the Old Clan Tzimisce in the Oradea League as others have said, but he could also be at home in the Inconnu or True Black Hand. Probably should have been a student in the Scholomance like Dracula. Koldunic Sorcery plus some well known, ancient Paths is fine.

                                                          Sixth, his pacts with the Vistani are probably best done with some Ravnos sorcerer who has Ravnos childer who wander the world and report back to grandma if they ever find someone who looks like Tatiana. These Ravnos could very well have been Roma when mortal, but that's not necessary. The head Ravnos likely owe's Strahd some lifeboon that he can call it in for something like this - it binds her and her childer and further lineage, but it is so inconsequential a favor even in perpetuity it's not much of a burden.

                                                          Seventh, instead of living alone like Dracula or having brides, I'd probably just give him a household staff of ghouls or dominated servants. They keep up the place in case any mortals find their way there, and they go with him when he voyages to somewhere else. To explain why he was not bothered during the Communist era, I'd probably just give him some high level of Obfuscate that confuses people as to whether that castle is inhabited.

                                                          Instead of making him some kind of prisoner in his domain, I'd just have that it prefers to dwell there. Like Dracula, he's prepared to leave his castle and travel the world if it fits his plans (find Tatiana). If I'm going to use him in a chronicle, I'll want him to be able to come to my chronicle's city so the PCs can interact with him. Well known that he's the childe of Dracula himself and that he has never sired another vampire. I'd also make it known he's single handedly destroyed several Sabbat packs who attacked him over the years. That will establish his fighting cred, and it makes sense that a solitary Old Clan Tzimisce who mostly stays in the same place would eventually attract Sabbat packs to diablerize him.
