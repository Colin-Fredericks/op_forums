    Unless you're tying this all together with some kind of theory of Metamorphosis that explains it all, it paints the picture of someone who doesn't really know *what* she's trying to achieve, just throwing everything at the wall to see what sticks.
    Which isn't by itself a bad concept, either, nor makes her a weak character. It's just a concept that don't mesh well with lots of dots on those disparate Disciplines, because to have lots of dots in a Discipline takes commitment, and someone with this mindset isn't going to commit so much to something they're not so sure about.

    And the idea of her being a serial diablerist does work if the goal itself is to have lots of Discipline dots just 'cause, but then it would be interesting to know how she became so good at it and what that implies to her life and overall situation. Is she persecuted? Is she having trouble even within the Sabbat, or does she have enough clout to get away with recurring murder? Or does she focus on killing Camarilla vampires? Just keep in mind that even with Diablerie, this kind of mystical benefit usually means taking down powerful targets, preferably just as powerful or more than herself, otherwise the only benefits are blood and satisfying her addiction. By the way, is she addict to diablerie? If not, how did she avoid that? Diablerie may even be her research project to achieve Azhi Dahaka, she just need a theory behind it, a reason to think it will work.

    No answer is right or wrong, as long as there's a story.

                                            Velya, to begin with, "only" has Vicissitude 7, so entirely within reach of a 6th Gen. And the effect doesn't seem more impressive, it seems to be pretty much the same.

                                            Also of note, Velya's write-up doesn't exactly imply this feat to be excepcional in execution, and even the consequences are more tied to the fact that Cassidy was already a Wight when they conjoined (not to mention that she was a poor choice overall, she was an "evil", overly destructive child, her descent into wightdom was a given, really).

                                            Unless the conjoining gives any benefit at all that can't be surmised by other traits (like giving greater Strength, but "actually" bought like normal), there's no reason to think this can be done with a Vicissitude 6 power, or even just with normal levels of Vicissitude, if coupled with a lot of Medicine and Fleshcraft rolls. Mechanically the most that can happen is that the subsumed characters may still have their own actions (although probably a very limited range of actions), but still acting on their own.

                                            Overall the idea with Velya isn't that conjoining was a challenge, but just that it was overall a bad idea. This is totally doable here, with less impact (but still some impact) since no Wights are involved. Yet. Of course, she also has to feed them individually, even if they're limited only to the Blood needed to awake each night.

                                            Originally posted by Samuelalpha86 View Post
                                            well, there's a combination discipline called Komnenos' Honor which helps with staying on a path.
                                            That isn't strictly necessary if you can explain her Path Rating in other ways. We have to first determine how the other minds affect her, how this may affect her Path (or not), and what other strategies she may have to deal with the problem and still advance in her Path. We also have to determine her Path Rating, as the lowest it is, the easier it is to maintain. Just because she's reaching for apotheosis doesn't means she's currently being as successful as she would like or even believe. In the end, Paths are hard, and they're supposed to be. Most vampires aren't very high even when they're dedicated.

                                            As an NPC you only should give her a power like Komnenos' Honor if you plan on having her regularly act against her Path in a story. This is not about explaining her, this is about characterizing her. A power has to ultimately be there to be used. Komnenos' Honor​implies a character that sins and stills don't fall, not a character that doesn't sin.

                                            Its also important to think what are the consequences of her having her lovers subsumed alive. While it doesn't have to be a low Path Rating, or a hard time staying on the Path, there has to be something. Derangements are a good option as Kharnov suggested, although there may be other options. It doesn't necessarily have to affect her stats directly, but you should give it serious consideration.​

                                            Originally posted by Samuelalpha86 View Post
                                            Ah, so you're saying that combination Disciplines can only be used by one person?
                                            In practice, both Elder Level Disciplines and Combo Disciplines are usually developed by the character by themselves, with no help, a lot of effort and no instinct guiding it, so most characters have a relatively low number of them. But this isn't a hard rule. A vampire that has one can teach another that fulfills the requisites, and that's a lot easier (if not cheaper in XP), but's also a lot rare precisely because they're too valuable. Parallel development also exists, so two unconnected characters may happen to have the same power, but that should be exceedingly rare.

                                            While for game effects the lists of such powers help building characters and giving players and STs options to work with, in-game nothing guarantees that any of them is available, or even exists at all at the moment.

                                            A lot of it is up to the ST, though, and while the frequency of a particular power should always be low, the frequency of any vampire having some Combo Disciplines is up in the air, as is even the age at which they usually appear. Because they're very rare in official stat blocks, it's usually accepted that they're very rare, to the point that even among Elders few have them. But this hasn't to be the case for your character.

                                            You should also keep in mind how attuned to the character they are. Don't choose Disciplines based on the Combos you want, you're free to made-up Combos and Elder Level powers that fit her specifically, based on whatever she in fact has that you think makes sense. This isn't a bad choice, it's actually how those powers are meant to be used.

                                            Going for my suggestion on Discipline distribution, one way to make it feel organic could be to give her one or two Combos, then trade Discipline dots for more combos if you want, a number of Combos equal to the level of the dot you gave up (so reducing a 5 dots Discipline to 4 dots would give you 5 powers). This isn't strictly how the XP for such powers work, but would give you a good ballpark on how much different powers she should have.

                                            Oh, and another thing: take care to not overdo it, in practice giving too many different powers to a character has a great chance of just making the character harder to use (for anything) than really making them more powerful or more effective.
