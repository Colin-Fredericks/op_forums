    Beauty is the Divine Truth

    Type: Fiction

    Synopsis: A rather lengthy letter explaining Michael the Archangel and the role of beauty among Kindred.

    Analysis: A role of the evolutionary role of beauty and God's design begins this work, which is one of the longer bits of short fiction in the book. This is a suitably artsy and drawn out introduction to the concept of Michael the Fallen Archangel as well as the Nephilim. I've always liked Michael as he's an interesting contrast to Mithras in the fact that the former's claim is clearly insane but the latter is someone who might legitimately be an Embraced god rather than just a Ventrue impersonating one.

    A commentary on Constantinople, I never actually have used Michael because I actually preferred to substitute Menele as the Patriarch of Constantinople. This is because I did it before the Dark Ages game and I always thought Byzantium made a great place for a Brujah to finally revive Carthage as a dream.
    I admit, that's part of the fun of Constantinople because it's a parody of the kind of ancients that rule cities like Chicago but not the kind of parody that LA by Night did. Michael is utterly batshit and undoubtedly predates Christiany by several millennia yet has fallen into the delusion of being Michael. However, because he's got Presence 9, PEOPLE BELIEVE HIM and it is able to create a cult that lasts millennia.

    Well after he's dust and nothing due to Mary the Black's efforts.

    One thing I like about the Nephilim is that they're not a remotely deep cult but fit perfectly to Toreador stereotypes. They embrace themselves in all of the beauty, art, sex, and drugs of Toreador like and pretend its enlightenment. I think of them as a somewhat less violent version of the Carthians. I can totally imagine other Toreador getting excessively tired of them too as they put on religious pretensions to just being arrogant elitist Toreador assholes.

        Bed the Bones

        Type: Fiction

        Synopsis: A Hecate lectures a racist Clan member about proper respect.

        Analysis: This is a funny story but sadly all too true to life where a pair of Hecate discuss one of the ups and downsides of living in the new regime: religious tolerance. Specifically, the fact that one of them has been invited to a gathering of the Samedi around their founder. The Elder also potentially gives her some necrophiliac advice about "bedding the bones" as well as the humorous fact it is the Elder who is surprised that the Neonate actually sleeps in a coffin.

        I also liked the explanation for why some Kindred attend Blood Cults. Not because they believe in them but because they're terrified of the other vampires involved being offended.

        I am getting a bit, "sigh", about how every scene in the Hecate does need a reference to incest or necrophilia, though.

                                                Hail Gorgo, Slayer of Men

                                                Type: Fiction

                                                Synopsis: A discussion of the legend of Medusa and her cult.

                                                Analysis: The tale of Medusa is one that has often been reinterpreted through a feminist lens. Much like Medea, a woman wronged horribly, the young priestess of Athena got the shit end of the stick in mythology and many people have given their own takes on it. My favorite was BLOOD TIES, the vampire detective series, where Medusa's victimhood is brought front and center but that doesn't stop her from being a serial killer.

                                                Gorgo's Nest is one of the minor cults established in this book and basically seem to be a Kindred version of the Black Furies with a focus on destroying Kindred who Embrace without permission, Blood Bond, and generally do all those horrible things that Elders do to their childer. It has a somewhat Anarch-esque theme but I doubt many of them would identify as such. The group is explictly "sisters" but doesn't really need them to be female from what I gather.

                                                It's a believable cult but one that could also very easily fall into self-parody. I am interested in the idea of Medusa being a vampire, though. Nosferatu? Toreador? Otherwise? Gorgo being her childe would make her a 5th generation vampire.

                                                    The Seeker and the Moon

                                                    Type: Fiction

                                                    Synopsis: A Golconda seeker meets with an indigineous blood cult.

                                                    Analysis: I'm going to be honest that I had a lot of trouble parsing this one because it is the kind of flowery language that a lot of people associate with religious people but is rarely actually employed by them. It is a short description of a vampire's attempt to make contact with a Lima religious sect, however, and has some interesting insights into the Drowned.

                                                    The Drowned are one of those concepts that I felt were left behind by 5th Edition and I suspect are something that will be primarily forwarded by OPP from this point on. They're in some respects a better handled version of the Kuei-Jin. In so much as they are an attempt to do local versions of vampires not related to the Biblical Caine myth and not astoundingly tone deaf/racist.

                                                    I much prefer every vampire to be descended from Cainites because, well, they're vampires and movie monsters are not the place to worry about mythological placement. However, I admit I am not the best perspective for this. I do like that the seeker is fascinated by the concept of them not being descended from the Dark Father, though. You'd think that would make them believe they DON'T have a connection to Golconda them.

                                                        On a mission for the goddess

                                                        Type: Fiction

                                                        Synopsis: Two members of the Gorgo discuss new recruitment.

                                                        Analysis: How do Blood Cults recruit? This transcript is something that provides a lot of insight into the concepts of how Blood Cults operate. After all, vampires are not a huge number of individuals to begin with so they are people who have to search long and hard for people not only interested in being religious but also the religion where you worship, say, Mithras. The fiction lightly touches on the fact that ancestor cults are a bit of a hard sell in the modern nights as well. As one of the two women say, "Yes, she's accepted Gorgo is real. She just believes she's an ancient vampire and not a goddess. That will come in time."

                                                        And here they murder a vampire to "free" an initiate while also wondering if they're being used. Good Kindred intrigue.

                                                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                            It's a believable cult but one that could also very easily fall into self-parody. I am interested in the idea of Medusa being a vampire, though. Nosferatu? Toreador? Otherwise? Gorgo being her childe would make her a 5th generation vampire.
                                                            This is a good point. I wonder how most STs and Players would handle it. How often will it be handled with finesse and how often will it simply become a bad parody? And this is true of most media depiction of cults and fringe groups, and so it will also be true of VtM.

                                                            On a related note, a podcast I listen to frequently is called "Behind the Bastards." It is fact based and carried off like a black comedy. A recent pair of episodes was about Paul Schäfer, a Nazi who once badly injured himself in an attempted to untie his shoes and later ran a religious cult compound in Chile, and helped torture and disappear people for the Augusto Pinochet regime. Schäfer was also a pedophile, and as best he could forbade sex between adults in his cult.

                                                            From the outside the cults always look unhinged. From the inside the cult feels sensible - even if the members are telling themselves this as a coping mechanism - while the outside world is wicked and insane.

                                                            Most players are not going to have the patience to run through indoctrination of their PCs. So it may get rushed or hand waved away. Which can tip it all over into parody.

                                                            How do any of you handle depicting cults in a game?
