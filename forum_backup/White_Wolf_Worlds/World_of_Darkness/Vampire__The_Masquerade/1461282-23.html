                                    Originally posted by Muad'Dib View Post
                                    I always thought it is at least 1000 years old (probably not literally, more like 1100-1500 minimum) and 4th or 5th Generation.
                                    This feels like a fair standard in the Modern Nights. Mithras was Embraced in ~1200 BCE and would have thus "become" a methuselah at ~200 BCE.

                                    In the Dark Medieval and earlier eras, standards are different. In Constantinople by Night, Caius is fifth generation and around ~900 years old, but his traits aren't significantly more impressive than the city's other elders. Back then I think it was still possible for many fifth-generation vampires to be considered "mere" elders.

                                    The Followers of Set specifically define a methuselah as a vampire who's survived a Sothic turn--that is, any vampire who is 1,461 or more years old. I always thought an even better definition was a vampire who's survived one Sothic turn per generation removed from Set. So a 4th-generation Setite "officially" becomes a methuselah at 1,461, a 5th-generation one becomes a methuselah at 2,922, a 6th-generation one becomes a methuselah at 4,383, and so on.

                                    Though I'd agree that the archetypal Methuselahs are those who are over 2000 years old (let's say at least 1800 years).
                                    This is also a good point on archetypes vs. strict definitions.

                                    Helena, Menele, and Mithras are some of the game's most archetypal methuselahs. They're ancient creatures who effortlessly manipulate lesser vampires and dominate their respective corners of the game setting.

                                    Hedeon Yaroslavich is ~1500 and fifth generation. He doesn't hide his existence to the extent that Helena and Menele do, but I never got the impression he was particularly well-known outside of Eastern Europe. He's also poised to potentially take over the Oradea League. Is he a methuselah? The term seems apt for him. What about Critias, who's fifth generation and ~2500 years old, but leads an existence largely identical to the other Chicago primogen? His only 6+ Discipline is an Auspex power that lets him looks (even) smarter than he is. He's more like an elder who just happens to be particularly old, rather than a true methuselah, despite the fact that "by the numbers" he's more of a methuselah than Hedeon is. Granted, Hedeon's Disciplines are also more powerful, so there you go.

                                    At the end of the day, I think methuselah is more of a social distinction than a hard set of criteria.

                                    An interesting question is whether the Inner Councilors of the Clan Tremere could be considered to be Methuselahs. I think that Meerlinda and Etrius certainly could be called Methuselahs, when their experience and insights from being former Mages are taken into account, and also the fact that they have been in the leadership of Clan Tremere since its creation.

                                    The rest of the Council of Seven, not so much. Though they do have Thaumaturgy 8-9 (as well as the immensely useful Auspex and Dominate as the other Clan Disciplines) and the unity of the Tremere Pyramid behind them.
                                    Etrius is described (out-of-character) as a methuselah in Transylvania Chronicles IV. Which I'd agree with. He's powerful enough to compete on the same level as vampires like Mithras.

                                    Goratrix is definitely a methuselah too for the same reasons.

                                    I agree the other councilors probably aren't methuselahs. I also tend to think no other councilors in the Modern Nights are fourth generation. Some might not even be fifth. Some councilors have had a lot of turnover (like Africa's) and it strains credulity to me that there would happen to be another fourth-generation Tremere waiting in the wings for a spot to open. There are probably Tremere like Karl Schrekt and Ulugh Begh who are more potent than councilors like Elaine de Calinot and simply don't want her job. Not all councilorships or councilors are created equal.

Methuselah is the word vampires use for the oldest vampires who nonetheless get their hands dirty often enough to be in the public eye. Those vamps tend to be about a 1,000.

                                            I think the known Methuselah, apart from just being the historical/mythical characters the authors thought would be fun to put, can be considered the ones who lived through this process and got back to participate enough for some of their shenanigans to become known. So younger Methuselah are either too uninvolved or didn't did enough yet to become known.

                                            Another interesting fact is that this leaves enough space for the ST to put their own Methuselah in the game at a more comfortable scale, being free to create the character from scratch.

Being a Methuselah is about behaving as a Methuselah. Taking the game to another level. But I also think that all Methuselah shouldn't be thought as on the same level exactly. This isn't helpful. What is important is that they're somehow set apart from Kindred society, they're "above" it in a way a "common" Autarkis or Inconnu isn't. But this can still cover a broad spectrum of characters.

Or not, even in mortals those changes in outlook and behavior are sometimes sudden.
