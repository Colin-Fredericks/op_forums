                                                        Okay, I'm actually going to respond to this and other elements because Justycar is right that we might ACTUALLY talk about the book in this thread about the book. Crazy, I know.

                                                        My opinion on the above two is as good a place to start as any and I'm going to speak to my thoughts regarding them in bullet points.

                                                        1. OPP's help: I think Onyx Path has pretty much been carrying water for 5th Edition from the beginning and a large part of that has been patching holes and spackling the holes in the setting, One thing Onyx Path has an interest in that Paradox Interactive doesn't is revisiting old material. 5th Edition was supposed to be something of a clean slate and jumping on point. Which makes sense because only the die hards (like the people here) wanted to follow up on plots that haven't been followed up on for 15 years. BJD aside.

                                                        Well they DID write BJD so they've been inserting as much material from that book and their own takes on past subject into their bonus books as possible. I'll be honest, without Chicago by Night, Let the Streets Run Red, The Chicago Folios, and Cults of the Blood Gods--I think V5 would have been an artistic failure. I am a stalwart defender of V5 as everyone here knows but let's be honest, I'm a stalwart V5 guy because of what OPP has done with it.

                                                        2. Disciples of Ur-Shulgi: I think this is a fairly direct adaptation of what they were doing in 3rd Edition and not much of an update. The followers of Ur-Shulgi are killing in the name of Haqim and are the same crazy ass religious terrorists they've always been. I actually was hoping they'd been wiped out by the Sabbat during the Gehenna Crusade since, bluntly, the Sabbat are kind of their antithesis yet similar enough they'd make a b-line straight for one another. Here, Ur-Shulgi just kind of disappears while the take on Alamut in THE BLACK HAND is a great deal different. I see the contradiction here as something bound to have happened eventually but disappointing.

                                                        I can't say too much praise here as while the Disciples of Ur-Shulgi are USEFUL, they're kind of useful in the way the Iron Sheik was in professional wrestling. They're one-dimensional professional heels that exist to be any group of vampires can hate. They do seem a lot less powerful and dangerous than they were in 3rd Edition but it's been 15 years and it seems the Banu Haqim in the Camarilla are still around so they can't have been doing too great.

                                                        3. Orphans of Enoch: I think the Tal'Ma'Re is kind of a sad scoop up of the concepts introduced in V20's version of the True Hand. They had some fascinating ideas about how to redeem the silly concept of Dirty Secrets and I think we can all agree OPP's version is superior in every way. Unfortunately, Paradox's official opinion seems to be EVERYTHING IS CANON (from the original game) so the nuking of both the Ravnos as well as the Black Hand is 100% correct. So if the Baali did join the True Hand, they were presumably nuked with the rest of Enoch. Such a shame.

                                                        Well these are an okay follow up to the Black Hand but they're not the Black Hand as we know them. They're a bunch of shell-shocked refugees who have lost everything. The fact they were nuked and Gehenna has resulted in nothing but massive death for most Elders (I presume most non-Chatterlings of the True Hand's survivors have been Beckoned), it's understandable why these guys are nothing but a shadow of their former selves.

                                                        In a way too much change vs the Disciples of Ur-Shulgi's lack of change.

                                                        4. The Praesidium: The Tremere Pyramid getting nuked was one of the changes that pissed off old school V:TM fans the most because the Illuminati within the Illuminati was one of the most popular clans. Remaking the Tremere Pyramid as a smaller, leaner, Kentucky-based version is something I think is a good idea. However, I know plenty of fans who still won't like them because they're a lot more combat focused than the original recipe Tremere rather than a straight recreation.

                                                        However, this makes perfect sense to me because the Tremere have just experienced a catastrophic fucking loss at the hands of the Second Inquisition's hunters. If you don't want to just ignore V5 like some old school fans (you know who you are), then the Tremere survivors deciding they need to take some levels in magic kung fu seems like a reasonable course of action. Too many Tremere have died and too many of their enemies are ready and willing to take the fight to them so investing some Discipline points in combat skills is a pretty good idea--especially since Lure of Flames is no longer available.

                                                        (Jason Carl canonized the lightning bolt, though, so I assume every one of these guys knows that one)

                                                        The biggest failure of this is that they ABSOLUTELY should have included more combat rituals with this group. If they had, it would easily have been the most popular cult here.

                                                        5. Random aside about the Praesidium but there's no way in hell that it would be located in Lousiville instead of Lexington. Lexington is the land of rich weirdos (including one transplanted Medieval castle), isolated estates, rich creepy inbred families, and the University of Kentucky.

                                                            As someone form Kentucky, I can tell you exactly why Louisville would be a better fit. The population of Lexington/Fayette Co. is about 250K; Louisville/Jefferson Co. is 650K with a GMA of 1.2 million. The higher population alone could support more vampires. Further, there is a higher amount of business and money there too. For comparison, the international HQ of Yum brands (KFC/Taco Bell/Pizza Hut) is located there. Lexington has the HQ of Long John Silver's (which was founded there)/A & W All-American diners. As to the other factors--the castle is one county over, outside of Lexington proper; the 'isolated' estates are mostly farms (horses/cattle/sheep/llamas, although this last one is iffy), mostly twenty to thirty minutes from downtown depending on the traffic; the 'inbred' family stereotype is more of an eastern Kentucky thing, if your are brave try googling 'Hillbilly Days Pikeville' for an example, just remember that it is for charity; and finally, Lexington is also the home of Transylvania University, the first institute of higher learning west of the Appalachians, a highly ranked private school.
