    Originally posted by Trippy View Post
    Well, this is wrong - because you are rolling contested dice pools through a chain. You are not rolling a bell curve of results - you are rolling dice pools that are weighted towards accumulating lower numbers of success rather than higher number of success. You are citing the wrong mathematical model, because we are not talking about rolling 3D6 like in D&D or GURPS, so this is not how the maths work in dice pool rolling.
    3d6 vs. 1d20 is just an example of how, despite having the same average result, the more dice you roll the more the results tend towards a bell curve result. And that's the problem with the dataset you linked to, it only shows the average not the full distributions.

    Contested pools actually push things towards the average range of a bell curve distribution faster because its effectively adding both sets into a single outcome... basically X dice result -Y dice result.

    Dice pools by their very nature produce bell curve distributions that favor the average outcome because you have multiple dice rolling for passes and the resultant value is the number of passes you get... i.e. the more dice you roll the closer you will skew on average to the average pass rate. You can actually calculate this out on anydice.com.

    Here's a link for the dice results of 10 dice at difficulty 6 (you can change the number of dice and difficulty in the formula above to check out other distributions). There is not an equal chance of getting 0-10 successes from rolling those 10 dice. More than half of all rolls (52.38%) will fall between 3-5 successes while there is only a 0.1% chance of scoring 10 successes. There is a 13.8% chance of getting 6 successes though.

    When you add in an opposing roll the number of successes countered will similarly fall into a bell curve distribution and subtracting that set from the attackers will push the results even further towards a large percentage of the outcomes falling closer to the average results with those results determined almost entirely by who's traits are higher and/or who can get the lowest difficulty to roll against (because 6 dice at difficulty 4 will score between 3-5 successes 64.55% of the time so a -2 difficulty in V20 can be worth as much as 4 bonus dice in V20).

    So again, its fine to say that you feel rolling so many dice is slower than making fewer checks (I don't even think that's a matter of opinion that it would) and its also fine to say you prefer a system with fewer dice rolls, but the math is the math and the math says the more dice you roll the more the results will conform to the average because the standard deviation is reduced with each additional die involved in the overall resolution.

                Personally, I wouldn't run a mugging as combat, as that's all geared towards inflicting damage and VTM can be lethal in any edition. I'd rather run it as an extended contest where no to minimal damage is caused.

                E.g., a mugging could be Wits + Stealth v Perception + Alertness to get close enough. Then either Dexterity + Larceny or Dexterity + Brawl to take what you want versus the target's Dexterity + Athletics or Dexterity + Brawl, with the aim being not to injure but to snatch, evade or immobilise, etc.

                Boxing probably would be combat, but you could argue that it obeys stricter rules--the intent isn't an all-out brawl but a slow match of skill and training. So that could also be an extended contest if you wanted. If it were combat, you'd want to pull your punches enough to avoid accidental murder.

                Old school VTM is considerably more lethal when people don't use their action to defend every time. But that favours the person with the higher initiative.

                My players rarely use their action to dodge or block, preferring to save their action for an attack. The exception is social- or mental-focussed characters who prefer to use their turn using all-out defence. But that might just be our table.

Having been hit with bare knuckles and gloves? They feel different, and gloved hits can feel like they didn't hit as hard at first. I guess it's kind of like bare knuckle punches sting more upfront and that sudden very intense pain masks the deeper tissue bruising to an extent. Gloved hits kinda go the other way where you don't have that same "wow shit that hurt" reaction, but then you try to use whatever just got hit and then you feel the pain of it.

                                That all said, you could say that boxing gloves making it slightly harder to land a blow, at least in a regulation match (in a street fight this wouldn't really matter). A standard modern boxing posture is designed so that the larger surface area created by the glove is more likely to land on your arms, or glance off your arms, making it harder to get solid his on vital areas. But this only works because a lot of areas are off limits to attack. If you look at older boxing stances, and most martial arts stances, the arms are projected much farther out from the body than a modern boxer because they need to defend against a larger number of angles of attack.

                                OK... probably went into too much detail there...

                                            OK.... we're probably getting way off-topic here, so I'll trying to bring things back after a bit more combat sports nerding on my part:

                                            The pace of combat sports is really complicated because that's just so many factors. Like, I pointed to earlier fencing happens insanely fast. An average fencing exchange lasts around six seconds, and will involve dozens of moves for both fencers. Standard competition fencing is to 5 points. Foil and epee are grueling competitions though, and frequently don't hit 5 points before regulation 3 minutes are up (somewhere between 15-20 exchanges since there are generally pauses of a few seconds as they reset and evaluate tactics). The muscles that let you fight that fast fatigue very quickly, so it takes an insane amount of body condition to actually keep up that long. Sabre tends to go much faster and requires less endurance. The only difference? Sabre lets you slash and thrust, not just thrust, so winning on 5 points before time is way more common. Boxing and martial arts based sports generally would think of 3 minutes as nothing for a competitive fighter, but that's because it's a completely different set of stresses on the body. It's like sprinters vs. marathon runners; the general idea of running from A to B is the same, but what you're asking the body to do in the process is radically different. Then you toss hurdlers into the mix and it gets even more insane to try to model any of this in a way that reflects the differences between them all.

                                            ----------------


                                            This does actually let me come back to answer an question that was lingering though:

                                            Why put up with V20's clunky combat system and slapdash social mechanics? Because options. V5 does a respectable job of trying to not get bogged down in details that might matter to an enthusiast such as myself, but really don't matter to playing the game, but in the end the advanced systems end up too much of a bad compromise where you're in a middle ground that's not really satisfactory to me. The added details aren't detailed enough to capture enough nuances to make me feel like the extra effort pays off rather than just using the standard resolution mechanics. V20's mechanics give their options enough differentiation that it actually feels like the process feels like a meaningful level of granularity while still abstract.

                                            For example, in V20, I can make a vampire that's actually good at fighting other vampires with guns. Guns aren't an easy choice for a vampire, because vampires are very resilient to bullets. And I'm not even talking about getting into some of the boardering on sci-fi ammunition VtM has let into the games over the years. But with a lot of system mastery, I once made a character that was basically vampire John Wick. He averaged ~5 Bashing damage after soak and halving in one fight, and a lot people apparently don't realize that if you have the damage output, Bashing torpors vampires just the same as Lethal does. Pulling that off required a constant use of taking advantage of all those little system details that V5 cut out to make things faster. And it wasn't like this was some sort of busted OP build... outside of the advantage of range, you could have easily out DPSed him with close combat attacks and Potence. And this wasn't a trenchcoat and katanas, or Vampions sort of game. My character quickly became a Scourge, and was angling for Sheriff, and his reputation for doing the equivalent of killing three guys with a pencil was part of his political rise to power. Getting him on the Path of Honorable Accord while being a public figure of the Camarilla, so he could have a Path rating over 3 was a much bigger deal to the overall story than all the ghouls he slaughtered.

                                            I just don't see how to make that character fun in V5. The combat systems just don't have the meat on the bones to make that sort of rise in power through violence anything more than luck. There's no enough rules rewards for fighting smart to ever get a reputation built around wasting dumb licks with a weapon that's a suboptimal choice to most vampires so they think it's a statement of showing off how much he's holding back. The slog of combat was worth it because the resulting power, and struggles with morality, felt earned in a fashion the V5 rules don't seem like they can really manage because the rules framework isn't there.

                                                I have pros and cons for both. Generally, V20 has the benefit of inspiring no deep feelings from me and being all around satisfactory. V5, meanwhile, is mixed bag of stuff I either love or loathe. They're different games set in different eras of the same setting, it's like there's Dark Ages, Victorian, the Masquerade (1990s/2000s) and the Masquerade (2020s).

                                                I compiled a list of pros for both, and then realised they were too long. So here's just the V20 list for now (which is still too long...). Naturally, these are my opinions, not statements of fact.

                                                Pro V20:
                                                • - The biggest advantage for V20 is that it's a complete work, all rules and lore available right now. No waiting to see if stuff will be added back in or worrying that stuff won't. Also the corebook had all clans, sects, bloodlines and disciplines from the start. I can play Sabbat and I didn't need to wait for a book for it, because the mechanics hadn't changed so much they were temporarily unplayable.
                                                  -
                                                • - I felt no need to homerule the hell out of V20 all the time. Most of V5 I like conceptually, but I don't like the mechanics. The lore is stuff I almost like sometimes, if you just tweak it a bit... or a lot.
                                                  -
                                                • - I could defy clan stereotypes. V5's clan banes kinda reinforce them.
                                                  -
                                                • - V5 leaves me feeling edgy about what they're going to do in the future metaplot, and not in a good way.
                                                  -
                                                • - Paths, paths, paths, paths, path-
                                                  Anyway, V5 does not (yet?) allow me to explore the metamorphosis of humanity into an inhuman monster (Camarilla and Anarch elders don't count, their monstrosity is still rooted in a human perspective). I love my inhuman vampire philosophies/religons.
                                                  -
                                                • - The Cappadocian/HoS and Samedi weaknesses. That's another thing I have to homerule. They could at least have merged them or something.
                                                  -
                                                • - The old Camarilla is preferable. I don't like its new exclusive status. Maybe if V5's Cam was present as a special premium membership for the "True Camarilla". They still claim responsibility/governance of all kindred, but since the Anarchs are such ungrateful shortsighted spoilt brats they're grounded and have to earn their feeding/Elysium/domain privilges back.
                                                  So what V5 is doing, basically, but with a different flavour. More like a secretive undead government, less like a VIP undead country club
                                                  .-
                                                • - The disciplines and blood sorcery are much more restrictive. To be a thaumaturge you need to be Tremere, to use vicisitude you need to be Tzimisce. I don't mind the older disciplines being different uses of a handful of core disciplines, but it feels like the clans are losing their distinctions. Dementation is a core part of the Malkavians, but in V5 anyone with dominate and obfuscate can use it. Obviously you can restrict it to the clan at your table, but unless I missed something there's nothing in the rules themselves that says "restriction: must be of/taught to outsiders by x clan".
                                                  -
                                                • - Elder power levels. Your Elders are and always will be way beyond your leage in terms of power and you will be beneath their heal for eternity, your choices are either to cope or to get good at playing Jyhad.
                                                  -
                                                • - The nature of vampirism in V20 already did a perfectly good job of leaving you alienated from mortal life. You could try, and would almost certainly fuck up because your unlife makes you a monster by circumstance, but it wasn't definite that you would fuck up. There was always the slimest chance you could maybe pull it off if you managed your feeding right and truly cared to try hard enough. That made it worth striving for. There was a much needed light at the end of the road in a grimdark setting.
                                                  The way V5 treats vampirism vs humanity is overkill. You're a ticking timebomb who can't ever not be risking killing people without... killing people. Great. There's no point trying to preserve your humanity because there's absolutely no chance of success. Well, guess it's time to embrace damnation and join the Sabbat, because they're right, and the only way not to be a constant danger to everyone around you is to embrace the fact that you're a vampire and humans are food. I prefer the question of "Which sect is right?" to be more ambiguous, thank you.
                                                  It's atmospheric, butit goes too far.

Plus it's interesting to shake things up with a second Inquisition. The kindred could use the exercise.
                                                              -
                                                            • - I also like the SI. They're fighting monsters, but they're also a government agency. There might be heroic individuals amognst them, but as an organisation they're certainly not on the side of the common human. They're vaguely like the Technocracy for vampires, I suppose. It would be interesting to play them.
                                                              -
                                                            • - Gehenna is likely misunderstood and most likely not the be-all-and-end-all of apocalypses.
                                                              -
                                                            • - More vampire religous cults, I love exploring the effect of becoming a vampire on characters and how they might rationalise and deal with it. A vampire specific religion is good for that, it was why I loved paths and the Setites.
                                                              -
                                                            • - Blood Sorcery as its own discipline, with Hermetic Thaumaturgy, Dur-An-Ki, Akhu and so on being different paradigms.
                                                              -
                                                            • - Necromancy was found to be too sanitary it seems, I love it. Want to go the shadowlands? No longer do you need to follow the Ash Path to five dots. Nowadays when we get five dots we stain our souls with the essence of Oblivion channeled through human sacrifice.
                                                              -
                                                            • - The Tzimisce weakness is more interesting and contributes to characterisation. Now you need to think about what the core of this character's identity is and how it manifests, which in V20 was generally devolved into a question of "where is the jar of dirt"?
                                                              -
                                                            • With their new, and vastly improved weakness, the Tremere just got a boost to one of the most interesting clans for me. I love it so much, I homeruled it so it was even worse and their blood is totally unable to bind anything anymore. The clan must be falling over itself to guard this knowledge.
                                                              You can make a ghoul, but they have no innate loyalty to you and without that they're a walking masquerade breach waiting to happen. You know who could make use of the superhuman powers of ghouls, but without the loyalty to a vampire master? The SI.
                                                              You're also a walking snack for elders. They've somehow aquired even more potential enemies, because those who didn't want them staked out for the sun are considering the possibility of opening a cafe with usurper vitae on the menu.
                                                              More than even the Tremere need to pull together for safety, but with several different opinions on the way to go and no blood bond to force them together they're having a hard time doing that. I find I can make Tremere plot hooks much easier now.
                                                              -
                                                            • - Less bloodline clutter, at the moment.
                                                              -
                                                            • - The thin bloods make for a different playstyle when I want a change of pace.
                                                              -
                                                            • - I do like the Hecata, albeit as a sect for the necromancers rather than a magically blended together clan. It also makes sense for the Clan of Death to be independent, since death is impartial and that is the theme these guys have going on.
                                                              -
                                                            • - Once I remove "must kill to sate the final hunger dot", I do appreciate how the struggle with the beast is a much bigger presence in a vampire's unlife now. V5 better depicts the concept that you're in an internal nightly battle for your soul.
                                                              -
                                                            • - I wasn't fond of discipline powers until I started playing with converting older combo disciplines and VtR devotions into amalgams and got totally different playstyles out of two characters with overlapping disciplines. I'm more sold on them now. Amalgams are great fun and put a lot of clan specific flavour in, turning common disciplines into very different things depending on the clan using them.
                                                              I think the current problem is just the limited selection.
                                                              -
                                                            • - Touchstones. While I still don't like the human restriction or how they work in game, I adore the concept that there are things, anchors, that resonate so strongly with your sense of self it can beat back the beast. Used in a certain way, they could be a little break of real love and joy - or at least a sense of purpose - in the world of darkness.
                                                              -
                                                            • - Here's a possibly contraversial one:
                                                              So, there's the view that "V5 is VtR with a VtM paintjob". While I can see why, V5 isn't VtR. It's not VtM1e-V20. It's V5, it's its own thing. But it does have enough similarities to the two that you can sort of play it that way, which works for me if I'm in the mood to do so. I prefer VtM's clans, paths, sects, blood magic and having vicissitude to VtR, but enjoy the lore flexibility and closer to actually-human default humanity of VtR which can be replicated in the tenets (spend time with people, have a life outside of vampire politics). It's not a one-to-one matchup, but V5 saves me time messing around with the translation guide and the changes to some of the lore makes blending them with VtR covenants easier..
