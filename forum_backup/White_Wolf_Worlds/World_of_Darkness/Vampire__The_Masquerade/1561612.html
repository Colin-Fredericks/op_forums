      The majority of the impact would be on the Mage side of things since it would rewrite a huge amount of Hermetic history.

      How much this would have changed VtM depends a lot on edition. In early VtM, the Tremere had a much stronger hold on blood magic. This made them vital to the pre-Camarilla and early Camarilla enough to justify overlooking the way they became vampires and how they created their gargoyles. In Revised and later... that role is reduced significantly. With the later takes on VtM where blood magic is more accessible, the Camarilla doesn't need the Tremere as much to have made it out on the other side of the late Medieval period in Europe. The biggest impact would probably be the geographic distribution of power:

      1) The Sabbat would have likely been able to hold on to more of Europe as Abyssal mystics and Kolduns could have more impact without the sudden influx of sorcerers on the Cam's side of things
      2) The Ashirra would probably remain more Banu Hakim controlled and possibly the outcome of their Schism would have gone differently, but they'd be in a stronger place to have a tense alliance with the Camarilla to keep the Sabbat on Setites at bay
      3) The fight to colonize the Americas would have likely been much more competitive and fractured. Instead of the Sabbat largely getting their first and the Camarilla having to push in later

          We do know his personality, and it suggests no infernalism. He, Goratrix, and the rest of House Tremere didn't like diabolism for a simple reason - if a demon is powerful enough to be worth summoning, it can't be controlled, and if it can be controlled, it isn't powerful enough to be worth it. He'd probably pursue lichdom or another Spell of Life variant.

          His remaining loyal to the Order of Hermes means not only that they don't have to deal with Grimgoth's betrayal, but also that they don't have to split resources between fighting the OoR and the Tremere, and that the Hermetics largely remain ignorant of the Camarilla, and likely don't have as big of a grudge against vampires. He or one of his House might eventually rise to political power within the Council of Nine, possibly even taking the role of Porthos in the main timeline. The OoR also might not win Mistridge, or at least have a much harder fight. If the OoR doesn't get Mistridge, then the Law of Shade likely doesn't exist, and Tradition magi operate openly, resulting in a radically different Consensus.

          As for the vampire history, the Banu Haqim don't get hit with the Tremere curse and continue being diablerists. This means that the Via Sanguinis mostly stays in its traditional form. This also means that when Ur-Shulgi wakes up, he doesn't have as much leverage, and in fact, may himself be on the menu.

          There are two realistic possibilities here - either the Banu Haqim join the Camarilla to fill the void in the timeline left by the Tremere, or the Sabbat fights the Camarilla on more equal ground as Koldunic sorcerers and Abyssal mystics beat them back (as Heavy Arms said). But then again, the Sabbat also loses troops; for example, the Salubri Warriors never join the Sabbat, since they're mostly there because the majority of Tremere are in the Camarilla. In fact, the Salubri in general are spared from the purges, and Saulot either never gets diablerized, or has to find another blood sorcerer to carry out his grand plan (possibly Ur-Shulgi). The Salubri likely become one of the pillars of the Camarilla, and make the Camarilla altogether a morally better group.

              I actually think the bigger change would be VtM.

              Tremere would be OoH. Being he would be "loyal", though, is a bit of a stretch.

              Overall I don't think they'd be invested enough to play a changing role in the events of Mage.

              Likely their efforts at immortality would have been limited but similar to others. The more they actually managed to achieve it, the less of a part they would have played in the events of Mage as they'd be more focused on their own affairs.

              Vampires would lack a big mover and shaker, not because Tremere were as powerful as they sold themselves, but because their desperation made them forcefully disruptive in the original timeline.

              Without them the Salubri would be a somewhat stronger presence. Mystics from other clans would have remained open and relevant, even growing stronger. We could have a variety of types of sorcery in the employ of various lords, possibly giving the Setites a place in "polite" vampiric society, attracting more Tzimisce Koldun defectors, even being possible a revival and strengthening of Ventrue sorcery.

              Given how much the Baali curse was a problem for the Banu Haqim, not a blessing, I don't think they'd change their trajectory so much. It would begin with a longer conflict, but peace would be brokered simply because it was unsustainable. And they were precisely the one sorcery tradition among the undead with the greater upper hand over the Sabbat, so they'd be sought after.

              Being diablerists would weight against them, but not enough to stop both their presence in the Camarilla and the formation of the Ashirra. I'd say their influence in the Ashirra would be smaller rather than greater, though, because they wouldn't be seen as sufficiently trustworthy.

              Details beyond those would depend a lot on the changed events, but finding a way to reverse the Baali Curse would be a possibility with many other sorcerous "allies" around and no other curse to fight. The Camarilla could also have devoted more time to fight the Baali às they'd have more reason to and wouldn't be wasting efforts with the Salubri.

                                      Without the Tremere, both the Camarilla and the Sabbat never form. The Tremere coming into existence with their self-embrace came at a pivotal time and really shook up the status-quo. The Tremere form in the latter half of the 1080s, and due to the nature of their turning, end up causing the Omen War through the next few centuries. So you have the Tremere on one side the the Tzimisce on the other. The Ventrue side with the Tremere because of their interest in Eastern Europe. Bam inquisition hits. This new pressure spurs on the Anarch revolt which in turn causes the "Founders" to begin forming the Camarilla. Yet without the Tremere there, the nasant Camarilla has no sorcery for communication and coordination, and likely never actually forms. With no Camarilla there is no Convention of Thorns, which means no Assamite curse. The Convention of Thorns is also what galvanized groups of Anarchs to form the Sabbat.

                                      By the 1500s you have a much different landscape of relationships and politics. The Ventrue, Toreador, Nosferatu, and Malkavian clans would be mostly the same. The Gangrel - Tzimisce alliances would have never happened. The Lasombra still attempt patricide but most likely the Tzimisce do not. The Cappodocian still get taken over by the Giovanni and the Salubri are still around in numbers. Most likely the system of High-Low clans stays in place as it did in the Dark Ages. If you want to have sects though I would see it like this:

                                      Sect 1: Ventrue, Toreador, Nosferatu, Malkavian, Setite, Salubri Healers. Camarilla like group.
                                      Sect 2: Brujah, Gangrel, Gratiano's Lasombra. This group would be more like modern day Anarchs
                                      Sect 3: Assamite, Lasombra, Salubri Warriors, and various "Antitribu." Your Sabbat group, but more closely resembling the Ashirra.
                                      Independents: Giovanni, Tzimisce, Ravnos, Gangrel.
