          I'm going to diverge a bit from the question because I tend not to have too strong of opinions about specific Paths given how many implementations have happened over the years that have had me waffle on my least favorites. I'm going to go over what annoys me broadly:

          1) Mono-Clan appeal. Paths, and even more-so Roads, work best as an additional minor social stratus in the game. It's another bit of politics you can add to the game on top of Sect and Clan conflicts. When a Path/Road really only exists for one Clan, it's just internal Clan politics with more rules. I will say I mean this more OOC, there are Paths/Roads heavily associated with one Clan, but really could appeal to a much wider group of vampires and there's no real reason in-universe why it hasn't (which is its own story hook to make more interesting).

          2) While very frustrating terminology, when DAV originally developed the Roads, they gave each Road a selection of Paths (like that's not confusing...). The idea was that each Road, as a broad philosophy, had a number of sub-roads (to which path is a decent word) to handle different approaches. These weren't mechanical changes (even if it's clear that some were meant to develop into modern nights fully independent Paths over time), just interesting ways to make things more variable. What does it look like to be on the Road of Kings if you're not the King? Well the paths gave options for that. Even without the formal designation DAV gave, I think the worst Paths are the most restrictive to ideological variation. Again, I give a bit of leeway if the books have multiple Paths that are supposed to be offshoots of the same thing (Beast gets the most of this), but you should be able to have debates between members of the Path on moral/ethical issues, and a lot of them really don't allow it in favor of very uniform members just out of what the Path entails.

          3) Major inconsistency between the descriptions of the Path (esp. the Path ethics sections) and the mechanics of the Path (the actual hierarchy and Virtues). Instinct is frequently a bad sign to me given what Instinct does differently mechanically, and how little sense it can make with give Paths. One of the worst examples if probably the idea that Metamorphosis uses Instinct despite being portrayed as a more scholarly Road/Path that's out to explore the boundaries of vampirism and shatter them. The ethics and Hierarchy talk about acts they control and unleash the Beast... something Instinct doesn't really do.

              Well... I mostly agree with the previous comments here

              And to me, Roads inside the Paths was just more content padding to fill more suplementary books. While I do like the Road Books, they ended up having that 'fill all slots in the most formulaic way possible'. Like, there is a lot of Roads of Kings, which should just be 'take hierarchy and promises seriously'. If I had to choose one, I'd say Road of Merchants, which for me is stupid. But Road of Tyrants is even worst. If you want a Road of do whatever I want, have the balls to go Road of Sin dammit

              Road of Paradox has a lot of interesting ideas. The whole concept of you having to keep zero kharma dept and fulfill both your destiny and not interfere into others I think is awesome. But all the one-off specific Sins, like 'don't embrace women' or 'go bother those who seek golconda' feels stupid.
              Path of Typhoon feels similar a lot of the time.

              But, in a way I never liked the system of Paths in Vampire. It basically forced the ST to keep in mind a list of 5-7 triggers of Sin, for every PC that followed a damn Path, besides the one of Humanity itself. It also wasn't clear if you rolled a single time for like each Sin or only the sum of all the Sin in that Scene. The difference between the base Virtues and the alternatives wasn't good either. I love the idea of Paths, but the system never gelled with me. And the Aura thing? also cool in concept, but just more babble to keep remembering and taking account.

              While there isn't an official Paths system in V5, I much prefer how the integrity (Humanity) works, and it wasn't hard to make a homebrew Path system over it. It was more converting them into V5 than anything else. Of course, homebrew is never a good solution, these thing should be solved officialy, but how much better it works, for me, makes it uncomparable with the janky original system.

                                              Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post

                                              To quote someone else about it "my arcane code of conduct says nothing about rape, innocents or mercy and making an example for stepping out of line is completly acceptable for killing"
                                              Yeah, there can be a lot of assumed chivalry in the Path of Honorable Accord, but it could not necessarily be the case. Bushido has a code of conduct, but it didn't prevent a lot of atrocities in either the Sengoku Jidai or World War II by its adherents. When you consider that Honorable Accord is a Sabbat path designed to protect the sect (and seemingly the Sabbat's elders), it's a problem.

                                              I think one major problem with a lot of Paths is that people forget the Path to constrain the Beast. The Path does not only need to be internally consistent, but always putting the vampire in a place to tell the Beast "no". No, I am not going to feed right now. No, I am not going to kill this other person. No, I am not going to eliminate this potential threat. No, I am not going to be sadistic or whatever it is the Beast wants me to do. Because if it doesn't do that, then the Beast is going to take over through the obvious exceptions (making it a failed Path). (The idea that some Paths control the Beast by satiating it so it doesn't act in when it would be inconvenient for the vampire is an interesting idea, but I don't think it works in practice.)

                                              That the Revised era Path of Honorable Accord is based on Conscience (and Self-Control) indicates the Path is supposed to be humane in some capacity and cover things other than just "being honorable". It requires a person to have a developed moral conscience and not violate it as well. But the original Path of Honorable Accord used Callousness, Instincts, and Morale just like every other Sabbat Path meaning it is not more humane than the other Paths.

                                              I figure that there are probably variants of Honorable Accord. One, a moral one somewhat comparable to Humanity, that does integrate a moral conscience with the concept of honor, and then a second based on Conviction that allows more monstrous conduct, though still honorable conduct (in the style of "honorable villains" that are popular in media).

                                                  While a bit of a retcon since the Sabbat Paths came first, I always figured DAV's take is that the Path of Honorable Accord is part of the Road of Kings family of Paths that took the Chivalry part of Road of Kings and adapted it to the Sabbat's needs. It's not like it's a big stretch... chivalry as practiced in reality was, like Bushido, not something that started as some noble code of righteous action. Both were cultural teachings meant to keep a military class under the thumb of the ruling class but socially superior to the other classes in society with broad range to treat their "inferiors" with little regard. Knights and samurai both got a lot of historical revisionism to paint them as much better people to justify their continued power in society despite the need for a landed gentry warrior class disappearing.

                                                  I'm not sure there needs to be two different versions, or that you need a Conviction version to be a brutal warrior. Conscience works fine for applying force incorrectly as a shameful act even if your views of what kinds of force are acceptable is wildly out of step with Humanity.
