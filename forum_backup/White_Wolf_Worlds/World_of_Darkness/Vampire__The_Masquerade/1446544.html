    Revenant communities

    04-29-2021, 10:24 PM
    I'm curious how people describe or detail Revenant homesteads, particularly the Grimaldi and Obertus.

    We're told that Zantosa and Bratovitch live in family estates. Bratovitches live on around 20 with 10-20 members each. So there's around 300 Bratovitch's total. Zantosa live on "at least" 17 estates between 5-20 members. So maybe around 200 plus members. Large, isolated family estates easily allow these Revenants to do whatever they want outside the eyes of mortals.

    But the Grimaldi and Obertus are different and present some challenges for the ST.

    Grimaldi are supposed to live among humanity, but as individuals or presumably nuclear family units. The problem is that revenants age slowly. Not an issue for adults, but children are supposed to take 50 years to reach adulthood. It's possible for people to interact with an adult for a long time without noticing they don't age normally. You can probably spend one to decades in the same place before moving on. But if children don't age, it'll be noticed quickly. That does not present a problem for Zantosa and Bratovitch who live isolated from humans, and can do various things to limit their interaction with them. Much harder for the Grimaldi. How do you handle this in the setting as the ST?

    Obertus have another issue. They do seem to live in isolation from humanity, but instead of living on family estates they gather in villages and even govern themselves "normally" like having municipal elections and such. The issue here isn't hiding among humanity. They probably keep outsiders out of their communities and create any false documents as needed like birth and death certificates. The issue is given the small number of Revenants, the Obertus likely number in the same range as Bratvotiches and Zantosa. Let's be generous and give them 500. How many villages can they possibly have? We're told they can be found in New England, the Midwest, and Central America. Does that mean all Obertus live in a total of three villages - one in each of those regions - of around 150-170 members each? It's possible to have villages this size, but they are very rare. There wouldn't be many places of business or public buildings. You could still theoretically elect a village president or mayor, and even a council, but they wouldn't have a whole lot to do. We can make it work, but the greater issue is should there really only be three villages total to account for the entirety of this revenant family - let's call them Dunwich, Gatlin, and Mayincateca for convenience. I imagine in addition to being remote, they are also isolated by geography in someway to prevent outsiders from stumbling upon them by accident and making the region around them unattractive for development. As home to around one-third of the whole revenant family (as opposed to just a clan) you can't just pick up and flee. You have to make yourself a hard target. Is that how you see it in your chronicles?

          The Addams family is one example that comes to mind regarding how Revenants live, obviously rich with a fancy house, yet its old and dilapidated. The family gets together for the big reunion and ancestral songs and dancing. Another good example good be the villains in season 1 of True Detective. Also, there are some real families that have been discovered living in complete isolation for generation, engaging in incest over those generations. Very strange, but true.

          As for Bratovitches, I see them living like Hillbillies. Deliverance maybe. I’m from Appalachia and sometimes I’ll drive through a community and think, hey this is a Bratovitch community.

          When it comes to Revenant children, I have always ignored the thing about taking decades for them to mature. I never liked that idea with D&D elves, who could still be toddlers at an age where humans are full grown, and I don’t like it with Revenants. Aging and growing are two different processes. Slower aging doesn’t mean slower growth.

          Anyway, even if you go with slow growth, all Revenant children are going to either be homeschooled, taught by tutors, or educated in some kind of special place designed to accompany them. In any case, the kids will be removed from mainstream society until adulthood, even if they will be expected to go out some day and enter the mortal world.

          For the Grimaldi, I see their lifestyles and dwellings as being the closest to normal human rich people. They are probably going to want mansions with some degree of isolation, as many rich humans do, but likely also maintain homes in the city for when they must do business there.

          I could see those Obertus villages as being these spooky sorts of places where things seem off. Like Wicket Man as NOoC says. Obertus, even preferring isolation, will likely want to have some interaction with human population centers because that is often where knowledge is concentrated and they are a scholarly family.

          I see the Zantosa as being the most like the Addams family. Rich but debased, removed from mainstream society yet still tenuously connected through various means. Really perverted too, which is played for laughed until the Addams family, but if it was played straight it would be pretty disturbing.

                                          The revenant’s extended childhood is another of those things that puts a lie to the “dhampirs and revenants are basically the same thing, just culturally different” statements.

                                          Having your physiological and cognitive development in sync until you reach young adulthood is a big deal for normal development. A Dhampir who looks twenty is probably in their mid-thirties at most and could still present themselves as their chronological age without difficulty (good genes are a thing) and would only really start getting implausible when they reached some point in their 40s.

                                          By contrast, because congnitive development is more a matter of experience forming synaptic pathways rather than physiological changes to the brain over time, it means that a revenant’s physical development will always lag significantly behind their cognition. Basically, a five year old revenant will have known HOW to speak and move their limbs in a coordinated enough fashion to walk for years before their bodies are physically developed enough to allow them to actually do so. The revenant who looks seven years old could have the academic side of being a brain surgeon completed even as their body is still working out the fine motor skills needed to actually perform the acts. The 40 year old revenant looks 8-10 years old and is still a couple decades away from hitting puberty.

                                          Basically, just on biology alone revenants are going to be messed up in the head. Throw in spending all of that time they’re developing surrounded by a culture based on servitude to blood drinking immortals and, frankly, there are probably Umbrood whose mental processes are more comprehensible to mortal humans (or to normal fledgling/neonate vampires for that matter).

                                          Now, one thing that could be a game-changer of sorts for younger revenants is the explosion of social media/online identities where you never need show your face in the past couple decades. A revenant who looks 5, but is cognitively in their early 20’s can hang with online friends in an MMO or post funny memes to their Twitter friends just like any other 20-something can (voice chat would need some thought, though just being able to discuss topics at a level of complexity expected of an adult, a mediocre mic and a little modulation will remove just about any suspicions).

                                          In other words, it’s possible that the era of socially maladjusted revenants might be waning as the early adopters of internet forums, MMO’s and chat rooms are probably in their 40’s by now and will finally be physically mature enough to pass as a (young) adult in another decade or two.

                                          This would particularly be true if the main physiological element of brain development (neural plasticity) was similarly extended. The short version of which is that plasticity refers to the ability of the brain to adapt to changes. Children’s brains are far more plastic than adults and reduced plasticity is one of the reasons people get more set in their ways and are less able to adapt to changes in technology or cultural trends.

                                          But if Revenants maintain brain plasticity in tune with their physiological age, they’d still be able to keep up with evolving trends and technology until well past the chronological century mark. A 60-75 year old revenant would be able to keep up with all the changes just like a human 15 year old can... and will probably still be able to adapt even as the teenage mortal friends they made at 70 are yelling about youngster needing to get off their lawns.

                                          A Revenant as a social chameleon who broke free of their family at, say, 75 (looking 15-ish) is now 120 (looks mid-twenties) and has been hanging out in human subcultures since the mid-70s (i.e. back when getting a job right out of high school was normal and 15 could pass as a baby-faced 18-20) is absolutely a viable concept if you run with the brain plasticity angle.

                                                      How should the Revenant families best be used? There seems to me to be a difference between how Revenants are usually depicted in games versus how they were originally described.

                                                      In the original ST's Guide to the Sabbat we were told that while the Bratovitch, Zantosa, and Grimaldi knew of each other, that the Obertus were almost totally unknown. Later sourcebooks ignored this and portrayed things as if they all knew of each other. It's purely a matter of taste, but I like the idea that the Obertus were practically unknown.

                                                      While the Revenant families once provided the Tzimisce and Sabbat with valuable retainers, spies, and recruits in previous times, by now the Revenant families don't serve much real purpose to the Sabbat and are not destroyed simply because the Tzimisce consider them part of their culture and are loathe to see any more harm done to them. Since 4 other Revenant families loyal to the Sabbat have already been destroyed, we should keep in mind how much they are loathed. In actual game play, the impression I get is that the Revenants are a much more utilized resource for the Sabbat, likely because STs just want to do something with the concept. Much like how the Kiasyd, explicitly not of the Sabbat, somehow became members of the Sabbat.

                                                      But I do think the Revenant families provide some some utility to the Sabbat.

                                                      I think the Bratovitch serve two purposes. First, their estates make good spots for nomad packs in North America to visit as way stations while they travel. They are a known safe space in a hostile wilderness of Lupines and Camarilla Archons/surveillance (I imagine there are several Archon packs similar to the Wolfpack of Chicago By Night that patrol various areas of North America). Bratovitch husbandry also provides lots of animal blood and perhaps human depending on how recently some poor travelers came across one of their estates.

                                                      Despite their reputation for being Lupine hunters, I think it should be obvious that most Bratovitch would be slaughtered going against a Lupine pack, and even fighting a lone Lupine would be risky and hazardous. And given their low numbers, they can't risk a destructive fight that might bring one or more packs that could wipe out an entire estate. The loss of such members would be catastrophic. But I don't think that means they don't serve some anti-Lupine role. First, Bratovitch likely have their estates in areas NOT known to have Lupines. So their presence indicates the area is safe. Second, there is likely enough hand me down lore that is still useful for them to identify evidence of nearby Lupine activity. They can recognize spor, have unique dog breeds that recognize scent, and they probably have lots of silver weapons on hand.

                                                      If they actually kill any Lupine near their estates, it'd be the equivalent of lone Ronin and Lost Cubs. Maybe a travelling Silent Strider exploring an area after a long while. In so far that they might attack an actual small sept of Lupines that represent a small number of Garou, I imagine it happens like this. First, the various Bratovitch estates do a lot of scouting. They explore the wilderness a lot and travel far away from their homes in order to know what areas are really dangerous or relatively safe. Maybe they might need to relocate some day. Or maybe it's just good information to have to share with Sabbat some time. Second, at some point they actually find a small sept of Lupines. And when that happens, the various Bratovitch clans plan an attack. They salvage abandoned automobiles from the junkyard, put false license plates on them, and coordinate a very large group to this small and isolated group of Lupines. They do as much necessary to stack the deck in their favor, and to hide anything on them that might be used to locate where they came from so if things go bad, the Lupines can't track them back home. Such attacks probably don't happen very often. Maybe not even once a generation, maybe only once or twice a century. And the Lupines killed aren't many - maybe 3-6 against 20-50 Bratovitches plus ghouled dog packs that attack them. But because they do succeed, they brag it up and they have that reputation.

                                                      The Zantosa are supposed to be almost entirely useless. Part of that was the metaplot reason that some of them were guardians of the Tzimisce Antedeluvian and thus actual traitors to the Sabbat. But even those loyal don't provide much. Having estates, they too can provide spots in the wilderness for nomad packs and others to camp out temporarily. However, I think they also provide another benefit that fits their debauched reputations. They probably act as procurers to the Sabbat for normal humans used in ritae like the Blood Feast when the big Sabbat gatherings occur. Their degenerate ways using drugs and sex likely enable them to find and groom lots of willing victims who don't learn what will really happen to them until too late.

                                                      The Obertus likely do research for only a small handful of elder Tzimisce who need obscure lore researched. That might include some kolduns. And the Obertus might be unacknowledged cocreators of a lot of Tzimisce experiments like the Blood Brothers. They might make a somewhat effective, though minor replacement for the loss of House Goratrix in terms of Tzimisce thaumaturgical research. Why shouldn't Obertus not be able to use Blood Sorcery if they have vitae of their own? And possible that some of their members might be Infernalists - not outright on the Path of Evil Revelations, but in the sense of knowing some Dark Thaumaturgy and trying to outwit the devil. Outside of this, they are likely left alone and work on their own goals.

                                                      Grimaldi of course are charged with providing for the Masquerade needs of the Sabbat and providing the archbishops they are blood bonded to with cash. This is actually an interesting statement, as wouldn't the vaulderie destroy such bonds? Even if the Grimaldi don't know or practice that rite themselves (though since they have their own vitae, it's possible they could do so), the vaulderie is very open knowledge in the Sabbat. Needless to say, relying on the blood bond in a sect based on destroying such things appears to be an error. I imagine somewhere in the Grimaldi larger family, someone knows how to perform the vaulderie and passes that knowledge on. Maybe long thought dead and unknown to the Sabbat. They're just waiting for the perfect moment. I think it was part of the Revised metaplot that the Grimaldi were to defect to the Ventrue. Since the Grimaldi are so closely tied to the Sabbat bishops, I imagine they are only present in cities where the Sabbat is in power. They could also be disguised as personal ghouls to Sabbat infiltrators who can pass messages along until they reach the Sabbat bishops. Needless to say, all these bishops should be Tzimisce since they'd be loathed by other Sabbat. So this gives the Tzimisce a lot of indirect influence that balances out what the Lasombra may have.

                                                      None of this is new - all pulled from existing material. But hopefully explored in more detail.

                                                      However, I think overall the Revenant families shouldn't be very prominent even in a Sabbat setting. They are more of a background element unless the ST has a particular plot element that utilizes them heavily. But I think they can be used to explain or justify certain strange things in the setting that might even show up in other games.

                                                      I think their main issue from an ST perspective, is that their relatively small numbers mean if a plot goes sideways, a significant portion of them could get wiped out and altering the game world setting to a large degree. That's not necessarily a bad thing, but many STs want to preserve these elements for further use, not have a chronicle detailing why 1/3 of the Obertus suddenly died and having the repercussions of which hijack the game.
