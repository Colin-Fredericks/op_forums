            I've yet to use revenant families in my games in any substantive capacity. But I like the idea of them, and they're there if I want to do anything with them.

            I don't think the Grimaldi slowed aging is a particularly big handicap. If their Tzimisce masters really want to, they can just fleshcraft a revenant to make their biological age match their actual age. Vicissitude isn't in-clan for the family, but there are probably a few who've learned it anyway. Their shtick is supposed to be upholding the Masquerade and it's too useful a Discipline for someone not to pick up.

            Beyond that, the family probably has their walled estates in posh neighborhoods where discretion is just another commodity their wealth can purchase. Children are home-schooled and sent to elite colleges once they look old enough to pass as members of the student body. Some may not bother, but they're supposed to be Masquerade experts and many probably maintain mortal identities. They pick up credentials, go to work at family-connected institutions, and stick around until their slowed aging becomes too obvious to conceal via makeup. Once that happens, or even if they get help from Vicissitude but enough years go by, they probably retire that identity, forge a new one, and pop up in another city with a Grimaldi presence as "Cousin X from out of town." Having the support infrastructure of an extended family is a big asset there.

            In my setting, the Grimaldi family replace some of the real-life wealthiest people in Mexico. Telecom feels like something the Technocracy would sink its claws into, but mining or retail are good sources for the Grimaldi wealth in the country. Given how old and widespread they are, they probably get their wealth from a lot of sources, like, uh, also being the royal family of Monaco. If I ever used them in a game, I'd replace Albert II with a fictional analogue to avoid 5e's mistake.

            A World of Darkness 2e also did something with the Grimaldis in Monaco. I don't remember if they presented the family's rule over the country being a secret or not. If it is, then "Grimaldi" probably is not the name that most vampires know them by. Then again, maybe not. The Sabbat is supposed to be an incredibly insular and secretive organization, and most Camarilla vampires probably can't name any of the revenant families. Much like the Obertus being a secret, "the Sabbat is extremely secretive" was an idea that setting books presented as fact but never really followed up on. I can't think of any canon instances where Camarilla vampires were especially ignorant of the Sabbat's history or culture at an inconvenient time. (This was supposed to be what made infiltrating the sect so hard, along with, obviously, the vaulderie.)

            I don't think the Sabbat would be inclined to include mere revenants in vaulderies, much less share the knowledge of how to perform independent vaulderies. The vaulderie is the heart of Sabbat culture and I don't think packs would see revenants as worthy to participate in that, even if they're a step up from human ghouls. I also don't like the idea of revenants/ghouls being able to "contribute" their blood towards vaulderies. I have no idea whether that's possible or not under V20's rules, but from a story perspective it doesn't make any sense; they already can't use their blood to bond other people. If a revenant participated in a vaulderie, it'd break their old blood bonds (and establish new vinculums), but that'd be it. They'd drain from the communal chalice without giving anything back.

            I like the Zantosas being procurers of vessels. Many Sabbat packs actively enjoy night-to-night hunting, but archbishops and other Sabbat luminaries probably turn to the Zantosas for help when they need lots of vessels for a blood feast.

            I don't like the Bratovitch reputation as werewolf hunters being essentially a load of hot air. From a story perspective/game mechanics aside, I like the idea that almost any individual revenant should lose against a Garou, but a well-coordinated team of revenants armed with silver should be a serious threat to lone werewolves. You (Black Fox) are probably right that most of their reputation comes from taking down lone Garou or rookie packs rather than seasoned ones, though.

            One thing that I don't remember lore answering anywhere is where, exactly, Obertus revenants come from. They obviously owe their origins to the Dracon's descendants in Constantinople, many of whom had ties to the city's monastic community. So when and why did those vampires start breeding revenants? Why aren't they simply Tzimisce descendants of the Dracon?

            Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.

                                                        The new revenant families mentioned in Libellus Sanguinus 1 were:
                                                        • Vlazy - remained loyal to the elders and destroyed during the Anarch Revolt.
                                                        • Khavi - wiped out by the Teutonic Knights possibly before the Anarch Revolt or during its early stages. Loyal to their methusaleh until they were destroyed.
                                                        It is also mentioned that besides these "major strains" (which includes the 4 known Sabbat families and the Krechevski/Duchevski) there are minor families like the Rustovitch and Ruthvenski. I imagine this was mentioned in order to give STs and PCs options to include additional revenant families in their backstories and chronicles without violating canon.

                                                        The new revenant families mentioned in Transylvania By Night. Since they were not mentioned in Libellus Sanguinus 1, we could say they are further examples of minor families. Though it is said these are "other families of note" which was likely intended to be "oh, these are major strains as well, just ignore that bit in LS1 that there were only 7 major strains, we'll introduce more whenever we want."
                                                        • Basarab - none survive by the 16th century. The 16th century begins at 1500 (or 1501 for you pendants out there). The Convention of Thorns happens in 1493. The Sabbat as an organization did not begin to form until afterwards, and wasn't anything we'd consider to be recognizable until Vestgeir saved the sect. Dracula was embraced in 1495 (the historical Dracula was long dead by then). So the Basarab were quickly wiped out before there could be a real Sabbat.
                                                        • Danislav - betrayed the Tzimisce using powers derived from their kinfolk status to the Shadow Lords, and subsequently destroyed by the Tzimisce. Since we're told the rebellion happened in the mid-14th century, and several Tzimisce united in 1399 to destroy them, they were likely destroyed very early during the Anarch Revolt.
                                                        So we have the names of at least 6 other Revenant families and know there are other unnamed minor ones. In other sourcebooks, we'll be given even more Tzimisce Revenant families.

                                                        I think it's a stretch of the imagination to say these four were meant to be the families that entered the Sabbat and were destroyed by them. They just fit the original idea that the Tzimisce Revenants had lots of various fates after the Anarch Revolt. Some were loyal to the elders and destroyed; some became independent or found new masters; and eight entered the Sabbat. At best only the Basarab (out of six known new families) might count as a revenant family that supported the Anarchs and thus be considered to have joined the Sabbat though this is not technically correct. However, this is just supposition as we're not told (as far as I know) that the Basarab sided with the Anarchs. It also doesn't fit the idea that other non-Tzimisce Sabbat wiped out the revenant families prompting the Tzimisce to save the others, as it were the Tzimisce themselves who destroyed the Basarab (and even Danislav).

                                                        So none of the four named families fit into what we know about the unnamed Sabbat families that were destroyed.

                                                        I think it's much more likely the writers either forgot entirely there were supposed to be four more Sabbat revenant families, or purposely left them out intending to develop them later or to remain a mystery.

                                                        Everyone's tastes are different though.

                                                        I'll likely need to create my own four Sabbat revenant families. I usually run historical chronicles (even if that means I begin my games in the 1920s, 50s, 70s, etc. before they reach "today") so it's entirely possible I might have a need for one of these destroyed families. But it's not a high priority.

                                                        However, the names dropped for the minor families of Rustovitch and Ruthvenski are likely important. We know the Tzimisce Vladimir Rustovitch becomes a Sabbat (although he is confusingly called a Bratovitch Revenant). We also know there was a line of powerful Tzimisce vampires which included Ruthven in their name, including the Anarch Lambech Ruthven. It's possible they followed these vampires into the Sabbat. Otherwise it's a curiosity they share the name of prominent Sabbat vampires. While their members might have married into other families at points, it's entirely possible that the "main line" of these families were the ones destroyed by other Sabbat. So I might just need to create the names of another two.
