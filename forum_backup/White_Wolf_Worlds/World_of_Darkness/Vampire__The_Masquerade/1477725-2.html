                Originally posted by wrxsv View Post
                1. The old KotE writeup was trash. 90's stereotyped high school history class sitcom foreign-man cliches superglued to street fighter-weeaboo fightan magic for extra dragon ball fandom. Because that's all their customers demanded of them at the time. Unfortunately, I think -some- actual east asian cultures are marginally interesting, and want nerd substance, depth, philosophy and anthropology, not just geek-porn pavlovian fandom. The biggest problem writers are going to have is simply that racism comes and goes in fads, and never ages well. Whatever they write to please the drooling(and paying) masses today will be anathema tomorrow, which is exactly how we got where we are now.
                2. Unlinking the Exalted from the O/N/nu-WoD was a colossal mistake. The splats are drawn from real world historical culture myths, with all the baggage and incompatibility that entails. Exalted gave the WoD it's own origin story and cosmological history separate from real world history. Specifically, the contemporary "western" vampires are the very distant incarnation of the abyssals, they're tied to elemental death and the dead old gods. The "eastern" vampires were highly evolved terrestrials, tied to the unique circumstance of the self-made and eclectic yama kings, and perhaps more than anyone but the sidhe, bound to human cultural tradition as an elemental force of it's own. That is why the linkage was important, it gave all the game lines a coherent cosmology to relate to each other, a shared meta-lore-framework for how they all relate. It also made the WoD it's own thing, separate from real world earth history problems and the fads of popular culture.
                3. There's no way contemporary WoD writers are going to produce anything but fad chasing drivel, there's just too much money and too much industrial process in the system now. They're churning out consumer product, not a game.
                I don't think that it's appropriate to say that the consumers of KotE are racist and "drooling masses"

                We even have two players from Asia who have commented, one of which really likes KotE and the other says that Chinese players think it's just okay.

                You say that you want substance, depth, philosophy, and anthropology - well I feel it must be said that a game based on playing make believe is not the best source for Asian philosophy and culture. There are actual books out there written by real experts about Asia and I would suggest those instead of a role playing book if you want to learn about Asia.

                One of the problems here is that the standards for KotE are unreasonably high. If the game doesn't have certain mutually exclusive qualities, then it's racist, which means people will see it as racist no matter what. It has to adhere to Eastern myth and religion (else it's cultural imperialism forcing Western religion and myth on Asia) but it can't be super special and must be compatible with V:tM. It can't depict Asia as being too different from the West, that would be exotification, and it can't depict Asia as being too similar to the West (or leave out Asian culture and history in general) because that would be erasure.

                I'm not saying that KotE didn't have problems, it certainly did, but I think one reason why it might be reasonable for the V5 authors to just not mention it is because any approach is to Asia is going to make people angry.

                I don't think that the standards for KotE (in regard to cultural accuracy and such issues) can reasonably be any higher than the standards for V:tM in general.

                                        Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post

                                        Yeah it's kinda of a tricky issue with any pan national faction in wod. Vtm for example can't really represent the abarhamic world collectively.
                                        Honestly it wouldn't be that bad with the Hungry Dead. After all it's the Dharmas that serve as the Clan analogue while the Thousand Hells are a multitude of Hells that we've only really seen a handful of. The Legend of the Ten Thousand Immortals and Grand Arhat Xue were always presented as Chinese-centric so it wouldn't be out of place for other regions to have their own divergent beliefs.

                                        After all, the Hungry Dead do draw a fair bit from vampire myths around the world from before Dracula and the modern iteration of vampires. At their core, the Hungry Dead are people who died, were dragged to hell and managed to claw their way out to their (or possibly someone else's) corpse. Hell, I've seen people over the years comment that you easily port the Hungry Dead to other settings with minimal fuss and that they are more crossover friendly than Cainites are. In fact, they are notably less overtly hostile to the other splats within Asia than Cainites are with theirs.

                                        I'd keep the Wan Kuei as we best know them from KotE as the Chinese Hungry Dead while other regions have their own beliefs on their origins, purpose and Dharmas. Though I would look into taking the four Major Dharmas tied to the four alignments, strip them down to the core and make them something universal among the Hungry Dead. The details may change between cultures but the general idea of the Dharma says the same. Of course that isn't to say that the influence of the Chinese Wan Kuei shouldn't be there among other Hungry Dead. Throughout it's history China influenced its neighbors and does so globally today. It would be absurd to think that the Wan Kuei wouldn't do so as well or that some non-Chinese wouldn't find their beliefs appealing.

                                        By that same token expand the Hells to include more inspired by other cultures. Like I said, we've only seen a few to any real degree and there are supposed to be a lot of them as you wouldn't call a few dozen Hells a thousand. Others and myself have in the past suggested making the Thousand Hells a global thing. Originally the Hells were largely tied to specific parts of the world but as the beliefs and cultures tied to those areas spread around the world, so too have the reach of the Hells and their Kings. A few of the Hells, like Mikaboshi's Wicked City, have become global in their reach. Which is a sign of dark things for Hungry Dead around the world whether it be a sign of the impending Sixth Age or another kind of apocalypse.

                                        Speaking of Mikaboshi, he and his agents would actually make for great cross-splat villains. The Hungry Dead hate him for obvious reasons. The Garou would hate him for embodying some of the worst aspects of the Weaver and the Wyrm together. His origins tie him to Chinese Mages, some of whom would likely be aware that such a powerful demon lord was once one of their own and gained his current power in part because of their teachings. And of course he's well-known to use all manner of agents in the WoD, including among the other splats and mortals so he could be a patron to many Infernalists around the world.

                                        Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. They're indomitable. Indomitable.

                                                    Honestly I've been seeing this argument or ones like it, claiming that the Hungry Dead are just like the Risen therefore they should be Rise, for years now. But beyond the surface level that's not the case at all.

                                                    For starters, for a Wraith to become a Risen is a conscious choice on the Wraith's part and there are particular requirements that they need to fulfill to become one. They need certain Arcanoi, the help of their Shadow, a Fetter and their corpse to still be in relatively decent condition. For the Hungry Dead they only need to have their soul taken to one of the Hells and to have escaped it. The state of their body doesn't matter as even if their remains are just ashes, their body will restore itself as they take their Second Breath. Nor do they require their own corpse to rise as there are the Yulan-Jin and the Different Body Flaw. The latter being where a HD takes their Second Breath from someone else's corpse and the former being a sub-species of HD that have fragile connection to their body and end up hopping from corpse to corpse.

                                                    And the HD don't even see to be fully aware of their actions when escaping from the Hells, being drawn to their corpse (or someone else's) by a force or compulsion they don't understand. In fact, the Resplendent Crane Dharma Book starts with a comic showing two Cranes witnessing another HD taking their Second Breath. When the soul comes to the spot where her body is buried, she starts frantically clawing at the ground, saying that she has to 'get back' while one of the Cranes comment that she's in the 'familiar madness'. A HD soul may choose to escape Hell but the choice to draw a Second Breath is out of their control.

                                                    There's also the fact that a Risen is a temporary state of being. The Wraith becomes one to fulfill some purpose they have and once that purpose is fulfilled, they don't last long, returning to their existence as Wraiths. A HD, even if they don't engage in a Dharma, could theoretically exist indefinitely.

                                                    Then there's the differences between the Passions that drive Wraiths and the Dharmic Paths of the Hungry Dead. Or how the P'o is more like the Cainite Beast than it is the Wraith's Shadow.

                                                    So yeah, there's as much of a connection between Risen and Hungry Dead as some people believe.
