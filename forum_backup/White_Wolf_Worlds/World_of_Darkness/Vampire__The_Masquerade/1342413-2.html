        Originally posted by Saga View Post

        Yeah, it's kinda a hypocrite point of view imposed by the elders that know their genealogy up to Caine.
        To me, it's explicable and reconcilable in that the Camarilla doesn't suppress that knowledge directly. The vampiric creation myth -- Caine, the curse, the flood, etc. -- as well as education about the clans, clan founders, and the generation system are all basic parts of any fledgling's education. If their sires don't teach them directly, they learn it from a mentor or peers. Any vampire who's learned their lineage, generation, or can simply count on their fingers can reason for themselves vampires came from somewhere, clans came from somewhere, and all those lineages have to converge somewhere along the way. Neither does the Camarilla expressly deny the existence of methuselahs, but like antediluvians simply explain them as elders of extreme low generation and great age.

        What the Camarilla is interested in suppressing, is Noddist lore beyond that as well as various forms of eschatologic belief, chiefly that of Gehenna. I'd wager the methuselah's thirst falls into that category as well, since that plays into Noddist/Gehenna lore. Antediluvians, as in ancient vampires who founded the clans way back when is fine, but "Antediluvians" the prehistoric angry blood gods who are coming to eat everyone during The End Times, is no fly.

                Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post
                So it's always seemed to me that in-setting, if the Camarilla's argument was that there are no Clan founders, nobody believes that for a second which would leave the Camarilla just looking like obvious dummies for trying to insist that the Clans don't have founders. Which is why I tend to believe they don't try to push that and instead just push the idea that Clan founders weren't particularly powerful, they weren't boogymen and that if any of them do still exist, they're no longer relevant, so who cares?
                This is how I've always portrayed the Camarilla's attitude towards the Antediluvians in my games too.

                In the opening fiction for Revised, the vampire tells his intended childe-to-be that the clans are descended from Caine and the Antediluvians. The clan books are all full of legends about their clan founders, passed down from sire to childe. There are tons of Kindred who sign letters with recitations of their lineages all the way back to Caine. There actually aren't that many (any?) specific examples of elders working to suppress knowledge of the Antediluvians among younger Kindred, but there's seemingly a thousand and one exceptions. None of this is helped by the fact how we as readers, and seemingly every character of consequence in the setting, all know the Antediluvians are real. Or how it's so indelibly baked into the game via the concept of generation, which basically every vampire who's not a rube knows about. It also begs the question of just what, according to the supposedly Antediluvian-denying Camarilla, actually did create vampires. I can buy that there isn't a unifying "alternative theory" being actively promoted, but the books don't even give us any example lies Kindred elders tell their childer to answer, "Where do we come from?" The idea just wasn't ever that developed.

                It's much neater to answer that the Camarilla believes in the Antediluvians. They just hold, seemingly quite reasonably, that the clan founders aren't relevant anymore. No one's seen them in millennia. (That isn't quite true, but that is something the elders outright lie about.) The Antes are either dead or in torpors too deep to ever awaken from. Some clans, such as the Ventrue and Malkavians, actively promote the idea their founders are dead and even claim to know how they died.

                Another lie the Camarilla can more subtly promote is that the Antediluvians aren't that powerful. The concept of generation and blood thickening with age may be widely known, but 6+ traits aren't. Most elders aren't casually showing those off, as they're bad for the Masquerade too, and many cities in the New World don't even have elders of sufficient age and generation to hold that coveted sixth dot in a Discipline. Most neonates probably figure traits cap out at 5 even for the Third Generation... if the Antediluvians were around, they'd be worldly, dangerous creatures like any elder, but they wouldn't be all-powerful blood gods. They'd be what elders basically are in V5.

                            You know, I always thought that the Camarilla always said that there were no Antideluvians, because that's a very Masquerade thing to do. Humanity doesn't get to know about vampires because it leads to things like the Inquisiton, makes it harder to feed, etc. Vampires don't get to hear about Antideluvians for the exact same reason - makes it harder for the ancients to control or feed on the young ones.

                            Its like... take the Ventrue Antideluvian. Word is, (s)he's dead and gone. But that's exactly what the Ventrue clan members do themselves. Fake their deaths, Dominate you to forget the truth, and pull the strings from the shadows while sipping on blood cocktails from their skull throne. The Camarilla is like a giant vampire-conspiracy onion. Peel back one layer, and there's another conspiracy there, followed by a third layer of string pulling conspiracies, and so on.

                            Gangrel, with their feral nature and animalistic tendencies, make poor fits for the conspiracy onion. Naturally, most fit better wild and free in the Anarch Baronies than trying to play the leashed hound, so they naturally gravitate that direction. Fundamentally, that's what its down to. Before V5, there wasn't really a choice between Anarch or Cam, but there is now. Everything else, the abuse and disdain, is just noise and friction on the way there.

                            Well, that, or the Sabbat. Feral hunters seem to fit with the diablarie-seeking theme of this sect.

                            ((As an aside, the three sects kind of remind me of the three Evils in D&D now. Tyrannical devils and their hierarchy, chaotic demons with dozens of lords, and the greedy daemons playing both sides for their payout.))

                                Well, if one clan has herd mentality is Gangrel. Most are conformists and just don't want to go taking action and making revolution, and neither do they want to be barked with orders by people they don't know or don't respect. Funny thing is they wouldn't make the Anarch Revolt 1 or 2, but now that there is a Anarch sect through and through where they can nestle themselves, that's where they will find they cool shade and fresh water.

                                About the Camarilla Antediluvian Issue, I do think they would say Antediluvians do not exist most times, like "pfff vampires aren't THAT old", not that powerful, mythological or etc., and I imagine most lineage citing vampires would stop at their House founder, not Clan founder. Like a grand-childe of Jan Pieterzoon would stop at Hardestadt and not go any further. With that, most lineages would stop at the Medieval period I assume. Most elders would probably say that no one is certain of the origins of the Kindred (Cainite is such and old and superstitious term, some might argue), which they aren't really, and that most Clan Founders (if there is such a thing) are dead, forgotten relics of ages past, more shrouded in myth than any facts, like the prophets and gods in most European religions. So all and all is a "don't think about it, no one cares" kind of deal.
                                Another point about this is that Camarilla acts like an association, where each vampire scratches another's back, so mostly they would be equals in theory. In lieu of this, I think most Elders wouldn't go showing off their herculean powers, mostly making a social dance of superiority to their fellows. At this point you can see 'okay, there are novices, journeyman and masters in this' (neonates, ancillae and elders right?), and it's alright, but what if this was true, and there were actually ultra-masters, and super hyper mega blast masters? Gods incarnate? The whole scheme would feel like a terribly shitty deal, and all the political game of the Camarilla would feel irrelevant. Besides... fear of these godly beings, and the full-on belief in them, is a Sabbat thing....
                                Camarilla does with Sabbat (and the opposite probably is true) what McCarthyism did in USA. Any relation with the material brands you then and there. Well, it actually sounds like a Farenheit 451 now.... or Brave New World. Besides, Camarilla acts modern and secular, Nodism is religions babble.

                                            Irrc, in Lore of the Clans, it said that the Gangrel joined mainly in the hope of using the other clans as canon-fodder for their enemies.                                             EVENTUALLY they went, well this experiment isn't working, screw it, dropping the Camarilla, but it took waaayyyy longer than it would have for creatures who don't live in slow-motion, and who actually care about their children.
