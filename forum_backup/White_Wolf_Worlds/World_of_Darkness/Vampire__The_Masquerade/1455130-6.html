            Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
            Other than the humanity/cash issue (and the part where the player should do missions for XP above all things) this mission is fine.
            The Humanity issue... is the issue. You can help bully someone to the point of suicide, and no matter what you get XP out of it, despite knowing exactly what you're doing, and you benefit from it mechanically. One of the larger problems with BL1 is how weakly Humanity is enforced. Model Citizen is an obviously evil side-quest, but you get to completely skip the "consequences of being evil" meter by not taking cash for the job. "It's not evil to break multiple laws for the express purpose of ruining a woman's career to such an extent that she kills herself from the public trauma, it's only evil if you take money for it," is an epic bad take. What's wrong with it is that the game doesn't give you any reason to (a) refuse the quest because XP and (b) feel guilty about it as the player.

            You're even free to bully the quest giver.
            Sure, if you want to give up on the XP, or spend a net $200 on another side quest to get the XP granting side quest back as an option.

            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
            It's a notable thing because there's so few examples of being able to be genuinely evil in the game that it stands out way more than it might normally.
            Huh... I think you're confusing, "so few examples of being able to genuinely risk losing Humanity in the game," with examples of doing things most people would consider morally repugnant.

            The whole reason Humanity is a joke in BL1 is because, as Jack lays out in the opening, it only applies to innocents (and even that is very poorly applied). The game basically encourages you to be Dexter Morgan: be a sociopath that targets criminals. That way you can easily end the game with Humanity 8-10 despite a body count of humans that would put you in the record books and get all the sweet sweet XP to actually beat the game instead of skipping out on all the XP if you want to actually play a somewhat moral character.

            The beginning main quest line is to get a powerful explosive, and then use it on a building full of living people.

            The most optimal way of dealing with Dennis and his thugs is killing them because on top of the XP/cash rewards sneaking gets (social gets the worst rewards) you can get a bunch of starter weapons for free and sell the ones you don't use to Trip for more cash.

            When you blow up the warehouse, stealth is actually the optimal path, but that means you still end up blowing up a ton of people. But that's OK, gang members working for the Sabbat don't count as people any more. Nevermind that one bum can kill (causing Humanity loss if you kill him to make sure he doesn't make noise) who's going to get blown up too since apparently killing him is wrong.. if you do it directly instead of with the boom.

            I don't know about you, but killing around three dozen humans in two acts of gang turf warfare (you're not even killing them as a vigilante or some appeal to that sort of thinking) is pretty evil. But the only Humanity loss is if you murder the homeless guy directly.

            I'm not against some evil quests in the game but I would have felt much better about it if you'd had the option of warning Tawni at the expense of a Masquerade point or something similar.
            Or if the Humanity loss wasn't avoidable. Or if you didn't get XP for it. There's no point in evil quests in a game with a morality meter if you can skip the morality meter while still benefiting from doing the evil quest.

            I dunno, discussing sex and sexuality in Vampire as well as gaming isn't an inherently bad topic for a horror game. Nor is it off topic that much since it relates to Bloodlines.
            It's a request to the posters in the thread considering how poorly the 21 page thread on sexism in BL1 went. I'm not asking you, as a mod, to force it.

We both love VtM, but you love V5, and I'm massively disappointed with it to the point of never wanting to play it. Because we have different perspectives and different wants out of a VtM TTRPG, just as we seem to have different wants out of a VtM cRPG. I confused that this is still something I have to explain.

                                                            I love action scenes. Being a vampire going hog wild on the Sabbat with a flame thrower, getting into fights with my katana, and the battle against La Croix's minions.
                                                            I love action scenes... I find them one of BL1's weakest aspects but something you have to engage with because the game gets more and more combat-mandatory as time goes on. BL1's combat scenes are generally tedious and boring as you face 2-3 repeated foes on repetitive maps with no effort at all into trying to add a sense of variety to the action via terrain and placement. The boss fights are generally just hard enough the casual players (esp. when deal with the janky physics) will get frustrated by them, but not challenging once more dedicated players figure out the one (and generally only one) way to beat them (which is generally also pretty tedious), making your losses feel cheap and your victories feel hollow.
