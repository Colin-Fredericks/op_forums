    Making Quietus not suck

    07-21-2020, 03:46 PM
    In V20, It does cool things, but it does not satisfy. The mechanics of powers beyond the second are sorely lacking.

    Silence of Death:
    Mechanically, the only complaint here is that V20's lore of the clans came up with cool Vizier/Sorcerer improvements to the power that are objectively better than the standard (warrior) version.
    Fluff wise; It fits the -cool assassin- vibe but there's a disconnect as literally every other power is about turning your blood into various poisons, so one could only explain away this power as POISONING SOUND.

    Scorpion's touch:
    Cool power. It should be clarified if stamina is lost before or after damage done with a weapon, however, or how long the poison may last (indefinitely if I keep it in my body and don't fill up on blood?)
    The DAV20 version is has a good amount of complexity and is nicely detailed. However, It's absurdly strong.

    Dagon's call.
    Here's where Quietus becomes a bad investment.
    For vampires, this doesn't work well unless your victim is a loser with terrible willpower (in which case easier methods of dispatching such a foe are almost surely available) and for humans it works well but... wouldn't mundane poison suffice?

    So the solutions to Dagon's call I thought of:

    - Everything works the same, but make the damage Aggravated. (that said, you should be able to incapacatate a vampire foe risking their final death)
    -Everything works the same, but the difficulty to attack is capped at 6 and the difficulty to defend is capped at 9
    - The Victim rolls stamina+fortitude, the attacker rolls stamina+Quietus, both at difficulty 6 Way too powerful.
    - The victim rolls stamina+fortitude against a difficulty of the attacker's permanent willpower (maximum 9), the attacker rolls stamina.
    -Both sides contest stamina, difficulty of the enemy's willpower. However, each dot of fortitude the defender possesses reduces the defence's difficulty, and each dot of quietus reduces the attack difficulty to a minimum of 4. (If Alan the Assamite Attacker had 3 quietus and his opponent had 7 willpower, he would roll his stamina at difficulty 4 (7-3=4) , and If Vincent the Ventrue Victim had 4 Fortitude and Alan had 9 willpower, Vincent would roll his stamina at a difficulty of 5 (9-4=5) )
    -Both roll stamina, the base difficulty is 6: This difficulty is increased for the attacker per dot of victim fortitude quietus, while the attack difficulty is decreased by 1 per dot of quietus beyond the fist two levels. (someone with quietus 3 attacking an opponent without fortitude does so at difficulty 5)
    -Both roll stamina, the base difficulty is 6, The victim adds their Fortitude minus the attacker's Quietus rating to their stamina pool (A higher quietus than fortitude does nothing)
    -Both roll willpower; Each success determines the difficulty of enemy's stamina roll. Too finicky.
    -Both roll stamina, the difficulty is the enemy's willpower, but the attacker reduces the difficulty by their quietus and the defender reduces the difficulty by their fortitude; Minimum 3, maximum 9.


    I think the first (It's simple and works well on vampires without becoming worse for humans) is pretty good. I had lots of ideas (some so shit I wouldn't even write em out)
    I feel Fortitude should be relevant in this roll, but I also don't want to make things too complicated or break things for other splats, and one must also keep in mind the synergy of Scorpion's touch...
    (Fortitude can of course be substituted by another splat's equivalent or whatever's relevant, Resist Toxin should be a good boon. )

    Baal's Caress
    perhaps a lawyer could point out there's no restriction on what kind of weapons you can use this on and win, but otherwise it's a level 2 power at level 4.
    Taste of Death:
    Also total shit. You can spit some acid. 2 dice of aggravated damage per blood spent (no per-turn wavier, so this might be effective when you're 7th gen)

    Now, I looked at DAV20. It did a smart thing by putting these two powers as one power. The Caveat there is that this power is absurdly powerful. Like, If you're going to make an Assamite, you're going to give yourself high willpower, and +4 or +5 damage added on to your now-aggravated +X weapon and it's carryover and your strength.... You should probably need a higher level power if you wanted to take on God like that.

    So, I'm thinking how can I make this level 4 power of aggravated acid poison good and balanced?

    Baal's Caress (possibly a good version)
    One blood point is converted into acid (normal per turn expenditure applies)
    When applied to a a brawl or melee weapon (you may coat your hands), you roll willpower (difficulty 6) and your successes give you a number of strikes (or turns grappling) that do +1 aggravated damage.
    You may instead leave the Ichor in your blood system, coat an object to be used as a trap, spit the poison, or otherwise leave the poison lying around. Should a victim make contact with the poison, roll willpower: Each success grants a turn where that poison does one level of aggravated damage. Like fire, A maximum of three levels of aggravated damage occurs per turn depending on exposure (A maximum of 5 damage per turn per points the victim ingested) The difficulty to soak is 8 when delivered this manner. One point of blood turned to acid effects one person; If a person touches multiple points of poison in a single turn without increasing the area of exposure (IE, touching two points worth of the ichor with one hand) only one point effects him (The other remains active and awaits either a new part of the body, a second person, or for the turn to pass) If an infected hand still burning from the ichor touches more poison in a latter turn, that poison may increase the duration (Roll the Assamite's willpower again)

    Finally, if you apply this poison to a ranged weapon, or you decide to stab a man with a poison weapon and leave the weapon in him, the initial strike does standard aggravated damage (normal soak difficulties) and the projectile or weapon's poison will follow the system of non-strike poison : It will cause damage at the rate of one level per turn, difficulty 8 to soak, maximum of three damage per turn, until the successes on the willpower roll have been used up. Firearms cannot deliver this poison as the heat destroys it.



    Therein, by merging powers 4 and 5 together, we have a level 5 power to work out...
    Quicken the Mortal blood from DAV20 is absurd. We need a new power.

    Lazy Idea 1:
    Blood of destruction.
    Super acid poison, like, difficulty 9 to soak, destroys inaminate objects with ease; Carve through safes and warship hulls with this power. Something to that effect. I'm lazy describing it, but I can imagine it'd be pretty versatile. Perhaps it destroys precisely what you want and leaves other stuff untouched.

    Idea 2:
    Poison the Well
    Your blood, or the poison of any quietus power you know, goes into a mortal without effecting them at all. Anyone who drinks from that mortal suffers the consequences, be it a blood bond, Scorpion's touch, Baal's Caress, you may launch Dagon's call at will on anyone who touches the mortal; All of this as if you were the one there. (your stamina and willpower is used, not the mortal's) This power would be the nightmare of all Ventrue. (There was a level 6 power that did almost this I think)



    So
    Thoughts? Prayers? How would you all fix Quietus?

          Random musings:

          So, my biggest issues with Quietus have always been that the first power has no thematic connection to the others (as mentioned), and that the powers feel…redundant. Turn your blood into poison, turn your blood into better poison, turn your blood into more versatile poison…

          Some other clan-specific disciplines have the same issues (like Vicissitude, which is three powers that all do the same thing to different extents, and two unrelated Protean powers). And I'm not sure it's something that can be entirely fixed within V20's paradigm. Maybe a good model would be Chimerstry, which does a good job of making the enhancements feel meaningful, imo: each one feels like a significant upgrade over Ignis Fatuus in and of itself, but comes with a significant cost that means you won't want to use it every time. But "poisonous blood" is a very narrow space to work within.

          Maybe borrow some ideas from the Tremere's Path of Blood? "Manipulate properties of vitae" is a larger thematic space to explore than "your blood is poison". Take some of the elder abilities, like "inflict the drawbacks of high Generation on someone", and move them down into the easily-accessible 1-5 progression; mix them with variants on some of the Tremere abilities, like stealing vitae from a distance, which could be an enhancement to Dagon's Call. And after all, the Banu Haqim were following the Path of Blood for thousands of years before the Tremere showed up. (That last one is a joke, if it wasn't clear.)

                          I wasn't suggesting the entire discipline be about keeping things quiet, just that how it is used matters a lot.

                          As for the sound of a riffle shot, there are three sounds that occur when a shot is taken.
                          1. The report of the rifle which is the gases pushing the bullet out of the barrel
                          2. The sound created by the bullet itself as it goes through the air which is a function of how fast it is traveling
                          3. The sound of the round hitting the target.

                          The initial rifle report is the thing which allows you to get a baring on the direction of the shot, especially if the shooter is stupid enough to fire more than once from the same position without a counter measure. This issue can be reduced by a suppressor or completely canceled by V20 Quietus 1.

                          The speed at which the bullet travels often goes above the speed of sound, especially in 50 cal or better rounds. This means the sound you hear is a small sonic boom that you catch only as it passes your position, so it is not a directionality thing. But this has an interesting side affect, namely that a bullet will hit the target before the sound of the shot can be heard by the people next to the target, depending on range and powder load this can be 5+ seconds worth of time difference. Thus the targets head explodes and then when everyone is screaming or diving for cover the sound hits the area.

                          Alternatively you can use sub-sonic rounds which don't hit nearly as hard or go as far but like wise don't create the mini-sonic boom sound as it flies through the air. Also if we are talking about planned shots, part of that is considering the sound levels near the target, traffic noise, a night club, a water fall, etc. So even minor bullet whistle isn't going to help figure out the shooters location.

                          As for the heavy metal core being removed and reducing effectiveness, that only applies if you are trying to maintain the rounds armor penetrating capability. When hitting soft targets it is often more advantageous to have the round hit and tumble around inside the target than pass straight through. So if you are hunting a werewolf, using a custom lead round with silver talons and an internal blood vial is far more lethal than a standard slap round that can cut through a manhole cover.

                          Of course there is nothing stopping a marksman from using a variety of rounds to achieve different jobs, like the first shot is a standard AP round to break a barrier or disable a vehicle, the second shot is the custom round for what ever you are hunting and the third is an incendiary round to touch off the gas tank or start a fire. As for the vitae spoiling, given the amount of planning that goes into a given shot and the distances involved it you wouldn't be using a full mag of these things, just one per target and its not like you can't just bring the press with you and assemble the blood rounds on site just before the shooting starts.

                          As for saving your hearing, yes and no. You might be safe from the sound of the bang in so much as the decibel level produced. But Quietus technically does nothing to counter pressure wave differences which is what blows out ear drums and depending which studies you look at can cause internal injuries when wearing body armor. The way the power is written it only counters half of the issue with explosives. But hey its all made up vampire magic so there you go.

                          Poison is a theme, but not a strong one. Namely because writers tend to think only about doing damage with poison and then they get stuck on the rails of only more damage. A better way to look at poison is as a tool kit. Like have one level be a movement debuff, another level being a hallucinogen that makes the target delirious, a different level be a damage boost, then a final level be something really nasty like paralysis or making the victims vitae become inert for a night. Its kind of the difference between the writer who has no idea how poison works verses some one like Paracelsus who understands that any substance can be dangerous it is merely a question of dosage. Thus the true assassination potential comes from a tool kit for a variety of situations rather than variations on just doing damage.

                          I can suggest some excellent reference sources if you would like to learn more.

When you put a round into a lupines skull from over a mile away, most of their protections don't work or don't have the range required to alert them ahead of time. Further more by the time the other lupines figure out what is happening the shooter is long gone, usually with a few claymores strategically placed to add to the chaos when they close in on the empty firing position.

                                                      The blood core is just something as simple as swapping the chemicals used in a tracer round with a blood capsule. It doesn't really affect the bullet that much, any damage reduction by mass is made up for by damage increase and type by the poison. Like I said the reduction in penetration on a soft target actually means more damage than the round going straight through the target and the next to walls.

                                                      You will notice I specifically mentioned silver talons, contrary to many gaming supplements, silver makes for a horrible bullet material. Silver is harder than lead, copper, or most other bullet materials. The result being that a silver bullet is too hard for the spiral grooves inside the barrel to bite cleanly into the material and force the round to spin. The result being that the bullet "bounces" down the barrel which absolutely kills accuracy, especially at long range. So you use an outer jacket of a softer metal to achieve proper spin, you use silver talons near the core of lead, with a blood capsule near the back of the round. On striking the target the softer and heavier material peels back like a hollow point, this frees up the silver talons to go different directions inside the body, the last thing to enter and interact with the wound is the blood capsule which breaks as it enters the body. The silver does damage as long as it is in the wound, but you can dig it out with some surgery skills, poison on the other hand goes right into the blood stream which is impossible to "dig out". So yeah, solid silver bullets are highly impractical.

                                                      I get the desire to keep things simple, but gaming is a lot like food. You can only eat so many Mcdonalds dollar burgers before you start wanting a steak and potato dinner with all the fixings. So there is fun to be had being more detail oriented in some aspects of the game.
