    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post


    1. OPP's help: I think Onyx Path has pretty much been carrying water for 5th Edition from the beginning and a large part of that has been patching holes and spackling the holes in the setting, One thing Onyx Path has an interest in that Paradox Interactive doesn't is revisiting old material. 5th Edition was supposed to be something of a clean slate and jumping on point. Which makes sense because only the die hards (like the people here) wanted to follow up on plots that haven't been followed up on for 15 years. BJD aside.
    I have a bit more nuanced vision of this: the original V5 Team (Ericsson et al.) were very keen for revisiting old material. Far from being a clean slate, V5 Core, Anarchs and Camarilla are loaded with kudos and nods to older material, and seem very much into establishing a direct continuation of the old setting (of course, with some variations).

    Nonetheless, I think we are now in a "soft reboot" moment: with Justin Achilli as Lead Designer, the move towards the "clean slate" is fairly evident, and also openly proclaimed. To be fair, the "clean slate" is also a bit evident in lates OPP stretch books: this is, perhaps, a consequence of Paradox directly taking the lead of the project. After all, Justin has already presided over two fairly huge shake-ups of the setting, Revised (which soft-rebooted many things of Second Edition) and Requiem (which hard-rebooted the whole game).


    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    3. Orphans of Enoch: I think the Tal'Ma'Re is kind of a sad scoop up of the concepts introduced in V20's version of the True Hand. They had some fascinating ideas about how to redeem the silly concept of Dirty Secrets and I think we can all agree OPP's version is superior in every way. Unfortunately, Paradox's official opinion seems to be EVERYTHING IS CANON (from the original game) so the nuking of both the Ravnos as well as the Black Hand is 100% correct. So if the Baali did join the True Hand, they were presumably nuked with the rest of Enoch. Such a shame.
    And now, I will say something very unpopoular: I think that V20's version of the True Hand is both the weakest V20 book and the less coherent version of the TBH in the history of metaplot. It's a hodge-podge of very different factions, which fall together for .... no particular reasons. 2nd Generation cultists, Baali, Antediluvian cultists, feminist Cultists worshipping a deity whose children were killed by Antediluvians, fundamentalist defender of "Old Clans" and Mutant Faerie Vampires ... what exactly does V20 True Hand stands for?

    Took separately, all the factions in the book make for very interesting Cults: put together, I really struggle to get the reasoning behind lumping them together.

    The Hecata, who are very much criticized for being a strange collection of people with too distant motives, are really a Happy Family compared to V20 True Black Hand !

                                    I already bought and read the book, I enjoyed it. Forbidden Religions is an interesting supplement which introduces cool cults. I liked the Praesidium and the Spear of Orthia as playable cults, and the Withered and the Soldiers of the Adversary as antagonist. I also get the easter egg reference to the Baali character, Annazir.

                                    About the Talmahera, well, it is fine. Just fine, they have find a way to recover some of their significance, but taking distance from the errors of the past. They notably disregard the recruitment of new neonates and they do not use Oblivion, never. Maybe for the consequences the discipline had in the past.

                                    The worst thing, for me, about FR was again the lack of research with the Spanish culture. As already happened with Aluc Romas de Leon, we receive a new character with an inexistent surname, Maria del Torro. I am thankful for having the South of Europe depicted in the book, but this is not the way, do not emphasize the exoticism of foreign countries letting them being ruled by mad cults with inexistent names, please.

                                    ---

                                    James_Willoughby , I agree with the others in that The guide of the Talmahera is probably not the best book of V20, with a mix of incoherent ideas that rivals the Dirtys secrets of the black hand, and with a chronicle that is hard to play, hard to storytell and even hard to understand. They also included the rules from Dark ages mage there, the Maeghar, garou abominations, advanced diablerie, demon-hunters baali and many more bizarre ideas.
