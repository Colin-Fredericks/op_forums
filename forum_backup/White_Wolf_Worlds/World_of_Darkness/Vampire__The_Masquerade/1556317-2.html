    Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post

    It is interesting to wonder which ‘Splats’ would best suit the individual regions and sub-regions of the USA (I know there’s at least one contributor to these forums who’d dearly love to bring out a RAGE ACROSS THE BERING/RAGE ACROSS ALASKA sourcebook).
    I've always been surprised that Rage Across Alaska wasn't Werewolf's second regional sourcebook back in 92 or 93. Besides the fact that it is vast swath of wilderness (almost the antithesis of NYC), had a native wolf population, was famously connected to the petroleum industry (among others), and a diversity of indigenous cultures, at the time the culling of the state's wolf population and environmentalist protests against it was something that made national news (if only briefly). I think it would have been interesting to see an area were the Red Talons and Wendigo were the most dominant Tribes, making up maybe half the population and getting to control the most powerful Septs/Caerns.


    Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
    Oh, and we can all agree that Florida is Absolutely CHANGELING: THE DREAMING territory, yes?
    The Northern part of Florida has always essentially been an extension of the Deep South. The lower portion, especially Miami, is a unique mix of that with the Caribbean and Northeast transplants.

    The Miami of the 80s is almost tailor made for Vampire (especially if you want to do something where current events provide excellent cover for violent confrontations between the Camarilla, Sabbat, and other factions).

    If you have never read any of Carl Hiaasen's novels, they are an excellent blend of environmental issues, local cultures (including the Seminole), and regional weirdness that will make you want to run a Werewolf game in southern Florida.

    Then there is Orlando and the noticeable presence of Walt Disney World. LA by Night flat out presented Disneyland in CA as the creation of the Technocracy, so WDW - which started its life as plans for a scientifically designed utopian "town of the future"- would probably be that by an order of magnitude.

    But yes, there is a lot of Changeling potential, especially in Miami.

                    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

                    I agree with James_Willoughby.

                    Doing the 50 states wouldn't just be an opportunity to better describe the distinctions of each area, which are many and worthwhile on their own.

                    More importantly, even, it would be an opportunity to do what WW couldn't at the time and explore how the fictional elements may vary, show how splats in different locations face distinct problems and find distinct solutions even to similar problems. As I said in another thread, we have a very, very big problem with four particular books, not because they're bad (although lack of locals and all that, they're quite messed up), but because they became staples for things that were supposed to not have a standard. A lot of how people think a Vampire city works isn't what's on the core books (especially the first two editions), but those four specific structures, rinse and repeat.

                    Also, I don't think dedicating each place to a main splat is a good idea. Every splats is everywhere from their respective perspectives, they don't usually limit each-other's presence even when they're not on good terms, as they spend most of their time hiding from each other. A state book may not have every splat cited, but should try to cover some bases and be clear that each one is, for their own account, the masters of the area. Or, if not, for quite specific reasons.
                    My concern is that by trying over every single state for every single ‘splat’ this project would risk spreading itself too thin trying to cover absolutely everything from every angle.

                    To my mind the best way to keep things interesting for the reader without placing too much of a burden on the writers would be to assign each state to the most suitable splat and then include just enough on other lines for interested parties to spin up their own ideas on the subject.

                    Remember, a good sourcebook is all about suggesting possibilities, rather than presenting the most complete picture.

                        Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                        Remember, a good sourcebook is all about suggesting possibilities, rather than presenting the most complete picture.
                        I would suggest that the main quality of a good sourcebook is a high utility value. This represents high usability for the reader, where the sourcebook completes work for the reader. A good example is the first edition of Chicago by Night, which could be used as plug-and-play module in a game; a Storyteller could read once, and then throw the players into this environment with almost no additional preparation beyond a basic idea of the framework of a chronicle. Conversely, an example of a book with low utility value (and we will go for a location sourcebook, because of the subject of the thread) is Iberia by Night. It’s not a badly written book in a technical sense, but has so little detail on the fundamentals that it barely helps with chronicle preparation. If I’m going to have to source some maps, generate the NPCs, work out the big background dynamics, and so on, it’s barely worth picking up the book; I may as well make everything up from scratch.
