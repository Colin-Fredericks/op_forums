    Originally posted by Sergeant Brother View Post
    True Love is so amazing that it can allow a farm boy to beat a fencing wizard in a duel, out-wrestle a giant, and defeat a man who makes Plato, Aristotle, and Socrates seem like morons in a battle of wits.

    But what about a mutual blood bond? The problem with the blood bond is that it is a magically imposed obsessive love, not actual love. Real love between people is based on qualities of those people, how they interact with each other, in shared values and experiences, on elevating each other. The blood bond doesn’t need those things, so the obsessive feelings of love could continue even if the person who shares your mutual obsession makes you miserable, even if you grow apart and are destructive towards each other - the unnatural love persists. That sounds very dangerous to me.
    I knew this was going to come up.

    It's also capable of making someone survive the horrors of war, getting raped by Bulgars, being married off to a governor to insure their financial well being, survive being hunted by the inquisition, leave behind an advanced society where the buildings are made of gold, enslavement by the Turks just to finally be reunited and find the girl's looks are gone, then settle on a farm only to find that they've all been changed so much by what they've seen and endured to find the life dull and quarellsome.
    The Project Gutenberg eBook of Candide, by Voltaire.
    https://www.gutenberg.org/files/19942/19942-h/19942-h.htm


    For every Buttercup and Wesley, there's probably a few Candides and Cunégondes.

    I'm not saying that true love between Vampires is impossible, just that it would be insanely hard to maintain. How many of the few that had it would even seek Golconda? How many are totally into the predator thing and would never even seek Golconda? How many of those that do seek it would even manage to find a tutor. How many wouldn't get in a situation where they sacrifice themselves for their lover? Making true love last for centuries as an undead predator should be insanely difficult. Not technically impossible, but so challenging that it's pretty much a legend.

        My own personal thought on this is that ultimately the Blood Bond can't help but be destructive -- primarily because it ACTIVELY interferes with the ability to have genuine emotions.

        Your lover asks you to do something for him or her -- to make some deep sacrifice. You know you want to do it... but you wonder, is it really what you want? Or is it just the bond? Your lover asks forgiveness for a serious trespass. You forgive him or her, but was it really because you loved him or her?

        Once you subject yourself to the bond, you can't trust that your own feelings are genuine. And if you can't trust them, you can't really say that you're feeling them.

        After even a few *decades* of this (let alone centuries) it would be enough to destroy any relationship.

        Except that you can't leave.

                    I feel strongly that the idea that the bloodbond isn't "real love" to be wishful thinking. IDK, maybe I've never experienced true love because I've seen some depictions in media and, though I've looked at people and genuinely felt happy that they were happy, happy to be with them, and absolutely sure that I'd be very upset if something happened to them, and retroactively I thought my IQ dropped a hundred points, My vision never went pink and flowers did not materialise in my vision.

                    If the bloodbond does that to you, why isn't it love? I think you're looking at it from an ethical point of view, not a realistic point of view.

                    Werewolves have a "You absolutely want to fuck me power" innate to them, and originally it only cared about target willpower and your good looks, but later on it got the caveat of "but this power absolutely respects sexual orientation and doesn't work on people who don't like you" and again, this is someone concerned about "ethics" rather than whats realistic (within a fantasy setting, yeah, nah, don't start that argument)

                    Love is love, magical or hormonal or both. Saying "blood bond is not real love" is purely a matter of putting up an ethical shield around a scary topic.

                                    The answer in simple terms is that the Blood Bond is not real life because it's mind-control. This isn't really a very hard concept to get. The Blood Bond makes you utterly and completely devoted to someone regardless of how they treat you or actually feel about you. The thing about real love is that you are capable of adapting to it. If your partner is an abusive awful jerk, you can get the hell out of there.

                                    With Kindred, you are stuck "standing by your man" or your woman no matter what because you are their slave. It is why most ghouls are 9 different kinds of fucked up. In addition to the fact the Blood is meth to the normal human, there's also the fact that most Kindred are abusive and dismissive to their slaves. So the ghouls try HARDER to impress their masters and this makes them MORE dismissive.

                                    With Kindred, it's the same thing and you are stuck with each other if you have a mutual Blood Bond. Everything that might irritate you about them is going to be replaced with a feeling of utter devotion even when you are STILL irritated by it. So it builds up until you have a double-consciousness about it. You hate them and you love them but you can't do anything about it because the blood controls your thoughts.

                                    The Paynes in the original Chicago by Night shows how this can become incredibly toxic.
