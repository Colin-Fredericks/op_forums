    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

    I feel like this is a drastic misrepresentation of 1st Edition and really undercuts a lot of what made the game revolutionary at the time. Previously, the player characters were all meant to be monster hunters either of the D&D or Call of Cthulhu or Cyberpunk kind. What made V:TM so awesome at the time was that YOU were the monster. Yes, you COULD be a superhero with fangs but that was coming with the caveat of the Beast and Hunger.

    The game is all about the melancholy of being a vampire and the whole "Curse of Caine" business that encouraged you to try to resist the Beast, be an Anarch, and be LESS of a monster than the Elders (or worse, Sabbat). That didn't mean you weren't a monster, though.
    Or it was capitalist satire. Your milage may vary, but as soon as most players get into vampire they start trying to get rich. It's all about money, status, power. They get greedy and want to devour elders (even though they don't have a single attribute at 5 waiting to get to 6). Most of the "oh it sucks to be a vampire" seems like Torador melodrama, because mechanically the only real downers concern sleeping for the majority of the day and the fact that people older and more powerful than you are likely to object to your power grabbing natures. That obscene drive for riches made players monsters far more quickly than the Beast ever could.

    I think you dramatically overstate just how easy it was to avoid that if you were enforcing the rules as written as you could easily go through a lot of blood in a very quick amount of time using your Disciplines.

    If you were enforcing the rules as written then you'd know what a load you've just spouted. I'm aware your playstyle is more anarch splatterpunk than methodical masterminds but even then you're not going to be frenzying much.
    1: you shouldn't be needing disciplines much if you're any good at the game. Disciplines are there to make unlife easier, not a crutch you need to depend on.
    2: It might've been different from 1e, but most common disciplines before V5 didn't use blood. Dominate? entirely Free. Obfuscate? Free except when you want to raise appearance. Auspex? Free. Potence? Free. Only presence, celerity and the path-side of thaumaturgy used a significant amount of blood.

    Yes, getting insulted can be a frenzy check, but if you're half-decent with people you're not going to be insulted much.

        Originally posted by Rhywbeth View Post

        Or, to look at it another way, just because you're a monster doesn't mean you can't do good.

        Yes, yes, vampires are monstrous.

        All things that live (or &quot;live&quot; in their case) want to live and avoid suffering. The price for this is the death and suffering of others. Creation can not be initiated or sustained without destruction. You cannot exist without the sacrifices of others. Your house is built of dead trees and minerals torn from the Earth (destroying habitat in its wake), warmed by the death of primordial beasts, when you got that house many others did not, it's paid for with a job that you got and so others didn't, you may feed on meat gained from the death of animals and even if not the death of animals was required at some point to grow the plant matter you eat. Animals, plants and human labor are exploited for you to live your life. The food chain demands death or the world collapses. Atoms strip each other of electrons. The universe cannibalises itself. Even if other life forms don't matter to you (which is how we got into the current climate mess) your existence will still lead to some degree of deprivation and suffering for other humans, somewhere. You were forged from history, which is a fucking hellhole.

        To exist is a monstrous act that brings suffering, harm and death to others. Nature is monstrous. We don't even need vampires, the stuff that actually exists is a fucking horror-show. But what are you supposed to do? Your own suffering and death doesn't actually change anything, in the end. You can decide &quot;that's just how it is&quot; and not care, you can develop standards by which you decide which lives are less important (deserve to suffer more than) others, you can look for ways to minimize the damage you do while you seek a better way forward.

        And this feeds into ethics, philosophy, religion and the cycles of conflict and misery that come from it. People have long looked at the world and each other, and themselves and asked &quot;why are we so fucked up?&quot; &quot;what can we do to stop this?&quot; &quot;is there a way?&quot; &quot;is there even any point?&quot;
        And so comes dharma, original sin and redemption, the illusions of desire and escape of enlightenment, the laws of God by which one should live to live a life that benefits you and the world, fascism, communism, Social Darwinism, so on so forth.

        Vampires all have ideas about how to cope with what they are, why they are, what to do about it, on levels both individual and as collectives, and conflict arises from it.

        To me that's the core of vampires. Exploring monstrosity through exaggeration. Our own monstrosity, the monstrosity built into a fundamentally awful (yet beautiful) universe, and how we cope and what it makes us into. A one note &quot;Vampire BAD WRONG EVUUUL,&quot; cheapens the whole experience. The idea that vampires should kill themselves drives me up the wall, and frankly feels like... victim blaming, for lack of a better word. You got murdered? Defiled forever, abomination, kys. Lovely. &quot;Even if you do good things, they don't matter because you're a monster&quot; means there's no reason to give a damn at all. A vampire is still a person, a vampire still experiences suffering. If you don't value a person's right to exist and avoid suffering, you shouldn't be surprised that they don't give a damn about you. Goes in both ways, for kindred and mortals.

        Good and evil are ultimately what you do. A vampire is a monster, yeah. They're not good, their motivations don't even need to be good, but they can do good. The Toreador who has a flourishing domain that is perfectly clear of litter and is a sanctuary for nature in the urban jungle because they find it aesthetically pleasing, has done good. Vampires who push for eco policies, and against wars, or just to make life better to live because they fucking live here (and will be here to deal with the fallout for centuries longer then us) are doing good. The people who live in the Koldun's domain are grateful not to live with the threat of extortion rackets, or dying in a drive by or a gang war, even if it's because the holder is a territorial nutcase who likes a tidy domain.

        Exploring this and coming to your own opinions is far more interesting than having EVIL printed in all caps fifteen times a page (exaggeration, but that's what it feels like. Not just in V5, this annoyed me in previous editions too. I got it in the first page, I'm literate and capable of critical thinking, you don't need to keep fuckin' telling me!)
        Reminds me a lot of a conversation my current pc had when she was at the mercy of a high ranking mage. She touched on a lot of the points you comment on here.

        Her-"I know I'm not human anymore but I'm still a person."

        Mage-"but are you a good person?"

        Her-"im as good as you can realistically be as a vampire. I think my good outweighs my bad."

                    The way blood was handled was probably one of my favorite elements of V5.
                    That is not to say the system is not without its faults, because it does have them. Some can easily be house ruled, others have been adjusted in the players guide supplement.
                    The randomness of a rouse check is a bit annoying, but over long term it does even out, even though short bursts of bad luck are discouraging, the bursts of good luck are equally encouraging.
                    I am not a huge messy criticals and bestial failures, but have found just to give a condition like the clan compulsions work splendidly, either overcome at the end of a session or when the session. A Messy critical with a computer roll may result in a Bane penalty to all rolls involving technology for the rest of the session as the beast no longer have patience for such things.

                    I am not a fan of keeping Generation. The concept was fairly poor to start with, and more felt like a legacy product that needed to be kept. I could buy it if Generation was, much like we count human generations, an indication of when you were created, so thus even a 6th generation Kindred would create a 13th generation Child. Add some automatic thickening of the blood, so the generations automatically consolidate downwards. Blood potency solves a lot of issues. The tie-in with Generation is sort of neat, but it felt like something kept for its own sake, rather than something that actually brings something to the table.

                    Diablerie is as someone has mentioned very powerful, but also exceedingly dangerous. One poor roll and Humanity is wiped. But a few good rolls and experience would skyrocketed. While I do like the idea, I think this could have been something they should have changed. Remove the power of it. For a lower generation/high blood potency, draining another vampire to death would be the only way to remove the last hunger. For younger Kindred, it would do nothing (more than fill their hunger, as if it was mortal). While this removes ones of the reasons why Elders fear the younger generations, just surviving a couple of centuries a monster would be ample to find unique reasons for the same paranoia.

                    Hunting and feeding seems to be a goal. As has previously been mentioned, a lot of powers now risks the chance of Hunger, where as they in previous editions did not. From a mechanical standpoint, this makes the early (free) Disciplines more desirable. Something reliable that can be used on a nightly basis. It also puts the stakes higher when the higher level powers are used, because they are not used lightly. I have found this to be a good pacing in the game, the amount of disciplines used are a lot less and less relied on, instead I feel more players are thinking on their feet until they actually need to push something through, not because it is simple.

                    V5 is more 'action'-oriented in the way it deals with a game session. There are higher chances of becoming hungry, something that was never really an issue in previous editions unless there was a fight basically. Now all sorts of actions drives this hunger. You can boost any attribute, making hunger relevant for any play style.
                    Pre-V5, seen from a 13th generation vampire, you could, without risk for you or your prey, hunt every third night.
                    In V5, there is a 12.5% chance that you are at 4 hunger by the third night (and a 12.5% that you are still at 1), with the average being 2 or 3. This is regardless of generation, some even have it harder, as their max hunger without killing is lower. This leads to a lot of questions about how the Masquerade could kept with these numbers, and I agree. There is a large difference between what is "in-game time" and what is "out-of-game" probabilities.
                    Then once again, if you look how (my experience) of the game is, pre-V5 everyone would mostly use up blood doing something they wanted accomplished and hunt every night anyway to fill up. Sometimes several times. Botched hunts was not a per-session experience, but it came up a lot, in particular in Revised when it was just Perception + Herd, so there were a lot of 2-3 dice pools flying around (adopting the hunting rules from revised Dark Ages was done quickly by our groups). The same in V5, there is basically a hunt every session, but hunting in V5 is not only a job, it is also something that can add to your character in form of resonance and dyscrasias.

                    Resonance and such a very addition, and adds a lot to the personal experience. Like everything else, there are things that could have been done better, thankfully it is also highly modular and easy to adjust or modulate.

                    Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post

                    I'd argue a lot of people would appreciate ghouling while we're at it. Not growing old, staying in great physical condition, and experiencing true love? With a good domitor that's a bargain.
                    True love? The blood bond is the very opposite of true love. Sure, there are some perks like mentioned, but add to it an extreme addiction, obsession and obedience. Even if your domitor is "good", it is still the sort of take all the money from their kids college funds to help their domitor out, even if said domitor would never ask for such a thing. It creates a life where basically everything centers around the needs of the domitor, someone who does in no way can feel the same way back. Holding employment is hard when all thoughts and concerns are on someone else, keeping friends and even family when all they want and need is to be around someone else. At the very best, it is what would be described as a very toxic relationship by any other standards, and that is with a "good" domitor.
                    This is why Kindred influence is not based around ghouling, but leverage and exchange of favors.
