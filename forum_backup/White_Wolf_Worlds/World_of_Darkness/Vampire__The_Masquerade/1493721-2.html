                    Originally posted by Baaldam View Post
                    But yeah, a name somehow resonant of appalachian or at least american folklore, native or not, as a whole would have been a much better fit.
                    I think the connection to Appalachia is incidental, not important, to the identity of the Ahrimanes. We know that they were originally Gangrel. We know Muricia established herself with the local Indian population and studied their WoD magical practices in an attempt to break her Vinculum with her sire (who is implied to be one of the two "founding" Gangrel elders of the anti-tribu). That created the ritual that could turn certain vampires into Ahrimanes, and allowed the development of Spiritus. They are known for being warriors and survivors. They are secretive and violent. They are also known for their material wealth. Muricia believes men are inferior to women and prone to conflict, and limits the Ahrimanes just to women. They usually only form packs among their own bloodline. They have limited contact with their own sect, but do provide aid to the Sabbat by keeping them informed of Lupine activity in the wilderness. Ahrimanes do not wander, but stay in their own communities and remain heavily involved in mortal communities and use mortal pawns (which is not a very Sabbat thing to do, as that usually gets passed on to the Revenants).

                    Muricia and the original Ahrimanes seem to have been from Spanish colonial stock originally, which implies Muricia and her followers were likely in the southern Appalachia in modern Georgia, Alabama, and Mississippi which was the area where the Spanish had the most contact. (And I just looked up the original bloodline description, it is this tier of southern states - plus Louisiana - that is mentioned, not Appalachia) If I had to pick one people that provided the "native shamans", I would pick the Muscogee. But I don't think there are too many cultural ties to the native Indians who happened to live here. What was important to Muricia was the WoD magical knowledge certain people there had, not necessarily any cultural connection.

                    This group sounds a lot like a cult that is building its own army and economic network to survive an upcoming apocalypse (probably Gehenna). Even Muricia's selection of only female seems less due to some kind of modern feminism (which would be anachronistic to the time she likely transformed - somewhere between 1500-1700), but more like she thought the two male Gangrel Sabbat elders were foolishly feuding and it was consuming the Gangrel anti-tribu and making it weaker. She just attributes this infighting as a natural flaw in males, and believes females are more group oriented and cooperative.

                    If we assume that the name Ahrimanes for this bloodline is supposed to make sense somehow, the best interpretation is that the name was taken because of Ahriman as an adversary figures. It's just that in the bloodline's case, it isn't against Ahura Mazda, but probably the antedeluvians. That may not have been the actual case, and the name was picked randomly as many suspect.

                    So if I renamed the bloodline, I'd probably wouldn't pick some random association with Appalachia. But try to tie it into something that can be associated with an apocalyptic doomsday cult of prepper survivalists... that happen to be exclusively women.

                                                    Much like with Sergeant Brother said previously, the name Nagaraja always bothered me - in my own games, it's the title of the foremost among the Followers of Shesha in a domain, meaning also that in areas where the clan has considerable cultural mileage the title has become synonymous with Prince or Primogen and may be used as either even by non-sheshite kindred across domains in Vietnam, Laos or Cambodia among other countries.

                                                    Meanwhile, the bloodline of flesh-eating necromancers is known under a number of different names in certain parts of the world.

                                                    - In India and nations that have been culturally influenced by it through the ages, they have been called Pishacha for centuries and perhaps more.
                                                    - Meanwhile in the middle east and much of the arab-speaking world Ghul was used for centuries but has been slowly but steadily replaced by Galla, out of either pretensions to ancient millenia old origins or want of distancing themselves of a term increasingly associated with vitae-empowered thralls.
                                                    - In the western world two names have gained milleage as representatives of major groups of the bloodline: the Nihilistics, a group with cultic/secret society underpinings not unlike the Tremere (and some theorize, formed from either a splinter group or as a sort of foil/response to the Warlocks) widespread through much of central and eastern europe from unclear beginnings somewhere in german-speaking countries or the russian empire between 17th to 19th centuries.
                                                    - And in the British Isles it's the Dunsirn family of banker-cannibals that has gained traction, at least in part due to support from Mithras as a foil to Tremere and Giovanni both, with something of a presence in a number of Ventrue-ruled domains across the english-speaking world where relations of Blue Bloods with either Warlocks or Necromancers tend toward unfriendly.

                                                    (In my games, due to me sticking to my own pre-clanbook Queen of Damned-inspired personal take of them, the idea of Giovanni "branch families" made no real sense, so i made them into rival families/traditions of death-magicians causing the venetians much grief instead. Redoing the Dunsirn as "Nagaraja" was just too convenient and fun to ignore - and in fact is what got me going into this remake of the families in the first place)


                                                    As an aside, the division of bloodlines in multiple enclaves of quasi-clan weight within a certain niche/region, semi- to fully independent from each other and sometimes with their own culture and particular mythology/origin tales is one of the major factors in the divide between clans and bloodlines: numbers and a sense of common identity (lacking on the later, obviously), that kind of contributes to make their numbers seem even smaller, since a Dunsirn might see a common identity with a Nihilistic as "distant relatives/brothers in the Way" and vice-versa depending on situation and individual, but the same happening with a Galla - or Pishacha - could be much less probable. Damn, they might accuse the Dunsirn of being a thieving pretender of a dirty british diablerist while at it!

                                                    Much of the same can go, for example, with Marconius' Kiasyd line in Alsace x the much older Dokkalfar from the scandinavian countries x the Moon Eyed People in their warrens within & under the Appalachia since the coming of the Cherokee.
