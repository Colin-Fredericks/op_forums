        DREAD THREAD NECROMANCY STRIKES AGAIN! I made an account just so I could give my two pence on this.

        I love everything about the Setites (down to the snake motif) except for the whole Egyptian shtick. Not only was it poorly implemented, but for a game whose primary mythos is based on Abrahamic religions, it sticks out awkwardly like a sore thumb. On the other hand, I love the Satanic aesthetics of the Baali but I don't care for their "let's end the world" nihilism nor their "let's kill babies to prevent the end of the world." So, what I do is combine them into a single clan, which I name The Serpents of Eden. They keep their snake theming, their gnostic philosophy, their utilizing sin to guide others to enlightenment, the whole "breaking the chains of God," but tie the main mythos to Satan/Lucifer and the Fall of Man. They definitely have splinter groups like (you guessed it) Egyptian Serpents who view Satan through the mask of Set, Nordic Serpents who worship Loki, and then the bastard children (the Baali) who don't want enlightenment but want destruction. In the modern nights, they are also commonly associated with The Fallen, seeing them as infernal divinity in human flesh (which is somewhat true).

        All of that said, I think V5 did a very nice job reconceptualizing the Setites into the Ministry, making it a lot more free-form and less dependent on Egyptian mythos, basically doing what I mentioned above but without forcing ties to any one mythos.
        I would also like to add that I like this take on the Setites. I like the tying them to the Abrahamic meta mythology of the VtM mythos, ditching the Baali (or rolling them into the Setites, as long as the Baali are gone I'm fine) and divorcing them from a specific human culture in their main cultural influence.

        Not to mention that while I can see the point of letting the Setites stick out from the Abrahamic meta mythology of VtM, it always felt more odd than interesting to me.

                        I like what was done with the Setites in VDA20, with three bloodlines (Followers of Set - the Priests -, Warriors of Glycon - the rulers, merchants and warriors -, and the Witches of Echidna - the mystics). The clan was more Meidterranean mythology oriented, with Typhon and Echidna as the Father and Mother, though local myths and theologies were welcome. There was a lot more emphasis on snakes and the Egyptian fanatics of Set were not yet the dominant force of the clan, which was therefore much more than just "Egyptian". Between the core manula, Tome of Secrets and the Companion there are many intriguing ideas to imagine what the real origin of the clan was and what was its shape and form in ancient times. The transformation of the clan into an Egyptian cult of Set is explained by the fact that the Priest bloodline gets rid of the other two, takes leadership of the clan from the Warriors of Glycon, takes magic from the Witches of Echidna, and creates its own pseudo-bloodline of bodyguards trained in Potence to replace the muscle of the Warriors of Glycon with brawn more loyal to the Priests.

                        If one likes the snake theme, it's worth perusing that material to come up with a true snake clan which was gradually overrun by its Egyptian faction worshipping the aardvark-headed, Apep-slaughtering Set.

                                Something that a thread like this gives me a chance to think about is that Egypt in general has a lot of interesting possibilities for the history of WoD/VtM that I’d be curious to hear others opinions about.

                                I recently read ‘The Mediterranean in the Ancient World’ by Fernand Braudel and one of the things I took away from it were how his historical insights into the ancient near East could be useful for WoD/VtM lore. Braudel argues that throughout the height of the Mediterranean Bronze Age (which he places c. 2500-1200 BCE) the two central nodes of civilization and trade and the region came from Mesopotamia & Egypt.

                                Mesopotamia is almost tailor made for vampires and the Jyhad. A large number of unique and independent city-states, constantly competing amongst each other for advantage, each boasting their own tutelary gods and claims to fame, while still being loosely interconnected by common ties of religion and language. It’s so easy for me to imagine how each of the clans (or at least most of them) could divide the cities up between there 4th & 5th Gen members in the Bronze Age and compete against one another. Even during periods of relative hegemony, such as under the Akkadian or Babylonian Empires, the individual cities still retained a lot of localized power. I can readily imagine that whatever clans represent the cities of Babylon or Ur (Ventrue or Toreador would probably be a good fit) could produce a number of early traditions to allow for relatively safe contact and commerce between the vampiric courts while still each city remained relatively independent of one another.

                                Egypt, on the other hand, seems to be different. After the early unification of the upper & lower kingdoms and the reunification into the Middle Kingdom after the First Intermediary Period, Egypt seems to have been relatively stably organized around the central power of the pharaoh’s court, with local courts only gaining relatively greater autonomy during intermediary periods. This isn’t really surprising if one takes a look at geography of course. While Mesopotamia has a vast flood plain for its relatively autonomous city-states to spread across, the vast majority of Egyptian civilization is united by the Nile: whoever controls the levers of central authority, at any time other than those of utter collapse, can maintain hegemony across almost all of Egypt with relative ease.

                                What this would seem to imply to me is that, until the period of Egypt’s conquest by outside powers, the vampiric leadership of Egypt ought to have been relatively homogenous. Perhaps in Bronze Age Egypt we would even see proto-types of the high-low clan distinction with different clans having designated roles in the social hierarchy of Egypt.

                                (Checking the wiki it seems to suggest that the Settites did predominate in Egypt until the conquests of the Hellenic & Roman empires which id agree with, although perhaps I’d even suggest the Assyrian or Persian empires of the early Iron Age as the point of divergence)

                                I imagine much of the strange and sometimes contradictory lore regarding Set/Sutekh/Horus/Osiris/Isis/etc. as having occurred sometime before the period of recorded Egyptian history proper, at the unification of the Upper & Lower Kingdoms at the latest, but probably occurring in pre-dynastic Egypt in general, sometime between the era of the Second City & the rise of the Old Kingdom.

                                This leaves us at least 2,000 years where the Settites may predominate in Egypt. The scope of that period is vast of course but developing ideas about what it was like could be helpful for ideas about the Settites in general. My mind is drawn towards the ancient vampire king & queen of Egypt from the Vampire Chronicles books (The Vampire Lestat & Queen of the Damned) in particular. The portrayal of those characters, who probably influenced the very concept of Methuselahs in general, seem like an interesting way that Egypt could be handled. A central god-like object of cult devotion, perhaps Set himself in the beginning or members of the 4th generation, with the rest of the clan emerging outward in expanding spheres to serve the god and maintain their holy domain in the Egyptian nights.

                                In this context, with the mysterious destruction of Set around the turn of the millennium and the waves of conquests washing over Egypt from the Assyrians to the Arabs, I think one gets an interesting image that preserves the early Settites vibe of being a defeated and scattered priesthood of a dead god, hailing back to a Hyperborean age which to the moderns may feel more akin to Atlantis or Lemuria than their own relatively recent and mundane histories, and gives the feeling of a central hook to the clan’s narrative.

                                    I may be the odd one out here, but to me, the "serpent" element is the least interesting part of the Setites, so doubling down on that seems the wrong way to go. Moreover, making the game even more Abrahamic (and really, mostly Christian) is just so obvious and has been done to death.

                                    Now, a clan of worship and rebellion/faith and taboo, and the cults that surround them, feels like it has more scope. Drop the Followers of Set name (except maybe for those old Egyptian priests), and the even worse Ministry name, and keep Mesu Bedshet (Children of the Revolution). That's what they called themselves anyway.

                                    Instead of snake-themed powers, they could have powers based on their religious animal affinities — everything from ibises to crocodiles for Egyptian Mesu Bedshet, but with room for jaguars or herons for the South American Tlacique, roosters or bulls for the Caribbean Wangateurs, and freshwater turtles or cobras for the Children of Damballah. I also had new lineages that were Italian, Iberian, and Iraqi/Iranian.

                                    Anyway, this flexibility justifies Protean over Serpentis with some unique and cool combo powers, or else a broadened version of Serpentis with different paths. If it's Protean, they take the form of sacred animals instead of the traditional wolf or bat. In my games, I'd already renamed their Discipline Djiwas (= Given Dominion/Power, from the was-sceptre of Set), with the different paths called "Serekhs" (literally "facade", but also a hieroglyph which symbolised the gate of a palace and indicated that the subject was royal). So the Serekh of Serpentis is the Greek-based Gnostic/Typhonic pathway, while the Serekh of Sha (=Typhonic beast) is the pathway of the traditional Set animal form.

                                    The focus of the Discipline would expand from just taking on animal features to adopting divine features which may include animal elements, so that you can essentially become a jackal-headed god or bird-headed garuda, etc. They can be biblical entities, if you would prefer that — think about how weird the angels are in the Old Testament, with four animal heads, etc, and you've got a good basis for the profane-divine forms of an Abrahamic Mesu Bedshet.

                                    In the original Clanbook: Setite, they also got in-clan access to the Path of Corruption, without needing to learn Thaumaturgy otherwise. That made a good alternative to Serpentis for many character concepts, so keeping the Priest/Warrior/Witch castes with the ability of the last to learn the Path of Corruption as part of Akhu is appealing.

                                    What I like best about this approach is that it doesn't invalidate what came before — snakey Setites are still a thing, but you also broaden the thematics of the clan without having to go as far as smooshing two different clans/bloodlines together. To me, this is just doubling down on the core elements that were already there and then adding extra options in for flexibility. Also, the divine/profane duality is really interesting to me, but doesn't force you to go down one particular route (either Gnostic or Ancient Egyptian).
