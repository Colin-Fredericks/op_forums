    [DAV20] Can Auspex detect sound silenced by Obfuscate 1 as long as Auspex is higher?

    08-22-2025, 08:13 PM
    See subject:
    Can Auspex detect sound suppressed by Obfuscate 1 as long as the Auspex is higher?

    From Auspex Descriptions:
    "For more on using Auspex to pierce Obfuscate and other mental illusions, see the sidebar See the Unseen (p. 195)."

    "Obfuscate: The Obfuscated target and the Auspex user compare total levels of their respective disciplines. If the Obfuscate user has higher levels in Obfuscate than the Auspex user has levels in Auspex, he remains hidden. If the Auspex user’s levels in Auspex exceed the Obfuscate user’s levels in Obfuscate, or they are evenly matched, the Auspex user rolls Perception + Awareness. Her difficulty is 8. Lower this difficulty by one for every dot of Auspex she has in excess of her opponent’s Obfuscate."

    From Obfuscate Descriptions:
    "Cainite with the Discipline of Auspex may be able to pierce a Cainite’s Obfuscate. For more, see Auspex on p. 195."

    The Silence of Death
    At this rudimentary understanding, the Cainite can make things around them fade to silence, although sounds from beyond the vicinity filter through, anything in the immediate area fades into nothingness. In most cases, this nimbus moves with the Silence of Death.
    System: There is no roll. The power creates a zone of absolute silence around the character, with a maximum radius of 7 meters. No sound occurs inside this zone, though sounds originating outside the area of effect may be heard by anyone in it. Additionally, if the player spends two blood points and the character spends five turns in concentration in a room or enclosure, the silence remains in it after the character leaves the room and lasts until she wills it to end. A character can’t create more than one zone of silence, so if she wishes to use Silence of Death on herself (or curse another room), the zone in the first room fades automatically.


    Auspex can pierce Obfuscate. But the Obfuscate 1 description is a zone of "Absolute Silence" in which "No sound occurs".

    I'm of the opinion that Auspex won't pierce it because of this but wanted to get some feedback.

      I'd say yes. I take "in which no sound occurs" as imprecise phrasing. Obfuscate does not prevent the occurrence of sound, but merely its perception, and Auspex should be able to pierce it.

      In reality there is an issue in how Obfuscate overall (all the powers) has been framed in its working. Keeping it simple, a more consistent framing of it is that the vampire produces a sphere of a radius of X by Wits + Stealth. X is whatever number of whatever unit of measure (yards? meters?) specified by the manual in the introduction to Obfuscate (I can't remember what it was, I think 5 metres per dot). Anyone in that sphere fails to notice what the vampire is concealing unless they have Auspex or similar supernatural powers, and Obfuscate counts as a mental power that affects the victims' minds, not their actual senses.

      Obfuscate 1 makes people oblivious to sounds produced in the chosen zone. Ex.: Wits 4 + Stealth 3 means my Obfuscate has a 35 (7×5) metres radius. The zone of silence itself can't exceed 7 metres, but people within 35 metres will be oblivious to the sounds produced in it, while those further away will not. They will be able to notice, so I may need separate Stealth rolls to see if I can be quiet enough for them not to hear. The idea of creating a silent room separated from the character is very nice, but incompatible with this way of looking at Obfuscate.

      Obfuscate 2, 3 and 4 do what they say they do, but within the radius specified above. Obfuscate 5 as well.

      Granted, I think Obfuscate is overall weaker when framed in this way, but it is st least consistent in terms of explaining how it works.

      Alternatively, one can say that it is not a mental, but a mystical power, and has no radius of effect, but simply conceals what one wants it to conceal (depending on the power used). Thus defined, the powers work as written and one should ignore the part in the introduction where the Wits+Stealth distance is specified for surprise attacks. Auspex can still pierce the illusion. I think this second framing (which I thought of just now) is actually better and easier and funnier to use.

      By both framings, sounds must occur in the area, but they are either mystically concealed, or people nearby are mentally made oblivious to them.

                  Originally posted by FrostFox View Post
                  But then it is not Absolute Silence. The sound occurs, the listeners just fail to recognize it. The word Absolute does a lot of lifting there and is a very strong word. Regarding the 7 meter range I'm not even sure why you would include the Wits + Stealth range as well on top of that. It's either silenced within 7 meters or it's not and anyone beyond that would hear it or not?
                  Regarding "absolute", the issue is that Obfuscate is inconsistent in VDA20. The sphere of silence used to be a Quietus 1 power in VtM revised. I think it's also Quietus 1 in VtM20. Quietus is based on blood doing weird things to facilitate assassination. 4 out of 5 powers are about turning vitae into venom/acid, while the first power lets you make a sphere in which there is no sound. Arguably, with Quietus sound simply does not occur for mystical reasons and Auspex may fail to reveal it.
                  In DAV20 they moved the sphere of silence from Quietus to Obfuscate. Obfuscate is described as a mental power. Even the table on True Faith puts it together with Dominate (and maybe Chimerstry, I can't remember) instead of Presence (classed as emotional manipulation) and Thaumaturgy (classed as mystical). I can't remember where they put Animalism.

                  If Obfuscate affects the mind, it makes sense that what matters is the ability to reach the victims' mind. In the paragraphs that introduce the discipline overall, they say one cannot make a surprise attack to someone further than 5 metres × (Wits+Stealth). It makes sense to assume that what should have been written is that is the range of the mental manipulation. People beyond such range are not affected by any Obfuscate powers. The different powers only differ in what can be concealed from those within such range. For Obfuscate 1, what can be concealed are the sounds within 7 metres, a distance unrelated to the Wits + Stealth one.

                  If one ignores the Wits+Stealth sentence in the intro to the discipline and does not apply it to the discipline overall, but only to surprise attacks, then Obfuscate 1 conceals all sounds occurring within 7 metres, and nobody will hear those sounds at any distance, near or far, unless they can pierce Obfuscate supernaturally, like with Auspex, or they are children or animals, if they count as an exception.

                  I read somewhere that children and animal could be treated as having Auspex 1 for the purpose of resisting Obfuscate, but that's useless, since then they can pierce the sphere of silence but not the classic Obfuscate powers (2 to 5).

                  There is no reason, whether as a mystical or mental power, to assume that Obfuscate prevents the very occurrence of sound rather than merely concealing it. If the writing makes it seems so, it is just because it was poorly adapted from its origin as a Quietus power.


                  Originally posted by FrostFox View Post
                  In fact Obfuscate in DAV20 seems to lessen the idea that Obfuscate is purely mental, not even mentioning mentalism aspects in its broad description or the Obfuscate 1 description.

                  I'm not saying I entirely disagree but enjoy a bit of debate on the subject to settle my mind.
                  If you read carefully the descriptions of the second and third power, you will see the words "mind" and "victim" used repeatedly. A bit also in the fourth power. Reading those sentences, it gives the strong impression that it is a mental power affecting specific victims, either those within an area or distance, or intended targets. However, it is not so clear, and the door is simply open for endless arguments between players and storytellers.

                  In short, the discipline's writing is not thorough or consistent enough. My recommendation is to houserule it to make its functioning clearer, also lore wise, for a more immersive experience and to avoid pointless debates that cannot be settled. Others may prefer other approaches.
