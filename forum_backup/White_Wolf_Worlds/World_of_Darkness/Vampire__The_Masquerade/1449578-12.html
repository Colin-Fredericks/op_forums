                                                    Honestly, I don't think Cults of the Blood Gods is a suitable replacement for Paths. Personally, I'd have beefed up the Paths to integrate more elements from DA Roads. Then you have several axes of alignment in the game that might conflict and present drama: Clan/Sect/Road.

                                                    Originally posted by Professor Phobos View Post
                                                    My point is just that the creative vision is rooted in a dispute over this particular setting element that goes way, way back. Part of the reason Vampire: The Requiem even came into existence was creative dissatisfaction with some of the path-dependency built up in Masquerade over time; the setting kind of became stagnant, difficult to shake up, revise.
                                                    And yet, they doubled down on Paths for Dark Ages, and that worked really well. You just have to make Paths nuanced and not 'easy'. If you allow murder, then there has to be a counterbalance to make things harder elsewhere.

                                                    I think VTR was borne of more than a rejection of the Paths, TBH. It was an attempt to start from scratch because the metaplot and setting had become so big it might be intimidating to new players and it was becoming harder to write new things for. Paths were only a tiny part of that puzzle.

                                                    If you want my personal opinion on what &quot;way&quot; they should have gone, I think attempting to put the Sabbat back in the mystery box is...well, I wouldn't call it a mistake, per se, since it's their vision to implement, so much as a lost opportunity. As pointed out, the vast majority of VtM's lifespan, the Sabbat has been the detailed organization we know and love. I would prefer V5 add a new antagonist group, one that they deliberately keep vague and/or toolkit in design approach, thereby creating a mysterious &quot;off the map&quot; antagonist.

                                                    The Sabbat, on the other hand, I'd like to see go through the Gehenna War and the V5 metaplot changes and all that fun stuff. Evolve it over time. Take some of the dated and juvenile elements that survived to VtM revised and dial them back, work the Paths to be more playable and interesting, and so on. One of the things I like about V5 is the Camarilla's regression into neo-feudalism as a defense mechanism against the Second Inquisition, it would be interesting to see how the Sabbat evolved to survive it as well in a playable sense.

                                                    They've been on the map so long I think it'll be difficult to slap a &quot;Here be Dragons&quot; sign on the Sabbat and have it stick for old-timers, and newcomers won't notice either way.

                                                    EDIT: But then again, I'd take a hatchet to every single WoD game, mechanics and setting, so I'm probably not the right person to ask.
                                                    These are very interesting ideas. And I think introducing a new mysterious faction is the way to go.

                                                    Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                                                    First edition released July 1991, PGtS released February 1993. That's 17 months, a year and a half, not a big leap, especially as with the first edition things were still getting worked out: the Cam was a global monopoly and the next biggest thing was the inconnu. Has V5 mentioned the Inconnu?
                                                    And this would be the mysterious group I'd work on. The Inconnu has several factions, such as the Monitors and the Golconda-chasers of Hunedora Castle. There's room to make a group that is actively antagonistic.

                                                    Maybe the Monitors, who've been waiting for aeons, have now been 'activated' by the arrival of the SI, and take strong action to 'correct' Masquerade breaches. Or perhaps they seek Golconda, but realise that they have to atone for the sins of their brethren to achieve it. Or maybe they see the fingers of the True Black Hand, even where the TMR doesn't have any influence, and are waging a shadow war against an imaginary foe they believe is working directly for the Antediluvians.

                                                    You could give them several mysterious motivations that remain theories, and that would increase the mystery.

                                                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                                        A pattern is forming here.
                                                        What do you mean? I don't see what I'm doing wrong here. V5 isn't a V1 throwback, at least going by core rulebooks. There are several issues: Disciplines being bloated in V5, while simple in v1. The Cam being big in V1 while small in V5, The differences in power between ancient and young were very stressed in v1, while greatly diminished in v5. V1 presents the possibility to become human again and move up and down the humanity scale, whilst V5 only lets you wallow in your lost humanity and buying humanity back is extremely cost prohibitive. The beast is a completely different animal, and the morality attitude has virtually reversed. The Corebook of V1 also didn't really care too much about cultural lines; everyone's cam, no matter where they're from, whilst V5 does it's absolute best to stress that it has so many cultural lines. V1 presented clans as self-selecting social archetypes, whilst V5 presents clans as bloodlines with diverse internal divisions. This idea that they're cutting back on the Sabbat to be "more like V1" is terribly wrong. If we weren't talking about a fantasy roleplaying game, but politics or something real, that kind of willfull misunderstanding could be dangerous.
