    I appreciate everyone's thoughts. On the subject of 'things in the mythic age dont have to match real world dates' - I agree completely. I just like to tether things to real world dates where there seems to be a close enough fit and no reason to pick another date - the reason I went with (possible) historic flood events to tie to my enoch and second city is because the dates were close enough for government work, and there is no other controlling, or even particularly persuasive authority. And who is to say that our modern, technocratic explanations about ice sheet melting and rising global water levels is not just that.. a modern paradigm reflection of mythic era events.

    I agree, and like, the concept of making vampire generations and 'powers' to some degree map to changes in the underlying metaphysical nature of the world. Caine, his children, and grandchildren, lived in a much messier, less regimented, more magical world than their descendants. The difference between what we now call clans, or between mages and socerers, between generations - symptomatic of a decrease in the overall flexibility and 'magicalness' of the universe. Perhaps all the Weig got tied up as things were named, and order was forced on these beings. Maybe generation was not a thing before the 'fall of Enoch' era... or it was, but it was not FIXED (I like this interpretation). Perhaps the reason there are no '4th generations' from the first city is because, even if some of Enoch's survivors would have been of the 2nd, or 4th generations... at the time, age and magical power could raise generation, and there is no 'generation', in terms of power, beyond 3rd.. Caine just had more experience. Consider that even in the modern nights, magics exist which can raise generation without diablerie, either temporarily or permanently, and which can allow more than one cainite to benefit from a single soul.

    Im also contemplating that in my setting (such as it matters).. there WAS a cursed murderer figure, who was cursed by a great power, and driven out from a sacred place - who made some undead kids. The Hebrew Eden and Able and Caine story is a view on those events, informed by a religion that would not exist for thousands of years. However, not all vampires trace their line to that cursed first murderer figure... some of what we call the second generation may have been similarly created, or come from other lineages. The flood of Enoch simply ties up the lineages, leaving nothing surviving that had not reached the '3rd Generation' 10-dot apex of power... and saying they all come from the same vampire is just to put a bow on it.

    I also like set ACTUALLY being 'self-embraced' or cursed himself, and/or a god prior to this... at around 5000 BCE. Long after the end of the high magic era that gave us the first antedeluvians, but as a god-thing, Set retained enough of that early, universal 'weig' that he too appeared at the apex, or potential apex, of power... 10 dot maxima, 3rd generation.

    Final note, and I'm wandering here... as I see it, the difference between the 3rd generation and their childer consists of some sort of ineffable 'power' no longer present or possible in the world... which is why that last hurdle from 4th to 3rd is so hard (see poor Augustus and Tremere) is so difficult. Yet at the same time the nature of that power is nebulous... other than 10 dot potential and the fact of a LOT of age, the 3rd gens are not so markedly 'beyond' their children as to make any conflict meaningless. The Baba Yaga would probably lose a fight with Set... but he would know he had been kissed. 3 high-dharma Kuei-Jin fought a founder to a standstill... and he was likely older than all of them, put together. The difference between 4th and 3rd is mostly metaphysical, having to do with breadth of scope rather than focused power - and may have a transformational character, if you consider certain versions of the Gehenna story. Though I'm kinda meh on vampire that were having tea at 8000 years of age, and suddenly turn into tentacled murder-cities or subsume the very earth itself a relatively short 2K years later. Then again, I'm kinda meh on Vasilia totally annihilating the Baba Yaga as a standard action which did not provoke attacks of opportunity (I chock this up to writers wanting to show how awesome the niktuku is, and thus 'worfing' the poor little grandmother. The only reason that Ur-Shulgi breaking the Tremere Curse doesnt deeply annoy me is because my loathing for Clan Tremere could, if harnessed, remove the need for fossil fuels.

                                                Originally posted by Marcus Smythe View Post
                                                Where do you put, in your personal opinion, the dates of Enoch and the Second City? Given that these topics are radically unclear and all supporting evidence is wildly contradictory of itself from the various sources?

                                                Thoughts?
                                                I don't try to date stuff from the Book of Nod. Might as well try to date stuff from other real world mythological sources. In my cWoD, the Book of Nod and it's event might have happened or they might not have happened. The whole system for counting generations might be a convenient fabrication, lies vampires have lived since times before the eldest cities or whatsoever. Same goes for my view on all other cWoD origin stories: they are myths that monsters tell themselves. Any embrace date before let's say 800 BCE is conjecture at best.

                                                Yes, you can point to Saulot, Tremere, Cappadocius and Augustus Giovanni as examples of active 3rd generation vampires. But are they? Is 3rd generation some arbitrary measure? I say it is, because we don't know about any 1st or 2nd generation vampires. All we know is that generation spreads out across roughly 12 steps (4th to 15th). The numbering of these steps is completely arbitrary and dependent upon the notion of Caine actually existing as... what? He isn't 1st generation, because the 1st generation would be one step removed from Caine. In the end, 3rd generation vampires are sometimes an observable phenomenon (at least if you are at the right time in the wrong place). Beyond that it's conjecture and fables.

                                                I know that the setting treats the antediluvians and the Caine mythology as objective truths (one of the greatest weaknesses of the whole setting). Heck, the largest and most successful vampire sect, the Camarilla, is founded on the believe that the antediluvians and Gehenna are myths! Yet, the setting proves them wrong time and time again. Nice way to underscore the bigotry of the Camarilla, but it's a disservice to the pillar of the setting. The Camarilla has always been the center piece of Vampire: The Masquerade (with that title, how could it be any different?), yet even the core rulebook always presents the Caine myth and generations as facts - instead of spending at least one paragraph to cast doubt on the whole story (hope V4 does with it's notion of Gehenna).

                                                I think in my V20 games from now on, the unspoken assumption will be: the Camarilla has it right, the Sabbat is a delusional sect of religious fanatics.

                                                            Originally posted by Resplendent Fire View Post
                                                            I don't think generation means "removed from Caine." I think it simply means number of generations, and Caine is the notional first generation, period. Not first generation removed, just first generation.
                                                            To quote the Lexicon in V20:
                                                            "Generation: The number of “steps” between a vampire and the mythical Caine; how far descended from the First Vampire a given vampire is."

                                                            Caine cannot be first generation, because Caine cannot be one step removed from himself. Someone even addressed that bit in Clanbook Toreador revised, but not in a satisfactory way.
                                                            The concept about what a vampire is, really stems from observing the 4th generation, not the 3rd. The 3rd is by all accounts something else, because they are tremendously powerful compared to even the 4th generation. This is substantiated by the fact that all 3rd generation vampires you have "died" in the past 1000 years allowed themselves to die (Saulot wanted to be diablerized, same goes for Cappadocius, Tzimisce isn't as dead as everybody thinks, and Lasombra merged with the Abyss). Only exception is Ravnos, but he wasn't killed by another vampire, but by "simply" destroying him.

                                                            As soon as you start stating the antediluvians by extending what we know about 4th generation vampires and their powers, they become riduculously killable (simply because they will start botching dice rolls...).

                                                            So my thesis: whatever the 3rd generation are, they don't follow the normal rules for vampires. Vampires as the rulebook describes them only start to exist at an arbitrarily named "4th generation".
