                Originally posted by Mahasamatman View Post
                Don't have the black hand book, so just going by the core:



                Revenge-obsessed elder vampire necromancer is a concept that fits an extremely small number of games. Hell, "elder vampire" is a concept that fits an extremely small number of games. And getting past the "they are all elders" part, I just don't see the point. Angry necromancer is great idea, but you don't need an entire clan of them. Any vampire at all can fit into the "angry necromancer" concept. While players frequently have a "my concept is my clan" problem, where they are playing a stereotypical Tremere/Ventrue/Brujah, White Wolf writers have an opposite problem: "my clan is my character's concept". Marconius is a tinker-bell vampire, so he's a member of the clan of tinker-bell vampires, Daughters of Cacophony are a clan of vampire singers, and so on. There'd probably be a clan dedicated to basketweaving if nWoD hadn't killed the company.
                What do Harbingers of Skulls as a clan bring to the table that can not be trivially expressed by a character of any other clan?
                I think you're focusing a little too much on the stereotype as concept. Just as not every Ventrue is a stockbroker, and not every Brujah is a punk, not every Harbinger is a revenge-obsessed elder vampire. As they're a bloodline, they've had less exposure and therefore less opportunity for exploration of different concepts. Let's break it down.

                Revenge-obsessed: You only need play this part of the concept if you're playing a Harbinger in the Japheth contingent (as described in the True Black Hand book) as they joined the Sabbat and began plotting against the Giovanni for reasons both real and imagined. Now, you're going by the core - which is fine - but even then an individual Harbinger isn't forced by his Sabbat pack to wage war on the Giovanni. That's very much a Harbinger trait, and one they can take or leave.

                Elder: Harbingers of Skulls as a bloodline are dominated by elders, it's true, but the True Black Hand book states they've been newly Embracing due to their acknowledgement of being out-of-touch with the world today. You don't have to play an elder Harbinger if you want to play one of their modern childer. It's left nebulous when they began Embracing, so I'd give leeway of up to 40 years prior to today, off the top of my head. Again, you're going off of the core book, so you can still play a Harbinger - they were Embraced very shortly prior to their enforced 500 year "torpor." When they wake in the modern nights they're still fledglings.

                Necromancer: Harbingers are necromancers, their line having spent a great deal of time in the Shadowlands and making deals with wraiths for survival. If a necromancer concept doesn't fit your chronicle then that's fine - be a Harbinger with focus in Fortitude. The Clan that spawned them may have been predominantly Necromancy/Mortis-driven, but just as with every other line, they have two other Disciplines. It is possible to survive in the Shadowlands without Necromancy as a Discipline. It's difficult, but possible; if you have Allies, Contacts, maybe a Mentor, and Retainers. My point is if your group doesn't interact with ghosts, zombies, or Giovanni, you can still play a character of Harbinger origins without feeling useless. Then - on the rare occasion you do use Necromancy - it becomes a fun novelty rather than an expected trick.

                Angry Necromancer: I believe the number of Harbingers is now well over 50, but that may be from somewhere in my notes. Either way, not every Harbinger of Skulls is angry. The bloodline do feel wronged by someone, or some group, but they don't exist in a permanent state of anger. Think of the coolly calculating necromancers, the voyeuristic oracles, the burly graverobbers, and fanatical death cultists among them. They all pursue their own purposes, and maybe each feel betrayal, but they're not possessed by it.

                My Clan is my character's concept: I agree that a lot of people play bloodlines - the Harbingers not excepted here - so they can play something weird and wonderful, matching exactly the bloodline stereotype. I don't think there's anything wrong with this if players and Storytellers enjoy it. I had a player who played a slave Gargoyle and loved it. People do the exact same things with the Clans. I've had players look at the Clan section of the book first, see Vykos, and want to play a fleshcrafting-obsessed Tzimisce forevermore. For those who don't, these bloodlines can spawn as many character concepts as any Clan. Want to play a Harbinger stockbroker recently Embraced so his sire can recoup his lost fortune? A Kiasyd pack priest dedicated to forging a new Path of Enlightenment? Daughter of Cacophony bio-weapons engineer who lacks a singing voice but can make a dirty bomb with a paperclip and rubber band? What you're describing are player decisions, not helped by narrow descriptions that were necessary when the bloodline was written - they're each a strict subset of an existing line, after all. Clans have the same issue, but have benefited from expansion across over a hundred books. Bloodlines give players a chance to forge their own backgrounds and create their own innovative concepts while playing with a part of the game they particularly enjoy.

                What do Harbingers bring to the table that any other Clan can't: I feel you could apply this question to any Clan or bloodline. Toreador are artists. Malkavians are mad. Tremere are a blood magic pyramid. Harbingers are revenge-obsessed necromancers. Yes - that description is tighter, because they're a bloodline. But by unpacking that stereotype you have concepts such as "Ancient vampire lost in a time he doesn't recognise," "Fledgling Embraced to educate her master," "Committed destroyer of all Giovanni plans," "Cold, calculating, eschatologist," "Seeker of the mysteries surrounding his line's fall," "Fanatical student of the Path of Death and the Soul," "Spy who uses the Shadowlands as his spotting post," "Dedicant to restoring her Clan," "Harbinger diplomat to the Camarilla," and so on.

                If a player wanted me to describe what the Harbingers have that no other Clan possesses, I would say they're a group of vampires who lost time. No other Clan has "history" as an intrinsic stereotype. They weren't around to witness 500+ years of technological and cultural development, forcing them in these nights to quickly adapt or die. I could well see a Harbinger exposing himself to as much media as possible in an effort to remain relevant. A band of vampires childlike in their naivete about the modern world is a lot of fun to my mind.

                With all that said, I'm actually now going to alter my next chronicle to incorporate these betrayed necromancers; dangerous in power, but utterly fearful of the world they find themselves thrust into.
