        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
        Do people not actually think V5 is succeeding fantastically? I mean, it's irrelevant to the quality of the product but I thought it was accepted that V5 is an unqualified sales success.
        One measure of success, and probably the best one available to us without sensitive market data which is unlikely to be generally available, are the icv2.com retailer surveys. A full version of these, going back to 2004, can be found here. What is quite noticeable is that the World of Darkness was a perpetually strong performer between 2004 and 2009, even though this was arguably a period in which interest in the product lines of White Wolf were declining. V5, meanwhile, appears in the autumn of 2018, remains in the chart in the spring and summer of 2019, and then disappears. This is, of course, a game with a massive marketing budget, a strong community of players and former players which could be tapped into, and strong potential relevance to a modern audience. By the end of 2020 V5 was being outperformed by games which should, in my opinion, be generating far less community interest, such as Fate and Alien. This is not the story of a sales success.

        This is notable given how V5 ought to be performing. Vampire: the Masquerade is the only roleplaying game other than Dungeons & Dragons to break out of being a niche interest and into mainstream consciousness. People tend to forget this now (and I expect that there are quite a few people on this forum who may be too young to have first-hand experience of this) but back in the 1990s Vampire: the Masquerade was a game that was played by people who didn't see themselves as gamers. In reality, it ought to be a very approachable game. You basically play a human being in the modern world, with a few added supernatural touches. Character creation in the early games was very simple and comprehensible to people who had no gaming experience. There was a sense of style and a relationship which current affairs which just wasn't seen in gaming. People mock the eco-warrior notions of Werewolf: the Apocalypse nowadays, but having a game in which environmentalism was a prominent feature was a mind-blowing idea in the 1990s. There were good reasons why Vampire: the Masquerade outsold Dungeons & Dragons by a significant margin back then.

        So, competently managed, V5 should be at the top of the icv2 charts. That it isn't shouldn't be seen as a disaster, but is an indication that it's massively underperforming its potential. This is a game that is modern and political, something different (in theory) to most other roleplaying games. It can effectively deploy sexy, mature themes, attract fans of horror and modern drama, and escape the nerd associations that generally surround other games. It can capitalise on popular franchises such as Twilight, and can support a wider range of game styles. That it isn't topping the charts shows that there is something going very, very badly wrong.

        And it is, I think, clear to understand the underlying problem if you look at the core rulebook from the viewpoint of a disinterested outsider, rather than as a fan. The first thing to notice is that, in terms of a product trying to communicate information, V5 is a mess. The writing and editing leave very much to be desired in terms of being a functional text. Visually, meanwhile, the point that a rulebook is supposed to be a reference book is completely missed. It is not an approachable volume for a person who is not already a player of the game. Ironically, for people who are not already players of the game it is not that approachable either.
