    V20 Various Doubts

    08-19-2023, 10:14 AM
    Greetings,

    I have multiple unrelated questions for V20, can you help me find the answers?
    1. Can you have both Disfigured and Monstrous flaws?
      The latter has a lower limit on Appearance but the former imposes a Social penalty so I think both have an actual effect without too much overlap.
    2. For the purpose of Methuselah’s Thirst flaw, what count as mortals? Can you feed on ghouls?​
      The flaw states you can only feed on Kindred or other supernatural creatures, so are ghouls supernatural enough to count?
    3. How do Tremere share rituals? If you develop a ritual, how gets disseminated to the world?
      Relevant to the "Ward versus Cathayans" ritual (San Francisco by Night) whose write up mentions that everyone that knows it is in Las Vegas Chantry... How long and what way will this ritual get shared to say, Vienna, Japan, etc.
    4. Did Augustus Giovanni swear any kind of fealty or loyalty oath, any kind of official promise to Cappadocious? Or was the expected loyalty to a sire what got broken there?
      I am thinking on socially how the betrayal was taken, and what were the shock factors: an Ante got eaten but what about Augustus reputation as someone "trustworthy" to deal with, if he ate his maker, why would I deal with him.
    5. Are the Kindred of the East books compatible with V20? What changes (if any) are needed to use them with V20?
      I am thinking only of the Dodge ability, the experience costs that are exaggerated for the Kuei-Jin and removing the non standard abilities (but the hobby talent/skill/knowledge could also put them to use).
    6. Are the Mummy books compatible with V20? What changes (if any) are needed to use them with V20?
    7. What do you roll in a crossover game when a discipline usage asks for some stat you lack?
      For example neither a W20 werewolf nor a M20 mage have Humanity or Virtues, what do you roll when a Discipline ask for those values?
    8. Which discipline better represents old civilization magic: Thaumaturgy, Koldunic Sorcery, or an assortment of Disciplines?
      Essentially, what would old Egypt/Mesopotamia vampires have, I understand that Thaumaturgy is a recent invention, and that Koldunic Sorcery is Tzimisce but which one repressents the primal magic (Same way that Cruac does for Requiem).​
    Thanks,

      Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
      For the purpose of Methuselah’s Thirst flaw, what count as mortals? Can you feed on ghouls?​​The flaw states you can only feed on Kindred or other supernatural creatures, so are ghouls supernatural enough to count?
      I would say no. It is ridiculously east for vampires to create ghouls, so allowing that would easily subvert what that does. However, since ghouls do have vitae in them in the form of actual Blood Points, I would allow the Methusaleh (or whoever had that flaw) to gain those Blood Points (and only those blood points which is usually only 1) as long as they drained the ghoul of their entire blood pool, killing them.

      Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
      [*]How do Tremere share rituals? If you develop a ritual, how gets disseminated to the world?
      Relevant to the "Ward versus Cathayans" ritual (San Francisco by Night) whose write up mentions that everyone that knows it is in Las Vegas Chantry... How long and what way will this ritual get shared to say, Vienna, Japan, etc.
      I would say it is the normal learning rituals (page 230). Other than time required, there does not need to be any rolls. Though if a gaming group wanted there to be some mechanic to do so, I'd probably pick an Intelligence + Occult roll with the number of successes needed equal to the level of the rite, with each roll coming after a set amount of time appropriate for each rite level (day for level 1, week for level 2, month for level 3, season for level 4, year for level 5). It's all about what works for the game group.

      In terms of how quickly a rite can spread, it all depends on how you view the setting and Clan Tremere. There is no one correct way. But here is what I do. I typically divide rites into "common", "uncommon", "rare" and "proprietary" rites. Common rites well known to most Tremere, and these can be learned at their chantry as long as a Tremere is in good standing. Regents are expected to teach these rites to Apprentices provided they follow the Pyramid. Uncommon rites are not so well known, but not exactly secret knowledge. These can get taught, but usually only as favors and gaining of boons. Rare rites are closely guarded secrets to the individual vampires who have them. They only teach them to trusted companions, minions, etc. and always require something in exchange (boons, other closely guarded knowledge and secrets, partial blood bonds, etc.). Proprietary rites are even more closely guarded secrets that belong to specific factions within the Pyramid, and they never get taught to those who are not of that faction. That might be members of a particularly isolated Chantry with fanatical loyalty to their Regent, one of the secret conspiracies within the Tremere, or whatever else you can imagine.

      As for Ward versus Cathayans? It depends on how much of a threat the Catahyans represent to the Tremere. In most chronicles, I'd likely make it a Rare rite. Its inventor wants favors from others to teach it to them. But if you go Revised metaplot and have the Cathayans conduct nonsensical mass attacks everywhere, its importance would grow and would quickly transition to an uncommon or even common rite for simple survival. Probably with rapid promotion of its inventor in the Pyramid hierarchy as thanks.

So in my chronicles, I portray them as being heavily discriminated against and disliked with extreme prejudice. However, I generally do not see this when playing in another ST's games.

          Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
          What do you roll in a crossover game when a discipline usage asks for some stat you lack?

          Situational, really
          Not many disciplines ask for those stats actually, and when it does, it is usually specified it works either vampire or human, but of course it is not only the case
          But I would say since Virtue is used in a character's sheet to show his spiritual and moral fiber — or lack thereof of a vampire/human? And Willpower is based on starting Courage, and Humanity (or its equivalent) is based on the total of starting Conscience and Self-Control (or some replacment)? That is a good guideline to figure out what to use.
          For example Kindred of the Ebony Kingdom use the Virtues of Orun ("Heaven") and Aye ("Earth"), which represent the vampire's spirituality and humanity, respectively. Orun partially governs the vampire's Disciplines and to their ability to resist the workings of hostile magic, while Aye is used to resist frenzy and function during the day. Both of these traits also reflect the vampire's appearance: vampires with high Aye can pass for mortals, vampires with high Orun appear beatific, and vampires with low Orun appear bizarre or even monstrous.
          It is easy to decided how and what of those to use
          Kindred of the East's Yin, Yang, P'o, Hun and Demon Chi is a bit tricky but just follow the general guidline

          Then again, as I said when it comes to such specified stats, usually it is described it works on Vampire and/or Mortal
          But let's see an example!
          Dread gaze if I recall correctly uses Charisma + Intimidation difficulty equal to the victim’s Wits + Courage
          If your vampire is fool enough to use it against a Garou in a crossower? Well the Garou don't have Courage stats, but have similar gifts, like True Fear that ask for a rolls Strength + Intimidation difficulty of the target's Willpower

          So as you can see, since in Vampire's system Willpower is based on starting Courage, and the gift/disicpline are similar, from that easy to deduct what to use. So just turn it around, a Garou's Courage is basically her Willpower
          ​
          But if you don't wantto complicate your life, just "fake it till' make it" and discuss with your storyteller
          Or if you are the storyteller, use your common sense and use what do you feel apropiate

                          Not all "Thaumaturgy" is a recent invention. Certainly the unique to Tremere Paths are recent in the sense they were developed in the past thousand years. But there has always been Thaumaturgy that pre-dated the Tremere and could be quite ancient. We should remember that Hermetic philosophy is supposed to be a rediscovery of magical knowledge that existed back in ancient Egyptian times.

                          I would put all the Blood Magic Paths that are considered to be traditional vampire powers in that category. Weather Control and Movement of the Mind should be fairly old. I can easily see a vampire knowing Weather Control all the way back in Sumeria.

                          Koldunic Sorcery should predate Tremere Hermeticism, but I do not believe it is incredibly ancient. It probably dates some time to the Iron Age. It might pre-date Tremere Paths by only 500 years or so. The most ancient Blood Magic paths should be dated to the Bronze Age and possibly even the Neolithic.
