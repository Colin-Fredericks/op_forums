                      The issue with asking how hard it is to survive revolves around how "realistic" you run the setting. The reality is that most characters players love to make wouldn't survive more than decade at most, let alone centuries or millennia.

                      Most clans have criteria for embraces, especially if you have to trade favors with the local prince for the right to do it. Thus, you won't bother with someone pretty or random, but someone who has skills or what equates to Backgrounds that would be immediately useful for you as the sire. At the very least the potential childer would need certain traits just have the potential to survive to be of use. To put it another way, we as players see our characters as RPG protagonists, but the sires see our characters as RTS hero characters that they have to expend resources/requisition points to get. Why bother to do it if the unit doesn't do anything?

                      This is then stacked on top of the individual clan or bloodline ideology which places value on certain traits, such as the Tremere favoring magical mortals, the Gangrel wanting survivors, or the Giovanni wanting family members, just to name a few. Then you get the faction related issues such as the Camarilla favoring the path of humanity, while the Sabbat prefer loads of violence, and the independents tend to lack allies.

                      Can it be relatively easy to survive as a vampire, absolutely it can, but you need to be a specific type of person to do so.
                      • Psychologically you can't be co-dependent or desire drama to feel fulfilled.
                      • You must be comfortable being alone or isolated for long periods of time.
                      • You should have the self-discipline to train in martial arts or maintain a regular gym routine.
                      • Mentally you need to hunger for knowledge and be adaptable when change happens.
                      • As a mortal you should have an income that is either passive (being a landlord) or can be done from home (stockmarket/programmer/etc).
                      • Be the sort who has backup plans for everything.
                      From there it is just a question of which clan and faction you get embraced by.

                      So with that sort of a character baseline, find the most defensible haven location, use your Resources to upgrade it, get a live in Herd so you don't have to bother with hunting, conduct all your vampire related business by phone or video call since Dominate and Presence don't tend to work over technology. From there it is just a question of how you grow your own personal power to catch up to the level of your local kindred.

                      Now will this make for a dynamic and interesting character to play, no, no it won't. But it will survive most upheavals because you are not out and about to get caught in the crossfire and your isolation means you are not important enough to prioritize killing, but due to your defense focused havens and backup plans you are seen as too much trouble to try to dig out for next to no gain.

                      Or to put it in simpler terms, psychologically you are an INTJ on the MBTI, get embraced by the Tremere, move your haven to a decomissioned missile silo/military bunker which has been converted to a luxury doomsday condo with warding, and slowly build boons with the local kindred by using Dominate to clean up masquerade breaches without having to bother the sheriff or the prince.

                                  If you really want a benchmark for how hard it is to survive in WoD as a vampire, run a "play yourself" game.

                                  Character generation works by comparing yourself to each attribute and skill ranking, then rounding down to account for ego, so if you can lift 300 lbs you have three dots in strength. Go through each stat and Background with the people you will be playing with, it can be surprising how many times your friends will see you as having higher stats than you think you do. Then you just play it out as a situational embrace, so that the clan/bloodline and generation are randomized. The discipline spread is theoretically also randomized, but I find that the ST might want to allocate them so that the coterie has some functionality, i.e. whoever has Dominate as a clan discipline has all three dots in it.

                                  This approach grounds the character behavior because you can't separate yourself from your character. You know exactly what your character knows, you will destroy your family life if you start beating whoever annoys you, bodies can be annoying to hide or make disappear, etc. That and the Brujah clan weaknesses becomes one of the worst in the game when you are rolling to not eviscerate your younger siblings.

                                  In a "play yourself" game, just surviving 6 months trying to stabilize your new nocturnal life, figure out how to consistently feed, and how to keep people who knew you from getting involved, is a nightmare. Forget about getting caught up in the local vampire politics, surviving a Sabbat incursion, or tripping over a werewolf den.

                                          Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post
                                          Do I get beginning character stats? Because you pretty much start out as either an olympic athlete, a top level politician/grifter, or a genius. If I'm using my real-life stats...not so much.
                                          Character stats are a lot like bullet caliber. Everyone loves talking about the big numbers which can blow a hole through anything, yet the humble 22 is often preferred for assassinations or guard suppression due to it being naturally sub-sonic and staying inside the body rather than exiting it. In other words, the stats are not as important as how you use them.

                                          For example, playing poker isn't particularly complicated which means you don't need Int 5 to play, but if you can use Auspex 1 to see the card reflections in your opponent's eyes, you already know what everyone has, which means you control the game. This can make for a great power dynamic scene in a game, but it can also explain where your character gets Resources 4 from that can't be traced or easily interrupted.

                                                      Originally posted by Iguazu View Post
                                                      Um, you can't embrace a werewolf.
                                                      You can attempt to embrace them, they just usually die from it. From the vampire point of view, you either get a uniquely powerful ally or you get rid of a potential enemy. Win win.

                                                      Originally posted by Iguazu View Post
                                                      Having to hide yourself in a place that gets zero sunlight is very difficult.
                                                      How so?

                                                      There are two main concerns, one is light safety, the other is enemy safety. Light safety only requires something like a body bag or coffin/large box. Enemy safety just requires your bag or box to be in the attic, basement, closet, or crawl space, preferably disguised as something else.

                                                      One of the best things to do is to have an ornate coffin in the basement rigged with claymores or IEDs so if anyone opens the lid the room gets shredded, while your actual sleeping space is in the attic. Alternatively, if you don't want to bother the neighbors with explosions, then just rig a steel plate door to the basement room with the coffin so that opening the coffin lid makes the door close and lock tight, at which point the halon fire suppression system goes off and displaces all the oxygen in the room.
