On one hand, the Camarilla has kept it's original lineup for 500ish years, and all the shakeups have occured within a fraction of it's history. Some people like to play outside of the present day, where smartphones aren't ubiquitous or where they can comfortably have century long timeskips without waking up in Megacity 1.         Yes. The Camarilla explicitly invited every clan into the Camarilla. I'm well aware. However, how sincere was that offer? I think the Camarilla knew damn-well that the other clans would say no, but extended the olive brand so that nobody would accuse them of not-trying or deliberately excluding a clan; such behaviour would be unfitting for a global body that claims to support all vampires. "we offered, you refused" is a good way of justifying why some clans should get better political representation than others.

        The Malkavians are indeed a clan with a lot of overlap and I was thinking about them a lot during my post. But I think that what ultimately seperates the Malks from the Lasombra or anyone else is.. Tact. Malks are more symbiotic or parasitic than competitive. They don't show off what they own, they find oppertunities others don't. A Malkavian can own part of a Ventrue's company and not bother the Ventrue, Both of them might gain from the event, or the Malkavian might leave the Ventrue non the wiser. A stereotypical Lasombra, on the otherhand, will either try to take the Ventrue's company, hedge their bets against it, or establish a competitor. In terms of espionage competition with the Nosferatu, the Malkavians have different strengths but more importantly they have a different approach: A Nosferatu is like a private investigator; you hire one and they'll go out and learn all about something for you. A malkavian is more often like an inside man who just happens to be in the field you're interested in, or they have a friend in such a field.

        Even if the Assamites join, they're in a rather shakey possition due to the effects of the schism and the parts where they're newcomers. Vampires are slow. They take centuries to get established. It'd be foolish to think they could just join a global organization en mass and then be equals within 30 years. Until V5 overturned the boat, the remaining Camarilla clans were in a good possition to exploit the Assamites. (also I've got a lot of doubt about the Gangrel leaving the Camarilla permanently. They'll be back, it's in their best interests)

        The Assamites in the Ashirra were renegades, outcasts and spies anyway. Schismatics before the Shism. It's completely wrong to think the Assamites will adapt well to the Cam because they did alright with the Ashirra. Maybe I'm standing with Ur-Shulgi too much but Islam isn't at all compatible with Assamite Orthadoxy, and things like Shih sorcery were always considered a sick parody few Assamites embraced. Correct me If I'm wrong but we still know fuck all about the Ashirra in modern nights. One of the earliest sources suggested it was a Saudi/Iran thing and meta wise this was a shot at how overly religious White Wolf thought they were. Other people suggest the Ashirra is the Cam but way better because it's Asian and thus spiritual and we all know how WW had skewed perceptions of anything asian back in the day.

        On the outsider clans having more issues with one another:
        This is obviously more true for some than it is for others. But they do tend to have more extreme outlooks than the more moderate 7 clans. If you could describe the Ventrue as Conservative and the Brujah as liberal, then you can describe the Assamites as Fascist and the Settites as AnCaps (The Snake imagery is not a coincidence) It's order VS Chaos. The Assamites are The Law and The Ravnos are all too frequently outlaws. Assamites protect mortals, Lasombra abuse them. Lasombra and Giovanni are in general just more agressive. Half of these clans are likely to compete over the drug trade. The Tzmisce are arch-conservatives while the Lasombra and Giovanni are modern go-getters... ok... yeah, that's not a great reason. But it's undeniable that these clans are politically a lot further out than their more center-right compatriots in the OG 7. Assamites, Lasombra and Giovanni are obsessed with order; Settites and Ravnos are creatures of Chaos, and the Tzmisce just want everyone to stay the fuck out of their territory with no law but their own (because they are doing some very questionable things there. Also feudalism is the end result of being an ancap so...)

        The Giovanni, I think, have the least issues. But they're obviously out of the Cam because Augustus had a bullshit plan. Honestly, I don't think the Cam would want necromancy to get big. Once the Necromancers come in, the hauntings begin, and people will start to ask the necromancers to get rid of the ghosts. Creating problems to sell solutions. Obviously, this is quite undesirable, and the Cam would do a lot better working with small scale necromancers like the Samedi or Nageraja rather than something large scale, well organized and threatening like the Giovanni (or worse, the Hecata)

                                            The following clans are deeply affected by changes in outlook from the authors. A.K.A. retconing racism out. They all start off as heavily racist and simplistic stereotypes of entire religions.

                                            So, for them, you have actually two versions that seem the same in the surface, but work differently, and applying the same argument to both isn't constructive. For all of them it is also extremely recommended that you take the retconned version.

                                            Although no one will stop you from using the first version on your game, the first version is extremely racist, must be acknowledged as such and is unlikely to be well received in the forum as acceptable canon.

                                            None of those retcons happened at once, and many started early in the WoD publication history but only fully developed at Revised, so it is a process.

                                            Banu Haqim/Assamites

                                            The entire idea of the Ashirra and the Web of Knifes are part of the transition from "Old Assamites" to Banu Haqim. And it is far more sensible than many people think, because a lot of aspects that seem as discrepancies and poetic license from the authors to create a more diversified thing are actually how the region and the Quran in fact work.

                                            So, the solution of the Assamite Problem was three-fold. On one hand, in the Dark Ages they are reinterpreted as fighting in the Anarch Revolt as allies to the Anarchs, and later fighting the Camarilla, so the European contact with them is mostly through Warrior Caste in an actual war. Those Banu Haqim are actively promoting prejudice against them as to instigate fear, so this creates the stereotype as such IC.

                                            On the second hand, the Web of Knifes is created as their modern group of extremist assassins. The Web isn't the majority of the clan and it isn't the majority of the Warrior caste, it is a very strict group while the majority of the clan is far more reclusive or occupied with their business in the Middle East. Those Banu Haqim, very few, but active under several aliases, are the ones the Camarilla really interacts with, explaining their modern interpretation.

                                            Finally, the Ashirra solves the other problems by providing an actual Middle Eastern vampire community, a reason for the Islamic overtones and a place for the real (Second Version) Banu Haqim to be in. In that, the entire thing ceases to be how the other clans see the Assamites and becomes how the Europeans see the Banu Haqim, as every clan has Middle Eastern members and this version of the clan simply isn't a mystery for them.

                                            And about how the region and the Quran really work?

                                            1 - In the 14th and 15th centuries the gigantic region known as the Middle East is objectively more developed than Europe, wealthier, safer, and they never had anything resembling the Inquisition. Receiving and allying with refugees to stop senseless carnage in Europe ( and its spilling to Turkey) is pretty much on spot;

                                            2 - The Quran distinguishes between the Faith and the Law. Although it does require that Muslims work towards making the Law of Muhammad in the law of the land, it does not require that unfaithful be persecuted or any similar nonsense, and it didn't happened at the time. So the Ashirra being under Islamic law but not requiring its members to convert, and the same happening inside the clan, is also on spot;

                                            3 - Actually, the Quran have three clear definitions that apply not only to all people, but all living things, material or spiritual. They are divided into the Faithful, the Unfaithful, and the Shayatin. The Unfaithful are any person that does not recognize the truth in the Quran, and they are second class citizens to the Faithful, but that's about it. The Shayatin are those who work directly to deviate the Faithful, and while they're kind of equivalent to Christian demons, being the ones who the Muslims must fight against in a Holy War (Jihad), they're not defined by species, power or spiritual nature. They have IRL legends about supernatural creatures, Djinns and Ifriti who were good Muslims. So the idea of vampires being accepted as Muslims? On spot;

                                            4 - Just as Christians and Jews at the time, Muslims didn't define sorcery as any work of magic when they condemn it. In fact, the Quran emphasize the positive role of those who study the laws of nature as Allah made them and that includes forms of magic. Bad sorcery is the magic you learn from Shayatin and not just because it is magic, but because it is a lure to make you a sinner. So much so that in the Quran there are angels and saints that not only use magic, but explicitly use the same magic as the Shayatin or the Shayatin try to teach people. So, a caste of scholar sorcerers, is, as you see, on spot.

                                            5 - More than anything, the Islam is extremely based on the study and the application of law as a science. The Quran is as much a book on Theory of Law as a religious text can be, and it put a greater emphasis on judges than priests. Priests are irrelevant, the Word of Allah is already with you. Judges, though, study and teach the Law and apply it. So a clan of judges and executioners dedicated to making Kindred follow suit, converting en masse to Islam? On spot.

                                            What else can be said about the Banu Haqim? Canonically they refused to join the Camarilla because they saw the end of the war against the elders who wronged their childer as cowardice and the Anarchs who went with the Cam as traitors. Already at the time the majority of the clan was Muslim, as canonically most of the older members converted. Haqim never asked them not to do such a thing and the Quran never required them to force the change upon others, so non-Muslims existed and were accepted.

                                            No religious-based schism happened until Ur-Shulgi, and otherwise the creepy bastard blatantly ignored many aspects of the culture of the clan that were already in place at his time. The Schism drove a lot of them out of the clan and I'm not sure if anywhere it is hinted that a majority stayed with him. Viziers and Sorcerers mostly went to the Cam while Warriors go to Sabbat, with very few exceptions in the opposite direction and some of them going Autarkis. It is made clear that the new "Loyalists" of Ur-Shulgi are actually more distant from how the clan were under Haqim than the Muslim version (if Haqim would actually approve of it is another matter).
