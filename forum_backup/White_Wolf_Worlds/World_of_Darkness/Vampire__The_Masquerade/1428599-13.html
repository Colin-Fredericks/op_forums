        For me, I think that all the new Clans in the Companion will make for antagonists more than PCs.

        I like how they have completely removed any ‘Gypsie’ references from Ravnos, and while some players will like playing a Loki-like trickster, I think the constant nomadic aspect will stop them from being used in a long term city campaign. They will make an excellent, short term, Loki-like disrupter though, and you could get them involved with the PCs with the travel agent concept. Basically, if the PCs need to travel anywhere, you could make them an awkward NPC to make a deal with...

        I like that Salubri have been clarified as a historical ex-Clan (usurped by the Tremere - which means that the mystical 13 Clans total remains in tact) and they are so rare they are basically a plot hook. I can see a scenario like Children of Men, where the PCs are involved in smuggling a hunted Salubri in order to make some sort of gambit towards Golconda.....or just undermining the Tremere in some way. If they have to travel, it may mean they have to deal with the aforementioned Ravnos travel agent....

        I like that the Tzmisce is one of the most archetypal types of monstrous vampire, and actually combines the Dracula myth with Dr. Frankenstein in a way. Their limited movement - contrasted against the Ravnos actually - would still make them more of an NPC antagonist than a PC to me. But actually, they could be involved in the aforementioned conspiracy to smuggle the Salubri insofar that their experimentation could involve the blood of the Salubri to reach a state of Golconda. Maybe the PCs are, unbeknownst to them, surreptitiously smuggling the Salubri to a Tzmisce overlord....

        Good Anarch-based chronicle concept that!

                                                    Originally posted by Resplendent Fire View Post
                                                    I keep seeing this and it bugs me, and I seem to recall this has come up on other occasions, but:

                                                    The Lasombra, Tzimisce, etc. were introduced along with their disciplines in the Player's Guide to the Sabbat in 1992, an early supplement for V:tM second edition
                                                    The Storyteller's Handbook to the Sabbat was a second edition sourcebook published in 1993, and was intended as a companion to the PGttS. It didn't repeat material from the PG, but added more stuff such as bloodlines, material on infernalists, and so on.

                                                    They were never introduced as "Storyteller only clans" that needed correcting to make them viable for PCs, they were always intended to be playable.
