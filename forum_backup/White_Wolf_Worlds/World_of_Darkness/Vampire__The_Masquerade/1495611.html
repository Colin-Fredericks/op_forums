    [V20] Temoch the Jackal

    10-26-2022, 02:36 AM
    Looking through some past threads for ideas for my Sabbat game recently, I kept coming across a reference to Temoch the Jackal being the new Regent in Rites of the Blood.

    I can't seem to find him in my PDF of the finished book, so I assume he was edited out of the early draft shared on the OPP blog.

    So here are my questions:

    1. What did the original version of Rites of the Blood say about Temoch? Did anyone hang onto that early draft excerpt? Were any other Sabbat luminaries mentioned as having switched/assumed important roles?

    2. I know Temoch also appears in MET V20. Did he appear first in MET, and then get added into the Rites of the Blood draft as an Easter egg to tie the two lines together? Or was it the other way around (i.e., did RotB come before or after BNS MET)?

    3. Does anyone know why he was removed from Rites of the Blood? Was it a command from on high (e.g., Paradox) or did the writers just want to keep it open for V20's metaplot agnostic stance? I know BJD specifically leaves the future Regent open to leave it to individual tables to decide, so maybe that's why?

                      The idea I did have previously was that Temoch would lead the New World Sabbat (from Mexico) and Lucita the Old World Sabbat (from Madrid). But I wasn't sure if that would get too complicated.

                      Post-Civil War

                      The Sabbat would've been torn apart by Civil War but managed to pull itself together via "devolution" -- with two leaders who could work together without having such an iron grip, while recognising the differences between the Old World and New World Sabbat.

                      So the Old World, who comprise more elders and founded packs, would be a tad more conservative and traditional under Lucita; while the New World Sabbat, full of nomads and neonates, would get to be more aggressive and "free" under Temoch. Neither would feel resentful of a far-off Regent who didn't represent their interests.

                      There might also be fewer calls to abolish the Regent if the Regent wasn't a singular totalitarian authority, but more like governors who rule smaller areas on behalf of the sect and who answer to a unified Consistory with a (slightly) more democratic process.

                      Politics, Prisci and Polonia

                      Lucita would've had to grant some titles to her supporters and bring some ambivalent folks on-side -- e.g., I figured she'd win Vykos over by giving them a bigger slice of the pie as Cardinal of the Balkans, after they were passed up for cardinalship in favour of Polonia, one of her rivals for the regency -- but she had some very powerful support as of BJD.

                      I see her getting Polonia out of the way by giving him a poisoned chalice as cardinal and "Warlord" of the Crusaders.

                      Monty Coven would get to be Cardinal of the British Isles, which is close enough to what Mithras wanted anyway that he'd be tempted.

                      This way, he has the backing of a whole sect backing his rule, while the UK's relative geographic isolation means he still gets to do as he wants without anyone overseeing him.

                      Crusaders on a bus

                      I also imagine Polonia's Crusaders forming a more formalised third subsect like the Black Hand and Sabbat Inquisition.

                      Most of the raving monster types would be shoved into the Crusade and sent off to the other side of the world, leaving the more sensible Sabbat behind.

                      They would eagerly run off to do this, getting rid of an entirely useless (or stupid) third of the sect so everyone else could focus on averting Gehenna instead of causing it.

                      Polonia would think he'd won the lottery, while everyone else laughed behind his back.

                      The Black Hand's Reach

                      The Black Hand would also use more information gathering techniques via the Watchtower, and would have more cadet training grounds.

                      This would help the sect at large avoid mortal hunters and the Camarilla, while increasing the Black Hand's usefulness to the clergy.

                      The columns would maintain order at the city level -- with stationed Black Hand guards in most sizeable Sabbat cities to prevent civil war and keep the peace -- but they'd maintain respect by appearing to be a necessary balance against the Sabbat Inquisition (who continue to scare everyone).

                      Inquisition 1 - Infernalism 0

                      The Inquisition would have mostly succeeded in getting rid of infernalism in the sect, with Lucita's sister at the helm meaning that Regent and Grand Inquisitor can present a united front.

                      Nergal might be about still, but the shifting of some of the sect's power back to Europe stopped the weird vibes in Mexico infecting everything else.

                      The triumphs of the Inquisition would have won them respect, but wouldn't have diminished the fear people have for them.

                      They're still zealots with the power to destroy anyone they investigate -- it's just that, after Galbraith, most licks know they need someone to root out the infernalists.

                      Some Archbishops have called in "talons" (permanent Inquisitor covens attached to a city) in those cities most at risk of infernalism -- named after the Talons of Montréal.

                      This means Mexico City is also being carefully monitored, and Sandoza gets to explore her suspicions about the place in great detail.

                      The Harbingers would provide a helping hand in the area, after getting rid of most of the local Pisanob.
                      The Inquisition probably also helped the Black Hand get rid of the Tal'mahe'Ra menace (at least, most of it), so the sect wouldn't be as undermined from within as it used to be, and there would be greater respect between the BH and Inquisition, even if this was all kept secret from the rank and file.

                      (And that works well, because the rank and file need to believe that the two subsects' rivalry is real to give the illusion of "balance" in the sect.)

                      Ravnos Rebounded

                      The Ravnos would have rebounded in the Sabbat, thanks to the mass embrace tactics of the 2000s. They would be numerous among the nomadic packs and some would serve the Black Hand.

                      They would do well to uphold the Silence of the Blood, since they can trick mortal eyes. With Malkavian antitribu driving witnesses mad, these two clans gain roles as specialists in keeping the secrets of the sect.

                      Silence of the Blood

                      The Grimaldi would also have a role in helping maintain the Silence of the Blood.

                      Most cities, in response to the Second Inquisition, would appoint a Bishop of Silence, who would empower templars to uphold the Silence. They would use Grimaldi connections to help control the media and local politics.

                      The Grimaldi would be rewarded with first dibs on assets when the Sabbat raid cities, and the right for a select number of their members to receive the Embrace (and choose their own site, to boot).

                      Furthermore, Bishops of Silence would empower their lackeys to kill in the line of duty, which would become one of the only times these revenants could get their revenge on vampires -- a right they use with cautious delight.

                      They'd still be rarely deputised in this way, but the family only needs one Grimaldi at a time to have that right for the rest to slowly get their payback.

                      The Salubri Brought Soul

                      Inspired by the V20DA Salubri Apocrypha, the antitribu would have also increased in number, taking vital roles in both the Sabbat Inquisition and the Black Hand, as well as serving as priests and ducti at the pack level.

                      Their presence, along with that of the Harbingers, means that the Sabbat officially doesn't recognise the Tremere or Giovanni as clans, preferring to number the Salubri and Harbingers as among the "true 13" instead.

                      This often puts them at war with those clans, but that's not really a change from the status quo.

                      The Furies would bring in new ritae based on the Warrior customs of the past, including a few that are inspired by Healer Valeren (Obeah) to tame the spirit. This makes them indispensable to those on paths.

                      The Assamites who ran off to Alamut were replaced by the many more who left Alamut in turn. This has allowed other clans and bloodlines to enter the leadership of the Black Hand, so it's less of an Assamite elite force than it used to be.

                      But the Assamites are still very populous within its ranks, and the ones who stayed from the beginning see themselves as the true "Unconquered".

                      This gives them an extra level of prestige -- which adds to the prestige they already have for maintaining the oldest traditions of the subsect (as the Lost Tribe).

                      The Road Backward... or Forward?

                      In the aftermath of the Fourth Civil War, some of the ways of the old roads have been rejuvenated.

                      A broader range of paths have become tolerated, with the need to keep the Beast in check especially important given the civil wars.

                      Notably, the Path of Lilith is now grudgingly accepted, on the basis that Bahari want to slay the Antediluvians, too, even if they have an ambivalent-to-hostile relationship with Caine himself.

                      They are, in a sense, still "children of the book" (Book of Nod), to steal a mortal term.

                      With paths flourishing, the Sabbat uses soft coercion (via these faiths) as a complement to the harder forms of coercion it is known for. Many elder autarkis are thus invited into the sect to teach the ways of the forgotten paths.

                      Although much of the lore and artefacts surrounding the DA roads was lost or destroyed, there are still a few elders who have knowledge of these oldest ways -- and the mentor-student relationship, as well as the nature of shared path ritae, helps forge inter-clan and inter-faction bonds in the Sabbat.

                      Greater Support for Religion

                      Every Cainite city now endeavours to provide for the spiritual needs of its members, with temples for larger paths and shrines for smaller ones.

                      More cities are like Montreal than Mexico now, minus the infernalism. (Though Montreal will struggle to rid itself of infernalism forever, at least while Metathiax is still bound there, it's no longer riddled with active infernalists.)

                      I also had some bloodline tweaks. E.g., the Erinyes are Gargoyles descended from Erinyi in Montreal, and have the Short Fuse Flaw in common.

                      The Hungry Sisters are the main lineage of Blood Brothers now that there's no one making new members and the rest of the bloodline can't reproduce (excepting Dr Varona's circle).

                      Some of the scattered Tremere antitribu survivors have developed a third eye, which the Salubri see as a sign of their submission to the Salubri and their rejection of Tremere (oh, the irony!).

                      Others develop mutations that have come to be called witch's nipples, due to the infecting nature of the White Worm.

                      The Ahrimanes and Lhiannon appear to be two sides of the same coin, long estranged but now conspiring in the wilderness.

                      The Harbingers also have these new bodyguards who appeared from nowhere but seem very strong...
