                    Originally posted by Murder-of-Crows View Post

                    From all accounts published so far, the Second Inquistion are not fanatical nutjobs, but rather highly-trained government agents and military types using latest technology. I don't think that any vampire without Fortitude could withstand a missile attack. Those that can, will be cleaned up afterwards by tactical strike teams. Factor in that the government can move by day, and even the main chantry (Fortschritt?) won't stand a chance. Thaumaturgy despite all it's paths and rituals is no true magick.
                    Could a vampire survive a direct missile strike? Not unless they're an Antediluvian.

                    But we're talking about a heavily enchanted fortress, with masters of dark magical abilities. You're assuming the missile would strike the vampire in the first place. The best way to survive an attack is to not be hit. EDIT: I was unclear in my defense of the chantry. First of all, I'm not certain where it's located, but you can't do a missile strike in the center of a city without civilian casualties, and that's something you avoid. If it's isolated though, that's not an issue. However, the ground invasion can be very easily thwarted and it has nothing to do with weapons, but the people *holding* them. We've seen several examples of chantries with the whole "non-euclidian geometry" thing going on, where unwelcome guests get lost in loops of corridors that double back and twist on themselves. No amount of GPS and body cams are going to fix that. As far as a daylight attack, that's what the ghouls and defenses are for.

                    Also, let's be blunt. Modern soldiers are not action movie heroes. They're normal people with some training and good gear. Humanity's strength is in its *numbers*. Vampires have been fighting off highly trained vampire hunters for centuries. Modern tech does give humanity an edge, but it doesn't make the soldiers invulnerable and able to One-Hit-Kill Methuselahs.

                    I'm not saying a SpecOps team couldn't assault the chantry. I'm just pointing out that it's not a clear cut victory simply because they have better funding than the Society of Leopold. Casaulties should have been high, and a pyrrhic victory at best. If it's just a "humanity, fuck yeah!" rolf-stomp, then it's just stupid. So far, every major plot twist of 5E I've been exposed to strikes me as "storytelling by fiat," rather than a natural development of the setting, barring the resurgence of an Inquisition as a concept.

                    Plus, as someone already pointed out, the Council of Seven didn't *live* there, and meetings were irregular. The idea that they somehow got all seven is patently ridiculous.

                    The more I hear about the 5E lore, the more it feels like the massive lore shift between 2nd and Revised, where the authors took a chainsaw to anything they didn't like from previous authors, and removed the elements with all the subtlety of your missile attack example. I may use the 5E rules, but methinks I'll use Beckett's Jyhad Diary as a starting point for my own lore, because I really don't like the 5E setting update so far.

                                                        I'd wait until I read the whole story, but at first glance, based on the interview, both London's falling and the beheading of the Tremere in Vienna is, honestly, and I'm sad I have to say that, sorry, but more than a little straining.

                                                        In the case of London: yeah-yeah, it's the most-surveilled city of the world... So what? Crime, gang crime specifically is still record-high right now, if I understood correctly. Even if I take the Technocracy into account and even if all the mentioned* agencies were working together... Even if the whole thing was an inner job, machinations of Mithras who underestimated the mortals... Even then, in my oppinion, a good bunch of kindreds could have fled. Also, when was this, in the 10-year gap? If it was years back, maybe more than 5 years, the assumption that those agencies could maintain total control and the vampires wasn't able to seep back, to some extent is... More than straining.

                                                        *About all the agencies: I'm not sure that the Leopold society helped here, but if yes, I dunno, Great Britain is very much not a catholic country, I'm not convinced they'd play well with the government spooks there (flashbacks to Hellsing...).

                                                        Vienna: okay, no. Just no. Blowing up the Vienna chantry, okay, I could get that, if it was a massive operation. It has interesting story follow-up possibilities with the white worm and all. That the clan is fractured among Saulot-as-Tremere loyalist Tremeres, Goratrix-Tremere loyalists and "free" Carna-ists is a good idea, IMO. One of my biggest problems with the Tremere was always the pyramid itslef, because I don1t like to play characters in an organization like that. I assume the writers had the same idea, making the Tremere less bound, giving the players more freedom. That's okay. My problem is, if the "beheading of the clan" means the thing others argued about above, that the whole, or most of the councl is wiped out, that is, IMO, highly-highly unlikely, to the point of breaking verisimilitude.

                                                        Overall. The two story points would be okay on themselves and as I said, I'll wait for making my final oppinion on the topic,until I've read the whole. BUT, hoestly, the whole thing feels like they really-really wanted to drive home the point that "the Second Inquisition is SCAAAAARYYYY!!!! Whooooo!". I can't help but feel that they're way overplaying humans. maybe because if the Second Inquisition is that scary, that really justifies all the heavy setting changes ( the word "Spellplague" surfaced in my barin, for some nebulous reason...), but I don't like that, because it's really stretching as I see it at this point, even to the point of feeling forced. Sure, humans could screw vampires a lot, if they start to pay attention, but it just feels too over-the-top. I just don't believe powerfull methuselahs would be that oblivious and powerless. I'm not even talking about a straight fight (although I really think they'd have ample tools to at least flee), but to avoid it, or counter the worst of it. I don't think the whole Tremere leadership would fall to an attack, even if they'd lose the main chantry (which would be a great loss, no doubt) and I don't think any agancy could keep all the vampires out of a city the size and population of London, for long, even if they could drive out them for a short time and undoubtedly destroying quite a few of them during the process.

                                                        So, that's my 2 cent, at this point.
