                        I've skim-read the book now. It's basically what everyone who liked the Sabbat was worried it would be. If you liked Revised Guide to the Sabbat, stick to that. If you want the Sabbat as bogeymen, pick up this book instead.

                        It feels very much like a GM-focussed guide on how to make VTM orcs, to borrow someone else's analogy upthread. If you want to kill shady NPCs without remorse (ironically), this is the book that shows you how. This Sabbat has no redeeming qualities and behaves with a high level of stupidity.

                        Besides the Discipline powers, which are quite cool, I don't think there's much of value to those who want to actually play Sabbat characters. The Path descriptions lose a tonne of nuance, making them barely recognisable, and all the Clans and Bloodlines have basically been relegated to one catch-all 'antitribu' because the Sabbat fully reject their heritage now.

                        So if you want to play Sabbat characters, you can probably save yourself $40 (and all the aggro with international shipping if you're outside the US). The layout is better than the core, though, and there's marginally more useful info than the Anarchs book (the content is more valuable on average but there's less of it).

                        All in all, I'd give it a 2/5, which is generous. It would be 1.5/5 if I were being uncharitable, and that's solely for the layout and Disciplines.

                                    Yes,

                                    I absolutely loved that this book got into the focus on what I love most about the Sabbat in their culture. It talked extensively about how the Paths influence sect life and how the day-to-day Sabbat acted. One of the things I HATED about the previous books was the fact that the Sabbat were constantly soft-pedaled and made into "Camarilla-Lite" at the top and "Anarch-Lite" at the bottom when the Sabbat were people who needed to have their own unique culture.

                                    The best part of the book for me is the fact that we get into the nightly life of Sabbat on the Path of Cathari, what their havens look like, how they indoctrinate other Kindred, and discussion of what it means to be trapped inside the cult life. It's one of those things that make me just roll my eyes at the description of treating the Sabbat as "orcs" because the book finally starts addressing what has been seriously lacking in previous books.

                                    1. How do Sabbat DIFFER from the other sects?
                                    2. How does it FEEL to be a Sabbat.
                                    3. What do Sabbat do on their nights?

                                    I have my complaints about the book but it is far less than the Camarilla and Anarch books that just seem to be edgy for the sake of edgy versus purposeful horror. Two books that lacked any real in-universe content and just meandered all over the place in weird directions (Why does the Camarilla book have Ashirra and Second Inquisition sections? What do Anarchs actually BELIEVE?). This is a massive-massive improvement.

                                    Justin Achilli did a fantastic job of managing to make sense of what had been an extremely weird and bizarre decision to put the Sabbat off to one side. He justified it in the Watsonian sense but also made the Sabbat decision make sense out of universe. The change form the Doyleist perspective was, "How do we make the Sabbat significantly different from the Anarchs and Camairlla?" That has always been a struggle and now they have a far more distinct identity with the role of Anti-Clans and Paths replacing the previous Camarilla lite qualities.

                                            My own Sabbat campaign

                                            I should note that part of my feelings toward this book are going to be influenced by my own Sabbat campaign where the question of "What is the nature of the Sabbat?" has been a question for roughly 20 years of gaming with my Sabbat PCs. My two favorite Sabbat player characters were a Tzimisce Elder who was one of the original Anarchs (Transylvania Chronicles) and a Serpent of Light Elder. I also enjoyed playing a Salubri Antitribu Templar.

                                            One of the constant undercurrents of the Sabbat in my games was the struggle between the Sabbat who were genuinely of the Paths of Enlightenment's "higher" levels (those who understand it was to become posthuman alternatively amoral ubermensch) vs. those who only had a cursory Beast-driven understanding of the tenets. In simpler terms, 2nd Edition Sabbat vs. Revised with the former being admirable noble demons in their own way and the Revised Sabbat representing the nasty mindless monsters. It was an idea the Sabbat were failing as a sect and had become obsessed with killing and territory over their original goals.

                                            I also really emphasized the role of Infernalism in the Sabbat as I've always enjoyed the idea that the Sabbat philosophically set themselves against God (even if many are atheists) and have a strong contingent of those who make pacts with demons or are corrupted by the Baali's teachings. In part because the Inquisition can't coherently make a statement as to why they shoudn't do this beyond, "demons bad." I've had many demon hunter Sabbat in my ganes and they're the best time to have Camarilla/Anarch and Sabbat team up.

                                            This book definitely feels like it could be an updated metaplot to my own games. It provides a coherent path of Point A to Point B that the "Bestial" Sabbat have won the war against the former. The Sabbat have failed as a sect and now there's a bunch of Kindred, alien and otherwise, being forced to flee the sinking ship. Failure is not a bad thing when roleplaying vampire, though. The Transylvania Chronicles makes the fact you are unable to "win" the Anarch Revolt a major part of the storyline.

                                            My current Sabbat campaign inspired by this game

                                            The roleplaying opportunities of this are tremendous and I've already started a new MIAMI BY NIGHT campaign this week with already a few sessions underway. The premise is that a pair of Lasombra women have been trapped in the Underworld for the past two decades and they emerge in Miami, Florida only to find that the Second Inquisition have purged the city and the Sabbat that have survived are a bunch of suicidal nutjobs. Furthermore, that the time in the Abyss has caused them to lose hold of their Paths somehow. It will become a question of what a Sabbat means and how they will adapt to this new world.

                                            This book is very helpful to that.

                                            I think their goal I will "nudge" them to will be to join the Church of Caine and Anarchs to form a New Sabbat out of the ashes of the old.

                                                        Previous editions answered all of these questions with more flavour and nuance than this book does. There's a social calendar around key ritae and observances. There are expectations from being in a pack, or on a Path, or in a war party.                                                         I mean, yeah, those books were mostly fluff so they didn't feel very meaty (nor were they popular). But the Camarilla and Anarch books being particularly bad doesn't make this book better than it is on its own merits. It just makes it *seem* better, relatively speaking.

                                                        The voice is a tad more familiar--echoes of the Revised style become shouts in places--but the concept of the Sabbat has been so gutted that there's very little left for that voice to talk about except how dumb and monstrous the Sabbat is.

                                                        Take this section: 'This lack of subtlety has had a side effect that the Black Hand willfully ignores: Hunters in this era of the Second Inquisition have the least trouble following the trail of the Sabbat, and have had the most success in reducing the sect’s ranks (at least in locales other than the front lines of the Gehenna War itself). Proud Sabbat try to spin this as a badge of honor, that the Camarilla and Anarchs deal with the era of the Second Inquisition by running and hiding rather than standing and fighting. That said, it’s hard to argue with how effective numerous vampire-hunting organizations have been in breaking the Sabbat’s domains and reducing its numbers.' This is the Sabbat as suicidally stupid.

                                                        In other iterations, we'd have had Moderates, the Ultra-Conservatives, the Orthodoxy, the Black Hand, the Inquisition, etc, all offering different perspectives on how to manage this, with the more sensible elders realising they need to work at this, while the gung-ho warmongers actively do as they please.

                                                        Which creates more drama? More intrigue? More conflict? The dumb monolith or the fractured sect of yore? There's maybe one story in the V5 version, but I could spin many more out of the previous presentation because there's so much more going on.

                                                        There's an irony, of course, that GttS says the following about what has now become canon: 'It’s a common misconception that the Sabbat is an organization of “bad guys.” It’s also a common fallacy that a Storyteller can’t use the sect as the central focus of a long-term chronicle. Why are these beliefs so strong and so pervasive? Why do they persist and gain momentum? They persist because many of Vampire: The Masquerade’s players believe that the Sabbat are the black-and-white villains of the world. Period.'

                                                            You don't have to convince me the Sabbat have been greatly simplified. The Black Hand (False Hand) and Inquisition being missing as well as removing so many Paths is ridiculous. I like that the Sabbat has managed to become far more united, though. The infighting being replaced with pure zeal is an interesting take and shows them as having far better in-house loyalty after the Lasombra defection, taking the sting out of it.

                                                            But my biggest complaint is one I've stated many times: this book is way-way too short.

                                                            130 pages is not enough to cover the Sword of Caine.

                                                            It is not enough to cover its subject.

                                                            200 is not enough for the Camarilla but this is doubly ridiculous.

                                                            And it is not worth its price as a PDF.

                                                            Justin Achilli should have been allowed to detail much-much more about the sect.

                                                            Mind you, I also feel like the book being compared to ALL OTHER SABBAT BOOKS COMBINED is silly too.
