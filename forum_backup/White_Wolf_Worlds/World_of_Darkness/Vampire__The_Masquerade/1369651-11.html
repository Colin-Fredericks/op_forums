                        Originally posted by Alqamar Alaswad View Post


                        Very, very interesting paragraph. Excuse me, what are the 4 Divine Beasts?
                        They are God Beast Guardians of the 4 cardinal directions. I had a secret project of some Talmaherah, the most prominent of whom is a Daitya who helped engineer a Taoist Vampiric Sorcery through his knowledge of Sadhana and the aid of a Dur-An-Ki Ashipu, and a Nagarajah Necromancer, create the first generation of the four bloodlines and a clan of mortal revenants who they are embraced from. They act as agents believing themselves literally descended from the blessings of the Gods in their blood.

                        白虎/Baihu/the White Tiger of the West
                        They serve as the Warriors and Assassins.

                        青龙/Qinlong/the Azure Dragon of the East
                        They serve as the sages, sorcerers, and probably the closest to the leaders.

                        玄武/Xuanwu/ the Ebon Tortoise of the North
                        They serve as the sentinels and guardians, very much the defense to the White Tiger's Offense.

                        朱雀/Zhuque/ the Vermillion Bird of the South
                        They serve as the scouts, diplomats, and spies, notably they have the Discipline of Flight as a favored Discipline.

                        Being the project multiple potent methuselahs and their strict control of who is embraced the Four Clans are of notably low generation, but of small but growing numbers. They understand if they misstep they could be crushed by the Wan Gui who outnumber them. But they have notable populations in China, Taiwan, Singapore, Japan, Vietnam and Korea.

                        You might recognize their Japanese names Byakko the White Tiger, Suzaku the Phoenix, Seiryu the Blue Dragon, and Genbu the Turtle. They are common in the stories and traditions of countries that they spread to from China. The principles of Fengshua tie them to the directions and Japan even has them in Buddhist and Shinto temples and gates.
