    [Discussion] The Golden Rule - Some issues with including it as is in the game!

    01-23-2022, 10:47 AM
    Hello everyone.

    So todays topic:
    The Golden Rule - Some issues with including it as is in the game .
    The rule in question:
    "This is the most important rule of all, and the only real rule worth following: There are no rules. This game should be whatever you want it to be, whether that’s a nearly dice-less chronicle of in-character socialization or a long-running tactical campaign with each player controlling a small coterie of vampires. If the rules in this book interfere with your enjoyment of the game, change them. The world is far too big – no set of inflexible rules can possibly reflect it. Think of this book as a collection of guidelines: suggested but not mandatory ways of capturing the World of Darkness in the format of a game. You decide what works best in your game. And you may freely use, alter, abuse, or ignore these rules at your whim."
    Personally, I've found more and more rpg game developers who're adopting this, which in my opinion is really bad, especially because it's basically sending various unfortunate hard and mixed signals, and to give a few examples:
    - "We aren't really sure our rules are good enough" - if you're not sure of your game, then don't publish it just yet!
    - "These rules are not necessary" - if these rules are not necessary for the game, why publish them?
    - "There are no rules!" - if there are no rules, what am I even playing, and why the hell write many books that cover so called non-existing rules?
    - "The rules are only guidelines!" - Saying they are mere guidelines, opens up rules interpretation shenanigan-land.

    Here is the thing, I don't think what's intended with The Golden Rule in of itself is bad, but defining it as a rule and saying there are no rules, introduce a whole heap of issues, including giving players something in writing that they can use to further their rule interpretation shenanigans. Also, I think it's lacking lot of important information regarding who is the rule intended for, when and how to use it. Also, I think it belongs in a ST specific book, as the players can't change the rules, or can they now? Because that's basically what this rule says they can.

    I get it, the developers essentially want the STs/GMs and players to be able to change the rules to suit them and their game they want to play, but the above is really lacking in many areas and produces problems of it's own.

    You decide what works best in your game. And you may freely use, alter, abuse, or ignore these rules at your whim.
    - this is the essential part, and should in my opinion have had it's own section and not just short single paragraph doing a little lip-service to it.

    The intention with the rule is good, to give a written permission to use it in any way that supports the game, including changing or ignoring them. However, with that being said, I think it's important to note, that it's something that's always been possible to do as long as everyone who's playing the game agrees to it, just not obvious to everyone, and some also feel they need permission to do so. But not clearly defining who, when and how to use this, does create a lot of problems - because as written, it's basically giving everyone a mandate do to as they please with the rules in the game, or should I really be saying, the non-existing rules? It's placement in my opinion should've been in a section clearly intended for the ST only, because

    What are your thoughts about The Golden Rule?

                      What's with this "now it's actually a rule," stuff?

                      This has been an actual rule in VtM for 30 years. The Golden Rule as a printed rule in the books has always been part of the WoD. It comes out the 80s RPG scene having a much strong faction of the hobby that were not down with the idea of "rules are guidelines, house rule what you like," and tried to normalize heavily discouraging house rules by "amateurs" that shouldn't challenge the wisdom of the big names of the industry. Yes, people have been house ruling the whole time anyway. The hobby was born of house ruling. But the current attitude of house ruling being the norm is because of games like VtM explicitly saying it should be the norm for decades now.

                              I think that Golden Rule is good. Obviously, no matter what the books say, any particular gaming group could change them in what ever ways they like or strictly adhere to them like some kind of religious canon. But I get much better vibes from an RPG book that says you should feel free to change the rules to have more fun than a book that acts like their rules are holy writ or produced through some kind of super science that players couldn’t understand. I remember there was a phase that WoD went through where a lot of the books chastised option options as bad-wrong-fun and it was obnoxious.

                              Also, plenty of great RPGs have rules and systems that are lacking. World of Darkness is in that category for me, their rules are highly flawed and sometimes completely unbalanced - but I’m so glad that they released V:tM back when they did instead of waiting for perfect rules.

                              People can ignore the books if they please, but explicitly supporting that decision in the books is good because it makes people feel more comfortable making those changes and it disabused some players of the notion that the rules are infallible and can’t be changed.

                                  The Golden Rule is simply there to make the GM the final arbiter of what happens in their own game. It's an official "empowerment" that the GM can change rules or the setting if they like or do whatever. It's basically there to make sure that 1) the game is fun, and 2) the GM can swat down complaining players who want to usurp the GM's authority at the table.

                                  It's not meant to allow players to undermine the GM's authority. Now, this is an old school consideration because I know many newer games have some philosophical problems with the idea that the GM is the authority, and want to strip that away. Regardless if that is a problem (and I say it isn't), that wasn't the intent when the WoD was first published.

                                      Even in some of the new design philosophies (though they're not that new, just more popular) it's not about stripping the GM of the practical applications of authority, but reframing the GM's position as a player with different rules rather than someone that isn't "part" of the group. Some games do this as things like GMs using metacurrencies to limit their exercise of authority (esp. as a way to encourage play where players give GMs more authority in exchange for getting to do special things), some games do this by having more explicit definitions of how to exercise GM authority.

                                      PbtA games are in the second category, and still tend to give GMs very broad power over how the game is run.
