    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    That's not necessarily contradictory. My point was the Sabbat was on a downward spiral from its previous position in terms of managing the Beast and that the Tzimsice and Lasombra were losing control due to how much of the organization was becoming less about being an organized post-human group and more just a bunch of vampire orcs with no Paths to guide them. The Sabbat was a house of cards that by becoming "Camarilla but meaner" was doomed.
    There's something to this, but is more complex. And again, one of the problems with how V5 did it is that it simply striped the Sabbat from its complexity, and that was not what they were heading to.

    First things first: Revised was in many ways a proto-V5 in the way it tried to steer the game towards a more specific and closed vision of how it should be, and the idea of the Sabbat as loosing control was pretty much a part of this. So there is some important nuance between the Sabbat of each edition, not only from pre-V5 to V5. Most of what you described was Revised Sabbat and no other version of it, for whatever it's worth.

    But even then, and admitting they're a more complex and bigger society than even I argued (I stand by my previous arguments, but want to add they're far from the full picture of the Sabbat), the Sabbat is not on this kind of downward spiral. Revised made the Paths less universal as a retcon, to begin with, not a movement. And while control of the Sabbat on a societal frame was at peril, that had nothing to do with personal self-control against the Beast.

    And the Sabbat as an army doesn't actually continues such a trend even if it was real. It's not about the Beast winning or the clans loosing their grip, it's actually about the Paths becoming even more effective and social control becoming tighter than ever. A degenerate Sabbat in this sense would be overtaken by Wassail and virtually impossible to organize in anything bigger than a pack with half a dozen members. Instead we have everyone on Paths, no apparent signs of anyone falling to the Beast with any regularity, and the whole thing organized into an actual army.

    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    I dunno, I think its unintentional but politically accurate to today's times that the true believers are manipulated by cynical political operators eventually overtake the movement. It has to be unintentional because it actually is a good allegory. *rimshot*
    I don't know where you get this idea.

    When extremists overtake the movement it isn't because they grow out of control, it is because an even more cynical operator takes their reins from those who want to compromise in the name of "normalcy".

    But the Sabbat is, in certain ways, going the opposite way in Revised. It has been under extremist control for a long time, but now it's getting hard for those guys to control because it's growing too complex and nuanced, with ever more members that don't follow the party creed, even if they follow their rules.

    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    This is where I'm going to argue because the Sabbat were depicted as an army first and by the time that V:TM's Old World of Darkness ended in 2004, obviously terrorism was on the mind of just about everyone. Yes, Waco happened in 1993 but that was after Second Edition had firmly established the Sabbat with the Players Guide to the Sabbat. Fundamentally, I think the Sabbat were always designed with vampire lore in mind and providing antagonists as well as enemies.
    Terrorists are not armies. States have armies. The reason why some state-like actors get called "terrorists" is political, not technical.

    It is part and parcel of how terrorists as an actual phenomenon operate that they don't have a proper army or anything like that. They're distinct from guerrillas, governing bodies (aspiring or not) or organized societies. All those can make heavy use of wanton violence and terror tactics, but they're not the same thing. As a quick fact, some groups are called "terrorists" precisely as a means to deny them recognition as anything else and justify the use of anti-terrorist tools when they're less efficient, but politically more interesting. As they say, "don't negotiate with terrorists".

    So cults can have standing armies when they're big enough, and some historically had. The LDS Church, for example, fought a number of local armed conflicts before settling in Utah, and they were a cult at the time and for quite some time.

    But you're right that, ultimately, the Sabbat is more about a role in Vampire than being specifically an allegory. Yet, this only means they have more nuance, not that they're not a cult, or that they're actually terrorists. As someone who claims domain over territory and has a standing army, by definition they're not terrorists.

    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    The Sabbat worship Caine, hate God, loathe the Church as wlel as Christian denominations, and believe that their actions are spiting the god that has damned them. They don't worship the Devil or demons not because they aren't a anti-Christian religion but because demons inevitably get the better of Sabbat in deals. Mind you, I think it's important to also note that the Bishop, Cardinal, Priest, and so on titles are meant to be satirical.
    I don't know where you got this info, because as I read the books, that's not what I see.

    While young members frequently use symbols like crucifixes as ironic affectation, the older members get annoyed at this precisely because they think it blasphemous to do so without believing. The titles are not satirical, even if not completely equivalent to the Church. They certainly don't think of themselves as part of the Catholic Church (except those who are), but they're pretty serious as far as the leadership goes (specially Lasombra) on being Christians.

    They're linked to the old Cainite Heresy, they think about Caine as a holy figure, not an enemy of YHWH. Even if they don't think of Caine as the first Messiah (and some do), they do believe in Jesus as the Messiah and they do believe themselves as YHWH servants.

    Even if there's possible non-Christian readings of the Book of Nod (and if you know anything about the other Abrahamic faiths, you know a lot of the Book of Nod is specifically Christian, even if it isn't nominally New Testament), the Sabbat specifically does a Christian reading of it.

    Just to be clear: Judaism and Islam are not simply Christanity sans New Testament. There's a lot of differences in theology beyond that that applies even to the other scripture, and also other texts beyond just the Christian Old Testament. Islam doesn't even deny the NT, it's scripture to them, and Jesus is a prophet. Don't try to argue something is "non-Christian Abrahamic" just because it's Old Testament, if it is a reading of it familiar to Christians, it's pretty much Christianity.

    Also, their hunting of Infernalism definitely goes far beyond just thinking demons get the better of them. They have an Inquisition precisely because of how important hunting demons is to them beyond just thinking it's a bad idea. They are not satanic vampires, although they have some trappings of this trope.

    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    Yeah, it's a weird vampire game that doesn't have immortals. It eliminates a huge chunk of the game's themes and ideas as well as the point of being a vampire.
    Yes, but you can't have a game where most vampires are new because shit is hitting the fan, yet most vampires are old because you want a regular vampire game.

    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
    I suppose my point is that I think the Second Inquisition is an overstretched, badly organized, fractured organization in my view versus the opposite. FIRSTLIGHT is an alliance of the Arcanum, SAD, NSA, loose hunter organizations with the Society of Leopold basically calling the shots by being the only people who 100% have an idea of what to do. I suppose I can't imagine that they're not chasing fires and extremely overstretched, especially since every time they show up in video games and canonical storylines, they take heavy casualties.
    Yes, but that's not the SI of the books, specially the early ones.

    I'll say again, we're specifically talking the organization that just wiped out vampires in those cities. Whatever we think of them, we have to start from this point, and it's not related as something that happened because they were leaving anyway. However stretched or fractured this organization is, at this point in time it managed to do quite an impressive job, especially considering that they did so in two big cities without making them into war zones or breaking the Masquerade.

    However Americans usually think violent foreign cities are, Ciudad de México is the biggest city in North America in population (yes, bigger than NY) and an economic powerhouse. Fighting a hidden enemy in there isn't any easier than doing it in any big US city. It isn't a frontier scenario, it doesn't have biker gangs running around like they own the whole place, it won't just ignore a paramilitary group doing whatever they want within it. Things don't get much different in Montreal, except it's safer.

    And the problem, again, is that keeping vigilance requires WAY less resources than this, and is in the ballpark of what the SI does according to the books. Even if this is not your SI, it is the SI in the books. And more importantly, the Camarilla doesn't have the means to know all that even if it's true. It's a fantastically weird movement for the Cam to move in so soon after the cleansing as they don't know if the thing that did it so thoroughly is still there in some way, and it isn't hard for them to be.

    The problem isn't just what the SI can actually do, but that the Camarilla assuming they can't do much was an incredibly stupid move, unless they had a lot of inside info the books don't give any reason to think they have. And then we have the whole logistics of moving an entire population of vampires from elsewhere, the problem of finding those interested parties in the first place, the work to make them into a functional Cam society that doesn't look like it was founded yesterday...

    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

Primarily because they know the SI can't bother wasting resources there and also because the Sabbat left most of the places with a minimum of remnants anyway because so many went to fight the Gehenna Wars.
    No, they don't.

The Camarilla knew nothing, and should expect the worse out of safety, which is what they did according to the books, except for taking those cities pronto for some reason.

    Of course you can easily say that most of the news were overblown by that time without technically changing the setting, but saying the books imply any of this is reading in them what you want to have as a headcanon. And yes, some people in the Cam may know in your game. But we're talking a lot of people moving in.

    However stretched and fragmentary the SI may be, it's still composed of independently functional parts. Setting up vigilance is one of the things that a lot of SI components can do by themselves. In fact, if they're starting to distrust each other, they may very well do so all at once, each keeping their own "watchtower".

    What makes the SI work is them knowing each other, being willing to share info on enemies, at least the presence of targets, and all of them wanting to hunt vampires. Coordination is secondary to them being dangerous, even if it makes them far more dangerous. Yes, they need coordination to bomb Vienna, but keeping vigilance in an already cleansed city isn't bombing Vienna.

                                A "Gold Rush" isn't a bad idea if there's a story behind it that takes into account both all the things that must happen and how distinct is the end result. The way it was done it's not a Gold Rush, it's a couple cities that simply don't make sense.

                                It could have a consideration for how risky it was to take the city right away, and how they did to make it safely. It could have an actual plan, or even a mystery, on how the Cam did it so boldly. It could and should be a city colored by the fact it was recently established, the inhabitants are mostly from elsewhere and most power structures are still in flux as they learn about each other while keeping their own secrets from each other, as they didn't had yet time to acclimate for real.

                                There's no story, not even a "Gold Rush" story.

                                You can't cross most of the US by car in an evening, by the way. It takes 40 to 50 hours of travel by car to cross it coast to coast, that's a lot of evenings. Here's again the matter of nuance and scale, traveling isn't all that hard for a vampire that does their homework, but it isn't something a vampire does without care, either, there are many potential risks. And we're talking dozens of vampires for Montreal and more than two hundred for Ciudad de México even at conservative numbers. At that scale, even a 1% chance becomes a frequency instead.

                                So there's a lot of stories that could and should be told with this endeavor. In fact the "Gold Rush" is a very good analogy for the kind of story you tell with those movements and the kind of cities you have at first (at least from the perspective of vampires). The problem, then, is those stories not existing.
