          I don't limit soak or other passive "resistances", but mostly limit all the rest.

          Depending on the chronicle and the scene, I let the vampire spend 1 Blood + 1 WP to ignore limitations for the entirety of the scene, but usually only for flight or fight situations.

          Other than that, what I do use as a dial of how harsh I want daytime to be (or not) is to tune the ease of access to magical ways to deal with it. There are Thaumaturgy Rituals that give you some respite from this disability, and they may be more guarded, non-existent, or quite common and available for cash on your city. Sometimes vampires come about strange artifacts with this kind of power, and you may have homebrew rituals that are more powerful without being more costly if you like. Other splats may also be able to create even more powerful protections. You may tune things up or down for specific character for specific reasons, and access to outside sources has the advantage that you can easily come up with reasons for things getting harder or easier.

          Quite simply, as a weakness that's more thematic than mechanically relevant, you can and should attune the Day Sleep to your chronicle's needs. In crossovers in particular I get rid of them altogether for PCs because it becomes pure inconvenience, usually through some Fetish or something from other splat that isn't a big deal under their own magical framework.

                  I think the humanity/Path restriction is ridiculous.
                  Should Vampires be penalized during the day? Yes.
                  But in accordance with how in control of the Beast they are? How Humane or practiced they are in the path? Not at all.

                  However, I've never really had this issue come up during my games. So I've not really thought too much about it.


                  Here's my simple, logical rules:

                  you have a 2 dice penalty towards physical attributes, to a minimum of 1. (alternatively: your physicals are reduced to 1. You're welcome to blood-buff or use disciplines to boost that)
                  Your disciplines levels are reduced to a capacity based on your generation. If you spend a willpower, you can raise the cap by 1 per willpower spent.
                  13th-8th 1
                  7th 2
                  6th 3
                  5th 4
                  4th 5
                  (Note: Chart is the same as it is for Ghoul's max discipline level, which is dependent on the generation of the domitor. This is intentional. The main difference between a ghoul and vampire during the day is that the vampire is dead, so their muscles don't work so well)

                      When I’m a player I handle it by playing a dhampir.

                      When I’m a storyteller I run in a “Christianity (Roman Catholic specifically) is True” setting so only Humanity (slightly tweaked) truly helps with sunlight and for NPCs (because PCs are exclusively on Humanity) I eyeball about where they’d be if they were still on humanity for how daytime penalizes them (most end up in the 3-4 range… even by the normal rules it takes a LOT to drag someone down as low as Humanity 1).

                      If I were telling a more general VtM story though I would set the penalties not based on Humanity/Path, but on the INVERSE of Generation… basically the opposite of @MyWifeIsScary’s approach. The stronger the Blood the more the curse is in effect.

                      Thinbloods are barely affected; -2 dice (except from virtues) and don’t even need to roll to wake up (they do have to spend a blood point, but it counts towards their rising for the following night too) they’re just like someone who hasn’t had their morning cup of coffee throughout the day. I also ruled that thinbloods only take damage from direct sunlight, indirect might lead to sunburns with extended exposure… basically, they function much more like the vampires of more modern franchises.

                      11-13th Gen are more seriously affected; -3 dice (except from virtues) and must spend a blood point to wake, which lasts until nightfall when they must spend another to rise normally. The vampire feels like it’s got a bad case of the flu; everything’s too bright, their stomach is queasy and their muscles are weak. A second blood point reduces the penalty to 2 dice for the duration.

                      8-10th Gen are nearly crippled; -4 dice (except from virtues) and spending a blood point is good for maybe an hour of activity at that level. Everything hurts and all you can think about is ending whatever disturbed you so you can get back to sleep. Each extra point of blood reduces the penalty by 1 (minimum 2 dice penalty). This makes you feel slightly better (akin to the levels above), but it takes a LOT of blood to even semi-function.

                      6-7th Gens are crippled by daylight; -5 dice and spending the blood point only gets you up for 10 minutes. Extra blood spent reduces the penalties as above. Only the threat of Final Death can even spur your torpid body to move during daylight and it is nearly as sluggish and unresponsive as a corpse unless you flush it with massive amounts of blood to achieve even brief awareness.

                      4-5th Gen only function through sheer force of will and inhumanly high stats; -8 dice and the blood expenditure gives you barely a minute of wakefulness. Spending more blood buys more wakefulness, but every moment of wakefulness is vital to your survival so your actions are typically brutal and direct so you can return to your slumber as quickly as possible. You created entire broods precisely so you didn’t have to be disturbed during daylight hours so someone’s going to pay for their failure once nightfall comes.

                      Antediluvians… my settings usually run with the BJD bit from the 2nd Gen that the Antediluvians aren’t as powerful as they pretend to be and so I treat them just like 4th Gens with a LOT of downtime experience and would use the same daytime penalties. If they’re actual blood gods then stats are irrelevant anyway suffice it to say that if you disturb their slumber they get VERY cranky.

                          Depends on what kind of game I'm running. For example, I've run a game in which the players were Path Paragons.                                       Well keep in mind: this mechanic is intended as a buff for player-characters. You're supposed to play a young vampire with a high humanity who fights the oppression of the old vampires. In fact, they've doubled down on this this edition by creating bane-ratings. In a way, you're not supposed to play a vampire, not really. You're supposed to play someone who uses the subtle but powerful advantages inherent in having a high humanity and low bane rating to beat the REAL vampires, the vampires who have high disciplines but also have high bane ratings and low humanity. That's actually one of the hidden meanings of this being "Vampire: the Masquerade". The hidden meaning being that it is a vampire-hunter game masquerading as a vampire game. Many PCs are supposed to hunt for Golconda, or for a cure to their vampirism, or, at a bare minimum, try to die protecting their human touchstone from the REAL vampires, preferably by taking the real vampires with them into the sunlight.

                                      It's not an accident that the older vampires have a glaring weakness in both low humanity and high bane ratings. Those weaknesses are intended as a buff to the player characters trying to kill them.
