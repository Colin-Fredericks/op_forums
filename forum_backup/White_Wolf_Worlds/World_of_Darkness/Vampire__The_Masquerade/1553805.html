    Eidectic Memory question

    09-13-2025, 11:04 PM
    I have a player who is trying to exploit the merit Eidectic Memory by claiming that it lets him remember everything that he's ever seen or heard and expects not to have to keep notes. Is there a book that explains the merit better than just a simple overview? I told him that the merit is basically photographic memoey and that even RL people who have this don't recall everything (a simple Google search shows that). Any suggestions on how to deal with this? Thanks.

      Kinda depends on what edition we're talking about.

      In V20, there's a roll for memories being formed under high stress situations; which could be asked for retroactively.

      As for "having to keep notes?" That's nothing the game is going to solve for you. If you have a character is obnoxiously good memory (perfect or not) in your game, it's ultimately on you to make sure this is a fun part of the game for the player. They paid the points for it, and you let it. It's not "exploiting" anything to want your character to know things you paid character points to know.

      I can sympathize with this being frustrating, but if you want the player to take a large role in managing a trait like this, you need to set those expectations during character creation.

              Overall your player is right. There are possible limitations to it depending on edition, but the core assumption is exactly what the player said: the character is expected to remember by default. They shouldn't be penalized just because the player isn't taking notes, unless you actually forbids other players from taking notes.

              Even the limitations Heavy Arms and Shadeprowler cited are there for narrative reasons. They're there to prevent a perfect memory coming in the way of a good story, and they should be exceptions, not the rule, otherwise you're punishing the player for choosing this trait. You should, by all means, remind the player of anything the character ever experienced, even retelling scenes if this is reasonable.

              Now, game management is something else entirely. If you need the player to take notes so the game flows better, you need the player to take notes and that's it. I usually have a lot on my hands and I ask my players to take notes so we have everything at hand when I forget something myself. When someone takes Eidetic Memory I do ask them to keep careful notes as a player, just to ease my work.

              If the lack of notes is frustrating, talk to them. The trait is there to help the character, not necessarily the player. If it really annoys you, you may even ask them to change it for something else, no ST is required to allow traits they find disruptive to their style. That said, as long as the character has it you shouldn't be stingy with information without reason.

                  If the player expects the Merit means you, the ST, to do all the work so he doesn't have to, he is wrong. The Merit is about the character. It is not about the player or ST.

                  As ST, you can certainly help the player out if he can't remember and you can. But the Merit doesn't force the OOC work onto the ST because the player is too lazy or simply has a bad memory.

                  If you, as the ST, have certain limits, the player can't expect the Merit to force you to do extra work. He either has to accept the limitations or put in the extra work OOC. If he wants more than what you can (reasonably) provide, it is up to him to rise to the challenge.

                  "Expects not to have to keep notes" is definitely an OOC demand, and that is not what the Merit implies.
