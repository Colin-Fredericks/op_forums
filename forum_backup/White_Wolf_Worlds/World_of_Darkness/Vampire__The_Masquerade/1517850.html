      It's okay.

      Which is really something that I think summarizes it. The write-up of London is pretty good and the premise of the Camarilla's second most important city (after Paris) falling to the Second Inquisition is handled probably about as well as it could be.

      A lot of players are allergic to 5th Edition but this is the least offensive adaptation of the Edition for haters as well as lovers with the exception of the OPP supplements. It takes the "cleansing" of London and makes use of enough lore and background from previous editions that you could use it for V20 without difficulty.
      Some general thoughts:

      1. The premise of the Second Inquisition wiping out London's vampires due to the fact Mithras arranged it is basically, the "The Vienna Chantry was an inside job" theory. Mithras' human cult is what gives the SI all the firepower and support it needs to kill every vampire in London and, you know what, it works. It doesn't neuter the vampires of the Camarilla but also explains why they could do it in London but nowhere else.

      2. However, the game is also a treasure hunt for Voldemort's Horocruxes. It is a VERY D&D-esque premise with you gathering all of Mithras' relics so he can do a ritual to restore himself to being a 4th generation Methusaleh.

      3. The actual premise can be completely skipped. The game sets you up as Mithras followerer possessed acolytes and that doesn't need to be done. All you need to do is have the PCs asked by
      the cult to do the job and have enough threat that they say "yes."

      4. The fundamental final choice of the game is the one you should be building up to throughout the game. Do the player characters side with the Camarilla, do they side with Mithras, do they side with the SI, or do they betray everyone?


      Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

                          Effectively, there are a couple of major problems, which are touched upon in previous comments:
                          1. This isn't the players' story; this is the story of Mithras and some other notables, in which the player characters participate. This is such a dominant theme in stories and chronicles set within the World of Darkness that the writers don't even notice that's what they are doing. The players should never be observers to the metaplot; the metaplot should be background to stories about the player characters in a chronicle.
                          2. And, in a stock piece of superhero comic logic, we have characters who are canonically dead returning because the writers like them too much to leave them dead. Lord Camden's death is an important plot point in the Giovanni Chronicles; it's meaningful and poignant because he's actually dead, and that it has some emotional charge. That chapter of the Giovanni Chronicles is called The Sun Has Set, and do you think, maybe, that this title is a statement of some kind? And then Camden back, and Richard de Worde (formerly dead) and even Mithras himself (pretty dead, and the second edition of A World of Darkness doesn't really justify the way the character is written here), it really robs this world of some consequence.

                                              Can you really say you're playing the "Fall of London" if you're mostly engaging in a fetch quest parallel to the action?                                                   Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                                                  Can you really say you're playing the "Fall of London" if you're mostly engaging in a fetch quest parallel to the action? Sure, Mithras is orchestrating the whole thing. But it's not really ABOUT London's fall. You, the PCs, certainly don't get any opportunities to affect whether London falls to the SI. Only who ends up taking the place of all the Kindred definitely doomed to die.

                                                  That's the problem. Not the technicality over whether it is or is not happening during the Fall of London. But that you, the PCs, are irrelevant to those proceedings. And are instead engaging in a different plot adjacent to it.
                                                  On my end, I suppose it's a question of what exactly you think THE FALL OF LONDON should be about. As well as how much agency the PCs can have in such a situation. For me, the thing I would make clear to my PCs in that situation is that they absolutely cannot have an affect. You can't kill the Hunters off because the cat is out of the bag. WIth the Masquerade broken, there's no victory that can be achieved by force of arms or even one single group of vampire powers. It's the DAYS OF FUTURE PAST scenario with vampires instead of mutants and vampire hunter squads instead of Sentinels.

                                                  Yes, you can beat a squad and should definitely have multiple encounters with the SI during the events but it should feel hunted and desperate.

                                                  In any case, here's my advice for running THE FALL OF LONDON:

                                                  ​1. Have the PCs be original PCs and Neonates. They should all be residents of London or people that have an attachment there.
                                                  2. Have Mithras' people contact the PCs early on and offer them vast wealth, power, or influence if they get Mithras' objects of power.
                                                  3. Have the PCs consider simply skipping town and find themselves bound by a mystical force of some kind that prevents them from doing so. This will make London claustrophobic. It might be Blood Sorcery, a geas, or simply Mithras having dominating them and they don't realize it,
                                                  4. Things get worse and worse as the proceeding events happen. It should be only a single week in terms of duration but havens getting blown up, vampire minions being grabbed off the street, and the destruction of clubs as well as more all being events.
                                                  5. It shouldn't be Blade but instead no one entirely knows what's going on. The police are rounding up ghouls, the military are providing checkpoints, and the banks are freezing assets but the actual vampire hunting is done by Mithras cultists/Special Ops/SI/Society of Leopold stuff.
                                                  6. Reward cleverness over "rip them to shreds with Protean 2 and Potence." Social Disciplines work just as well if not better.
                                                  7. Things should make PCs paranoid like car bombs, helicopters in the air, and an atmosphere of Paranoid. I recommend WATCH_DOGS: LEGION for a good example of a London that has changed dramatically.
                                                  8. Queen Anne will be the Camarilla's side and you might also introduce some honest-to-God Punk Anarchs. Both of these will believe Mithras is behind all of this (or Roger De Camden) over believing humans could ever get their ass in gear to do anything.
                                                  9. Have the finale something where the PCs really can make a big difference. They can finish Mithras off forever, they can betray the Camarilla, or they can soften both up by sending in the SI then killing the survivors or--they can leave AFTER giving Mithras his objects and let Caine sort it out.
                                                  10. Throw in some mini-encounters like discovering a group of Thin Bloods are just giving havesn to the Second Inquisition and pretending to be ghouls or feeding people. Not a smatr play but they all register as alive too.​
                                                  11. When I ran the campaign, I also had the Sabbat attack the city during events and gave a brief reprive as the SI vs, the Sabbat caused things to get completely crazy. The Sabbat ends up losing horribly (not expecting humans who can fight back) but it also shows the old power structures no longer matter.
