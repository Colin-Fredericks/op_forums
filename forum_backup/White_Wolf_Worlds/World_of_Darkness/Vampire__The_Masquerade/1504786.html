          I see people suggesting Malkavians with "multiple personalities" so much I think I've developed an allergy now, but yes.
          We lean heavily on the "seer" part of mad seer, so at my table the Malkavian's derangement must be something that can be used to impart foreshadowing, misinformation, red-herrings and headaches.

          I had one Malk whose insight manifested as other presences living inside her, which are under storyteller control. They comment on things, gossip, often distract from events going on around her by dragging her into a conversation in her head, draw her into a dream-like inner world to interact with them (at the expense of losing time sitting like a vegetable for unknown lengths of time in physical reality) and sometimes take control of the body for their own purposes which she may or may not remember, but often end up having an effect on events further down the line.

          Also, a personal gripe: Generally if somebody comes to me and says "I want to play a character with [insert psychiatric condition here]" I'll sit them down and have them consider any other clans that this character could work in (stigma or no, sires don't always do a psych test before embrace and those of us who can function can often mask it enough to go unnoticed). Automatically throwing a mentally ill character into Clan Malkavian just for that is uncreative, imo. Yes, Malks draw on those who can handle the stresses of their existences, so it's possible for a member of the clan to have had the real disorder in life (the Voerman sisters, for example). But I think Dissociative Identity Disorder, as in the traumagenic condition that usually (always?) develops in childhood, is something that wouldn't be unlikely to develop in potential childer of Giovanni and Tzimisce revenant stock (and the Tzimisce bring their own twist on being multiple personalities in one body, what with being able to mold it like clay). If you've had a traumatic life from hell that started in childhood and still manage to pull yourself together and make a go of it, and have the requisite attitude, consider a Lasombra.​

                  While I personally avoid them, my concern is not so much fishmalks or Malks being funny, so much as the concept of:
                  "My PC is a Malkavian. He's a paranoiac/narcissist/megalomaniac/sociopath/whatever."
                  "Well, that sure sets you apart from the other 12 clans populated with of paranoid narcissists, megalomaniacs and sociopaths."

                  I don't like people just slapping whatever page of the DSMV or equivalent catches their eye onto the table and just going "Malkavian," I feel like it does the clan a disservice. Wanting to roleplay a mentally ill character isn't justification enough for me for there to be a clan specifically just to be the "insane vampire containment pen". If that was all they were, you could just shift it over to Caitiff, ones who are the childer who aren't destroyed when their vulnerability is discovered that are sometimes be abandoned if the sire or clan feels embarrassed by them. Meanwhile the Ravnos do the trickster archetype much better, as far as I'm concerned. You could just have the Malks take their origin as a collection of abandoned mentally ill Caitiff who banded together to form their own clan identity, but I'm attached to their niche as the oracles, therefore their "madness" is a subconscious form of precognition in my version.

                  Personally, I much prefer subdued malkavians. Characters that can actually live a hundred years yeah? I hold to the belief that The Beast cares about efficiency and getting what you want/need and the Clan curse is all about forming clan bonds and it would be against all interests to screw over your character hard enough to make them non-functional.
                  The curse shouldn't screw a character over bad enough to stop them functioning, that I agree on. And in pcs at least, it should generally be more subtle, and not too intrusive. But i should definitely impair them in some way (but the example character I gave wasn't terminally unaware of her surroundings a listless vegetable 6/7 days of the week or anything.)

                  Compulsive lying
                  Compulsively telling the truth (but being able to word it in a deceptive way)
                  Compulsive lying or unwelcome truth telling is something I'd use for a Ravnos. Although a Malkavian who finds themselves compelled to lie or tell truth at times where it would unknowingly trigger some event that they want or subconsciously believe needs to happen is something I could see in my interpretation of the clan.

                  Increased difficulty for courage rolls.
                  Territoriality/Being unable to stay in the same area for long out of persecution fears.
                  Reasonable enough. Anxiety and paranoia are likely developments in the world of darkness, particularly if one who struggles to differentiate between their own insight into what's going to happen and their mundane worries and personal beliefs about what they fear is going to happen. Also knowing secrets is a good way to be persecuted.

                      A concept that I created based on this, very basic in fact, was a character who is naturally intelligent, but has difficulty communicating normally and is easily intimidated, which is why many even abuse him. Willpower. It's a very simple concept, but I found it functional, at least, what you guys think.

                          I think the best way to handle a multiple personality malkavian is along the lines of what one of my players did many years ago.

                          They ran each personality as a separate character, complete with a character sheet for each personality. Each persona that came out during a session gained XP and advanced independently of the others. This had the added benefit of ensuring that the player kept things clear on which personalities had what stats. This meant that the stats would grow in different ways, but they couldn't borrow from each other.

                          It wasn't unusual for the Player to get over 30 XP per session, but that was divided between the 10 different personalities, so it was no more than the usual 3 or so XP per session like everyone else. Now the player was pretty good about role playing the various personalities, but there were enough of them that we started using little place holder pictures at the table so we could keep up with the shifts that would occur, sometimes in rapid succession. This became thematically correct a little later on when all the personalities learned Obfuscate rank three and would just present a different face/body with each persona.

                          Is this a ton of bookkeeping and heavy on the role play commitment, yes, it is, but it also means that it weeds out the would be fishmalks because the amount of effort outstrips the attention spans of the players who are just playing the character for the lols.

                                          Originally posted by Psicose View Post
                                          I don't know if it was just bad luck on my part, but from my experience, and this doesn't just apply to roleplaying, others are quite likely to get annoyed when you complicate things, with a case of multiple personalities and juggling them. this forces the other players to have to adapt because of you and in the worst case they either kill the character or they don't want to play with you anymore, so I think two or three personalities is fine and it's possible to discuss how to keep that.
                                          The problem is that the issue of complicating things swings both ways. The player who is wanting to play a multi-personality malk is complicating the game for everyone else, including the ST. Half the time in my old groups we couldn't remember each other's character names, so expecting a group to deal with essentially a mini-coterie inside one character is a bit of an ask.

                                          TTRPGs are a group activity and should be fun for everyone, not just the one player. If the player can't wrap their heads around the idea that they might need to take on some additional work so that they can play the character they want and not bother everyone else, then they are not mature enough to be playing with in the first place. As an ST you have to be able to talk to your players about this sort of thing otherwise you are dooming your game due to bad communication skills.

                                          There is a saying over in the D&D community that applies to any RPG really. "No D&D is better than bad D&D" which means it is often better to scrap a game than to play in one that is a pain. So don't be afraid to say that a character concept will be too disruptive without some adjustment and who knows, maybe with a little back and forth discussion, you and the player might come up with something even better.
