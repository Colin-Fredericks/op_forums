                                            Stereotypes are very useful to story telling though. So long as you stay away from overtly negative real world ones, like many point at the Ravnos as being a bad example of. Having a clan stereotype gives a base that lets players come up with better stand out characters, and also gives them a "comfortable" niche that they can use to see if they have an affinity for the clan. Not all stereotypes are bad, and a lot have a grain of truth in them somewhere. Pretty much everyone has "stereotypical" mental images of certain groups, and designing characters to conform to or buck these trends is fairly central to storytelling in any medium.

                                            Introducing new players to a setting can use stereotypes to make adjusting easier by giving a shorthand that almost everyone will grasp very easily. Those stereotypes you're listing are nothing to do with characters, they are clan related, and with good reasons. If a Malkavian wants to blend in, being around a concentration of people who act erratically by "normal" standards is a reasonable choice. Nosferatu hanging around sewers, is again a fairly reasonable choice, there are large homeless populations that form communities in real world city sewers that they can blend into. Not sure how a crappy apartment would nod in the direction of a Gangrel though

                                            Now, to reiterate, Negative Stereotypes Are Bad but not all stereotypes are negative.

                                            Yup, that's what people tend to forget, when smirking at stereotypes and tropes and clichés and such. They work. Either because they touch upon some bigger concept or universal idea, or because they have, at least some of the truth in them. I'd say even negative stereotypes have that, it's just, they tend to lead to persecution, rather than positive solutions.

                                                            I also want to give my two cents about the game since there are still no real updates or further reveals. There are 3 things that really concern me at the moment?

                                                            1. The transition from thin-blood to a full clan member. I know this is possible via diablerie, but honestly it just sounds like a bit of a forced plot. The idea, according to the developers, is for the PC to make a more informed decision about which clan they would like to join. OK, so far so good. Problem with that is it's very difficult to invent a way to join a clan rationally without involving diablerie. Retroactive "revelation" of your clan wouldn't work in particular for Nosferatu and Malkavian, whose curses would be more noticeable, not to mention even if your sire is revealed you are still thin-blood. Ok, let's say you diablerize a clan member - since you've just spent time learning about the clans and, therefore, they know whoyou are it seems a bit weird that some of them would be ok with a diablerie. Toreador, Ventrue and Tremere would all have issues with it. I won't pre-order anything until they reveal how this is handled.

                                                            2. Tremere houses. Unlike a lot of people in this thread I actually like House Carna. Yes, I know her arguments are not that solid and yes I know she is a product of the growing feminist perspective in the setting, but I'm actually relieved to finally not be Pyramid-bound as a long time Tremere fan and player. Sure, the Pyramid is one of the cool things, it ties into the actual Tremere weakness, makes you paranoid and anxious, restricts character concepts and actions, offers great power and protection at a cost of personal freedom. Cool, but after so many years of that I'm actually tired of having such a monolithic clan. Now, finally, Tremere are split into social, political and paradigm lines and it's finally exciting to make a decision about what kind of Tremere you want to be, not to mention that a bazillion character concepts suddenly became plausible. Traditionalist Camarilla vs Anarch-leaning progressive Camarilla, elitist or inclusive (when it comes to siring), monolithic or cellular, hermetic paradigm or old magic paradigm, strict hierarchy or flat hierarchy with more personal freedom, established vs struggling. For the above reasons I'm really hoping they introduce two houses for clan Tremere, even if this requires more content. Otherwise the whole talk about being in sync with V5 is false. Hell, even give some Thaumaturgy variation based on whether you're a House Tremere hermetic or a House Carna witch, that would be fun.

                                                            3. Actual content. I mainly lurk around the Bloodlines 2 facebook group and someone just sent me a screenshot of Hardsuit Labs confirming that the entire content of the release (yes, including side quests) will be roughly 25-30 hours. Ok. For a game that's been in development for 3 years (at least) this is nothing. As a regular gamer I've played narrative-heavy games with many branching paths that easily give you 70-80 hours of gameplay, at least. Even if we account for story DLCs in the future I can't see VtM:B2 to have more than 40-45 hours of gameplay. That's quite disappointing.

                                                            So, I'm still waiting for Hardsuit Labs to convince me this game is worth it. Maybe they nailed the atmosphere, who knows, but I need to see some actual content and to get some answers for the above concerns.
