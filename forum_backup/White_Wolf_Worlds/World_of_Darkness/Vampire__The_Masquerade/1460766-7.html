                Originally posted by Matt the Bruins fan View Post
                Hmm, it strikes me that doing just that and giving a head start to some elder who can barely digest even human blood anymore would be a good way to legitimize their feeding on vampires and get in their good graces.
                Thin Blood hunts aside, it wouldn't work as a strategy.

                A Blood Hunt is for destruction. So it is a single meal. Elders with such level of Hunger need far more than just an eventual Hunt, they need a regular vessel. It wouldn't be worth to give them a Blood Hunt as reward.

                But for high gens and addicts it is another matter. The former want to low their generation, the latter will be happy with any opportunity for a fix. But the former are the most reliable to reward.

                Originally posted by Kammerer View Post
                In regards to diablerie, however... I dunno. I'm inclined to believe that vampires are still people, and people are fundamentally good. In the words of Penn: "I'm already doing all the raping and murdering that I want - which is zero."
                I completely agree with you in that regard, but there are caveats. Lots of caveats.

                From the Doylist perspective, what I'm stating is the way the game is designed. If you read carefully, you'll see that the game have built-in situations where the moral quandaries of Diablerie are weakened. Low gen vampires aren't just a bag of goodies, they're frequently a threat. When you are going to kill anyway, because circumstances so require, why don't go the step further?

                The key main tropes where this is thrown more frequently are the Blood Hunt and the torpid Elder. The first is a criminal. As much as a Blood Hunt can be used for political purposes, the game usually makes it clear that it isn't a good idea to abuse it frequently, so most BHs are for real, the target is a monster that fully deserves whats coming, even more than any other Kindred in the city. The second is usually too clearly a monster, and one that will be unstoppable if it ever awakens.

                Of course, it isn't ever without consequences. Marks your aura, may cause crippling addiction, and harms your Humanity. But at the same time none of those are instant game enders. They are themes to be explored, and they wouldn't be so if the game didn't intended for PCs doing it. You can resist the addiction or learn to live with it, find ways to pay for the silence of any Auspex user that gets you, live with a little lower Humanity or buy it back. Those things aren't that hard, they don't remove the sting but pretty much make it something you can live with.

                Under the Watsonian perspective, Kindred society is hypocrite and shows it. Diablerists are talked about in a way that makes it clear that they still exist in polite society and you just don't point fingers without good reason.

                Blood Hunts, especially, are explicitly that. A circumstance under which Diablerie isn't just possible, but sanctioned. If you can pull it, it's your right, as the criminal forfeited their own right to live.

                None of those make Diablerie common, by any means. But it definitely is built to happen in a game, and to be a possible reward for the story, if not from the Prince or something, at least from the Storyteller.

                            Ashfinders part 5 (Amber Freeman AKA Thrivehive)



                            Type: NPC

                            Synopsis: Amber Freeman (Thrivehive) is a horrifying stereotype of an Instagram influencer who has managed to fall her way into becoming a cult leader as well as cannibal.

                            Analysis: I like Amber Freeman because she is possibly one of the best uses of Golconda I've had in my game. Which is to say, she's someone who is using it as a buzzword and jargon without any real knowledge of the spiritual elements behind it. Golconda is hard to use in a game as it is a goal to escape playing V:TM but someone who is just grossly distorting it for financial gain. There's a lot of real-life appropriation of Buddhism, Native American practices, and animism for this. Which is interesting because monteparnas is already in the LA by Night thread discussing the cultural appropriation of Chaos Factor.

                            Interestingly, Amber Freeman is the childe of Naomi Stewart the progressive Ventrue, via a proxy. I suspect Naomi probably thought making Amber a Thin Blood would be less of a burdensome curse. However, the Curse of Caine is not so easily stymied and Amber reacted horribly to becoming a blood drinking monster even if it was the Diet Coke of Damnation. I always like when even well-intentioned sires unwittingly destroy the people they think they're helping as with Arthur Caldwell and Eric Sharpe. Indeed, Amber has ended up under the influence of THE INSIDIOUS DOCTOR MORTIUS (Da duum!) who is a much-much worse Kindred than Naomi Stewart.

                            Part of what I like about Amber Freeman is the fact that she's seemingly so wholesome and even thinks she's a hippie granola girl but is actually an absolutely horrifying monster. She's after all manufacturing an empire about snorting the remains of her fellow Kindred and selling enlightenment by the bottle. This reminds me of Love in YOU and Jason Stackhouse's girlfriend in TRUE BLOOD during the first season. Just because they're hippies and progressives doesn't mean that they're not a bunch of psychotically selfish assholes after all. Amber probably has never killed anyone in her life (a rare feat even for a Thin Blood) but she has killed dozens if not hundreds via her network.

                            I also love her design. It's very cute and reminds me of STRANGER THINGS.

                            The only thing I have a slight issue with is the Cults of the Blood Gods insistence of referring to the Ashfinders as all Millennials as if that makes them a trendy youth orientated group. I'm a millennial and I'm forty. Amber is 32 as of 2021 and while that's certainly a great deal younger than most Kindred, I honestly think its best to remember they're adult men and women pretending to be teenagers and taking advantage of youth trends. That may be the point as it does fit with the themes of V:TM but I could see some people acting like they really are people in their twenties versus parasiting off it.

                                    Ashfinders part 6 (Doctor Mortius)



                                    Type: NPC

                                    Synopsis: The life and times of the MOST DIABOLICAL TREMERE OF THEM ALL.

                                    Analysis: The Insidious Doctor Moritus (Da dum!) is not actually the B-movie mad scientist and supervillain that I long portrayed him as in my Milwaukee by Night Chronicles. Indeed, this write-up of the man pretty much states that he is not morbid or weird but I come at my V:TM games from a different place than Matthew Dawkins AKA The Gentleman Gamer .

                                    Part of what I love about V5 is the fact that it has gone back to 1st Edition and revived so many elements that were left by the wayside. Raising Doctor Mortius from being an obscure character in a long-forgotten sourcebook to being the secret hand behind the Ashfinders is the kind of thing that I absolutely love. If I was a member of OPP's staff, I would have attempted to do the same with Maldavis in the Anarchs and Modius as a leader of the Nephilim or Cult of Helena (while bilking them all).

                                    To be honest, I think that Doctor Moritus kind of comes across as a "fix fic" to certain questions regarding Thin Bloods and Alchemy in V5. Thin Blooded Alchemy is one of the more controversial elements of their new status and there's a serious question how the most ignorant and modern of Kindred are able to create such an enormous occult achievement. Even if they just lucked into it, why is it "Alchemy?" That's not a natural choice of a word for people to call their supernatural power. I'd be more inclined to think they'd call it Heisenberg after Breaking Bad or Blood Chemistry.

                                    Instead, putting Doctor Mortius behind it and maybe starting the ball rolling before his Ashfinder allies spread Thin Blood alchemy via social media is quite believable. I mean, I imagine it would put Doctor Mortius on the Red List (and with GOOD FUCKING REASON) if it was discovered not only that he armed all of the Duskborn with powerful magic but created a cannibal drug cult. However, as far as Doctor Mortius is concerned, it's all about the work. I also like that he is our first identified member of the Anarch Tremere and we get the sense they're not so much righteous revoluties like the Carna claim to be but just don't give a shit about the Ivory Tower or its machinations.

                                    Which is interesting because Carna is motivated by resentment but wants to stick with the Camarilla because they're still Tremere and manipulators while the Ipsissimus are apolitical but this very stance is a political one when everyone else is choosing sides.
