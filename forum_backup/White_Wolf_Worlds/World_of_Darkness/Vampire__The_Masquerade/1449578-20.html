If you're part of a tiny group of vampires who scramble to get blood and avoid hunters every night, the idea of getting a huge group together and waging war on someone in a skyscrapper with multiple well-armed ghouls isn't really a thing for you. If you ARE the guy in the skyscrapper, things are a little better for you, but you need to protect the masquerade even harder lest you find your bank-accounts frozen by the government. The result is that there are no "Sects" as previously understood. There are some rich guys quietly conspiring to eliminate threats to their fragile existence, and some poor people too weak and fragile to do any more than deal with the problem of the moment.

                                    Some clarification, as I think about it in more detail:

                                    As of 2e, Requiem vampires can lash out and have keen senses by default. The latter is a small thing, but it’s cool and I wouldn’t have minded V5 swiping it. Their disciplines are also designed differently, in that you tend to get the main power upfront and add to and refine it with subsequent dots. The first dot of any discipline in Requiem is a much bigger deal than the equivalent in Masquerade, where you might have to get to the 2nd or 3rd dot to do something similar (as mentioned above).

                                    As of V5, Masquerade vampires are physically a little bit harder to kill, with very little that causes them aggravated damage (V5 doesn’t distinguish between bashing and lethal) and no chance of instantly killing them without it. They also are not subject to fear frenzy from small fires, which is a nice change. Their ability to rouse the beast to add to attributes is somewhat more generous, as it applies to *any* attribute, not just physical ones. Their blush of life is also more generous, lasting the whole night instead of a scene. Discipline-wise, again, the design is significantly different, with V5’s disciplines being collections of similarly-themed powers rather than refinements of a single core power. They’re more versatile and variable but not as potent as the ones in Requiem, at least not upfront.

                                    Unlike Requiem, V5 limits all traits at 5 dots and doesn’t allow bloodlines to have unique disciplines, though you can see in 2e Requiem is already moving away from the latter. This may make elders appear weaker in V5 if one’s only understanding of elder strength is having disciplines over 5 dots, but in reality they have a wide and unpredictable range of powers, bonus dice to use and resist disciplines, faster healing, more bonus dice to attributes when rousing the beast, and a greater ability to do all those things without increasing hunger (which means they can do more of them at one time and/or keep it up for longer). In effect it’s not that different from what you see in Requiem’s elders, except with greater unpredictability because of how the dice and hunger system works, so that it’s possible for a Masquerade elder to use disciplines and heal and add to attributes nonstop without ever running out of power during the scene). They’re operating on the same scale as neonates but are generally much better at everything.
