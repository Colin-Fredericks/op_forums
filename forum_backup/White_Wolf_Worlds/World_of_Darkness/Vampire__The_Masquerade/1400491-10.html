    Originally posted by Haquim View Post

    I quoted the very first VtM book to prove the notion that diablerie was a crime punishable with death was a thing even back then (yep, what you call "purely social perils" were a bloody death sentence on diablerists...).

    If you want to say that mechanically diablerie had few drawbacks in 1st ed that's true but that's a consequence of the fact the concept of the diablerized vampire being able to take control of the diablerist if the diablerie failed was introduced in later editions and rules followed suit (because the narrative parts of the game determine its mechanical aspects...). So Mithras was diablerized but only partially so, Tremere did not really subsume Saulot's soul after all and Augustus managed to turn Cappadocius into a wraith intead of wholly consuming him, and rules for diablerie slowly adpated and changed for players as well.
    Did you notice that these are all unfathomable ancients rather than the vampires you'll usually meet?
    One last thing: I wasn't making an argument about the actual rules of diablerie but mechanically speaking the current version of diablerie is an high risk/high reward enterprise. If you manage to accomplish the deed you can get large amounts of experience you can spend to increase your disciplines but you also risk eroding your humanity really quickly. Since the act is you eating someone's soul for power it seems to me the rules manage to represent quite well what diablerie is.
    The thing with eating is that you're supposed to chew... Perhaps I'm a bit sensitive, but you've made the suggestion that you're one of those GM's that instills a lot of extra, mostly fluff based penalties on diablerists. I've played with a (new) storyteller that wanted to spice up Diablerie with vast amounts of attributes, abilities and disciplines (but also had the prince, who diablerised a sabbat elder, 'have a tussel' with the consciousness of his victim) and I've played a lot more with a storyteller that gave me, in one instance, a fear of intimacy and touching people, and in another instance (where I was on a Diablerie friendly path) I was possessed to send coded warnings to my victim's packmates of my ingenious plan to stake them all during the day and have them delivered... Suffice to say I'm not a fan of storytellers taking artistic liberties with Diablerie. It's best discouraged. (also note: These three diableries were the only ones I've ever commited with my characters, and the first one wasn't my choice)

    The latest edition is the worst possible Diablerie system. This isn't me just hating on V5; It's just a fucking terrible system and I'd hate it if it was in any other edition.
    It encourages high humanity vampires to Diablerise, while it makes diablerie a low reward, incredible risk action for low humanity vampires.
    Generation isn't the benefit here, since low generation comes with disadvantages like losing thin blood alchemy or the ability to feed from animals. The Benefit is lots of discipline XP; It destroys party balance (Imagine the one high-humanity vampire diablerises and gets 30 xp on disciplines, and then they do so again and get an extra 25xp on disciplines, while player B Diablerises once, loses an extra 2 humanity, and knows she shouldn't do it again.

    Since the emphasis has gone from lowering generation and moving closer to Caine, and instead moved towards gaining experience points for disciplines, Diablerie really has moved towards "becoming a quick and dirty path to power" rather than something you'll eventually need to do if you want to remove the limitations of your generation. I think that's a terrible thing; It's gone from a tool granted by the heavens to provide savvy vampires with the means close the gap with ancients into the vampire equivelent of skipping some grind by paying to win.

                                            For me personally, here are some of the reasons I like the Sabbat:

                                            •They make great antagonists - no matter what kind of vampire you’re playing, the Sabbat can fuck up your shit or provide an interesting counter argument

                                            •Contrast
                                            -The Sabbat are an interesting contrast to the Camarilla as a militant theocratic conspiracy rather then a conservative neo-feudal conspiracy. The Sabbat allows for exploration of mysticism, religion, and monstrosity without the restraints of the Camarilla or Anarchs - but with their own restraints of course

                                            •What Could Have Been
                                            -The Sabbat are the result of a justified revolt against abusive elders and a seismic shift in nocturnal politics. They were the first Anarchs, pioneering for brotherhood and freedom from the tyranny of the elders. They are a revolution that was co-opted by counter-revolutionaries that were theocrats and politicians who cared more for their own power rather then the true ideas of the revolution and the clash between these conservative leaders and the revolutionary bodies continues to this night

                                            •Fraternitas
                                            -Vampires are addicted, ruthless, amoral, sociopathic bastard who would sell their mother’s for a blood bag and burn their best friend for having a nicer house then them - before moving into said house. The Camarilla and Anarchs both show this in different ways. So does the Sabbat, to be fair. However, the Sabbat is also different. Sure, bishops and priscus play the jyhad; but down at the street level? Packs care about each other - the bond might be mystically imposes but the feelings seem real. Pack mates would kill or die for one another; they fight together, they mourn together, they celebrate together. They reject the solitary hell of Caine’s Curse and rejoice in each other’s company and when you feel alone... that sounds pretty good. Just ask most cults.

                                            •Lore
                                            -You’re getting low on humanity and need a new path to survive? Find a Sabbat mystic and learn their ways. You want to know the truth of the jyhad and the secrets of Noddist lore? The Sabbat has your back - just report any torpid elders you find. After interrogation, we feast!

                                            •In A Way, They’re Right
                                            -The Sabbat, like all groups - particularly groups of vampires, is flawed. However, they also aren’t entirely wrong. Elders do rule over their children with iron fists and without concern for the safety of the childer. Founders do rule over clans for their own eldritch purposes. The Antediluvians may well be the champions of Gehenna. The Camarilla willfully ignores the threat and most Anarchs are ignorant of it - most independent clans are actively AIDING their founders! The Sabbat are the only ones that seem to be willing to treat this like the threat it is and depending on the ST, the efforts of the Sabbat may be one of the things keeping infernalists and antediluvians at bay.

                                            •Finally...
                                            -Well, like, listen, I’m a normal and well-adjusted person but, I mean, come on...

                                            Sometimes I just want to be a Brujah Antitribu named ‘Tony the Hammer’ who likes to play golf with human heads, is that so wrong?!?!
