            We can pretend the two systems are equally good if you want, but it doesn't change that V5's approach to Generations leads to a fundamentally different attitude towards it.

            In V1, Generation functions as a "glass ceiling". It's unfair, it determines who can Dominate who, it determines how much blood you can hold and how much you can expend in a short period, and it determines the maximum level of your attributes. It's something everyone covets. you literally plan murders for it, and this instils a deep sense of distrust and paranoia in kindred society. If you just murder some elders, you gain their better blood capacity, you become the person who Dominates, not the person Dominated, and your limitations are pushed aside, allowing you to become more than you could've became before. It's a natural part of the order of things. The downsides of Diablerie are purely related to the social fallout of murdering another vampire.

            In V5, Generation is a a slide towards damnation. The closer you are to Caine, the hungrier you are, the less satisfying your meals are, your beast is resultingly more unstable, your clan curse becomes worse, the ancients have more sway over you. These are hugely undesirable traits, punishments for the transgression of Diablerie. What are the benefits of Diablerie? you can heal faster, blood buff more, and you can get some XP quick if you're diablerizing someone of higher gen and you have high humanity; these are helpful in moments of desperation, but not worth the consequences in the long run. Because of this, there are societal changes; It's not beneficial for a neonate to diablerize elders, and resultingly the Elders aught to be less worried about the younger gen trying to gobble them up.

            (also, due to how bad the mechanics are, Less humane vampires are paradoxically going to suffer more and benefit less from the inhumane act of Diablerie, which is really not what you want. )

            In V1, Generation underlines much of the Jyhad, because vampires want power and they need to take it from others. In V5, Generation undermines the Jyhad, because it's a mess. V1 Generation encourages you to eat the rich. V5's Generation would encourage you to only eat the "poor", were it not for the humanity paradox (I need high humanity to for maximum diablerie benefits, but if I'm looking for maximum diablerie benefits then obviously I'm not that humane...)

            Thematically, they're just massively different things.

                I don't use V5's mechanics, so those problems are all non-problems for me. V5 doesn't model the kind of the setting I want.

                What I do like is the idea of blood pools, blood per turn, and "who can Dominate who" (or their equivalents in any system) being determined by a trait that increases with a vampire's age, but is capped by their generation. Ie, Blood Potency. It frees players from needing to spend Background dots during character creation if they don't want to be 13th generation while making diablerie even more tempting. A 7th generation fledgling has no incentive to diablerize a 7th generation elder if they both have the same generation-derived benefits; but if they have different Blood Potency scores, then diablerie becomes more tempting. A 13th generation fledgling will never reach high Blood Potency without diablerie, but a 7th generation fledgling will still have to wait hundreds of years for their blood to thicken without diablerie. It's a tempting shortcut to all neonates no matter who their sire is.

                V5 had a good idea in marrying Masquerade's generation with Requiem's Blood Potency. But like a lot of things with the edition, the execution was problematic and had implications for the setting in ways the writers didn't appear to fully think through.

                        Generation, Requiem, Masquerade, Power

                        My opinion is that they HAVE done a lot of attempting to mix V5 and VTR for creating a kind of "merged" Vampire: The Masquerade and I'm kind of back and forth on it. As stated, I'm very positive about V5 in general but that doesn't mean that I am 100% pro-V5 because there's some fundamentally different design philosophies about V5 and Requiem. Requiem does what it does VERY well and it is a fantastic game but it is incompatible in its depiction of vampires with V:TM.

                        In V:TR there's a "glass ceiling" (yes, MyWifeIsScary I'm using your metaphor) for vampires in terms of power level and it is MUCH-MUCH lower than generation. There are no Antediluvians and there's arguably no Methuselahs in terms of godlike and demigod power. While not quite 100% accurate, basically you can reach 8th generation and some level 5 Disciplines and that is IT. You are done whether you are as old as Caine or as old as Disco.

                        Vampires do not even have the kind of power they did to hold cities. They are not the Illuminati but chiefly concerned with their hunting grounds. It's less about controlling the cops, newspapers, and billion dollar corporations and more about controlling the local gas station and a single city block where you feed. Being a vampire is not NEARLY as cool or sexy and there's not even that much power being the Prince if you can somehow herd the cats of five or six different factions in your hometown to say you're one.

                        There's a lot to be said for Requiem's books on storytelling, low level personal horror, and more. However, I feel like you can fit Requiem's style into vampire WITHOUT restricting Elder Play and the big epic conspiracy theories that previous editions had. I feel the rules are too restrictive in terms of how much power you can accumulate and also some just flat out don't make sense with what we know of the lore. The Antediluvians and Methuselahs will DESTROY THE WORLD when Gehenna arises and dominate humanity. Ur-Shulgi is so old he terrifies an entire Clan. That isn't really possible with the current rules set.

                        Right now, Helena's biggest advantage is the fact that she can use her Disciplines 1-4 without Rouse Checks and while spamming Presence is impressive--it's not exactly godlike.

                        I will say that I DO like the changes to feeding and how its impacted by Generation. Generation was previously a purely good thing that everyone should want to have as low as possible. It was the motivation behind large amounts of diablerie and part of the War on Ages. The changes to feeding means that the lower your generation, the more inhuman you are and the less capable of "sustainable feeding" you are. Animals, Blood Bags, the blood of the dead, and so on are all things that higher generation vampires can survive on. It helps add a new dimension to the War of Ages that high generation vampires are likely to keep their Humanity longer.

                        It makes the quest for POWER more POWER also even more explicitly one that will turn you into a monster.

                                    Kindred Society is Darwinist because generation is coveted. If it isn't coveted, then all you're really fighting over is territory or some personal grudges, and the simplicity of these conflict creates a tendancy to stable out. If everyone has land and there isn't much to gain by persecuting others, vampires won't do it. Sit back, relax, maintain your territory, age up to gain power, and attend the occasional vampire social. If anyone wants to have a power fantasy, there are mortals for that, you don't need to rile up the vampires. Blood potency being more important than generation creates this kind of environment.

                                    Getting rid of blood potency and putting it all-in on generation creates a fundamental, inescapable conflict for vampire existence. Vampire A embraces Vampire B to fulfil a role that needs doing. Vampire B improves over the years and hits a bottleneck; the only way past this bottleneck is to devour Vampire A. Vampire A, fearing this, Embraces Vampire C to fulfil the role vampire B is performing, and then tries to remove vampire B from the equation. Vampire B, Fearing that, creates vampire D to help him, and tries to usurp vampire A. Vampire B essentially becomes the new vampire A, tries to play vampire D off against vampire C, and things get progressively messier. That's the core of the Jyhad. Without the need, there isn't fear, and without fear, elders aren't screwing over their childer, and without elders screwing over their childer, there is no need.

                                        Kindred society is also Darwinist because every Kindred has a violent psychopathic rage-monster screaming RIP!TEAR!KILL! inside of their soul 24/7. Dipping back into VtR for a second, I actually liked the mechanic where vampires that had never met for the first time had to deal with a feral fight-or-flight moment because it emphasized the animalistic violence inherent in the Kindred that would inevitably have impacts on their social interactions. Not to mention that plenty of people find reasons to fight and kill each other over plenty of things without being vampires that need to maintain control over the source of their power/immortality/addiction. I can't speak to how good or bad V5's Diablerie and Generation mechanics are, but the argument that fighting over Generation via Diablerie is the only realistic motivation for violence and danger in Kindred unlife does not seem reasonable to me.

                                        P.S. regarding the idea that there's plenty of "land" (aka: herd) to go around, remember that the cities presented in games constantly have vampire populations that significantly exceed the hypothetical "one vampire per fifty thousand humans" ratio that has commonly been stated as ideal or typical. For whatever reason, the cities that we see tend to feature vampire populations that have scarce resources that they will need to struggle to control. I suspect it's because the cities chosen tend to be big cities, therefore seats of influence and power, and therefore vampires interested in gaining power (a lot of them) flock to those areas to fight over it. There is a LOT of potential for conflict between Kindred without even considering the struggle over Generation/Blood Potency. That comes up after, when most vampires find they need that more esoteric power to support their struggle for the more mundane power that gives them a Haven from the sun and Vitae in their veins. Vampires that prioritize mystical or esoteric goals are the exception, not the rule; I say that as someone whose favourite character-types are almost always said exception, but they are the exception.

                                        Funny enough, Generation was one of the things that led me to VtR before really getting into VtM, because I actually prefer the idea that power comes from age; the First Monster is the baddest mofo because they have been around longer than literally any other member of their species, not because power flows from a "source" only to inevitably degenerate. It's a supernatural version of a theory of history-as-decline that I've grown very tired of hearing. It was something that I accepted as I became attached to other parts of the lore, and more recently I've really leaned into the idea of Generation being part of Caine's Curse (or alternatively, from the Antediluvians), and that some Vampires may not even experience it, such as the Laibon.

                                        Vampire-as-Gnostic-Empowerment-via-Generation is a neat idea, and fine to put centre at your own table, but a lot of your arguments really seem to lean on it being an obvious thing that everyone should accept, when just as many people don't buy that particular interpretation. And I say that as someone who does actually like that take to some extent, though I tend to prefer the Vampiric Becoming as one of several valid options rather than the only obvious choice.
