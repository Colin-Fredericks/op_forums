        Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post

        The impression I always had from Constantinople by Night was that you had something similar in Greece and Anatolia, with Michael being the super-Prince with various Princes (or whatever the suitable Roman term would be; Praetor maybe?) around the region owing fealty to him to one degree or another. I presume that these two examples are what led to the design decision for Dark Ages: Vampire and the War of Princes, with its regional superpowers led by powerful ancient or elder vampires. My own personal head canon is that before the Islamic conquests, North Africa and Southwest Asia were in a very similar state, with powerful vampires ruling regional domains out of places like Alexandria, Antioch, Carthage and the like. (The 4th generation Ravnos Ramessu out of Alexandria, for example.)

        I think my own biggest question about the Ashirra is how do all of those elder vampires - those embraced before the 7th century AD (about 22 years BH/Before the Hijra; 4360 AM/Year After Creation) - who haven't converted to Islam (or pretended to for some reason) fit in there. During the Dark Ages at least, it feels like you should have this notable collection of regional elders associated with Judaism, Zoroastrianism, the various flavors of Christianity (Nestorian, Coptic, Armenian, etc.), Gnosticism, the Roman cults devoted to Mithras, Isis, etc., Arabian paganism, Ancient Greeks, Egyptians, Canaanites, Hittites, and so on who aren't going to accept "but you don't follow the right mortal religion" as a justification for not holding power any more so than Mithras would. (This isn't as much of an issue during the Modern Nights, but during the Dark Ages, it's a little jarring. At least it is to me.)
        You know what's weird? It's almost certainly not intentional but there's actually an answer to this question. The ancient Pagan Methuselahs of the Region who would normally be the rulers of this place are mostly dead by this point:

        * Carthage being the seat of High Brujah North African and Middle Eastern power being purged of its ancients.
        * The Followers of Set having had their power mostly broken by their constant war with Lupines and Mummies.
        * The fact that Pre-Islamic Assamites were struggling with the rise of a diablerist cult within their ranks.
        * We can presume the Old Testament and its purges of the region affected local vampires.

        But yes, Cairo By Night and Jerusalem by Night seem to be how we're meant to view the region.

                                                            Originally posted by CTPhipps
                                                            Why is it different from Christianity to Islam?

                                                            I took it to the view that Muslim vampires are going to react not terribly differently to the choice of "wanting to live" vs. "suicide due to religious tenet."

                                                            But I've always felt that vampires were monsters and not aliens, basically. You have to adjust to being part of a curse, not think its awesome.
                                                            Originally posted by CTPhipps
                                                            1. The Ashirra help explain what vampires were doing on their own in the Middle East when the Camarilla was established as being a pretty European based organization when Europe was the land of barbarians.

                                                            2. Because the Camarilla is established as a European thing that didn't exist until 1493. There's no need for the Arabic world to follow the lead of Europeans.
                                                            I fully agree with all of this. It isn't as if some Vampires in WoD abandon their religion after being Embraced (see: all of Dark Age setting books). And it isn't out of the norm for some Vampires to be highly religious as group, ever despite their condition (see: Cappodocian clan) collectively (to a point in Cappodicus' obsession with Christianity wanted him to diablerize God to become it). It's also really ludicrous for a relatively young organization like the Camarilla to waltz over and somehow "takeover" it ("it" being the region found where the Ashirra inhabit, coming to this point now); and the arguments for the Elder Vampires in the Middle-East to rather join it than fight, is even more iffy.

                                                            The other arguments for just because the same Clans inhabit the Middle-East doesn't mean they should just keel into the Camarilla make sense either. Look at the Camarilla, the Anarchs, and the Sabbat; all of them more or less majority have the same Clans, barring an exempt few; all of them have radically different views and ideologues, which is why they have conflict and endemic warfare among one another.

                                                            Vampires are also Embraced from mortals. While many things of their past way of viewing things and semblance of what's real will be shattered, they are still the summation of all their qualities and former ideals leading up to becoming Embraced. Humans despite all being Human are far from homogenous and the same will ESPECIALLY go for Vampires. It's funny to even think an organization founded in the 16th century mostly based in Europe (then much parts of the Americas, from you guessed it, European-Colonial expansion) will have much similarities with an organization that predated it in the Middle-East/"SWANA" (Southwest Asia and North Africa, as many from this region or adjacent via Diaspora have come to call in, an act of "decolonization" from the former moniker they feel has been tacked onto them due to Colonialism); along with the other factors mentioned earlier; would join hands outright with eachother.

                                                            It would atleast suggest an extended shadow conflict proceeding into multiple centuries to suggest the Camarilla to gain or support such a feat of gaining the Middle-East. I would even see the Assamites (this is all pre-V5 of course) staunchly aiding the Ashirra in such a conflict, as means NOT to have the Camarilla to have such influence; especially with the fiat with the Tremere being ascendant in the Camarilla (again, with pre-V5 in mind).

                                                            I honestly I fully disagree with the notion that EVERYTHING needs to be apart of the Camarilla. That's just extremely lazy. It is more interesting to have different sects in the motion of the Jyhad/overall game. Look how in V5 where they completely and basically absconded the Sabbat; now its just Camarilla and Anarch, and that's it ("Red vs Blue" instead of "Red vs Orange vs Blue"). Admittedly to some who favored the Anarchs, they felt like only a minor roll-tide to things previously, so it is good they have more greater stake as now.. but taking out the Sabbat whole was horrible, because now its just a boring dynamic. Even before when the Anarchs weren't as strong however, atleast there were still IN the moving and shaking of things and added to the dynamic of possible in-game plots along with the Sabbat still being there. So it was a balanced and interesting chessboard of clashing ideologues.

                                                            I can see why the Ashirra was initially introduced, because its even more stupid to justify how something as young forming as the Camarilla can categorically subsume already established networks and Domains in MENA. Like Gryffon15 pointed out, the Ashirra aren't just a religious sect; they're a political one aswell. They have their own vested interests that are different from the Camarilla. The Camarilla's full backdrop had parallel backdrops to the dramatic changes going on in mortal Europe vis-à-vis vampire Europe (with them obviously having direct dynamics with one another). Whatever happened in Europe however, as no precedence or care what was already going on with an established Sect coming or touching upon points I made earlier.

                                                            Some people dislike the Wan Kuei because they function entirely different from how mainline Kindred function, that's fine. So in efforts to "improve" them, they instead suggest or curry alternate Vampires (but are Cainite derived; i.e same Clans as seen elsewhere), but with a different Sect/Faction style backdrop. I disagree with the notion of removing the Wan Kuei, but the latter idea of making interesting Vampire Cainite style Factions in Asia is interesting atleast and maybe can in some way function parallel with the Wan Kuei; maybe at odds for the majority of the time, and sometimes allying wholesale for other times (maybe when fighting off Camarilla or Sabbat incursions; with the justification that despite their vast internal differences in how they're even Undead, culturally the Asian Cainites and Wan Kuei would work together against outside incursion or intervention).

                                                            The aforementioned suggestion is SOMETHING not half-way lazy of subsuming entire regions into the Camarilla with no contexts, no interesting plot points, or interesting Jyhad material. Too formulaic and like I said before, lazy. Same way it was lazy to kill off all the Necromantic Clans wholesale into the Hecata (which is ironic, considering I'm arguing for more interesting Sects to begin with; but the Hecata aren't a Sect, they're a thinly veiled Clan altogether so the point stands), same way it was lazy to abscond the entirety of the Sabbat nebulously for the ""Gehenna"" War and then removing almost all their holdings, and same way they lazily shrugged off all the Elders because they're not really supported with how V5 does mechanic now.

                                                            Conspiracy isn't "fun" or interesting when its kept in minutiae pliant binaries (see: Camarilla vs Anarch as of now; or Camarilla vs Sabbat as some felt previous editions were to an extent, but I don't necessarily agree with that notion). Conspiracy is actually interesting when it is multifaceted and comprises of multiple stakes vying against one another. Maybe when revisiting the Ashirra, put less emphasis or fix up the things found """problematic""" in the depiction of religion and fix it there. But giving a vastly different region something of interest instead of a same-old mainstream is more interesting.
