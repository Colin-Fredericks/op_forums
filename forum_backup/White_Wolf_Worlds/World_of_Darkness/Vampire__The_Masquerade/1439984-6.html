                                        All of the above is definitely why Mage would be the most practical thing to adapt first. Everyone is human and, generally, a part of contemporary culture so no special wardrobes or prosthetics would be needed.

                                        As an added bonus, “ranking up” in skill for a new Mage means going from probably fairly vulgar effects on first awakening (so flashy fx for the first episode to draw viewers in) to learning to use coincidental effects (i.e. minor or even no fx needed).

                                        In other words, cutting your fx costs for later episodes can be portrayed as the protagonists getting BETTER at magic. That’s a huge advantage in terms of being able to sell a series to the bean counters.

                                        People mock the idea of a CW series, but it’s worth considering how the Arrowverse got itself off the ground. “Arrow” was grounded, down to earth and, most importantly, far less expensive in terms of production relative to “Smallville” which also derived from the same DC source precisely because it was a hero where they could rely on mostly practical effects and stunts (no super strength or speed or heat vision or freeze breath or x-ray vision to portray). As such, it was a fairly safe bet in terms of getting the series greenlit because it’s production costs would be pretty typical of any other action oriented series.

                                        Only once it was established as a hit did they expand out to The Flash with its bigger FX budget (and one main super power), then once that was established it was Legends of Tomorrow (a whole team of supers) and Supergirl (a team’s worth of powers in one character... also launched on CBS with a much bigger budget than a CW series), by the time we got Stargirl and Superman & Lois both series were being primarily produced not for the CW but as streaming content for HBOmax with comparatively higher budgets and production values.

                                        The point of which is, if you want to grow the World of Darkness into its own franchise, you need to start not necessarily with the biggest name in the stable, but with the one it’s easiest to get investors to back as a reasonable risk, but which also has enough from similar faire so it can be seen as a launching point for a larger universe.

                                        That’s partly why I think Mage is a stronger starting option than anything hunter-related. Arrow got itself noticed by doing a grounded and realistic take on a vigilante superhero; a novel take coming off Smallville and with the lighter tones of the early MCU at the time). I just don’t think there’s enough with mortal hunters to set it apart from the likes of say, Supernatural, in its budget range.

                                        By contrast, I think something akin to The Fugitive with Magic could be something that would stand out. Have the protagonists hunted by mysterious government agents (black suits, black cars, black helicopters) while meeting various covert magic factions (i.e. the Traditions) as they learn to use their magic subtly and stay off the radar as they help people while staying one step ahead of the MIBs (the only face of those hunting them at first) and eventually learning the true nature of what hunts them... i.e. that they’re really a cabal of techno-mages bent on eliminating the competition.

                                        Along the way you introduce vampires and werewolves and ghosts and fairies and hunters and demons and mummies, etc. with an eye to spinning those out into other series once the foundation is proven.

                                                        In the 90’s after the Soviet Union collapsed, America kicked Iraq’s ass, the giant sucking sound of corporate greed hadn’t started hollowing out what’s now the Rust Belt, and the message that endless peacekeeping wars were a good thing was still fresh enough to be plausible; Gen-Xers had the luxury of sympathizing with vampires forced to feed from the innocent to survive.

                                                        These days people don’t sympathize with the crony capitalists, the short-selling vulture investors who steal people’s jobs just to pad portfolios already stuffed with more than they can spend in a lifetime, with elites who victimize women and children for their twisted pleasures.                                                         VtM as written is toxic in the current entertainment environment. If your vampires aren’t of the friendly neighborhood variety right now they’re only going to be usable as villains for actual protagonists to slay. Seriously, try finding something featuring vampires in the last decade or so that hasn’t either portrayed them as monsters to be destroyed (ex. 28 Days of Night, Van Helsing) or softened them down to friendly neighborhood levels (ex. Vampire Academy, Legacies, Twilight). Even the proposed Disney+ Blade series is about a vampire HUNTER with vampires as villains.

                                                        Short version; vampires aren’t cool protagonists these days outside of the skewed demographics of a forum about a game where you play vampires (where comments are mostly limited to the same half-dozen or so people again and again).

                                                        If you want a WoD shared universe to get off the ground in the present day you’re going to need GENUINE protagonists to launch with, not villain protagonists like VtMs vampires. Hunters (of the HH variety) and Mages are the lowest production budget options for those with some wraiths and werewolves as runners up if you had a bigger budget.
