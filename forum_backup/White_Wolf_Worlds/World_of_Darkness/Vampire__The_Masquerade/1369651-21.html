    Originally posted by Kharnov View Post

    I think the answer tends to come down to "the people who actually live in those places that might want to buy the product otherwise aren't interested in having those superficial (if not dangerous: see above post) stereotypes fisted into the textbook description of the part of the world that they would presumably be playing in." And combined with those of us that think "that's a reasonable thing to want," the business tries to decide which group will earn them more dollars.

    The thing to remember when complaining about pop culture infecting the "Western" depictions of vampires in Vampire: the Masquerade is that the authors are "Western." They recycled Western tropes for Western audiences, having some understanding of the context of what they were influenced by and what they were creating. And even that doesn't save them from occasionally fucking up horribly (WoD: G****s). But we are all fallible humans, learning everyday, and hindsight is 20/20.

    To the extent that pop culture "anime-esque" tropes are not necessarily harmful, I don't think that removing them from the Wan Kuei, or removing the Wan Kuei over them, is absolutely necessary. But I think that fans who actually live in those places have as much, if not more, right to have their opinions and feelings considered by the writers as the fans who don't live in those places but have enjoyed the superficial portrayal of those places up to now. Ultimately, the writers themselves have the right to decide how they might want to update their setting, and the business will consider the decision that they think will produce the most profit.

    I am saying I think there is an achievable version of say "Vampire Japan" that leans heavily into anime tropes.. if written by LOCAL voices. It probably looks very different from KOTE but going for an explicitly "Realistic" take of say Japanese myth(much less other cultures in Asia) would perhaps be just as fetishing. It also might be something like "Vampires didn't show up until Admiral Perry" I am not an expert on Japanese Pop culture, though I do part take of it and have theories.

    Generally Vampires are seen as Western Supernatural beings. One could litterally tie the spread of Vamprism with Western Imperialism... the metaphor would be unsubtle.

    The other is vampire aren't "created" They are born that way. This would be an interesting take a local fera holding the Niche until Western imperalism pushed them back.

    Jiang-shi are sort of theirown thing? They aren't any more vampires than Kitsune. Honestly given how they treat sapient Zombies in anime I'd say there was Millage to make WK a universe phenomemon but that's its own thing.

    But I am just an ignorant white man on the internet so I don't really have a deep dive to give.

        Lots of people here would appreciate a heavily updated game that would be very different from KotE, as posts above have indicated.         It wasn't really directed at me, but I want to respond to your comment about the "dumb unrealistic setting."

        The unfortunate thing is that the World of Darkness is stuck in a bind that many other RPGs avoid. It's not set in some far off other-place with other-people, where even when there are clearly real-world influences the authors usually manage to "file the serial numbers off," so the people being stereotyped usually have to be looking really hard to catch it. The World of Darkness is set on Earth. Not OUR Earth, but Earth, with all the same countries and ethnicities and religions for the most part. And that means that real people, real history, is getting dragged up and churned out as "content." And simply being a "darker" version of our world can't work as a blanket pass, because there often isn't some obvious line between what is "grimdarkification" and what is a genuine expression of superficial attitudes and understandings about REAL people.

        In a D&D-esque LARP a player can probably get away with playing as an anti-Elf racist or bigot without anyone's feelings being hurt. But using CTPhipps' example from above, REAL racists can use the FAKE racism in the game at LARPs to cover for their REAL racism against other players. It's just not the same thing, and it can't be treated as the same thing.

        PS Perhaps even more importantly, they cannot control whether or not their readers can properly distinguish between "grimdarkification," ignorant stereotypes, or "reality."

                                                I wonder if anyone even really read Kindred of the East beside me and few minority others to begin with. So really, if anything is to be tackled about """"fixing"""" (and I put that in heavy quotations because I feel like nu-Wolf hasn't even done VTM oWoD good so far, and I don't even know they'll revisit Werewolf and in a very distant future, much less Mage), spruce out the weird parts that some find trouble with, but try and keep the game intact for what it is.

So really, if anything is to be tackled about """"fixing"""" (and I put that in heavy quotations because I feel like nu-Wolf hasn't even done VTM oWoD good so far, and I don't even know they'll revisit Werewolf and in a very distant future, much less Mage), spruce out the weird parts that some find trouble with, but try and keep the game intact for what it is.
