    [Project] Make an Anarch domain

    07-12-2022, 01:06 AM
    One of the biggest problems with the Anarchs is not so much that they're uninteresting but they stand against rather than for. We know what a Camarilla domain looks like because Chicago by Night provided the Prince/Primogen/Harpies template. However, LA by Night failed to really convey what an Anarch domain should be like with the Barons just being, "Prince in miniature." So this is a thread where we share ideas of how exactly an Anarch domain might be governed and maybe even some ideas about how they should function.

    Go into as much detail or as little detail as you like.

          Although the game doubled down on the idea of the Movement as a sect of its own, I think an "Anarch Domain" would be less of a Domain under sect control and more a Domain where the Camarilla lost to the local rebels and... those rebels built whatever made sense for them in the specific case. Those may be a huge variety of things, including actually Domains that are still Camarilla, but under big concessions or a locally appointed and popular Prince. At most such Domains could offer some limited support for Anarchs elsewhere and be standing examples that the Revolution is possible, but their actual individual workings would hardly be inspiring to many outsiders.

              In my rendition of San Jose the Anarchs moved in and set up a Council. Coteries with enough size or influence get a seat on the council while those who are not part of a coterie choose a representative to speak on their behalf and cast a vote for them.

                  Here's some things I created for my own Anarch games.

                  Pact Lands

                  "No one can hurt my brother other than me."
                  -Yani, Ravnos, 10th generation

                  A Pact domain is something of a variant on "rulership by coterie." Basically, a Pact is an Anarch domain in the most literal sense in there is almost no law whatsoever beyond the Masquerade. However, the Pact gives free right of feeding and Creation as long as one observes the conditions of a mutual agreement between all the other Kindred. In general, a pact that agrees the various Kindred of the domain will lend their power to driving out all Camarilla, Sabbat, Blood Cults, or other rivals as well as give shelter to the other members. Some even have complicated Magna Cartas or Constitutions.

                  In general, Pact Lands are inherently unstable but not for the usual reasons. Inevitably, one vampire becomes "first among equals" or more powerful than all of the others and becomes Prince or Baron. Either that or the others install a puppet while ruling as a coterie over the lessers of their rank.

                  Gladhand Domains

                  "I promise that every Kindred gets at least one free killing per year. My opponent is insane about how accidents should never happen. Do you want her in?"
                  -Glorious Gary, 10th generation Ventrue

                  Gladhand Domains are the Kindred's most derided but still-practiced wildly groups due to the fact they practice the most ridiculous form of government for the Damned: democracy. Gladhand Domains generally elect their Mayors, Presidents, CEOs, or Barons once per year or decade depending on the age of the average Kindred involved.

                  Gladhand Domains are derisively called this due to the simple fact that democracy among vampires is inherently ridiculous. The vast majority of Gladhand Domains are inevitably ruled by Toreador, Ventrue, or Brujah with the general assumption that whoever has the most powerful Presence will rule. Bribes are also very common as are threats with the general result being a rubber stamp on whatever the strongest Kindred rules. Blood Bonds are also not uncommon with them frequently forced on "outliers."

                  Ironically, Gladhand Domains are still reasonably popular. After all, if the populace is happy, does it matter how its achieved?

                  Renunciation Domains

                  "We are all equal brothers and sisters before the blood. Yes, I used to work for the Ministry. Wait, don't go!"
                  -Carissa, self-proclaimed Caitiff

                  Called "Caitiff Kingdoms" or more derisively "Godless Lands", Renunciation Domains are domains where the locals have forbidden the Traditions, Clans, Generation, and any mythology of their kind. All are officially Caitiff and they are equals before the domain. Renunciation Domains generally are fairly good at avoiding Barons, Princes, and Primogen. They are, instead, nightmares of personal intrigue and behind the scenes favor trading that leaves it opaque as to who is actually in charge if anyone. Renunciation domains very often hold "Rants" to judge Kindred among their number via mass-decision.

                  The mob controls these lands and whoever controls the mob. As a general rule, Renunciation Domains tend to be founded by Caitiff or outcasts of other Kindred who proceed to find themselves overwhelmed with Thin Bloods or other Clanless as Embraces pile up. They either eventually become isolationist or proceed to appoint their own Scourges and population control that often make them even more hostile to newcomers than other more traditionally-ruled lands.

                  Coplands

                  "I am the law."
                  -Bill Plickens, 9th generation Gangrel

                  Bizarrely, a large number of Embraced cops drift toward the Anarchs. This is not something that is lost on many Brujah who have as much a tradition of turning law enforcement as they do radical revolutionaries. Cops are frequently angry, violent, and full of their own ideologies that attract the Learned Clan. The Camarilla appeals to some but others find the Anarchs just need their special touch--especially if their members are more pragmatic revolutionaries than nihilistic rabble. Other times, preexisting Anarch territories note that they absolutely despise leaders but that the chaos of their existences demands SOMEONE handle the ass-beating necessary to keep order.

                  Coplands, simply put, don't have Princes but do have Sheriffs who effectively serve as the whole of the law. They and their deputies wield the power normally afforded to the Prince and Primogen with the power to interpret the law as they see fit. Many Anarchs derisively compare them to the small hick small town Sheriff domains that used to rid the South or even compare them to Chicago's Balthazar.

                  Bizarrely, Coplands are fairly stable Anarch domains despite how much they're against the typical Anarch screed. More "moderate" Anarchs appreciate the relative straightforwardness of their society than in the constant backstabbing of Camarilla courts. They're tyrannies but you know where you stand.

                                          Old Skool Domainz

                                          Those Domains have a common set of laws, but instead of focusing on Masquerade and such, the focus are the rules for the claim of territory. Every Kindred or Coterie (depending on local relationships and practical consideration) is afforded the rights to claim a territory they have a right to govern and obligation to protect.

                                          Such codes can be simple or byzantine, but they usually give a limit to size for such territories, rigidly set or not, like "a plot of land you can watch from your haven", and an overall idea of what kind of protection is expected from the territory holders. Signs of some sort are used to mark territories, and some such Domains have a central record with territories, their Lords and their "heraldry and sigils".

                                          As long as you don't cause trouble for other territories, you're the lord of your own. You can deal with invaders however you see fit, you extend hospitality to whomever and whenever you want, and set whatever laws you wanna try. If someone tries to fuck the system, their territory is forfeited until they're dealt with by mob justice from all the territories..

                                          Most such Domains are quiet from the vampire perspective at a first glance. The system is meant to make Kindred spend as much time as possible in their own territories, minding their own businesses. They may have an Elysium-like space to meet in neutral ground, but that's about it.

                                          Under the surface, though, a problem is obvious: most territories won't have all the resources they need. Prized mortals and institutions wander about, crossing territories and leaving your "protection". Food and shelter may be plenty or scarce, territories of equal size are not of equal value. The rules of Prestation are the laws of survival, as negotiating with your neighbors is a necessity.

                                                      Originally posted by The smiling gun-knight View Post
                                                      but how is it not a domain if the building or haven is owed by a vampire?
                                                      I cannot comment on omenseer game, I want to make a general point as this issue is something that often gets raised. It certainly does by players in my gaming group. So I want to make a general comment on how I handle this in my chronicles.

                                                      There is a distinction between private property and the use or jurisdiction of any kind of political power or privilege over it. "Domain" in Vampire does not mean one's private property. It means the exercise of political power under the Traditions. It's the difference between someone in the 21st century owning an old aristocratic manor as his personal home, and the actual feudal lord who lived there with actual political power over the people - both serf and free - in his domain during the 12th century.

                                                      "Domain" is often used quite liberally by players to mean anything they control or influence. In the chronicles I run, I try to distinguish the domain of the Prince versus anything a vampire may exclusively control. I call "recognized" claims, meaning a "sub-domain" that has acknowledgement of the Prince, "Estates". If you have an exclusive feeding territory that others cannot enter, and the Prince openly acknowledges you or your coterie have exclusive feeding rights there, it is an "estate". If the Prince doesn't recognize it, well you don't have any support from the Prince if someone violates your claim - it's a normal vampire turf war. Most players would call that "their domain", but I don't because it is still the Prince that has that domain. He is putting his own support behind it in some fashion, meaning he could punish someone who attempts to violate it.

                                                      "Estates" are most often feeding territory, but can sometimes include other things like influence in certain institutions. This is most often done in a negative way - the Prince informs everyone that nobody can have influence over the police for example - rather than the Prince positively acknowledging another person's exclusive right to have influence somewhere. But this does happen. in CbN we're told that Doyle "controls" the stockyards, that Tyler "controls" the airports, that Drummond "controls" the railroads and so on. These seem to be things that other vampires acknowledge.

                                                      Now a certain amount of possessions are customarily acknowledged, but it's more in terms of "every man is king of his own castle". One's haven, one's ghouls, most personal possessions - not necessarily one's private property - and so on are supposed to be respected by other Kindred. Vampires aren't serfs in the Camarilla. (Again, individual vampires may think this is part of their "domain", but it isn't.) But these amount of protections are much less as a vampire in vampire society, than mortals in mortal society.



                                                      Another distinction that should be made is between property rights recognized by mortals, and what vampires recognize. Simply put, vampires don't recognize mortal law as applying to them at all. It's like a farmer putting what his livestock thinks or wants before his own needs. It doesn't matter to the farmer if the sheep does not want to be sheared. It gets sheared.

                                                      Now there is probably some customary measures over what a vampire owned as a mortal still belonging to him, but only if there wasn't some previous vampire claim over it. I've had more than one PC suddenly realize that his company was already part of some other vampire's influence with that vampire already having agents - bonded, dominated, or some other means - inside it. So which vampire "owns" it? The elder vampire who already "owned" that, or the new neonate whose mortal identity owns it by mortal law? Now in these cases, the Prince is usually happy to step in and arbitrate a solution. The neonate will preserve something, as long as the interests of the elder are protected.

                                                      We see something similar in CbN with Drummond and his "control" over the railroads. Now, what does that mean? Anyone can buy a ticket, right? So most vampire PCs don't see any reason why they need to pay something else (like boons, or some other arrangement) to Drummond to use it. That worked fine in minor circumstances. Drummond knew it was foolish to try to stop anyone who just wanted to travel within the city or to nearby suburbs and back. But the PCs learned on a longer trip outside Chicago going to another city that the train had a "malfunction" that stopped it, threatening they'd not make their destination before sunrise, and that - oops - their luggage was lost. Of course, one of the heavily conditioned railroad employees told them that his master could fix everything if only they could come to agreement with him. Since the game was taking place in the 1920s before air travel or the interstate highway system was established, they didn't have too many other options but to come to some sort of agreement.

                                                      Humans got to use the railroad by paying the ticket price because that's the equivalent of letting one's cows out to pasture. Vampires need to come to some sort of terms because they're "people" (unlike humans) wanting to use his property (the rail network).

                                                      The same thing would happen if people wanted to use the airports, it's just that they would need to make arrangements with Tyler instead.

                                                      Players hate this of course. It seems unfair to them. They want the old rules that applied (when they were livestock who could get eaten at any time) instead of the new rules (which protects them under the Traditions). And they'll do everything they can to avoid making such deals and finding alternate means. That's fine too. It's expected. Drive a car instead of using the railroad. (This is why Drummond's power collapsed after the 1950s - the railroads weren't essential anymore to vampires wanting to travel.)

                                                      And smart vampires who "control" such things make sure that the cost (boons) to use them is within reason (like trivial boons for single use, or a minor boon to cover most personal travel over the long term, only getting to major if there really is some kind major effort to accommodate some special need or service) and that they must provide excellent service in exchange. So most vampires get pacified especially once they learn that this is typical, and that the vampire in question actually CAN deny them the use of these services (by various means). Paying one minor boon for access, or one or more trivial boons isn't THAT much of a cost. Of course, over time a lot of trivial and minor boons can be collected and called in.


                                                      Don't like it? Well, this is one reason why some vampires go Anarch.
