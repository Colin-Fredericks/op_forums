          If your player is being hard headed about min-maxing, then just run your game and let them find out the hard way that being versatile is the better option. Alternatively just explain that Danny Trejo's character in Desperado didn't exactly win his combat against gun toting enemies, cool though he was.

          Also who does the Player think they will be hurling knives at? A thrown knife doesn't really cause that much damage against kindred, and against mortals while it can be lethal it just leaves evidence that you have to go find after tossing. Additionally how many knives is the Player wanting to carry and what quality are they? If they are hand crafted specialty items, they should be hesitant at tossing them randomly or if they are cheap mass produced internet throwing knives, then good luck finding ones that are actually balanced. From personal experience I can tell you that most knife tips won't hold up to the.....extremes of WoD combat situations, which means within a few throws, the weapons will be useless.

          Don't get me wrong, I have played Knife/Dagger specialists in Vampire before and they were fun to play, but it really came down to close in carving or surprise tackles, rather than ranged ambushes. For a movie reference try The Hunted with Tommy Lee Jones and Benicio Del Toro. A good knife fighter in Vampire opens up their targets rather that leaving the knife in the wound. While a vampire will more than likely heal the knife wounds, the fact that half their Blood Pool is dripping onto the floor tends to complicated their ability to fight.

          Failing that, just have one of the neutral NPCS be a Gargoyle, it is an undead block of stone, go ahead and try to poke it into submission.

                                  Well part of the problem seems to be that the player doesn't seem to want to play VtM. Because the whole "a bad roll affects your character" is a fundamental component of the game.

                                  But I would actually disagree about having a wide spread being the betters strategy for a VtM character. Sure, you want a number of bases covered like enough Athletics to dodge attacks and enough Awareness to notice threats, but it's hard with the stat generation system to be so narrow-minded that you don't have anything to spare for those. But because of the nature of opposed rolls and the inconsistency of a pool, a "jack of all trades" character who has 2-4 dice in every single conceivable pool will barely ever actually succeed at anything they do. It's a far better strategy, and I would argue it also makes for a better group game, to have characters with great strengths and glaring weaknesses which are covered for by other Coterie members.
