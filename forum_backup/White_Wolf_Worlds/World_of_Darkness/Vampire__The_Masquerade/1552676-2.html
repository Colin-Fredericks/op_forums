                Almost all of the Revised era's setting changes can fit into two buckets.

                The first bucket is "I think this idea is quite stupid so I'm getting rid of it, and replacing it with my own, much better, idea."

                The second bucket is "We are actually going to run a Gehenna storyline, and we need to do things to showcase that the End Times are happening now."

                The Ravnos Week of Nightmares is in that second category. As such that event - and similar "countdown" events like the destruction of the Tremere anti-tribu - should just be ignored if you didn't want the world to end in 2004 or whenever it was. It's a storyline driven event. And if you are not telling a Gehenna story, there's no reason to include it in your game. (And personally if I was to run an actual Gehenna chronicle, I probably wouldn't use the metaplot story as laid out although I might steal a few ideas.)

                Other setting changes in Revised were not of this "countdown to Gehenna" variety. It was just because the new developers and writers didn't like certain aspects of the old setting and just got rid of it. Getting rid of the entire Tal'mahe'Ra and eliminating the Souleaters is of that nature of change. They just didn't like it as a setting element so they dumped it. This is more like a DC Comics "Earth-One" and "Earth-Two" scenario in which there is no story need for these changes. It's just an indication that they are two different settings, reusing a lot of the same names and tropes while detailing a different "world".

                        I also don't really think the "bucket" analogy holds. Too many elements of Revised are clearly written with multiple goals.

                        To move away from VtM for a moment: the Avatar Storm in Mage. The Avatar Storm served to both be an in-universe rationale for instituting major changes to the setting to help replace elements deemed negatives for the game according to the developers of the time (the Mage team - and with far less animus than many fans seem to want to acknowledge - felt late Mage 2e had gone far too epic in scope and need to be returned to a more grounded point where the huge conflicts between archwizards and fleets of voidships weren't something for normal games to get into), as well as setting off the Time of Judgement metaplot for Mage by breaking the stalemate between the Traditions and Technocracy so they and other factions would start to make their big moves.

                        I think it's easier to just think of Revised's changes as having some mixture of two overarching goals: do what the developers at the time felt was best for making the setting better, and moving the metaplot forward. While it's certainly simple to point to individual examples that are heavily biased towards one goal or the other, it doesn't seem to serve much purpose when editions aren't bound to only following one design goal at a time.

They have actual complex reasons and arguments behind it. And they don't agree or disagree with your opinions just because they're less invested in the metaplot than you are, nor are they more or less invested in the metaplot because of how much they're invested in the setting's lore in general.
