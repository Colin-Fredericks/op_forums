    Originally posted by Elphilm View Post
    ...Cutting out your own heart is just how the Heart of Darkness is flavored; the actual power is "You become immune to staking"...
    This is exactly where concept doesn't match execution, perception doesn't meet reality, and player expectation doesn't line up with outcome.

    Staking...isn't really a big deal in the big picture. Were it a viable alternative to knocking a vampire into torpor through raw damage, or simply killing them, it would be a bigger deal but it isn't. Staking an active vampire is prohibitively difficult, and staking a debilitated or sleeping vampire is easier but the cost-to-benefit ratio is out of whack. Either an aggressor was capable of debilitating a vampire, thus in all likelihood they were capable of killing it, or they did the legwork of tracking a vampire's haven down and still being able to just kill it.

    In any case, staking is only an ideal resolution to a given problem, in such a small sliver of generally plot-related circumstances, it's frankly hardly worth the thought. Realistically, a storyteller needs to build a story from the ground up, with the specific intent of a vampire ending up staked (or getting un-staked), for it to even measure on the radar of dramatic events which might happen during a given chronicle.

    That's not a great basis for a capstone discipline power.

    Least of all when other disciplines -- namely, Vicissitude -- provide an analogous (I'd argue, superior) benefit to the possessor at level 1. To others, level 2. And a more powerful benefit overall that doesn't impart a pivotal weakness to the character, level 3. And that's available to all regardless whether they have Vicissitude or have a friendly local Tzimisce to do it, care of a two-point merit. Because the heart's still in the vampire and therefore doesn't require additional resources to protect, and in the case of Vicissitude, it can be up-armored.

    This is where the Robert E. Howard, "Setites were originally written to be designated bad guys for PC's to eventually hunt/kill", shit rears its ugly head again. The discipline was purpose-built to give Setites a targetable Achilles' heel for a coterie of PC vampires to exploit, as you'd expect of any overblown, melodramatic, pulp fiction-inspired BBEG.

        Originally posted by Lynceus View Post
        I'm a little confused. You say that Serpentis has a central theme, but then point out that the 1-5 powers, the ones that are mostly used and seen in any game, make no sense. To me, that just reinforces my statement that Serpentis LACKS a central theme. It would be like if Presence was the power to manipulate emotions, but the 1-5 abilities made you better at shooting stuff because "John Woo/The Matrix/John Wick is cool". Not trying to start a fight here, just hoping you see my point.
        It's your other statements -- that the clan should have had Obtenebration from the beginning, and that the higher levels of Serpentis are stolen from other disciplines -- that I was also reacting to. The claims make little sense in the original context. I don't disagree with you that Serpentis was always a grab-bag of various ideas that don't form a coherent whole, and I even speculated idly on why that might have been. I would only phrase the criticism a little differently:

        We have the designers' own words about what the discipline was originally meant to represent -- "the concept of corruption in one way or another." It's not that Serpentis lacks a central theme, it's that only the Elder level powers successfully capture that theme. I don't know the thinking process behind the original design, but if I had to wager a guess, the designers probably viewed the snake-themed powers as manifestations of, or vehicles for, physical corruption. Beginners learn to corrupt the vampiric body in a very tangible manner, and only at higher levels do Serpentis users work toward the subtler, more spiritual corruption that used to be the raison d'être of the clan.

        A modern rewrite of the discipline could probably tease out the original intent and sharpen it up so the discipline wouldn't seem so incoherent. Such a rewrite would certainly be a more sophisticated effort than just giving Setites Protean and calling it a day.

                    Another thing to consider when talking about the original design of the Setites and Serpentis, is that the Setites were closely tied to them being servants and priests of the Wyrm. The Wyrm being one of those things that served to tie the various game lines together as part of a common metaphysics of the setting, but most closely associated with Werewolf. This usually is not stated directly in Vampire sourcebooks, but is quickly gleaned from other material where the Setites are mentioned.

                    The Wyrm being both a source of corruption and associated with serpents, lends itself to most of the original powers of Serpentis. It could be looked at as "how to be a servant to the Wyrm".

                    In this scenario, the hodgepodge powers make better sense thematically although it seems best emulated as a kind of unique path of Dark Thaumaturgy than its own Discipline. But even that can be overlooked if we assume the Wyrm was somehow involved in the creation of vampires (and even reconciled with Vampire's Noddist mythology if we just call it the Serpent of Eden) and therefore Serpentis as "inherent" and not sorcery.

                            Originally posted by Lynceus View Post
                            Indeed, the Tlacique make this very obvious, since they don't even have Serpentis, but use Protean instead. It's a bit lazy, and an approach the developers took more than once (I'm looking at you, Visceratika). It's simply that Protean is very well designed, it does what it's supposed to do, and has great utility, so why not copy it? If it ain't broke, etc.. Serpentis is really good in it's current form, although level 4 could use a buff (I simply give it the same stat points that Revised Gangrel get for their animal forms in their Clanbook, giving players the option of a small venomous snake or a larger constrictor). 5 has always been goofy, and the Tlacique solve this by making it an actual Ritual. I rather liked the DAV20 version just because it's unique, if a bit strong.

                            The weird part of Serpentis is it's always lacked a central theme. It doesn't even really make you all that snake-like (last I checked, snakes don't have feeding tongues), and the higher levels start stealing the powers of other Disciplines to reflect this. Seriously, there's one higher level Serpentis power that is basically Obtenebration (something Set should have anyways, being a "God of Darkness"). It's as if they said "Well, they should really have Obtenebration, Protean, and Sorcery, but that's way too good". Then, satisfied with a half-baked job well done, they went ahead and created the original Samedi and Kiasyd...lol.
                            There appears to be an in-universe reason for this. When we started assembling the various pieces of WoD historical lore from the editions for the "Set and Osiris" thread, they formed a very coherent story:

                            Set was originally embraced as a Gangrel by Ennoia (His oldest bloodline has Protean rather than Serpentis, he has animal features, is childer get animal features, he was embraced long after the killing of the Second Generation, and a number of other issues). Set diablerized the Antediluvian Typhon, an obtenebration-user to get to the Third Generation. Typhon took him over or is taking him over. (Set was originally a storm and warrior god, the myths about Typhon has him killed by a redheaded storm god. Set had a big personality change not long after, went from a storm and warrior god to the the snakes and corruption theme, changed his name to Typhon Set, his clan got exterminated and restarted, he wiped out all records from before that, he named his philosophy and his pet after Typhon, etc.)

                            So you originally had this Gangrel who diablerized his way to the 3rd. He redeveloped Protean into Serpentis, and during the process the original Antediluvian influences/takes him over. Typhon was the one with the snakes and corruption motif, not originally Set. So the shifts in focus for the discipline makes a lot of sense given its in-universe history.
