                    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                    It isn't a terrible thing, but it annoyed me nonetheless to have a combat you can't win just because.
                    I think the problem is that you can kill the werewolf, but not through straight up combat power. The game doesn't really make how to beat it with violence obvious, because it's clear the game is trying to push you to run instead. Killing it is actually pretty easy once you know how.

                    For me, that's a problem because it's so divergent from the rest of the game: if you want to beat the werewolf, there's only one way to do it. The game could have easily had a hard fight (or added a gimmick like the Rokea fight has) that's really just a show-off challenge for highly invested combat players to actually pull off, while most players will run and some players will figure out the stealth/speed way as another option.

                    As is, the werewolf fight in BL1 is like a game with no QTEs suddenly having a boss fight that you win not only via QTE mechanics, but QTE mechanics that don't even tell you that they're there.

                                                        Mechanics wise, the game is primarily a brawler where you punch hordes of enemies on rooftops if you're bored and in the various enemy arenas the main game goes through from your Seattle Hub area. During the brawl sections, your primary thing will be to use 4 Disciplines selected from your screen (and cause pause and switch between them) that give you powers like Mass Domination, exploding people, blood daggers, and Charm Person. Phyre can use telekinesis to pick up guns and fire them but not actually pick up the guns themselves, which is weird and cheap. However, all the discarded guns can do seemingly way more damage than they do to you.

                                                        I never really mastered dodging but didn't need to as the primary means of healing and regaining power in the game is to sink your fangs into any enemy that is stunned (zero health) and draining them. This is also an auto-kill.

                                                        Stealth plays a role in the game and you can go up to the ceiling or rooftops or hide behind things to execute enemies from behind. Except more powerful enemies will tank some of these stealth kills and alert everyone else. There's no way to stealth past large chunks of the game and you have to eliminate all of the enemies in an area. This is something a lot of fans were annoyed by.

                                                        Phyre has a huge amount of mobility, though, implied to be related to his telekinesis and you will jump up on crates, up the side of walls, and double jump over people in order to survive. He can't fly but he can glide and the fastest way to move around the city without fast travel is to go up onto a rooftop and use super-speed to jump from rooftop to rooftop like a superhero. The rooftop enemies will always be Anarchs, Thin Bloods, Ghouls, the SI, and sometimes Seattle PD. I feel people who call this game a walking simulator have seemingly missed that you're not supposed to stay on the ground level as this is where 90% of the hub area gameplay is.

                                                        You up your Disciplines by doing fetch quests for the various Primogen members who give you a sample of their blood (it doesn't blood bond you if it's from a cup). You need to get one of three different kinds of resonances, though, plus Skill Points to buy Disciplines, though. This includes angry people who either try to mug you or get into fights, people who are sad who are primarily vandals or thieves, and horny people who Phyre can seduce. Biting people in public will get the police to gun you down and probably your victim and cause a Masquerade violation. Get enough and the Nosferatu Tolly will stake Phyre from behind and send them to their final death.

                                                        The arenas are basically levels consisting of parking garages, abandoned docks, old factories, and so on. You will visit several throughout the Hub over the course of the game and there are three boss fights against fellow Kindred.

                                                            Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                                            I don't particularly care about the alias Phyre. It's stupid, which somehow detracts from the mood of the game, but nothing that terrible.

                                                            Sure, I don't think you should spin it as a good decision, either. We can argue all day and night it it's likely that an Elder would chose this stupid alias, and there's no definitive answer, but the reality is that:

                                                            This is a piece of media. Everything in it has, first and foremost, a storytelling purpose. As a piece of storytelling Phyre is a bad name. It detracts from the mood without giving anything in return. It has some potential for humor, but if the game doesn't use this potential (which is hard to do in a horror game, but not impossible), then it has no reason to be there.

                                                            Maybe it could be a sign of Phyre's personality. But as far as I can see, it isn't. It's just random (Doylist random, Whatsonian random isn't important).
                                                            My issue is different. "Phyre" as an alias serves no functionality in the game. Everyone in Seattle already knows the MC as "The Nomad". MC constantly introduces himself as "The Nomad". "Phyre" is a second name that exists just for the sake of being a second name. Hell, I think the person who uses "Phyre" the most is Fabien.
                                                            It almost feels like they got embarrassed "Phyre" and indecisively rolled back its usage, so now it's just a stump of a name.

                                                            Originally posted by Knightingale View Post

                                                            Also, Phyre is a bad alias. Another bit of bad writing.
