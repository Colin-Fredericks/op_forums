                Originally posted by Kharnov View Post
                I do think there is a significant difference between the approach of the Tzimisce, who embrace their vampirism in a more comprehensive and philosophical way, than other examples like the Gangrel, who embrace the more animalistic and predatory parts of their vampiric nature, or the Lasombra, who embrace the more authoritarian and predatory parts of their vampiric nature. They embrace particular aspects, while what makes the Tzimisce distinct is their attempt to really explore the fundamental nature of their condition, and then try and surpass its limitations. But I still don't consider that to be fundamentally Alien, because vampires do not come from a fundamentally different world compared to humanity.
                This is close to the approach I take in my chronicles. I simply don't like Necroscope and find their version of vampires incompatible to traditional kinds. So I removed the Vicissitude Discipline in my game and gave the Tzimisce Protean instead. I still wanted to depict the Tzimisce close to what they are in canon, but obviously Vicissitude is so deeply mixed with the clan's culture I had to do a lot of surgery.

                One of my solutions is that the Tzimisce see vampires as a completely different than humanity. They aren't humans with a disease or curse. The blood makes them something else, similar to how an insect shifts between its instars. But humans can't do that unless Vitae is involved. So I made that kind of belief in metamorphosis important to the clan and basis for the Path of the same name, but focusing more on the essence of Vitae and its effects and what it means rather than the use of Vicissitude. Less body horror, but more emphasis on truly alien mindset from humanity.

                                                            The difference between V:TM and W:TA

                                                            Well focusing on that, I suppose it's not just what VAMPIRE: THE MASQUERADE is but also what WEREWOLF: THE APOCALYPSE is. I've mentioned that V:TM is Gothic Punk horror fiction about sin, damnation, and remorse from your activities. It is about coping with a dreadful curse and dealing with the fact that you are now divorced from whoever you are while alive. Also, the fact that you are immortal and trapped in a feudalist exploitative super-capitalist society where the rich are literal predators on the poor.

                                                            W:TA by contrast is Tragic Heroic Mythology. Whereas vampire is Jane Eye, Wuthering Heights, and The Anarchist's Cookbook, W:TA is Beowulf, Oedipus Rex, and Last of the Mohicans. Werewolves have a lot of curse-like elements but their actual curse is the fact that they are going to lose Ragnarok and die fighting the Dragon and realize they've married their mother.

                                                            Werewolves don't have the kind of self-loathing narcissistic tragedy that vampires do and, generally, all love being werewolves (I'm simplifying). They, however, are destined to throw themselves into the jaws of death to try to save the world. In V:TM, you are the monster. In Werewolf, you are not THE monster but slay the monster. It's just THE monster is going to win.

                                                            Souleaters don't work for V:TM because, while there's nothing wrong with it, Angel: The Series is not how you're meant to run being undead. Generally, most vampires are not heroes and fighting evil. A single vampire or coterie of vampires fighting evil is fine, I've run many a game like that but they're an aberration. Most vampires do not give a shit and if they found out there was a corporation making a deal with Satan to make more money, 50% of all vampires would react with, "Okay, THAT is a hot stock tip. Where do I invest in this Pentex?" Another 25% will go, "Okay, not my problem." Another 10% will be like, "Ah ha! A rival!" With maybe 5% appropriately horrified.

                                                            Werewolf: The Apocalypse, by contrast, is a game where you need an assortment of monsters and horrifying cannon fodder because they are defined by throwing themselves into the jaws of Fenris. It doesn't matter that Formori are victims of the Wyrm, they're people to be killed because a werewolf lives for violence and dies by violence.

                                                            At least that's my view.
