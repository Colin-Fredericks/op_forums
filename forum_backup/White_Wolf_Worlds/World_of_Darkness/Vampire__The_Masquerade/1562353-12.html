                Keep in mind that having memories from a past life isn't the same as being a perfect continuation of it.

                Even with topped Remembrance and in the Fae Dreaming you're still short from being literally a resurrection, rather than a life that remembers having been many others before.

                It's in contrast to some descriptions that seem to think Changelings are just possessing spirits or something like that, jumping bodies but still retaining full consciousness and memory through the process, like how Demons actually do because they are, in fact, possessor spirits.

                    I mean to dive into the metaphysics of it more seriously for a moment, your original faerie life, as it were, is still "you" in a lot of ways, it just gets filtered through an ongoing cycle of other experiences. Your fae soul is still in there somewhere, but it's perspectives get shifted around and even transformed by new lifetimes. It doesn't mean you're a possessing spirit (barring the Arcadian way, and even there some Arcadians opted to be born into the world as babies and grow up naturally, as it were), it just means there is a certain quality of you carrying through lifetimes.

                    I see it less as Michael Moorcock's Eternal Champion, who very much are vastly different people expressing the same incarnation in ways that can have little to do with each other, and more in some cases like the Doctor, for whom there is always a certain underlying measure of "Doctor"-ness, if you will, that still flavours the different ways they end up engaging with and processing the world.

                    We see in the material that the rare person who does come into their full original selves/memories (whether by actually making it back to Arcadia, or Siochain-ing, or what have you) has something of a coming into the fullness of themselves, as it were. It's not the equivalent of reading a book about someone else's life.

                        What are the metaphysics of reincarnation if not an essential "you" continuing through lives while being filtered by new experiences?

                        This filtering, though, is still an indelible reality, and in Changeling it is extremely important due to the theme of balancing mundane needs with living fantasy. To ignore your current life in its particular expression, relationships, obligations and roles is to neglect a part of you and court with Bedlam.

                        Here I have to counter your description also on grounds of diversity. Changelings come in all levels of Remembrance and relationships with this past. You're insisting in a reading that exist in the game, but is far from a rule. I don't think either the Eternal Champion or the Doctor are good representations of reincarnation as they are quite particular takes for quite particular stories, and both use premises uncommon for stories or beliefs about reincarnation.

                        Changeling is open ended about this. No one is wrong in playing their character very differently from what you're describing, just as you're not wrong in playing like that. A Changeling that is very different each life is valid, as is one that even upon remembering rejects the person they were previously. One is almost exactly the same each life even without memories, other is reinventing themselves every time based more on the person they grew to become rather than the fairy they were hundreds of years ago.

                        For every Time Lord Changeling there is also an Eternal Champion, yet the vast majority is anything in between and beyond.

                                                Yeah, in some ways, the fae soul is recycled like an Avatar. It carries memories and maybe even personality, but it's only ever a component in the final being (except in the case of Arcadian sidhe). It merges with a human soul, but the imbalance of Glamour and Banality means the human soul tends to win out more often (and wins for good in the end, if the changeling survives long enough to finally be Undone).

                                                As for demons, I like the idea that the world(s) they built might no longer exist, or have evolved in their absence, and so they're emerging into a world that often seems vastly different (but with just enough details that they are convinced it's the same world). Re-reading Revelations of the Dark Mother recently, this seems even more likely, given that the book seems to seed ideas picked up on in later books.

                                                E.g., Yahweh and Lucifer are both described as Elohim/Shining Ones, and as "brothers". This implies the different Godheads are all manifestations of the One (also called the Ancient One or the greater YHVH in the book). The seraphim, cherubim and "daemoni" are said to be some of Yahweh's servants, although it's also fair to assume his siblings have similar servants of their own. As Yahweh's brother, Lucifer is his peer, so stands above such angels (though he's still below Yahweh, who is the "Firstborn").

                                                Each Shining One (and we're given the names of various deities here) creates their own garden, and after Lilith gives the fruit to Eve (who gives it to Adam), they all have a terrible argument (maybe a war?) that rains down stones from the sky (fallen angels?). When the Shining Ones finally leave to retire to their own worlds, they leave three angels behind to protect humans on Earth (possibly the Ministers/Aralu/surviving trio of angels from DTF).
