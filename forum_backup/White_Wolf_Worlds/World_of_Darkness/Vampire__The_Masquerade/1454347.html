    Please help me flesh out a houserule I'm considering.

    07-06-2021, 09:31 AM
    I don't like that there doesn't seem to be much innate difference between the clans beyond their discipline spread. So I'm considering giving each clan two skills that they can develop for half price, rounded up. Or maybe a flat -2xp cost per dot. I'll have to playtest it a bit.

    So far I've come up with:
    Brujah: Intimidation and Streetwise

    Gangrel: Brawl and Survival

    Nosferatu: Stealth and Investigation

    Toreador: Performance and Persuasion

    Tremere: Occult and Awareness

    Ventrue: Leadership and Politics

    Past that I'm kind of at a loss.

                      My suggestion is to develop clan differences using the city/setting in which you are going to play. Not an "always true" rule but a situational set of advantages (and eventually disadvantages).

                      E.g.: due to the fact that one of the Brujah faction in the city is well connected in the union, all the Brujah have a Contact rating +1 if its reasonable that the are using this peculiar connection (and this could shape differences between Brujah factions, one with the union one with the street thug and so on). Or due to the well established ruling of the Toreador prince, all the Toreador under his wings gain +1 to Resource, thanks to this indirect patron relationship. I'd put no more than 2 of this clan differences/faction, otherwise the players could miss the additional setting layer. It should be a way to enforce the theme of a faction, not to give only an advantage.

                      So no hard-homeruling for clan blood but instead thanks to the setting.

                              Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post

                              I don't like that at all.

                              I think each of the clans should have some innate advantages beyond simply having access to their disciplines. It's part of the lore that, for example, Gangrel are greatly feared in combat, when their disciplines alone simply don't justify it. Each of the clans should have a little something more to justify why they are feared in their main role.
                              The key thing to remember and balance around, is that clans aren't classes. They're loose archetypes that synergize with nature and nurture, given how each clan also has preferences for what sort of mortal they embrace, and how they train fledglings.

                              Gangrel aren't feared in combat because they can land a solid hit or take one. They're feared in combat because they're brutal survivalists who never fight fair, use any and every tool at their disposal to win, and their discipline spread is perfectly suited for survival and asymmetric fighting on a level few if any vampires can compete. "Fairness" and "honor" are for gathers, and even then it's on Gangrels' terms, as applied to other Gangrel and other Gangrel alone. Brujah may be fantastic at the smashy-smash, but they don't hold a candle to the arsenal a Gangrel can bring to bear when it comes to guerilla (or gorilla) warfare.

                              Or to put it another way, if you think Arms of Ahriman is overpowered due to its implications on action economy, try a Gangrel with a pack of animal ghouls that have been taught Celerity. Sure, Arms of Ahriman can be replaced by reusing Obtenebration 2 if they go down in combat, but Arms of Ahriman can't scout, convey messages, spy, or be possessed allowing their master to be active in daytime or with minimal risk to themselves, can they.

                              And likewise, off-archetype play can be incredibly fun and powerful, and any house rule needs to thread the needle of not disrupting potential for off-type play or discouraging players from exploration. Again, clans aren't classes, and embrace preference/natural synergy with discipline spread only goes so far. To go with the earlier example, one of the nastier character concepts I ever put in a player's hands -- when one of my players in TC couldn't decide what they wanted to play -- was a Toreador with ECD (Animalism). They didn't realize what I'd handed them, but a couple other players at the table, who were vet players as well and played with me long enough to know my predilection towards Animalism, Auspex, and Presence, immediately recognized the implications of the character and were completely taken aback I'd actively suggest it at the table.

                              That player figured it out pretty damn fast as soon as they figured out they could move more or less freely in action scenes with Celerity, dread gazing at will and allowing their animal ghouls to pin down and tear opponents to shreds.
