    V5 Discipline debate

    01-07-2024, 05:52 PM
    I'm primarily a lurker on these boards, so I didn't necessarily expect to start any threads on them ever. I definitely didn't expect my first thread to be one dedicated to porting a discussion from a different thread into its own space, but under the circumstances separating them seemed worthwhile. In all likelihood, there are a fair number of people involved in the Ministry Discipline thread who don't particularly care about the back-and-forth between Theodrim and myself within it, and the more we clog that thread up with it, the more of a nuisance we're likely to be to them.

    This conversation started over in another thread about preferences on the Setites/The Ministry across editions, or more specifically about Serpentis in previous editions vs their use of Protean in V5. Increasingly, related but distinct features of V5 Disciplines in general became the focus of this conversation, along with connected subjects such as how to effectively 'translate' prior edition power sets into the new edition, the part Disciplines play both thematically and mechanically in establishing clan roles and enabling or constraining the expression of character/clan identities, and whether V5 or preceding editions handle Discipline-related matters 'better'. At this point, I think the Serpentis vs Protean comparisons that were the start of this discussion are almost incidental to where it is now, and this strikes me as another good reason to move it.

    For the benefit of anyone who is interested in these subjects and/or the previous discussion but isn't entirely clear on where this discussion currently stands/is about in more concrete terms, and I suppose to organize my own thoughts a bit, I'll take a moment to categorize and summarize its various topics in the following three posts; I'm not sure that splitting them up this way actually helps, but the single post preview with all of them looked ridiculous to me. I'll start with some background information that may give perspective and then mark the starting point for the recap of the arguments themselves with double asterisks for people who are familiar with this information already. Feel free to pipe in if you think I've missed anything important/interesting and/or misrepresented anything in my overviews.

      I. V5 Disciplines: Progressions and costs

      The way that V5 approaches Disciplines is one of the biggest changes from the previous editions. V5 collapses the 30 plus Disciplines that the older editions features into a core set of 11 Disciplines that, aside from one exception that I can think of, always have multiple Discipline power options available for each level: with your first dot in Protean, for example, you have the choice between Eyes of the Beast, a power that lets you see in both mundane and supernatural darkness while also granting a bonus on Intimidation rolls against mortals, and Weight of the Feather, which protects from damage caused by falls and collisions while also potentially allowing you to avoid activating pressure-based devices like alarms or mines if the Storyteller decides that it should. You'll also find amalgam Disciplines that require dots in multiple Disciplines as part of the general power sets, and these are often (but not always) the form that this edition's versions of the near-exclusive clan Discipline powers from older editions take.

      For a good example of how V5 translates older signature powers into both amalgams and normal Discipline powers, let's take a closer look at Dementation. In order, its five starting powers in older editions are: Passion, The Haunting, Eyes of Chaos, Voice of Madness, and Total Insanity. Eyes of Chaos doesn't really exist in a singular form anymore: seeing the true Nature (as in Nature and Demeanor from prior editions) of others is covered by Scry the Soul from Auspex, foreshadowing future events is covered by Premonition from Auspex, and tracking patterns/impressions from objects is covered by The Spirit's Touch from Auspex. In V5, Dominate features an amalgam power requiring Obfuscate ••: Dementation. It is described as basically talking a victim into a nervous breakdown or psychotic break, the specifics of which are based on the victim's personality and emotional state/current Resonance, but tend to take the mechanical form of Willpower damage and behavioral Compulsions, which in V5 basically translate to "do this or take a (usually -2) penalty". As you may already be aware, hallucinations/delusions and disturbed/confused thoughts and emotions are noted symptoms of psychotic breaks, so conceptually I'd say that the Dementation amalgam covers the same ground as Passion, The Haunting, and Voice of Madness; how well it works as a functional equivalent is a topic better suited for section III (though I won't get into it there either).

      But let's circle back to more general characteristics of Disciplines: your choice in Discipline powers is limited to one power per dot you gain in that Discipline, and your options include lower-level powers that you previously passed on for whatever reason. For example, at Presence •••, you could elect to gain a second level one Presence power or a level two Presence power instead of a level three one. The experience cost for Discipline dots in V5 increases the more you have just like in older editions: with V5, the only difference is that the cost is slightly higher because it multiplies the new level that you gain instead of the level at the time of the purchase. Regardless of their native levels, Discipline powers often have scaling characteristics: examples of this include the bonuses rising with the number of dots that you have in the Discipline and dice pools for Discipline powers increase by virtue of the fact that they frequently take the form of Attribute + Discipline. Since these scaling effects are based on the number of dots that you have in a Discipline, they are the same for any given power no matter what level you choose to purchase it at provided that as the Discipline's rating remains constant: getting Daunt at level one, for example, would still lead to it providing three bonus dice to Intimidation rolls at Presence ••• in the same way that getting it as your power of choice for Presence ••• would. Naturally, Daunt would have cost you 5-7 experience points at level one, as opposed to 15, 18, or 21 experience points at level three.

      They're rare, but there are also times when you may actually have to purchase a lower level power as you progress in a Discipline since higher level powers may have lower level powers that you didn't choose as prerequisites (or amalgam requirements like I mentioned above); the two Protean •••• options, Horrid Form and Metamorphosis, are examples of this.

      **For my part, regardless of whether you want to purchase a lower level power at a higher level or you have to, the fact that doing so can cost you twice as it would have earlier on, if not more, does not strike me as a good design choice.

      There are two reasons that Theodrim provides for why this isn't actually an issue that I want to discuss here: 1) the way that Disciplines scale means you're never 'just' getting a lower level power when you choose it at higher levels, and 2) some Disciplines are designed in a way where the native choices for certain levels can more comfortably be passed over.

      I do agree that scaling effects described here could reasonably factor into the value of any dot you purchase in a Discipline, but I don't agree that this tendency for various Discipline effects to scale is enough to balance things out to the point that cost disparities involved in getting a lower level power earlier as opposed to later don't matter. I have three main reasons for this: 1) a significant component of scaling benefits from Disciplines will apply regardless of what powers you choose, 2) it is often the case that scaled effects of lower level powers still won't match up to the impacts of higher level powers, and 3) there are, in fact, an assortment of powers that don't actually scale in any way.

      As far as reason #1 goes, the component I'm referring to is dice pool increases that come from adding dots to a given Discipline: taking Daunt for Presence ••• definitely does improve your dice pools for all Presence powers that roll Presence in them, but it's also true that taking Dread Gaze, a level three Presence power, provides this same benefit. This may well be the most important scaling effect of all for many builds since it can boost multiple powers, and it still applies even if you never choose a lower level power over a higher level.

      The respective effects of the powers themselves are still a significant point of difference, though, and the Daunt vs Dread Gaze provides a useful framework for discussing reason #2. At Presence •••, Daunt adds three bonus dice to Intimidation roll, like I mentioned above, while Dread Gaze can prevent mortals from acting even on a failure, send mortals running and keep vampires from acting during a turn (where the baseline assumption for combat in this edition is that it shouldn't last more three turns per character) with a success, or render mortals catatonic with fear/make vampires succumb to terror frenzies. Theodrim is right that Dread Gaze comes with costs and drawbacks of its own. It requires a Rouse Check to use, potentially leaving you hungrier and less in control if you fail, it can be a Masquerade risk, and hell, I throw in one more: it could end up scaring people way more than you actually want to. Even so, I'd also consider what a power uniquely brings to the table as an important factor in gauging its potential impact: with Dread Gaze on the table, making people lose their **** from fear is introduced as a baseline expectation when it makes sense to use it, and this is not in any way guaranteed through the successful use of Intimidation alone. Compare this to Daunt as a choice for Presence •••: the meat of it here is the bonus to Intimidation, which can be replicated more cheaply in a variety of ways. Free substitute options include leveraging teamwork bonuses, spending Willpower for rerolls, and/or making Rouse Checks for bonuses, while non-free but less expensive options include buying dots in Intimidation itself, buying Specialties for the Skill, buying Merits or Loresheet Advantages that can also improve Intimidation, and purchasing lower level powers for other Disciplines that also boost Intimidation, such as Eyes of the Beast or Shadow Cloak, while you can still get them on the cheap. I don't intend to suggest that Daunt is useless by any means - I actually think it's pretty good for what it is (which is not a level 3 Discipline power) - or that these alternatives are perfect replacements for Daunt's benefits, but I am saying that they can cover enough ground that it's hard to justify Daunt as an equally sound investment for Presence ••• if you want to scare people. I would be remiss to not acknowledge that Daunt stacks with these alternatives as well, but I don't think this significantly changes the equation. Experience points are a particularly precious resource in V5 given how slowly they will accrue by default (1 per session), so it will typically make more sense to settle for being merely 'good' at Intimidation to keep room for other options rather than sacrifice these options so that you can be 'absolutely amazing' at Intimidation.

      But let's say whoever is reading this is still not convinced that I've made an effective case for Dread Gaze over Daunt. Fair enough; maybe I'm wrong with this example. There are plenty of other power choices to consider: is getting Draught of Might, which lets you temporarily bestow two dots of Potence on someone else, for Potence ••••• as good as getting Fist of Caine, which lets you deal aggravated damage with Brawl/Melee attacks for a full scene in an edition where that pretty much isn't a thing otherwise? Or how about getting Weight of the Feather instead of Shape Change or Metamorphosis, which along with its general utility may also let you avoid taking damage from falls by flying, depending on your choice of animal form? Would Cat's Grace, which lets you auto-succeed on rolls to keep your balance, make sense to purchase over Fleetness, which adds Celerity to Dexterity rolls out of combat and occasionally to Dexterity rolls for defense, when their cost is equal? So long as there is a reasonable basis for expecting more impactful effects/options from higher level powers as a general rule, then Theodrim's second point about the power options at certain levels for certain Disciplines making the rising costs for lower level powers easier to swallow just introduces a different problem: it doesn't raise the actual value of lower level powers, it just drags particular higher level powers closer down to their level.

      Still, a given reader's answer might be 'yes' to all of the questions I asked above and to any other lower vs higher level power comparison questions that I could come up with besides. Even then, scaling can only reasonably be counted as a mitigating factor for increasing experience costs for these lower level Discipline powers when one's chosen Discipline powers scale at all, which brings us to reason #3. As far as non-scaling Discipline powers go, this includes lots of Celerity powers and every Protean power from the core book, along with various others I listed in the other thread. Any calculations that assume scaling will pick the slack will go out the window if most or all of one's set of Discipline powers don't scale, and even one or two would throw off the balance as the level differences, and thus experience cost differences, grow more pronounced. In response to this, Theodrim asked me if non-scaling powers need to scale in order for them to be good: they don't necessarily (even if I think that a lack of any scaling benefits whatsoever will probably further limit their ability to punch above their weight class), but that does nothing to change the simple fact that scaling has to occur for it to balance out high vs low level Discipline power costs.

          II. V5 Disciplines, Identity, and Interactions

          Like I mentioned earlier, the number of Disciplines in V5 has been drastically cut down, both to eliminate redundancies and to put some more distance between more niche clans and their representative Disciplines/powers (e.g., the Lasombra and Obtenebration, the Ravnos and Chimerstry, the Tzimisce and Vicissitude, etc.) to open up space for them to develop and express their themes in other ways. Even with Disciplines that are shared by many clans, an expanded range of individual options per level can still produce meaningful differences in how these Disciplines, and the clans that share them, function both through the individual powers themselves and their potential to interact with other powers in interesting/novel ways. Naturally, this also applies to potential variations between individual Kindred of the same or different clans.

          **I think that's basically all the background needed for this section, and there's less to pick apart and sort through on my disagreements with Theodrim here. Our difference of opinion mainly concerns the part that clan amalgams, which I'll use as a shorthand for the sorts of amalgam-based ports of signature powers from prior editions that I described in the background info for part I, play in all this. We both agree that exclusive (or near-exclusive) access to specific Disciplines and powers runs counter to fundamental design goals for this edition, but for my part, I argue that clan affinities and blind spots in the form of having an easier or more difficult time of accessing particular powers, especially clan amalgams, remains an important port in exploring clan identities. If there's a particular power, or set of powers, that is supposed to reflect a special niche for a given clan that distinguishes it from others, then making these clans work harder to attain the power(s) helps reinforce the identities of both the clan that claims this niche and the ones that don't. I don't think that any one power or set of powers is the be-all and end-all for clan identity, but that doesn't mean that powers, and what the ease or difficulty with which the various clans attain such powers says about them lore-wise or identity-wise in turn, should be dismissed as unimportant.

          It can be worthwhile to discuss whether a given clan can or should claim a particular niche as their thematic 'turf', and maybe it's not so much that Theodrim doesn't think this easy vs. hard access distinction for powers isn't worth maintaining so much as certain niches shouldn't be the privileged territory of one clan because of thematic overlap with other clans: the example we discussed is whether opening up the Ravnos clan amalgams of Chimerstry and Fata Morgana to Setites/The Ministry is a good or bad thing on balance, and the matter of thematic overlap is of particular significance here. I agree that it's interesting and fitting for Ministers/Setites to add illusions to their arsenal as corruptors, but what it takes away from the Ravnos tempers my enthusiasm because there's no real balance or reciprocity in the exchange.

          At least according to me. Theodrim has also argued that what access to Obfuscate and Presence opens up to them more than makes up for any drawbacks from adding Chimerstry to the Setite/Ministry wheelhouse. As far as clan identity goes, I don't quite agree: it's an expansion of common options at the cost of unique advantages, and I consider that a wash here. It's also arguable whether there is much of an addition here at all. They were always going have three Disciplines and Chimerstry was always going to function in part through Obfuscate in this edition: tying it to Presence rather than Fortitude does less for the clan's trickster/daredevil schtick when you consider that Fortitude also has options to resist mind control and conceal motives in this edition. Obfuscate + Fortitude for Chimerstry and Fata Morgana has the advantage of allowing for expanded general options while retaining advantaged clan access to both, but if I absolutely had to choose between them, I would probably be more inclined to find in favor of the expansion of common options if there were more of these options. Alas, there aren't: even at this stage in V5's development it's not uncommon to find two Discipline powers to a given level, and even the exceptions come with the proviso that they're often clan amalgams themselves with the added costs that this usually entails. This isn't me disagreeing with earlier me that higher clan amalgam costs are a good thing for clan identity; it's just me saying that these costs have to be considered when evaluating how many opportunities are really opened up here.

              III. V5 Disciplines vs Legacy Disciplines

              The argument is the background for this section, so no asterisks here.

              I'm not sure how much of this discussion is really conducive to constructive discussion given the ease with which cross-edition comparisons like this can devolve into edition warring, but in the interests of discouraging the latter I'll focus less on the bits about how much the Ventrue and Setites/Ministry's old Discipline constraints purportedly made them suck at living up to their archetypes and lore in past editions and more on the general contention that V5 provides more options in terms of Discipline powers and more opportunities to establish or introduce twists on player/clan identities in more novel and varied ways as a result. Having more options for Discipline powers per level might make this seem self-evident, but it's not actually as clear-cut as that. Theodrim admits that there's a Merit in past editions to add to one's existing clan Discipline spread, much like certain Loresheets allow you to in V5, and the requirements for learning out-of-clan Disciplines in V20 are no more onerous than in V5. It's probably quite a bit less onerous to invest in out-of-clan Disciplines in earlier editions since Disciplines themselves cost less per dot and the default arrangement was more experience per session and per story.

              Of course, this does nothing to address the argument that V5 offers more options per Discipline, but there are two key counters to this: the way Disciplines open up at six dots and above, and the existence of combination Disciplines. I mention 6+ dot Discipline powers more to acknowledge that they exist and contradict the claim rather than to suggest that I expect players normally get them; combination Disciplines are more important here they are available in addition to rather in place of the standard Discipline powers, in marked contrast to how V5 approaches Discipline amalgams. There's also the fact that some things that are now Discipline powers in V5 used to be Merits in prior editions: Cat's Grace being a 1-point Merit called Catlike Balance and much of the scope of V5 the Vicissitude amalgam being covered by the Vicissitude Modifications Merit in earlier editions are examples that come to mind. Also, it is noteworthy some V5 powers themselves are separated components of what individual powers in previous editions could do, like the earlier example I provided of what the functional components of Eyes of Chaos map to in V5 (I don't intend this as a particular criticism of V5 since I tend to agree that Dementation itself is largely of a muddle of tricks from other Disciplines that require less ST fiat to make work in play); similarly, Shape of the Beast has been broken up into Shapechange and Metamorphosis for Protean ••• and •••• respectively in V5. On top of that, there's the fact that certain older 1-5 dots Discipline powers no longer exist at all in V5, such as Astral/Psychic Projection, pretty much anything from Valeren aside from Corpore Sano, and various Thaumaturgy/Necromancy Path powers; the extent to which their absence is a good or bad thing will vary according to taste, but they are quite clearly options that no longer exist in V5.

              I'm not entirely sure where I land on the issue of which Discipline setup provides more opportunities for unique characterization, but it's definitely not a settled manner in favor of V5.

                      Ragged Robin I was going to ask about the third when I remembered you said 'groups', not clans, and Thin-blood Alchemy popped into my head. Good catch.

                      It's probably verging on a cliche at this point, but I prefer the way Requiem 2e approaches Disciplines. That is, the majority of the time particular bloodline or other powers will still be expressed as Devotions (which are similar to combination Disciplines and Discipline amalgams but can also include unusual powers from a single Discipline) with ties to one or more of the ten 'standard' Disciplines, or powers/rituals tied to Covenant Disciplines like Crúac or Theban Sorcery, but they aren't so committed to this setup that there's no room whatsoever for new Disciplines to be introduced when they make sense.
