    V5 armour is useless?

    04-24-2020, 04:17 PM
    Now, prior editions had plenty of problems with armour. In some ways better than reality (Thick clothes are wonderful) and in other ways far worse (A kevlar vest protects for 3 but a common small pistol starts at 4 damage? The best armour protects for only 5 or 6?)

    I can see why it's like that, even if I don't like the reasoning I can see why it's done, and players still have incentive to obtain armour (providing they're willing to take on those ludicrus dex penalties)


    But in V5
    Armour is functionally paper for vampires. Ok, Maybe it works to take the kick out of incindiary rounds, but a plate carrier filled with steel becomes a cheap fabric shirt so long as it's donned by a vampire. Don't argue that vampires don't need armour because they're tough and undead. That's like saying a castle shouldn't benefit from walls if it's on a hill. It just seems rather unfair and breaks a lot of versmillitude for me. I was wondering if people house rule V5 armour heavily or if you're happy with these 'cinematic' rules.

          Eh. Armor in this edition is more like how armor functions, by and large, in real circles; it changes traumatic damage into bruises and scrapes, because the armor absorbs the impact. Bulletproof vests turn pierced organis into massive (but not often life-threatening) bruising; plate male deflects the stabs of a spear or slashes of a sword, but leaves bruising and cracked bones.

          Vampires don't take traumatic, life-threatening injury from things like guns and swords, so it has no real use for them. It would effect a Bite attack though (and downgrade appropriately assuming an armored neck) and other things that the Armor would help protect against like reducing the damage from an incendiary round (as it prevents the flames from getting to the burnable part of the vampire, mostly). A vampire wearing plate mail and hit by a battle axe is going to still get the force multiplier behind the blow, just like a human will, but the axe won't cut through (to use a random example). Conversely, a vampire not wearing armor isn't going to care that the axe just cut his spleen in two, but that human is going to take heavy damage, bleeding and shock from the traumatic damage of getting an axe to the side.

          It's fine within the balance of V5 because V5's system is not the exact same nuts and bolts and prior editions. House ruling it can easily skew the system in unintended ways though; house ruling it to be automatic damage reduction will break down the system, numerically, especially when you get into any characters with Fortitude and Armor (which become functionally invincible unless you always have damage do 1 regardless of reduction). Adding dice modifiers will skew things in the same way that lots of traits higher than 5 skew, with hypercompetency in dodging (and then it becomes another type of functional invincibility).

                          What?

                          Unless you're talking about trying to shoot small arms at vehicular armor, this just isn't the case. Most modern personal armor is built around materials that degrade with each impact. Every time a Kelvar vest stops a bullet, it becomes less effective against the next one. Adding in trauma plates extends things, but those are designed to deform/break so you don't and can't take that many hits before becoming ineffective.

                          What you said is the exact opposite of how armor works in reality.

                          RL armor isn't RPG chance-of-dice armor...
                          Sure it is as much as anything else is. Armor doesn't cover 100% of your body, so there's always a chance a bullet strikes an unprotected spot or whatnot.

                          The big way that armor in VtM (all of them, even V5) is unrealistic is that armor vs. type of weapon doesn't matter. A standard Kevlar vest might save your life from a bullet, but unless you've added some stab plates to it, a knife will cut through it far more easily than a bullet will.

                          I amusingly used convert-to-bashing armor as an armor type in my custom oWoD armor writeup because of this, way before V5 got even released.
                          In pre-V5, this wouldn't make armor meaningless like V5's implementation does though. Having armor convert a sword's damage to bashing instead of lethal is still a great bonus for a vampire (probably better than a few soak dice really) since vampires soak bashing way better than they soak lethal, and don't automatically take bashing damage already from a sword.

                          So, in a way the new rules are more realistic, and it makes sense why vampires hardly need the armor in the first place.
                          The problem is that vampires still need bones to move around. If you break a vampire's arm, the vampire can't attack as effectively. Having armor to reduce how much force is imparted to the vampire should help them avoid injuries that can impeded them.

                          Vampires might not "need" armor, but there's no logical reason why armor wouldn't help them in a serious fight.
