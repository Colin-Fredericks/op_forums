    Originally posted by Black Fox View Post

    As I was rereading some sections of Children of the Inquisition for the other thread on Dracula's diablerie of Tabak, I noticed something that I thought was quite relevant to this thread which I remembered.

    In chapter on Dracula (page 18), it provides this bit of information concerning the Inconnu that I cannot recall being mentioned anywhere else:



    Does anybody recall similar language elsewhere? Reading it, it changes how I think about the Inconnu.

    Working to cure vampires of their need to blood doesn't exactly sound like Golconda. Although achieving Golconda can be one aspect of that search, it is definitely not the same thing as vampire never needing blood again. So that would greatly expand the Inconnu from a group of elders searching for the secrets of Golconda (which I and I think many other players merely see them as doing), and into a society of elders wanting to exploring the general nature of the Kindred existence and seeking to eliminate negative aspects of their curse. That includes reducing or eliminating their need to drink blood, but also maybe eliminating the danger from sunlight, preventing themselves falling into torpor, removing the need to drink vampire vitae later on as they age, and lots of other things as well. It could also mean information that allows them to return to being a mortal.

    It doesn't mean all Inconnu members want them exact same thing, but it does mean all of them want to better understand their vampiric condition and looking at a way to improve it.

    That transforms the sect from being mostly passive, into a more active one investigating a lot of mysteries, experimenting, contacting dangerous spirits in the search for answers, etc. That might also mean active investigation into the whereabouts of Caine and the Antedeluvians, who could know a lot more about the nature of vampirism, not to destroy them, but to learn from them. So they are doing lots of things as well. That could explain why they have Monitors in cities. Not only does that allow them to observe potential recruits, it is not a passive monitoring. They are looking for clues that could help them and their sect for the specific knowledge that helps them. And lots of things might potentially be interesting to them - Tremere experiments with blood, the activities of the Salubri, Noddist scholars uncovering new information, and lots more.

    I think I will be revising my understanding of the Inconnu for my chronicles based on this. I might even begin including them in my chronicles in some capacity.
    This is a very interesting way to look at the Inconnu. Their preoccupations can be myriad and idiosyncratic, but they are united by their desire to "solve" vampirism, as a general goal. What "solving vampirism" means depends on the Inconnu member, which lends them to a number of different character and narrative dynamics, and many plot hooks.

    It feeds in my conception of the sect as being of vampires who seek, above all, an "Alternate Win Condition" for the Jyhad, to use a term from games like Magic: The Gathering. Trying to struggle with other vampires for power, influence, and pride is a sucker's game. In trying to dominate your fellow lick, you get dominated (or Dominated) in turn, usually by older, more powerful Kindred. The need to secure blood, evade vampiric banes, and maintain a base of power and safety all make a Kindred vulnerable. Trying to "win" the Jyhad through accumulating power, and eliminating or subborning all rivals, is impossible for all but the Antedeluvians (if even they can "win").

    Finding ways to circumvent or transcend the vampiric condition - to push beyond the boundaries of one's nature - is itself a means of circumventing or transcending the Jyhad. You need never struggle with your peers for feeding rights, if you no longer require blood. Etc, etc. Whether this is through Golconda - whatever that is - or through some more esoteric function.

    It's also for this reason why, though their goals may differ, the Inconnu generally don't war against each other. Getting into slap-fights with other Kindred is, to the Inconnu, a waste of time and effort. It's why the excused themselves from the Jyhad at large. Conflicts may still break out, but it's the exception rather than the norm. The result of Inconnu members judging the means or ends of another to be so unacceptable that intercession, or even violence, is necessary.

    The Inconnu gather lore and keep it secret for twin reasons. First because any scrap of data could be calamitous (or at least inconvenient) if it falls into the wrong hands. Whether it's those outside the Inconnu, or rival factions within it. Second, that same scrap of data could be the key to unraveling the mysteries of vampirism. The lynchpin of some Inconnu member's chosen "win con". And so hoarding information and keeping it dispersed is an important function of the sect, as is gathering more of it. But Inconnu must also pool information at times, as they pool resources, to further their ends.

    So the Inconnu, in practice, is a mix of hierarchical and cellular. Members using agents to monitor the Jyhad for answers and as go-betweens with other Inconnu. They don't get involved in wars where they can help it, because they seek "victory" through end-running vampirism itself. But any given Inconnu is not necessarily "good" in the traditional sense, just because they mind their own business as a general rule. The form of transcendence they seek may not be pleasant, and the means to get there may be even less so. So they can easily be slotted into an antagonist role, albeit one the Coterie is unlikely to encounter until well into the chronicle.

    Magestuck: The Ascension

                            That should make them very invested in the Children of Osiris, Bardo seems like a tailor-made Discipline for them. Children of the Inquisition is also 1e era, right? I don't remember if Hunters Hunted came before or after, but I wonder if the two are related in some way.

                            I think the main sticking point for Golconda seekers, vis a vis Bardo, is that it's not really a "win condition" to use my earlier terminology. Bardo allows you to curb the worst aspects of the vampiric condition, but only through great personal effort and restraint, and a complete dedication to a certain unlifestyle. If you follow the path laid by the Children of Osiris, you're signing up for an eternity of aestheticism. All with the knowledge that you're only managing the curse, not overcoming it.

                            It's not a bad gig, if you can get it. There are worse ways to spend eternity, and ones far less moral. But not to the taste of someone who wants to exit the vampirism game entirely (while still sticking around in the world, of course; catching a sunrise is always an option if you're REALLY sick of being a vampire).

                            To someone of the Inconnu, Bardo can be a useful tool to pursue, but it's ultimately a stopgap. Maintaining high Humanity has its benefits and lets you sleep more easily at day, but you're not "winning" the Jyhad or escaping the curse. Golconda promises true release, though what that means is vague and poorly understood.

                            The difference between Bardo and Golconda is like the difference between living an aesthetic lifestyle to limit the bad karma you do, and achieving Enlightenment and escaping Samsara. While the former is admirable, from a Buddhist standpoint, you're really aiming for the latter.

                                    I agree that Bardo (and the Children of Osirises paradigm as ascetics) would not be seen as a ultimate goal, but rather a possible venue. Which is an interesting point, because individual members of the Inconnu then could have vastly different opinions while sharing the same goal.                                                     Originally posted by Lysander View Post
                                                    Quick question. Was this Children of Osirsis bloodline ever a major faction in the older settings of Vampire The Masquerade or a poorly defined idea that never got fleshed out?
                                                    There were very few mentions of the Children of Osiris in the sourcebooks. They were mentioned early in the original Hunters Hunted. They were actually never referred to as a bloodline (and their members in fact, do not embrace new childer), but because they were given a unique Discipline, Bardo, people began referring to them as a Bloodline, and that was soon how they were mentioned in subsequent sourcebooks. But they are more like a (monastic) sect.

                                                    Since they were mentioned as one of the Hunter groups, it seems to me they were meant to be antagonists in the setting. Unlike even the Salubri, mentioned in the Players Guide, they weren't meant as a PC option, but a tool for the ST.

                                                    The Children of Osiris would continue to be mentioned in a few subsequent sourcebooks, but not too much. I think outside of Hunters Hunted they got the most mentioned in the Kemintiri chapter of Kindred Most Wanted for 1e/2e. So except for those people who managed to buy the original Hunters Hunted, I think most VtM players only heard about them from third parties. Hunters Hunted was an early sourcebook, and I don't remember new copies arriving in game stores after the run sold out. It was a very early product and didn't look as professional as the 2e products. This was pre-PDF days, pre-Internet stores, so unless you picked up a used copy at some used book store or game store, you had very little information on them to go by.

                                                    Because they were seen as "good guys" vampires, they got purged out of existence in the Revised era (along with a lot of other things).

                                                    If you want a rundown on the Children of Osiris, they were discussed on a thread, often comparing them to the Salubri, some years ago. The thread ended up being very useful to me, so I'll share it here.

                                                            While I firmly believe that vampires are monsters and should all be destroyed, there are many examples in the genre of reluctant vampires who try to hold onto their humanity and avoid evil acts. Barnabas Collins from Dark Shadows TV show, Hannibal King in Marvel's Tomb of Dracula comic book, Louis from Anne Rice's novels, Nick Knight from Forever Knight. I think even Marie from Innocent Blood and Caleb & Mae from Near Dark qualify. So I think it's OK to have them in the setting. Just make it clear these are very rare exceptions, and over time their numbers dwindle.

                                                            So I don't even mind if there are maybe two or three dozen or so vampires who exist in some kind of monastic order trying to limit the harm they do. The Children of Osiris are probably not a setting element that should appear in every chronicle or without reason. But it can make sense for them to make an actual presence in certain chronicles if done correctly.

                                                            But the Revised era was less about providing tools for individual STs to use in their own chronicles, and more about trying to force everyone to play in a specific chronicle designed by one person (or a small group of people working together). No matter how much fun some people might think that one chronicle was, it was a mutilating limitation to the game. If players didn't want the Children of Osiris, or the Tal'Mahe'Ra or any other element in the setting, they already had the power to simply not mention them or even erase them in their own chronicles.
