    Your favorite and least favorite V:TM cities

    11-10-2025, 08:55 PM
    Basically, what the title says.

    The city sourcebooks were something of a poisoned chalice in terms of V:TM's development. Chicago by Night helped illustrate how the game world functioned and gave a huge boost to shared terminology, titles, and archetypes (Lodin seems like he's the basis for a lot of Ventrue princes, IMHO)

    However, the game books have always had a limited appeal because unless you planned to do your game in one of those cities, there wasn't much appeal to it. After all, if you didn't intend to run a game in New Orleans then you probably don't need the sourcebook. It's probably too much for a night trip too.

    The sourcebooks are obvious but feel free to also include the cities described in things like Let the Streets Run Red like Indianapolis.

    Favorite

    Gary Indiana

    Yeah, no surprise here and I should probably include it as part of the greater Chicago Chronicles but I think it was a fantastic introduction to how V:TM works. The game isn't about fighting an
    enemy. The game is about...talking. It's the Seinfeld effect except instead of a diner, you just go to the Succubus Club.

    Here, Modius has enslaved the beautiful Allicia and is an insane Ms. Havisham-esque fop of a prince.

    Juggler claims to be the heroic champion of the Anarchs and Neonates but he also has enslaved the beautiful Evelyn in the same way.

    There's hunters after the PCs but killing them would make it worse and the best thing to do is to figure out something nonviolent to do it.

    Lucian is a 2000 year old Methusaleh who is 8th generation and thus not a Jhyad major player. There's also a Nosferatu seeking Golconda.

    Michael? He's Of Mice and Men in the making, isn't he?

    Least

    Los Angeles by Night

    This may surprise some people as this since most people agree this is the 2nd best sourcebook and it certainly led to a lot of awesome stuff like Bloodlines and LA by Night. However, I think that was because of what was built on Los Angeles by Night and retconned a lot of it.

    However, I think Los Angeles by Night seriously hurt the Anarch movement for the future of the line.

    Basically, so much of the Anarchs are defined by LA Gang culture, feuding over turf, and just being Brujah as well as Caiitiff with rare exceptions. I feel like compared to Maldavis, they really don't have anyone inspiring to join the Anarchs.

    On the other hand, it has Bela and Bela is awesome.​
    ​

    Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

      I'm not a fan of city books, but I have to agree about LA.

      One overall problem I have is that the city books ended up calcifying a lot of stereotypes instead of exploring possibilities. A lot of what both fans and writers expect about how Kindred will be and organize is just copying the early city books to some extent instead of diverging from them.

      LA defined and destroyed the Anarchs in this process. No one ever dared to build alternatives and so their bland brand of "anarchy" became what the movement is in the game. It sucks.

          I feel like Chicago by Night had a much better and still chaotic idea of what the Anarchs represented:

          * You have Tyler as the original Brujah sellout, someone who killed an ancient Ventrue (that became way more important overall) and helped found the Anarchs as we know them but now is just comfortable being a richer than God Kindred on the Primogen.
          * You had the Trotsky-esque socialists with Blackjack, Modius (who was an Anarch sellout), and characters from The Jungle.
          * You had the 1970s Black Panther movement that had become angry and violent BECAUSE of the way they'd been treated as angry and violent.
          * You had Maldavis representing the next generation of Civil Rights leadership stabbed in the back by their Primogen sponsors. People who sought to reform the system only to find out that the system had no interest in reform.
          * You had Juggler representing controlled opposition with him working for Tyler, the original Brujah sellout. Also, people like Uriah, Gordon Keaton, and Gengis who are Anarchs in name only.

          They didn't get along but they all had ideas of how to reform Kindred society and an intellectual side (except for Gengis).

                      As a Washingtonian by birth, have a special fondness some parts of DC by night. Peep my forum handle if you don't believe me.

                      First, let me say that I don't think its unusual for a native of a region depicted in a "By Night" or "Rage Across" book to have, shall we say, pointed opinions on its portrayal. It doesn't seem there was any sort of requirement for the writer to have lived in the region for any length of time. And, to be fair, most of these were written before Wikipedia was a thing (or at least widely used). But I still feel like, despite some big hits, DC by Night had several problems.

                      The Geography section reads like a tourist guide, with far too much attention on the various federal buildings but far too little attention to the neighborhoods where people actually live. The history section is phenomenal from the Colonial period up through the civil war. After that his interest in, ya know, the actual city of Washington DC seems to have waned. They misses out on a lot of the cities development as a center of (particularly African American) cultural expression and political struggle.

                      I don't want to imply the author is, like committing a racism or something. But such disinterest in the cities actual residents has knock on effects elsewhere in the quality of the final product. Simply put, next time I wouldn't have someone from Mechanicsburg VA (300 miles away) write a book about Washington DC.

                      In my opinion the fact it skips out on the cities diverse cultural history is made evident by the fact so few of the Vampires feel particularly tied to or part of the city. Most of them are broad enough vampiric stereotypes they could be slotted into almost any generic fictional ST created city. Ballet dancer Toreador. Serial Killer Malkavian. Drug runner Settite. Le yawn! Even Monica and Cynthia black are just kinda...there. No former intelligence agency Nossies? No Toreador with connection with any sort of black cultural product or era from the Chitlins Cricut through to Go-Go Music? No Brujah with any appreciable politics? In DC?!

                      My dream book that never got published would be City of Magik by Night: Washington DC. Mage/Vamp crossover. My two favorite games? Set in my home town? Ancient vampires and technocratic masters playing 5th dimensional chess while walking on eggshells around mortal hunters? A vibrant sub-altern culture for Traditions mages and neonates of all sects to rub shoulders in? STICK IN IN MY VIENS!

                              Reviving my opinion, I think SAN FRANSISCO BY NIGHT is probably my least favorite of the WOD books as it infuriates me on virtually every level. I understand what the author was going for but I disagree that was a good idea in the first place and it manages to tick every one of those boxes that annoy me.

                              Basically, the first issue is the lore ignorance. The author clearly wasn't familiar or interested in the Keith Herber San Fransisco created for the novels. That San Fransisco managed to incorporate the history of the Gold rush, gay rights movement, Spanish colonies, and the Chinese history of the city in a fascinating mixture of culture that Vannevar Thomas was the Prince of. All of that was discarded for the new Prince and characters because I'm fairly sure the authors didn't want to use any of that.

                              That's not something I'm fond of as a lore junkie but then there's what San Fransisco by Night was about: The Great Leap Outward and being a city where the Kindred are occupied. It more or less turns into a French Occupation story with the Kindred being oppressed victims of the Wan Kuei. Which is WEIRD and a bad allegory.

                                          City books are always useful, even if not for the city they're intended for, but I find nearly all of them have flaws. I would like to think we'd get better ones now, since Wikipedia and the internet make it easier to find local experts who can do a city well.

                                          Fall of London was terribad. WoD: Hong Kong is all kinds of goofy, offensive and OP, but at least is fun. I remember running a crossover game online for that, c. 2000, and I was inundated with requests to join. LA by Night has problems, but has a few good ideas and characters throughout.

                                          Montreal by Night is probably my favourite, minus some of the art choices and the trying-too-hard torture/snuff stuff. Yes, we get that it's a Sabbat city, but the edgelordiness is pretty cringe now. That said, the Irish Catholic in me obviously really digged all the dark mysticism of it.

                                          I think Montreal by Night is to the Sabbat what Chicago by Night was to the Camarilla, and the baroque decadence of the city just oozes ambience. Mexico City by Night has some cool stuff for showing the other parts of the Sabbat (especially its gratuitous violence), but felt like it was missing a little something.

                                          I've often wanted to write something about Manchester, where I live, as I've done a lot of research about the city over the years.

                                              Pro

                                              •Chicago of course is the gold standard really. While I’m someone who isn’t a huge fan of all the 2nd edition killings, both are still all-timers in my opinion

                                              •Washington D.C. by Night: I’ll admit I have a couple biases here. My first ever VtM or WoD books were used copies of VtM 2e Core and Washington DC by Night from a local gaming shop & I’m a DelMarVa area native. That said, while I think it’s got some really good gothic-punk imagery and vibes in it, if I’m being totally honest with myself, Marcus Vitel does a lot of heavy lifting of actually making the book memorable or noteworthy. If it weren’t for him becoming a popular and well known character this book probably would be read about as often as New Orleans or Milwaukee by Night. Still love it tho even though it’s in desperate need of serious revisions and an actually good dramatic cast outside of the secret Lasombra and secret Sabbat characters

                                              Anti

                                              To be clear, I don’t so much hate these books as feel that they have a lot of missed potential

                                              •New Orleans: there’s some cool ideas in here but IMO it completely drops the ball on living up to how important and good a city like NO could have been for a setting like VtM that is so heavily indebted to Anne Rice. The Xaviar conclaves are cool and it’s got some neat early cross-over stuff but it’s largely forgettable. Really needs a stronger rewrite. I think the way that New Orleans got used for the VtR metaplot was really interesting and could be a possible reference point.

                                              •L.A.: tbf I don’t think it’s that bad, but it’s another case of missed opportunity IMO and it has to unlive with the reputation of having partly killed off interest in the Anarchs as a sect (not that Revised wouldn’t help with that). I’m not as against the gang elements as some people are, I think that any gothic-punk setting from the early-mid 90s would have had to be in dialogue with the massive cultural influence of those symbols in motifs in that era. But what L.A. needed to be was what Chicago by Night had been for the VtM Status Quo, LA had to be for the Anarchs. That’s a tall order and it’s not surprising that it came up short but it’s still a mark on its record IMO. Plus the timeline gives me a headache when I try to square it with Demon, KotE, & Bloodlines (which tbh I largely ignore in practice). Still some great work has been done on these very forums to flesh out the setting and that deserves a mention.

                                              •NYC: this might be a hot take - I think the Tzimisce Antedeluviam being under NYC thing, while pretty funny and cool in theory, does more harm than good to the setting in theory long run. I admit this is totally subjective and that I would probably thing it was sick af in a particular chronicle (especially one with some Resident Evil type vibes) but IMO it feels a little too… I don’t know, comic book-y. Maybe that’s just the meme of it rubbing off unfavorably though, YMMV. Also while NYC has some neat ideas and characters in it, the overall setting never really clicked for me. The whole Midnight Siege for NYC metaplot thing is a cool and all but I honestly think that NYC before the Camarilla takeover would have been a more interesting setting: the Camarilla stronghold on Manhattan Island, the quarreling Sabbat bishoprics in the Outer Bureaus, the Anarch & Independent enclaves strewn throughout - I think it would have had more sauce, but maybe that’s just me.
