    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    The very fact that the Lasombra Antitribu were a thing pretty much means that they were already an option in the Camarilla, so the defection had no purpose in this sense.

    And the headhunting? Seriously, how feasible this headhunt really is? Once you're already safe in a Camarilla city what will they do? And now that the Sabbat is in the full "clans don't matter" mode it is even more nonsense.

    But the defection had nothing to do with the current state of the Sabbat. It didn't existed yet when the defection was written. It was, indeed, just a play to favor LARP players in a time when the Sabbat was still removed from the setting for all practical purposes (since they seem to think that the Middle East is in another world).
    Well the purpose of the defection is itself a justification: a big setting-shaking up event that causes a change to the status quo.

    I've mentioned how much contempt I have for static settings. They just don't hold any interest for me and it's ultimately what killed my love for Eberron. It's not that I can't make my own setting events but metaplot has an important role in my games that it gives a shared experience that players and Storytellers can write their campaigns around. I love "living" campaigns and shake ups have a factor to them that forces player characters to react to them as the kind of events that are unpredictable and change their world. When WW2 happens in a Pulp campaign, it's something that your player characters have to adapt to and react to despite it not being centered on them.

    It makes the world feel "bigger" and more authentic because the events are not entirely centered around the player characters. How do they feel about the Lasombra defection? What's their opinion on the Sabbat seemingly collapsing? Do they have an opinion on the Second Inquisition? Lots of things to discuss in-character and many a chronicle I have made have started this way: "You wake up from torpor and find out everything is different!"

        There's a rather big difference between, "I don't like how they changed the setting's world," and, "I don't like changing the setting period."

        And most games figure out a very simple compromise here: adventure modules. If you want a book that deals with, "you go into a long torpor and everything is different when you wake up," you can do that without radically changing the game's setting for everyone that doesn't want that much change.

                        An explanation for why I like Sabbat: The Black Hand for Heavy Arms .

                        1. To be frank, the majority of the **** 1/2star rating comes immediately from the fact that it was absolutely absurd to have to wait as long as we did for a Sabbat supplement. Not only did we have to have the Tzimisce and Lasombra waited on in supplements away from the main book but we also had the Sabbat exiled to nowhere (the Middle East being a place I could reach in a day) as if it wasn't a third pillar sect. The Sabbat main book should have always been one of the original three supplements for V:TM 5th Edition.

                        2. I'm a huge fan of Justin Achilli and want to state that the quality of the writing for this book is just flat out superior to the Camarilla and Anarch books. It's also third person and not in-universe, which means that it is clearer, more concise and more USEFUL than the previous two books. It gives a description of what the Sabbat is, what the Sabbat's culture is, Disciplines, and storytelling advice.

                        3. I don't feel comfortable downgrading a book's rating for what it is NOT. Speaking as a guy who reviews books as part of his profession (I review at Booknest.EU and have over 200 reviews there), I feel like you cannot be upset with a book for not being the way you want it. It doesn't matter if the V5 Sabbat SHOULD have had a playable faction, it's something they chose not to do and I'm not the writer. I can only judge the material on what it sets out to do.

                        Did I *WANT* the following?:

                        * Path rules
                        * Loresheets
                        * The (False) Black Hand having at least a paragraph?
                        * Rules for playing Sabbat defectors
                        * More History about how this happened
                        * The Sabbat Inquisition
                        * Infernalism

                        Absolutely, but I feel like I can't be mad it DIDN'T have these things.

                        4. I removed the half star that I did because the book is ridiculously short and overpriced. It should be 200 pages at the very least and it really should be 300+ pages for the fact it's so goddamn expensive. The book quality is tremendous but it's deeply underdeveloped. I feel like they should have combined this book and the V5 Companion (Ravnos, Tzimisce, and Salubri as Clans in the Sabbat isn't a bad combination either). It would have resolved a lot of issues I had and bumped it up to 5 stars. I was tempted to go 4 stars for the book but I admit I decided to ere on the side of positive because the Sabbat were finally back and no longer being un-personed like someone in 1984.

                        5. I felt the book was consistent in its vision and while I may prefer a Sabbat that is more intellectual, erudite, and distinct (as well as mostly on Humanity), I can't say that it was an unbelievable development for the Sword of Caine. I admit that I took the book was presently what the Sabbat had degenerated INTO rather than always were meant to be. The savages had taken over and the original Anarchs had either defected or were pulling the strings. The new Sabbat seems to be a metaphor for terrorism and religious fascism, which is something that certainly exists and was a version of the Sword of Caine I recognized. Its a tragedy that the Sabbat has become THIS but that doesn't mean it's a bad story.

                        6. Ratings are always a summary of my overall positivity to a subject and not going to cover all the actual ups and downs of something. What I liked, I liked very much. What I didn't like, I pointed out in the main part of the review.
