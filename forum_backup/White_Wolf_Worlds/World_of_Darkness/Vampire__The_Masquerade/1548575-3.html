    It also occurs to me that one of the pairs could be passing themselves off as some sort of New Age hippy-era holdover neopagan mystical sect, while the other pair have assembled a collection of bikers, metal heads, and other outlaw types.

            Yeah, it's always been something of a surprise to me that he does not have a bigger film career. Pretty much from the first few episodes of Remington Steele, people were saying he would make a great James Bond. And while he eventually did play James Bond, it took a decade longer than it should. Other than a few starring roles here and there, he's often cast in supporting roles. He obviously has a lot of screen credits and a successful career, but much like Rutger Hauer I expected him to have become a huge superstar. Enormously popular and liked by fans, but not someone who's had a career like Tom Hanks or Tom Cruise.

            = = =
            I want to talk about Edith and Enid Blount, in particular their diablerie. LABN indicates that they regularly diablerize lone Kindred who happen to fall into their clutches. Although they've been able to advance their generation from 12 to 10, most of their victims don't add to their generation at all. Since even one diablerie creates black stains on their aura for years, I imagine the vast majority of the time they've been around (or at least since 1944), their aura clearly marks them as diablerists.

            Their character description acknowledges this issue and says as an excuse the sisters claim they've needed to defend themselves and their territory from invaders. However, defending themselves by killing Kindred trespassers is not the same as diablerizing them. The writer of LABN clearly wants the extent of the sisters' crimes to be somewhat of a mystery, but I can't see how much of a mystery this is for anyone who has auspex and been around a significant amount of time. I certainly don't think any PCs will have trouble putting together the Blount sisters as their primary suspect once they hear how many Kindred have mysteriously disappeared around Glendale (despite the Lupine threat also being prominent here). It would just make all the other Kindred of LA to be incredibly stupid to not figure it out as well.

            So is there anything that can be done to revive this, or introduce any kind of mystery or suspense? Or introduce some sort of doubt among PCs that the Blount sisters aren't actually horrifying monsters? While not important characters, they are interesting enough flavor I want to include them in some ongoing capacity. As is, I only see the PCs either completely avoiding them, or ending them in short order. If the PCs do destroy the Blount sisters, I want it to be a real decision for the characters, and not done simply because it's the "obvious" thing to do. Plus I've always loved Arsenic and Old Lace.

            Also, what should be done about their Humanity? Like a lot of callous and dangerous Kindred in early sourcebooks, they are given a Humanity of 0. However, this was before the game settled on Humanity zero vampires degenerating into wights, and no matter how wights might be portrayed, the Blount sisters clearly have enough mental capacity that they aren't that. So what should we do? Simply make them stable, but low 2-3 Humanity vampires? Put them on some kind of Path, possibly one they obtained from a captured Sabbat they cannibalized back in 1965? If so, what Path? Make them secret infernalists on the Path of Evil Revelations? Make them low, but stable members of the Path of Typhon by making them Kindred secretly recruited by the Setites earlier? They may not be any higher on the Path of Typhon than Humanity, but I imagine they'd be less likely to become wights if on a path.

            The Blount sisters clearly destroyed their own father/sire in 1927 only three years after their embrace, but there is no reason to think they cannibalized other Kindred during the rule of Don Sebastian. They might have still murdered many mortals by their deceptions since this may just be part of their normal hunting method, but I think they would have only moved onto diablerie after the Free States were established.

                Regarding the Blount sisters, the answer to that question really revolves around how common the ‘Auspex’ ability happens to be and how likely two little old lady vampires are to attract close attention from Interested parties - I suspect their being relatively quiet monsters has played a key role in keeping them a going concern, coupled with what I suspect to be a deeply policing-averse wider community in the Anarch Free States (Itself violent enough to produce a significant casualty rate for young, friendless vampires whom very few would miss).

                If nothing else, if lonely vampires go missing in the vicinity of two well-established members of the Kindred community, locals would presume murder and not outright diablerie (Especially if the sisters have long experience of properly disposing of their victims), which means they might well have avoided being subject to the old ‘Soul Gaze’ to date.


                As for keeping them relevant, one of the more entertaining methods wouod be to set them up as the resident cross between Jessica Fletcher and Hannibal Lecter - little old ladies with a spectacularly sharp nose for murder and other homicide related mysteries who are just delighted to share their expertise (“ahem, sensible ideas and long experience”) with coteries of an enquiring mind.

                Given there are two sisters, you might do worse than to give one low Humanity and the other a non-Humanity Path, if only because this gives these two Old Lady Vampires something to talk about!

                    Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                    Regarding the Blount sisters, the answer to that question really revolves around how common the ‘Auspex’ ability happens to be and how likely two little old lady vampires are to attract close attention from Interested parties - I suspect their being relatively quiet monsters has played a key role in keeping them a going concern, coupled with what I suspect to be a deeply policing-averse wider community in the Anarch Free States (Itself violent enough to produce a significant casualty rate for young, friendless vampires whom very few would miss).
                    I think there's enough for it to be something easily known after a few decades. Aura Perception is just Auspex 2. Lots of clans have it, and it tends to be a fairly popular Discipline even for Kindred for whom it is an out-of-clan Discipline. A quick eyeball survey of LABN shows about 15 NPCs have it, and CBN1e has about 30 NPCs with it. It's between 1/3 to 1/2 of all named characters with stats in those sourcebooks. Now the WoD is notorious for wanky NPC stats that don't make sense, and once the ST throws in his own NPCs that can go down. But it seems to be a fairly prevalent power. It won't be on all the time, but since diablerie stains last for years and the Blount sisters seem to practice it regularly, it'll get noticed at some point. And once one person notices, the news should spread and it's easily confirmed.

                    Now if all the Blount sisters do is stay in Glendale and never come out, that'll be easier to miss. But then you have almost zero chance of PCs running into them unless the STs send them on a chase that causes them to directly interact with the Blounts. I'm not fond of that approach personally. I'd prefer a more natural interaction. Some early interaction with the sisters first just to introduce the character. Then at some point have some minor NPC they befriend just disappear, and if the PCs investigate they'll learn the last place he was seen was around Glendale. And then everything after that depends on what the PCs do, if anything.

                    Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                    If nothing else, if lonely vampires go missing in the vicinity of two well-established members of the Kindred community, locals would presume murder and not outright diablerie (Especially if the sisters have long experience of properly disposing of their victims), which means they might well have avoided being subject to the old ‘Soul Gaze’ to date.
                    Normally I agree, but at least in canon those disappearances are noteworthy. It gets mentioned in several places in the sourcebook, leaving a bit of a mystery until you get to the Blount sisters character description and then the reason why becomes obvious.

                    And there's enough NPCs who should have learned about the diablerie stains over the past few decades that someone should make the connection. One can ignore black stains that last a few years and go away, but it's harder to do that if they keep lasting for decades.

                    Now it's possible to say that the Kindred of LA just don't care enough about diablerie to do anything about it. But even then, I imagine there has to at least be some small group of Kindred who would just say, "Fuck it, let's lynch mob them" at some point, and then it'll be over for the Blount sisters. (Even if all the Kinded who disappear are insignificant loners or newcomers without strong ties to others, I think at some point a small, but sufficient, mob forms since it's just these two insignificant Toreador who also lack others who would protect them.) Who is really going to defend them or retaliate in vengeance afterwards? Edward Vignes might initially, but any mention of decades lasting diablerie stains would probably cause him to back down.

                    So my preference would be some way that the Blounts can somehow hide their stains. It might not be 100% effective or available all the time, but enough that it can drop suspicions. (PCs may be able to see it successfully, and maybe someone mentions that back in the day they knew at least one of the sisters had black stains.) Maybe it's some Sabbat relic the sisters obtained during the Sabbat invasion. Or some very rare Anarch Blood Magic ritual that hides it somehow, or the effect of a demonic investment because of a pact made to some demon. If someone know a canonical item or ritual that can accomplish this, I'm interested in knowing.

                                        I'm somewhat heading away from actual anarchs to a sourcebook that on the surface has no connection to the Free States, 1999's Time of Thin Blood. Despite a lot of disdain I have for the Revised period, it did produce some good materials and interesting ideas. I think this sourcebook is one of them, mostly because the issue of what comes after the 13th generation was always a big question ever since the game was introduced.

                                        Some of the concepts introduced here I feel are very appropriate for a Free States chronicle because if any place would provide a critical mass for them, it would have to be the Free States. One can understand that Princes, almost unanimously, would simply prohibit any known 13th generation vampire from siring, even someone like Modius. There is nothing to stop them in Los Angeles. It would also be a haven for any thin blooded vampires that were illegally embraced and now fleeing for their lives. The Free States are the perfect place to introduce and deal with these kind of issues.

                                        So the first thing is just having a noticeable amount of 14th and 15th generation vampires around. I think some - especially those of the 14th generation - can be integrated into many of the existing coteries and gangs. Many anarchs won't care whatsoever the official generation of Kindred or even ask (or know what they are themselves). And while the anarch gangs do prioritize combat strength for their own street wars, the bog standard 14th generation vampire may not appear to be any physically weaker than the vast majority of other neonates. And some Thin Bloods could prove themselves very useful. ToTB introduces the concept of Thin Blood "seers" who have a unique mystic Background Insight that produces supernatural visions and premonitions of intrigues of the elder. Wouldn't that be useful to the anarchs? I'll be introducing one 14th generation vampire who has strong Insight that was recruited by Jeremy MacNeil into his personal coterie, and she's extremely thankful for having such powerful and influential allies/protectors.

                                        Those who do seem much weaker - those 14th gen with the Thin Blood flaw, the 15th gen, or those with additional Flaws associated with Thin Blood - might be isolated or forced to join together only among themselves. I wonder if such Thin Bloods often become victims of Kindred like the Blount sisters. While "officially" anarchs shouldn't care about this, I think most do. Some of this might be residual prejudice - especially from the older anarchs, but I think more often this would be simply more dangerous predators sensing weakness and seeking to prey on the weak. That is especially the case in an overcrowded domain where feeding competition becomes fierce.

                                        To me, the main question is how many Thin Bloods should be in Los Angeles, and how do other Kindred interact with them, particularly those who might prey on them. All together the great LA metro area probably has between 150-200 vampires. I am thinking keeping the Thin Bloods between 5-10 in number, although who that is may change as old ones disappear and new ones arrive.

                                            The other concept in ToTB that I'll be using is the idea of the Unbound. The Unbound is simply "a loose social movement" who reject the entire concept of clans entirely. If this idea is ever to gain critical mass that it spreads to many domains, it has to begin in the Free States. I can't imagine it is ever going to get any traction in a traditional domain when anarchs have more pressing issues to deal with and often have to make deals with elders opposed to the Prince just to get some breathing room.

                                            So I am going to make "The Unbound" the name of an actual gang/coterie. So many places in LA are simply labelled as "gang of 10 Kindred" or "gang of 15 Kindred", so giving these small, completely undefined, gangs a real identity is so important. It's primarily a gang of Brujah and Caitiff but may have a Malkavian in it. I think the other clans may be too protective of their identity, but I might have more Kindred of different clans join later on in the chronicle.

                                            The members are very weak generation and very young, so they don't see the reason for the importance of clans. If they become friends with vampire X, why should that Kindred's genealogy matter? Clans are just another tool of the elders to divide the young, aren't they? Their homespun philosophy would be quickly crushed in any other domain, but they are able to create their own philosophy and propagandize it in the Free States.
