    VTM Revised (1998) - Is aggravate damage even needed?

    07-18-2021, 04:36 PM
    Looking at 1998 Revised rules for VTM (still the most popular version in my country, probaby because noone wants to buy new books every few years) I wonder if you even need agg damage to deal with other vampires cosidering Potence is no Blood cost auto success per dot and Celerity is 1 BP for as many extra actions as you you have dots in celerity. So Brujah with 2 Potence and 2 Celerity on start with Sword STR+3 could own almost anything. Also since Initiative is DEX+WITS, you have those high to start first (and have high dodges and high parries) and you could deal like what: 7 average dmg per attack with sword? 3 action that's 21 lethal damage to soak.

    But on the other hand you had OP Hold+Bite with auto-success Potence but then you are sitting ducks.

    I don't recommend 1v1 a warewolf but with Resources 5 you can that sword silvered no issue for 21 unsoakable dmg if you win Initiative (otherwise you die so it's last fight for one of you). With Clerity 5 and Potence 5 that would be...ugh.

    Would probably add Auspex to counter Obfuscate.

    Crazy when you compare it to V5 now.

      Ultimately? No. You're correct that Agg is not the king of combat. Having Agg is better than not everything else being equal, but you don't just pick an Agg attack and rule the fight.

      The big advantages for Agg are:

      1) Anti-healing. A soak monster to counter your damage monster is at a disadvantage (that's not a bad thing, you don't want combat to stall completely), but if they're not spending blood while you are, they can use it to heal bashing/lethal damage. Once Agg comes into play combat-healing is off the table (even for lupines combat healing is harder to do once Agg is being tossed around). If you ambush someone with non-Agg and they get away but took some damage, they can probably feed and heal up completely before you get to them again. If you do the same with Agg, you have a lot more time to track them down and finish them while they're still weakened.

      2) Concealed. Most Agg sources for vampires are things they always have on them. It might get obvious once it's clear violence is going to break out, but you can walk down the street as a vampire with your fangs hidden, powers like Protean and Serpentis not having any outward signs, and so on. The Potence route means relying on hand-to-hand weapons. In most places where you're going to find a significant number of vampires, walking around with a sword isn't going to go well. At best it's going to draw attention and bar you from specific locations, at worst you're going to be dealing with the police instead of whatever vampire business you had to deal with. If you want to not be obvious, you limit your average damage by using low damage-tag weapons (and good concealable armors are easier to get these days).

      So agg isn't needed, but it has its uses. Plus, if we're looking at the wider WoD, there are things that can get good lethal soak pools, but have shit Agg soak. So what you're dealing with can vastly shift the value of lethal vs. agg.

      You might want to check out V20 though, there was a 13 year gap there....and it rebalanced Potence and Celerity a bit. The vast majority of V20 is backwards compatible with Revised, so it's pretty easy to use your Revised collection with the V20 rules.

          It's to emphasize how you can't bounce back from getting charred by the sun. You will remain crispy critter made up of well-done and outright burnt meat until you drain a lot of humans (roughly as many as typical slices in a loaf of bread, most likely). You also get to roll Stamina + Fortitude against lethal, and unless you're beheaded or your heart is destroyed, it might not result in final death like agg does.

          As far as W:tA goes, you're better off not trying to make the comparison. It's a different genre where they're not punished nearly as much for butting heads, though there's always a background risk of dying in a horribly grotesque fashion that would make V:tM very discouraging if it had to play by the same flow of action, since the sell for Vampire is being immortal as long as you avoid fighting. Which is why Kindred in Revised, IIRC, had a one-turn warmup for Celerity actions, meaning that they took effect on the turn after the blood was spent, and why Garou and Fera have a Gift that flat out adds ten to their initiative calculation, plus ten more for every point of Gnosis they bother to spend to crank it higher, and it isn't even the most dramatic influence on combat. Or, in other words, you will not win initiative, you will not conceal your presence (Sense Silver is another Gift that the same character type will have), and you will not get the drop on them. You engaged in the most predictable move of all time, and will summarily end up discovering what the Splatterpunk genre is and how it differs from Personal Horror.

If your vampire's enemies know you carry around one, they can just have variously minor pawns call in reports of a person threatening people with a sword cane, and then you have to deal with cops that need to search you, and then depending on where you are, could confiscate the weapon or even arrest you; forcing you to roll up those social Disciplines all the time instead of getting any of your goals done. In many jurisdictions sword canes are actually a worse idea to get caught with because they come with a worse penalty as "disguised" weapons (that is, weapons made to look like something that isn't a weapon) instead of simply a concealed weapon.

Spotting concealed blades s a lot easier than people seem to think because blade long enough to be serious combat weapons don't flex or bend like a body does, and lots of really innocent motions or gestures completely give them away even if they look completely hidden when you're standing still.

Trenchcoats don't really do you any good when it's 85 F outside, muggy, and walking around in a loose fitting trench just makes you even more suspicious looking. Loose fitting clothing goes in and out of fashion. When tight fitting is in, concealing weapons gets significantly harder even if you're wearing something called a trench coat.

                  This is actually significantly worse in modern nights because well off individuals can afford to, and are expect to, have lots of different outfits for different occasions. If you want to avoid attention, it's harder to keep to clothing that gives you tactical advantages while remaining "normal" in various social contexts.

                          So something that happened in my games, is that I changed how soaking aggravated damage works. Kindred can soak aggravated with Stamina, and Fortitude, instead of granting soak dice, grants additional Health Levels and lowers soak difficulties. So aggravated damage remains difficult to heal, but isn't as ridiculous in combat. At first everything was fine, but once we got into some combats, the Gangrel player started having difficulty. It turned out that her Str +1 claws weren't cutting it at all, and she had difficulty inflicting damage (she only has Str 3). In V20 and earlier versions of VtM, 4 dice of aggravated damage can still be dangerous (especially post-Revised, as she has a high Dexterity). Now? Not so much. Sure, my rules prevent the uber-strong Potence using Gangrel from dominating combat, but it makes claws a lot less useful for less optimized Gangrel. I haven't decided what I want to do about this, but I just wanted to point out that aggravated damage is just fine as long as people aren't throwing around massive amounts of it- even Lure of Flames only does 3 damage a turn (albeit at diff 9 to soak; same as intense sunlight).

                                                      Personally, I allow all vampires to soak everything but Bane Aggravated (fire, sunlight, faith) with Stamina + Fortitude, and Fortitude also gives extra Health Levels*. It gives Fortitude a boost to bring it more in line with Potence.

                                                      But, and this is a load-bearing but, I use the Streamlined Combat rules from V20DA. That means you don't roll damage or soak; they're just static values. As that winds up being net successes + Strength + weapon modifier + Potence damage, versus Stamina + Fortitude, you're more likely to do some serious damage.

                                                      (These combat rules also negate the need to spend blood to get auto successes for Potence, since damage is no longer rolled, which is why Fortitude is now stronger. This may have been an oversight in V20DA, as they never reference this or how to handle it. Likewise, they don't mention how to deal with fire and sunlight, which will do no damage if static versus higher levels of Fortitude; I got around this by saying that all environmental damage requires you roll soak.)

                                                      Using those fast combat rules, it's quite easy to kill a mortal in a single blow and vampires without Fortitude go down in a couple of turns if they don't heal. That seems about right me.

                                                      *Each dot of Fortitude grants additional health levels as follows:

                                                      • Mauled (-2), Crippled (-5)
                                                      •• Wounded (-2)
                                                      ••• Injured (-1)
                                                      •••• Hurt (-1)
