                Originally posted by Father Enoch View Post
                Obviously, flavor text and rules text are very different, but that page made it sound like Demons were pretty powerful to me.
                Demons are also liars, and that goes for both the Fallen and the other many entities in WoD that can be called "demons".

                Is the thing the Vampire summoned really that powerful? It doesn't really matter at all. The thing will say it is, and it will have enough tricks to back it off. Are you going to challenge that? Being outright immune to mental control, messing with emotions and possession, plus resistant to illusions, is wonderful to convince the people that rely on such powers that you are stronger than them. And being able to come back from the dead as many times as needed, barring some very specific things you know your mark can't pull off, gives you a sense of security and a confidence that makes it all too easy to put a tough face.

                They don't believe in you, you just come back with another face, throw another pawn on them, and you can really scare those people. Just imagine the situation, an Elder Kindred face to face with a demon. The undead thinks they have complete control over the hapless "victim" until the entity inside starts to do whatever they want and state in no uncertain terms they're the one running the show. All their mental powers are useless against this thing, and it is completely fearless against life threats. While it isn't really immune to mundane persuasion, Torment means there's little the Vampire can threat it with that it didn't saw before. At most it says calmly and confidently that it won't fight back because it would just harm an asset for no gain, and this body is just an expendable vessel. "Kill me if you want, enjoy your time, I'll come back, no hard feelings!"

                When you come back from Hell possessing a new body, you approach the vampire with the same confidence, the same smile, and just starts with something like, "expendable, see? A dime a dozen. Are you better to talk, now?"

                Most Umbral entities can pull off something to that effect, the more dangerous can do that reliably. The Fallen folloe distinct rules because they're a Main Splat, but they're otherwise no exception to this: they're exceedingly powerful Umbral entities. Some are as powerful as the Infernalists think. None need to be to just be pretty convincing, and even if you don't believe, you simply have no recourse to prove them wrong.

                        But the Demon is a possessing entity, and Werewolves don't have a lot of resources to deal with the entity itself. It isn't just a Bane and Gifts and Rituals meant to affect spirits don't have a clear mechanic for affecting Demons. Indeed, the lack of reference on them in the DtF's Storyteller's Companion indicates that they just don't affect the Fallen.

                        On the setting being boring... I don't know, but the core book surely has a problem driving home what's there to see and do. The supplements are a must in this regard, with special consideration to City of Angels, Damned and Deceived and Earthbound. DnD here has special attention because, while it focus on Thralls, it has really, really good story pieces showcasing conflict with and also between Fallen.

Their powers on top of that do have a recurring theme of Truth, of messing with reality by reaching its actual workings, of playing the tools that crafted reality to begin with, but here is where the set is messily written.                                         Originally posted by Herr Meister View Post
                                        I only meant that people would usually think "Heck! Don't mess with him, he's a demon!" in a way to show that culturally, we are more or less inclined to fear a demon much more than a werewolf.
                                        I can understand that, but then we have a more nuanced situation here.

                                        First, again, Werewolf is the most combat-oriented splat, so there aren't much we can do about that. Making any splat more fearsome in physical confrontation would defeat their purpose in the setting. Also considering that Garou are also more than what a werewolf is in most settings, they have a far greater and more complex role in WoD. All in all making the Fallen clearly superior wasn't an option from the design perspective.

                                        Other RPGs don't necessarily make it so simple, either. D&D comes to mind (obviously) and Fiends there run the whole gamut of power, most being clearly below the standard werewolf. You don't fear them because they're all individually strong, you fear them because of all the connotations they bring with them.

                                        Here again, not that you have to personally be impressed, but the idea is that the Fallen are to be feared by what they can bring in a greater scale. From a Werewolf perspective, for example, this is a foe that can patiently work towards their corruption while they can do little beyond causing it minor setbacks. Death for a well prepared Fallen just means an XP tax to recover Faith and Torment, and other splats won't know even that cost. The Fallen is an opponent that has infinite time in their hands and the stakes are cosmic, the fate of your immortal soul just the least important, and you simply can't win in their game through physical confrontation.
