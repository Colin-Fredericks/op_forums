                                It's worth remembering that White Wolf was an American company, and, initially, were almost exclusively selling to an American audience, which obviously shaped their publication choices. There was also a specific in-game reason for concentrating on America, which was that, in the early editions, Europe was portrayed as a stable region, dominated by elders, whilst America was dynamic and riven by conflict. This characterisation had largely receded by revised, but was certainly there initially.

                                And ... it's fine saying that certain cities ought to have books devoted to them, but these books need to have a reason to exist in terms of their contents. So, take a notional Hamburg by Night, for instance. What's so interesting about the city in terms of the World of Darkness which would make it a compelling place to write about? Or Dresden? And, whilst, in reality, it's not too hard to create one or two interesting hooks, as a publisher you may well run out of steam quite quickly. You also have to sell these books to people. A sourcebook devoted to a key city which is supposed to exemplify the setting, like Chicago, is not too bad. But, as a publisher, you also have to justify why a potential purchaser might want to buy not just Chicago by Night, but also Detroit by Night, Indianapolis by Night, and St Louis by Night, which are at least in the vicinity of Chicago, and can therefore be used directly in a chronicle set in Chicago.

                                            Originally posted by James_Willoughby View Post
                                            And ... it's fine saying that certain cities ought to have books devoted to them, but these books need to have a reason to exist in terms of their contents.
                                            As someone that doesn't see a lot of inherent value in any of these books, I don't find it that compelling to demand too much of a bar for more of them. People like them. If people like them, and people buy them, then there's no real other reason for companies to make them. One of the reasons for wider ranging books geographically is to allow more variety to them and thus keep things fresh.

                                            Additionally, it's not inherently a bad idea to encourage people to broaden their horizons a bit. The books have to be compelling, but there's a certain value it taking a city your target audience wouldn't immediately think of and showing how any city can be something special with some research and creative presentation.

                                            For example:

                                            So, take a notional Hamburg by Night, for instance. What's so interesting about the city in terms of the World of Darkness which would make it a compelling place to write about? Or Dresden?
                                            Hamburg is an industrial powerhouse (it's the largest port city in Germany, and one of the largest in Europe) with history as a trade center going back to the Holy Roman Empire, making it a natural vampire hub for influence over power. Not just in Germany, but in the EU and even globally as Hamburg is home to a number of institutes that work on international trade law and frequently it hosts UN/etc. meetings on such topics.

                                            Hamburg is also one of Europe's largest centers of journalism, making it an essential place for vampires to vie for control to keep the Masquerade intact. The large number of UNESCO heritage sites is also a nice bit of fun for vampires that want their homes to be maintained for generations.

                                            Hamburg might not be my first choice for a German city... but if it's not in your top 5... you probably know very little about modern Germany or it's history.

                                            Dresden could be a bit repetitive, but frankly it has such a strong hook as a Vampire/Wraith crossover book after the Allied destruction of it that exploring the supernatural repercussions of the firebombing is more than enough of a hook to carry a book on top of Dresden's unique history as a major German city.

                                                    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post

                                                    As someone that doesn't see a lot of inherent value in any of these books, I don't find it that compelling to demand too much of a bar for more of them. People like them. If people like them, and people buy them, then there's no real other reason for companies to make them. One of the reasons for wider ranging books geographically is to allow more variety to them and thus keep things fresh.

                                                    Additionally, it's not inherently a bad idea to encourage people to broaden their horizons a bit. The books have to be compelling, but there's a certain value it taking a city your target audience wouldn't immediately think of and showing how any city can be something special with some research and creative presentation.
                                                    Yes, as you don't see the inherent value in these books, you're not understanding the problems found within producing them either. This isn't, for instance, travel writing; there's nothing wrong with broadening the horizons of your audience, but this shouldn't ever going to be the primary motivation behind creating a location sourcebook for a game. So, if we take the first edition of Chicago by Night, we have a book ostensibly about Chicago, whilst actually being about obtaining and exercising power, the blurring of history and literature, and an epic and almost concealed struggle within the World of Darkness. The book was very intentionally designed to explain how Vampire: the Masquerade worked on a "show not tell" basis, taking the reader through various power levels, right back to a pair of Methuselahs caught up in a two-thousand-year conflict. It had a very clear reason for existing, which is why it met with critical acclaim.

                                                    Now, compare this with London by Night, which existed because someone thought it was about time for a London sourcebook, and was written by a team of authors who seem to have no great interest in either London or the Victorian period.

                                                    Now, leaving aside opportunity costs in publishing (which is actually a massive issue if you want to make a profit), books exist, or at least ought to exist, for a reason. If you want to create (for instance) an equivalent to Chicago by Night, you, as the author, need to have something to say, and some greater insights than "this place exists and is kind of cool". The latter thinking is what led us to most of the worst location sourcebooks which have been published.
