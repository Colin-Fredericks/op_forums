    The Black Hand or Tal'Mahe'Ra thread

    02-15-2024, 03:46 PM
    This is thread to discuss the Tal'Mah'Re and the Black Hand of DIRTY SECRETS OF THE BLACK HAND and the V20's THE BLACK HAND: GUIDE TO THE TAL'MAHE'RA with also nods towards the Orphans of Enoch from FORBIDDEN RELIGIONS.

    Basically, what do you love, hate, and in-between about them.

    Which was the best depiction of them and how should they be in modern games?

    Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

      I thought the V20 guide struck a good balance with them as something you could build a campaign around (the writing in Dirty Secrets, whatever else can be said, I don't feel like it was completely there as a usable thing, if you will, so much as it threw a bunch of big insane ideas at you) without ignoring how gonzo they should be at heart (because if we're being honest, part of the allure of the True Hand is just how much it goes hard at being a trip to epic crazytown), but framing it with better writing. The V5 version is comparatively.. I don't really see the point, from my experience fans of the Tal'Mahe'Ra are entirely into how overwrought they are, and some toned down echo thing, it hits my "who is this even for?" buttons. People deeply into the True Hand aren't going to be happy with it, and people who feel validated by V5's general style of redoing things would likely have been just as happy if it were never mentioned at all again.

      Anyway, I say that as someone who initially hated the True Hand, felt what was in retrospect petty vindictive glee at them being nuked in revised, and ultimately came around to "you can just easily ignore them if you don't like them, it's not hurting anyone/the game if they exist and their fans are having fun, they don't need to be reduced to some pathetic echo to validate fanwars and authorial disdain". Not quite full on "how I learned to stop worrying and love the True Black Hand", but, somewhere adjacent.

      I did generally like the True Brujah as an *idea*, with all that said, the concept of them, if not 100% the execution. The whole living relics channeling the power of their own decayed temporal stasis thing. Also you can go full bore "The Timelord Victorious" egomania/moral fall in a way that feels like it has some VtM resonance. I played one in a 7 year+ irl elder game and all, had a good time with exploring their thematics and historical angsts.

          My own take on the subject is that I feel that the Tal'Mahe'Ra works perfectly fine in Vampire: The Masquerade but it's presented with 20% "too much" in Dirty Secrtes of the Black Hand. This is a very different percentage than fans who act like it's 200% too much or even 2000% too much. There's a lot of great ideas, many of which were incorporated into other parts of the setting but they all seem to run together when they're one.

          Dirty Secrets

          The Good

          * The Black Hand as a secret playing both sides against one another. It's vampire, conspiracies matter.
          * The existence of Revenant Families.
          * Worshipers of the Antediluvians.
          * Chatterlings as a concept are profoundly creepy.
          * The Old Clan Tzimisce
          * The description of Enoch.

          The Bad

          * Enoch as a physical place you can visit in the Underworld.
          * Making it so that there's ANOTHER clan of mages that have seized undeath in the Nagaraja.
          * The Souleater concept feels out of step with the themes of V:TM and more werewolf.
          * The Black Hand are a bunch of inhuman, Antediluvian worshiping, Path walking, child kidnapping, manipulative Elders...and they are treated as "the good guys."

          Basically, I feel the Black Hand is fine and about 1000x more interesting than what they did with the Inconnu in LAIR OF THE HIDDEN. One of the oldest and most interesting sects in V:TM ended up being a bunch of Elders living in a castle doing a regular Fawlty Towers plot. The Black Hand at least have some dignity and I kind of wish this was the write-up for the Inconnu.

          The Nuking

          I think the nuking is pretty much embellic of the absolute worst elements of Revised because it's symbolic of just how utterly lacking in self-awareness 3rd Edition could be at time and how utterly arbitrary it was in its disdain. Basically, Revised treats the Black Hand as some sort of over-the-top ridiculous embarrassment before dropping a Nuke on the Underworld and having an Antediluvian kill 3,000,000 people in India with no one noticing. Oh and having nukes dropped on that Antediluvian along with a space laser.

          It acted above the silliness when it was silly itself.

          Pretty much like the Dark Age of Comics when so many superheroes had guns and black outfit while pretending that was somehow more mature than Superman or Wonder Woman.

          V20 Black Hand

          I mean there's very little to say here other than, "the people at Onyx Path Publishing seem to have generally agreed with my above complaints." The version presented in V20 is toned down just enough that it fits the themes of V:TM a lot more. Linking the Tal'Mahe'Ra to the Servitors of Irad and other existing Gehenna Cults more or less helps cement the idea that they're definitely potent movers and shakers in Camarilla society but they're not all powerful either.

          V20's Black Hand also seems to have writers much more aware that antediluvian worshiping Gehenna cult is POSSIBLY a bunch of bad guys. I will say that having the BAALI join them and be accepted was a bit much for me and I wonder if it was genius or over the top for suggesting the Tal'Mahe'Re were idiots that were in way over their heads trying to control the Jyhad from behind the scenes.

          It also convinced me the Aralu were the Baali founders even if that doesn't quite much sense.

          Orphans of Enoch

          The Orphans of Enoch are a group that I'm mostly okay with even if I don't see how I'd use them in a game versus the "classic" Tal'Mahe'Re. I like them because they're an attempt by OPP to keep fitting in as much of the old lore as possible when V5 is full of writers who like to pretend it is is a *hock spit* reboot. The fact that they portrayed the after effects of something as goofy and ludicrous as the spirit nuke as deeply traumatizing and serious is sosmething I have mad respect for.

          The Orphans aren't the Black Hand but they can rebuild them.

              Last year, I tried my hand at actually reading Dirty Secrets. Didn't get through the whole thing before my attention drifted elsewhere. But my assessment was that it was trying to work with some pretty neat ideas, that ultimately came to the table half baked. Not to mention the book itself was half baked. It kind of bounces around with seemingly no rhyme or reason. It takes multiple chapters before the book drops the bombshell that it thinks the Tal'Mahe'Ra are "the good guys". (Oh, and that there are spiritual parasites infesting the world. That bombshell too).

              While we're on the subject of the Souleaters, I see what Dirty Secrets was going for. They wanted to create a new set of antagonists for True Black Hand members to fight, so it isn't all just manipulating other factions. The content would seem very thin, if the book only showed up with a single WoD faction to add to the proceedings, whose only function was to infiltrate and subvert other factions.

              Unfortunately, the Souleaters aren't really their own antagonists. They're every other game line's antagonists roughly bolted together and given the weakest motivations. It didn't add anything to the World of Darkness. If anything, it detracted from the nuances of other game lines, especially Werewolf.

              The only way it would really function without ruining the other games would be to explicitly state that the "Souleaters" concept was the Tal'Mahe'Ra barking up the wrong tree. That they were aware of all sorts of distinct forces acting in the world, including Banes and Fomori, but made the error of assuming they were all part of the same otherwordly force. In essence, supernatural conspiracy theorists. Just knowledgeable enough to see the threats, clueless enough to make incorrect assumptions about what it meant. Which, combined with their general paranoia and obsession with other conspiracies, makes them think there are infiltrators into their own organization where none exist. (Or at least aren't nebulous "spiritual infection" working within their members).

              Which I think is how V20 sort of boiled Souleaters down to. It's a fringe belief some Old Clan Tzimisce have, to justify not using Vicissitude.

              I'm kind of glad VtM decided to ignore Souleaters as a concept after Dirty Secrets. Could it have been reworked into something with more meat, that didn't step on every other splat's toes? Probably. But given how (arguably) central to the True Black Hand it was, I understand why White Wolf dropped it. As stated above, once you take the Souleaters out, there's not a lot for the Tal'Mahe'Ra to really stand on as a faction. You'd need to reorder the faction from the ground up. I don't blame White Wolf for not bothering. (The disdain with which they destroyed them in Revised, though, I can kind of blame them for).

                      I was one of those people who hated Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand when it first came out, but my opinion has since shifted. It was a long time before I actually ever read Dirty Secrets, as it was an incredibly rare supplement to find from my experience. So everything I knew about it was secondhand. However, the multiplicity of new bloodlines, strange Disciplines like Temporis, and many gonzo sounding ideas seemed like everything I didn't like about Vampire and not much that I did. Even when I did find a copy and read it, I still didn't like it though I was more impressed by what was actually written compared to how it was described to me. It's since grown on me.

                      I do like some things about the True Black Hand.

                      I like they are the next level of the Jyhad above the sects of the Camarilla and Sabbat. They are higher players in the Jyhad. There are other groups that I also place in this category - the Inconnu, the Children of Osiris, some of the Gehenna cults, some secret societies within each of the clans, and certain alliances of Methusalehs. They have very specific goals, have ancient and powerful vampires leading them, and they know deeper mysteries than other vampires. They are the string pullers that manipulate the sects below them. I think "layering" the Jyhad like this is important in concept, even if in practical terms it does not affect routine game play. There is probably another layer between them and the Antedeluvians, but this is probably the last practical level of higher Jyhad you can get to in any chronicle.

                      I do like certain payoffs from earlier Easter eggs. The Brujah clanbook first introduced the concept of the True Brujah. Here they are actually shown. Viccisitude was clearly inspired by Necroscope vampires so the Souleaters completed that concept. It explains the origins of the Revenants. It shows the Sabbat is just as badly manipulated by ancient vampires as the Camarilla is.

                      I also think it's the first sourcebook to introduce the Bahari and Lilith as an alternate figure for the "true" origin of vampirism. That's probably has been the most successful thing in it.

                      I also like the fact that the game had these deeper mysteries and secrets. The problem was that by publishing them, they were no longer secrets. Every gormless jackass who had the book now knew all this stuff, rendering their use as mysteries in your chronicle moot. I did a lot of eye rolling every time someone used their metagame knowledge to "solve" or "guess" things IC in a chronicle that they could never have done if this book was not published. So instead of using this stuff outright as written, I now like to think of DSotBH as more of a showcase of the kind of mysteries you should be creating for your own version of the setting. I keep some things that were introduced in this sourcebook, change others, and ignore anything I don't like.

                      Once I stopped treating it like "canon" I had to use in my chronicle, and saw it more like a toolbox I could use to build my own chronicle setting, I became a fan. It's better as a supplement for NPCs than using it to create PCs who are members of the sect.

                      It makes sense given the "physics" of the World of Darkness. None of Enoch is of course "physical". This is just the relic of the destroyed city in the underworld because that's what happens when things of significance get destroyed. I did find it odd that it's located in the Shadowlands (somewhere in the middle of the Atlantic Ocean) rather than being some island in the Tempest like Stygia or the other Dark Kingdoms. Because of its location in the Atlantic Ocean, I've always wondered if that was a clue that the antedeluvian world was in fact some version of Atlantis rather than a strictly Biblical version.
