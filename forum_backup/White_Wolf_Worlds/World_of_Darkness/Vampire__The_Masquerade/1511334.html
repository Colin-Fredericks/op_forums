      The following is from a Cairo game I was ST'ing . The metaplot is quite different from the official one, by you may still find useful ideas

      THE ASHIRRA
      The Ashirra is a vampire sect that gathers all vampires who declare their faith in Allah and believe that redemption for the Kindred was one of the promises He made to the Prophet Muhammad, Peace Be Upon Him.
      This sect was founded in the first years of Islam and, as the religion it was based on, spread quickly from Spain and North Africa to India and beyond. Following the Islamic model of political organization, and very much unlike the Camarilla, the Ashirra has always abhorred strongly centralized institutions, and relies on a system of local Emirs or Sultans exerting direct power and assisted in spiritual matters by Ulemas and Mullahs, who form a loose confederation from all Ashirra lands. Only in counted occasions a powerful religious leader will claim the title of Supreme Imam, and his authority would only extend to those who respected him on his own merits and personal virtue. No one has claimed the title, at least seriously, since the 16th century.
      The Ashirra has been the majority sect in the core lands of Islam since, but it lost a lot of its influence during the era of great European expansion, when the Camarilla tenets of secular humanism and centralized power expanded all over the globe and were adopted by many younger Muslim Kindred. By the end of the 20th century Ashirra were virtually indistinguishable from Camarilla Kindred, except by the fact that barely any Kindred identifying as a Muslim could be found among the Camarilla Justicar, Archon or any other sect-wide positions.
      The Ashirra as a sect took back the centre stage in the Muslim Kindred society during the events of the Year of the Herald, when powerful Elders from the previous ages used their faith as a rallying point to fight against the Sabbat, Gehena cults, demon worshipers and other terrors of the night. On the mortal side, the reaction to the war on terror policy gave young Muslims a renewed sense of identity, which they brought into the night when they got Embraced. As a result, nowdays the sect is, once again, the main player in the Muslim world. The Ashirra follow the exact same Six Traditions as the Camarilla, even if sometimes interpreted or implemented in a different way. For example, the role of the Elder outlined in the Traditions is often not fulfilled by the Prince, but by the Clan Elder, as it used to be during the Middle Ages.
      On top of the Traditions, the Ashirra also follow the Five Pillars of Islam. Obviously, because of their condition as vampires, some of these pillars have to be adapted or compromised on by the Ashirra. Fasting is followed in a lot of different ways, and most Ashirra only pray three times a night, at the times that are suitable for then. The Pilgrimage is a difficult endeavour because of the strong Faith of Mecca and most Ashirra prepare for years before undertaking it. But no Kindred would call themself Ashirra without at least the Profession of Faith.​
      Most Ashirra follow Tariq el-Umma (the Road of the Community), a philosophical path similar enough to Humanity to be fully accepted by the Camarilla without any problems. Its base tenets are practically the same, and the higher ones merely emphasize the beleiver's duty to the community more than to an abstract sense of self. Others, specially among the Hajj, follow Sharia el-Sama' (the Law of Heaven), a path that emphasizes the believer's duty to Allah and can sometimes justify "righteous violence". More worrying are the rumours of groups of Ashirra secretly following Sharia el-Harb (the Law of War), a modern adaptation of the tenets of jyhad against the infidels; this path is considered an aberration by most mainstream Ashirra and its adepts are shunned, ostracized or actively persecuted by most sect members.
      Because of its decentralized structure, the Ashirra is theoretically open to every Clan and Bloodline and doesn't have a clear divide between Founding Clans and others. On the other hand, Lineage is as important as personal virtue, and having Ancestors who are well known for their devotion to the sect can help open some doors and similarly, coming from a "tainted" Lineage can bar many more; in this particular, Clan Tremere and Giovanni are seen as aberrations by the Ashirra Elders who remember the Middle Ages and their usurpations, and a member of these Clans would only be allowed to join the Umma if they fully renounced their Lineage and became a Caitiff. The Clanless are not openly discriminated against, by their lack of Lineage usually works against them.
      As much as the Ashirra would like to present themselves as a spiritual community, their are not beyond engaging in power plays, political manouvering or even open internal conflict. As their Camarilla counterparts, they try to influence or control mortal institutions and often use them in proxy conflicts against each other.
      There are different factions among the Ashirra, based mostly on their specific beliefs.
      The Progressives are mostly secular, culturally Muslim Kindred who don't follow the Pillars very strictly. Most of them are very much like Camarilla Kindred, and sometimes have either been Embraced by them or Sired one of them.
      The Moderates don't necessarily have the middle ground in every issue. Instead, they treat every point of debate isolatedly and try to arrive to the right conclusion, following the style of al-Azhar Mosque and University.
      The Conservatives look at Tradition as the source of law. They usually follow wahhabism or other conservative Muslim school and apply it's principles to Kindred unlife.
      The Hajj are an ultraconservative faction formed mostly by Bay't Mutasharid, who see themselves as guardians of the holy places and monsters for the infidels. Unlike regular conservatives, they have their own school of thought, that, among others extreme principles, forbids the use of Obfuscate.
      Banu Haqim usually follow their own branch of Islam, based in Ismailist Shi'a, which encourages mysticism, devotion and the principle of Taqiya (Simulation of Conformity).
      The Shepherds of Islam are a diminute but influential group of Elders dedicated to the preservation of the Traditions of Islam. They are fairly conservative but try to avoid conflict with any with other Ashirra and even monotheistic or agnostic Kindred. On the other hand, they firmly oppose paganism and other pre abrahamic religions.

                      I always preferred the earlier idea that the Ashirra was just the term the vampires in Arabia used for themselves, while those Muslim vampires who were particularly devout and desired to protect the hold sites of Mecca and Medina was the "Hajj" (although technically this was a particular group of Nosferatu). "Ashirra" was just the local term for Kindred, and earlier WoD writers loved to sprinkle alternative, local names for their standard game terms to create exotic flavor. They weren't any kind of alternate sect. I believe they were first mentioned in 1996's A World of Darkness (2e).

                      But in order to generate more product to create more revenue, the Revised era Developers sought to use modern day notions of multiculturalism as an easy way to pump out more sourcebooks. And therefore the (very cool) idea of a worldwide conspiracy of vampires called the Camarilla was diminished and replaced by the (very lame) idea that centuries or even thousands of years ago vampires all around the world would instead have organized themselves to anachronistic notions of late 20th century multiculturalism.

                      I much preferred the idea that just like late 20th century Chicago could be home to a Nosferatu elder who was a former jihadist as a mortal, that a vampire who had been a Greek philosopher, former Crusader zealot, Roman senator, or Zoroastrian priest (and not currently a Muslim) could be a Prince or primogen in the Middle East. That excites my imagination in ways that current canon does not.

                          In my personal canon, I tend to view the (re)creation of Ashirra as mostly a response to colonialism. The Ashirra first came into being back in the 6th and 7th centuries as a loose organization of vampires in the Middle East. When the Camarilla was created, many of the vampires in the Middle East were initially interested in joining and quite a few did. The early Camarilla included most of the Middle East and even parts of India. The Ashirra were essentially subsumed by the Camarilla and the idea of a universal and equal vampire organization.

                          This lasted for a number of centuries. But later in the 18th century, when Europe began engaging in conquest and colonialism in the Middle East and elsewhere, this tended to put off many local vampires who, even if they were undead, understandably weren't big fans of outsiders coming in and taking over. The Camarilla, for the most part chose to support those colonial actions. "Well, it's just the mortal's doing and we shouldn't interfere," they'd say, even while many European vampires worked with colonial oppressors to make massive money off the suffering of people in the Middle East, India and elsewhere. Then, you started seeing vampires from France or Britain or elsewhere show up and try to take over or set up their own courts in Egypt, Tunisia, Algeria, India, etc, mimicking the actions of their mortal brethren.

                          This ended up causing many vampires in the Middle East to view the Camarilla as an oppressive organization or at least as an organization that supported their oppression. Many local princes chose to leave the Camarilla, viewing the organization as one that was more interested in the wellbeing of European vampires at the expense of everyone else (which wasn't exactly wrong). They then set up their own organization, essentially recreating the Ashirra of old, which was based on decided non-European principles.
