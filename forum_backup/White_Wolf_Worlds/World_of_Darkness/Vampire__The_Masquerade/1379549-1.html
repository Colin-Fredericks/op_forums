    I'm not interested in the V5 metaplot and haven't bought any of the books so keep that in mind when reading my comments. I think it's one thing to "struggle against the Man" when everything is about being against what X is doing (when X is Lodin, Petrodon, the Inner Circle or whatever). All that person's opponents can agree on that. It's another once one group actually seizes power and has to rule. At that point everyone's different agendas start working against each other.

    It is much easier for Modius. Juggler, Hinds, and Maldavis to all agree Lodin must be overthrown. But once you start discussing what the governance will look like after that, everything falls apart.

    So when we're talking about Anarchs seizing control of Washington, Berlin, New Zealand, Indianapolis, or whatever, what exactly are we talking about? How are the Anarchs ruling those cities? Do the other Anarchs agree? Is this really a movement coming together, or is it a movement coming apart? What does it mean that these Anarchs are ruling these cities - how is it different? How is it the same?

    I don't mind there being a "revolutionary vanguard" group within the Anarchs that could seize power. The Bolsheviks did that, of course. But the important thing to remember about the Bolsheviks is that the only way they could do it was through an unprecedented set of conditions that couldn't be duplicated. (I know the various metaplot changes in V5 is another unprecedented set of conditions, but everyone's mileage will vary). Another thing to remember is that once the Bolsheviks grabbed power, they destroyed the unity of International Socialism with most of the factions going against them, or even other radicals splitting from them. Once any group of Anarchs seize power and begin to rule, inevitably a large number of Anarchs who think differently will break from them, forcing one group or the other into a new name.

                                            I think Anarchs will fall into the same old trope if you let them. I can see enough in The Anarch to actually use the Anarchs in ways that I hadn't considered when I was playing in the 90s. I can give you an example using the campaign I am working on. In my New York City-based campaign, the Camarilla is the largest faction and exerts the most control because it's unified. The Anarchs are slightly smaller than the Cam, but are composed of different factions which are not working with the same unity of purpose as the Cam, so NYC is, in effect, a Camarilla city.

                                            THE ANARCH shows there is diversity in the Anarch mindset. And this is what I ran with, creating the following Anarch factions:

                                            Boss Callihan (from NY by Night and Coteries of New York), Baron of Staten Island: Claims to be the Baron among Barons, but his authority is being challenged by the other Barons. A Sellout.
                                            Karol Zdrada, Baron of Brooklyn: An old-school socialist who believes in authority through political councils and ideology.
                                            Reginald Yorke, Baron of The Bronx: Militant rebel. Violently protects his group from any Camarilla or outside influence. Would love to violently overthrow them.
                                            Anderson Jean, Minister of the Church of Set: Devotee of the Church and of Hesha Ruhadze. Seeks forbidden knowledge and pushes the boundaries of desires.
                                            Josephine Hebert, Regent of House Carna's New York Chapter: Carna's blood-sister, her group is aligned with the Anarchs out of convenience and opposition to the more orthodox Chantry of the Five Boroughs.
                                            Connie Perez, Baron of Harlem: Community Organizer who attempts to elevate both Kindred and Kine and is an avid follower of Salvador Garcia.
                                            Maria Rodriguez, Baron of Queens: Devotee of Agata Starek, would probably fit in with the Sabbat, minus the religious overtones.

                                            So, with these groups, you can have a rich diversity in what being an Anarch means. In fact, the strength and appeal (as well as the weakness) of the Anarch Movement is that it is so diverse. Each Baron has a very different idea of what the ideal post-Camarilla society looks like, different ideas of how to achieve these goals, and different tolerances for "appropriate behavior."
