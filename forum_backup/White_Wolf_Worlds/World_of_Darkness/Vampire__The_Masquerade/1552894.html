    How does one recognize a Caitiff?

    08-04-2025, 09:45 PM
    For my upcoming chronicle, there is a PC who will be playing a Caitiff. In terms of his backstory, he was illegally embraced as part of some ploy. He doesn't know who his sire is. However, he quickly realized he needed to get out of his current city and is trying to set himself elsewhere.

    This kind of backstory isn't actually that rare in Vampire chronicles. It's almost the default background for Gangrel. It happens to a lot of Brujah characters too. Eventually those characters are recognized as being from their clan and accepted by them as clanmates. For some of the other clans, this presents more problems, but it is not impossible - perhaps not even improbable - for such characters to be accepted as Ventrue or Toreador. Maybe even Tremere. Obviously, both Malkavians and Nosferatu should be able to recognize their clanmates without much issue because there are objective signs that will lead to that (physical appearance for Nosferatu, and a link to the Madness Network/Cobweb for Malkavians - although I'll note without the Malkavian Time ability, they might not be detected). Such characters might first be recognized as social caitiffs (outcasts and exiles from their clans), but eventually their actual connection to the clan will be identified (they are recognized as not game mechanic Caitiff with no clan weakness and no clan disciplines).

    But what about actual Caitiffs (clanless)? At what point (and how) do vampires determine someone is actually "clanless" and not just an unidentified member of a real clan?

    With some exceptions (like Malkavians and Nosferatu), it probably is not by clan weakness. Not only do many clan weaknesses be hard to tell off hand (or not show up for a long time), some clan weaknesses are duplicated by Flaws any vampire could potentially have. Is that guy who is prone to frenzy a Brujah, a Ventrue with the Short Fuse flaw, or perhaps someone who just has low Self-Control and Willpower? Is she a Ventrue or a Toreador with the Prey Exclusion flaw? And so on.

    So what gives it away? Why is Gordon Keaton from Chicago By Night 1e considered to be a Caitiff and not a Brujah given his sire is known to be Dooley and Dooley's other childe Anita is accepted as a Brujah?

    It is that his initial Disciplines don't seem to be Brujah clan Disciplines (Keaton has mostly Dominate and Auspex; Anita has all three Brujah clan Disciplines)? But what about Jimmy Holcombe (Neon) whose sire was Balthazar? Neon's only Discipline is Celerity which is a Brujah clan discipline. So why would people think he's simply a Caitiff as opposed to "clan unknown"? (Or maybe in Neon's case we should roleplay it like others simply think he's clan unknown until they realize it later.) One could say the same thing about Maldavis who shares Discipline combinations that could be assigned to multiple clans (other than the fact that as Caitiff she is presumably spending XP according to the Caitiff Discipline XP track, Carol could easily be mechanically what she is socially - an illegally made and abandoned Tremere who was never bonded to the Inner Circle and never taught Thaumaturgy). So is it a combination of their starting Disciplines and possibly what Disciplines their childer demonstrate? Is this something that may take years to determine?

    What do others think?

    I think this is important to figure out. For while some clans - like Ventrue or Toreador - may exclude such characters from their clan gatherings and social life until it becomes "obvious" who they are, there will also be clans - like Brujah or Gangrel - who will less exclusionary UNTIL they learn their real clan or if they are actually clanless.

    For my chronicle purposes, while my players and I know the PC is Caitiff, I'm thinking the NPCs might just simply put him into the category of "unknown clan, we'll figure it out later". At that point, some clans might be more accepting than others. Then at some point, the character is recognized as "Caitiff" and any social stigma associated with that really kicks in. "What is this PCs's actual clan?" might therefore be a real "mystery" in the chronicle for a while before the NPCs decide he truly is Caitiff.

    I'm still trying to decide how to play this out in game. I think everyone simply deciding he is Caitiff from the beginning is probably the most boring option, but interested in other people's opinions.

      So, there's a number of things to unpack, here.

      1- What defines a Caitiff is the lack of mystical ties with the clan. It is literally the game effects of having no Clan Weakness and no In-Clans, instead buying every Discipline at a middle-point cost;

      2- Most of the time, the social aspect overlaps with the mystical aspect. In short, Caitiff will almost always be vampires with unknown sire, not even a sire that watches them from afar. There are exceptions on both sides, though, a lot of vampires with unknown sires have Clan traits, so they're not Caitiff, and a few Caitiff have known sires, and even some were adequately raised by their sires, but for whatever reason it "didn't stick";

      3- Only a few clans and bloodlines really have the means to readily identify someone isn't from their clan, much less a Caitiff, and the Malkavian actually aren't among them. From the Clans, old and new, only the Nosferatu can objectively tell someone isn't from their clan without magic, and even then only before 5th edition, now you can't even tell the difference between a Nos and someone really ugly;

      4- The Tremere can divine someone's clan with magic, and only them can do so. Even other sorcerer vampires don't have access to this specific ritual. On the other hand, though, it is a rather common ritual among the Tremere and most chantries have at least one member that can cast it and offer as a service. On top of that, their hierarchy and practices mean that most Tremere have actually went through this very ritual, so passing as one of them isn't an easy task. It is doable, though;

      5- In the end, recognizing a Caitiff is a matter of long time observation, with a number of errors in the way. Most Caitiffs are "identified" simply by not having a known sire and not knowing anything at first about the vampiric society. If with time they consistently show the telltales of a given clan, they may be "uplifted" to full clan status, simply because calling them Caitiff was an error in the first place;

      6- As such, if a Caitiff tries to pass as a Clan vampire, they can actually pull the stunt for a long time, maybe forever, depending only on how well they perform.

          I think monteparnas has covered a lot of the 'How' questions. But I do think that there's a couple of interesting possibilities raised by the question that are worth exploring:

          1) It could be considered an error of judgment to use the word 'Caitiff' to refer to both a social/informational condition and a separate biological/physiological/??? condition. It's particularly noteworthy that, as mentioned above, social-Caitiff can eventually be linked to a Clan even if you never identify their sire, while a physiological-Caitiff can be such even when you know who their sire is.

          2) Is it possible that there is, in fact, a correlation and/or causal relationship between being a social-Caitiff and a physiological-Caitiff? It would make sense to use the same term for both if the two conditions often overlap, even if exceptions exist. It makes me wonder if something akin to a sympathetic connection influences the development of Clan traits. If I recall correctly, Clan traits generally develop over time after the Embrace, rather than immediately, which might support the idea that proximity to the sire might influence the expression of Clan traits. There would probably be other complicating factors, or else it would be a bit more obvious, but it would make sense how the two conditions would be conflated if there was a lot of overlap between them.

              As much as it important in the setting regardless of edition the creation of Caitiff never really were defined and set in stone
              A Caitiff is someone who does not belong to any established clan thus often referred to as "clanless".
              This can be due to a variety of reasons, including a flawed or unknown lineage, being abandoned by their sire, or their blood being too weak to manifest clan-specific traits.
              The vast majority of Caitiff bear the term in a purely political sense and most of the Kindred's history, the terms "Caitiff" and "Thin-Blood" were used interchangeably
              A Caitiff Vitae contains the ties to a clan (a Brujah who leave their child behind would be still a Brujah logically), but due to their lineage being unclear for too long they never were able to establish themselves in the structure of a Clan, and therefore lack that Clan's support.
              It's a rare few who end up without any particularity to their Vitae, who are truly Clanless.
              As far as those rare few go, the truth about becoming a Caitiff is that no one really knows how exactly it happens.
              The process of becoming a Vampire is not instantaneous, for example Ventrue can take a few weeks for their feeding preference to solidify, and Nosferatu can take several days to even weeks for their physical transformation to complete and even the Malkavian maddness not instantly obvious or kicks in.
              If a Vampire goes too long without learning of their Sire's Clan, sometimes it causes them not to develop the particularities of the Vitae that should've come with a Clan. Sometimes, though, even with their lineage being clear, sometimes the Vitae just does that, and you just end up a Caitiff for seemingly no real reason whatsoever.
              So we have two types of Caitiff: social outcast and physical/spiritual/Mysical anomalies.

              This presents an interesting situation:
              If a Sire with a Clan abbandons its Child at Embrace that child became a Caitiff and despite her Sire had a clan, the lack of interaction barrs them to develop a "Clan"?
              Good question
              And most of the time the answer is a yes... no... maybe?

              It seems most of the time the Child inherite some "residue" Clan traits, or will become a Child with all the inhertiance of a Clan/blodline (Clan flaws, disciplines, etc) or will be a blank slate - or sometims branch out to be a Bloodline with an affinity to a certain Discipline and/or a falw of thei own
              I think Dr. Douglas Netchurch made some experiments on this, like switching two freshly Embraced Child's Sire, and observed their developement and their affinites and so on

              Generally speaking it seem having a Sire around who teach you and simply be around of him and your Clan molds the Child to be the member of the Clan, helps the Vitae to develope so to speak, akin to a mystical resonance that tunes up the Vitae to a certain (eg. Clan) "frequency"
              Yet there are many exaples this not really happens, like the Gangrel who not really keep an eye on their Child or very rarely watching them from a distance, yet they are Gangrel from day 1

              So in this case the main question is: what kind of Caitiff we are talking about?
              Social or mystical?
              And build from up there in my humble oppinion

                  Originally posted by Shadeprowler View Post
                  most of the Kindred's history, the terms "Caitiff" and "Thin-Blood" were used interchangeably
                  That's not completely true.

                  While there's a strong correlation between Caitiff and "Thin-Bloods", it isn't direct, 1:1. They overlap a lot, but both can also happen in isolation, and that has always been the case.

                  It's also curious that both always existed in Kindred society, focusing on the highest commonly occurring Generation at the time. There was a time when 6th Gen vampires were regularly Caitiff and/or Thin-Bloods. It's unclear if they just died out since them, or changed to normal vampires over time. What can be said for sure that also differentiates them is that Thin-Bloods completely stop occurring at a given Generation as new Generations arrive, while Caitiff can still exist at any Generation, they're just rarer in the lower ones.

                  Also, and that was my point in my post, while Kharnov and Shadeprowler are right that there are "social" and "mystical" Caitiff, the definition itself is only for the mystical. Social Caitiffs come more from the suspicion that someone is a Caitiff. Sure, not every vampire has a firm grasp on this definition, and on many Domains the very fact that you can't say who's your sire is a problem regardless. But the whole idea behind calling someone a Caitiff is the supposition that they don't carry the signs of their clan.

                  Once a Caitiff is knowledgeable enough to pass as a clan vampire, they would know if they're actually from a clan, just with unknown sire, and at this point assuming this clan isn't wrong (or hard) even if they don't know their sire. So the very question as presented only makes sense for a "mystical" Caitiff anyway.

                                                  Anyways, the basic introduction was important because of a) status and b) paranoia for spies. If it's a known Sire, verification was as simple as writing a brief letter informing them that their Childe X had arrived in the Domain. If it's an unknown Sire, you could at least politely ask in their previous Domain. Their line is unknown, destroyed, or anything seems off (Auspex...)? Their status in the Domain is in doubt until someone verifies their story- that's usually the point where the Tremere came in for paranoid Seneschals or Princes. Impolite, maybe, but better than accepting a potential spy.

                                                  Overall, most Caitiff that pulled through with posing as Clan members had to rely on a careful house of cards backstory that could collapse at any moment. It got easier once they established themselves in the new Domain, but the chance of a visitor pointing out "that illegitimate Childe" to the Harpy was still there.

                                                      I've always regarding being a Caitiff as being almost entirely political. It would be extremely rare for another vampire to truly know the Clan of a vampire when it isn't obvious like the Nosferatu. Some probably can, especially older Caitiff who have had time to establish some contacts. But for young Caitiff, they likely don't have the connections to do so and they aren't going to find a Clan to vouch for them because of the Tradition of Progeny. If an established vampire claims a Caitiff as their childe, they become subject to execution for violating the Tradition of Progeny. Why take that risk for some Caitiff?
