        Originally posted by Elphilm View Post
        My problem with this take is that in order to hype up Achilli's supposed contributions, you have to build up a complete caricature of The Players Guide to the Sabbat that most people who have read the book will, I assume, recognize as a caricature. Nowhere in the book does it state that 100% of the Sabbat are Path followers. It explicitly states otherwise, saying that a minority of vampires survive the Creation Rites with their Humanity intact. The book only forbids characters with Humanity from starting the game with a rating higher than 5 (before freebie points), to reflect the trauma of their creation and the fact that simply running with the Sabbat takes its toll on the character's previous values (pages 59-60).

        More importantly, the Sabbat of 2nd Edition are supposed to be a much smaller sect than the Camarilla. They are the elites, the products of not just the Creation Rites but of continual indoctrination through years of brutality and atrocity. It's not the sect for every Tom, Dick, and Harry, because those people fell by the wayside long before they could ever become Path followers. To me it seems that everything you say Revised Edition did to improve the Sabbat is already there in the original 2nd Edition depiction, and all Revised managed to do was bloat the numbers of the sect with additional low-Humanity goons. How is that supposed to make the Sabbat more interesting and compelling?
        I feel like the discussion here is the assumption the PLAYERS GUIDE TO THE SABBAT is better than the GUIDE TO THE SABBAT. I feel like the Sabbat benefited from being a "Core" part of the experience versus a supplemental one and that Revised was designed with the idea that the Sabbat were one of the pillars of the setting alongside Anarchs and Camarilla.

        I certainly prefer the Revised Sabbat and think part of the issue may be here that I'm a fan of the Sabbat that is very different from the one you're promoting.

        Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

                                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                I think a more fair reading is that Justin doesn't like what the Sabbat became in the hands of people playing the game. He likes his vision of the Sabbat just fine. That doesn't mean he likes what the Sabbat is to everyone else.
                                Yeah, I remember being on the old White Wolf mailing list back in the late 90's and very early 2000's, and Justin would occasionally post. He liked a lot of aspects of the World of Darkness but didn't like what players did with them. Especially in regards to the Sabbat, he was always unhappy with the way those games nearly always turned into "trenchcoats and katanas" and personal horror was totally absent.

                                That said, I don't think Justin would have personally wanted to wall off the Sabbat as PCs, because he always made it clear that even if people were having fun with things in ways he didn't care about, if they were having fun that was the most important thing. So I have to imagine that the, "No Sabbat player characters" decision came down from higher up at Paradox, and Justin's response was to include a bunch of extensive rules so that players could still play the Sabbat regardless, fulfilling the "no Sabbat player characters" mandate while still providing enough material for people who really wanted to play them.

                                But who knows. Vt1 came out almost 30 years ago, and things change. Maybe Justin does look back and think that, in retrospect, playable Sabbat were a major mistake, but if that was the case he probably wouldn't have included extensive rules for people who really wanted to play Sabbat. Ultimately having a book with systems and rules for Sabbat PCs along with a note that says, "No Sabbat PCs" seems more like his way to comply with a mandate to not allow Sabbat PCs than something he chose to write because he doesn't want to see Sabbat PCs in any way whatsoever. If that was the case it would have been easier to have no rules or anything that people could use as PC options.

                                                    It's really not that hard (esp. in the video game and TV/movie arenas) to make something that can turn a profit on some level; at least if you have the starting capital. It's a lot hard to actually make something people go back and play/watch/read again. To make things that people tell their friends about and try to get them into as well. To make things people will pay full price for because of how much they enjoyed the previous game and not wait until it's on sale on Steam for 75% off.

                                                    Getting fans isn't the same thing as making sales. The point of cultivating fans, is because they drive sales. It's a tight line to walk, because companies don't want to cede full control to the fans, but balancing keeping old fans with attracting new ones is important. Just betting on new fans has a very bad track record. Of course so does just focusing on old ones. Hence the need to make sure you're working to satisfy both.

                                                    2) There's also plenty of people that have said the V5 Sabbat announcement was a deal breaker for them. Complainers or not, people that were buying V5 stuff, have said they're not buying this V5 thing, or done with V5 completely over this. I own V1 through V20, with the majority of the supplements, both editions of DA, and Victorian. Vampire is one of my least favorite WoD games and I have more books for it than I have for dozens of RPGs I like better. I have both VtM video games and I've played them within the last year. I once owned the DVD set of the old TV show.

I can think of at least two dozen people that know I'm a big WoD fan, and asked me about V5 because they heard about it, and my negativity towards it convinced them to skip it too. Are 25 sales going to kill Paradox's bottom line. No. But do you think I'm alone? Is banking on all those potential fans a good idea if you're pissing off the hardcore ones that already exist?
