    I don’t mind Caine all that much.

    One of the aspects in Judeo-Christian cosmology is a human soul with free will (i.e. the image and likeness of God) that animals lack (animals have no choice but to follow their nature).

    Similarly, the Catholic Church holds that one of the means by which God could have chosen to create humankind was by setting up the process of evolution and then, at the proper time, breathing a divine soul into one of our ancestors. We know from genetic research that a mitochondrial Eve from which we all descended existed c. 150-200k years ago.

    So who’s to say that “Eve” and her mate (called Adam for convenience) couldn’t be the point when God stepped in and finished His creation of humankind by adding free-willed souls to them (if you want to incorporate the Mage cosmology it could be that Adam, Lilith and then Eve were the first to Awaken).

    One of the key elements of murder (vs. a justifiable killing; the proper translation of the Commandment is “Thou shalt not murder.”) is free will and intent. Before the addition of a soul, our ancestors lacked the free will to go against their natures; they may have killed others, but lacking intent it couldn’t be considered murder any more than one wolf killing another to secure food or a mate would be considered murder.

    One of Adam and Eve’s descendants though (call him Caine for convenience) used their free will to deliberately end the life of one of their kin (I use “descendant” and “kin” in place of son and brother because very primitive tribes rarely distinguished generations of ancestors from each other, nor siblings from cousins). This would be the first actual murder.

    Per the Book of Nod, Caine fled to a land where he found Lilith and, one botched attempt at Awakening later, we’ve got the first vampire. The key point here though is that after leaving Lilith he didn’t actually return to realms of men, he first spent an “eon” alone.

    Given the prose it’s unlikely it refers to the recent astronomical usage and is instead, as originally intended, an indeterminately long time; likely well over 100k years, probably slipping in and out of torpor until, sometime in the Neolithic period, he arrived in the Middle East and established a community on the shores of what was then the freshwater Black Sea.

    Many scholars believe the flood narrative is a remembrance (filtered through untold generations of oral history) of when the Black Sea flooded due to post Ice Age sea levels rising by more than a hundred meters c. 5500 BCE over the course of about a year.

    That would put the Second City in the late Neolithic with the collapse and subsequent diaspora possibly coinciding with the dawn of the Copper/Bronze Age.

                        Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                        Abel worked out agriculture, god was delighted as this would bring humanity closer to making cities. Caine killed Abel, either because he liked life as it was, or to spite the god that had trapped him.
                        Actually, Abel was the shepherd and Cain the farmer. After Cain was banished according to tradition he became the forefather of the Canaanites.

                        Historically, the Hebrews were shepherds in conflict with the agriculture-based Canaanites and so Cain and Abel can be taken as an allegory for these conflicts, particularly as justification for why the Hebrews were justified in driving the Canaanites out of the Holy Land.

                        The funniest part for me if we’re crossing the splats though is the notion that Caine isn’t even a vampire. Lilith’s botched effort to Awaken him caused Cain to go Marauder and create his own personal reality buffer where he’s punishing himself eternally for all his sins... denied the community that fire provided, the light of the sun, able to sustain himself only on blood. This is why he could invent disciplines on the fly. Later he learned how to inflict a static version of his curse on others that was inserted into the consensus early enough that it’s a permanent mythic thread which allows those suffering his curse to exist.

                        But back on the topic at hand... I like the concept of generations because it does allow for “elder evils” that only a group can hope to defeat; but also because of the one exception that sorta throws everything into question; Dhampirs are mechanically stronger than their 15th Gen parents. They can use all 10 self-regenerating blood points, can soak lethal damage with Stamina, are immune to sunlight and all aggravated damage (including fire) can be healed as if it were lethal. Sure, they’re limited to one dot in disciplines, but there’s a LOT you can do with those first dots and they don’t have to pay double blood for any that use it either.

                        Basically, nature went and came up with something stronger than all the blood sorcery of the ancients ever could (i.e. Revenants); likely due to the concept of hybrid vigor (basically the opposite of Revenant inbreeding).

                        I’ve sometimes toyed with the idea that the Last Daughter of Eve would be a Dhampir who becomes a New Eve to the hybrid generations which continue on after Gehenna.

                                                Getting a little bit back to the main topic, one thing that would be nice is if Generation was a little more of a trade off.

                                                We only really see it with the 15/16th Gen that they only take lethal damage from sunlight, can hold down food without a merit and occasional bouts of biological function. But I think it’d be interesting if it were a little more gradated.

                                                For example, say Gens 7- gets those rank 6+ disciplines and other traits, but Gens 8-14 can soak sunlight with Stamina (its still aggravated so you really can’t stand out in it, but incidental indirect sunlight because a curtain gets moved might only result in a stinging sensation and some hissing instead of needing 24 hours and 5 blood points).

                                                Maybe gens 10-14 can hold down drink (possibly soup) for an hour.

                                                Maybe there’s a sliding difficulty scale on the Beast where those of 7- have +1 diff to resist Frenzy while those 10-13 are -1 difficulty and 14-16 are -2 difficulty.

                                                Basically you’re lacking in vampiric power, but are ever so slightly more human with each step away from Caine you take until it culminates with Dhampirs as actual live births from those vampires whose curse is so weak they can’t even pass it on through the Embrace.
