    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    Another option is to give up on making VtM/WoD about "now" and just have the default setting be the tail end of 1999. While that's 25 years ago from now, it is both a fairly pivotal era in modern history, and we have a lot more insight into what's going to matter now. It plays into the nostalgia of the WoD's original run (and it would hardly be the first time present media explores the past), with foresight into how to direct the games to what will still be relevant later.

    It also gives you lots of freedom to branch out in lots of directions and thus unify the player base. Does Y2K turn out to be a huge deal and the WoD explodes into a gonzo dark fantasy? There can be books exploring that. Does the WoD continue to mirror own our fairly closely as the powers that be ride the ups and downs of human history as they always have? Sure, explore that. Does the upheavals in human society lead to upheavals in supernatural societies that forces them to adapt to new eras? Do that too. This also drastically frees up the brand for different media, because anything after 1999 becomes a self-contained possible future for the WoD; whether is be one book, or a series of video games, or whatever.
    I like this idea a lot, actually

    Basically, reset it to the status quo of the Revised Edition corebook and have the more contentious metaplot issues like Independent Gangrel, the Week of Nightmares, Ur-Shulgi and the Assamite Schism (tbh, I refuse to use the term "Banu Haqim", fuck that V5 shit) and so forth be treated as having just plain not happened (but nothing prevents an ST from incorporating them into their own games)

    V20 took this approach when it first launched and I'd say the "metaplot agnostic" approach that Parawolf seems to loathe so much is a major key for why it was so successful. All the prior editions and their commonly associated styles were given a seat at the table and treated equally and fairly.

    Though whereas V20 simply took back the most egregious decisions of the Achilli regime by acting like they never happened and just updated the setting to the 2010's, a good V6 would be an explicit late 90's/early 2000's period piece.

    Given the popularity of the "Y2K Aesthetic" online and how it's popular for both nostalgic Gen X and Millennials as well as Zoomers and Gen Alpha kids who just find that era aesthetically cool compared to the bland corporate hipster minimalist aesthetics of today, acting like V5 never happened and just making V6 a "Y2K Era" period piece might be the best decision.

    Just go all out, revert back to old names and old settings, make it an explicit period piece and everything. Go the extra mile to lean into that late 90's/early 2000's edge and "Rule of Cool" mindset. Go full unironic "trenchcoats & katanas" mall goth.

    Have the metaplot stuff from Revised, Beckett's Jyhad Diary, and V5 be treated as potential alternate settings or options for ST's to use (alongside other plot hooks) as opposed to hard setting facts.

    I'm just a trans guy and gaming fan, unabashed hedonist and unusually upbeat nihilist, fairly laid back. He/Him.

    If everything is political, then nothing is!

        I believe that the different metaplots and the need to keep each splat both unique, yet still connected, is something that World of Darkness was groundbreaking for. Other game lines had brought metaplot to the forefront (Battletech, Shadowrun, Cyberpunk, to name a few), but none had the ambition for the variety of stories that the WOD had.

        I think it failed miserably on an objective level but that failure SUBJECTIVELY made the original World of Darkness so beloved to so many and created the interesting culture that surrounds it to this day.

        The COMBINED World of Darkness is a mess. Even the original game lines in and of themselves were a nightmare when it came to cohesiveness, balance, and consistency. Vampire, being the oldest, definitely evolved the most, having to integrate each new paradigm shift brought on by a new splat. As the most popular, it had to be relevant with each new game and also evolve within itself to sell more books. This patchwork of contradictions, errors, and logical backflips is a gold mine for an internet subculture to dig into and analyze, hypothesize, and argue about. It is what killed an objectively more cohesive and consistent successor (nWOD/COD) and created the "generation gap" between 1e-20Ae lore/metaplot fans and the current developers because 5e is taking a very different approach to lore, metaplot, and interaction between the splats.

        The thing is, I'm not sure what you could do now with the World of Darkness as it existed up through the end of Revised. It had, developmentally, reached the end of its lifespan. Mechanically or conceptually, there was nothing new in its future, just the perpetuation of the metaplot. It would become akin to the Rolling Stones: chugging along decades after relevance performing "greatest hits" tours. There's nothing wrong with that but it's artistically stagnant. Hanging out in newer gaming spaces, I am realizing that the kids playing 5e are playing a very different game than what I played in 1991 by candlelight with Joy Division playing in the background. The game has moved on from us old-heads and I am here for it. I never would have thought about some of the directions the game is going these days and exploring them has been great.

        When we say "this is what Paradox should do," we're old men yelling at clouds. Paradox is going to do what they think will make them money and what we think is irrelevant to that. I am perfectly content to be the old man standing in the back of the punk rock show, only half understanding what these kids are doing these days but loving the energy.

            Vampire didn't really change shit in response to the other games. It had funhouse, theme-park versions of the other games' characters as antagonists in the back that stayed more or less the same for the entire run. The other games, meanwhile, had their own themes and matured in different directions. Werewolf discarded most notions of Personal Horror in exchange for Splatter. Mage flat out refused to engage with their existence as horrific and declared it wondrous. Wraith went in even harder. Changeling was maudlin but still hopeful - approaching things from an angle similar to Mage, but more maudlin.

            The reason why the "whole line" was a mess was because you weren't supposed to throw everything together; the books really did not like it and the character types clashes with each other fiercely, with a few exceptions (ghosts can fit in the background of anything, changelings can get along somewhat well with werewolves and other werebeasts).

            And if 5e changed anything, it's to being a stifling, One True Way as canon.

                    Don't get me wrong. I really like the idea of setting Vampire in the 90s. It's a Chronicle idea I've wanted to do for a while now, where the game starts in 1991 (the year Vampire was released) and going forward from there. I think it would be fun, and a way to sort of dig into the entire vibe of the 90s with the benefit of hindsight.

                    That said, I can't in good conscience sign off on making the 90s the default era of World of Darkness. Simply because the default should be "Current Year", as a jumping on point for new players. There would need to be a good reason to set a game specifically outside the present. Call of Cthulhu, by default, takes place in the same general era as Lovecraft's stories, for genre consistency. Same with Ars Magica, which heavily relies on the conceit of exploring medieval society and its esoteric worldview.

                    Whereas while the World of Darkness is an extremely 90s setting, it has grown and changed with the times. It WAS set in Current Year when it came out, and at least tried to be "Modern". Always has been. Dips into earlier eras explored both those eras, and the timelessness of the vampire (and human) experience.

                    If anything, there should definitely be a VtM supplement that specifically examines the 90s as a milieu in and of itself. Much like how New Wave Requiem was a sourcebook for running Vampire: The Requiem in the 80s. Have the 90s setting exist alongside Dark Ages Vampire and Victorian Age Vampire. The main game would explore how vampires work "now", while the supplement(s) cover(s) how vampires work in the age of katanas & trenchcoats.
