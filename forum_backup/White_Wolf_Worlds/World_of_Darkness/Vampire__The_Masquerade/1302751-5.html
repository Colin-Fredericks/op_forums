                        Something that is on a different axis from the "1 to 10" vs "4 to 6" issue though, is that the Garou have, for no direct XP costs, access to an nearly Mage-like level of versatility in three common Rites: Binding, Spirit Awakening, and Fetish. Garou having very cheap, and very early, access to magical items and no mechanical hurdles on what type of effects these items can do (aka mages needing Mind and Prime to make an amulet to protect them from mental Disciplines). While starting level Fetish crafters is pretty hard to pull off unless you take one of the Totems that boosts that, a starting level Talen crafting Theurge is barely a blip on starting character resources. The ability to pump out single use magical items of effects limited only by imagination and ST judgement is something to keep in consideration.

                        While it might reduce the raw violence of the earlier starting level rawr-smash Garou if they have to make these things themselves, the ability to take the (small i on purpose) initiative from vampires in how much you can get out of preparation would vastly outdo a few less raw damage per hit in application.

                            I just reread the original post that started this thread. When does a combat-oriented vampire begin to match werewolves.
                            I appreciate that comparison-wise it is easier to compare starting characters, but I think the question asked by the thread's originator would not be answered satisfactorily in this way. Choosing a certain amount of experience points and giving it to both a vampire and a garou seems better.

                            That being said, without even needing to do those maths, something jumps to my attention: a combat-oriented vampire is one who invests most of his/her xp in Potence, Celerity, Fortitude, at least one combat ability (Brawl, Melee, Firearms) and physical attributes + generation (at start or via diablerie, since it can't be purchased with xp but changes greatly a character's martial prowess through ease of physical attributes boost). Such a vampire would usually manage to fulfill his blood and social needs with minimum further investment in complementary traits, since martial prowess can ensure patronage as a bodyguard of a socially powerful vampire or something similar or as tank for a coterie or underling for the sheriff. Some xp can go in Auspex, Obfuscate or Protean for additional rounding and versatility, though claws are not necessary at all: Potence ensures holds hold and Celerity ensures one can grapple with their opponent and bite him for aggravated damage in the same turn. Lethal weapons are also important to send enemies in torpor. A vampire will do all this and be a functional member of vampire society, and each and every of these dots will benefit him/her also in an encounter with Werewolves.
                            By contrast, a combat oriented Garou, in terms of sheer physical attributes and related gifts, eventually falls behind such a vampire, and - and this is key - in order to remain functional in garou society, he/she has to buy other things with xp that have no or little use in a fight with a vampire because vampires are not the garou's only enemies. I think this makes a big difference, confirming what's already been said: young garou eat vampires, but old vampires eat garou.

                            Of course, a garou specialised against vampires can buy many gifts and things to utterly incinerate them, but that's not nearly as common in garou society, just like vampires rarely specilalise in dealing with garou.
