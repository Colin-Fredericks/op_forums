    At what point do you get called a Caitiff?

    06-25-2020, 02:26 PM
    So you arrive in a new city. You are recently embraced and do not know your sire. But if you frenzy you get animal features.
    Or as above, but you are hideously ugly and deformed with a zero appearance. Et cetera. Derangement, fugue state in the presence of beauty, limited feeding preference, painful bite, casts no reflection.

    At what point are you obviously a Gangrel etc with an unknown sire, and where do you get called "Caitiff"? Is it if your clan considers it close enough?

    And in reverse, if you are not particularly good with your clan disciplines and don't frenzy that much, but know your Brujah sire, do the other Brujah really care? The Brujah tend to deny that they have a frenzy problem, they are the most varied clan, and they have occasionally adopted Caitiff.

              Game-wise, you are a Caitiff if that's what says your character sheet.

              In-setting, a Caitiff depends widely from the environment.

              - Not knowing you sire doesn't mean you are automatically a Caitiff, 'cause that's what the Gangrel usually do, and most of them end up Gangrel, not Caitiff.

              - On the other end, many Caitiff are born from Brujah, Ventrue and Toreador that Embrace on the spur of the moment, sometime the Childe ends up a proper Kindred, mostly a Caitiff. A Clanbook (I don't remember which, probably the Brujah one) said that being Caitiff or not is about knowing the lore of your Clan.

              - OR you're Caitiff if you can't perfectly point out the whole line of your ancestor, name by name (or so said another Clanbook, probably the Ventrue one)

              - But then, we have Bloodlines, that are born basically from Caitiff that are able to propagate some kind of weirdness (included proprietary Disciplines, of course). These are Caitiff too, until they get enough power to make people afraid of calling them with that name.


              So, it all comes down to:
              If most of Kindred think you're Caitiff, you are a Caitiff, and you have no say in the matter. Until you do.

                      But that's still dependent on the environment.
                      Out of the character sheet, "Caitiff" is only an word that (like "thin blood") is used to define the bottom-feeders of the Kindred society.
                      For some Elders, for example, all the members of the bloodlines are Caitiff.
                      For others all the thin bloods are Caitiff too (and "thin blood" often depends on your generation, not really if your character is a thin blood).
                      Kindred aren't required to be fair. If they can leverage on your (lack of) power to have an edge on anything, they'll do.
                      And if this means calling you a Caitiff because there's any reason to call you that, they'll do it.
                      It's an elastic concept that reflects the elitism of the setting you are in.
                      (So you can expect that the exact definition changes between a city and the other)

                          I think originally Caitiff was a term used when no other vampire claimed that this vampire was part of their claim. It was truly used for exiled or abandoned childer, or outright outcasts. In these cases though, the vampires are not mechanically different from their clans.

                          Later, the term began to be used for mechanical Caitiff that lacked the Clan Weaknesses and XP mechanics of the Clan Disciplines. The former is just a social distinction. The latter is something like learning your child has an atrocious birth defect that will prevent it from ever entering and living in society.

                          The term still gets used in both cases so it can be confusing. However, I think the first case is quite clear. In the second case, such childer are declared Caitiff when their sires (or some other "expert" in the clan) makes the determination that they do indeed lack the traits expected of their clan. In that case, it is like a father learning (or at least believing) that their child is actually the child of some other father and their wife had cheated on them. There is probably a time of increasing suspicion until the father finally has (or at least believes to have) proof.

                              In a social sense you are called Caitiff until your clan is known to the society. Clans are more or less able to tell other clans by their weaknesses, at least the more obvious weaknesses.

                              From a mechanical sense, caitiffs don't technically have a clan. They do not suffer from clan weaknesses nor do they gain discipline benefits. Everything comes at an out of clan cost.

                                  Ways You Can Wind Up A Caitiff
                                  • You don't know the clan of your ancestors or deny a heritage you do know, and no one else is able to make a good guess at it.
                                  • A clan admits one of their members embraced you, but they have rejected you and declared you Caitiff.
                                  • A clan admits one of their members embraced you, but you have rejected them and declared yourself Caitiff.

                                  *Note, that any of the above options work whether you character sheet says "Caitiff" or if it lists an actual clan. Admittedly, it is less likely if you are mechanically a member of certain "visible minority" clans, such as Gangrel and Nosferatu.

                                  Ways You Wind Up Not A Caitiff
                                  • Your sire (and/or their clan-mates) has clan membership and publicly acknowledges you are a member of the clan. Your sire need not be happy they embraced you, they just need to admit it.
                                  • Someone with some amount of status, usually a primogen or better, says they know your lineage and vouches for you. This usually makes you a member of their clan, but they might have witnessed another clan embracing you. Bribery is an option.
                                  • Often the "vouching for" scenario results from a Tremere blood test. Very expensive bribery might be an option.
                                  • You declare yourself a member of a clan, seem to roughly match what people expect of that clan, and no one in the clan bothers to challenge your claim.
                                  • You don't declare yourself a member of a clan, but so closely match what people expect of a clan they just assume your are a member of that clan.
                                  • You declare yourself a member of a bloodline (real or fictitious!), and people in power accept your claim. Remember, the Prince can't just log onto "ObscureBloodlines.com/membership_records" to check your story.

                                  **Note, that any of the above options work whether your character sheet says "Caitiff", or if it lists an actual clan. Admittedly, some are more likely if you are mechanically a member of certain "visible minority" clans, such as Gangrel and Nosferatu.
