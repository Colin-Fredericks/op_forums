    I run a V20 Chronicle set in contemporary times, which takes sporadic direction from V5. No Beckoning, but the Brujah and Gangrel have left the Camarilla. The Anarch Free State controls the cities of Southern California, and a few other American cities like Las Vegas and Indianapolis have become new Free States in the last couple of years. Nevertheless, the Camarilla is more concerned about the Sabbat, with the Sect War raging along the East Coast. The opinion of the Justicars and most Camarilla Elders is that the Anarchs can wait to be dealt with; the loss of a few minor domains out West is no immediate concern to the powerful Elders of Chicago and NYC. For the Camarilla in the West however, the Anarchs are no longer a nuisance to be laughed off. What they lack in organization they make up for in numbers, and recent victories have given them a renewed sense of confidence.

    Fortunately(?), the Anarchs spend as much--really more--time jockying and feuding with each other as they do focusing on the Ivory Tower. Portland, OR, a relatively small city (pop 650k, metro pop 2.6 million, the setting of my chronicle) is home to over 30 Anarchs, divided into at least four gangs. The largest of these gangs has become a miniature Camarilla in its own right, with a Baron who has established a court and holds the licks in her Domain to a stringent set of behavior. Other gangs occupy the ragged edges of the region They hold to a bewildering variety of philosophies and beliefs--various kinds of blood cults, worshipping an Antediluvian/Lilith, filtered through new age or pagan spirituality. While all the Anarchs have nominally promised to aid each other in the face of any aggression from the Camarilla, the smaller gangs would love nothing more than to knock the largest from their pedestal. Not all these Unbound are true believers in The Movement, many are simply Unbound with beliefs or philosophies at odds with rules of the Camarilla (which in my setting, still officially denies the truth of Noddism or existence of so-called Antediluvians).

    tl;dr - My Anarchs are a grab-bag of all kinds of Kindred. They're numerous, powerful and have the Camarilla on the backfoot at least on the West Coast, but there's little to no real unity among them. Attempting any sort of organization across the board would be like herding cats, and requires dealing with types ranging from esoteric blood cults to rambunctious bloodbangers no better than shovelheads.

        Previous ed anarchs were disenfranchised rebels against an unjust system pretty interesting dynamic at a local level

        V5 anarchs......[bites lip]


        V5 Anarchs are founded on the idea of a soft reset back to 1st ed Anarchs vs the 'the man' as the primary conflict of the setting this is either a corporate decision from a company who release buggy overpriced dlc and game breaking patches on a regular basis or the pet obessions of the designers who seemed dismissive how the setting and game developed over 20 odd years. This hit critical mass with Anarch book which is effectively 20 pages stretched to 200 and a irl representation of why exactly the Sabbat and Carmarilla quickly stole the spotlight. this is a real problem as for the supposed protagonists the Anarchs are crimminally undeverloped vs the antagonists. With this in mind I've done a large scale re-write of the Anarchs to make them a viable threat to the Carmarilla and the Sabbat. Here are the big changes I implimented.

        1) The Anarchs form of governance is direct democracy on a local level with the Assembly forming a parliment of sorts, this allows a meaningful distinction in how the sect operates vs the Carmarilla, the Baron is closer to a magistrate/style style figure whose obligation is to engage in day to day sect bullshit but is accountable to the peoples Assembly. The relatively smaller numbers of kindred make this far more viable and yes I am aware it will be a shitshow. their is a talk of representative democracy as a pan-city level but this is in early development

        2)anarchs have a mixture of Civil liberties and obligations

        Liberties
        -Total freedom of speech outside of the advocation of treason or murder of sect mates
        -Right to disciplines, no clan may hold monopolies on disciplines... learning them is another issue
        -freedom of bloodline/freedom from bloodline, all clans are welcome in the sect and no vampire is expected to show clan loyalty if they do not wish to.
        -Right to life no sectmate may murder another, execution must be sanctioned by the Baron and/or the Assembly
        -Partial freedom of embrace, Vampires may embrace who they wish however bad embraces is a crime andwill result in censorship or death if found guilty by the Assembly
        -Freedom of religion, Vampire may follow paths and alternative religious perspective unless explicitly prohibited by the local assembly.

        Obligations
        -All Vampires must defend the free states in times of war.
        -Freedom from dominion-No Vampire may control more than 10% of a cities territory as domain, no more than 50% of a city may be privatly held by kindred. Property rights are however respected and haven is sacrosanct
        -All other sects are enemies of the Anarch revolution any vampire engaging or aiding other sects is a traitor to the people. the Hecata are considered a neutral 3rd party and are formally invited to join the Anarch cause but no aid may be provided them at the expense of sectmates.
        -All Anarchs must serve at least once a year in the Assembly, additional services are expected.


        3)Anarchs have a Pagan vibe in contrast to the Catholic Sabbat and the Secular Carmarilla (I'm scraping the faith stuff in V5 its stupid and does nothing), while not a legal issue their is a strong cultural bias from the three major clans as well as dissident bloodlines, the only exceptions is a strong contingent of Lilith defectors from the Sabbat who obviously follow a weird abrahamic heresy and a strong gnostic minority/

        4)Anarchs as a sect don't really mesh with human politics, they might have some overlaps of interest but no irl group can really present their concerns-for example while Anarchs are anti police it's mostly so they can get away with killing, stealing from and exploiting humans unapposed by armed proffesionals. General sect policy towards humans is 'animal wellfare' were utilization/exploitation is norm but brutalization and cruelty is discouraged

        5)Brujah, Setite and Gangrel Eldars/anchille are a prominant group in the sect with them being responsable for a lot of the strutural organization, The dynamic is generally less toxic than Camarilla.

        6)Anarch success is currently due to the descimation of the Sabbat and Carmarilla in the 2nd inquisition. Effectively the sect has often found itself winning by default. As the 2 inquisitions is slowly dying down. The sect is very likely to come under increasing pressure from attempts to re-take lost cities by both sect.

        7)Masquarade is taken very seriously, a lot of mid 10's sect set backs were due to inquisition purges. Now the sect has a specific coverup culture and titles for that duty.

        Hope people find this useful.

            Cults

                        Originally posted by Chris24601 View Post
                        It really makes the 1:100k number feel like it was pulled out of the ether by a bunch of people who’d never lived in a big city (even a sleepy midwestern one like mine) and explored it’s nightlife and got all their ideas from reading about city life in the 90’s from books printed in the 80’s.
                        I think this is an extremely difficult position to argue for, given what is actually written in the early books. From the 2nd Edition core, page 30 (emphasis mine):

                        Some Kindred take no part in the society of the Damned, and maintain no connection with others of their kind. They are known as the Clanless, or the Caitiff. Cities are extremely large places and it is not difficult to isolate oneself from one's peers; indeed, it is said that the only Kindred who ever meet are the ones who wish to meet. However, sometimes even the most solitary vampires can be thrust into the politics of the day -- in times of need, the Caitiff are mercilessly rooted out and questioned.
                        The only Kindred who ever meet are the ones who wish to meet. This is one of the basic assumptions of the early edition setting. The impression I get of the original writers is that, rather than pulling numbers and ideas out of the ether with no real life experience with big cities, they took the concept of the Masquerade seriously. More seriously, I would argue, than the idea is treated in the later game line. The famous Kindred to mortal ratio of 1:100,000 -- which was always intended to apply to greater metropolitan areas, not city populations -- is one of the consequences of thinking through the implications of just how plausible the Masquerade would be if vampires really existed in our world.

                        I know that it is tempting to lower the ratio significantly to make room for all the political factions, cabals, secret societies, cliques, and conspiracies that the setting has since become inundated with. But this is exactly the kind of impulse that James_Willoughby called out in another recent thread: "Writing about the game world has slipped from concrete work on specific places, characters, and experiences, to generalised writing about clans." Personally, I have a great dislike for this sort of approach, and I would seriously urge anyone populating a metropolitan area for their chronicle to consider how rare vampires really were supposed to be in the beginning. Think about specific characters, not Random Ventrue #7 that you need in order to fill out, say, the ridiculously bloated number of official positions within the Camarilla.

                        If it is true that the anarchs don't make sense as a faction in a world where the 1:100,000 ratio of Kindred to mortals is true, my preference would be to modify the idea of the anarchs instead of overpopulating the setting with faceless NPCs just to swell up the ranks of each faction.

One coiterie and on coiterie battle to the death would take out 10-15% of the city's whole vampire population; one heavy action adventure could wipe out half the vampires in a city in a night.

                                            Another issue is that my preferred GM style is a sandbox (i.e. set up a world with events going on and let the PCs find their own adventures within it via their own choices) so I design based on what the city needs for interesting stories to happen and having more than I need because the PCs won't find them all. Potential enemies need mooks. PCs need potential allies. My campaign started with more detailed NPCs than the chosen metropolitan area (4.8 million) could hold at the 1:100k ratio. By detailed I mean names, generations, natures/demeanors, virtues, humanity/path, willpower, disciplines, their resources and their desires.

                                            There was no room for meaningful Anarchs without lowering the ratio and the leaders of the Camarilla have no one to even boss around unless you lower it still further after that (what's the point of leaders without underlings) and every vampire on vampire fight to the death was going to be against a named and detailed NPC if I kept to 100k. Boston is also one of the oldest cities in the US (est. 1630) too so the idea that its not at least partially operating on pre-Industrial Revolution assumptions (the IR wouldn't kick off for another century and the Convention of Thorns hadn't even been dry for two centuries when the vampires started making Boston their home).

                                            1:100k is great if you're running a short duration campaign or one where most of the characters stand around talking like its a LARP. Once you throw any degree of recurring vampire on vampire violence into it you've got barely enough vampire antagonists for a two-hour action flick even in larger metro areas.

                                                    Originally posted by Chris24601 View Post
                                                    See, the issue I have is that, once you count the 5-6 PCs, there's almost no place that can have sufficient numbers for any meaningful politics. When your PCs account for an eighth of the ideal vampire population is a metro area as big as Boston's (4.8 million or 48 ideal vampires) there's just not enough to work with unless you go busting ratios. One coiterie and on coiterie battle to the death would take out 10-15% of the city's whole vampire population; one heavy action adventure could wipe out half the vampires in a city in a night.
                                                    This is a fine illustration of the various assumptions that different people bring to the game. I have always approached Vampire: The Masquerade with the premise that lethal violence against other vampires is, because of the way the Humanity rules work, always a Very Big Deal. Wiping out your opposition should be way down on the list of options for the coterie in just about every given situation. Vampires are simply not common enough to be staked and burned on a nightly basis, and no vampire struggling to maintain their Humanity would engage in such bloodshed. Moreoever, I try to never relegate actual vampires to the status of mooks. A vampire leader's mooks should not be other vampires. That's a role that ghouls should play.

                                                    So, yes -- if I were to do a Boston by Night chronicle, I would start with the assumption that there are around 50 Kindred in the Greater Boston area, plus all their non-Kindred lackeys, and work from there. What would the politics of the place look like? The number of vampires is probably not enough to support the kinds of conflict that you see in the largest population centers of the World of Darkness, but not every city in the US needs to look like Chicago by Night. That book was never supposed to be a template for how the rest of the World of Darkness worked, but an example of a failed, borderline overpopulated city on the brink of collapse.

                                                    My problem with busting open the ratio of vampires to mortals is that when it comes to the larger population centers of the world, the number of vampires is quickly inflated to ludicrous levels. Take one of the classic Sabbat strongholds of the setting, Mexico City. Even using the conservative 1:100,000 ratio, the Mexico City metropolitan area is populous enough to support at least 160 Cainites. How many detailed NPCs with individual writeups did the 2002 Mexico City by Night have? About forty. Even if you took all the NPCs from Mexico City by Night and put them together with the 100 characters from the original Chicago by Night, you would still not get to 160!

                                                    Using the Revised era suggestion of 1 Cainite for every 50,000 mortals in Sabbat-controlled cities makes the numbers grow even more out of control. The mind boggles at the chronicle where you would need to stat up 320 individual Sabbat vampires in Mexico City before you even begin play!
