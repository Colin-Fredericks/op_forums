                                Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                [USER="3046"]I think the problem with that argument is that the entire basis of the Sabbat is the belief that the Antediluvians can be slain if you rise up against them.
                                I don't think that's a problem. Certainly some Sabbat do think they killed Antedeluvians, and/or that they can be killed easily. It's just not all. Others think the antedeluvians can be killed, but it's much harder and gauge the Sabbat at various degrees of capability at being able to do so. And some remember the Anarch Revolt was never about the antedeluvians, and resent certain Sabbat's claims about it since it's just a pretext for turning the sect authoritarian instead of remaining true to its original aims.

                                There's room for lots of opinions about the antedeluvians, even in the Sabbat. Although I do think overall at this point in time, that the "official" view of the Sabbat leadership is that the antedeluvians exist and a threat, but the sect can kill them.

                                The issue isn't making one thing right or wrong, but having enough mystery so that any debating side can be seen as equally legitimate.

                                No, the antedeluvians do not exist, or are all dead. In any case, they are not a threat.
                                Yes, the antedeluvians exist. They are a threat, but we can kill them.
                                Yes, the antedeluvians exist. And we can do little about them. Once Gehenna arrives, God help us.

                                These are all existing claims in the setting. They are all mutually exclusive. Whatever the ST decides is actually correct in his own chronicles, I think it's best if the way the setting is presented IC, that players think each side could plausibly be correct. There shouldn't be enough information in anyone's specific chronicles that the players know which is correct (unless the point of that chronicle is for the PCs to discover the truth for themselves).

                                That's my preference and is how I run my games. Although every ST and player will have their own preferences.

                                    I had a big ass post
                                    Unfortunately, someone knocked my computer and it restarted. So I'll give the abridged version

                                    -The Tremere became more accessible in V5; you can play them in any sect and might have a good bit of leniancy as to your paradigm. The Hecata went the other direction; More bloodlines shoved in the naughty corner for necromancer-only games. They are less accessible, and because of such, it is less simple to play them. So whilst on the outside it might seem nice to "simplify" the Hecata, really all they've done is make them more difficult to actually use from at least a player perspective. The Bloodlines that formed the Hecata would have benefited more from a -breaking of the house- split.

                                    -From a story perspective, Augustus putting the Clan in the corner made sense, but he didn't need to do it to the degree he did, and the degree he did it to harms gameplay.

                                    -My ideal; Necromancers shouldn't have big clans; Much of necromancy has no real place in the modern era (the physical half of it absolutely renders the masquerade), and it is quite a solitary practice. and so it would be better if instead, Necromancers were a collection of Bloodlines, none of which is significantly more powerful than any other (Samedi and "The Bankers" are quite large, Flesh eaters and Capadocian survivors remain small but are likely to band together should the Bankers repeat their Capadocian cull.) These bloodlines tend to look to larger clans for patronage and representation: Clan Nosferatu is by far the biggest backer (most Necromancers are gutter pariahs after all), followed by Ventrue and Tzmisce. The Toreador in particular don't like necromancers and mock any among their number who patronizes them (Ironically, this leads to them needing such services often)

                                    (This assumes that, after the Giovanni wiped out the Capadocians, the other clans put the boot on their face. If we want this to be more 'canonical' then we can assume the Hecata will fall apart, as it reasonably should, and usher in this new age of necromancy)

                                                            Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                                            Counterpoint, if Jimmy Hoffa disappeared after a bunch of guys went into his castle where he knew he was located and killed everyone in the building, it would be a bit more credulous to believe them.

                                                            The big issue with Lasombra's death is that he was living like the Council of Seven in an enormous castle covered in Stygian darkness.

                                                            So it's like the Vienna attack.
                                                            I'll grant your counterpoint, but with a few addendums; Hypothetical castle-owning Jimmy Hoffa has vast power to access and utilize a shadowy pocket dimention, memory-altering powers, and who knows what else at his disposal. Lord Hoffa has survived for millennia, undoubtably facing assassination attempts during that time and having plans to avoid death that make paranoia seem tame and mundane. Oh, and of course multiple contradictory stories around how Sir James of Hoffalandia died, who was in the castle with Sire James or if there were even any guardians to begin with, and who exactly made up the attack force.

                                                            The idea behind [Lasombie] and [Tzim-Tzim] dying and leading to vast change was that things like this don't happen. It was unheard of, unbelievable, unexpected. And we see when the unbelievable, unheard of and unexpected happen in the real world, there are people who disbelieve. In both deaths, there are contradictory stories known in-universe. And both have clan-specifc disciplines that could easily allow for a falsified death in the pre-max level range. With in-universe characters having all that information, it's hardly surprising that there would be some who question if these deaths even occurred.
