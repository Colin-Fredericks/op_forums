    The Inquisition (part three)

    And our final two.

    The Quisling
    A vampire ghoul who infiltrated the Society at the behest of his master. We don't really get much in the way of backstory or the like, aside from that he was able to attract the Inquisition's attention by setting up one of his master's enemies. However, we do get a pretty interesting look at his inner psychology. Part of this is that he "willingly" became a ghoul out of love for his master, which ties in with what we were discussing earlier regarding how Presence can screw with matters of consent and free will among its victims. Also, and I presume this is a reflection of having to spend so much time away from the master, but one could also decide to interpret it as perhaps some sort of divine influence, he keeps having these internal doubts, wondering why Master seems so distant and doesn't seem to care much about his safety. I actually find this to be a nice touch, and in hindsight wish that one or more of the Ghoul templates had done this also. Mental is primary, all at three. I've no idea if Physicals or Socials are meant to be secondary, as at least two extra dots have been purchased with freebies, resulting in Dexterity, Stamina, and Manipulation all at three. Firearms is at four dots, and Brawl and Dodge are at three. Acting, Alertness, Athletics, Melee, Security, Bureaucracy and Computer are at two. I find myself wishing that there'd been a stronger focus on the abilities used for being a deep cover spy than the usual collection of combat ones. Stuff like Subterfuge, Investigation, or Stealth. No Numina, per say, but he has one dot of Potence. Backgrounds include two each in Allies, Contacts (Outside), and Resources, and one each in Mentor (Kindred) and Status. Humanity is seven and Willpower is four (the lowest in this book). Overall, this is a pretty interesting idea for an Inquisition character, and one I probably wouldn't have thought of on my own. My only real issues are that I wish I knew more about him as an individual instead of just as a ghoul, and that I wish his skills were a little different. (I'll also point out that the character sheet doesn't add up. With ten freebies spent on extra attributes, six on abilities, three on backgrounds, and none on Humanity or WP, that leaves all of two points for that dot of Potence. I'll also note that the art is particularly terrible, having very long hair flying straight up in the air and an arm that not only seems to reach past his knee, but also has an extra joint between the elbow and wrist.)

    The Young Turk
    So, if our earlier Gladius Dei aspirant was a fairly straight arrow, this is essentially that turned on its head. A high school drop out and petty criminal with a lifelong distain for authority. Then what sounds like it was probably a half-assed effort at gay bashing (it's written rather vaguely, but involves him and his friends hassling a "pretty boy" at a bar) ended with his friends killed by a vampire and him seeking sanctuary in a church. The priest allowed him to stay there for a few weeks and eventually talked him into joining the Society. Unfortunately, his distain for authority and discipline make him something of a problem child. So he's kind of got that whole Lone Wolf McEdgelord thing going on. Physicals, naturally, are primary, with an extra dot to bump Stamina up to four. Mental is secondary, with Wits also at three. His abilities are pretty combat focused, but ironically a little more restrained than some of the others. Alertness, Brawl, Firearms, and Melee are all three. Athletics, Dodge, Stealth, and Occult are two. And then a few ones, which include Acting, Computer, and Politics. No Numina (making it so half the templates are normals and half have powers). Backgrounds include four dots in Mentor (the single highest background in this book), and one each in Allies, Resources, and Status. Humanity and Willpower are both at seven. The art is extremely crude, and he looks like he's trying for some sort of paramilitary look, including what has to be one of the worst efforts to draw an UZI I've seen since ... well, when I last tried to draw one. I'm not a huge fan of this one. The idea of a low-life turned vampire hunter is a good idea, but I think the execution could be a lot better. A (very) lapsed Catholic who has rediscovered his faith in the face of real monsters and now tries to put his criminal skills to use for the Inquisition sounds like a potentially interesting character to play. But this isn't really that character. Instead, it's one of those characters that seems likely to turn into a problem when he keeps insisting on acting like a jerkass.

    So, that's our Inquisitors. Overall, they're pretty decent. The ideas are all good ones, but a few of them wobble in the execution. The Young Turk is probably my least favorite, and I really do wish the Puritan had been a Catholic with True Faith instead. The Quisling wins for "idea I'd not have thought of on my own".
    Representation .... They are all men. And all white, at least as near as I can tell given the poor artwork. I find this rather mind-boggling. Back when this book came out, WWGS was really at the forefront of the gaming industry in showcasing female characters. I realize that there is this weird tendency to try to stereotype the Catholic Church and faith based witch hunters as a male thing, but the book does devote a fair amount of attention to the issue of women in the Inquisition, including two different orders of nuns and two previous leaders of the entire Society. And I find the lack of Latin characters very odd.
    What's missing? Well, a nun would've been nice. One with True Faith, or with the healing Theurgy power, or with prophetic ability. And at least one other female character. I also would've liked to see at least one Investigation oriented character. The stereotype of Irish, Italian and Latin police officers from major metropolitan cities makes me wonder how it was skipped. (If you ever saw the 90s tv series Homicide: Life on the Streets, Andre Braugher's character of Frank Pembleton was a Catholic who attended a Jesuit high school in NYC, and in addition to being an excellent detective and interrogator was also knowledgable in Greek, Latin and Catholic theology. I have long thought he'd have made a very interesting member of the Inquisition.) I also think it would've been interesting to see an Inquisitor who was focused on the Christian idea of mercy and redemption, as sort of a forerunner to the Redeemer creed from Hunter the Reckoning.

    (Of the hunter books, I think this is the one that probably could've most benefited from a sequel - an Inquisition Companion or something - that gave chapter length treatments on the various non-Catholic faith based groups, like the Orthodox Akritai, the Muslim Brethren of Purity, and others.)

    Anyway, that's it for this book. Next, I will finally be talking about the Ravnos, and then the final book, the Giovanni.

                        Clanbook: Ravnos (part one)

                        Finally getting around to this. So, this book was still in the "bad old days" when the Ravnos clan was very much a bunch of stereotypical The Rom. And this book goes hard core into that, including using a bunch of stuff from the much maligned (deservedly so) WOD: Gypsies. This includes the total BS "history" of the The Rom with them originating in the First City and getting more "please stop" from there. This is also when the clan weakness was full on Cryme Tyme. Quite frankly, it's a miracle that all seven of these templates aren't toxic dumpster fires.
                        So, this book was written by Christopher Walters, whose name I'm not familiar with from anything else. It was developed by Robert Hatch, who also did "additional material". The art is, somewhat to my surprise, by Christopher Shy. This is well before his "Celtic facial tattoos" phase from Vampire's revised era, and when he's not doing weird ass photoshop effects, he's actually a pretty good artist.
                        And this one once again does that annoying thing where the Concept on the character sheet is different from that of the write up's title.

                        The Ace of Spades (Natural Born Killer)
                        So right out of the gate, we get a serial killer. Basically a sociopath from birth, somewhat obsessed with chance, a habitual liar, and a torturer or small animals, as an adult he graduated to killing people. Apparently he worked for the mob for a little while, but got bored with it and, after rolling some dice, killed all his employers. Then some Ravnos came along and embraced him. Now he just has more interesting ways to kill people, and will eventually work up to killing other vampires, werewolves, etc. Mental is primary, with a 3/3/4 split. Physicals are secondary, with an extra dot from freebies worked into Dexterity (the KillNinja attribute) for a 3/4/2 split. Charisma is one so that Manipulation can be three. Ability-wise, Firearms and Melee are both four dots, with Stealth at three. Alertness, Brawl, Dodge, Intimidation, Streetwise, Subterfuge, Computer and Investigation are all two dots. Disciplines are two dots in Chimerstry and one in Fortitude. Backgrounds are one in Contacts, three in Generation, and two in Resources. (No Mentor, so no idea what happened to his sire; Maybe he flipped a coin and killed him.) He's on the Path of Paradox, the special Ravnos path of enlightenment introduced in the second ed Players Guide. As my copy is in danger of falling apart, I can't really consult it, but it was one of those that I remember had a strong reputation of being a "path of doing whatever I want" deals. Anyway, he's got that at six, and is the only one in the book on that path. His Willpower is seven. The art work is pretty good, even if it's got that whole trench coat and long hair (and bare chest) thing going on that makes it a contender for The Most 90s-est 90s Thing to Have Ever 90s. I'm kind of on the fence about this character. One the pro side, he's not one of the The Rom. And his Cryme Tyme of Choice is at least sort of interesting. His stats are OK (and I get the sense that before becoming a full on killer for hire, he may have been an office drone of some sort, which I honestly would've found an interesting angle to explore more). On the con side, well, he's a serial killer, which is a little old hat at this point. And he's also very much a Lone Wolf who doesn't look like he'd have much interest in joining a coterie (unless his die roll came up a certain way) or working well with others. I think this one could probably be reworked a little into a much stronger character. Maybe as a Sabbat member who was a cube farm worker before getting snatched up and is now kind of obsessed with randomness and chance when he's the only survivor of the bunch of shovel heads he was created along side. Or perhaps someone who did enforcement work for the mob but didn't really enjoy it but now finds himself compelled to hurt and kill people.

                        The Carjacker (Young Thug)
                        F&@$ you, book. Sigh. So carjacking was one of those things from the 90s that the news media loved to talk about in its "if it bleeds, it leads" mentality. Usually part and parcel with all the talk about the coming wave of young "super predator" criminals (meaning young black males from the inner city). So this is a racial stereotype of that era in a book that's already got that problem coming out of its ears. But in a fit of irony, this character is not terrible. He's got a decent back story - the son of a Miami mechanic who picked up some of his father's skills, but decided to use them for easy money instead of honest work - and got recruited into the Sabbat after pissing off what's clearly meant to be the Setites (which ties back into the Voodoo priest from that book, interestingly enough). He acts like a total stereotype when he's relaxed but when getting ready to do a job becomes a lot more serious and professional. (While the art work is technically good, the fact that its a stereotypical looking black youth with a gun and a pair of pantyhose on his head makes me hate it.) Physicals are primary, with Strength and Dexterity at four. Mental is secondary with a 3/2/3 split. Drive is at four dots. Brawl, Intimidation, Repair and Security are all at three. Streetwise and Firearms are both at two (you'd think that first one would be higher), as is the secondary ability of Military Science (and wow has it been a while since I've seen that one). No idea where he picked that up. I guess from the rest of his pack. Disciplines are one in Chimerstry, two in Fortitude, and one in Celerity. No idea why he has the dot of Special Sauce, other than the fact that every character in the book has at least one, because I guess they have to. Backgrounds are two in Contacts and three in Herd. He's on the Path of Honorable Accord at five and has a Willpower of six. I don't think he spent all of his freebies, as two seem to be MIA. Obviously, his Cryme Tyme of Choice is stealing cars. As I said, this is not a terrible character, mostly owing to the effort put into his back story, but he really could be a lot better. The idea of a car thief - especially a Sabbat one - is a pretty good idea. But I wish it had avoided the "urban super predator" stereotype angle and gone for someone who was good enough not to have to resort to violence. Even better if his compulsion was something that either involved being violent or made him try to take much bigger risks than he knew he should.

                        And that ends part one. I will probably have part two up by Sunday or Monday.

                            As always, great job with these posts. I really enjoy your take on these templates.

                            I've been saving up my two cents for a little while, since none of the comments were interesting enough on their own.

                            Assamites
                            • The Gamester would have made a perfect Vizier.
                            • Psycho killer. Hmm, not bad in it's own right. As far as publishing history goes, though, this was past the point when developers should have said serial killers had been done, and writers should move on.
                            • I would like to have seen a Disparate who happens to be Assamite, but just wants get on with their own agenda.

                            Setites
                            • Weird personal note about the templates. Two of the drawings used looked exactly like people I know. No, really, like portraits. The dancer looked like a close friend in my gaming group. The closet setite looked like my best friend in college. In the Revised Setite clanbook, the guy who sells Egyptian tchotchkes looked a great deal like my boyfriend at the time. For a few years after the books came out new people in our gaming group... without prompting... would ask if we had won some sort of contest to be featured in a clanbook.
                            • The Gangsta. Umm, yeah. Bravo nature and demeanor. High strength and appearance. Low intelligence. I really want to believe the image of a scary, strong-backed, sexy, and stupid, Black man was just accidentally racist. But... damn.
                            • Path assignment is complicated by the fact that Path of the Warrior is one of the more under-utilized paths in the game. It's way more than just "I hit stuff". If you believe in physical/spiritual dualism, the inherent corruptibility of the flesh, a mild degree of self-mortification, and the need to speed up Set's return, then Warrior works for you.

                            Tzimisce
                            • I am a big fan of the Tzimisce, especially Old Clan, but main clan, too. Yes, they became too fleshcraft-centric over the years, but there's a lot there to work with.
                            • What I am not a fan of is the asscrack art in this roach-covered porta-potty of a visual shitfest. (I had to tone down my reaction quite a bit. You just got the edited, calm version.) Okay, so I used to be a tattoo artist. I trained an apprentice once, and taught her how to work on skin that might be moving around or even jumping from time to time by having her draw while I drove her through an old parking lot with lots of potholes. No clipboards or flat surfaces were allowed; she had to draw on paper in mid-air. Her resulting linework was cleaner, neater and less painful to the eyes than this. The Tzimisce are a clan known for their fleshcrafting. It would have been helpful to have illustrations giving some idea of what that winds up looking like. Creating scribble-scribble sketches and turning them in as art doesn't make an illustrator daring or creative. It makes them look lazy and disinterested in finishing a project. Arghhhhh.
                            • I am going to go take a few deep breaths.
                            • Okay, I'm back. The Carnivorous Carny has a good reason to have unfocused abilities, he is pretty much a random embrace. An Archon stumbled into his house, and he somehow managed to stake him. Given the difference in power levels, this was mostly sheer luck.
                            • Similarly, the Count seems lackluster because he was embraced for one and only one reason: his pedigree. My only gripe is his being on Honorable Accord, when he is said to basically roll his eyes at the entire concept of the Sabbat.
                            • Torturer: Oh, look, somebody embraced a serial killer. That's novel and creative. Somebody should have thought of that before. Yawn.
                            • This book was the first (and, I think, last) time anybody mentioned how the mass embrace leads to Tzimisce who use their Vicissitude for the stuff most people would use it for: making their... ahem... bigger, and fixing their friends' bald spots. ("Generation X", page 28) This was a delightful concept that never gets used. It would have been nice to have a template based on this. Maybe, a fashion model, body builder, or personal stylist? We saw a small glimpse of the possibilities in Revised's "Star, Fucker".

                            Lasombra
                            • Why they're often Catholic? Beyond historical inertia, I always figured it was easier. When you infilitrate any of the thousand or so Protestant denominations you have to keep up to date with the different theologies and modes of worship. A newly arrived Lasombra working to infiltrate a Lutheran church had better know the difference between the Evangelical Lutheran Church of America, ELC in Namibia, Church of Norway, and Missouri Synod... including all the subtle, unwritten social traditions. Catholics, being simultaneously more centralized in doctrine and more culturally diverse, are simpler to research and blend into.

                                    Man, this is a great thread! I keep thinking of stuff I want to agree with/disagree with/maunder into a senile digression about, but have been disciplining myself thus far...

                                    Originally posted by Black Fox View Post
                                    The Vicissitude power really took over the clan. Signature Disciplines often did that because they were the most distinctive things about them.
                                    I was just boggled that so few (none, IIRC) of the signature characters had any focus on Animalism, at all. It's one of the big three Tzimisce disciplines, and modifying animal ghouls was a thing, so it could have easily been developed. But no, Vicissitude all the way, with a mild flirtation with Auspex. I remember a thread on the WW fora way back when asking 'what are the most creative things you can do with Vicissitude' and some wag quipped 'not take it?' Because *every* Tzimisce seemed to be all about it. (And every Lasombra about Obtenebration, when their clan's goals seemed to get vastly more potential usage out of Dominate, Presence, Auspex and / or Obfuscate, Obtenebration almost feeling like a point-trap, to keep you from being good at your job, in a way.)

                                    As a result, when I ST my own Vampire chronicles, I don't use Vicissitude at all. I replaced it with standard Protean twenty years ago. None of my friends minded. None of them liked that Discipline anyway. It did mean I had to throw out much of the canon established around the clan and reinvent archetypes. But I thought the basics of the clan being territorial (as a result of their clan weakness), being "fiends" in the sense they relished tormenting the living, and that had remnant ties to feudalism - whether as being tied to estates or having "serfs" among the Revenants - was more than enough to create a strong clan identity.
                                    Based on their strong ties to the ground / land / ancestral homes, I thought the original Viseratika (sp?) Discipline of the Gargoyle Bloodline, all about linking up to the walls and stonework of their warded places (and minus the gargoyle wings that came with it), was thematically far more suited to the Tzimisce than fleshcraft. Toss the fleshcraft into a popular path of their sorcery (along with weather control), and have their three Clan Disciplines be Auspex, Animalism and Viceratika. All about bonding to their territory, and it's creatures (and, through their magic, even influencing it's weather).

                                    As for the other clanbooks, in short.

                                    I remember liking the Malkavian Clanbook in all it's whackiness at the time, but after seeing the prominence of Fishmalks over the years, not so much.

                                    Clanbook Nosferatu was the best thing ever, at the time, and I remember being disappointed that the Revised edition's version didn't really match up. They were my favorite Clan for a time, because of that book.

                                    The Tremere somehow dodged being 'special sauced' quite so much as the other Clans with a special discipline, in that a couple of the sample characters didn't even have it, but focused on the perfectly cromulent Dominate and Auspex. I liked that. Although, IIRC, one of the characters writeups (Politico?) mentioned influencing things with a little blood magic, and had exactly bupkiss Thaumaturgy to back that up...

                                    Oh, those Setites. Another special sauce Clan that can do their actual thing much more effectively if they use disciplines like Auspex and Dominate, and avoid Serpentis like the plague. As the Evil Overlord's Handbook tells us, 'Turning into a giant snake never helps.' A better Path of the Warrior Setite example might have been cool.

                                    And the Assamites, so one-note. More focus on Vizier types or information brokers or middlemen who *don't* do the actual killing (but gather information on local kindred and maintain safehouses for visiting assassins) would have been neat.

                                                    That just goes back to the Assamites being an ill-conceived clan from the very beginning. They were intended to be a one note threat. The first edition clanbook was written with that in mind. So when they later wanted to expand it, they made even more bad decisions by the creation of the sub-clans that ultimately subverted the core concept. Today the clan is just a big hot mess from what I read about V5.

                                                    The problems with Ravnos is mainly a problem with finding a good core concept. The problem with Assamites is that it was fine (a mysterious sect of assassins is a good hook), but too restrictive (and Quietus, like many of the Signature Disciplines, doesn't actually make sense as a coherent set of powers which is why V5 has messed with them).

                                                    It would have been better if the writers had actually researched the clan's obvious inspiration - the real life assassins. The real world assassins didn't kill people simply because they wanted to kill people. They killed people who were a threat to them because they were an esoteric religious movement that was actively persecuted. Assassination was merely a side "business" that was an inadvertent result.

                                                    If I had been in charge of the clan, I would have tried to duplicate that as best I could given the context of vampires and the game's history. So you need to make the Assamites believe in some really heretical/blasphemous thing that all the clans would persecute them for. I'm not sure what would be good, but it would have to be something fundamental - like an alternate set of traditions that permits the Assamites to act in ways the other clans can't accept. Something that directly threatened the other clans. Probably include something about Diablerie being OK, and even preferred since that fits the clan. I'm sure if I gave sufficient thought to it, I'd come up with something. Besides that, you need an esoteric interpretation of things - in other words, it's not the explicit reading of the text that reveals the truths. That is only for suckers. It's the hidden mean behind them. So they still look for a Book of Nod, but have completely crazy interpretations of it. So they're Noddist scholars as well, but their scholarly tradition is completely at odds with everyone elses. And create the clan culture around that. Make it really heretical, like if you didn't know a thing about VtM's mythology around vampirism and you had to come up with something new for a different game. Something that makes sense, but just doesn't fit (though the perfect thing would be to make sure the two contending myths had some elements in common so it is really a result of different interpretation and missing knowledge). And as a result, the early Assamites were despised and mistreated, and as a result they eventually struck back and started killing other vampires (though all the other clans say it was the Assamites who murdered them from the beginning, and their reactions were a result of that). That way you could explain the assassination function yet not have the clan be defined by it. They could have done this with the revised clanbook, but botched that up as well.

                                                    I have never used the Assamites in any of my chronicles, but this is in my notes. So if I ever do introduce them as a plot element, I have something to work with.
