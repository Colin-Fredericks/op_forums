                                                I think part of the answer to this question depends on a group/ST’s gaming philosophy.

                                                For instance on one hand, if I’m running a chronicle using a more character-path method of storytelling, I may look at the sheets of all the player characters and think to myself “Okay John’s Ventrue has Finance 5 & Resources 5, he probably wants some challenges that can be solved with money and some type of financial challenges and storylines. Maybe I’ll design a vampiric rival who’s seeking to undercut his PC’s investments as part of the larger schemes of the campaign”. In a situation like that you could say that the ST is forcing conflict but in some ways that’s the Storytellers job (like how a writer’s job for a movie is to create conflict in the story). Now I agree that it can be done poorly and feel forced but if played naturally and written well it’s just classic storytelling techniques.

                                                On the other hand maybe I’m running a more sandbox/open-world style chronicle. In that case the scenarios may become more realistic although perhaps having a double edged sword depending on perspective. If I’m setting up a sandbox campaign then I probably want to seed it with a wide variety of potential events, challenges, and threats that could occur in play; some of which might be very easy to solve just by dumping money into them. But that’s part of what makes a sandbox shine, sometimes you’ll be able to solve problems immediately and sometimes you really won’t. So if you’re running a money build certain challenges (all the money in the world won’t stop the mayor’s daughter from being a mage who wants to buy your ancestral estate) might not have immediate answers. That’s how the play style goes and it’s up to the ST to try and keep things interesting by keeping in mind the consequences of what the various story actors are doing and what effects they might have.

                                                Also, if I’m running a chronicle and someone has basically made a character with a perfected hermit-crab build who never needs to leave a controlled environment and interact with the chronicle in a meaningful way, I may just talk to that player and ask them to make that PC a secondary character that we can blue-book over longer time spans but that they should make a new primary character who can be more involved in the mean level of play that the group is engaged in.

                                                Those are some of my thoughts on the resource question, I hope that they can be of some use.

                                                            Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                                            Bulk withdraws don't actually set off loads of flags. Big transactions happen all the time. It's 1 to 2 pages of paperwork to tack on to it for the BSA filing you bank has to do.
                                                            By your own admission any transfer of $10,000 is automatically flagged, whereas anything below that isn't automatically flagged. You are not proving your point, if anything you are proving mine.

                                                            Structuring is a charge that was created not to be a crime by itself, but to add on top of other crimes to create weight to the charges. Otherwise, every senior citizen that withdraws a couple hundred a month could be charged with structuring. Once again, this only comes into play when someone is already certain of your guilt and is looking for additional stuff to throw at you.

                                                            When it comes to records and financial institutions you have to pick one of two routes, the first is invisibility and the second is immunity. If you pick immunity, you do everything on the up and up, which means even if someone gets cranky and tries to maliciously audit you, there is nothing to attack. Alternatively with invisibility option you are going to do criminal things, but everything is setup to avoid attention and misdirect any investigation.

                                                            Since we are discussing vampires here, of course there is a level of criminality that will always go on, so the key is to avoid attention and any extra paperwork. Which in turn means you make sure all taxes on traceable funds are paid in full so no one can leverage the financials, you always keep payments in small amounts spread across multiple businesses so no one can easily see how the money moves, and you routinely convert traceable funds into untraceable goods which are stored in multiple locations.
