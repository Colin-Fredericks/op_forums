                                        The whole "Gangrel live in the sticks and hate urban living" thing is really a simplistic view on them. Yes, they can live away from civilization, the clan weakness indeed encourages older Gangrel to remain some distance away. Their natural disciplines don't have them thrive in the city as much as other clans do, which gives them a competitive disadvantage.

                                        First, the Gangrel aren't nature hippies, and only oppose urbanization when it's to the extent that other clans will have an advantage over them. People are the prefered source of food for any vampire, and so long as an area isn't stable and prosperous enough that the other clans have their attention drawn to it, the Gangrel will relish the development. A small city or large town where the Gangrel can easily squash any interloper without reprisal? Ideal. Most cities aren't big things with high vampire counts where vampires have to worry about politics. A Gangrel can excel in these small cities.

                                        Second, most young Gangrel aren't powerful enough to survive the wilds, but aren't yet inhuman enough to need to brave the wilds. As young vampires grossly outnumber older vampires, this is rather important.

                                        Third, and most importantly, in a city big enough for political organization, the Gangrel can easily leverage the skills they are good at to stake claim to a Domain that's legally protected. The reason why Gangrel so often live in the sticks is competition, but if the Government backs your claim, and you're not yet a chimera, why not take something nice in the inner city? The Camarilla is great for this because the Gangrel, in theory, have claims just as valid as any of the other 6 clans. The Camarilla supports the Traditions, and the Traditions support the Gangrel more than they do many other clans. In an Anarch city, the Gangrel don't have laws to rely on, so their Territorial claims go out the window whenever a Brujah, Toreador or Ventrue wants the land: They simply can't compete with an equally aged member of those clans in the city. (my God this realization makes me want to do a whole thread on why the gangrel leaving was dumb as fuck)


                                        There's one thing to note:
                                        Before the Camarilla, the Gangrel often railed against high clans and the establishment. They did the best in surviving the inquisition and the anarch revolt. Suspicious? Well, there's lots of rational reasons for it, but there's also plenty of excuses for the high clans were they to ever decide that one clan needed to be removed from existence. The Greatest threat to Gangrel was all the other clans banding against them in an age where cities were clearly the future. Joining the cam for their own protection was very much a necessity.

                                            The Second Inquisition and Modern Times

                                            Personally, I think the big reason for the existence of the Second Inquisition is due to the fact that so much of our relationship to conspiracy theories, government, surveillance, and so much else that was more "believable" in the Nineties has changed in the wake of the War on Terror. The dramatic increase in power of the state following 9/11 and during the War on Terror was only beginning when the Old World of Darkness was destroyed.

                                            Nowadays, it's hard to believe with cell phone tracking, social media, the NSA tapping all of our phones, and so on that it is hard for a secret organization of immortals like the Camarilla to exist without being detected by the government.

                                            There's a couple of ways you can deal with this kind of problem:

                                            1:] The Supernaturals Run Everything

                                            The Technocracy or Camarilla or both are completely in charge of global media with no real danger of the Masquerade ever being destroyed. I dislike this because it basically removes the central premise of the gameline.

                                            2:] Humanity is deeply stupid

                                            I mean, the way the Dresden Files does it.

                                            3:] Humanity is on it

                                            Which is kind of what the Second Inquisition is about. They may be hunting vampires but they're also keeping the existence of the undead secret while they're attempting to conduct their shadow war.

                                            I really liked the set up in PROJECT: TWILIGHT where the government was a mixture of incompetent and capable. The NSA was heavily compromised by the Tremere and Technocracy, the SAD was compromised by FOUR groups, and otehr secret societies but there were still dangerous agents.
