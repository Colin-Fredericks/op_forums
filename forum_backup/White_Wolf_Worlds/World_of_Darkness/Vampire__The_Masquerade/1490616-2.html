    The way I see is that the lineages of the Samedi, Infitiores, Harbingers, and Giovanni are still ultimately all derived from the same Antedeluvian. The vitae of Cappadocious is all in their veins. Like it or not, that is the definition of a clan.

    It's perfectly fine for that clan to be heavily divided and not cooperative. The Brujah are famously divided even if they do flashmob when the clan as a whole is threatened. The Gangrel are known for their individualism. Other clans also have deep cleavages. It's really only about which group of elders are in nominal or effective charge. Now its rule by some kind of join committee of elders of all these various bloodlines rather than the whims of Augustus Giovanni.

    The idea that the different Cappadocian bloodlines finally got together and decided to end the Giovanni purge, and start acting as a single clan again - in whatever fashion - seems fine to me. It makes sense. It is even inevitable.

    But the Nagaraja are very different. They have no connection to the Cappadocians (or indeed any other Clans, unlike many Bloodlines, we do know their actual origins). I don't mind if people choose not to use the True Black Hand in their games. But if they don't, there is no reason to utilize the Nagaraja at all. But if for some reason you want to get rid of, but still use the Nagaraja - as the V5 metaplot users want to do - then the newly reformed Cappadocians are not the best place for them. They have nothing in common with them except that they happen to have a unique Discipline that has been retconned into a Path of Necromancy. That is rather poor in-game motivation to join up with some group they have no connection to. It's like saying any sub-clans that now have access to Blood Magic must be part of the Tremere. Or that every clan with Presence must now be considered one clan - Brujah, Toreador, Ventrue, Setites, what's the difference?

    Even if the True Black Hand received a major setback, I don't see why that would compel the Nagaraja to flee to another sect. They are very few in number. The members of the True Black Hand always shifted between Enoch and the mortal world. They already had a network of vampires, ghouls, Revenants, mortals, and other retainers to help them. They can easily hide just like they've always been doing. It's just bad writing to say they simply could not continue on with whatever tasks they already wanted along with any other surviving members of the True Black Hand. And if they did absolutely had to come out of the shadows and join a sect to protect them, I would think the Camarilla would best fit that. It has the greatest numbers and power. Why choose a newly reformed Independent Clan than them?

                                Again, there are several major issues:

                                The Nagaraja have to eat human flesh. They can get around 10 days worth of sustenance from one corpse, so each one needs ~35 corpses every year. Those corpses have to come from somewhere, and there's a huge risk for a Masquerade breach if someone finds out corpses are going missing or finds a Nagaraja's larder. Each additional Nagaraja in a city massively increases the risk of discovery. Having to hide, transport and store hundreds of corpses every year is a big logistical problem. I can see a lot of Camarilla Vampires writing the Nagaraja off just for that. Whatever they could provide isn't worth the risk of keeping them around.

                                The second is that the Camarilla requires vampires to retain Humanity, but Humanity doesn't work very well at all for the Nagaraja. Those who try and retain Humanity are noted to be feral and very low Humanity. Almost all of them end up on Paths simply because they have no other real choice.

                                Those are two massive strikes against them. And while some Camarilla vampires might be willing to welcome a brood of corpse eating feral vampires in exchange for their Necromantic knowledge, I think most of them would say "fuck it, it's not worth it." Especially since (in 5e) they just got the Lasombra to join, those guys are bringing Necromancy with them and they don't bring any of the problems that the Nagaraja do.

                                On the other side, even if the Camarilla offered to allow the bloodline to join, I don't see many Nagaraja accepting. They'd have to get off their Paths and get back onto Humanity and then they'd have to struggle every night to avoid falling to wrightdom. I just can't see the bloodline being willing to waltz with wassail every night in order to join the Camarilla. And what would joining the Camarilla even get them? The Camarilla is a political organization, but the Nagaraja have always been distinctly apolitical. Their interest is primarily in the Underworld and the supernatural. I don't feel that the Camarilla has much that would entice most of them, especially considering the drawback of giving up Paths that would come with joining.

                                Honestly between the Camarilla, Anarchs, Sabbat and Hecata I think the Nagaraja would be the least likely to join the Camarilla. At least with the other sects they could maintain their Paths and that's incredibly important for their nightly existence.

                                            While I agree with Black Fox on how relatively easy it would be for the Nagaraja to get into the Camarilla, I mostly agree with AnubisXy on why their joining the Hecata makes sense.

                                            However the take of the ST on DSotBH, the Nagaraja are characterized as a bloodline that frequently resides within the Dark Umbra. Even if they don't do that all the time, and some may never go there, that does means that their link to other Necromancers isn't simply a matter of shared Discipline, but in their specific case a matter of literal common ground. Necromancers are more likely than any other vampires to have any kind of contact with them, simply.

                                            From that comes the rest. While they do fine in low numbers, they also have a higher than average need for a support system due to their need for flesh. Even if they feed from corpses, it still has far more involved logistics than the Kiss, so they need friends with less pronounced needs. Seeking an allegiance that can contribute to their survival seems pretty natural to me, and nothing would be more organic than looking for the people you're more likely to know and them to know you, even if your deeper interests are somewhere else.

                                            But then, I think as with all the Hecata bloodlines, doubly so for the Nagaraja, the alliance must be seen as a pragmatic partnership and not a dogmatic following, contarry to what other sects are and the TBH even more so. I don't see the Hecata as an institution denying a member the right to participate in another sect, so in this sense they are, indeed, more a clan than a sect. They shouldn't imo be a clan rules-wise, but they're more a support chain than a political or religious affiliation.

                                            So in this context they make sense. Actually, because of their actual history, most Hecata bloodlines make some sense to be there, and the Nagaraja are actually one of the easiest for me to imagine being there, as they don't have reasons to actually hate others within the group. Although even for those I think the V5 story most flies well enough. The people they really had reason and right to hate got killed in the process, so its a sweet deal, but to me it is ultimately a pragmatic survival deal.

                                            Not survival in the sense of needed to literally survive, mind you, but survival in the sense of making life safer and easier for each other at a relatively low cost.
