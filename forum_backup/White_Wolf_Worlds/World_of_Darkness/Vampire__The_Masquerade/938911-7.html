                        I never equated the Antedeluvians to the scale of the Great Old Ones, but they were supposed to be supremely powerful when it comes to other vampires, and obviously that meant they were a risk to most other things on Earth. The original World of Darkness implied there was to be an end to the world - Gehenna, Apocalypse, Ascension, Doomsday, and Endless Winter were all essentially the same thing. The current world was going to be destroyed, but there was a chance for a new one to be born after the defeat of ultimate evil according to whatever eschatology the ST wanted. I always considered Gehenna to simply be one event during the Apocalypse that would lead to Doomsday. The Antedeluvians were likely to be destroyed at some point during the Apocalypse - just not by other vampires - but by other powerful beings who would have appeared during the Apocalypse.

                        If the Antedeluvians - remnants of a legendary world that was destroyed - exist like a Sword of Damocles hanging over the fates of every vampire on Earth, they are an interesting setting element. If they are just another powerful vampire, not so interesting. So while I would not use the same language of CajunKhan , I agree with her.

                        ​
                        As an aside, I laugh when people think the ending of the Call of Cthulhu implies Cthulhu isn't extremely powerful (and he isn't all powerful, he's simply the High Priest of his alien race). Cthulhu wasn't defeated at all. The steam powered ship - supreme technology of the 1920s when the story was written - could only temporarily disrupt him as he reformed immediately after. He was like a hibernating bear temporarily chasing away an intruder in his cave before going back to hibernation. The entire point of the story is that the stars are not right, not yet. There was just a minor conjunction that temporarily allowed a portion of Cthulhu's mind and might to influence the world which resulted in a series of death and madness. But I think Baldaam actually knows that and is just having fun.

                                                            To go with my earlier statements about the Antediluvians existing as a religion. The point is that they manage to establish a lot of the fear and supernatural dread that millennialist Christianity induces without having to really get into the specifics of apocalyptic Christianity.

                                                            By what I mean:

                                                            1:] The Antediluvians are going to rise eventually to kill you

                                                            A vampire is immortal but the existence of Gehenna effectively means that their immortality is a lie. They can live for a year, ten years, a thousand years, or longer but when the Antediluvians rise up, they will destroy you and it will end. It is thus an existential fear of Elders and even Methuselahs.

                                                            2:] The Antediluvians are mysterious

                                                            The thing about the Antediluvians is they're usually spoken about as a group rather than the singular. When vampires think of Nosferatu, Toreador, or Ventrue then they have a personality but very few of them are people the Clan despises with exceptions like Nossie and Cappadocias. The Followers of Set and Haqimites exempted. There's not enough known about them for you to appease them and thus they induce more dread.

                                                            3:] The Antediluvians make you feel like a victim

                                                            The symbolism of cannibalism among the undead with diablerie is something that exists both in and out of universe. Vampires feed on humans and are terrifying to them. Humans are food, cattle, and slaves. There's thus no greater existential fear than being made into food and cattle by a vampire more powerful than you.

                                                            4:] Weird cults offer you a way out

                                                            The Book of Nod is something vampires turn to out of a desire to figure out a "cheat" for what is their inevitable Final Deaths. There are also weird cults like the kind in Forbidden Religion or the ENTIRE EXISTENCE of the Followers of Set and Banu Haqim that the Antediluvians can be appeased.

                                                            5:] The Antediluvians are probably real. Probably

                                                            I think it was a mistake to have Gehenna happen because part of the fun is that the Antediluvians can be completely misunderstood. The Dark Ages called a lot of the lore about them into question because Lasombra was up and about 1000 years ago without turning anyone into a meal.
