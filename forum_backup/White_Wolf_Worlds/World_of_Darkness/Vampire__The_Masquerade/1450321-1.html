                    That's true. But the new rules at least have the possibility of working perfectly.
                    There are several problems with the above example. Firstly, none of the other PCs are getting a turn while this encounter goes on, meaning they hang around waiting. That's just bad STing.

                    Second, Virtue rolls are usually reflexive, so the PC could do something rather than just standing there looking like he's straining to defecate. You can then adjust the difficulty of the extended Self-Control roll based on the actions he takes (e.g., if he proceeds to batter the guy, I'd raise the difficulty; if he backs off and tries to remove himself from the situation, I'd reduce the difficulty).

                    Also, if you know you're only rolling three dice and you don't want to fail, you'd spend the WP from the first roll to ensure you can't botch. When it comes to Frenzy rolls, you only take the risk when you're not in a very public location with lots of mortals around.

                    I agree with you that a 'between Frenzy and cool' option is nice, but the example you gave is also a relatively skewed one.
