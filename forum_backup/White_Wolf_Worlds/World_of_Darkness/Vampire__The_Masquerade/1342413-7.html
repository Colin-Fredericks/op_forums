                Originally posted by MyWifeIsScary View Post
                The Gangrel really aren't that bad at making money and building up territory (with animals) . They can ghoul and accumulate resources just the same as any other vampire. They do benefit from being able to take territory other vampires would struggle in. The Stereotype of poor, peniless Gangrel really only works for the very young or the vampires that actively eschew wealth for... whatever reason. (I suppose if your luck hates you and you get a facial animal feature, that can knock you down a bit, but you can still acquire great resources through ghouls and other proxies)

                The Low Clan/High Clan divide is just a bit of prejudice. Actively building a sect around it is just short-sighted lunacy. The worst you could do is not invite the low clans to parties, or give them bad seating arrangements. if you wanted to go further with it the "high clans" would eventually face the possibility of a slaughter.
                The thing is, Gangrel are only good at gaining wealth and power in a vacuum, since they aren't just competing with humans, they are competing with other vampires. They lack Presence and Dominate, and because of this they need to work on Attributes and Skills that compliment their actual Discipline spread. Meaning they will typically spend much of their younger years developing Stealth, Alertness, Survival, Brawl. By the time they are old enough to even think about anything other than survival, they have already spent huge amounts of time mastering the skills of a feral monster, and largely neglecting Social Attributes, and money/politics related Skills. Then you throw in their Clan Compulsion and Bane, which hurts their social interaction more than the High Clan's does their's.

                Sure, an individual Gangrel might be able to overcome all of those hurdles and become an economic/political powerhouse, but he/she is facing a huge uphill climb. And the few who do will be pretty much alone if the local High Clans close ranks to push him/her out of economic/political power. Something they can do as easily as declaring a certain economic/political area to be the Domain of the Toreador/Ventrue/Tremere Primogen. You managed to push that Ventrue out of the solar panel business? Too bad he's got the ear of the Prince, and the Prince says that solar panels are an extension of energy industry, and the energy industry is the Domain of that Ventrue, meaning you have no right to intrude on it, regardless of your business skill.

                Basically it's a lot of little things adding up over time that keeps the Low Clans as Low Clans. Discipline spreads, the mundane Attributes and Skills needed when young to compliment those Discipline spreads, socially crippling clan banes/compulsions, and finally the old boys club preventing remarkable individual exceptions from making much progress.

                            Originally posted by Father Enoch View Post
                            Is taking what you want from VtM and chucking the rest a new thing? It seems weird for me to read this after seeing all the metaplot or census books made over the years.
                            I literally try to keep my setting as close to books as possible, with a few extrapolations or "corrections" to keep things smooth and consistent or more in line with reality; nothing radical.
                            But that's from core books and the suplements that are purely setting/mechanics; The Thaumaturgy companions, Midnight seige, Hunters Hunted II, clanbooks.
                            City books and chronicle books don't appeal to me, and I imagine they don't appeal to a lot of people. Now while there are name drops in something like Hunters Hunted II or clanbooks, there's nothing in these things that really endears me to a character like, say, a chronicle would, as chronicles spend a lot more time around a limited number of characters. I might have the vague idea to use Gratiano or Mithras, again "event" guys, if I ever set a game in their cities, but I'd run these fellows how I'd think they'd be, I wouldn't read up some roleplaying hints and closely follow his stat block.

                            Maybe some people have a great attachment to, say, Rudi after helping him in his possibly good chronicle, but his summary sounds kinda stupid to me, so I'd ignore him. I played a longrunning game with a player who really liked the story of Jonas Albrecht, but again, I'd rather work with my own characters, and don't think this Jonas SNPC fellow matches the tone I take with the Garou/Silver fangs and I don't really like SNPCs having king-level sway in the plot. Just feels like someone got a bit self indulgent. The Margrave works better because he doesn't sound like someone's self indulgence. Characters like Jonas or Rudi or whoever just sound like someone got carried away with their own games and thought they should impose it on everyone else. That's why it's very easy to dismiss them.

                            The best vampire example of someone who changes things in modern nights is of course, Ur-Motherfucking-Shulgi. Because he's not an SNPC, and the ST has plenty of leeway with how they want to work him.
                            1: His background is mysterious and the storyteller can make their own mystery adventure surrounding him. One storyteller's interpretation can differ wildly from anothers, but they can both be correct.
                            2. The Assamite schism was really something with a lot of buildup that needed an inciting incident. The Assamite sect was wonderfully fluffed with conflict. It's like WW1; If Franz didn't get Ferdinan'd, that war would've kicked off anyway because dreadnaughts and nationalism and Alsace-Lorraine and... stuff. Meanwhile the Gangrel... There's no good pretext.
                            3:It gave players more options with Assamites, not less. It's a win for both Cam and Anarch players because schismatics give them new character options (both welcoming and hostile), Loyalists get to double down and hunt traitors. It's a win for people who want to play religious assamites, Path of Blood fanatics and secular moderates, and it's also interesting for characters who hate assamites but now face them trying to intergrate.

                            The Gangrel leaving the Cam is just... Loss.
                            Someone had an opinion and now a lot of Gangrel with Cushy Cam possitions are jeapardized? Uh? No thanks. The Cam loses something. The Anarchs and Sabbat don't gain anything. The Gangrel lose something.
