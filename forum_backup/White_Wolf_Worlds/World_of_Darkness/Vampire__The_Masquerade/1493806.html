    Bending, but not Breaching, the Masquerade

    09-27-2022, 03:28 AM
    I've been catching up on 'What We Do in the Shadows' the last week or so and had a mini epiphany over the title sequence which shows various paintings and photographs of the characters over the years. I realised that in Camarilla terms that having these paintings and photographs, especially as a collection, is a breach of the masquerade, albeit not necessarily a serious one.

    Which got me to thinking about what masquerade breaking mementoes would any vampire collect? Have any of you considered what your characters/NPCs would have collected over the years?

      I recall being in a game years and years ago. One of the NPCs was an elder Lasombra who kept a series of pictures of herself. She had learned Viscissitude and wanted to make sure she remembered what she looked like. She couldn't see herself in mirrors or anything, and was terrified that if she changed her appearance she wouldn't be able to change it back. Occasionally she would use Visscsitude and then after turning back to "normal" she would have a portrait made so that she could compare her current appearance to the old one. So she had a new portrait of herself made very every decade or so. And then she'd look through her collection checking for any alteration or imperfection and ensuring that they were all identical and that she hadn't forgotten what she looked like.

      Our characters ended up needing to deal with her and her portrait collection played a big role. Our Malkavian artist managed to get sneak into her collection and changed up many of the more recent pictures slightly, making her nose a little bigger in one or moving her eyes very slightly in another and then burned all of the old ones. When she saw discovered what had happened she completely lost it. She didn't know what she had used to look like and would now never know. But, the Malkavian had saved the oldest painting and we managed to use it as blackmail material.

      Was a pretty fun game.

      How is What We Do in the Shadows anyway? My brother made me watch the episode with Mark Hamill and it was pretty fun. Are the rest of the episodes good?

          This is one of those grey zones where it sounds great at first, but then falls apart once you apply a reality test to it. I say this because I have spent a good portion of my life in the antiques market and the vast majority of people wouldn't realize what they are looking at.

          You would only be in potential danger if you had something in front of a history buff or outright professional historian, and even then it would have to fall inside their area of interest. A civil war reenactment actor and historian might be able to rattle off the dates of battles and interesting trivia, but they are unlikely to be able to explain European fashions or poetry of the same era.

          Paintings are even worse because unlike a photograph, it depends on the artists skill to be accurate. Even so just by looking at it you can off handedly say "it was an ancestor" which accounts for the age of the item and the similar look, or you can say "I had it made on a bet" making it sound like an expensive part of cosplay. The key thing is that without doing a chemical analysis and getting expert opinions on the brush strokes matching to known painters, you can't prove anything with a painting. Even if it was a photograph, is it really that hard to use a sepia tone filter and some cosplay to make you and your friends look like they are in the old west or any other time period once photography is a thing?

          When you think about it, what item can't be explained by "I inherited it" or "I bought it at an estate sale"?

          Furthermore, if you say that the item in question is one of a kind, first the viewer would have to recognize it, and more importantly they would somehow have to come to the conclusion that the owner was a vampire, rather than an antiques dealer or a collector. Does owning a bunch of old junk somehow equal vampire, because if so then every person associated with antiques roadshow is on the second inquisitions hit list.

          The biggest issue would be things like old drivers licenses or passports that had photographs or things like pictures from events where the vampire is at a fundraiser shaking hands with other recognizable influencers. But then again few kindred are stupid enough to have a series of such pictures setup in practically a power point presentation where even the dimmest would be vampire hunter goes "oh hey, this doesn't seem right".

                                  I think that is one of those things STs can easily invent and add behind the scenes if they think it is necessary.

                                  I have something like this in my own chronicles. It is not a full cipher or language. It is much less extensive than the Garou Glyphs. But it's rudimentary enough to indicate things like "this is a safe haven", "there is a ghoul here who can help," and other basic things. More similar to hobo signs than anything else. Mainly as an attempt to help reawakened vampires from torpor or perhaps even the odd abandoned embrace who nevertheless might have been taught some rudimentary lore. It exists basically to help prevent a Masquerade breach, as the Camarilla needs to worry about the Sabbat or other enemies using it as a means to identify targets. But these places are monitored by someone, somehow (ghouls, dominated humans, someone using Astral Projection, magical sigil, whatever).

                                  Most of the things in Blade that their glyph system uses are covered by other means in order to prevent an enemy using it to attack Kindred. Various Kindred - often Harpies but others as well - know where an actual safehouse (or more) is located in various cities so that new Kindred arriving elsewhere can know where to "book in" so that messages are sent to a Prince to get Hospitality as well as one or more places in the Rack that anyone can feed on. So if random Kindred wants to leave this city and go to another one, they don't have to learn everything on their own. They just need to pay a minor boon to a Harpy and get the basic information needed in order to move and be accepted. That Kindred can then do whatever they need to establish their own residence.

                                  But I agree in practice that some of the "logistics" of running an actual vampire conspiracy requires some sort of marks, ciphers, sigils, etc. to be present that can be easily dismissed as graffitti or meaningless. Some thing basic that can be quickly taught and remembered. But I don't think it requires actual depiction in game.

If you reread my original post the question asked was 'what mementoes would your vampire characters collect over the years, even if they were potentially masquerade breaking?', not 'does collecting mementoes break the masquerade?'
                                                          I merely disagreed with your core premise; it is neither inherently negative nor subverts the thread.

                                                          Contrary to what most media portrays most artifacts as, you can't keep using them after a certain age.

                                                          You can't handle documents because the oils from your skin or the oils from other people's skin that you picked up from transfer damages the paper. Bronze can shatter like glass after a few centuries. Leather and cloth unless carefully preserved rarely last more than 500 years.

                                                          Iron and Steel both can last a relatively long time, but there is a difference between a functional item made of those materials verses an item made of those materials that is still in good condition. A good example of this would be firearms from the old west, sure they might be in good condition and make excellent display pieces, but after a certain age, using gun powder in them will destroy the item. Likewise, most swords will break during use after a certain age range.

                                                          Most people have no idea how to preserve items or at the very least prevent damage. The number of absolute morons who put silver plate or sterling flatware in the dishwasher are too numerous to count. So given enough time everything a vampire owns will eventually be destroyed by time or will end up as collateral damage during some supernatural conflict. There is a reason why the most important items in collections are kept in airtight vaults that are climate controlled with very specific settings for humidity, not to mention detailed protocols for handling or upkeep.

                                                          From the vampiric point of view the things mortals see as long lasting are quite transitory. Kind of like how some food in the fridge goes bad after an amount of time passes. Imagine being a vampire a realizing that your suit of armor or sword has "spoiled" due to age. Thus, it has to be retired from front line duty and put in the museum.

                                                          So, anything older than say a two or three hundred years old needs to be kept in a proper museum style collection. If the items are in such a collection, that probably means they are kept well away from prying eyes or range of enemy hand grenades. Thus, no masquerade violation is probable.

                                                          But regardless the quality or age of the collection there really isn't much that is inherently masquerade breaking just by virtue of ownership. If you are just asking what a random vampire will collect, then just look at what any random human would collect.

                                                          I would say that vampires fit into a few categories when it comes to stuff.

                                                          1. The Concept Collector - very specific items, from a specific era and culture. This could be as grand as Roman artifacts or as simple as a serial killer keeping trophies from their victims.

                                                          2. The Prepper - gathers equipment and resources to face any situation. The collection represents power and the ability to survive. This could be an armory of modern weapons, or a Tremere vault filled with rare spell components.

                                                          3. The Memento keeper - items from their mortal days. A collection of wedding rings from mortal spouses taken through the decades or centuries, or perhaps a huge collection of diaries.

                                                          4. The Prestige owner - doesn't inherently care about the items, they just want the bragging rights to say they own them.

                                                          Of course, all of this assumes the vampire in question is the type with a home base, enough funds to gather a collection and maintain/preserve it and has no reason to change locations. I can say from personal experience that trying to move over a ton of silverplate and sterling flatware across country is so not worth the trouble or the back aches.

                                                          A vampire biker on the other hand wouldn't carry much beyond a change of clothes, weapons, and whatever tools were needed to repair their bike since all night vehicle repair shops are somewhat rare. Other than something small like a pendant with a photo in it, most stuff is just useless dead weight.
