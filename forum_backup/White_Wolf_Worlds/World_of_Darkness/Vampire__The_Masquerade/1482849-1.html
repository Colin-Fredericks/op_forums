                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                OK, so with better clarity on the desired feedback:



                Thumbs up. Good fit for the general change in combat mechanics.



                Also thumbs up. The older game had too many variables and something needs to go. Target numbers being set at X is the best of the options because it makes it much easier to understand the math of the game and how to value different adjustments. Needing to roll 7s instead of 6s for +1 dice of damage is not intuitive to understand if that's a good or bad deal. Taking a -1 dice penalty to inflict 1 damage is easier to see as, on average, a fairly good deal on average damage, but still tactically variable because of how V5 combat works.



                Thumbs down. Hunger Dice are too present leading to Bestial Failures and Messy Criticals as too much of a constant risk regardless of the stakes of the scene. The justification the "blood points are a boring gas tank," given by Martin I think loses both the utility of tracking set points over random outcomes,and how to connect the random outcomes effectively as consequence for other choices.



                Hard to answer as I don't entirely agree on the premise of treating the three Chronicle Tenets as equivalent to the old sins charts (there are other ways to get stains that apply regardless of Chronicle Tenets).

                But to give some level of answer to the intended comparison: thumbs up to negotiated for the group chronicle "morality" as a tool to help get the group on the same page as to the moral themes that will be present in a given instance, but thumbs down to the implementation and specifically of how it interacts with the Stains system. Most specifically I find the guidance on how severe a Stain penalty should be very lacking. Once you get to "how bad is this violation"as a ST, you have incredibly broad fiat to interpret and assign Stains for the action, and the players have to trust you to be fair and fun in it and have minimal recourse in the text regarding best practices to point to if they have trouble with the call.
                Second all that.

                I'm under the impression that some people that gave thumbs down did so in relation to the classic system instead of V5, so the OP is really badly phrased in this regard.

                All in all there is polishing to be made on V5 rules-wise (lore wise is beyond this thread), but those are mostly good changes.

                Hunger Dice may work if their use is better implemented, but as a constant feature of random problems arising at every action, not really useful. It could be a fixed penalty/bonus to some specific actions, or go into effect only at certain times. It could even derive some ideas from how Nightmare works in C20, which is a far better mechanic for a tangentially similar concept.

                Chronicle Tenets are in general a good idea and good implementation. But the Stains system needs a whole lot of tweaking and testing to properly polish it, so the Tenets are a good rule linked to a problematic system.

                                    Hmmm...

                                    1. Stamina as Health Levels I'm neutral on. It makes Stamina more valuable in one sense, while making it less valuable in another. Also, it requires rejigging all the damage numbers. So I can roll with it if done well. I personally like soak, though, and use fixed damage and soak values instead of dice pools. Works well so far.

                                    2. Fixed target numbers are solid. I don't like how V5 did it, though. I think VTR2e's method is the most transparent (one die gives 0.33 successes), but it requires rerolling 10s to give a neat number (doubling 10s gives 0.4 successes per die, which is less neat).

                                    Exalted is my preferred fixed TN system, so you aim for 7+ and 10s double. Ignore 1s unless you fail. It works out that each die gives you 0.5 successes, on average.

                                    I also really dislike 'two 10s double' in V5. It just seems unintuitive. But if you had a botch system, and made botches happen on any failed roll with two 1s, I'd hate it less, because at least there's symmetry. It just stands out as this weird exception. Which leads me to...

                                    3. I dislike BFs and MCs as they are implemented, but think they could be good. Personally, I'd make BFs much rarer (so you'd need to botch by failing and rolling two 1s, with at least one of those 1s rolled on a Hunger Die).

                                    MCs shouldn't be a thing, except on Disciplines or rolls where you Blood Surge, and I think you could probably make them an option for succeeding at a cost instead. Allow a PC to succeed if they take a messy complication on any partial success, scaling the complication according to however many successes short of the difficulty you were. In that way, the Beast pushes you through, at a cost.

                                    4. I don't like this. I'd rather something like HTV's Integrity and Code. So you have three broad categories (Initiate, Adherent. Paragon), with each representing 1-3, 4-6 and 7-10.

                                    Paths allow you to intentionally violate a breaking point in order to switch it for one from your Path, at the risk of degeneration if it doesn't take.

                                    When you have replaced all your core Humanity breaking points with those from a Path, you can start increasing your rating again.

                                    This way, you only need three broad categories of sins, sins matter for longer (you don't fail it once and then ignore it, unless you take a Bane to strike it off or have an opportunity to replace it), switching to a Path is a clearer route, and you can always add in Tenets and Convictions as additional breaking points to flesh a character out.

                                                The value of Stamina boosting Health instead of damage reduction, as noted by monteparnas, really needs to be looked at in relation to the rest of the rules.

                                                For example "first strike wins," is a less of a thing in V5's mechanics by virtue of active defense being a contested roll where the winner (even if that's the defender) is the one that does damage. In most relatively even fights, combat is going to be a series of close rolls for minimal damage. This means higher health is a huge advantage, because a protracted exchange of generally even combatants is an attrition war... and more health means you start out with more of the resource in question.

                                                Building simply to "not get hit" in V5 is perfectly doable, but not optimal if you actually want to defeat opponents in combat. If you want to avoid fights at all costs? Sure. But then Stamina as soak vs. Stamina as health is a largely irrelevant point: Stamina isn't important to not getting hit in the first place, and either version is just extending how many times you can afford get hit before running away against someone that has a chance of hitting you. If you want to get hit as little as possible, and beat opponents in combat, you're maximizing your offensive roll to use as your attack roll or your active defense roll, and then we go back to my previous point.

                                                The big difference in the overall picture really falls on armor. If you look at the CofD or Storypath systems (consider that every ST based game is moving to the more Stamina = more Health model here), access to armor is still pretty essential for a "tank" build. A VtR 2e vampire with good armor can turn blows that would be fight ending to a mortal into ones that aren't even worth spending a bloodpoint on to heal; and the more health the less important it is for them to worry about healing mid-combat instead of using blood for other things and then feed later. V5 vampires gain virtually no practical benefit from armor (or a more different way to look at it could be that all V5 vampires are innately armored). The fact that wearing plate armor, or a modern ballistic vest with metal trauma plates, means nothing against a bunch of hunters trying to pepper you with wooden shafted crossbow bolts to stake you is the biggest issue in the "dodge everything vs. tank" effectiveness topic.
