    Why Patricia of Bollingbroke sucks??

    09-16-2024, 02:33 AM
    For some reason, one of the - and against my preferred view - if not the first big Anarch, who is built in text as the greatest agitator and rebel of the Anarchs and Brujah, also lacks any weight to her doings and will. Putting it plainly she sucks, and I wished it weren't so.

    Let's start with the fact that she was the wife of a killed peasant who took a famous peasant leader as a lover, all of whom seem to be way more important than herself in her own story. And that trend continue... Robin freaking Hood Embraces her and takes her out of jail. And she takes the name of her late lover, so she buries her own identity even further in lieu of other guys.

    Than she kills the crap baron who made her miserable, which is very cool. But instead of staying in England, and fighting... I don't know... a thousand year old tyrant like Mithras, or Queen Anne, she runs away. And goes to... Spain, where she... basically stumbles on Anarchs... who basically stumble on Hardestadt's castle? which sometimes is in Bavaria and sometimes in Spain? and then a band of untrained, kind of lost fledlings, murder one of the most powerful Ventrue of the current time, in his own castle... Someone Patricia could've never had heard of before.
    And ... in the end, Hardestadt is not dead! So she probably looked like a chump?! I don't know. This 'Hardestadt is not dead' spiel always was weird for me, did they call the Anarchs out? What happened? Either way... Anarch War goes, nothing important occurs for Patricia, maybe just some agitating or not

    Then Convention of Thorns! Our Rebel Leader, who is determined to kill Hardestadt now, for some reason. Tries to kill him, and fails because guns are nothing to vampires.... she didn't tried before? She think someone with Fortitude wouldn't take those shot? And let me remind you that of all the elders she could attack, like the ones in her homeland, she decided to attack the guy she just kind of stumbled into killing, and "failed".


    And... we skip a lot of time, she changes her name; she runs, Helena comes into contact with Patricia, and bam, methuseleah puppet of Helena forever.... than they go to America (because why would two centuries-old elders go anywhere else) and bicker with Critias, because Helena. And then she starts to like... planes? funny, but I can understand it perfectly, just a bit random.
    So she jobs for Helena, then runs again to NY, and starts jobbing for Lambach Ruthven... and that's that... Great leader she is, great symbol; not even a tragic story, it just feels accidentally pathetic I don't know.

    Any information I'm missing that throws new light into her?
    Are my views a bit true?
    Is there a way to make her stand with dignity alongside Gratiano, Vykos, Dominique de Tourraine, Vasantasena, Lugoj?

    Strange... When coincidence seems too convenient, I prefer to call it fate.

          I'm not sure how much "dignity" those other Anarchs have over Tyler. Lugoj actually died, his identity stolen by the ancient he supposedly destroyed. Dominique at this point has very little influence within the sect, her ideals having been defeated in several Sabbat Civil Wars. Gratiano is either a liar or a deluded pawn who does not even know he is a pawn. Only Vykos is possibly an Anarch/Sabbat who triumphed over the long courses of the centuries, and if we don't think he is a Loyalist he is actually someone who betrayed his ideals for power.

          As for Tyler herself, I don't think someone who chooses to remember her lover means she "buries her own identity." I don't think the character has much to apologize for prior to Helena enslaving her. If she is somehow forgotten among the Sabbat, it says more for the OOC writers of the Sabbat than the NPC members of the Sabbat and the Brujah anti-tribu who should be remembering her as a pillar of the sect.

          Yeah, being enslaved by Helena is a tragedy, but there are lots of tragedies among all vampires of all sects. The Sabbat as a sect is hopelessly compromised according to the original lore, so if you are going to call Tyler out you must call out the entire sect. The only thing that distinguishes Tyler from other "failures" is that instead of being known in the Sabbat, she's known within the Camarilla instead in an alternate identity. Yet when you compare the Camarilla to the Sabbat post-third civil war, which sect is actually more in favor of freedom?

          If you really want to make Tyler a significant figure among the Anarchs and Sabbat, you just need to fill in the relatively sparse history between the diablerie of Hardestadt and the early Camarilla-Sabbat wars. Unfortunately, the writers and developers choose to not consider the Brujah anti-tribu as one of the pillars of the Sabbat. Yet any critical reading of the early sources clearly shows they should be considered one of the pillars of that sect along with the Lasombra and Tzimisce. In my chronicles, I have that be so.

                                      Fantastic post.

                                      Patricia Bollingbrook AKA Tyler is one of my favorite characters in V:TM and I'm very glad that she managed to make a return to prominence in 5th Edition proper even if it is as a "legend" the Anarchs have decided to trot out again. I feel like so many of the Chicago by Night characters got dropped like hot potatoes after CBN Second Edition and never got mentioned after that book even when they would have had major importance to things like the Anarch Movement. Transylvania Chronicles averted this with Tyler.

                                      I do think your description lacks a bit of nuance, though, that deserves to have a few points brought up.

                                      1. Being a sellout is Tyler's thing: One of the early things in the depiction of the Brujah was that they were supposed to follow a trajectory of starting as Anarchs then moving to compromisers then becoming indistinguishable from other Elders. Basically, the Winston Churchill quote about anyone who isn't a liberal while young has no heart and isn't conservative when they're old has no brain. She's an illustration of someone who was a firebrand rebel that saw her revolution fail and decided to get smart by becoming a Camarilla Elder. Even so, she helps the Anarchs behind the scenes while using them for her own ends. Probably telling herself she's playing the long game (while actually being Helena's puppet).

                                      2. The Sabbat are worse than the Camarilla: This is a matter of opinion but one that I believe Transylvania Chronicles was trying to forward. Because the Kindred who refuse to buckle down and accept the Camarilla's peace respond by...massacring an entire village of men, women, and children unrelated to the Convention of Thorns. This was my reaction when my Tremere (pretending to be Tzimisce) character on Humanity saw the aftermath of starting the Sabbat.



                                      3. Tyler isn't in charge of her own opinion anyway: As stated, she goes where Helena says for her to do and just rationalizes it the way she wants to later. This is EVERY vampire and railroady plots like TC actually are probably how they have to live. Gratiano, Lujo, and others are all dancing to the Antediluvians tunes anyway. Indeed, it's how I justify why Lucita chose to do a 180 and become Moncada.
