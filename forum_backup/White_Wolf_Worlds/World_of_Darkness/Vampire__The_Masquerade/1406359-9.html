                        Enlightenment in the Blood

                        In this section an unnamed vampire provides commentary on an ongoing series of stories about a Masquerade breach in Berlin.

                        First up, the vampire making these comments is named by himself or others. However, does identify themselves as a former Archon turned Anarch. I think it is probably Theo Bell – I cannot think of any other Archon’s who defected to the Anarchs.

                        The Masquerade breach occurs in Friedrichshain in 2017. Friedrichshain is a district in Berlin, Germany. Formerly part of East Berlin, Friedrichshain is now one of the trendy districts of Berlin and has experienced gentrification. This breach occurred near nightclub hopping crowds of mortals and involved the destruction of the then Prince of the city by Anarchs. Though not named the Prince was probably Abraham Wolff (he appears in Beckett’s Jyhad Diary).

                        The evolving story, and the comments from the former Archon, basically shows how a Masquerade breach is more of a process than an event. It also shows how these things will bring about SI involvement, and that is also a process. Do not forget that the SI itself is a major player.

                        The Anarchs are not, and cannot be, as centralized and tyrannical as the Camarilla. This means its leadership is less prone to dealing out random cruelties and humiliations. It also means the Anarchs are less able to control and cover up a Masquerade breach. In this case, Wolff had compromised and corrupted local law enforcement. None of the Anarchs had that level of control. Without his singular control, no one was around to keep the Masquerade neat and tidy. And this goes on for months, leads to the revelations by the media of wide spread corruption inside the police force and so on. It further leads to an “international task force, coordinated by Interpol” stepping in to handle the situation because of connections to international criminal syndicates. That international group is (or at least easily could be) a front for the SI.

                        Berlin is in the hands of the Anarchs, and has been since the destruction of Wolff in 2017. But how long will it take for the SI to do to Berlin what they did to London?

                        All of which leads to the idea of using a Masquerade breach, and the ensuing SI hammer drop, as a strategy in the jyhad. Some Anarchs cause a breach in a Camarilla domain, or the other way around, and then get out of the way. The SI comes in a kills a lot of vampires. This makes the domain vulnerable to the one who triggered the breach in the first place.

                                            No Prince, No Caine

                                            Right. This section. It’s not bad, or good, but that rather forgettable middle ground.

                                            Here Salvador Garcia (who Dee described as a "Che" Guevara figure - or at least he wants to be), writes about the different types of Anarch groups and individual roles of vampires in Anarch communities.

                                            According to Garcia’s essay, different Anarch groups include; a simple life (those withdrawing from the jyhad and Anarch v Camarilla battles as much as possible to do their own thing), gangs (your proto typical biker outfit or street gang or gun running outfit or smugglers or some such), cells (groups acting in a covert manner for some reason), and everything else.

                                            Different roles include; Barons (local leaders, ideally there are a number of them in any large territory the Anarch hold), Defense (keeping the Camarilla out), Emessaries (messengers and ambassadors between Barons and Anarch groups), Sweepers (Anarch who patrol the territory and eliminate problems), spies (who, well, spy on other group – usually but not always the Camarilla).

                                            Garcia also says that suicide among Anarch is high – it is high among the Camarilla as well. I wonder for which group the rate is higher?

                                            He also describes Rants, which are apparently the only time Anarchs get together – so they can bitch about the Man.

                                            As an aside, the title appears to be a play on the Irish phrase “Bona na Croin.” In English this is “Neither collar nor crown.” Which is to say, “I will not be a slave (held by a collar) and will acknowledge no king above me.” Probably all of Anarch philosophy might be boiled down to this single sentence.

                                            I appreciate the brevity of the sections in this book – and brevity is the soul of wit. However, here it could have used some examples, which Garcia does not really get into. Some of sections which follow give examples of the different groups, but they are scattered about. Likewise the idea that the only time Anarchs really get together is for a Rant is both dull and hard to believe – that’s it, really?

                                                My examples…

                                                Simple Life Group – Omega Omega Omega; an unofficial frat house at Humbolt University in Berlin. They rarely get up to the usual frat house stuff, but spend a lot of time reading ancient tomes, experimenting with things man was not meant to know, and so forth. They are home to a lot of Ipsissimus Tremere, but are not limited to that group.

                                                Gangs - Perros Malvados; a group of coyotes (human traffickers) who smuggle people across the US / Mexico border. Someone is every group they move across always disappears into the darkness with them, forever.

                                                Cells – Chicago Underground; a group of Anarchs in the Windy City trying to stay off the radar of Prince Jackson and Sheriff Damien. Baby Chorus is too well known to really be a cell.

                                                Event – The Anniversary of the death of the old prince is usually a time for Anarch to get together and enjoy themselves. This includes a Rant, but it is not limited to such an event. Barons may offer minor boons to whomever gives the best performance during the event.

                                                            Blood Vagrants

                                                            An Anarch by the name of Auntie Juniper talks about hunting and gaining admittance to Camarilla and Anarch territories when you are a nomadic vampire. Basically, lie your undead ass off. The advice she offers is solid enough, though I debate her dismissal of hotels. For one thing, you can hang that like “Do not clean” sign on your door. For another, you can hide under the bed.

                                                            In any case, this section has a punchy style, and offers generally solid guidance.

                                                            It does occur to me that a vampire could probably make a killing by owning a hotel, or motel, and providing some “special” services to itinerant vampires who are just passing through.

                                                            Blood of the Redeemer

                                                            I used to live in Alabama, and this passage is set in Alabama, which amuses me. Here a preacher smoothly, and with a large amount of chutzpah, inverts the actual and clear moral of a Biblical passage into something that endorses vampires and vampiric behavior.

                                                            I’ve not known any actual vampire preachers, but I’ve known bloody minded preachers and others who distort Biblical teaching smoother than an experienced gambler can perform a card trick.

                                                            With the collapse of the traditional Sabbat, the Camarilla has moved from being a strictly secular institution to one which does permit pursuit if faith, assuming some elder benefits from that pursuit. This section does address Anarch spirituality, such as it is, and acts as a counterpart to the Camarilla’s efforts.

                                                            That said, the only thing that keeps this Preacher in the Anarchs (as compared to the Camarilla) is likely his theatrically. Because nothing in his statements or message are political, or against anything the Camarilla does or says.

                                                            The section also makes for an interesting Masquerade breach just asking for SI involvement.

                                                            The Parasite Way

                                                            Here Agata “that sound made when to run your finger horizontally across your lips quickly” Sterek discusses vampire nature with a vampire named Rudi. This is probably the Rudi who wrote “Monsters of the Recent Past.”

                                                            Anyway, Sterek compares vampires to tapeworms, blood diseases and fungal infections. Again, Sterek is written with the fictional insanity – which is to say writers who have done no research into insanity say she is crazy, and the depiction of it are all over the place. Even so, Sterek has the strongest and most recognizable voice of any of the characters in this book, and the clearest grasp of vampire nature. That she has no illusions about her nature, and vampire nature, might be way others take her to be insane.

                                                            Also, she is a bit like the Joker in that she is not as crazy as she seems, or at least more rational than people assume. To wit, Sterek’s fucking with Rudi via a death threat.

                                                            The section is brief, and amusing but ends with an error in that the last lines are needlessly repeated.
