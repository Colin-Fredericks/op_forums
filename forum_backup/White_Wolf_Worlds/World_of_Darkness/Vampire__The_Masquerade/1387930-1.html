        Originally posted by Rock113 View Post
        So in the WoD I see three different theories:
        1. ALL VAMPIRES are infernalists, which mainly comes from the Werewolf I think. Garou think all vampires are minions of Wyrm except few who has Humanity 7+ or follows Path of Beast and so on. Wan Gui think they are wicked, destroying karma, and the messengers of the Sixth Age. Other splats also wield an attitude which is not much better.
        One of the ideas is that as a vampire's humanity gets lower, they start to stink more and more of evil (or the Wyrm). Those who follow Paths always do (except I think the Road of Heaven?). Most vampires end up coming off as evil. But there are exceptions. I remember mention of alliances between the Silent Striders and the Children of Osiris in their mutual battles against the Followers of Set. That said, some vampires sell their souls to demons, but not all of them do. They might be Wyrm-ridden creatures in Werewolf terminology, but they're not Infernalists.

        With the Wan Kuei it is a bit different. The Hengeyokai courts recognize them as the inheritors of the Wan Xian and have certain levels of alliances and respect for them even Devil Tigers (even demons have a place, the problem is when they don't stick to their place), and of course when a Wan Kuei crosses the line and acts like a monster or attacks those the Hengeyokai choose to protect they don't hesitate to put them down.
        Originally posted by Rock113 View Post
        2. Sabbat are infernalists. They make a deal with Black Spiral Dancers and Pentex, which are great spawns of Wyrm. Demonic Investments are legal among them. I can't remember other materials but IIRC they are considered "wicked" and "infernal" by most supernaturals
        Not really. The Sabbat prizes freedom and independence. Selling your soul to become a demon's eternal slave goes against that. The Sabbat even has a full on Inquisition that goes around searching for vampires who have become practicing Infernalists to kill them. That said, there are definitely Infernalists among the Sabbat, and it's safe to say among the Camarilla too. The Sabbat does have alliances, to some degree, with Black Spiral Dancers but more in the sense that both groups believe they're using the other to their own end and that those alliances typically don't go much further than hiring out some muscle and those alliances are temporary at best.
        Originally posted by Rock113 View Post
        3. Baali are infernalists. Needless ot say, make a deal with Demon. They're even considered infernalists by other "infernal" kindreds including "infernal" Sabbat
        The Baali are basically split up into three groups. The first group are full on Infernalists. They sell their souls to demons, make pacts with horrible monsters and look to damn the world. For the most part their goal is, "Become a favored slave prince when the demon I sold my soul to takes over." Traditionally most groups thought that all Baali were part of this group. For the most part these guys were Embraced and taught to become Infernalists. For most Infernalists it tends to be an individual route but for these guys you have entire families of Baali Infernalists who all pledge their souls to the same demon and work together.

        The second group are the opposite - they know there are horrible creatures (The Children) that sleep beneath the earth and if those things wake up that's it for everyone (in Werewolf terms they would be super-powerful bane spirits, each on par with something like the Storm Eater). These Baali commit atrocities in order to keep those things asleep forever since the more violence in the world the better they slumber. These Baali are very low humanity (at best) and in their actions there's little distinction between what these Baali do and what the Infernalists do. They both act like monsters, both do the most heinous things, it's just the second group believes it's commit atrocities for the greater good. Again, most people don't make distinction between these guys and the Infernalists because their actions and the end results essentially end up being identical. Whether you're burning down the orphanage because your Demon Lord told you to, or you're burning down the orphanage because you believe if you don't some monster will wake up doesn't really change the fact that you burned down an orphanage.

        There's a third group of Baali that's really mysterious and nobody knows much about them at all, what they're doing or what they have planned (they're more for ST's to do their own thing). They have their own Path (Path of the Hive) so in Werewolf terms most of them would come out as extremely Wyrm tainted.

                Back in earlier editions I tended to assume that the Sabbat had a tremendous amount of wealth. Sure the Camarilla might have a fair amount more, and the Giovanni might have a little bit less (though they were one Clan with a fairly strict hierarchy so it was easier for them to direct that money), but regardless the Sabbat had enough cash to to do pretty much anything it wants and no doubt it would have been comparable to the GDP of a major country. Zettler may have tithed hundreds of millions or even billions of dollars to the Sabbat every year, in but it would have been the proverbial bucket of water into a lake.

                In 5th edition, with the defection of the Lasombra, I imagine the Sabbat's financial security tanked considerably. The Lasombra were the Clan most likely to accumulate and amass wealth, and I have to imagine they took every cent they possibly could. Further, with many of the Elder's being beckoned/leaving to go fight, the Sabbat likely lost access to a lot of what wealth was left (having Elder's leave and no longer having access to their money is no doubt an issue for the Camarilla as well).

                It might also be worth considering the Second Inquistion. No doubt when the Inquisition decides to go after a wealthy vampire, one thing they try and do is freeze that vampire's assets to the best of their ability. Sabbat vampires tend to be a bit more openly inhuman, and thus are more easily able to be identified as vampires. As a result, they would be more likely to get targeted by the Second Inquistion and have their assets frozen by various governments.

                In this situation, with the Lasombra taking much of the Sabbat's wealth, the Sabbat losing access to much of its remaining wealth when the Elders are Beckoned, and then finally the Second Inquisition freezing chunks of money the Sabbat has left, suddenly the money Zettler sends to the Sabbat would be much, much more important than it used to be.

                        Harold Zettler dates back to an ancient edition of the games when the Sabbat might as well be buddies with the Black Spiral Dancers, because both are meant to be unplayable antagonists. Notably, he's never appeared in a Vampire book. He's a Werewolf brand (tm) character.

                        By Revised Edition, the writers' interest in making the Sabbat playable went to such an extent that the Sabbat's Infernalist Regent, Melinda Galbraith, was killed offstage and between books. The divorcing of the Sabbat from Infernalism in the years leading up to the publication of Gehenna came straight from White Wolf.

                        Each edition and its corresponding take on these characters fit the needs of the time. They can't all be canon if they contradict each other, and if one of them wasn't canon handed down from the mountaintop on stone tablets, then none of them were. Take what's useful at your gaming table and don't worry about the rest.

                        One potential use for all of these different interpretations could be to possibly have the NPC's of your world have a similar variety of beliefs about who's a devil worshipper and who isn't.

                        ETA: In the interest of disclosure, I have a nostalgic soft spot for the Sabbat as Infernalists. When the Lasombra were first introduced, there wasn't much more to them than, "they have spooky powers, they don't respect the Masquerade, and they have a Spanish name." They were Armand, from Interview With The Vampire, with the serial numbers filed off, and I loved them for it. I love how you can still see Armand's complicated relationship with his own damnation in several of the various iterations of the Path of Night that have been published over the years.

                                        Harold Zettler dates back to an ancient edition of the games when the Sabbat might as well be buddies with the Black Spiral Dancers, because both are meant to be unplayable antagonists. Notably, he's never appeared in a Vampire book. He's a Werewolf brand (tm) character.

                                        By Revised Edition, the writers' interest in making the Sabbat playable went to such an extent that the Sabbat's Infernalist Regent, Melinda Galbraith, was killed offstage and between books. The divorcing of the Sabbat from Infernalism in the years leading up to the publication of Gehenna came straight from White Wolf.
                                        the BSD's purpose was to protect sabbat cities during the day while sabbat would protect during the night,
                                        Zettler's purpose was to hide Pentex's agenda to the Sabbat,
                                        that was the case in 2nd ed, where sabbat were playable

                                        of the three possible scenarios for the regent's death, the most likely one was that she was killed by 'another' infernalist (a nahualli to be specific)

                                        Demons living on Sabbat ground was a recurring theme, all major sabbat cities except milan had one, they were simultaneously a threat and a protection for the sabbat (and that stayed true in revised)

                                        the murder of the regent, and the retcon of the BSD/Pentex in revised wasn't a divorce from Infernalism, it was a change in how Infernalism was portrayed and how it played in Sabbat stories

                                        Infernalism prior to revised was pretty bad,

                                        - the Infernalists were too prolific (couldn't throw a stone without hitting one),
                                        - their ideology was flawed, infernalism was a bad deal in every way,
                                        since half the sabbat had blood magic or massive stat blocks, there was no reason to resort to infernalism for power or anything,
                                        most Infernalists were either edgy, tricked by some cultist or forced by their demon lord, and most had pledged their souls
                                        - they relied too much on dark thaumaturgy plot devices, and the conflict between infernalists and non-infernalists was reduced to who has the best plot device/magic
                                        - the Decani didn't follow the traditional Demon paradigm, they had no need for pacts (except for souls) and could just force anyone into slavery

                                        in Revised, they fixed most of (not all) of the issues:

                                        - sabbat power was scaled back, so they were more pressed for power, making infernalism a tempting offer
                                        - the conflicts between infernalists and non-infernalists became more ideological and less about who has the better toys,
                                        (exemple, Koldun would know infernalists in their circles, but would just discourage the practice without freaking out and resorting to immediate hostilities)
                                        - Demons no longer needed vampiric souls (though some spirits still did), they now rely more on tempting vampires with pacts, and selling one's soul isn't automatic (in most cases)
                                        - Infernalists are less prolific, they are chosen more carefully.
