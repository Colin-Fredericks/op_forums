        It's extremely bizarre, what you just said.
        It completely ignores all previous editions, as the Sabbat being playable isn't some "un-canon" new notion – they've been playable since 1992.
        The very first guide to the Sabbat released back then was the Players' Handbook to the Sabbat.

        As for Good vs Evil, originally Vampire was designed as a shades-of-grey game and the original 1st edition contained an essay where the Dotmeister talked about how the game was meant as an exploration of evil, with players playing as the monster.

        The Camarilla and the Anarchs are not "the Good vampires", they are still fucking vampires and their entire society is a den of snakes.
        Intrigue and betrayals are core parts of vampire society and player characters (even fluffy bunny Anarchs) can absolutely be nasty, violent or treacherous individuals.

        In the Revised Edition's Guide to the Sabbat there is an entire chapter devoted to discussing the Sabbat's supposed "evil" and the appeal to players.
        There were entire essays across many Vampire: The Masquerade books devoted to quashing any notion of "Camarilla = good, Sabbat = evil".

        Comparing Vampire with Star Wars is… Extremely wrong. In Vampire, you play monsters, not superheroes (or psionic space paladins).
        A closer comparison would be if Exalted suddenly turned around and removed Dragon-Blooded and Abyssals as playable characters, making them NPC antagonists only.

        Also, this entire statement is just confounding and smacks of 80s D&D attitudes.

        You know what? I don't mind if you maintain that "Sabbat = Evil".
        As consumers, we gamers have repeatedly demonstrated that people fucking love playing evil characters.


        Ok, let's step away from RPGs for just a second.
        Dead by Daylight is a very successful video game. It's not the biggest one out there, but it has many times the numbers of all Vampire video games and every Shadowrun video game combined and ever since its release DBD has only seen growth.
        It's a multiplayer survival-horror game where four survivors try to evade a monstrous killer tasked with preventing their escape.

        Here's the kicker: you can play as the killer.

        In fact, all the killers in the game are player-controlled. That was the selling point of the game: as a survivor you're not just trying to dupe some shoddy AI ("NPC antagonist") – you're facing off against a human player behind the controls.
        That premise appealed to a lot of people and while there are many more survivor players than there are killers, there are nevertheless many killers and many people who only play killer.

        To date, DBD has done better than every single one of its competitors.

        As for the struggle against the Beast being central to the game – that theme was still central for the Sabbat in previous editions and I don't understand how suddenly Sabbat characters would have "no place for that".
        In Revised Edition and V20, most Sabbat characters still maintained Humanity (albeit in low scores). Paths of Enlightenment were ways for vampires to contend with the Beast – but contend with the Beast is something that every vampire, Sabbat or otherwise, has to do on a nightly basis.

                            Originally posted by Serpent Axis View Post

                            Paths of Enlightenment were ways for vampires to contend with the Beast – but contend with the Beast is something that every vampire, Sabbat or otherwise, has to do on a nightly basis.

                            I am not convinced by your logic, not at all.

                            Indeed, because "The Beast" is misnamed. That thing inside a vampire would happily jump face-first into a wood-chipper to sate its hunger or rage. That's nothing like, say, a wolf, which avoids suicidal situations and is loyal to its mate, cubs, and pack. Most Sabbat paths are trying to emulate that sort of tribalism in some form.

                            Feral Heart is the most obvious, but Honorable Accord is also basically about being loyal to your own and indifferent to everyone else, just with different details. Even Power and the Inner Voice is often about increasing the strength of the tribe via competition, respect for successful leaders, and only replacing those leaders when you are a genuinely superior choice. The Sabbat are tribalists, and have encouraged Paths or variations on Paths that that support fighting the badly misnamed "Beast" with tribalism.

                            You don't resist "the Beast" because you are human, you resist the "Beast" because not resisting it hurts your tribe. Not resisting the "Beast" causes behavior that would make the Feral Hearter hurt her pack. Not resisting the "Beast" causes behavior that would make the Honorable Accorder break his oaths of loyalty to the Archbishop. Not resisting the "Beast" causes behavior that would make the Power and the Inner Voiceer be unworthy of being called a wise leader destined to rise in the Sabbat. The Sabbat resist the "Beast" via variations on tribalism rather than humanity.

                            Indeed, the "Beast's" behavior is so unbeastlike that I prefer to call it the Inner Demon.

                                                            I sometimes have the reputation on these forums as the V5 cheerleader. I may be overestimating my importance but I do feel that I sometimes am the guy taking the vanguard of defending it while singing its praises.

                                                            Reviving Chicago does a lot for my good will as does the Blood Gods.

                                                            Still, I am a long-time Sabbat player and while I tend to play the Sabbat as a dysfunctional mess that is always on the verge of imploding, I do that with a kind of RED DEAD REDEMPTION 2 sort of feeling that while they're murderers, outlaws, lunatics, and psychopaths--they're also a weird sort of family that has their own internal logic.

                                                            I love the occult, demonic, alien Call of Cthulhu Cult thing combined with the fact that they're also the most EXTREME version of the Anarchs. So devoted to the cause of freedom that they have arguably lost all real freedom. They're a tremendously fun bunch of vampires and they also don't get the central premise of V:TM lost in the process.

                                                            "You are playing the Bad Guy."

                                                            V:TM has its share of Louis, Nick Knight, and Angel types. However, you're usually playing Lestat, The Lost Boys (David), or Dracula rather than the good guys. You are drinkers of blood and mobsters who seek power over mortals as well as USUALLY have the dubious honor of being less evil than the guy against you--for now.

                                                            The Sabbat are IMPORTANT to Vampire: The Masquerade because they represent "but for the grace of Caine go I" and in Camarilla and Anarch games were needed as a necessary third party to get them to work together under extreme circumstances. However, there's no reason not to let people play them.

                                                            You're already a serial killer and gangster. One of the silliest ever things to happen in gaming was when TSR folded like a deck of cards to BADD and tried to remove references to demons and devils in their game (Baatezu! Ta'anari!). You know, despite the fact that they were still embodiments of evil living in the afterlives of gods. For Mystra's sake. I'm really hoping this is not something motivated by controversy and I have too much respect for White Wolf's creators to think it is.

                                                            So I don't get it.

                                                            I love V5 but the absence of the Sword of Caine has been a conspicuous from the main book onward. The Gehenna Crusade is something I wanted information on and updates for the Paths of Enlightenment. I'm hoping that this book will contain everything you need to make Sabbat PCs as well as NPCs but I still can't help but be confused by the decision to not provide a book with the assumption of Sabbat PCs. The barn door has been open for 25 years. Both the Camarilla and Anarchs in V5 have been portrayed as complete scumbags.

                                                            Why no Sabbat PCs?

                                                            I'd like to know.

                                                            Humor Mode: I should note my current character is a Malkavian Anarch that's a member of the Church of Caine. One of my players pointed out, "Why not just play a Sabbat member?"
