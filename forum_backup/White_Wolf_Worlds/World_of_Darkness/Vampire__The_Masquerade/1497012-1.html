    To really make "Blood Potency" work in VtM, it needs to be rebalanced with "Generation" reworked to match. I have house rules from the days of Requiem where we used BP, and Generation set your starting BP (under the idea that a powerful sire gets you a more powerful start), and set the upper cap of BP (which goes up naturally for every 1K years of unlife, harkening back to the original methuselah concept). My group thought this gave a "best of both worlds" approach of both not making Generation the end-all-be-all for Vampires, as well as expanding on what exactly Diablerie could do in the moment.

    As for Hunger Dice, I'm split. I like the idea in concept but like others, its execution in practice leaves something to be desired. Around the same time we tried out house-ruling BP, we added a more complex hunger system that was, in retrospect, more than a little fiddly and still needed book-keeping. The desire was to put more emphasis on the act of feeding itself, with hunger and humanity influencing your ability to control how you feed. I'd have to dig up the old .doc file, but it was basically along the lines of "feed frequently to avoid Hunger, but increased risk of being unable to stop when you want and risk feeding frenzies and masquerade breeches." It encouraged my players to keep a more RP-ish eye on feeding as something more than just "filling up the tank" for their super-powers.

            Originally posted by Rhywbeth View Post
            I actually sometimes prefer the hunger dice to regular frenzy mechanics, though not clarifying that you don't apply them to every single roll came a bit late so many people still think that's how they work.
            Do you have a page reference for that? Because I think many people would prefer that.

            Hunger itself is something I'm currently playing around with for V20 purposes. But I have issues with it.
            Same, really. I like the idea, just not the execution. And I don't like that Hunger Dice actually require you to roll a lot more in some cases (where you'd previously spend blood as a reflexive action) while making you avoid rolling in others (such as where you take a conscious action). That seems the wrong way around.

            I've played around with Hunger Conditions, Hunger Levels (making the blood pool more like the health track with penalties at each level), Hunger Dice on their own, blood pool with Hunger Dice at certain thresholds, and so on. Hunger Conditions work well, especially if you tie bonuses to them. Hunger Dice less so.

            You can roll your blood pool for things, to make it more active. But it only really feels dangerous below five dice, so I gave up on that. Maybe you could remove the WP track altogether and use current blood pool to resist things, but that would require some work. I kind of like the idea of current blood serving as a measure of restraint and composure, but that would probably lead to people topping off their vitae more frequently to keep their "WP" up. And then everyone would have ~8-10 WP all the time.

            So I keep coming back to the idea that Hunger just doesn't work in isolation and that what you need is a system that reflects the Beast, in all its aspects, and not just Hunger.

            So, perhaps use something like the VTR2e model: when you succeed in resisting frenzy, you get a Condition like Gluttonous (Hunger Frenzy triggers), Monstrous (Rage Frenzy triggers) or Animalistic (Fear Frenzy triggers). Only if you score 5+ successes do you avoid that. Failure results in frenzy as usual.

            In some cases (e.g., low difficulty triggers), you could simply take the Condition instead of rolling. There's less risk of frenzy, but you have to roleplay the Beast as a trade-off.

            Likewise, if you succeed on a degeneration roll, you might gain a Condition like Guilty (Conscience) or Ashamed (Conviction) unless you score 5+ successes. Failure might trigger the Callous Condition for a scene, as your Humanity slips for a moment.

            Essentially, the Beast is less of a binary on/off thing, and more of a spectrum of behaviour that rarely gets switched entirely on or off. And the more triggers you hit, the more these Conditions stack, resulting in a big explosion if you don't release the pressure, but being less frequent and less random than Hunger Dice.

            Or, as I mentioned before, you could replace all those Conditions with a Beast Nature that emerges in different situations, with different resolution conditions based on the trigger. Your Beast Nature thus becomes dominant in play, and you can't regain WP or restore your Human Nature until you give in just enough to satisfy its needs.

            The different resolution conditions would be tailored for your Beast Nature. The Barbarian maybe resolves Rage Frenzy triggers when it dominates or humiliates a rival of equal or greater strength. Maybe it resolves Hunger Frenzy triggers when it gluts more than twice its blood pool in a single scene. Maybe it resolves Fear Frenzy triggers when it punishes anyone who saw its moment of weakness.

            Each Beast Nature might also have one additional trigger that's unique to it -- so, for the Barbarian, it might be being shown up as stupid or weak, because it threatens your desire for control through might. For the Demon, it might be seeing innocence being defended, because that insults your inner need to corrupt and pervert. For the Bandit, it may be seeing others prosper because of something you don't have. And so on.

            I picture the format being like Archetypes with a few extra bits added, so:

            Barbarian
            The Barbarian embodies rage, "might is right" and "the law of the jungle". In life, you were likely passionate but perhaps a little conceited, and competitive but occasionally a sore loser. Now, those flaws have been warped by your Hunger to become something far worse. When gripped by your Beast Nature, you attempt to assert control via force of will and flesh, and break those who would defy you. You do not suffer fools and despise weakness, and eschew subtlety for gross displays of your prowess.
            Rage: When enraged, you seek to dominate and humiliate others. You may defeat a target who displays equal or greater prowess in your Primary Attribute category, or humiliate a target who commands authority and respect above your own.
            Hunger: When tempted, you attempt to gorge yourself or satisfy your desires. Find a vessel and consume them whole. Keep drinking until your blood pool is full.
            Fear: When afraid, you seek to make others even more afraid than you are. No witnesses should be allowed to think of you as vulnerable.
            Special: You despise being shown as weak in any area. This counts as an additional rage frenzy trigger (difficulty 6). You will respond by punishing the person who reveals your vulnerability, or else by asserting your dominance over a target who is strong where you are weak.

            (Needs work, but you get the idea.)

            You could also add incentives like VTR2e's system of beats, so that players choose to roleplay the Beast in little increments along the way. Maybe you can retroactively add (10-Humanity) dice to a failed roll to try and succeed via the strength of the Beast, at the cost of flipping to your Beast Nature for a number of turns. This would be the alternative to a Messy Critical, and entirely optional.

            You could also replace Blood Surge/the Quickening with a single bonus of +(10-Humanity) dice, with those bonus dice causing your Beast Nature to assert itself for one turn for every 1 rolled on one of those dice.

            Or maybe the 1s imbalance your Willpower (like in C20), so you circle one WP point (starting with the leftmost uncircled box) for every 1 rolled, and when you spend the WP point associated with that box, you face another frenzy trigger.

            When you resolve a frenzy trigger according to your Beast Nature, you remove the rightmost circle from around that box. When you frenzy, you lose all of them (and refresh your WP?).

            These are just random thoughts, but I think this shows that there are other ways to do this that don't rely entirely on dice rolls.

            Ideally, players should *want* to play their dark sides a little bit, rather than feeling forced by randomness to derail the game. You do that by making the experience fun/a guilty pleasure, and giving the PCs more control over how that dark side presents itself.

                                        The history of VTM kind of reminds me of the history of Monopoly. When Monopoly was first created, it had two rule sets. One was much like the current game, where you try to unironically play a capitalist seeking to horde all the wealth. The other rule set was one in which you basically went socialist and everyone benefited. The idea was that you would play the capitalist version, decide it sucked, and then play the socialist one forever. It was basically an educational tool.

                                        There was one problem: no one played the socialist version. Everyone preferred to unironically play a greedy capitalist. Soon even the option to play the socialist version was dropped, because it was a waste of space in the box.

                                        VTM feels similar. The first edition was really a game about vampire-hunters cursed to be vampires seeking to become human again by killing the vampires who had cursed them. Except almost no one played it like that. Most players wanted to unironically enjoy playing vampires. They wanted to hold fancy parties where they dressed up the latest fashions and drank blood out of wineglasses. They wanted to leap across rooftops with their superhuman strength. They wanted to fly through the sky as bats.

                                        The game adapted to this, created options for players who wanted to unironically play vampires, and not humans laboring under a curse they would soon lift by killing their sires.

                                        This edition feels like an angry rejection of that adaptation. It's as if the makers of Monopoly twisted your arm behind your back, and shouted, "DAMMIT, YOU'RE NOT SUPPOSED TO LIKE THE UNIRONICALLY CAPITALIST RULE SET! THAT RULE SET IS ONLY IN THE GAME FOR YOU TO HATE! YOU'RE SUPPOSED TO PLAY THAT ONCE, THEN SWITCH TO SOCIALISM FOREVER!"

                                        It's as if the latest makers of Monopoly were so mad that you weren't following the original intent of the game, that they removed the unironically capitalist rule set from the game, only included the socialist version from a hundred years ago, and even deliberately included special mechanics just to make it hard for players to even houserule the unironically capitalist version of the game back into existence. Just twisting the arms of Monopoly players behind their back as hard as they can while shouting, "PLAY SOCIALISTS! LOVE SOCIALISTS! GOD DAMN YOU TO HELL LOVE PLAYING SOCIALISTS! AAARRRRRGH!!!!"

                                                    I write vampire fiction in addition to doing vampire games and one of the things I discussed with my horror publishers was the fact that if you portray vampirism as all positives, you are missing like 90% of the fun.

                                                    I don't think "woe is me" is fun but you know what is fun? Fucking Spider-Man. And Spider-Man is always getting shit from his supowers. It creates an endless amount of fun for his drama and Spider-Man doesn't have to eat people.

                                                    As a simple tool, you should always have vampirism have MAJOR drawbacks that interfere in your life: The Beast, The Hunger, Sunlight, Rotschrek, or the Masquerade to compensate for your powers. Not because you're FORCING your players to deal with this.

                                                    But that if the players can go off and lament the horror of their condition, they have ample tools for it.

                                                    Vampirism with no drawbacks is silly and uninteresting, IMHO. You can have a vampire, and I've played plenty, who sees it as a good trade off but the limitations are part of the fun.

                                                    If I were to make a comparison, it's like doing a Mobster game where you have no issues of morality or betrayal. You just love being a gangster. Yeah, that's a valid playstyle but there's FUN to be had with the downsides.

                                                    Goodfellas and the Godfather benefit from the downsides.
