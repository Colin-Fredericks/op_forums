            I think that the two of you are more arguing on the definition of the Masquerade rather than what the situation is in present.

            Morbus - The Argument that it's not "Broken" is that it's not public knowledge that Vampires exist, they're still working to cover up their activities from the general populace.
            Yes, this is the worst situation for vampires being hunted since the times of the Inquisition (When Vampires realized the need for Masquerade instead of Silence-of-the-blood) but in general if someone on the street says that they "Saw a Vampire" or their defense for killing someone was that "they were a vampire" then they're still treated as crazy, because the truth isn't in public awareness yet.

            If the Masquerade was "Broken" completely, then the protections that Vampires do wouldn't help hide them because the secret is out. Like the Sookie Stackhouse series of books and TV - Vampires are out of the coffin, if you say "A Vampire kidnapped me" the police investigate it instead of throwing you in an asylum.

            I think that the intent of the designers is to make everyone more aware that it's an issue and _close_ to breaking.

                Yes honestly I'm seeing a lot of random detritus getting in the way of the story. The classic comparison for me is how VtM: Bloodlines handles feeding compared with how V5 handles feeding. VtMB has a huge plot all about plaguebearers and how careless feeding spreads these diseases. Both the Anarchs and Tremere want you to look into it, and both reveal a lot about themselves and how they view the other when you engage with those offers. You learn a lot about the strengths and weaknesses of both groups from such a simple thing. We're seeing how feeding habits, hunting domain, and the "ecology" of Vampires mix together to give a story. The whole thing is about feeding, but in a very abstract sense.

                As you go through you discover more is afoot, and uncover a huge cult of world-ending vampiric typhoid maries.

                Compare with how V5 seemingly wants you to devote endless RP and rolls to... feeding on individual kine? In relatively normal situations? Oh boy, I do remember my favourite thing in VtMB, the bit where I ran into a nightclub to drink for a few seconds from a dancer and then leave again. What excitement. Such a bracing story.

                Same goes RE hunters. Wider narratives should trump a ubiquitous hunter menace.

                Think about it like a movie set on an island. Focus on the wider issue of reliably getting clean water, reliably getting fresh food, and figuring out how to defend yourselves. Don't focus on the minutia once a system is in place. So when a Vampire feeds it should be an issue of functionality. Conflicts over ACCESS to feeding, over the safety and reliability of feeding; those are real stories! "You can't do that, it's so obvious. Hunters will find you." The drama that unfolds from finding a new niche when your old one isn't possible, and having to learn new skills. V5 seems to have lost sight of that. And it applies to everything else too, sadly.

                            Agree. I'm saying since the development, practically, that this whole focusing on blood and feeding is misleading. It sounds convincing that "this is the core of the vampiric experience" and stuff like that. It's great when a movie focuses on it in a good way (like Only Lovers Left Alive showed the rush caused by drinking the blood), or when you're playing a one-shot LARP, focusing on it, or a suchlike tabletop game, or the first few sessions as a new vampire player. Because it's basic sustenance, you're doing all-the-fucking-time. That's why. It's a thing you do to be able to do the more interesting stuff. Yes, it's fun to do a detailed feeding scene, with all the hunting and/or seduction once in a while, but when you're forced, or expected to do them all the time, it starts losing its sheen pretty fast and let's face it, most groups will just handwave it away.

                                        1. to me sustainability isn't the goal, outliving the other kindred who can't keep their nose clean is.

                                        2. This is why you hunt smarter, which v5 emphasizes that need to, which ties into #1.

                                        - Generations shouldn't be removed, at most deemphasized, generation is still dictating of some elements of V5/Vampires such as siring and also how high some powers can go. Also I believe it should take some emphasis in the organized clans & sects where generation has always mattered.

                                        - I'm of the belief that embracing should come at a cost, namely it should kick start your hunger (like automatically getting hunger points) or more extreme, should cost you experience. in the former it stops people from being able to do it willy nilly out of nowhere, in the latter it does that AND it has you as player/character investing into this new kindred with something you can't get back without it being as extreme as humanity or willpower. I say extreme as if you're trying to save someone who's otherwise dying or willingly wishes to be embraced, giving/losing humanity feels a bit high of a cost for saving someone's life/doing what they requested. In the case of willpower that's also potentially a high cost as well and it's effecting your character's ability to function as they were before the embrace.

                                        - Kiss should remain the same, at most alter it slighly due to the nature and intimacy of the act, if it comes as the result of violence I think it should be a traumatic moment for most, similar to an assault of any kind even if they're vague on the details. If it comes as the result of a night out hook up deal, then it should feel kind of just bleh/mixed on it. and of course in the POV of a blood doll, it should be kind of the rush.

                                        - Distortion and everything should be rather per clan basis, some should rather be self reliant on their own abilities (like gangrel's natural physical speed and agility or Nos' stealth focus and technology centric identity) while some can use tech or make up techniques to hide in plain sight and others should have natural repellant to it due to disciplines/clan advantages.
