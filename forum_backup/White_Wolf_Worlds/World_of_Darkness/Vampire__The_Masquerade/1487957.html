      REPOST:

      I once did some notes up for Healer Salubri that were able to survive in Africa, the Middle East, and Asia that the Tremere eventually had to realize that they were never going to be able to deal with for a "Global Camarilla" campaign I had. The rub being the Tremere discovered most of them gave absolutely no shits about the diablerie of Saulot and were powerful enough the Tremere had to make peace with.

      The interpretation I went with was admittedy much darker but not ditching the concept entirely. The idea of trying to make a Clan of Salubri that has the same basic concept but dark enough to fit with the other Clans as well as a game of personal horror.

      Here's my notes:

      * The Salubri Clan weakness remained being unable to feed from unwilling vessels. Unwilling being defined as those not entranced, involuntarily there, or otherwise resisting. They are masters of loophole abuse on this.
      * The Salubri Embrace doctors, morticians, hospice owners, religious leaders, and people involved in the medical trade.
      * Lots of people do die because of the Salubri influence but they try to minimize their "vampire footprint."
      * They are heavily involved in the local blood trades and frequently pass off spikes in deaths as the result of disease outbreaks.
      * Salubri have their own Roads that have survived since the Dark Ages that basically amount to "God said it was okay to feed on you" and "Feeding from the already dying and elderly is fine."
      * Some Salubri blood bond people they consider undesirable and keep herds that are left to lose ghouldom but not their devotion. These people are willing vessels they feed on over the course of years until death.
      * Other vampires consider Salubri less saints and more, "Hospital feeders."
      * Local variants of the Salubri included Animalism and Presence to go with Obeah because these allow a lot more feeding alternatives.

          I would do a heavy overhaul with them, but it is almost entirely fluff and lore reworks, rather than mechanics.

          Make them a Camarilla clan, with the Healers being numerous, and the Warriors being the fabled low generation group who act as watch dogs for the return of the Baali. If nothing else, it would smooth out the Camarillas stewardship of mortals and help cement the pro humanity path ideal.

          Considering the long history of problems between the Brujah and Ventrue, yet they can still be factional allies, the Salubri and Tremere wouldn't be that big of an issue work on the same side. Just rework the Salubri purge narrative from the fluff and have them purge the Warrior branch of the line due to some leftover demonic taint from the Baali wars. Yeah the Salubri are going to be cranky at the Tremere for purging a portion of the clan and eating their founder, but that is only in the last 500 years, where as the Brujah/Ventrue feud is millennia long at this point.

          The Salubri Healers are still a hard mode play style due to their feeding restrictions and low initial combat potential, which retains their feel but it makes more sense from a setting perspective.

                  I'd pretty much ignore the purge to begin with, it is wholly unnecessary for this. There was a conflict where the Tremere Diablerized a lot of their elders, but a full blown persecution simply isn't necessary. Before the Camarilla the Salubri simply couldn't politically stand to the Prestation machine of the Tremere and after, if the Gangrel and Nosferatu could agree to end hostilities with the Warlocks in exchange for a peace treaty, why wouldn't the Salubri? I also personally find any clan whatsoever being attached to crimes against half-forgotten elders a ludicrous idea.

                  Warrior Salubri are rare and extremely careful to Embrace as they were in the Dark Ages. Many act as enforcers or on military roles of some kind for the Camarilla. Healers are the bulk of the clan and somehow compensate greater numbers and political presence with the expected lessened demand on moral compliance. They offer the benefits of Obeah in a similar way the Tremere offer magic, sometimes with competition arising, others with both clans reaching a profitable partnership.

                  One thing I would borrow from the Wu-Zao is the ability to switch Castes: with help of a member from the other caste, a Salubri can go through a process that change their Discipline between Obeah and Valeren (even if they have it at 0 dots). Some few can even chose to Embrace into the other caste instead of their own, although this is a rare ability, rarer still to be used, but not unheard of.

                  Healers that want to become Warriors must go through rigorous selection as if it was for the Embrace, or present proof of dire need for such change. On the other hand, Healers accept to turn Warriors with ease, frequently receiving among them those Warriors that failed the high standards of their peers. Sometimes this is used so the Warriors can muster higher numbers for a coming battle, dismissing the extra hands after as Healers, the "Veterans".

                  And finally, if there is a member of the clan among the Inner Circle, at this point I would just put Saulot himself there. If there's one Ante to whom the position fits, it's him.

                                                  I'm perfectly fine with the original depiction of the Salubri.

                                                  Vampires who heal or act humanely is not any kind of trope that is needed in a vampire game. An individual vampire who reacts against the horror of being an evil monster that lusts after the blood of the living is fine. You can have various of those. They prop up from time to time in modern vampire literature. But an entire lineage of them is not fulfilling any kind of genre need.

                                                  The only reason they exist is that they serve a specific role in the setting lore - they explain why House Tremere of the Ars Magica game became a vampire clan despite not having the same vampire origins as the other clans. The Salubri were created specifically to be destroyed. That being the case, it was OK for them to be very unvampire-like vampires. It would explain why they were not many of them, or why the other clans quickly agreed to wipe them out - because they never liked them anyway. (Let's face it, while the Tremere started it, they could have never destroyed all the Salubri on their own, the other clans did the vast majority of work on this.) If the Tremere were not in the game, neither would the Salubri be.

                                                  I do have issues with the Dark Ages Salubri because I don't think they were developed in a way that lined up with previous canon. So that would need to be slightly fixed. I'd change the natures of and relations between Saulot's original childer (the Warrior line) and the new childer he created after returning from the East/achieved Golconda (the Healer line). There would not be a "choice" in this matter for characters. You were either embraced by one lineage or the other.

                                                  The original Salubri description implied there was something different about this second batch of Salubri from the first. The second batch all followed Saulot's quest for Golconda, implying the first did not (at least no more than other vampires did), and that when the Tremere purge began this first batch was easier to hunt down and kill because the second batch was "somehow resistant to the order's magics." So I'd give the first batch (the Warriors) a feeling that their founder considered them to be something of a disappointment, and thus why they were "replaced". The two lines were still "family", but more in the case of step-siblings or half-siblings than full siblings. So there was some distrust and resentment of the elder, discarded brother against the younger, favored one. (I think such disunity, perhaps even feud, makes it more plausible that the Salubri could be wiped out).

                                                  I'd also make that Obeah/Healing Valeren only appeared with Saulot's second batch. While anyone could learn that Discipline, that was not the in-clan Discipline of the Warrior line. I'd make Valeren solely the warrior version of Valeren, and it being the in-clan Discipline of that first batch of childer. The two Disciplines are not related except that both sprung from the blood of Saulot. And Valeren does not produce the third eye - that only happens with Obeah.

                                                  I think making it clear that these lines are different, albeit related, increases the plausibility of the theory that Saulot was experimenting with different lines of childer (Warrior, Healer, Baali, Tremere, and possibly yet another, earlier line that perished in the Baali Wars) for some reason if an ST wanted to go with that idea.

                                                  I also like the idea that Golconda somehow transformed Saulot, and thus his new batch of childer is different in some real way from his first batch. That provides enough ambiguity for people to wonder whether it was really Golconda that changed Salubri, or whether it was something else that transformed Saulot which he simply told people was "Golconda". Again, the ST gets to decide.

                                                  I still don't know how to explain why there is a group of seven surviving Salubri (a magical number) and are all exactly eighth generation. All of whom have the exact same beliefs and act the same. Who state that vampirism is an awful condition that dooms the human soul, but who keep on embracing new people anyway. Whom they force to commit diablerie on them. And that their new childer will then behave exactly the same way they did. These are the only Salubri that survived the purge (sorry Salubri anti-tribu, you don't exist). These survivors clearly come from the Healer line, the second batch of Saulot's childer. But they couldn't have been chosen (or survived) at random. They could not be yet another batch of childer, since Saulot did not embrace them. But they were likely a coterie that Saulot recruited. Perhaps either before he went into voluntary torpor (where he was found by the Tremere) or even during it (since powerful vampires do seem to be somehow "active" mentally during torpor). Perhaps Salubri entered torpor only after he finally found the right Salubri who could fulfill this role. They have a part to play in Saulot's hands - he made sure these were protected well enough to survive the purge - but what that part will be is unknown.

                                                  The original Salubri description implies any PC would be an 8th generation character who has already committed diablerie on their sire and finished their indoctrination. I would have it so that any PCs would be a 9th generation childe who has not yet committed diablerie. They're still in the "testing" phase to determine if they are worthy. That way if they screw up, it's not an end to the magical number of Salubri needed. And if they do screw up, defy their sire, or otherwise rebel, it's just really, really bad luck that the nearest Tremere have found out about them, and the local Prince has ordered a blood hunt....

                                                  (I might, just might, have some other lone Healer Salubri who survived the purge. Maybe a survivor who lucked out by being a member of the Children of Osiris or the Inconnu. Or who lies in deep torpor somewhere. Of course, they would be paranoid as hell because every other Salubri that survived past a date seems to be immediately targeted by their enemies. It's shocking on quickly it happens that they get discovered and killed. And they don't know when the hammer might fall on them, or what is protecting them, or actively targeting their clanmates. This is to just give the STs some other means of introducing Salubri lore or plot if they want. But none of the Warrior line ever survived.)
