      The exact rules seem to vary by edition. However, typically Holy Water counts the same as placing a cross, Bible, or other holy object against the vampire's body. In Revised this would be rolling one's True Faith against the vampire's Willpower with each success dealing a level of aggravated damage. I don't know what edition you are using, but most editions have some variant of the above procedure.

      If you don't want the person using the holy water to be the one whose Faith counts, I'd rule you can use the Faith of the person who originally blessed it.

              That's a rather specific view on what Holy Water is, and more based on games than on real world definitions.

              Holy water is holy because it was blessed by a priest, a person with the religious authority to do so. The faith os such priest either isn't relevant at all, or is only tangentially relevant.

              A catholic character with True Faith wouldn't go around blessing water unless they're a Reverend, nor would doubt the ability of other Reverends to bless water for a single second. That's simply how the theology goes. There are exceptions (saints and, to a lesser extend, the Blessed), but a true believer would hardly think of themselves one such exception.

              So I'd mostly go with Black Fox's take on it: the person using the Holy Water uses their True Faith, if any, because their belief in its properties is the main thing going on. I'd accept the idea of someone with TF making Holy Water that always works, but that would be harder, akin to creating any other sort of Blessed item.

              "Psychological" effects are already baked into the game as Flaws. Vampires are definitely not affected by religious symbols, unless the individual vampire has one such weakness. Regular Holy Water, that finds common use in many rites and ceremonies, shouldn't affect vampires by default.

              For those who do want vampires to be more readily affected by faith and religion, I'd suggest to read Demon the Fallen and take inspiration there. Demons are, by design, far more affected by faith.

                                              Any rules as written are likely to only partly emulate whatever vampire trope you want. So some players may be dissatisfied with a canon answer. They want something more in tune with actual religious significance or better emulates literary, cinematic, or folkloric tropes. In that case, they can make whatever changes they need.

                                              Items that are blessed can be used by those without Faith provided the blessed items retain their sacral quality for a time dependent on the # of successes of True Faith role. That can be anytime between one round to permanent. Such items are considered to have a TF rating of one. Thus an ST could decide that holy water (or whatever) retains its quality for a certain amount of time, and thus can be treated accordingly despite the person using it not having True Faith of their own. The ST would just need to determine how long these items retain its quality.

                                              In Vampire, in addition to people having True Faith, the game also has many places that have their own True Faith ranking to reflect how people - particularly devout congregants - see that place as holy ground. Such holy ground typically does not affect the vampire in and of itself, but does add its True Faith rating to the person attempting to use TF against the vampire. Thus holy ground itself can ward and prevent vampires from entering, forcing the vampire to avoid it.

                                              So it would not be out of line if an ST wanted to use that kind of rule to determine the sacral quality of certain items instead of the actual user (or perhaps the consecrator). If the expectation is that the holy water, eucharist, or any other blessed or sacred item in and of itself has the power of True Faith, then the ST could assign that to them - effectively giving the user a higher rating of TF than they would normally have. Thus even people without True Faith might indeed be able to utilize it. To a Catholic who believes the sacramental bread is truly the body of Christ, that might be TF 5, 8, or some other number. To someone who believes it is just symbolic, maybe that falls to just 1, and to a non-believer it is zero. Holy water would probably be lower than the eucharist, but still as high as whatever the ST thinks would be appropriate.

                                              Of course, now the ST has the responsibility of deciding what level of True Faith of each item has, determine by what means those items might lose their sacral quality (most often these kind of things need to be properly handled and kept isolated lest they become "ordinary") and perhaps keep track of all kinds of things that might impact it. Respectfully handling holy water in a vial by a priest is one thing. But what about the same handling by a layman? What about the Frog brothers putting it in a supersoaker water gun? Does it decline in TF ranking or even end completely? For some STs it will be worth the work to figure out these things, but for others it'll be easier to default to the original version. It really depends on what the ST wants for their game and the expectations of the players.
