                From the trailer, the sort of game I envision is this:
                • 80% of the time you're playing a walking-simulator - lots of environmental storytelling, little interaction, some simple puzzles
                • By picking a Clan you can change the appearance of your character but there's only one male VA and one female VA who deliver the same lines for 99% of the time no matter what you pick (because the story is pretty linear and always plays out the same)
                • Then the other 20% are combat-encounters where in a limited space you're gonna use your powers (and depending on what Clan you picked you have different ones) but in terms of gameplay it's mostly just a bunch of button-mashing and (depending on the Clan) various kinds of visual effects happen on the screen
                • And in terms of length, a playthrough takes 4 to 5 hours and there's little replay-value as the big focus is on the story and that one always plays out the same no matter what Clan you pick
                • Maybe it has one of those endings where right at the end you get presented with 2 or 3 choices and then you get different endings depending on what choice you make
                I think another big thing would be if it ends up being this sort of mid-tier modest game but still costs 70 bucks (or even more if you get the various limited editions). But that's sort of my best guess for what this game could end up being like.

                            I'm not sure I'm that pessimistic, but I don't really know TCR's work well enough to say. I've seen small studios step up to big games successfully.

                            My red flags are:

                            1) Tencent (via Sumo) is always a small red flag, simply because they are not in the industry to make art, or promote art. They'll gladly sacrifice anything remotely aesthetically good about the game to squeeze in some sort of monetization if they' can. Tencent is notorious for either copying other companies, or buying those it can't successfully imitate, because the company doesn't value creativity or innovation. Plus Tencent has used it's ever growing portfolio to push programs with shitty digital security, and to pressure for Chinese state censorship. A 10% share in TCR isn't enough to control it outright, but it's enough money that if they want something and are willing to take their money away if they don't get it, it could be a problem (though Paradox is big enough to shore that up if they wanted to).

                            2) Like most video game fans, I'm exceedingly skeptical of no real game play demos. It's easy to make a pretty video that looks like game play, but it's just a pre-render that looks far better than the final product ever will. If this is supposed to come out next year, they are quickly running out of time to demonstrate that they have an actual game to play, instead of some fancy visuals.

                            3) The whole style of faux-interview always rubs me the wrong way. They're not being asked real questions, they're reading ad copy from a script. It's not authentic, and we all know it. I'm not saying anyone in those ads doesn't believe it, but it's not a great way to sell because it just highlights how much they're saying the things they think we want to hear instead of having anything to say about the things we should hear.

                            Basically, they've told us a bunch of things, but shown us nothing, and TCR doesn't have a reputation to trade on to put that aside.

                                                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                                        Can you explain what the proper course of action is?
                                                        Sure.
                                                        • Transparency: The switch to Chinese Room should've been announced the moment it happened. Instead of leaving people in the dark, just state that another studio is working on it now. The situation with Hardsuit Labs was bad, of course, and deciding to switch doesn't look good. But to keep hype for the game alive, the switch should've been announced to at least make clear that things are happening - instead of the limbo the public has been left in for years. Nobody thinks big games are made overnight but the initial announcement of Bloodlines 2 had hype and to keep it alive, they should've made the public aware that the game is being worked on, that it will see the light of day as soon as they had switched to Chinese Room.
                                                        • Also, people kept on pestering Outstar on the WOD-news-show on Twitch about Bloodlines 2 news (it pretty much came up every time I watched her show). And the fact that there had been news (the switch to Chinese Room) but instead, Paradox's PR-strategy had been to tell her to say nothing just... feels bad. People were indeed still invested and instead, Paradox chose to leave them in the dark.
                                                        • The moment the game reaches the state in development where it's presentable and finished (or at least close to finished), in order to counter all the doubts and issues of the past, the main-thing should've been gameplay. Of course, game-companies have even lied about supposed "game-play footage" in the past but assuming it's legit, that stuff works better than these trailers that only give you a tiny glimpse of what the game is like.
                                                        • Also, this tiny-portions-approach seems to me more frustrating than a way to build up hype. We got the announcement of the announcement a couple of months ago, now we got the announcement of the development-studio and at the start of next year we're gonna get gameplay-footage. Why does this process have to be drawn out over half a year? The game is gonna come out in a year anyway (if everything goes as planned). How many people who have now become aware of Bloodlines 2 again will remember to look for the Clan-stuff at the end of the year and the gameplay-stuff at the start of next year? You already had the announcement of an announcement but then said announcement was used to tell people that the details will be announced at yet another preview later on.
                                                        • Instead, the formula should be "Gameplay, gameplay and MORE gameplay.". Show off what's cool about the game and make people interested in playing it. Even if it's a linear story with tiny variations like I fear, even then the gameplay-preview is about getting people invested in the story, so that they want to play the game and find out what happens.
                                                        The game is supposed to come out in a year. This close to launch there's really no need to hide stuff anymore and tease things. Assuming development isn't massively behind schedule, this announcement should've featured someone playing the actual game.
