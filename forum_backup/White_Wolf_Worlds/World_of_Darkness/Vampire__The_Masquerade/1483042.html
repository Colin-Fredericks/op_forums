    Did Sasha Vykos keep some spark of creativity in undeath?

    06-04-2022, 12:20 PM
    It's said in VTM that the children of Caine are static creatures who have lost a vital spark of creativity and adaptabilty and it gets more pronounced as they get older. It's seems that Vykos seems to put that into doubt as it's continues to adapt quite well to an everchanging world and is able to learn and master new paths of enlightment and skills. Where many of it's peers of the same age have trouble with that and sometimes withdraw and even go into topor. What is your take on this notion and do you think kindred still have that spark?

          Yes, they still have that "spark." Or, rather, they're not inherently without it.

          My take on the "stasis" of vampires is that it's psychological. The loss of creativity is ennui and depression. You live forever surrounded by assholes in a shitty world that doesn't get better as most of the world you knew dies. There is nothing new under the sun, and you'll struggle to connect to future eras because the Masquerade makes long lasting, direct personal connections difficult. Whatever you create you will inevitably watch succumb to entropy and disappear from the world. It's pointless. You get bored, you get lonely, you stop caring and you stop trying or paying attention.

          If you can maintain the drive to keep going, have something to care about such as a greater purpose, you're less likely to succumb

          Crankiness at one's changing surroundings is also part of human thought. I'm still irrationally offended by a lot of the changes in my city that have occurred since I was a child. My grandparents feel that even stronger. It's like watching parts of your life being erased, in a way, to make room for somebody elses world - especially if it's replaced with something you don't particularly care for. Elders and Methuselahs may have lost entire cultures, nationalities and languages to progress.

              I like to think mostly, vampires, especially older ones, mostly lose their 'spark' little by little. I think it would have something to do with the fall of Humanity (or whatever morality and sensibility it has) in most cases, besides de ennui, jading and etc..

              But, I think something that would allow vamps to surpass this block, this deafening of their bright creativity, would be the blood of humans. The emotions, the humors and resonances, the memories, it would all mingle with the Kindred's and its other victims. I like to treat vampires thematically as copycats, maintainers, alien tinkerers and stealers; even their most inspired creation would be (maybe only in metaknowledge) because of the blood they drink, and the Life and Emotion they absorb through it.

              So yeah, I think Vykos could be very much a creative fella, drinking blood without reservations as an inhuman thing they "is". That is a way I think justifies how Toreador can (but not always) keep inspiration, because they take care and invest in their herd and vessels, and Tzimisce are all about refining and producing the best Herd. Brujah just seem attracted to the emotional and livid mortals. While Ventrue would be that boring archetype because they mostly drink with much restriction, without interest in making a grand and fantastic drinking vessel, just a clean, quality and guaranteed feeding herd.

              That is how I like to think anyhow

                      In my chronicles, I hold that 1) vampires are inherently parasitic creatures and 2) that only the living can truly possess the creative inspiration to create something "new". Vampires can be very intelligent, technically proficient, and learn new things, but like Morgoth and Sauron they can only imitate or corrupt, not innovate or really create. They could create very attractive and pleasing work of arts that surpass much of the product of ordinary human artists, but they will just never match the "wow" factor of Van Gogh, Picasso, Bouguereau, the Renaissance greats, and so on (in whatever field).

                      High humanity vampires (say 8+) might be able to sometimes approximate that lost spark, but it's rare.

                      Ultimately, I believe becoming a vampire is an awful thing. Despite the cool powers and other seductive elements of the undead, it's not a good thing and always comes at too high a price. If that isn't the case, I believe the genre is being done wrong.

                                      One thing to consider is that the setting distinguishes creativity and adaptability​. The core books lore conforms to Black Fox's interpretation, but it also states that vampires are exceptionally good at adapting to blend with the prey, things like getting new accents and learning new technologies comes as second nature. As anyone can notice, of course, it isn't completely consistent not even in the same book. But it is still there.

                                      By myself, while anguishing over lost creativity is fun, sometimes interpreting what it means is more confusing than useful and staying true to it end up harming the game, either because some player want to explore some creative outlet with the character, or because it stays in the way for making an interesting NPC. It even stays true for some vampire stories you may want to tell elsewhere.

                                      For this reason I not only disagree that letting vampires be creative is doing the genre wrong, I hold that being too strict with it may be worse for your game.

                                      Not to say that, in the end, telling a vampire story is a creative exercise. How can you be sure that you're properly conveying lack of creativity through your character when the character itself is a creative outlet?

                                      How do you even define creativity to do that? I can guarantee that by most technical definitions of creativity the notion of "losing the spark" is nonsense, as that would mean a loss of the ability to develop solutions to presented problems instead of only recurring to set solutions, which isn't something I've ever saw any vampire being bound to.

                                              To me, there's creativity and then there's creativity. You can creatively experiment and combine known elements and impressions to something that's more impressive than the sum of its parts, and you can pour something out that's unmistakable and uniquely you and yours. Kindred should be able to accomplish the former or they'd just never be able to function, and Toreadors wouldn't have any authority as artists whatsoever, while being doomed to feel like hacks and fakers for never managing the latter after their dying day.

                                              Am currently playing a Toreador musician who's spent his unlife composing masterful - but never groundbreaking - songs while completely failing to finish what would have been his magnum opus, who has rationalized the Embrace and teaching of his Childe as his one and only true act of creation since his death to balance out his bitterness and grief at the loss of his own potential.

                                              A vampire can paint a gorgeous painting or write a cool movie, but no-one is ever going to see or watch it and wonder where in the world the ideas came from. You're doomed to, at best, be on the banal side of brilliant. Which is the sort of fate that would drive most true artists to near-suicide.

                                              It just so happens that Vykos doesn't have a whole lot of critics to point out that all her experiments are a little bit obvious given the means, motivation and the time to think of them she's had.
