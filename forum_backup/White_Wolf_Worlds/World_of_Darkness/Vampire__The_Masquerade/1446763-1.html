            I think this can still be compatible. The Beast doesn't "know" anything. It is just reacting on predatory instinct. I would say that as long as the frenzied diablerist keeps rolling 9 on their Strength check to consume the life force, the Beast keeps pushing the vampire on. The moment it fails its Strength check, it doesn't get any reward and would stop, and then it would move on the next thing. Because once the rush isn't fed, why keep going? We don't expect vampires to keep feeding on humans after their blood is gone, so without reward the vampire should move on. Since rolling against difficulty 9 is hard, it fails most of the time.             This is really a matter of taste. To a lot of players, diablerie should be omnipresent in the setting. I prefer things to be more mysterious that PCs can learn during the game, and to provide a starker contrast between the Sabbat and the Camarilla.

                            I'd argue Gangrel would be less likely to do it than other clans put in the same circumstance. Gangrel, for what they are, are insular, largely isolated, tightly-knit, communal, aren't necessarily out for power for its own sake, and their clan culture is built on a latticework of social connections and oral tradition. It's not the case they're unique among vampire clans in holding any of those traits, but the combination of those traits is what matters. They may be individualistic survivalists, but the clan-wide social network provides survival opportunities unavailable to a "true" loner and a smart Gangrel isn't going to decline those opportunities to choose an increase of personal power of potentially less utility.

                            This isn't about whether a would-be diablerist is going to get caught in or after the act. It's about the value the potential diablerie victim brings to the clan, and by extension the individual, and the potential ramifications and harm done by removing the victim from the aforementioned social latticework. The victim may have news or tales to tell of value to others in the clan that would be lost to diablerie. The victim may have knowledge of disciplines they may be able to teach others in the clan, which may well be lost to diablerie. The victim may be another vampire's messenger, and if removed from the social latticework a valuable lifeline of information spread in the clan may be lost.

                            Yeah, if two Gangrel meet in the wild, they're going to figure out where in the pecking order each sits, however they choose to do it. But diablerie? too high risk, too little reward.

                                            Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post

                                            This isn't about race, it's about vampires being vampires under circumstances where there is no interference, and no witnesses, and therefore no consequences to doing something evil for power.
                                            Okay, let's do the math on this.

                                            One Gangrel meets another on the road. Those Gangrel may or may not know each other, and if they don't they have no way of telling how old the other is or what generation. An attempt to diablerize means the claws and fangs come out, and blood gets spent to buff and fuel disciplines. More likely than not, if the vampire was worth diablerizing to begin with, the victor is limping away low on blood and nursing a nasty aggravated wound or two.

                                            That means the victor has to hunt, spend a night or two healing up, and hunt even more to compensate for the blood they spent healing aggravated wounds, and spend even more blood to fuel earth melds and animal forms in the meantime. And all that equates to vulnerability, on the road, more likely than not in lupine territory where the most fearsome pack hunters in the whole-ass World of Darkness are going to waste no time picking up a blood and scent trail from the scene of a lethal fight or starving-Gangrel-wild-animal-massacre to...wherever the diablerist Gangrel thinks is safe. And flight form is absolutely no insurance against that.

                                            A measly drop in generation is cold comfort to a Gangrel who gets dug out of the soil at noon by a pack of werewolves a day or two later, because they were too injured and too hungry to get a safe distance away from the scene of the crime.

                                            And for what? A dead Gangrel is a dead Gangrel. That's one fewer messenger, one fewer story-teller and carrier of the clan's oral traditions, one fewer clan mate to potentially call on in times of hardship, and one fewer clan-mate to find fledglings and train in turn. Being counter-cultural and individualist doesn't mean Gangrel lack a civic mindset, or can't see the big picture.

                                            In fact, being counter-cultural and individualist means Gangrel have to have an incredibly well-honed sense of risk-to-reward merely to survive, and there's no scenario in which one Gangrel meets another on the road where the reward of diablerie meets or exceeds the risks of it.

                                                            I just had a thought (if you can excuse the V5 diatribe)

                                                            In V5, the Gangrel are going to be the absolute least likely clan to diablerize. Obviously, there's the angle of -Animal Blood is an important fallback for my diet and I need the resonance so gaining blood potency is an absolutely terrible idea for me-. But there's also the shift of Diablerie from -I need to do this to push the glass ceiling and raise my limits- towards -This'll give me a quick and dirty pile of XP to spend on my disciplines-
                                                            The issue with that, for the Gangrel, is that they're generally the clan with the LEAST amount of pressure on them. Political pressure? Pack up and leave. Hunters? Well they can narrow your sleeping ground to within... four killometres, good luck with the digging. Your domain and herd is threatened? They're a convenience, you don't need them. Gangrel are survivors, they're self reliant, they always have something to fall back to. The Ventrue and Tzmisce are rather extreme counterexamples, but 'normal' clans like Toreador, Brujah or Malkavians draw a lot of their worth from what's around them, often feeling it's better to die on the hill than to start over. Another vampire feels threatened, they believe they have to defend, they have to accumulate, and diablerie is a radical idea that'll grant them the boost they'll need over their enemies. A Gangrel will very much fight, if she thinks she can win, but she's more happy to cut her losses and run before she needs to consider risky ideas like that.
