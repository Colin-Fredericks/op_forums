    What’s a Storyteller to do?
    I suggest that you talk to them about the issue out-of-character. Not including actual story details, of course, but talk to them straight and clearly.

    Be sure that they know the game is about a corrupted society, even if some of its members aren't so bad, that some NPC's may lie to them and stab them in the back, that vampiric secrecy is deadly serious and that they generally occupy an "evil illuminatti" role on society.

    Use the embezzlement as an asset here. It gives you something you can discuss with them outside of the game what they think about it versus what it really is. You surely don't have to tell them that they shouldn't do it, but just be sure that they understand what it is and that this is relevant in the game.

    Ask them directly how they'll feel if some character they trust betrays them. They may surprise you being chill about it, but parse first all the NPCs to make it clear that any character at all can betray them for reasons they may not be seeing at the moment and that they're thinking it through.

    Finally, weigh in the value of changing the game a bit. If after all that your players say they'll be ok, and then they don't, its on them, they're consenting adults after all. But if an open conversation shows that you should adapt yourself and adjust the setting to be more Blake-like, go for it. Your time with this group of real people is far more important than the game's purity.

    Not saying any of this lightly, I know this sometimes happen and even with experienced players this is a tough situation, but sometimes an outside perspective helps.

                Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                I don't think that the title question is affected by their power spread. Destruction is always easier than Creation, even for humans, but the question is about how NPCs in general behave and that depends on the many factors of how the ST see vampiric society. The lack of supernatural powers to create lasting good seems like weak reason to conclude they're going to be bad people, too simplistic.
                I focused narrowly on Disciplines to add something to the discussion where others had commented in different aspects of Vampirism. If you want another example, I would say that The Camarilla's Masquerade necessitates a lot of institutional fuckery that has disastrous consequences for society. For practically every major institution, there is a Declared Mandate and a Hidden Function carried out on behalf of Vampire overlords that are in conflict.

                The local police department is the simplest example. A group of teenagers are messing around after dark and trespassing in a seemingly abandoned place. A high status Vampire who considers this his territory frenzies and kills the intruders. The growling and screaming attracts attention and the Vampire is forced to flee before he can drag away the bodies. The police respond and have the duty of informing the families of these kids that they were murdered. They promise to find the killer.

                The high status vampire visits the Prince, making apologies and excuses. The Prince exerts his already established power over the police department, turning the investigation into a coverup. Imagine what this does to the police officers who swore to grieving parents they would catch the killer. Now they are framing some poor socially awkward man for the rampage.

                The Declared Mandate of the police department, to protect citizens, investigate crime and arrest criminals, is totally undermined by the Hidden Function, keeping Vampires safe from scrutiny, which takes priority. When people do not believe in institutions they pay taxes for and have to obey, they turn to often worse alternatives, like Vampiric blood cults or straight forward radicalism. All new Vampires are drawn from this hopeless mortal population, adding to the problem.

                An even more basic and important institution, The Family, is undermined by the creation of Ghouls. Let's say that the captain of the police force is a married man. His wife is proud of his dedication and bravery. They love each other. They have kids old enough to understand things they overhear or see in the news. Along comes a sexy female Ventrue Primogen who blood-bonds the police captain. Now, he is more deeply in love with this Vampire than he ever was with his wife. On top of that, she is having him carry out a Hidden Function of covering up Vampires' crimes. This is going to have an erosive influence on the captain, as a person, and his family.

                It's not so much that individual Vampires are all bad. It's that, collectively, they cannot help but be extremely bad for human society.

                        Originally posted by Chris24601 View Post
                        If they at least implied this was just the attitude of their PCs and they understood what’s going on I could handle it fine; but it seems to be the player perspective and that’s just… What’s a Storyteller to do?
                        Fuck' em in the ear. You've already warned them OOC. They should know by now.

                        But it sounds like so far they are reacting to the rewards you (the ST) have been giving them through the NPCs. So they're just acting on the carrots you've trained them to accept. (Players can be oblivious. In one LARP I had an NPC that I told everyone up front had the Untrustworthy x3 characteristic. But he acted very nice to people, was willing to help out in minor ways, and had enough power and prestige he could be a useful associate. One PC found him too useful and began thinking that NPC was his friend and ally. So I waited for the right moment and then the NPC did something that directly harmed the PC. He was shocked and started hating the NPC. He couldn't believe it. The other players had to remind him that the NPC was always said to have Untrustworthy x3 so why did he ever trust him? Of course, most of them were doing something similar with that NPC. They started changing their behavior though once they saw what happened to the first PC. People always learn better through experience than talk. Only after the LARP PCs actually saw that NPC do something that demonstrated that Untrustworthy x3 trait did they really know that the NPC was in fact untrustworthy.)

                        So you need one of those NPCs who likes them so much and is such a good friend to betray them.

                        Now there's some guidelines on how to do that. First, it should be fairly minor. You want the PCs to be able to recover from said betrayal, not put them so far down the hole the players get discouraged and give up. Second, the betrayal needs to make sense. The NPCs needs to benefit more from his betrayal than he is giving up by burning his relationship with the NPC (otherwise it is just stupid and makes no sense. The PCs must see that the NPC benefited from the action). You just need to figure that out.

                        Once it has happened once, they should be much more wary of the other NPCs and reset expectations.

                        However, if they still act naive, then that's a signal to the other vampire NPCs that the coterie is stupid and can be exploited. Better to use them now before they're used up and useless. So if they continue, have them start meeting a lot of new "friends." And after some time, these friends have a problem they need some help with. They should catch on soon.

                                        Humanity for PCs is something we've been struggling with ever since we started playing in 1995, and I'm not sure we've reached a mutual understanding, as the game mechanics rarely explain the very essence of humanity for a vampire (or mortal for that matter). "Evil deeds" are not only often subjective - they also tend to change over time as society and morality does, and if you're an undead creature passing eons of time, you'll quickly get out of touch with what is "good" and what is "evil". Hell, I'm 50 years old and I quite often have a rather hard time figuring out what is good and bad these days - can't imagine how off I would feel as a 200-year-old.

                                        Therefore, we made the decision to make Humanity a sort of anchor for the players ability to understand and interact with mortal society. To make it the filter through which they use their social skills when speaking to mortals, or even try to do business on a daily basis. We've made 7 the norm - lowering the diff. when above 7 and raising it when below (I believe it was thus i Revised).

                                        The conscience rolls are of course still debated vigorously in our group, as they still go by the whole "good vs. evil" aspect in the books, which doesn't really take old age and the lack of understanding your surroundings into account.

                                        So, to answer the initial question; my players are rarely bad in the sense of commiting evil acts ... they are more a challenge for me as an ST when having to explain to them why a 200 year old Nosferatu, who's been secluded from society for the most part of his unlife, has a hard time buying the right flowers at Leicester Square on a busy Friday night.

                                                    What I have generally tried to keep in mind in my settings is that vampires are ultimately people first, albeit with the added burden of the beast pulling them towards destructive and addictive behaviors. I'd say the average Humanity level for most NPCs is around 5 (on the V20 scale). Decidedly more callous and jaded towards the suffering they inflict than most "normal" people (for a given value of normal), but not necessarily given to deliberate depravity unless in exceptional circumstances. For the older or more alien vampires I generally default to them hovering around Humanity 3. Still not cavalier about killing in cold blood unless provoked, but unfazed by most collateral damage they might leave in their wake.

                                                    The Sabbat is always a bit tricky given the different takes on them over the years, but I tend to have the majority of rank and file Sabbat hanging out in that Humanity 3 zone. They won't go out of their way to commit atrocities unless they have a reason (siege, ritae, etc.) but they're pretty accustomed to casual evils. In my settings I like to have Paths be a bit special, with those that achieve them kind of becoming cult leaders and centers of gravity in their own right. In my version of the Sabbat feuds between incompatible Paths are a constantly simmering cold war within the Sword of Caine.
