    Improve Diablerie?

    09-26-2022, 10:55 AM
    Diablerie as described allow you to steal your victim's powers. On the other hand the RAW tell that it allow you to lower you Gen by 1, the ST can allow to gain more if there is a significant difference between the diablerist and the victim. It has no other effect in v20

    Diablerie is described as the worst crime you can commit in vampire society and the main fear of the oldest vampire but the benefits seems so smalls in comparison to the risk that none of my pc are tempted to accomplish it, especially when they know that their character will keep traces of their act in their aura for years and risk to be chased and put to death before the chance to enjoy their new potential.

    A quick fix would be to tell you immediatly gain your victim generation (many other players has adopted this method from what i found on few other forums). But still it give nothing more to the character, no xp to spend, no Discipline dot or possibility to gain the victim's...

    Do you think it would worth it to improve it's benefits to give the pc's a real dilemma between gain power and get stronger but loose their humanity bit by bit or keep what they have and stay as "humans" as they can be? And if yes what other benefits would fit?

                  Giving too many benefits to diablerie is a great way to completely derail the campaign and ruin party balance.

                  Better you reduce the penalties.
                  In first edition, Diablerie was just murder: You killed someone, roll conscience, gain a dot of generation or more if they were lower gen than you. That would be it but also 1st edition suggested if you drank elder blood you could use their disciplines temporarily. So you'd get a power up for a few hours too. But for the most part it's just murder that gets you a background dot with no mystical drama attached.

                  Then revised came along and made diablerists a bunch of deranged junkies who lose a whole point of humanity every time they do it so invariably your players would avoid diablerie once their humanity was low enough. Self-defeating really. Revised ruined it.

                      I play in a DaV20 campaign, where Diablerie has a few perks:

                      First off, our ST gave us all 4 dots of generation to start. This meant that a single diablerie of someone older would unlock Elder powers, which immediately became quite exciting. The blood pool increase from 15 to 20 was also quite tasty

                      Second, you could use your diablerized victim as a teacher for disciplines. Their clan disciplines became in-clan for you, and you could spend XP to learn their stuff without anyone else helping you. You could also learn their discipline techniques this way.

                      Finally, it opened the door to switching roads. Upon committing the diablerie, you could surrender to the instincts of your prey, effectively changing roads from yours to theirs. You still lost a few points as per the normal rules.

                      Furthermore, I believe Carthage should be destroyed.

                              Boosts won't solve the problem, they'll worsen it. If Diablerie gives more benefits, then it is an even worse crime since anyone committing it becomes more dangerous. Other vampires have less reason to not go all-out on a Diablerist. It will also wreck your XP balance if your players become too efficient at that, with the game quickly derailing into an action-packed murder fest instead of the tension of the decision.

                              Even more so if you put it in terms of the aura being a give-away for a known elder going missing. You're already assuming your PCs hunting down the active movers and shakers of the city without as much as trying to be subtle about it. This is the kind of thing a vampire would only do if they're stupid, and on most games a player would do if they want to be a jerk about the game itself.

                              If you actually want a game about hunting elders and getting away of the city before long, with action-packed scenes when things go wrong, by all means suit yourself, it may be a lot of fun. But if your intent is to make your players be tempted, then you must take a lot of care with benefits.

                              Since your problem is with the aura evidence, tackle this problem instead of compensating it. An easy way to fix may be for the city to have an NPC that offers relief, a ritual to cleanse the markings in the aura. You don't need to come up with the specifics of the ritual unless at some point you want for a PC to learn the ritual. Until then, just have this fellow offer the PCs such a service for a reasonable price. If you want to make things more weird, but at the same time less problematic for the plot as a whole, this effect may not come from a vampire, but some other creature, or a local natural phenomenon, or some mystical object or place. Other supernaturals have the advantage that you may make their real intentions completely irrelevant and immaterial for the Kindred society, so it won't disturb the game.

                                          Diablerie Benefits
                                          • Get the first level of an in-clan discipline the victim has
                                          • If the victim blood potency is higher increase your blood potency/generation to the average between you and the victim rounded up.
                                          • Ability to pay XP on "the spot" to learn disciplines as if the victim was your mentor.


                                          Diablerie Downsides
                                          • 1 year of evil black aura around you speaking of your crimes
                                          • Every body fears and hates you
                                          • You make a clash of wills each success your victim has above you can make you loss 1 point in humanity or gain a weakness he has like an extra clan bane or supernatural aspect.
                                          • If you lose all humanity points you will get taken over by the victim soul


                                          Diablerie is a high risk, high reward situation.

                                              10-05-2022, 09:10 AM
                                              Originally posted by Cain Loup-Noir View Post
                                              What do you think of a potential 3rd benefit that would be the ability to keep the memory of the victim to learn his secrets, not to develop unknown abilities just to discover what he knew and use it later, like the combination of a safe, the name of a contact, the motivation of a crime etc...
                                              One single piece of information or two may be a fitting reward, but more than that becomes problematic again, although not necessarily in a bad sense.

                                              Vampires, specially old ones, are full of secrets. And of each other's secrets on top of that. If Diablerie confers memories, then any elder worth their salt would be extremely wary of both allies and enemies being eaten, exchanging known holders of their own secrets for unknown ones. It would make Diablerie an even more strategic crime, with greater political rewards and more effort put into protecting a potential victim, since now many vampires have an invested interest in protecting other vampires.

                                              It would also make it extremely useful for elders. As in the book, Diablerie is addictive, making for Diablerist elders a particular brand of problem to be dealt with, but otherwise there's no benefit in eating a weaker vampire. This change, if too intense, makes for a very tangible benefit for doing so, one that may very well make it a vital strategic action. Diablerizing someone who knows your secrets will be the simplest way to both get info out of them and protect your own secrets.

                                              Together those two aspects alter the game's dynamics considerably. Elders are usually expected to be far more powerful, and so there's a reason why a politically oriented game avoided giving them little reason to resort to such measures other than sheer depravity (that is also a theme). With Diablerie conferring significant amounts of memory it becomes a rather more predatorial game, where you always have to be on your toes against others of your kind and a society of equals may become unfeasible.

                                              Diablerie was heavily based on Highlander, specially the second movie and the series. But as is, Highlander is a story about loners, the immortals keep in some sort of contact but avoid each other more than seek, as they are the greatest prize for each other. They have no meaningful politics as a result, just very occasional interactions. Any idea that gives tangible benefits for Diablerizing anyone, but even more so the idea with memories, can lead to a similar situation in Vampire, as both your PCs will react accordingly as they perceive the threat and your NPCs will also do so as you adapt their reactions to your own changes. This isn't a bad proposition per se, but again will depend a lot on the game you want to run.

                                              My own suggestion would be the following:

                                              Benefits:
                                              • * If the victim is of lower Generation, you reduce your own in 1. Do not alter Blood Potency, even if that leaves you bellow the Generation's floor, you're not naturally at it;
                                                * Access the Disciplines/Powers of the victim for the scene, keep their in-clans for the night;
                                                * You may immediately spend XP on those powers to keep them, at the best cost between your's and your victim's;
                                                * For a year you can buy the other powers back with XP, but at your own cost, and/or buy up to the victim's Blood Potency;
                                                * The next Childe you Embrace within a year may be from the victim's clan, at your discretion;
                                                * Question the Soul: you may spend 1 XP to ask a question whose answer would be within the victim in some fashion and get a truthful and complete answer, limited to one line;

                                              Drawbacks:
                                              • * Black Aura, RAW, plus Humanity loss/Stains as per murder;
                                                * Diablerist Curse: a rating similar to Bane Severity that equals your Original BP - Victim's BP + 1, minimum 1. It reduces by 1 each year, and you have parallel tracks for each victim;
                                                * Addiction: make it a Compulsion, using Diablerist Curse for Severity. It can mimic any Clan Compulsion, but usually it is more fitting to mimic the victim's, twisted to direct the vampire into committing Diablerie again, against any target;
                                                * Quirks: once per night in a meaningful scene you get caught by an impulse to act in a way your victim would, not you. This may work similarly to a Compulsion or Bane, but isn't limited to any particular effect. Otherwise, use the Diablerist Curse as a pool for the quirk to be recognized by others. Behavior without consequence doesn't count for the night;

                                              Optional Trades:
                                              • * Humanity Sink: reduce Humanity and cash-in the XP. You must spend it immediately on Diablerie Benefits, leftovers may only be used on regaining this lost Humanity later, if it is ever possible again;
                                                * Penance: if you don't use any of the Benefits for the first night, or after one week without using any of them or the powers bought with them afterwards, you cloud your Black Aura. Any attempt to read your Aura must overcome your Diablerist Curse first or won't get the Black Aura (it will still get any other info), but using any of the gained powers also have to overcome it until the Aura is gone (if there's no roll, roll Blood Potency);
                                                * New Flaws: you can immediately get any Flaw from the victim or any that can simulate their characteristics, and use the XP as in Humanity Sink;
                                                * Eternal Quirk/No Quirk: The Quirk effects become permanent, but one of the victim's 5-dot Disciplines become permanently in-clan for you. Optionally you can get the victim's Clan Bane and Compulsion to also have their in-clans as in-clan for you. On the other hand, if you give up at any moment on your remaining time with benefits, you can also ditch the Quirks entirely;


                                              Personally I dislike permanent punishment for edginess as much as permanent benefits that throw growth balance through the window. And permanent access to in-Clans is in general a complicated proposition on the long run to keep track of (not necessarily unbalanced, not too much, but it will be and promote more bookkeeping than it is worth). So the idea is to have most of the traditional effects, plus some options, but keep then as trade-ins or temporary measures, plus something for the problem of the Aura.

                                              Just keep in mind that the descriptions above are for the ST. You may let the players decide on trade-offs or impose them yourself, or yet make them random through a dice roll. The same for choosing the powers to be kept at any point. And however it happens, the characters are never making choices other than commit the act. For them, everything is just happenstance. The character may at most try to focus on developing specific powers, but you're the final arbiter if the effectiveness of it.
