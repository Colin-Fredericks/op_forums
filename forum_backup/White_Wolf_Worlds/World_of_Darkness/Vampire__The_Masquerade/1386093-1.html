        Here's some of the ones I've used in my game.

        1. The Hawaiian Hut: A shady no-tell motel that has missing neon-letters, a tropical theme in only the vaguest sense, and a general reputation as a complete shit hole. The Hawaiian Hut is notable for the fact its controlled by the 69ers that are a local street gang that uses it as a brothel for their activities as well as a place to drug pot, crack, and opiods. Weirdly enough, there is a very nice set pair of rooms that are available for rent that are extremely luxurious, approaching 3 1/2 star level if not 4 stars. These are never rented out unless you know about them.

        2. The Scarlet Woman: A strip club that is extremely high end with good music, lighting, fog, talented dancers, and a lovely neon-theme. The Scarlet Woman tends to host bachelor parties, business celebrations, and other works. Light prostitution goes on but the woman seem remarkably happy about the arrangement and speak of the owner in somewhat glowing terms. The place is extremely expensive but actually has been known to turn away guests like a nightclub or let in others for free.

        3. K-Witchcraft: Witchcraft Joe runs a pirate radio station that operates out of the second floor of an old department store. Witchcraft Joe is a Gulf War veteran who looks and acts more like a Vietnam War veteran. He is usually on acid, pot, pills, or some combination of the three. Joe is a somewhat apolitical conspiracy theorist and actually has a fairly decent number of True Crime reporters as well as informants around the city. He lives in his "office" but seems to have a surprising amount of money to buy stories or give away people or bribe people to leave him alone.

        4. The Banana Mobile: An absurdly kitsch van with an enormous banana on the top of it that sells banana ice cream and banana splits, theoretically. In truth, the Banana Mobile is actually a vehicle for buying illegal weapons around the city. Its operator, Morty, has a deal with gun shows and the local gun dealers that allows him to acquire whatever he needs before selling to the city's criminals.

        5. The Phantom Police Station: Something so ridiculous that it can only be true. The Phantom Police Station is an entirely functional appearing police station that is completely run by criminals. They have police cars, uniforms, weapons, and their own collection of operatives that do work for the local crime syndicates as well as provide security on demand for those who will pay for it. The police in town are fairly certain that it HAS to be a real police station because that would be ridiculous otherwise, right? They have been known to interfere in numerous other investigations, take care of calls, and shut down things that would be uncomfortable. Prisoners who go into their cells also don't always come out.

        6. Greenplace Security and Holding: A detention center that has gained a great deal of business in recent years. The organization agrees to hold illegal immigrants and other individuals that are picked up by ICE officers as well as law enforcement. It's fairly an open secrey that Greenplace is actually a front for human trafficking with someone regularly buying up its detainees. Not only children and young women either but everyone. They're not often seen again either. Whether they're being sold to brothels, cartels, vampires, or serial killers--it doesn't matter to the people who take home an extra 60K per year.

        7. The Magadon Truck: A transport truck with a mobile clinic in the back. The Magadon Truck pulls into the homeless population camps for free check-ups, inoculations, and treatment every week. The Magadon Truck offers the homeless population cash payments in exchange for taking blood samples and giving them regular injections of perfectly safe materials. Members who get sick afterward often seek them out for more treatments, which occasionally ends in them disappearing.

        8. Lucien's Coffin: There's nothing more pathetic than a middle aged Goth, unless he's at Lucien's Coffin where the Nineties never ended. Men and women in their 40s put on their capes, leather, bustiers, and pancake makeup to go to one of the world's only Goth nightclubs that cater exclusively to soccer moms as well as corporate drones seeking to recapture their Hot Topic Youth. The Sisters of Mercy and Nine Inch Nails will never stop being popular here as it mainly exists as a place for casual hook ups, drinking, and marital affairs. The place has been described as a cheap version of the Succubus Club but the fact is that it actually is making fairly decent bank since the guests have disposable income as well as the owner owning the beauty parlor as well as costume shop next door. He also gets a cut of the "Halloween crowd" making use of the hotel across the street.

            Pavello's World-O-Books

            Pavello's occupies a 1920's era bank building, with cracked terrazzo floors and soaring, yet leak stained, ceilings. The two-story arched windows face onto a busy retail strip a few blocks on the "bad side" of an urban university campus. The store is flanked by a tattoo shop, with ratty apartments above, and a three-story thrift store which specializes in used furniture, vintage men's suits, and bedbugs.

            Local lore states that every book ever printed can be found somewhere in this store, if you can only find it. There are whole rooms of publisher over-runs, used text books, and review copies. Stacks of Popular Mechanics, National Geographics, and muscle magazines dating back to the 1930s threaten to topple over right next to Estonian language translations of the Iliad, and tax codes from the Ottoman Empire. Somewhere in here, if you can find it, are a signed first-edition of Neruda's Cancion de gesta, a detailed map of the city's sewer system, and a Tamil translation of the Book of Nod.

            There is, in fact, a strange logic to the stacks, if you know how to search. For example, there are two rooms of sheet music upstairs. One is filled with music Pavello likes, the other with what he calls "crude abuses of a G clef".

            In 1974, Mr. Pavello left his job as a research engineer to take over his parents' bookstore when they retired. He has a regular income from several inventions, mostly early printing techniques for circuit boards. Despite being in his nineties, most locals call him "the younger Mr. Pavello"; his first name is a closely guarded secret. Pavello has amassed one of the largest collections of books on the continent. Unfortunately, his beautiful mind isn't what it once was. Pavello sleeps in the basement of his store, on an antique four-poster bed. One corner of it is held up with an auto jack. A family of rats live in the mattress.

            Pavello is a short man of thin build. He has an olive complexion, and stick-thin, arthritic fingers. Once or twice a year he dyes his hair shoe-polish black; between these beauty treatments he has varying lengths of white roots. He always wears a grey flannel suit, with the slacks pulled up to his chest. He walks with a foosh-fooshing shuffle. This is partly due to his advanced age, and partly due to the fluffy slippers he insists on wearing at all times.

            It can be difficult to engage Pavello in anything beyond perfunctory customer service, as he is painfully shy. Once you hit on a topic he enjoys, he will brighten and follow you around the store handing you related books, and cracking decades old dad-jokes and groaner puns. Be careful, however, if you are in his store in the hours straddling sunset and sunrise as he is prone to fits of rage in which he furiously restocks books, and throws small objects in frustration. There have been a few incidents with browsers, but no one has been hurt so far. In truth, he finds these times more frightening than witnesses do; these are his most confused moments and they can leave him in tears afterward.

            Pavello has a habit of opening and closing the store at odd times, though he always opens it when the sun is out and closes it during the night time hours. So, today he might be open 5pm to 4am. Tomorrow, the schedule is a more conventional 7am to 7pm.

            Story Use: The store is popular among undergrads and professors alike. It also has a following among local mages, who use it for coincidental research. This would be an excellent place for cross-species meetings for academic supernaturals. It's also useful for research, perhaps moreso than the nearby University Library.

                Rotary Park Transverse Comfort Stations

                In a busy neighborhood downtown, five streets meet at odd angles. In 1890, tired of traffic confusion, the city tore down four blocks of tenements and built a massive roundabout with a large park in the middle. Ignoring the advice of writers concerned with redundancy, the city named the circle road "Rotary Circle Drive". The park, however, was not popular at all. The problem? Crossing heavy traffic on foot made it too dangerous to get there.

                In 1935, as a WPA project, a transverse tunnel was installed. A small art-deco building on each corner held grand staircases descending three stories to a circular hallway directly under Rotary Circle Drive above. The floors were of inlaid terrazzo. Walls were covered in monumental mosaics depicting the myth of Persephone and Hades. Deep alcoves held lunch counters and news stands. Most spectacular were the restrooms. The walls were tiled in aqua-blue glass beads, ranging in size from a ball bearing up to a fist. The floors and stall panels were white marble.

                Three grand arches on the inner wall of the Transverse lead to a grand, curving staircase. A geometric waterfall cascaded three stories through the atrium of the stairwell. Visitors arrived back at ground level surrounded by planters, paths, and trees. A verdant oasis in the city met them. The Rotary Park Transverse Comfort Stations were featured in magazines across the country when it opened, hailed as an architectural triumph.

                The years have not been kind to the Transverse. Budget cuts in the 70's ceased maintenance, and forced the businesses to close. Drug activity in Rotary Park during the crack epidemic of the 80's led to the padlocking of the access buildings and the Transverse's full closure. Crosswalks were installed and all the trees in the park were cut down to make police patrols easier. Today, the park is a brick plaza, with occasional benches. It is so disused, the city is debating selling the land for development.

                The Transverse has seen little activity in forty years. City engineers inspect the structure every six months. Maintenance crews use the grand staircase in the park to store equipment and supplies. Otherwise, the murals are crumbling, the floors are cracking, and the copper fixtures are more verdigris than metal. There is no electricity. A six-foot hatch in a side room is rusted shut, but is beginning to crack. A swampy smell emanates from the city's main rain sewer beyond.

                Story Use: No other vampires have claimed the Transverse, but if your players can't figure out something to do with a 50000 square foot underground space in the heart of downtown, they should go back to playing Toon

                            Something that I have rarely heard people discuss with vampires is long term storage.

                            Centennial Storage:
                            Located in a modern well kept industrial park, Centennial Storage offers 24 hour service with a variety of storage options from smaller closet lockers to large 25' x 25' rooms to your average customer. Their security is above average for such locations and the staff tend to have ex-military and mercenary backgrounds rather than standard rent-a-cops.

                            A service that isn't advertised is that one of the empty storage lockers actually conceals a hidden entrance to an underground facility which has climate controlled vaults with air tight seals. Mortals and cainites alike can rent the above ground storage, but only kindred can rent the underground vaults. The shortest rental agreement for the vaults is 50 years, while there are accounts that are quite a bit older.

                            If anyone comes around asking about a given kindred using their services, the staff are instructed to respond that the kindred used to own a storage locker, but cleared it out the last time they came by. Of course all kindred files point to the same empty storage space. No records are kept of what is in the vaults, just contact information and specifics for vault atmosphere, moisture levels and types of light installed.

                            Depending on your game needs the storage location might be granted the Elysium status to ensure that kindred don't mess with each other. The underground vaults are excellent for any Thaumaturgy or Necromancy practitioner who want to keep their ritual space away from their haven, alternatively the ability to control the environment means that many kindred with older items can safely protect them against the ravages of time. A private library of first editions would greatly benefit from such a storage option.

                                Kowloon Walled Alley
                                • Nestled between two large buildings, a space designed to replicate the famous Kowloon Walled City of Hong Kong, not unlike this (sadly closed) arcade. Just on a smaller scale.
                                • Contains a small shop for buying East Asian imports (some of dubious legality), a vendor selling mostly authentic Hong Kong street food (some ingredients just aren't available in the west), and a corner stuffed with arcade cabinets.
                                • For reasons elucidated below, location hasn't had to pay taxes or stand up to city ordinance for a few years now.
                                • Far larger on the inside than the dimensions of the surrounding buildings would imply.
                                • A technomancer Marauder makes his home inside the space, which has slowly expanded following him moving in. An original resident of the famous slum, being made to leave and seeing his home destroyed broke his mind, turning him into a Quiet-addled Mage. Drawn to the minor attraction by means unknown, he finds it the only place that truly, naturally conforms to his cyberpunk view of the world. Originally an engineer by trade, the impossible upper (deeper?) reaches of the Reality Zone have become his lab, where he perfects his cybernetic inventions.
                                • Casual enthusiasts and cosplayers come to take in the atmosphere. Hong Kong expatriates and tourists come for a bite of familiar food or to grab some familiar products. Virtual Adepts and Etherites come to buy and sell materials and tech with the Marauder.
                                • Original creator of the attraction puts on a positive face, but is very aware of how much the whole thing has gone beyond his control. Deeply afraid of whoever the "Technocratic Union" he keeps overhearing about, and whether they'll come to shut the place down (or worse).

                                    The Bauernbruderen Kolonie

                                    In 1680, a pietist, anabaptist sect in Germany fled religious warfare and economic disruption by fleeing to the New World. They established a farming colony which has lived separately from the secular world ever since. They speak an antiquated dialect of German, which modern German speakers can sort of understand if they ignore all standards of grammar and vocabulary. As a plain sect, they eschew all modern technology. They have retained 17th century working class clothing and manners. They live in tiny villages, with a total population of 2000 souls.

                                    The sect is governed by the Gemeinhaus, a council of a dozen elderly churchmen, who set the rules by which the community live, called the Ordnung.

                                    This is all very quaint and scenic. Dozens of cheap motels, gift shops, and all-you-can-eat buffets line Main Street. Teeming crowds of tourists from the nearby city spend their weekends here, wiping melted ice-cream off their children's hex sign t-shirts. Somehow, they manage to get lost in a five street town. Air-conditioned bus tours leave every half-hour. Don't forget to buy some shoo-fly pie for the guys at work on Monday!

                                    Humans aren't the only tourists. Beyond the boundaries of modern Muhler Borough, tourist honky-tonks give way to the Bauernbruderen communities proper. Rolling hillsides and endless miles of cornfields and bank barns overlook cobblestoned villages. Torches and lanterns light stone farmhouses and the tidy gardens which surround them. Horse-drawn buggies clip-clop from town to town. Elder vampires come here to forget the modern world and stroll through their memories. It's become common practice to transition recently awakened elders into the modern world here.

                                    The Gemeinhaus are aware of the Kindred among them. They view it as a regrettable necessity. The modern world would surely corrupt the members of the sect and intrude on their seclusion if the Anglischer Wampiren didn't intercede to help maintain isolation. In exchange, the Gemeinhaus arrange for sun-proof havens and a supply of healthy vessels. The blood of the Bauernbruderen youth are know for its remarkably clean flavor, free of tobacco, alcohol, preservatives, and chemical flavorings.

                                    Story Role: A sentimental favorite for elders looking to hold meetings, from romantic rendezvous to contract negotiations.

                                        Hank's Body Shop
                                        • Auto mechanic garage, nestled inconspicuously between a thick copse of trees and several mostly abandoned buildings. If not for strategically placed signs and locals willing to give directions, you might not even be aware it's there. This is by design.
                                        • The eponymous Hank performs regular auto repair and customization work, and does so quickly. He doesn't like "normal" clients hanging around.
                                        • Primary business is with underworld types, as Hank specializes in adding hidden features to cars. Secret compartments, covert weapon holsters, and various "Bond gadgets" (machine guns in the headlights, oil slicks, etc).
                                        • If it can be accomplished with mundane technology (or borderline technomancy), Hank can put it in a car and make it inconspicuous.
                                        • Smugglers, drug dealers, and underground street racers are his primary clientele, and they pay top dollar.
                                        • A hidden elevator on the garage floor can lower a vehicle to a secret basement level, where much of the heavy modification takes place.
                                        • As insurance, Hank secretly maintains extensive records of his illicit work, in case he needs to bring everyone down with him. These records are booby trapped with incendiary devices, to burn the evidence and anyone who would try to steal it. (A nasty surprise to any incautious vampire burglar).

                                                Borderlands Cowboy Apparel
                                                • Sells cowboy hats, cowboy boots, belts and belt buckles, jeans, rope, denim jackets, and various cowboy-themed knick-knacks.
                                                • Old style country/western music playing over loud speaker.
                                                • Afternoon hours, counter is manned by bored teenager, who barely feigns interest in "cowboy stuff". Early and noon hours manned by septuagenarian gentleman with thick white mustache, a full ten gallon hat, an eyepatch, and nasty scars on his face and arms. If asked, says he got mauled by mountain lions while hunting.
                                                • Hidden back room is the meeting place for a cell of the Tin Stars, a minor monster hunter organization operating in the American southwest. Founded in the 1800s by a group of sheriffs and deputies, the Tin Hats were always a small and deeply superstitious group, passing duties down through family lines. (They long ago learned to distrust anyone else; infiltrators and blood-addled spies burned them once too many times). The cell working out of this store has been less active for years now, but have ramped up activity now that a new generation is of age to start training.
                                                • Lock-box in the back room contains a number of weapons - including revolvers loaded with silver bullets - and a number of spare tin star badges.

                                                    University Steam Tunnels
                                                    • Extensive network of utility tunnels and shafts beneath the local university campus.
                                                    • Connected to sewer system via poor municipal planning and weak concrete walls. An exposed pipe near the river provides relatively easy access from the surface.
                                                    • Most direct entrances sealed or locked by school administrators, after a student got lost in the tunnels and disappeared.
                                                    • Previously Nosferatu territory, the student in question was part of a LARPing group that used the tunnels in a game. Fell prey to a Nosferatu neonate (later executed). Resulting week-long search of the tunnels forced Nosferatu to abandon the steam tunnels and much of the surrounding sewer system. Lost student was never found.
                                                    • Decades later, gamers still make pilgrimage to the infamous steam tunnels, holding semi-annual LARPs, despite attempts by the school and police to stop it. Continued regular presence of individuals keep local Nosferatu wary of moving back in.
                                                    • A hidden cache of weapons, filing cabinets, and stashed bodies is said to exist, sealed, inside the steam tunnels. Nosferatu refuse to confirm or deny its existence.
                                                    • If the various entrances on campus weren't locked or sealed, the tunnels would make for a perfect means of getting around campus unseen.
                                                    • Sound of dripping, pumping water can be heard. Smells of mildew, rust, and rat droppings.

                                                        North Side Pet Supply
                                                        • Sells pet food, toys, accessories, etc. Many are clearance items bought on cheap from rotated out stock of chain stores. Anything not perishable will tend to be old.
                                                        • Small animals - hamsters, gerbils, small birds, occasionally kittens or puppies - for sale. Gets them from shady breeders, or whenever someone in the area posts having found a litter by happenstance. Borderline illegal, but the store keeps them healthy and fed enough. Store also sells mice, mostly as snake food (both live and frozen).
                                                        • Nestled between other businesses of greater note (basically next to whatever location the PCs may find themselves visiting). By default, neighbors are a law firm and a thrift store.
                                                        • During the day, counter maintained by a veterinary school drop-out, who had to leave school due to burnout. Is saving money to start classes again, works at the store because the owner is too desperate to keep an employee to refuse paying well. Wouldn't pass up a chance to make a quick buck, within most ethical constraints (they didn't get into veterinary work for the money).
                                                        • Owner is wanted for connection to a dog-fighting ring. Paranoid, in hiding under an assumed name. Is haunted by the memory of mutilated dogs (as he should be), has trouble sleeping.
                                                        • Store was broken into months ago, during the dead of night, multiple times in the span of a few weeks. Shelves of product disturbed, objects broken, several animals taken, with blood spilled on the scene.
                                                        • Unwilling to involve the cops (not that they'd help), owner has taken to maintaining vigil in the store during night hours. Had to hire an employee just to keep the store running. Sleeps fitfully in a hammock in the back room during the day. Carries a shotgun during night, kept in locker during day (combination lock).
                                                        • Sounds: Chirping birds, audible (but functional) AC, scurrying in metal cages, recorded lectures on a laptop, muffled snoring.
                                                        • Smells: Fur, feathers, bird seed, dog/cat food, animal feces, disinfectant.

                                                            Crowe Road Motel
                                                            • Situated within two hours drive from major city, in a small town with a few tourist attractions. Mostly a stop along the highway.
                                                            • Motel kept in reasonably good condition, for a motel. Preserves 70s era decor and wallpaper. Cheaper than the closest chain hotel.
                                                            • Owned by a set of ghouls in thrall to a Caitiff, who is also the regnant to the nearby diner. Caitiff has Obfuscate 3, and uses it to maintain a few faces, including a father and son pair (really just the same face kept aging to keep up with the times; will be replaced soon when the father "retires to out of state").
                                                            • Caitiff the only permanent Kindred resident. Driven out of most cities, he settled here and took control of the motel. Keeps decor the same out of nostalgia.
                                                            • Motel acts as a common stop for Caitiff and Thin Bloods, as well as Gangrel or Ravnos willing to play nice and cause no trouble. Ask about the "maximum shut-eye" accommodations, to make the room more amenable to sensitive skin.
                                                            • Motel is an unofficial Elysium. Agents working for the nearby city's sheriff will hang around town, looking for known trouble-makers, or to warn Caitiff/Thin Bloods heading into the city to keep their visits brief. The motel owners have an understanding with these men. They let the Sheriff have a measure of influence here and contact them if Sabbat packs move through, in return they are left mostly alone and receive regular payment.
                                                            • A paranoid conspiracy theorist has been staying in one of the rooms for three weeks, afraid of some mysterious "Them" that's out to get him. If guns start going off, he's sure to crack and come out blasting himself.
                                                            • No black lights. Official motel policy.
                                                            • Smells of industrial cleaner and bacon, at all hours. Complimentary breakfast.
