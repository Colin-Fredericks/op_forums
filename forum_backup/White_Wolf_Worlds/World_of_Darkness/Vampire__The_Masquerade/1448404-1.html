        I think context matters. In this case especially the answer will differ depending on that context. Like Doylist/Watsonian views.

        In a pure mechanical view (Doylist) you have Caine being 'super plot device' powerful so he basically has whatever he needs to do what he needs when he needs. It's entirely up to the storyteller. The hows and whys might matter for story purposes, or they might not. For story purposes Caine might not even exist (as some pointed out.)

        Narrative view (Watsonian) it gets more complicated. If you assume the legends have some truth to them and Caine exists, it gets a bit more interesting. One of the things I always remember is how much WW mythology loves cycles. What goes around, comes around. And with the way the vampiric curse works... you have the ancients who created many of the modern disciplines (including the Clan specific ones)... then you have the caitiff (and/or ultimately thin bloods) who have some measure of that same power themselves despite their 'weaker' blood. Logically it follows that if you loop around from Thin Bloods you get to... well, Caine. The ultimate innovator (which reflects his 'Even more Deus Ex Machina' approach to disciplines than Antediluvians do.)

        Some sources (like Blood Magic) draw parallels between blood magic and what Caine was purported to do (this may have just been a Tremere conceit though) and in a sense you can also see Caine as the ultimate Blood Magician, albeit in a more instinctive way (Lilith would have been the more skilled, powerful one.) As MyWifeisScary suggested Caine's powers (which derive in part from Lilith's teachings) can in a sense be considered a mage (although I envisioned it working for vampires more like Requiem's Theban Sorcery or Cruac) although one can argue the ability for Caitiff, very old and very young vampires to create their own disciplines is itself akin to what mages do (again Tremere parallels come to mind.) And Lilith's ties to Awakend magic lend that some credence too I think, although Caine wouldn't incorporate it nearly the same way (in a sense he used what Lilith taught to create cruder more 'linear' approaches and down the generations and years it calcified into what became known as Disciplines.) If you borrow from 5th 'Thin Blood Alchemy' could be seen as a very weak, debaesd form of Cain's 'original' ability too much like Thaumaturgy is.

        I've also believed that 'Thin Blood Syndrome' (to borrow Netchurch's term) could occur at any generation (although it becomes more frequent at higher ones) which may reflect a form of Caitiff as well. The Curse can mutate and in some it may not fully take hold no matter how ancient your sire or great their blood (There was a 6th generation Vampire in the Giovanni Chronicles who still aged once every ten years, like some Thin Blood do) Caine himself may not be 'fully' a vampire due to the unusual circumstances of his creation (but he's vampire-like enough to be treated like it in most ways) and thus likewise the 'curse' affects him differently and different rules can apply. We can also go back to 'cycles' and note that Thin Bloods circle back to Caine.

            So, I've mentioned my wildest head-canon, but the fact is that I adjust the canon to fit my current chronicle (if its even necessary), so I thought I'd bring up another version I've run with.

            Basically it borrows from one of the options presented in BJD where Enoch's coffin is found and opened. In the version I'm thinking of Enoch is not only inside the coffin he's actually lucid and reveals that the Antediluvians aren't nearly as powerful as they pretend to be (and that the point of the Jyhad is to hide this weakness).

            So I ran with that idea and that, in actuality the power level we call 4th Generation actually IS the top of the scale. Now, an Antediluvian has way more XP so more things at 8-9, but they're fundamentally just 4th Gens and the Blood is constantly thickening... every vampire should they live long enough (on the scale of millennia so not generally a play consideration) will see their blood potency reach that of the 4th Generation.

            And here's the trick of it... because everything 4th Gen and lower has the same potency its entirely possible that they aren't even the REAL 4th Generation... just the 4th Generation recorded from a figure calling himself Caine... who might not be the first vampire... just the first one any of the Western Kindred can trace their lineages back to. The actual first vampire (who may as well be called "Caine" in the same way we call our oldest Mitochondrial ancestor "Eve" even though they may not have even been a "he") might be dozens of generations further back. The Antediluvians might actually be the 13th Generation from the actual first vampire, but are regarded as the 3rd because all records of the prior generations of vampires are just lost in pre-history.

            In which case; Caine whether he's the actual first or just the first recorded would be a 4th Gen with maxed out stats and an outsized legend that makes people think he's second only to God in power.
