                This is my theory...some us (myself included) view TTPRGs as like the main focus and business model for the games we love. Be that D&D with wizards of the coast, cyberpunk, etc. They tend to be TTRPG first in their mindset. couple of years ago, Wizards did a reprint of all their old editions and put them into stories. It was kind of a celebration of the game. Paradox is, lets be honest here, a video game company first and foremost. If i had to guess, the TTRPG to them is not the kind of money they are looking for from the IP they purchased. They want that video game and movie money. Since, for me, if you look at it that way...V5 makes a lot of sense. Its streamlined and simplified for a mass audience of video games and moves. They even said as much when the declared V5 was the IP "bible" so to speak for everything else being made.

                Lets be real...converting Revised into movies and video games, most people won't get it. Love it as a TTRPG, but that's got to be a nightmare to get someone who doesn't know it and love it for decades playing the TTRPG to be all about it. Making it simple in this effectively reboot of the IP makes sense to a degree when you look at from that end.

                I am very happy for all the new people V5 brought to the thing we all love. I really am, that's great! And if people love it, there should absolutely be allowed to do so and I am happy for them. I can see the appeal for a new consumer. That being said, I just want my cake and eat it too. I want to support them and give them my money to help with new products for the things I do run. V5 isn't for me, and that's ok. Like we have been told, just use the old editions. Again, I think the point is wouldn't it be nice in a world where both could exist and be enjoyed? Cast a wide net, not a small net.

                    Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post
                    plus moar money.
                    Not necessarily. Doing a reprint will have costs, many of them non scaling properly. So doing a too short reprint is costly, but doing a too long reprint risks loses. The big advantage of a kickstart project in this regard is precisely to have a notion of scale before printing and covering most costs in advance.

                    Given the availability of the PDFs I'm with CTPhipps on that, just doing a reprint of the V20 core wouldn't answer our question properly (too many loose variables) and would be a completely unnecessary risk for the company. Not to say that it would divert resources meant for the upcoming projects, so it would weaken the V5 in the current time-frame of production.

                    A proper survey would be a more feasible prospect. Still challenging to create (those things are far harder to make than it seems), but incredibly cheaper to make and apply and, with a good survey strategy, it would give us more reliable data.

                                                        Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post
                                                        I think marvel and dc are having simular issue from what you're suggesting might occur with the comics were no ones buying them. I'm unsure how that will be an issue with those excessive numbers of movies but It seems an problem somehow.
                                                        Running a business is tricky, even if you have a good revenue elsewhere you can't just throw money at things without justification. The success of an arm of the company can't be used to justify others not bringing numbers. There are several reasons, but many can be boiled down to a simple statement: an organization never have enough money. The more you grow, the more you have projects that have costs, and you're all the time making choices about how to allocate resources. And to the general public the company is just a rich monolith, but inside each project and arm is a complex network of interests and stakeholders, going from big investors to workers that need to justify their jobs.

                                                        Of course, you can do that in several ways. Currently WotC's main strategy is the crossing over of D&D and MtG, so they're willing to suffer a lesser revenue in D&D as long as it drives up Magic's sales (that make a helluva lot of money). That way D&D does bring revenue anyway, it just isn't direct. But as I see the current Marvel's strategy do not have a transfer point where comics sales help movie sales, and the comics are declining. I seriously think the webcomic format is the future, to be honest, but changing format on your core business is another big challenge.

                                                        Paradox is a games company first AFAIK. The WoD IP is a business asset, but the TTRPG isn't their core business anyway. To anyone that thinks it to be unfair I point first to the fact that without them there would not be a 5th edition in any way or form, and I do think it is a good development in itself. But it doesn't mean that their decisions for the setting are all the best for their purposes. To be sincere I think it have more to do with a specific vision for the setting than with it being more or less welcoming for games.
