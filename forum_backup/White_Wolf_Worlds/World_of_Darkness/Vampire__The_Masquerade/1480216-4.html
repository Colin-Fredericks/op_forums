    Thank you for the shout out, CTPhipps

    (The book he references can be found here.)

    My own issue with the Tzimisce in the past is that for all that could be done with them, they were rather one note. Just a bunch of sado-masochism fetishists with tentacles. That is only striking once and quickly becomes neither frightening nor memorable. It also stops being "alien" almost immediately. The entire set up can also become cheap and comic quickly.

    The fiends should have been presented with a subtler touch. Let the strangeness, and alien qualities in body and mind, slowly be revealed.

    There is something potentially tragic in that the Tzimisce are desperate to give up humanity in body, mind and soul - but for all of that they cannot escape from humanity (and morality) and still retain their identity. To truly escape means they can simply consumed by the monstrosity of their line. Which the Sabbat is rebelling against.

    But generally the nuance and possibility were rejected for an SnM freakshow at the top of its lungs.

                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                        Mind you, if I was a writer on Werewolf, I'd make the Werewolves more prone to being criticized (it's already halfway to the Sabbat) and monsters more ambiguous.
                        There's definitely a lot to criticize, and early Werewolf material is fairly blatant about. 1e is all about Garou society being too out of date and too hidebound. Just like 1e Vampire really encouraged PCs to be Anarchs, 1e Werewolf encouraged PCs to be critical of Garou society. But that wasn't followed through.

                        I think one of the reasons is that the black and white nature of Garou society was something many players liked. They liked a simple Law versus Chaos narrative.

                        Another reason is that many STs and PCs did not want self-critical games. I see all sorts of asshole PCs in Vampire. I rarely see them in Werewolf. If you have a LARP or Online Game in Vampire, you'll find many PC concepts that are villainous. You'll see many analogues to canon NPCs that are antagonists. Asshold Ventrue Prince wannabees. Shitheel Setites. Narcissistic Toreadors that put the Heathers to shame. Power hungry, untrustworthy Tremere. And so on. If go to LARP or Online Games of Werewolf, you won't. You won't find the Shadow Lord PCs conspiring with vampires of other Wyrm minions. You won't find insane Silver Fang kings screaming about lese majeste. You won't find narrow minded Fianna bigots discriminating against Metis. And so on. Instead, everyone is a fairly reasonable and open minded 21st century Western moderate instead of an old style pre-modern people of an aristocratic warrior culture.

                        But that is more about players than the setting itself. Though I agree Revised deliberately downplayed the more objectionable elements of Garou society and the setting. It's one reason I really liked 1e's depiction of the less savory rampant crime and collaboration of its members with their race's enemies and against their own laws, and every tribe nursing ancient grudges against one or more other tribes.

                                OK, Tzimisce and the alien nature of vampires. I actually feel like hyperfocusing on vicissitude as a blatantly obvious "freak show" body-horror discipline makes the Tzimisce less creepy/alien (it is fun sometimes, though). I have it that such uses are considered immature, and a sign that one is a fledgling who by so fervently rejecting a human shape is still defining themselves by humanity. Older Tzimisce have come to understand that their human shell is simply a tool. It isn't what they are, any more than the light at the front of an anglerfish is the fish. Or, for a closer example, a large blue butterfly larva appears to ants like an ant larva, which it uses to inflitrate ant nests and prey on the ants who have been fooled into thinking it's one of them. A caterpillar isn't a baby ant, a vampire isn't a human.

                                The horror of vampires, for kine and even many kindred, is the uncanny valley effect and the fear of parasites. It looks human, it can think like a human, yes. Because it's an act by a specialised predator that utilises aggressive mimicry to hunt humans. The body it inhabits is dead. The vampire is the thing inside the corpse. Their reproductive process is parasitical and we're the host species. Lucky us!
                                Their faux-humanity is exactly what makes vampires alien horrors, in my view, and vicissitude is the exaggeration of that. Not an obvious threat at first glance, there's no reason to have your guard up. The edits are a subtle advantage and rarely come out until it's too late when the deception is revealed and oh god no. Edit your joints to move in for inhuman range and flexibility. Alter your vocal chords to sound like someone or something else. Maybe adjust your voice to a permanent pitch that triggers ASMR to endear, relax and further manipulate your prey, or distort it to induce a fear response. Limbs, necks and waists that rotate 360° or whatever the hell you want to do that gives you an advantage.
                                But their biggest advantage is that unlike most vampires, who steal one person's identity at their birth, Tzimisce can do this as much as they damn well want.

                                A Tzimisce can kill you, trade faces with you, and steal your whole life without anybody ever knowing. Works on kindred too. Announce at Elysium that the court has been infiltrated by Tzimisce. We don't know how many or whose face they're using. Any and all signs of people acting "out of character" are now worthy of pitchforks and torches. Enjoy your paranoia. In some ways they're better at camoflauge than obfuscate and protean users. They make wonderful spies and assassins.

                                Vampires are parasitic pseudohuman mimics that modify and utilitse their host bodies as tools, and the Tzimisce are the epitome of this. They can explore and evolve the nature of vampirism to a greater extent than their cousins.
                                Nah, I think vicissitude is great. As far as I'm concerned, Old Clan Tzimisce is nothing special and is as limiting a concept as the vicissitude monsters. But that said, I can kind of see both being redundant. If I were to cut down on the clans, I think Old Clan would be a Ventrue bloodline and vicissitude users would be merged with the Gangrel or reclassified as one of their bloodlines.

                                Edit: but the territoriality is also an important aspect of the clan, and V5's take on their weakness is the one I use. I just don't like that aspect of them being limited to "one-note Dracula rip off" any more than I like vicissitude being reduced to splatterpunk.

                                    Thinking back, I don't think my first encounters with Tzimisce was a positive one, much like the Sabbat as given for the first and second editions of VtM. I think someone said splatterpunk before, and I think that is a good description. Not my cup of tea.

                                    However, with the release of the Vampire: the Dark Ages, something much better came out, and for a while it was a much better clan. It felt like they did something great with what they had to work with from the legacy of The Players Handbook to the Sabbat. We got characters such as Rustovich, Radu and Vykos, who at least at this stage were not simply defined by a single discipline. There was some variations, naturally, memory is best when looked at with a less critical eye. They were a noble but still ruthless clan. Tradition meant something.
                                    With revenants added (in Ghouls: Fatal Addiction, if I remember correctly?), this put a whole new layer to the Tzimisce and the way they lorded over their ghouls and lands. Not everything was based on Vicissitude. There were still the monstrosities, if you wanted that aspect, but there were choices.
                                    I'll bring up the Dirty Secrets. There seems to be two camps, those who enjoyed it, and those who did not. I am part of the camp that did not enjoy it. The best part of the book was the Old Clan Tzimisce. Perhaps this was what the Dark Ages used as a foundation for the clan?

                                    Then came the revised editions. To its credit, it mostly made the Sabbat playable, and by that Tzimisce. On the other hand, it started to snowball, with more and more focus about Vicissitude. They have an entire Path that requires the Discipline. I do not necessarily feel this is something strange, as with every discipline, there is bound to be a morality if it is pushed to far, and I would love to have seen the same work with other disciplines - particularly the common ones. But Tzimisce, at this point, had just one clan discipline, and two other that were cheaper. It was never a discussion if a Tzimisce was going to have Vicissitude, but rather how they were going to work in having Body Crafts into their concept. I felt the foundation Vampire: the Dark Ages was being eroded, and I lost interest in the Clan.

                                    In the end, we had a hiatus due to the world ending and all that, but old habits die hard. Ran a Sabbat campaign where only "the common eight" disciplines were available, and had someone directly drawn to Tzimisce with no Vicissitude. It was nice to run tyrannical Tzimisce and not having to figure out how they would out-do each other in freakshow.

                                    I read the V20 versions. It is nice to see love for a game that I played so much in my youth, but life had gotten in the way at this point for gaming.

                                    On to V5. They captured what I like most about Tzimisce. Vicissitude is still there, but it is an expensive option. Expensive as in the powers given up. I do not think trading Dominate for Auspex undervalues them at all. The Tzimisce were, in my opinion, made awesome despite their disciplines, not because. They resemble much more a feral VtR Ventrue now, which once again, in my opinion is a clear upgrade that this is the 'default' Tzimisce, not the 'splatterpunk' Tzimisce - but again, this is my opinion.
                                    That V5 requires a human touchstone would somehow remove the playability of Tzimisce as they are to 'cold and callous' is something I would consider a fallacy. While this may be a correct stereotype for the Sabbat/"old world" Tzimisce, it is not true on an individual level. The way touchstones are setup is to play young vampires in a Camarilla or Anarch setting. It is a highly flexible and independent system, where it is quite possible to both remove and replace parts of it at to better suit the theme and mood that one wishes to play by. I think in general this is a major strength with V5, as it is very modular, something apparently the credit goes to Kenneth Hite for.

                                    They have been a favorite clan of mine, and I would say despite their disciplines. Successively, it became less of a clan and more of a discipline, and for me, that lost my interest. The same goes for a lot of Clans. The number of Tremere characters without Thaumaturgy could probably be counted on one hand, and an arguments could be made for every bloodline, Ravnos, Giovanni, Dementation Malkavians. Lasombra were successively pushed into this niche as well, partly because of the strong mechanics of Obtenebration, but also because of paths and abyss rituals and what not.
                                    Once a discipline becomes this integrated and critical into a clan, it is my opinion that it looses something and the whole becomes a lot less.
                                    In V5, I think the Tzimisce once again back on the right track. Or at least the track I find interesting.

                                                        There's definitely a lot to criticize, and early Werewolf material is fairly blatant about. 1e is all about Garou society being too out of date and too hidebound. Just like 1e Vampire really encouraged PCs to be Anarchs, 1e Werewolf encouraged PCs to be critical of Garou society. But that wasn't followed through.

                                                        I think the big difference, ultimately, is the overall storyline. In Werewolf you're playing monsters, but monsters who ultimately make the world a better place. If the Garou all vanished, companies like Pentex would be free to run roughshod over everything, and that would be horrific. As bad as werewolves can be, ultimately the world is a better place because they exist.

                                                        Conversely, vampires are parasites who drink blood and manipulate humans. They're monsters and the world is worse because of it. You may have the rare example of a vampire who spends his unlife trying to improve the lives of those around him, or vampires who use their powers to destroy evil beings, but they're very much exceptions. And Kindred don't even have the plausibility of Wan Kuei, where a Devil Tiger can justify their actions by pointing towards their battle against the Yama Kings, or the need for devils to inflict punishment on humanity, as proof that they're making the world a better place.

                                                        Ultimately even when the Garou elders are hidebound, they're still fighting the good fight and whatnot. And werewolf players are encouraged to make characters that will be putting the good of Gaia and the good of other werewolves first, rather than playing the character who is out for numero uno. Vampires are definitely not fighting the good fight, and when making a vampire, there's no narrative pressure to make one who's putting the good of humanity first or anything like that. So vampire players are more free to play selfish assholes than Garou since even a selfish asshole Garou is, narratively, still balanced by the fact that he's putting his life on the line to fight greater evils to save the world.
