    My home Los Angeles game got into the politics of BLOODLINES a bit more than the actual game did (since the Fledgling or "Los Angeles Ripper" as they became known was not privy to these sort of things).

    Basically, poor Sebastian La Croix was subject to a Genghis Gambit from the Camarilla.

    https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.p.../GenghisGambit

    The Anarch Free States were exhausted from fighting their initial war against Kuei Jin and no longer able to keep the Camarilla from asserting praxis in the area. However, whatever Prince was installed had no real chance of asserting true control over the area regardless of how exhausted the Anarchs or Kuei Jin were (and neither was GONE).

    Sebastian La Croix despite being one of Napoleon's calavryman was an Elder who commanded almost no respect or loyalty among the Ventrue. He acts like a preening pompous fool because, well, he is, but he's also under tremendous stress because he KNOWS he's been sent to this prestigious assignment with the full expectation he's going to die.

    The Camarilla's plan, which only Strauss really knows the full parts of, is that they hope Sebastian will do the dirty work enough of killing the Anarchs and Cathayans enough that they will collapse but also being such an annoyance that eventually someone will polish him off. This will end with the Camarilla making a treaty with the survivors and putting their real candidate in charge that will look good by comparison to dear old Sebastian.

            As I perform archaeology on old sourcebooks, I find things I'd completely forgotten about. The Ventrue Clanbook 1e has a "distinguished Ventrue" named Stephan Workman. He's a young Ventrue involved in the computer industry who is obviously an analogue of Steve Jobs (the relationship between their names is self-evident). This poor sap isn't even mention in the revised clanbook, and doesn't even have his own page on the White Wolf Wiki despite unknown Kindred, whose only mention is on single V:TES cards, getting their own pages that just repeat what clan they are as shown on their card. One reason might be there is mention of various crossover elements like the Virtual Adepts and Glass Walkers, as well as an attack on him by Eastern Kindred by what is obvious a penanggalan. Revised would have its own very distinct opinions on crossover elements and how the Kindred of the East should be presented.

            However, for our purposes on this thread, this is a great character. Steve Jobs as a Ventrue vampire in San Jose is an interesting element. He's obviously not an anarch, but must somehow be tolerated by them. Possibly because a lot of older Ventrue hate him for disrupting their influence over older technology companies. A major difference is that Stephan Workman is probably not known by the general public, unlike Steve Jobs. He'd be more like an almost anonymous venture capitalist who supports and funds entrepreneurs like Steve Jobs. I imagine many anarchs simply enjoy him annoying older, more established Ventrue without realizing he's going to become king of Silicon Valley and an incredibly important member of clan Ventrue's global directorate. He's interesting flavor in early nineties California. He'd be the new Horatio Ballard in 2025 controlling vast swaths of the global economy.

            As a Ventrue in San Jose, his sire is most likely a Ventrue from San Francisco.

                                I learn something new everyday. I found another mention of San Jose. It's something that seems to contradict prior established canon, but I expect that now. The Revised era's setting pretty much is its own Earth-Two to the Earth-One of the 1//2e era.

                                In the sourcebook Havens of the Damned, there's actually an entry on the Winchester Mystery House. It is inhabited by the secret "architect" of the house, the Malkavian Jonathon Smith. Very creative name there. Also creative is that his sire was the Malkavian Ivo Shandor who in a mad fit convinced Smith to diablerize him so Smith now hears Ivo's voice telling him what to do.


                                I'm all in favor of people's home chronicles using whatever they want. I think this would be hilarious and nice homage in someone's chronicle. But in an officially published sourcebook this kind of direct reference to another media should not be done. Isn't that why all the characters from Upton Sinclair's The Jungle were killed off in Under a Blood Red Moon?

                                Anyway, Smith followed Winchester to her new home in San Jose, hid in her house, and by gaslighting her during seances, indirectly controlled how the house would be built. Smith may (or may not) have combined Dementation and some blood ritual to be able to mentally control the layout of the house in some kind of non-euclidean nightmare that may or may not lead to extradimensional travel. Or that may just be his delusion. Sara Winchester herself now haunts the house herself since Smith was the one who killed her by drinking her blood.

                                The LABN sourcebook obviously does not mention this, but does mention in its San Jose entry. It says several Kindred have tried claiming it as a haven, but that none have survived their first night. Obviously, it is possible to reconcile these two versions by saying that it is Smith who is somehow destroying any new Kindred who intrude.

                                In the Changeling game, the Winchester Mystery House is also a powerful source of Glamour with hundreds of chimerical creatures and objects. No changelings live here because there's too many dangerous creatures, but some do visit to harvest glamour and screw around with things. Again, this does not technically contradict the haven in Havens of the Damned, but I'm pretty sure the author of the Havens entry ignored everything previously mentioned.

                                Will I use this in my chronicle? Probably not directly, but I might use the general idea of some powerful Malkavian dwelling within the house be responsible for a lot of odd things in San Jose. Maybe his occasional activities outside the house creates enough uncertainty and confusion that it keeps the anarchs unbalanced and uncertain how to proceed. So they take a live and let live approach to things. I'm more inclined the make house itself some kind of haunt with a circle of wraiths there because I think Wraith works better with Vampire than Changeling. Maybe they can make use of this particular Malkavian so they help him out if other Kindred intrude in it. Maybe the wraiths here are skilled in classic haunting Arcanoi like Pandemonium, but also the forbidden Arcanoi like Flux and possibly Intimation and Mnemosynis. It would make them capable defenders that could really confuse any intruders exploring the house. Also being the home of a crazy vampire with mental powers might make it less suspicious to the Hierarchy if they hear of "hauntings" there.

                                                Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                                                On an equally serious note, having just worked out that the Bay Area of the early AD 1990s could support roughly 60-90 vampires (The former based on standard Camarilla ratio, the latter on an ‘Anarch ratio’ about halfway between the Camarilla and the Sabbat), then estimating that San Francisco proper could support about 7 of those vampires (By strict Camarilla ratio) and noting that there are said to be 10 Anarchs in San Jose, it occurs to me that this leaves between 40-70 vampires in locations to be determined.
                                                Canonical vampire populations are based on the mortal population of the metropolitan area, not the city itself. I think the idea is that even though people in the suburbs don't live in the city (and the suburbs are considered to be "barrens" where hunting is difficult because of lack of nightlight, low crime, and lack of anonymity), the people there are constantly commuting to and from the city making themselves available. Like all game conventions, it's just meant as a convenient way to quickly calculate an "appropriate" amount of vampires. While it has no basis in reality, I accept it in my games.

                                                The Bay Area is obviously quite large geographically and divided into several distinct urban zones. I would use three, meaning there are three separate domains. However, it's entirely possible it is (or could be) one domain that is useful to think of consisting of three parts. Roughly speaking that is San Francisco area itself, the East Bay around Oakland, and the South Bay around San Jose

                                                San Francisco metro area itself likely encompasses San Francisco and San Mateo counties for a combined population of around 1.5 million in 1990. So probably around 15-20 vampires.

                                                Oakland metro area probably consists of Contra Costa and Alameda counties for a combined population of around 2.0 million in 1990. While San Francisco itself is the larger city, this is where most of the population is at. Probably another 20 vampires. I'll admit I did not know how large the East Bay counties were until I just did research right now.

                                                Then there is the San Jose metro area in Santa Clara county which is another 1.7 million in 1990. LABN only shows San Jose's actual population of 782k at the time, and thus estimates 10 vampires here. If we use the county's population and the great ratio of anarch vampires, 17-25 vampires.

                                                That is over five million people all together. Maybe 52-65 vampires total. The SF metro area turns out to be the smallest one of the three, but definitely the oldest with the most established vampires and a thriving tourist industry.

                                                The North Bay comprises four counties - Marin, Sonoma, Napa, and Solano counties. Their combined population is around 1.1 million in 1990, but that is spread out over a fairly large area. Most of it rural. There is probably very little Kindred activity in Marin and Sonoma because of the presence of a Garou caern. I would place this in a separate category and remove it from normal Kindred population except for some Gangrel and occasional loner vampire keeping low in wine country.

                                                Back in 1945, urban sprawl was low enough that Vannevar Thomas was probably the only real prince. Oakland would have had vampires, but would either have been considered part of Vannevar's domain, or its prince would probably be in a defacto or even dejure subordinate to Vannevar. San Jose would not even be considered as its own entity in terms of vampires.

                                                San Jose probably had the presence of anarchs to make it a contested territory, but that would probably not have been enough to be its own "barony" until the boom years of Silicon Valley in the seventies. At that point the presence of anarchs likely made it be considered its own "domain" separate from Vannevar's rule in San Francisco itself.

                                                The decision to make San Francisco some kind of separate, independent "palatinate" would have made Oakland and the East Bay an important Camarilla domain. It's probably there that any archons "officially" reside, and the Camarilla would have supported (and possibly controlled) any Prince of the East Bay. The urban growth of the East Bay might even have been a plan by the Camarilla to cement its power in the area.

                                                The East Bay is essentially ignored by LABN probably because nobody thought Oakland and its environs was important enough to mention. Based on the hard to read map of California there, it appears Oakland is supposed to be part of the Free States. However, there is no mention of an Oakland barony. Oakland also doesn't seem to be mentioned in the Anarch Cookbook either. We could see a theoretical "three way" contest between the anarchs in the South Bay, the palatinate in SF proper, and the Camarilla in the East Bay. Of the three, the Camarilla's control of the East Bay would mean they have the slightly larger population between the three.

                                                That might also be another reason the anarchs don't move on SF. They could be caught in a pincer move between Vannevar and the Camarilla if they attack one. Instead, Vannevar plays up his independence and the possible "common enemy" he and the anarchs have. SF and the East Bay could play a good cop/bad cop thing between them to keep the local anarchs in control. Indeed, since the palatinate likely only exists because of the anarch threat, a complete victory over the Free States would soon see another Conclave that revokes the Treaty. Therefore, Vannevar may even help the anarchs in some capacity some of the time, earning some grudging respect and showcasing how important it is that SF keeps its status quo.

                                                It might be interesting to have the anarch NPCs mention Oakland as the big Camarilla threat then rather than San Francisco. That should surprise players. And as San Jose grows in population and economic power (today it is the largest city in the Bay Area and third largest in the state), simply keeping it anarch would be a sufficient challenge.

                                                            So based on the above, I'm using this for the population of vampires in San Francisco Bay circa 1992:

                                                            San Francisco itself is around twenty vampires. A bit higher than the metro area suggests because SF is a tourist hotspot with lots of intransigents passing through. East Bay is also twenty vampires.

                                                            I'm also putting San Jose as twenty as well. Ten anarchs to use the figure in LABN plus another ten vampires loosely affiliated with the Camarilla or "neutral". That allows for vampires like the Ventrue Stephan Workman. The anarchs and non-anarchs more or less tolerate each other. No one is really in charge, and each side respects the other's turf. Nobody wants to be responsible for initiating a conflict that might suck in SF, Oakland, or LA. San Jose and Santa Clara County is booming at this time with large increases in population so nobody is hurting for feeding territory.

                                                            The VtM fiction stories set in San Francisco makes the standard assumption of seven primogen, each representing one of the clans of the Inner Circle. While popular, I have never liked this conceit. Chicago By Night did not follow this. Neither the Malkavians or Ventrue had a member on the primogen council, and the Brujah had two (possibly three at one time) members. IMO, the primogen are simply the most powerful individual vampires in a domain who seek to wield influence collectively. They aren't some official clan representatives. There's no problem for a chronicle to have one from each clan, but it isn't some Camarilla rule.

                                                            In regards to San Francisco, having seven primogen means the primogen alone are one third to one half the entire vampire population of the city. That seems far too high for me. I don't think domains should be that top heavy. You need a lot more lower status and neonate vampires than high status elders. It's likely Keith Herbert, the author of Dark Prince, never bothered to consider what the population of San Francisco should be when he populated his domain, or perhaps he simply considered the domain to be the entire San Francisco Bay area (a defensible choice, but not one I am using here). So I am arbitrarily splitting the canonical SF primogen between SF and Oakland. So who to keep and who to remove?

                                                            The canonical members (at the time of the first novel, Dark Prince):
                                                            • Tomaine (Brujah Primogen)
                                                            • Alicia McGreb (Gangrel Primogen)
                                                            • Bloody Mary (Malkavian Primogen)
                                                            • The Dowager (Nosferatu Primogen)
                                                            • Allanyan Serata (Toreador Primogen)
                                                            • Honerius (Tremere Primogen)
                                                            • Nickolai (Ventrue Primogen)
                                                            Who to keep in San Francisco? The Dowager is strongly associated with Chinatown so she has to stay. Likewise there is a strong Toreador influence in the city. Since Sebastian Melmoth is non-political, I'm keeping Allanyan Serata. Honerius is involved in several high stakes plots in canon, so I'll keep him there as well. I am also keeping Nickolai as a character although whether he's actually a primogen I'll decide later. Those four plus the Prince, Sebastian Melmoth, and Tex R. Cainen means there's still only 8-12 more vampires total. Not a whole lot if you're hoping to include vampires from the other clans and multiple factions. This is basically a domain of Ventrue, Nosferatu, and Toreador plus one Tremere chantry. Very few characters of anyone else. Very much a "high clans" situation.

                                                            That means I'm moving Tomaine, Alicia McGreb, and Bloody Mary over to the East Bay. They all seem to have the least personality and least overt ties to iconic San Francisco elements so moving them to Oakland isn't much of a loss.
