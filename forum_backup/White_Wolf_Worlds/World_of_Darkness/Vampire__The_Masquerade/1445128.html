    vampire relic - the Eye of the All-High

    04-21-2021, 05:53 PM
    For one of my chronicles, I needed a potent occult item as a quest goal for one of the antagonists. I decided what fit best was something similar to one of those vampire artifacts originally listed in the old Storyteller's Handbook and Sabbat Player's Guide. They had things like a preserved Giovanni tongue that allowed you to lie real well, a magic powder that could prevent someone from shapechanging (one of whose ingredients was blood from a Gangrel), and a femur from a 4th generation Tzimisce that could be used as an aggravated weapon and might have other powers.

    Vampire stopped including lists like these in books because I think they felt it might be becoming too much like a D&D treasure item list. But I feel that an individual item by itself was perfectly fine, especially if a major plot was built around it rather than it being something there in the background.

    So I decided this NPC's quest item was the Eye of Odin, aka the plucked eye from the Gangrel Methusaleh known as the All-High.

    The NPC seeking this doesn't know Odin is/was a vampire. He just knows the eye has occult significance regardless whether Odin was a materialized god walking the lands, an ancient magician, legendary human king, or something else. Once he obtains the Eye, he intends to perform an occult ritual with it. If the NPC discovers the Eye has specific powers of its own, it'll be an unexpected bonus.

    What powers should the Eye have? I have my own ideas, but while appropriate they're kind of ordinary and obvious. So I decided to crowdsource and see if you have any good ideas that I can steal! Besides powers, feel free to put down any curses or interesting stories the Eye might have accrued down the centuries.

    Thanks for your help.

              My Rules for the Eye of Hazmiel were based around the idea the primary benefit of the Eye was the fact that the Kindred who does the Vecna thing (removes their perfectly healthy eye to put in a Methuselahs), gain the ability to use the Disciplines of the Kindred in question.

              My assumption being that he's a 4th generation Gangrel.

              However, being a Methuselah and a tricky one at that, I presume that the Eye of Odin would also have a pretty hefty curse to it. In my Esoterrorism novel, the wizard who actually had this exact same artifact was eventually transformed into a mental doppelganger for Odin who carried out the will of the All-High. Presumably Odin in the World of Darkness would also gain the ability to possess the Eye holder at any time even if he's in torpor.

                      Thanks for your contributions. These are the kind of ideas I hoped to hear.

                      I admit I had not thought of the comparison to the Eye of Hazimel, but then again I thought the Clan Novels were awful and it was around this time the Revised metaplot drove me away from buying any more VtM sourcebooks. So that wasn't lodged in my mind.

                      I think I'll stop short of outright D&D level artifact powers. It's a little too strong for me. But I do think I'll bump up its power to be more than the examples given of vampire relics. People obviously think that such an item should be more powerful than the relics who are named after their colorful, but rather anonymous vampires. And that makes a lot of sense. Toomler, of the Femur of Toomler relic, is supposed to be a fourth generation Tzmisce which is similar to the All High. But who has ever heard of Toomler? Everybody knows who Odin is. Expectations are different, and my players will likely react the same way as you.

                      I think I will go two routes. First are the "ordinary" powers the possessor could use by simply holding the eye. The second route would be what is possible should he remove his own eye and place this in his own socket. That last is more like a high fantasy darklord or comic book supervillain style thing to do, but it's certainly still an option. Both are in addition to its usefulness in whatever occult rituals the NPC would do.

                      I like the idea of a curse where the Eye dominates the possessor. However, I'll make it more long term and subtle. And rather than the Gangrel Methuselah possessing him outright, it'll be more like the person slowly becomes - mentally speaking - Odin: thinking like him and eventually acting like him. But that's probably over a timeline greater than what will happen in the chronicle. But at least I have something where this can go if this does somehow become an ongoing thing. I try to structure my chronicles so they can occur over a long period of time in game (like several decades) so conceivably this could still have an impact during the chronicle.

                                  It's probably a given that you have to rip out your own eye to make use of the All-High's, and that the methuselah remains the Eye's true master.

                                  I like the idea of the Eye granting use of the All-High's Disciplines. But that feels like a repeat of Hazimel and less than true to the mythological Odin, who sacrifices his eye for wisdom. In the vampiric context, wisdom is knowedge of the Jyhad. The Eye could grant its bearer the gift of prophecy and allow them to see visions and hints of things to pass. The wiser and more powerful the bearer, the more they see, much like the One Ring only let Gollum turn invisible but would have made Galadriel into a new Sauron. In a methuselah's hands, the Eye is an incredible oracular tool, and gives the All-High powers of foresight to rival the greatest Malkavian seers. He uses the Eye's insights to influence the Jyhad on a global scale in ways vast and small. In a neonate's hands, the Eye's utility is more personal, and it gives them warnings of things soon to come--your coterie-mate is plotting against you, the harpies will pick on you at Elysium tonight, the blood you're about to consume is laced with LSD.

                                  One interesting idea in D&D 4e was the concept of artifact attunement. Or whatever they called it. The idea was that the more you pleased an artifact by acting in line with its interests, the greater the powers it bestowed. If you displeased an artifact, you wouldn't receive fewer powers than the baseline level, but they would start to go haywire or the artifact might occasionally attack you. This was a neat way to have artifacts not simply grant all of their powers up front and make how the PC interacted with the artifact more important.

                                  The Eye might only grant minor powers of insight to its bearer at first, such as warnings of imminent danger. As the bearer acts in tune with the All-High's desires (perhaps freely, perhaps as a result of the artifact's corrupting influence), the Eye might grant visions of more distant futures, or visions of opportunities the bearer might seize to obtain power over others, rather than simply escape trouble. Artifacts rarely stay indifferent to their bearers, and will either grant more power over time as the bearer proves worthy/easy to influence, or move on to someone else if the bearer proves unworthy/too hard to influence, ala the One Ring slipping away Gollum.

                                  I also like the idea that if the bearer displeases the All-High, the Eye doesn't betray them--instead, it shows too much. The bearer sees visions of future doom and calamity they are powerless to avoid. They stumble across dangerous secrets which get them in way over their head. The Eye might never slip away from "unworthy" bearers, but instead drive them to such despair that they gouge it out to rid themselves of the accursed thing. This makes it very easy to find former bearers--they all have one eye...

                                  The Eye also might not demand the sacrifice of the bearer's actual eye at first. Only someone who knows what the artifact is and badly desires its powers will make that trade. Instead, the Eye might offer minor insights initially, so long as the bearer carries the Eye somewhere on their person--seems harmless enough. Over time, the Eye might urge the bearer to feed it blood in return for more potent visions, and only require gouging out one's own eye for its greatest insights. But once you do, there's no going back.

                                  It goes without saying that any vampire who rips out their eye for the artifact shouldn't be able to regrow it.

                                  The Eye of Odin also showed up as an artifact in the TV show Gargoyles, if you're looking for more inspiration.

                                  Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.
