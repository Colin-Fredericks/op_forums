                                            Well, there is an entire thread about Wan Kuei and I would repeat a lot of talk here so I'm not going into that, otherwise I would be a bore. But I believe (headcanon if you will) that the Wan Kuei might just "work" as Laibon, Drowned Legacy, Cainites.... blood vampires, but they developed such a different study, comprehension and spirituality of their condition that it seems like they are different beings. I think like this just not to go with "the Asian lands breeds spirit-vamps just because" (it's not such a problem for me, but by my call it is unnecessary). And so, the different game mechanics represent this deep, intrinsical understanding (not necessarily the "truth"), that diverges from Cainites.


                                            As for the Setites, as all Clans I prefer not to think them so much politically and socially, but conceptually. To worship Set isn't a conceptual key for them, not for my interpretation, it is a hard and embedded expression of their themes. All those ideas of understanding that Gods, Institutions, Values, Morals.... all those (mostly) are chains, they tie you down, and bar you from the truth. So they look to undermine all those axiomatic forces, that are almost reflexive and subconscious in us. Vampires are closer than most to be free, but they still clang to these icons, powers... paradoxically they worship Set because he makes itself an antithesis to these things, because he is the God that was banished, the enemy God, the anti-God, the anti-Establishment God... etc.
                                            Of course, through their many cults, cultures, groups and philosophies they either use these controlling tools to liberate, to control, to addict or to worship their Ideas. Many may just look to benefit themselves with no grand esoteric and spiritual goals. Others might fall for these tools themselves. If not for the Ministry and the Lore of the Clans, I would never see much depth in the Setites.

                                            Strange... When coincidence seems too convenient, I prefer to call it fate.
