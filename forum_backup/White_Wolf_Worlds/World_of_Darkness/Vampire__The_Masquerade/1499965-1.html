            Off the top of my head;
            • Neutralize Celerity
            • Counter Dominate (if somebody under the control of dominate is possessed by a wraith then the domitor and the wraith have a battle of wills for control of the person)
            • Steal your blood pool.
            • Make blood rot before you can eat it, effectively starving you.
            • Send you into frenzy without warning. If you try to ride the wave, they can drag you out of it.
            • Mess up your plans by possessing your thralls. Or murdering them. Or driving them insane.
            • Edit and erase memories. Dominate is all fun and games until somebody starts using effects like that on you and your toys.
            • Possess you. (Don't remember exactly if Wr20 addressed skinriding and torpor, but V5 has the possibility of wraiths possessing vampires while they daysleep and almost walking them into the sunlight.)
            • Set fire to your haven, your domain, your businesses, your car, your hair and clothes, etc
            • Give you horrific nightmares that make daysleep undesirable.
            • Pick up an object with which to decapitate you, stake you, etc
            • Drop kick you straight into Oblivion (Final Death, no coming back. Bye.)
            • Read your entire browser history and message archives (and so on). Eavesdrop on your phone calls. Possibly send them to somebody you really don't want to know about the contents.
            • Did you have important data that was crucial to your plans or would lead to terrible consequences if you acted upon this data not knowing certain details or believing lies? Well now it's been edited, or deleted to make you do so.
            • I'm pretty sure making doors and entrances disappear is a thing.
            • Spacial loops. Your own haven can become a maze, trapping you inside and unable to get out.


            Prone to hyperbole

                Patterns in storytelling is an occupational hazard if you go by The Seven Basic Plots. All you can really do is try to disguise which ever plot you are using.

                As far as the mechanics interactions go... part of the problem is that many mechanics are made for balance rather than storytelling. For example, let's say a Necromancer wanted to make a ring they always wore a fetish for their most powerful ghost minions to allow for easy contact and access. The mechanics say each ghost costs one permanent willpower to bind. This sounds fine until you realize that if you have anything but low-level willpower, this gets prohibitively expensive in terms of XP costs. So, the only Necromancers who can regularly make fetishes are those with the weakest wills regardless of their necromantic proficiency. Even if you rule that each ghost needs their own fetish rather than a shared one, the cost ends up being the same.

                So being overly attached to exact mechanics interactions are not always beneficial to the storytelling process.

                        Wraiths can be very potent across the Shroud since it is their home turf. More than just being individually powerful, it's their sheer numbers and organization of groups like the Hierarchy that have enormous resources. A vampire crossing the Shroud and entering the Shadowlands in most Necropoli better be discrete and on good behavior because the ST can throw a lot at them.

                        Wraiths are much more constrained in the Skinlands because either their powers don't work, require an area where the Shroud is thin, or face other limitations. That doesn't mean they can't harass or even threaten others, just that it is rare. You can build a very strong character who can do a lot across the Shroud and load them with relics and artifacts, but those should be very rare and exceptional characters.

                        If you are looking for a straight character versus character fight (wraith versus any non-wraith like a vampire, werewolf, etc.), I think that would be a mistake. It is not a good emulation of ghost stories which is what most players would want. If I wanted to run one or more scenes showing a terrifying encounter with Wraiths, I'd look to the best kinds of ghost stories that deal with people feeling physically threatened by ghosts - Blatty's The Exorcist, Matheson's Hell House, the Poltergeist movie, or the ghost stories of M.R. James - and try to emulate one of them.

                        The initial tendency of many STs is to want to do one-on-one fights between splats, but in practice almost the best thing for any splat to do is avoid a one-on-one fight and utilize the natural advantages they have against the other splay and wage asymmetric warfare. If a wraith needs to fight vampires, he doesn't haunt them directly. He possesses someone in the Skinlands during the day and helps hunters locate the vampire's haven. He haunts the people in someone's Herds so they flee or commit suicide. He causes a car accident that kills someone's ghoul. He lures them into a haunted house at night where they don't encounter that wraith, but an entire circle of Renegades or Spectres angry at being disturbed. Torment them with sounds and fleeting sights at odd times and places in the night so they're never quite sure what is going on, and that may cause them to make a crucial mistake like run into a Sabbat pack, stay out too long and not able to safely hide before the sun comes up, or wander into a rival's domain.

                        My general advice to most STs who want to include wraiths in their games, but don't have the material or time to master an entirely different gameline is just do their best to emulate the popular ghost stories they know.

                                    Originally posted by Herr Meister View Post
                                    How can I make a Wraith threatening to the players so that they learn to stop thinking they can just "summon and compel any Wraith to do as they please at any time" (in case they're Giovanni, Cappadocian, etc.) or just outright ignore them?
                                    Originally posted by Herr Meister View Post
                                    And yes, the way RPGs work, lead us to straight one-on-one fights, especially because many players like that, in the way that familiarity brings some comfort, but especially in this case scenario, I agree that it's by far not the best approach.
                                    If you are looking for individual scenes that can get across to certain PCs that their powers do not make them immune, here are some examples of the kind of scenes I'd do.

                                    1) Wraiths can invade your dreams and manipulate them (the Phantasm Arcanos). I would not go full Freddy Krueger, but I would use that as opportunity for wraiths to communicate to a vampire (who is slumbering in the daytime) in a safe environment for all sides. If you want to impress upon the vampire their power, you could have the Hierarchy hold a mock trial of the vampire for their crimes against wraiths. No powers are on either side can be used against each other, but you could show a lot of wraiths in a Star Chamber style court, wraiths in the jury, wraiths as bailiffs, etc. to showcase their numbers and resources. End the scene in a way that clearly (and hopefully ominously) warns the PC.

                                    2) You can start the scene whenever the PC is driving somewhere. Describe that as they look in their rear view mirror they see someone smiling at them in their backseat. There is no one in the back seat of course, the mirror is simply showing the wraith (through the Embody Arcanos). If they turn their head and look back, they see nothing. But regardless their car suddenly jerks and it crashes (through either the Inhabit or Outrage Arcanoi).

                                    3) When the vampire awakes, he finds out his haven is destroyed by a fire. Maybe he is in the basement and survived the flames. Maybe a retainer managed to move his resting spot to a new haven which he wakes up in. Maybe he awakens as the flames are erupting at sunset. But whether it is through some kind of spontaneous combustion or faulty wiring (through either the Inhabit or Outrage Arcanoi) a fire started, and the PC is lucky to have survived.

                                    4) The vampire PC loses a prized retainer, ally, contact, etc. because a wraith possessed her and caused her death (the Puppetry Arcanos). Perhaps with a slow build up beforehand of the wraith making the vessel their consort.


                                    5) When the vampire PC is listening to the radio at home or in a car, the wraith directly or indirectly threatens the PC. Maybe the NPR announcer's voice suddenly begins describing all the people the vampire controls through their influence and if they aren't smart they will lose all of them; or a commercial begins describing the estate sale of all the PCs property and possessions because they're recently deceased, or even an outright threat (Inhabit).

                                    6) The wraith uses Pandemonium to perform subtle illusions to make the PC think they are being followed, or that another rival is observing them, etc.

                                    7) And of course the blatant display of poltergeist phenomenon is an obvious way to grab their attention. Maybe not as dangerous to a vampire than to a mortal, but it should force them to take the wraith seriously.


                                    Obviously certain defenses or wards used by the vampire could make one or more of these moot. But not everything can be warded or protected. Instead of a home or car radio, it is done while they are in an electronics store and the TVs in the display area starts being manipulated. Maybe it is not their prime haven that burns down, but a secondary haven. And so on.

                                    The issue is not to kill the PC, it is to teach him the lesson that wraiths can retaliate, and hopefully it will result in the PC wanting to make a deal that limits their actions to a reasonable course. And not all wraithly powers need to be exercise by one wraith. The wraith's friends, associates, or the political authorities can lend a hand.

                                    The important thing with any ghost story is atmosphere and mystery. You want to communicate with the player that they are dealing with the unknown, and let their own imagination conjure their fears of the unknown.
