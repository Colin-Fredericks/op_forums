    Now I’m not great expert on DARK SHADOWS in any incarnation, but the notion of a ‘World of Darkness Barnabas Collins’ fascinated me, so here’s what I’ve come up with for the concept so far:-


    - As mentioned above, I tend to assume that he is of the same generation as Georgia Rowlands and quite probably embraced at roughly the same time (Intended to serve as a ‘holdout’ in a New England where Ventrue were at the nadir of their fortunes), though their sire may have taken precautions to ensure that these holdouts were only vaguely aware of each other, as a security precaution against their getting together only to be mobbed.


    - The name ‘Bartholomew Stephens of Kingsport’ occurred to me as a possibility: I thought this had the right element of New England Gothic and one liked the idea of making the name an homage to both Barnabas Collins & Mr Stephen King.


    - I’m assuming that a key reason Bartholomew Stephens has remained largely under the radar in terms of vampire
    politics is that he’s situated on the fringes of a no-man’s land where Lupines and the Sabbat keep (or kept, depending on the edition) the Camarilla at arm’s length and are too busy hammering at each other to worry about a single, fairly-discrete vampire.

    Alternatively, it’s possible he’s worked out some kind of arrangement with non-Kindred forces and leverages that to keep Kingsport (I’m not sure about the name, but I needed a working title) mostly to himself, so far as vampires go.


    - One further assumes that, unlike Barnabas Collins, Mr Stephens is NOT a time-traveller and has been active enough to live through various historical periods as they happened; this is mostly a product of my awareness that Barnabas Collins’ long period of imprisonment would be difficult to replicate without making his Humanity frightfully low.


    - In terms Humanity, I would tend to put the character somewhere around ‘6’: not quite human, but more than human enough to feel more than the Thirst for his family and various other associates (Though of course not all those feelings are positive and a universal good).


    - It has occurred to me that showing him with one or two childer/body doubles might allow one to homage the fact that DARK SHADOWS has gone through more than one incarnation (With each of these individuals freely using … and sometimes misusing ... the name ‘Bartholomew Stephens’ to keep opponents & rivals guessing).


    - One should also admit that my notion for his feeding restriction was ‘French-speakers’ (As a nod to the character’s uncomfortable relationship with Angelique and Maine’s significant population of French-speaking individuals), but this
    might be a trifle too specific.

    My pet theory is that a Ventrue’s feeding restriction should say something - usually something uncomfortable - about the deeper reaches of their psychology: the best possible choice should leave those who uncover it less than comfortable with the revelation, ESPECIALLY when they know the character fairly well.


    - Finally, one gets the mental image of his experience with non-Sabbat vampires being mostly limited to local Gangrel, since they offer a fascinating contrast with the very Old World Mr Stephens.


    That’s all I have for now and hopefully it isn’t too feeble a skeleton on which someone else might further flesh out these bare bones (I’m not sufficiently familiar with the franchise to make more detailed suggestions).

        I'd have to give a lot more thought to the best way to utilize a Barnabas Collins type character so that he would be useful in play - someone the PCs could conceivably meet, and more importantly would want to interact with. Otherwise he's just useless as an RPG character. But this is what I currently think.

        Barnabas (or Batholomew or however he is named) lives in a coastal town of around 10,000 people. So there's not much room for any vampire other than him. His feeding has to be very non-invasive for him to feed without discovery. If he ever has childer, the childe would probably have to leave and dwell somewhere else. However, since Barnabas is a key figure of sympathetic vampires, I'd probably have him not sire any childer at all.

        However, he might indeed employ one or more "body doubles" as younger men who pose as him or one of his sons in order to explain his eventual "replacement". Rather than remain in town, they'd travel throughout Maine on business - dealing with property or other investments. Not sure where he finds them. He may just ask some legal firm to employ such subterfuge, but giving them permission, power of attorney, etc. to conduct business on his behalf. Maybe he's thought to be agoraphobic or something, unwilling to live his ancestral home. There might even be a belief that there is something "wrong" with the house that causes all the men who inherit the place to succumb to the same seclusion. It may even be thought haunted (and maybe it is).

        The small town he lives in would be considered his exclusive domain, but he wouldn't be called Prince because that's just silly when you're alone. But he might have the same Status as Elsa Linden though.

        He'd probably live in the family estate in town, constantly reinventing himself as yet another generation when his current mortal identity gets too old. He's known to be a recluse. His mortal descendants would live elsewhere - perhaps not even in the same town. Though he'd host regular family reunions of some kind, and every generation he'd invite a family (or at least some "nephews" and "nieces" or distant cousins) of his descendants to move in and live in with him. He might blood bond some, but never ghoul them. Most likely he'd just use Dominate and Presence to maintain control. He would have one or two retainers, but they'd likely be people who feel loyalty to him personally rather than be subjugated by him. Someone whom would keep a secret because he or she owed him a big favor. But perhaps they might also be some especially beloved family members he offered to ghoul in order to keep them alive.

        I agree that he might host the occasional local Gangrel, and he might be seen as a friend of the clan, willing to let them stay for a night and share stories. It might be a good spot for non-political Camarilla vampires to meet away from all the intrigue of the Triad and its opponents. In any case, that friendship would be needed anytime a random Sabbat pack might enter the town.

        I have no preference for the Ventrue feeding restriction, but I do think an exclusion based on French speakers could work. Our fictional town might have a significant minority of people who speak French at home, perhaps it even has the last French language newspaper in New England, though perhaps only published monthly or quarterly and being only a few pages or perhaps being online only now. Perhaps the town is known for its preservation efforts for the French language with French taught even in elementary school or a small parochial Catholic school that has French immersion (the schools may get a small number of boarding students whose parents send their kids here precisely in order to become fluent). All, of course, supported in some fashion by Barnabas. Maybe 10% of the population or so speaks French at home.

        I'm not sure what a good hook might be to make Barnabas or the city something that PCs might want to visit at some point. Does Barnabas have some kind of important or secret knowledge that would cause people to seek him out? Does the town have some resource - a rare bookstore, a private library, a place of occult power, a haunted house - that would cause people to seek it out and thus gain permission from its "Prince" to be there? Is Barnabas some kind of important, but hidden, business man or local political influence that his name keeps popping up as a person of interest that could cause one or more PCs to investigate and prompt them to find out more or get his support? Maybe he happens to be the eldest Ventrue in New England, and therefore even though he eschews politics and involvement in Kindred affairs, the Ventrue clan sometimes calls on him to mediate disputes between the Ventrue in the region. Something else? Likely it would have to be some combination to provide enough hooks that at least one of them could attract a PC to want to interact with him.

            Black Fox , thank you for taking the time to put together an excellent reply! In return for your own thoughts:-

            - Given the close ties between the character and his family (Not to mention DARK SHADOWS’ habit of recasting members of the ensemble as members of the same family) it’s quite possible these ‘Body Doubles’ are male relations whom he’s he’s particularly close to and to whom he bears a significant resemblance (His close relationship with his kin may well be a case of “Doing well by doing good” as this proximity allows him to masquerade as younger members of the family, at least for a time).


            - The title ‘Squire’ (In the sense of a country gentleman of influence and significant estate, but no claim to hereditary title rather than what one wit dubbed a ‘Murder Caddy’) occurs to me as a plausible alternative to ‘Prince’ or ‘Baron’ though ‘Sheriff’ might be more Yankee.

            If this title were bestowed, it would probably be through the Good offices of Roman Pendragon*, given he seems to be the farthest North of all the Camarilla Princes (Though it’s
            possible this could be an effort to set up an exclave by one of his opponents in the Ivory Tower) and would logically welcome a chance to establish a base (or at least cultivate an ally) in the region, possibly as an olive branch to Clan Ventrue.


            *Incidentally, I assume that ‘Roman Pendragon’ as a whole is a nom de plume, ‘Roman’ presumably being adopted as a way of asserting that Clan Ventrue do not have exclusive title to the Legacy of Rome (Incidentally, it’s interesting to note that Roman Pendragon’s effort to break the Ventrue stronghold on the British Isles seems to have occurred during a period when Mithras was absent and his seneschal Valerius was acting Prince of London: in fact the period between 1848 and 1885 CE sees Valerius open London up to the Camarilla - in the wake of a Sabbat attack - triggering something of a land rush, which may have provided the key opportunity for Pendragon to make his move).


            - In terms of plot hooks that would persuade PCs to enter ‘Bartholomew Sharp’s house as a guest (and possibly even return), there’s always the chance of a family connection (A PC could be related to the old man, either directly or through a relationship with some family member); they might, as you noted, be looking for the ‘Last Homely House’ in an area with very few options for vampires (In the same way that Beorn from THE HOBBIT is almost the only option for shelter in a howling wilderness, but not the easiest host to get on with); they could very well be seeking to learn how one survives and prospers as an autarkis; given the sheer wealth of weirdness in DARK SHADOWS, they might be looking for advice on how to deal with other supernatural beings when running for cover is not an option; and I believe that there’s at least one plotline where the House of Collins is sitting on buried treasure, so there’s always that to catch Our Heroes’ interest.

            If nothing else, ‘Kingsport’ might be an excellent site for a ‘Breather Episode’ where the PCs can take a dip in a little soap opera, rather than get chewed up by Vampire Power Polltics.

                By the way, I’ve been working to try and sketch out some characters based on the various concepts you posted a few pages back, as well as one or two characters mentioned in this thread but with no article on the White Wolf wiki: amongst the latter was the ‘Osric’ (Clan Nosferatu, of the 8th Generation) mentioned as having sired Prudence Stone.

                - Per your suggestion that he would have been sired at some point between the arrival of the Saxons in England and the Norman Conquest, I imagine that he was Embraced when the Great Heathen Army ravaged the English-speaking kingdoms my idea was that the Fenrir were hip-deep in the whole business, vampires got nervous and embraced childer (Whether as reinforcements, meat shields or an insurance policy against their bloodline dying out I leave to your imagination).


                - I like the idea of his having been long associated with the port of Dunwich, for reasons that should be obvious to any nerd with a fondness for Weird Tales from New England (Though in a sourcebook one might disguise this Lovecraft reference by running with the theory that Dunwich was the former capital of East Anglia known as ‘Dommoc’ making the character ‘Osric of Dommoc’ during his breathing days).

                In any case, the history of Dunwich as a significant port on England’s east coast that was almost literally chewed away by the sea until it was a mere village is a splendid element to include as background for a character straight out of a horror
                movie (and the loss of his home base forcing Osric under the praxis of another Cainite would provide an excellent stressor pushing him towards the New World, once the opportunity presented itself).


                - Assuming that Osric was embraced during a Werewolf Panic, it’s possible he may have had a sideline as a ‘Lupine Expert’ amongst his Clan, which would provide another strong incentive to move into the New World (Possibly at about the same time King Aaron Everstone was making England a very dangerous place for things he didn’t like, while the loss of the Croatan left a serious gap in the defences of the Eastern Seaboard).

                It’s quite possible Osric might have been amongst the first of his clan in the area for the simple reason that he not only had experience of surviving in Werewolf country, but as a Nosferatu could move around this sort of region without attracting any dangerous degree of attention.


                - Going by the story of Prudence Stone’s origin, he
                might simply have been a reasonably-high Humanity Kindred who enjoyed the idea of getting out from under the Jyhad; alternatively he might have taken a keen interest in Mistress Stone’s foresight because he was keeping a weather eye on the Tremere and eager to acquire assets that would counter their occult supremacy.

                Certainly one supposes he was in the Americas (and possibly to stay) by the later 1600s (If memory serves, the Witch Trils tended to occur in the latter part of the 17th century).


                - It’s also possible that Osric was a Nosferatu of the old school, who favoured lairing in the wilder regions of the world (Caves, rather than tombs or ancient ruins) which would definitely have boosted his chances of survival in New England (Especially if he preferred to feed on beasts more often than humans, which was likely an increasingly-unfashionable choice as population numbers continued to climb).


                - I’m assuming that he and his descendants would be a little to one side of Warwick’s net: not necessarily hostile, but independent and quite capable of making life difficult for the Nosferatu leg of the Triad if they tried to make life difficult for their clanmates (Not least because they were rather older in New England).

                It’s possible they were able to do business with Clan Ventrue when the latter had the catbird seat in this area and have quietly continued to keep up relatively positive relations (Possibly complicated by local Ventrue’s ties to the Tremere).

                You’ve noted that Prudence Stone could act as a sort of Pythia for Roman Pendragon: I wonder if she does so as part of a quid pro quo that allows her bloodline to mostly go it’s own way independent of Warwick’s network?


                Anyway, these are the notions that have occurred to me for old Osric: I’ll do my best to post a character concept for one of his possible descendants tomorrow, which I’ll preview and sum up with two words.

                ‘Innsmouth Look’.

                                    Black Fox, thank you for your kind replies and for taking the time to think over my notions - I enjoy embroidering on the ideas of others and love to develop a setting, so it’s a positive delight to know that you enjoy some of my concepts.


                                    That mention of the Innsmouth Look is, of course, a segue leading into another character for New England’s Nosferatu, since the Clan’s particular curse is the perfect excuse to imagine an extended family of New England vampires disfigured in a way that suggests that, while every single human being has ancestors who emerged from the oceans, sea life made an appearance somewhat closer to the present day in the family tree of these particular specimens.

                                    - I’m assuming this lineage started with a vampire whom I shall refer to as PEAQUOD, in life a seagoing man who shipped aboard the New England whaling fleet in the early decades of the Nineteenth Century (He has, on occasion, noted that he served with Herman Melville, but does not boast the connection and has never specified his exact position*) until an encounter with the Supernatural somewhere in the Pacific resulted in his being discharged.

                                    Making his way home at length, he seems to have arrived in the United States with a fund of strange stories and strange knowledge (Of the sea, amongst other things) that persuaded his sire to consider him for the embrace (Not least because his knowledge of the sea and his own seamanship was expected to prove useful): some claim he might even have brought his sire into New England in the first place (though variations on the same rumour claim he only returned a native son or daughter to their old familiar home).

                                    *Rumours that he might have been the Edward C. Starbuck discharged under what one source describes as ‘mysterious circumstances’ on Tahiti have never been confirmed or denied by Peaquod himself, a man of discretion amounting to secretiveness.


                                    - Who that sure ought to be I’m not entirely sure: my first inclination was towards Osric, but it struck me that making Patience Stone ‘Peaquod’s’ sire would not only be a delightfully New England Gothic element (A ‘witch’ from the old days creating another Creature of the Night), but would also allow Goody Stone to keep in touch with the regional HQ of her Clan (With her childe acting as go-between and, if necessary, an escape route).

                                    If I remember correctly, this would make her childer of the 10th Generation.


                                    - I’m assuming that Vampirism put something of a crimp in Peaquod’s seafaring (Especially during the days when a crewman had to be available at all hours, rain or shine, on an open deck), since one assumes he would be loathe to feed on his shipmates and even more reluctant to ship out as cargo, rather than crew.

                                    One imagines that he would become more of a shadowy influence on the shipping industry, as owner of various vessels (Whaling - until the industry collapsed - fishing and merchant shipping), while running his own small boat up and down the coast as circumstances and the requirements of his Clan demanded.

                                    Presumably he embraced Childer to act as local agents in various New England ports (So that he and they could work a rotation throughout the region, making sure that there was at least one messenger/escape route in each major port - and, in Special Emergencies, to put together a wholly-vampire crew), who might well have embraced childer of their own, spreading their subtly-fishy look throughout the region to the point where even HP Lovecraft noticed (Though he never got a close look at them and, so far as we know, if there was ever a cult of Deep Ones, it was not comprised of Nosferatu).

                                    *I would, in fact, suggest that Lovecraft combined rumours of the Setite colony at Cape Cod with the ‘Innsmouth Look’ of Peaquod and his descendants, all embellished with his own vivid imagination.


                                    - I tend to imagine that Peaquod’s job is to keep up Nosferatu sea-power (Not to mention keep the various New England Nosferatu in touch with developments on the docks), but that he’s also willing to hire out to non-Nosferatu Kindred should they require the use of a small boat.

                                    Also that this latter service as Ferryman is partly based on his strong desire to keep abreast of the grisly & macabre: one assumes that Peaquod, with all the superstitions of a seagoing man reinforced by centuries of life as a Vampire and many another experience of Supernatural Weirdness, would act as something of a Miner’s Canary for coteries delving into the more obscure mysteries and unexplained phenomena along the coastline - when he starts giving exposition on the haunted past of a location it’s wise to listen and when he decides that things are getting Dangerously Weird you would be very wise to join him as he makes an escape.

                                    Assuming, of course, that you reach the boat before it disappears over the horizon: Peaquod’s fund of knowledge is extensive, but it’s accuracy has been known to vary - because when you’re the only survivor of an expedition, having taken great care to be left guarding the boat, you’re not going to know all that occurred.


                                    - Oh, and one imagines the character to be played by Mr Robert Shaw, circa JAWS; it might therefore be amusing to suggest that his unhappy experience with the supernatural in the Pacific Ocean might somehow have been connected with the Rokea.

                                                        Here are some of the ‘rough sketches’ for those archetypes suggested on a previous page, basically the ‘Character Concept’ name that occurred to me and a possible Clan - though in some cases only a name & concept occurred to me.

                                                        One concept not mentioned in the list of archetypes which struck me as very New England was ‘Puritan Runaway’ - that is, somebody who wearied of the New England quasi-theocracy and decided to head into the wilderness.

                                                        It’s quite easy to imagine this character being snapped up by the Sabbat, assuming the Gangrel don’t take an interest in them.


                                                        ‘A veteran of the French & Indian or American Revolutionary War’

                                                        ROYAL AMERICAN (Ventrue) A veteran Loyalist who may well have served in both conflicts, but like Robert Rogers wound up on the wrong side of history (Bonus points if he bears a resemblance to Mr John Carradine in DRUMS ALONG THE MOHAWK).

                                                        FRONTIER WOLF (Gangrel) A scout during the French & Indian War who may well have been making war along what is now the New England/Canada border ever since, but is more likely to favour the Camarilla over the Sabbat - because while they don’t want company often, when they do want it they prefer civilised company.

                                                        REDCOAT REVOLUTIONARY (Brujah) One of the many deserters from His Majesty’s forces who was persuaded to enter the American service: unlike most of the others, he’s been a Rebel with a variety of causes ever since.


                                                        ‘A Hessian Mercenary’

                                                        AMERIKA-JÄGER (Tremere) I believe that the ‘Hessians’ entered British service only after His Majesty’s forces were ousted from Boston, so I wonder if this military contractor may have seen service elsewhere in the Thirteen Colonies, but been ‘acquired’ after some disaster (Like Saratoga) left them at a loose end.

                                                        Incidentally, one thought ‘Tremere’ because the Warlocks do need soldiers and may well have appreciated a professional without local ties to compromise their loyalty to the Clan.

                                                        My mental image of the character might have returned to Germany at some point, only to be ousted by the Prince of Berlin when that horrible man started his purges (and having returned to New England because he had some experience of the local personalities).


                                                        ‘New England lawyer-politician’

                                                        BY THE BOOK ANARCH (Brujah) A scholarly member of a frequently-roughneck Clan who made his bones pleading down the penalties inflicted upon their Clan mates by local princes and seeking to improve the excessively Feudal status quo prevailing in Camarilla territories.

                                                        Might be an especially interesting twist on the archetype if this is a Political WIFE who found the Embrace offered her opportunities not open to her in life.


                                                        JUDGE OF THE UNSEEN (Tremere) An educated gentleman who tried to have evidence acquired by ‘Supernatural’ sources dismissed from consideration during the Witch Trials (or at least seriously reconsidered), having done sufficient due diligence to attract the interest of Clan Tremere.

                                                        Had been doing his best to avert future Witch Hunts while ferreting out Malevolent Sorcerers ever since.


                                                        ‘A Trancendentalist philosopher/author’

                                                        EVERYDAY MIRACLE (Malkavian) My brief research into this particular school of thought taught me enough to recognise a relatively optimistic view of the world somewhat difficult to sustain amidst a society as inherently Damnable as that of the Kindred.

                                                        On the other hand, given the philosophies’ deep attachment to the idea of divinity pervading all nature and humanity, a Malkavian character deeply attached to this philosophy would be a potentially interesting case study in a person adhering to their beliefs even under the most stringent tests … especially if they believe that God literally speaks to them and what they hear worries them!


                                                        ‘A Boston Abolitionist’

                                                        BOSTON BEGUM (Ventrue) Since we have no fewer than two black, Brujah conductors on the Underground Railroad, it stuck me that making this character a Boston lady (The other pillar of the Abolitionists, back in the day) might be interesting.

                                                        It also struck me that, since one assumes that the Slave South could well have been a stronghold for Ventrue antitribu of the most feudal sort, showing an ‘Old Money’ Ventrue seek to ‘do well by doing good’ in the interests of tearing down the semi-feudal order of the Old South (In the interests of creating a more modern United States) might offer an interesting angle on the Abolitionist movement.

                                                        Incidentally, the name is a nod to the ‘Boston Brahmin’ (With a ‘Begum’ being an Indian rank for a Royal lady).


                                                        ‘Someone Involved with the Hartford Convention’

                                                        GREEN MOUNTAIN REBEL (Brujah) Someone who served the Vermont Republic before it was officially part of the United States and was perfectly willing to see it become part of British North America if the Pennsylvania crowd didn’t see sense: the perceived follies of ‘Mr Madison’s War’ saw them revive the idea of New England going it’s own way, but once again forestalled by events as they developed.

                                                        Doubled down on All-American Unity to compensate, then found their career stalled out in the long-term because this drove them collaborate enthusiastically with the Slave South (At least to the extent of backing some unseemly concessions), with rumours of their being an outright Copperhead during the War Between the States being kept current by rivals.


                                                        ‘Someone involved with the New England shipping industry’

                                                        YOUNG IRONSIDES (Brujah) Claims to have served on ‘Old Ironsides’ itself and verifiably a naval officer in the War of 1812 - has been Roman Pendragon’s amphibious operations expert and one of his lieutenants since being convinced that the man wasn’t so British as to compromise the interests of the United States.

                                                        His coterie-patrol are affectionately known as the ‘Sea Dogs’.


                                                        ‘Boston Irish’

                                                        TEAGUE (Brujah) An Irishman of Protestant ancestry who played some role in the protests culminating in the ‘King Street Incident’ (and who STILL resents Lawyer John Adams’ rather rude description of the protestors during his arguments in defence of the British regulars involved.

                                                        Sometimes claims to have been an indentured servant auctioned off to the highest bidder, but usually only as a way of downplaying their acute disinterest of being roped into the Underground Railroad while Irishmen were being attacked and mistreated in the streets of Boston.

                                                        Has been a stalwart (or depending on your perspective part of the scenery) on the Boston docks for a very long time.


                                                        PURITAN WHISPERER (Nosferatu) An Irish American who, having arrived in Boston as a very young child in the 1820s, was well-placed to serve his community and advocate for the tidal wave of new Irish who arrived as a result of the potato famine.

                                                        A good Catholic lad and contributor to the Parish newsletter, he was lucky enough to stumble onto vampires feeding off the sickly & vulnerable in the quarantine hospitals on Deer Island only after he’d made himself useful by making life more difficult for Caiphas Smith - the latter being almost as eager to pillory Catholics as he was to prosecute his war on the Undead - resulting in his becoming a deeply unhappy Kindred.

                                                        On the other hand, his transformation gave him plenty of time to polish his writing, make life a little easier for his old community AND put his pre- and post-mortem knowledge of Old Smith to good use.


                                                        WILD COLONIAL (Brujah) Smiling Jack, being a vampire powerfully against all manner of authority, has been known to signal his defiance of a local Prince by embracing childer without asking consent.

                                                        Being a git, Smiling Jack frequently leaves these childer to face the consequences of his actions: luckily for this particular creation, Roman Pendragon needed recruits and was willing to accept this roaring puppy as one of his Hounds.

                                                        This Wild Colonial Boy has been dividing his time between wrecking the Sabbat and running amuck on Rest & Recuperation ever since … and him a former Altar Boy too.


                                                        ‘Someone representing the financial & insurance industry’

                                                        INSURANCE DOUBLE AGENT (Giovanni/Milner/Hecata) Given the role of the Milner-Giovanni as ‘bankroll’ for the Triat in preference for the still-somewhat hostile Ventrue of New England and given the intelligence gathering opportunities offerred to insurance agencies in a time & place where various damages need to be collected and/or hushed up, it seemed credible to imagine a Milner quietly making themselves useful by keeping an eye on the Insurance Business.

                                                        Bonus points if they DO NOT run the occasional ‘Insurance Job’ for the extra dollars.


                                                        ‘Someone representing Old Industries’

                                                        STAKEHOLDER (Toreador) That name is a pun on the ease with which somebody associated with the furniture business can lay their hands on the traditional instrument for dispatching a vampire and I will be making NO APOLOGIES.

                                                        Now please excuse me while I twirl my moustache and point out that there’s something inherently interesting in a Kindred who has had an eye for woodwork since the items in question were the Next Big Thing and hasn’t given up that interest now that they’re antiques.
