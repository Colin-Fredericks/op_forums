    Why don't more vampires follow the path of harmony instead of humanity?

    02-21-2022, 10:12 PM
    Now I know what some of you are going to say, "VTM is about struggling with keeping your humanity!". And I agree with that statement, its just that if you stop thinking of vtm as a game and as a real world, your opinion would change. Humanity is perhaps one of the most limiting morality system within the the vtm world. I say this because as a vampire you are going to have to break a lot of its rules nightly. There was someone on reddit who posted why humanity was such a terrible path to follow and explained it great. This was kind of inspired by them.

    Feel free to read their post here= https://www.reddit.com/r/vtm/comment...r_less_all_it/

    (You may have to copy and paste it in another tab for it to open)

    I don't think I really need to explain anything about the path of harmony, since the wiki already explains the gist of it. The link of which I will post below.

    https://whitewolf.fandom.com/wiki/Humanity_(VTM)

    My question to all of you is why don't more vampires follow the path of Harmony?

      The Path of Harmony is actually really hard to follow. It's almost a monk's path. You can't act bestial towards humans, but you can't get too close to them either. Which, in practice, leads to this very cold ascetic existence, where you're generally very polite to humans and don't disrupt their lives, but periodically end them for the sake of food and secrecy. It's a difficult and lonely path to follow.

              It really isn't, especially when you consider what simply having Instinct instead of Self-Control means for your character.

              People complain about the Paths being "the Path of what I was going to do anyway," but that can be Humanity too. Humanity only seems limiting because it's the baseline.

              I say this because as a vampire you are going to have to break a lot of its rules nightly.
              Such as?

              There was someone on reddit who posted why humanity was such a terrible path to follow and explained it great. This was kind of inspired by them.
              The poster there is treating the mathematical abstractions of the game as the game's "physics" rather than mechanics for highlighting specific moments in a vampire's life. A vampire has zero chance of botching or Frenzying feeding 99.9999999999% of the time because you don't roll for all those times. The rules themselves say that. The whole attempt to "prove Humanity bad" with math doesn't follow the rules of the game, so it's inherently faulty.

              There's also a huge amount of "bad STs can screw you if you're on Humanity," without any recognition that can be done with any Path.

              Just look at Harmony. Vandalism is a level 1 sin for Harmony. Surprising someone such that they drop and break something value ten times could turn you into a Wight. The idea that Humanity is uniquely fragile doesn't hold up.

              My question to all of you is why don't more vampires follow the path of Harmony?
              1) Harmony is a shitty Path for most people.
              2) You start on Humanity and changing to a different Path is difficult. Lots of vampires lose themselves to the Beast trying.
              3) Instinct is a horrible Virtue for most vampires.
              etc.

                              It was a poorly thought out Path. But second edition at least had the excuse it was infodumping a lot of stuff and couldn't afford to detail everything as much as we'd like. Based on the information provided in the original Sabbat Guides, I thought there was a significant faction of Sabbat that were vampires of early Native Americans embraced by Country Gangrel early in colonial history. They reconciled their beliefs they had when they were mortals with their new existence. This is based on stuff written for the Path of Harmony, Gangrel anti-tribu, mentiosn of the Penumbra of Gray Squirrels, and a few other places. They represented a distinct faction of the Sabbat, primarily nomad packs (or at the very least not urban based) that were in the Sabbat as default simply because that is how their sires were. But they weren't very interested in the sect. Likely aligned with the Loyalist movement as that did not impose any restrictions to them.

                              Originally posted by Reasor View Post
                              This is a prime example of the kind of nonsense Second Edition is infamous for..
                              The Path itself was poorly written, but so were all the other pacts. It needed to be updated as well. Second Edition could be very gonzo at times, and that's not to everyone's tastes. (It's not mine either.) But it provided a lot of tools for STs to do what they wanted in the setting which was a good thing. It was just up to STs to prune away the branches they didn't want and make adjustments.

                              Originally posted by Reasor View Post
                              Nothing of value was lost when the Sabbat canonically purged its followers.
                              I disagree, but understand your point of view. If all it is was a path for white guys who watched Dances with Wolves, it was a shitty aspect and nothing was missed by its absence. But I feel the Path of Harmony had two things that made it interesting. First that it was primarily made up of vampires who were Native Americans and represented a unique take on people adapting to their vampire status. That alone made it an interesting faction within the Sabbat. The second was that as one of the more humane Paths, it was an interesting "heresy" within the Sabbat that showed the sect was more than just doing evil (which was important as well).

                              Neither aspect was important to the metaplot railroad that said the only narrative use an ST could have for the Sabbat was as a paramilitary sect to kill Camarilla vampires and make the game solely about sect wars right before Gehenna. I think that metaplot was a huge mistake because STs could have lots of other uses of the Sabbat in their chronicles, and taking away those tools to do something different disempowered STs. It said that what they were doing was wrong.

                                          The in game, because people already talked the raw and meta reasons, reason basically boils down to one fact: you start human. Only humans can easily become vampires, which means humanity is the natural path to start with. In the modern night, almost no path exists outside the independents or Sabbat, so humanity is all you got.

                                          Btw, paths are corruptions of the old roads. Old roads could be as similar, or completely alien, to humanity as they get. This ironically is best represented in the ability to appear alive, something the modern paths lack. During the formation of the Camarilla and Sabbat, the Sabbat began to take the old roads and twist their philosophies and ideals to fit with their political agenda. This is why in every edition of vtm, paths are a Sabbat thing. This made to where only paths couldn't pass for the living with ease, and so the Camarilla began to discourage Paths completely, or more actually prevent the newly embraced from hear about paths at all.
