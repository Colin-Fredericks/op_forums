                                        This topic prompted me to review some of my old notes on the Free States and my old, second hand copy of LAbN. I wanted to see what I had written in terms of Anarch factions and Baronies that would be in my chronicle's version of the Free States. So I reread the description of Jeremy MacNeil in LAbN and something popped out that I had not considered before.

                                        We're told that Jeremy was born in 1631 and his clan fought on behalf of Charles I. He was embraced in 1657. This means as a young mortal that Jeremy would have fought in the English Civil Wars. His enemy would not have been the king (who upheld the idea of the divine rights of kings and probably the idea of absolute monarchy). His enemy would have been Parliament. This is not what we would normally expect from someone who became an "Anarch". The most likely image of a tyrant then, in Jeremy MacNeil's eyes, would be Oliver Cromwell. I find this very interesting.

                                        It's also important to note that in VtM's lore, the Wars of the Three Kingdoms was a prolonged power struggle by the Toreador (as Scotland was a Toreador bastion, and they held influence in the Stuart Dynasty that came from Scotland) against the Ventrue (who had influence in Parliament). This should have influenced his perception of Vampire politics.

                                        Furthermore, his biography notes he later went to Ireland to fight there. There's actually not many prominent Irish rebellions after 1657 and when he departed for America (as he was there during the War of Independence). The most likely one was the Jacobite War in Ireland after the Glorious Revolution. This may simply be because at this point in time he hates the English (or perhaps as a Brujah, sees the Ventrue in England as the enemy), but I find it noteworthy that once again Jeremy is on the "wrong side" of what we would expect an Anarch to be. (An interesting idea is that around this the British Toreador might have seen Jeremy as a useful ally or pawn in their struggles against the Ventrue.) It's not until he arrives in the British colonies in America that the mortal side of things begin to conform to what we would expect of an Anarch.

                                        I bring this up because it is not something that is apparent. I doubt the original writers of Jeremy MacNeil intended it. But I think it's something to be considered when we think about the character of Jeremy MacNeil.

                                        Now I would not expect an uneducated Highlands warrior with a Rebel Nature to have any real political beliefs or ideology. Most of his views of freedom likely came later while he was a vampire. And probably more because of his Rebel nature than a coherent philosophical view. He seems to be principled and have an instinctive grasp on injustice, but he's probably not very politically astute or motivated. But as a natural leader of men, he attracts a lot of others who feel the same way on local issues.

                                        It makes sense then that while he is a symbol of the modern Anarchs, that it is others who are producing anything like a political manifesto. Salvador Garcia wrote the Status Perfectus and the Anarch Manifesto. However, Jeremy is smart. He's probably self-educated himself during his existence as a vampire, and like many autodidacts he has an eclectic view on things. It shouldn't be a surprise that his primary friend and ally, Crispus Attucks, is described as "not a deep thinker."

                                        I wonder if a good model for Jeremy MacNeil might possibly be Ernst Junger, another man who was a non-conformist, a warrior, originally loyal to a monarchy, once considered to be a "conservative revolutionary" but who increasingly rejected government's attempts to control people. Eventually Junger would develop the concept of an "Anarch" - as opposed to an anarchist. An Anarch championed the solitary individual who remains true to himself regardless of whatever external circumstances happened to be present. Junger would develop his theory on the Anarch more as philosophical problem for the individual than a political one for society. Eventually he describes that the Anarch is to anarchy what a monarch is to monarchy. The Anarch only wants to rule himself, and instead of opposing forms of government like an anarchist, just ignores them and acts appropriately. The Anarch represents total freedom and mankind's truest form, a kind of natural ubermensch that people should aspire to.

                                        While I don't think Ernst Junger's work served as inspiration to the creation of the character of Jeremy MacNeil, I feel it is in many ways appropriate to it. We don't need to adopt Junger's philosophy, just take certain elements and apply them to MacNeil. It would explain his reluctance to govern or develop any kind of political manifesto. He just wants to be left alone, and feels other vampires should feel that way too. He just doesn't have any political solution to the problem that others want power, that justice is needed to be imposed on the criminal element, or that there is a competition for scarce resources. Instead, he just falls back on "be a man and enforce justice yourself if someone tries to violate you." At least I feel that more properly depicts MacNeil than the idea that he is some kind of Social Darwinist who sees the strong winnowing out the weak. It would be more like an idea of "If you want to be free, only YOU can do that. I can't make you free yourself."

                                        There was an old notion that Anarchs believed in a "Libertas", which like Humanity, was something "real" for a vampire and meant vampires should be free. It was a strange concept, and it doesn't seem to have been promoted much in the books after the Anarch Cookbook (I don't know if this got much attention in V20 or V5). But if we take the idea seriously, we could even see Jeremy's attitude as being "Hey, dummy, you need to increase your Libertas and fight back or you'll never truly be free." Well, at least it'd be an excuse.

                                        That's why he and his coterie of friends defend their territory of Los Angeles, but he mostly refuses to play any additional role in "the Movement." He doesn't want one because he doesn't want power over others although he believes the Camarilla needs to end for Kindred freedom to happen, and he is willing to fight. He may be politically naive, but very principled as an individual. He's just doomed to be a symbol - a "modern day Patricia of Bolingbroke", and only get involved in local matters occasionally when he sees the local situation deteriorating too much and affecting him. In so far that he plays a useful role for the Anarch Movement, it would to provide a safe haven for other Anarchs who are incapable of ruling a domain (a barony) of their own, and need a local protector. Some of these theorists and provocateurs can then develop their politics and actions, and they perpetuate the idea that their "patron" and protector MacNeil is an important force for the Anarchs. If we want a positive view of MacNeil's barony, it would be as an "incubator" of ideas for the Anarch movement.

You could probably argue they're "as bad" but most pre modern societies have a problem with endemic violence and kratocratic tendencies.
