    Originally posted by Microcuchon View Post

    And how does denying the myth does exactly achieve control?
    The story in the book of Nod is a tale of how rebels are punished. First God is cursed for rebelling against God. Later, the Third Generation is cursed by Caine for rebelling against the second. It's a perfect control tool.
    Why in hell would Camarilla Elders reject this extremely convenient for them tale?

    because knowing the truth of the origin of vampires and the power of the eldest minimise the power of the camarilla.

    If all you know is that the biggest and baddest kindred are those in the inner council of the camarilla, you bend the knee and hopes to never attract their ire, for they are the illuminati and will rule forever.

    But then, if you know that there are blood gods that could crush them and every prince in with a thought (litterally, thanks auspex 8), and that soon, very soon, their awakening will mark the end of known vampire society, the threat of the inner council vanish.

    Why would you care about the claim of praxis of a prince? He's gonna kneel or die before the progenitors as much as you will.


    As far as most camarilla vampires, and a big portion of anarchs are concerned, the camarilla is indeed the most powerful vampiric power to have ever existed. As such, rebellion is at most a fool's hope.

    But if you know that the story of nod is true, then not only can you see past their pretense, but you may even, in your rebellion, try to wake up antediluvians, which the inner council fear more than anything, for they couldn't stand against such a being.

    ​​​​​


    By the way, this isn't a conjecture of mine, I'm basically paraphrasing multiple vtm books, nitably the guide to the camarilla.


    ​​​​​​​And it's also one of the in game argument of sabbat vampires against the camarilla, that they're at best pawn of the eldests, at worse nothing but a sand castle ignoring their inevitable demise.

    As for the myth of nod being about rebels being punished, this is pretty much extrapolating.

    Caine is depicted as the victim of a cosmic injustice, he's a sympathetic protagonist of the text, not a rebel deserving punishment.

    And while he is cursed, he embrace his cursed state, creating a mighty kingdom and a godlike family. If anything, the story is about being prideful and grandiose in one's damnation.

    As for the 13, whule they are all cursed for their paricide, they aren't suffering any more repercussion, caine leaving them to do as they please. In fact, that's exactly what they do next.
    If anything, they were better off than most rebels under the camarilla, for which many breaking of traditions ends in final death.

    Furthermore, the story of nod includes the prophecies of gehenna, the inevitable event in which, after many destructions, caine will come back to rule all vampires. This very part is opposed to any pretense of a vampiric authority like the camarilla.

        I've never entirely bought the whole "Caine cursing them" anyway. If the weaknesses are Caine's curse, then why do new bloodlines develop their own banes whenever they come into existence? I think a bane is just the side-effect of obsessing so much about a certain lifestyle that the blood calcifies around that obsession, granting the vampire an instinctive ability to develop related disciplines, but also giving them a related weakness.

        Gangrel, for example, obsessing so much over animals that ease with Protean and Animalism became instinctive for her, and her desiring so much to savagely get into brawls without consequence that Fort became instinctive as well. But the side effect was that she developed animal features.

        Then again Nosferatu is hard to explain via that model. So perhaps at least some of them legitimately were cursed.

The God of Fallen and Masquerade is one such entity, arguably the largest and most powerful of such entities, and it is a certifiable fucking prick.                                     EDIT: Elaborating a bit for clarity, two factors play into this.

                                    1) As the original development of World went along and interest started emerging in officially allowing gamelines to operate within each other, the "certain metpahysical actors have more weight on the scales of consensus that others" thing emerged as a sort of answer. "People don't believe in werewolvs modernly" asks one person, "so how can werewolves exist without causing Paradox with their existence", and the answer to that was "Well, Gaia 'believes' werewolves exist, and Gaia's opinion matters more than any given amount of humanity on the nature of reality." Basically, Gaia gets to veto humanity's mass belief on account of being the biosphere they live in.

                                    2) Demon the Fallen, for better or worse, took that notion and tried to answer and tie everything together by asserting a sort of superpositioned, "all things at once" version reality, where in angels literally dance around AND at the same time background radiation shapes the backdrop of reality, and that the Fall collapsed Schrodinger's reality into consensus dominancy-but the God of Fallen is still the creator of everything in the World of Darkness, and their presence(hell, their absence, as its more likely the case) has an enormous impact on the World of Darkness, even in it's fallen, consensus dominance ridden state. And That God is, in the canon of World, a giant fucking prick, because World was a lot more willing to smack talk Christianity.

                                    If the One of the Celestial Chorus is somehow not the God of Fallen (and Masquerade, because it seems weird to cut lines that particular way), then it's a construct of consensus, and doesn't have the primacy over reality that the God of Fallen has.
