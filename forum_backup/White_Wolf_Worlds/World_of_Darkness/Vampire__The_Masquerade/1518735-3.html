    Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post

    For those, I give them the Humanistic tenets from the main book. Such characters tend to lose Humanity quickly in my games, which is generally a prelude to them learning a Path. Such characters sometimes have Convictions early on, but they rarely keep them for long, since the Sabbat looks down on Touchstones, and punish Shovelheads who maintain them. Moreover, those Convictions are the usual Humanity Convictions, i.e. slightly darker but still very human beliefs.

    I find that at no point are Convictions especially important. Some players like to roleplay the tragedy of having Convictions, and therefore Touchstones, and eventually being forced to kill those Touchstones or face execution themselves. But that's just one story in a long campaign. And, of course, once the Touchstone dies, the Conviction dies with it.

    Most players start with Humanity, and therefore the standard Humanistic tenets, and either never have or quickly lose their Touchstones, and then switch to Paths, which don't lend themselves to Conviction mitigation for the reasons I stated in earlier posts and therefore no Touchstones are required.

    A Path is not a set of Convictions. It is not a way to mitigate stains from actions in opposition to Humanity. A Path is a fanatical set of beliefs so strong that it functions in a mechanically identical way to Humanity tenets, but requires a dogmatic fanaticism that doesn't lend itself to having Convictions or Touchstones.
    I think you're missing some minutiae about the points I'm making. The first thing is convictions serve more purpose than simply mitigating stains -- a character gains stains from violating convictions, as well. Functionally, they're just individualized tenets which can be lost over time.

    I personally dislike the "touchstone = conviction, and if the touchstone is lost so is the conviction" rule, and would disregard it or allow for non-human touchstones. But even then, touchstones merely represent the personification of that conviction, and humanity is a varied beast with many personifications, and nothing states vampires must interact with or have personal connection to their touchstone (to wit, the book gives examples of touchstones in which there's no feasible way the vampire could ever have met their touchstone). An inhumane vampire could well have a touchstone that is, for example, a serial killer, a third-world despot, or a war criminal walking free or imprisoned in the Hague. Hell, there's lots of room for some really meaty storytelling in having a touchstone that is a completely disgusting human being, and how one might deal with that.

    I'm gonna go into some pretty skeevy real-world politics on this one, so be forewarned but bear with me. Let's take, for example, a Lasombra on a Path of Night analog: a conviction for that might be something to the tune of "leave no being untainted by sin". For all intents and purposes, the character's touchstone for this conviction could be a former CIA operative who participated in raids, armed and trained terrorists, ran a black site, coordinated death squads, and/or personally tortured suspects during the war on terror. Just some of the most monstrous shit humanity has on tap, and to the Lasombra this mortal represents perfection in the art of spreading pain, hatred, and damnation to humanity.

    We don't even have to go to that extreme. A vampire on a Path of Power and the Inner Voice analog, with a conviction of "strive for control by any means necessary" could have as a touchstone...hell, pick a tech bro, the sleazier and wealthier the better. You can't convince me Steve Jobs wasn't on the Path of Power and the Inner Voice, in real life. Dig hard enough, you can identify possibilities for entirely human, still mortal, touchstones even for a path like Caine: a pimp, human trafficker, sweatshop owner, or slumlord treats humans like livestock (and gets away with it) just fine.

    All that loops back to the original point: tenets and convictions don't have to be moral, ethical, or humane. Just because the "Beast control" tracker is called Humanity, doesn't mean it's equitable with the Road/Path of Humanity. Regardless what v5's authors and designers want to claim about "getting back to gothic horror roots" and morality play-esque one true wayism. Touchstones likewise don't have to be good or innocent people, and to be frank, it's better storytelling if they aren't. This is something we already inherently know, as "vampire discovers touchstone isn't the person the vampire thought they were" is practically a rote plot hook for a chronicle.

    Hypocritical, contradictory, even antithetical to professed Sabbat virtues for them to have "favorite humans"? absolutely. Just like everything else about the Sabbat.

    Now, having discussed issues with touchstones, let's loop back around to chronicle tenets. Like I said in the other thread, not every path plays well with others. Those which do, are the ones with shared ethics. A feral, necronomist, and nihilist differ on a whole lot of things, but one thing they all share in common is they only kill out of necessity; they'll beg to differ with a noddist or unifier on the subject, because the former attributes zero innate value to life and the latter kills as a matter of expediency.

    Chronicle tenets is the perfect place for those shared ethics, because it builds the basis for a cohesive pack which won't be at each other's throats over major quandaries like the utility of the Beast, the value of killing versus dispensation by non-lethal means, the meaning of vampirism or the purpose of vampires, or simple ingroup/outgroup dynamics (for those paths which feature them). That's where the storyteller can say "this group of paths is okay, but that group of paths don't fit this chronicle and therefore won't be allowed".

        Lore about the history of various Roads and Paths also doesn't really line up with this idea that pledging yourself to one only allows for unswerving adherence to its decrees or unmitigated degeneration. The Paths themselves were deliberate and concerted efforts to alter the old Roads to make them more suited to Sabbat agendas in many cases, such as the Road of the Beast in comparison to the Path of the Feral Heart, the Path of Blood vs the Path of Caine, the Path of Chivalry vs the Path of Honorable Accord, the Road of Night vs the modern Path of Night (which is also notable for having internal variants with different Virtues in previous editions), and so forth. Some of this could simply be outsiders creating new frameworks for fresh recruits to be inducted into without having to reconcile their divergence from the "source material", but this can't be 100% true for all of them. For example, the Path of Caine is notably tied to the developing belief systems of Dastur Anosh as he struggled with the teachings of the Path of Blood.

        We also see similar variations with Paths/Roads outside of the Sabbat, such as with the different versions of the Path of Paradox, and the Path of the Warrior being developed as a reaction against perceived inadequacies of the Path of Typhon (and the Path of Typhon itself has some notable differences from the Road of the Serpent that could suggest similar ideological drift). Perhaps even more dramatically, there's the fact that adherents of the Path of Blood could conceive of and internalize changes to the Path for the sake of survival while subject to the Tremere curse, as opposed to simply wallowing just a step short of Wassail at all times as a best-case scenario until Ur-Shulgi intervened.

        I don't see how these sorts of changes would be possible without at least some ability to develop idiosyncratic beliefs about what truly matters in a Road or Path and prioritize certain values or teachings over others accordingly, and this sounds like something that would occupy the same conceptual space as V5 Convictions.

                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                        I think the current status of the Sabbat is due to the fact that the current leadership of the Sabbat aren't teaching them the REAL Paths anymore.                         I hadn't even thought of the V5 changes to Path ethics when I was writing, probably because I try not to think of them as a general rule, but they would be quite consistent with this view. The Necronomists are exhorted to "hasten death’s arrival, from humblest insect to the much-lamented flower of human life", which almost makes me think that the Beelzebeatit ritual from the same book was devised just so they wouldn't be too busy with their contractual obligation to squash bugs to do anything else with their time. The Albigensians now strive to "do whatever the Beast wants them to do"; this means a lot of impassioned and arbitrary killing without any apparent fear of degeneration, which would basically leave them at Path ratings of 3 at best (or more likely 1) in terms of the V20 hierarchy of sins.

                        Sure, I've mentioned examples of variants with shifting values, and in a similar vein there is one Path of Lilith variant even in the V20 Black Hand book that doesn't count killing among the sins against the Path, but it's hard to imagine "doing whatever the Beast wants you to do" being a sufficient basis for an actual functioning Path. The Path of Screams comes close, but even they have slavish devotion to infernal masters to keep their own Beasts at bay.

                        Edit: I was lazy and took my direct quote for the Path of Cathari from the Paradox wiki summary for the Path. In the interest of referencing the actual source, I'll quote the pertinent corresponding bit from its listed ethics: "Indulge every temptation; give oneself wholly to the wiles of the Beast." So yeah. Six of one and half a dozen of the other.
