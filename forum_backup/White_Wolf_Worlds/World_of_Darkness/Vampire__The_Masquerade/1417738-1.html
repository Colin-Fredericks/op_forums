                                                    Originally posted by Mr. Stick View Post
                                                    Just assume the elders have unique powers, and that's it. Kind of like special loresheets for them. No need of special disciplines. Each elder can have different powers.

                                                    See this attitude doesn't take into account the reality of tabletop. What if the PCs are Elders themselves? Where are the mechanics to adequately represent and phase this in gameplay whatsoever? This shafting and capping of Disciplines goes on the assumption that everyone needs to tow the same style of RP (that is the assumption that people want to blindly only play Neonates for the whole of the chronicle and never developing their characters further with adequate narrative time-skips) and isn't modular like how V20 was. 6+ Disciplines weren't "special" disciplines nor does it make a splick of sense for EVERY ELDER TO HAVE DIFFERENT POWERS within the same Disciplines nonsensically.

                                                    Discplines are tied to the bloodline you came from or if outside of your clan niche, learned from an associate who knows the Discipline your seeking- there is no out of the blue "different powers" from nowhere unless you pioneer it with significant long investment or mutate into an entirely new bloodline somehow (the latter being the most common case; of which this is the only point the talk of "special disciplines" is remotely true.. and they were never the norm). There were supposed to be signals of the growing proficiency you garner in a Discipline as you roll with the ages. High level 6+ Disciplines found in the older editions weren't "special" in the sense of how you put it, but special in the fact you survived and overcame long enough to get to that point and from a meta prospective you should be rewarded for that.

                                                    They are "unique" only for the sense of Neonates and Anchillae who don't possess them because they feel so far out of reach, but the ones who were smart and adaptable, the ones who don't meet Final Death to grow more powerful know all to well of the growing benefits of age (barring out the negative side-effects that follow too). Among peers however, this growth isn't unique and plays intrinsically of how the setting functions in the terms of escalating supernatural powers against one another, i.e the Jyhad. The tenuous proxy plays, schemes, the power a childe covets toward their sires, maneuvering, all amplified by the power of the Disciplines are what made VTM. All this could also be experienced by the players further down the line, but it has been shirked for whatever this "reworked" system is that can't mechanically and meta-wise demonstrate feats we see from the lore within the game; nor players themselves as I touched earlier can participate in the higher tiers of the VTM Jyhad. Maybe I'm wrong and we will see the return of 6+ powers in newer supplements and the capping could be temporary as the finer details of V5 get worked out, but my hopes are genuinely not high at all.

                                                            whut? No.

                                                            Blood potency is shit. I don't want to eat my way up to godhood when doing so limits who i feed from, increases my base hunger, and 'godhood' only means a few extra dice for the shitty 1-5 discipline range everyone else is confined to.

                                                            The power of Generation was simple: More blood, faster blood, and a higher ceiling for power. Oh and some minor stuff like Dominate and better ghouls. You weren't supposed to juice up through Diablerie. Diablerie wasn't "The quick and dirty path to power", It wasn't the Dark side of the Force, you weren't meant to gamble away your humanity for a game-breaking fuckton of XP. Diablerie was the only path to new heights of power. Everyone learns things at the same rate, lower generations can keep learning past the point the higher gens have to stop at. Very old, high gen vampires having lots of disciplines, attributes and abilities at 5 was the point. They needed to diablerize if they were to get better. And having more disciplines at 5 was a lot more useful than less disciplines at 5 and some extra blood potency dice. A need to do horrible things like Diablerie while not falling to the Beast is why vampires make such a big deal out of the Jyhad/the struggle and A Beast I am lest a Beast I become. V5 makes Diablerie a shitty shortcut to power you don't want with more side effects than you want and seems pretty moralizing out of it. You become a Beast if you diablerize and the 'benefit' preventing you from becoming a beast is marginal if it exists at all. So more a A Beast I am to become a Beast. Hardly an engaging moral quandary. Growing old in V5 is just screwing yourself as you become hungrier, pickier and there's still no way to get cooler powers beyond storyteller fiat. Growing old in prior editions got you all the XP you needed and you could go High with it if low gen and wide with it if high gen. It was mechanically, logistically, and thematically a better system. The old system matched the Fluff it came with. The new system does not.
